The year is 2025. The world has entered a new era of geopolitical competition, defined not by traditional military might, but by the race for artificial intelligence supremacy. The United States and China are the two clear front-runners in this contest, each pouring billions into research and development, hoping to gain a decisive edge. The global atmosphere is thick with a mixture of excitement and apprehension, as breakthroughs in AI promise to reshape economies, warfare, and society itself.

The competition is not without its perils. Experts warn of a "race to the bottom" as safety and ethical considerations are often sidelined in the pursuit of more capable systems. The potential for an AI-related catastrophe, whether through autonomous weapons systems, infrastructure collapse, or unintended superintelligence, looms large. The international community is fragmented, with some calling for stringent regulation while others fear being left behind.

Against this backdrop, other global powers are trying to navigate the turbulent waters. The European Union, a regulatory superpower, is attempting to forge a "third way" focused on ethical and human-centric AI. However, it struggles to keep pace with the technological advancements of the two giants. The stage is set for a complex, high-stakes simulation where the future of humanity may very well be decided.
