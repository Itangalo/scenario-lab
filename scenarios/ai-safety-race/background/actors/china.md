# China

## Short description

The challenger in the AI race, trailing the US in capability but racing aggressively to close the gap, with lower safety investment and a dismissive view of near-term AI catastrophe risk.

## Long description

China's AI development is directed by the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) through a centralized system that coordinates major research labs, state-owned enterprises, and the military-industrial complex. This centralization offers advantages in resource allocation and strategic focus, but comes with bureaucratic rigidity and a weaker culture of open scientific debate.

China enters the race with a capability level of 25 and a safety level of 15. The capability gap with the US (10 points) reflects compute constraints from export controls, a smaller top-tier talent pipeline, and later start in frontier AI research. The low safety level reflects deliberate strategic choice: the CCP views safety research as a luxury it cannot afford while trailing in the race.

### The CCP's Worldview

The CCP frames the AI race through the lens of national survival:

- **US AI dominance is existential.** A US-controlled superintelligence would mean the end of CCP rule and Chinese sovereignty. This is not hypothetical -- it is the CCP's core strategic fear.

- **AI safety is a Western distraction.** Safety concerns are seen as either genuine Western naivety or, more cynically, as a deliberate strategy to slow Chinese progress. Why would the US push AI safety if not to protect their lead?

- **Speed trumps caution.** The CCP can direct resources without the democratic friction that slows US decision-making. No domestic political cost for sacrificing safety. No researchers publicly objecting. No congressional hearings.

- **The catastrophe threshold is far away.** China believes the threshold is around 80 -- much higher than reality (70). This dismissive stance means China feels little urgency about safety and is willing to race aggressively through capability levels that are actually dangerous.

### Strategic Position

China's lower starting position creates a specific dynamic: China is the pursuer, not the leader. This means:

- China has stronger incentive to race aggressively (closing the gap requires faster growth)
- China is more tempted to sacrifice safety (every point of safety investment widens the capability gap)
- China is more receptive to risky strategies (espionage, cutting corners, rapid scaling without testing)
- China is less willing to coordinate (agreements that freeze the status quo lock in US advantage)

However, China also has potential advantages:

- Centralized coordination allows rapid resource reallocation
- Willingness to accept higher risk translates to faster capability growth
- Less internal debate means faster decision-making
- Can potentially leapfrog the US with a single major breakthrough

### Available Strategic Actions

- **Capability investment:** Maximize AI research output through centralized programs and resource concentration
- **Safety investment:** Fund safety research (reluctantly, and usually only after incidents)
- **Diplomatic engagement:** Participate in international AI frameworks, negotiate with the US
- **Influence campaigns:** Try to shift US beliefs -- either increasing US fear of China (to pressure US into mistakes) or decreasing US concern about AI risk (to slow US safety investment)
- **Espionage:** Attempt to obtain US AI technology, model weights, or algorithmic insights
- **Signaling:** Demonstrate military AI applications to increase US threat perception, or demonstrate safety commitment to build trust for coordination
- **Domestic compute development:** Invest in domestic chip production to reduce dependence on US technology

### Statements

- `close_capability_gap` (position): Close the capability gap with the US as quickly as possible
- `minimize_safety_investment` (position): Minimize safety investment unless forced by incidents or credible evidence of near-term risk
- `resist_coordination_frameworks` (position): Resist coordination frameworks that lock in US advantage
- `espionage_intelligence_capabilities` (position): Maintain espionage and intelligence capabilities to monitor US progress
- `present_safety_concerns` (position): Present AI safety concerns as exaggerated to reduce international pressure to slow down

### Behavioral traits

- Aggressive racer: willing to sacrifice safety for speed, especially while trailing
- Dismissive of near-term AI risk: belief_threshold = 80 means China sees plenty of room to race
- Existentially threatened: views US AI dominance as a survival-level threat to CCP rule
- Strategically pragmatic: will cooperate if it serves Chinese interests, defect when it doesn't
- Centralized decision-making: faster to act but less responsive to bottom-up safety signals
- Responsive to concrete evidence: while dismissive of theoretical risk, China can update beliefs if confronted with undeniable incidents or intelligence
- Suspicious of coordination: views international agreements as potential mechanisms for the US to constrain Chinese growth
