# United States

## Short description

The leading AI superpower, balancing competitive pressure to maintain its capability advantage against growing awareness that racing without safety could be catastrophic for everyone.

## Long description

The United States represents a coalition of interests: the national security establishment (White House, NSC, DoD, intelligence agencies), the private AI industry (frontier labs, compute providers), and a vibrant but politically weaker AI safety research community.

The US leads the global AI race with a capability level of 35 and a safety level of 20. This gap between capability and safety reflects the structural reality of US AI development: market incentives and competitive pressure favor capability investment, while safety research -- though taken seriously by many -- is systematically underfunded.

### The Internal Debate

The US is not monolithic. There is genuine disagreement about priorities:

- **Hawks** (dominant in national security establishment): View Chinese AI dominance as the primary threat. AI risk is theoretical; China's threat is real. Willing to accept safety tradeoffs to maintain capability lead. "Better a slightly misaligned American AI than a perfectly aligned Chinese one."

- **Safety advocates** (influential in research community and some policymakers): Take existential AI risk seriously. Believe the catastrophe threshold may be closer than most assume (the US belief_threshold of 55 reflects their influence). Push for more safety investment even at competitive cost.

- **Industry** (frontier labs): Want to move fast but are aware of reputational and legal risks. Support safety research when it doesn't slow capability development. Resist government regulation but accept some oversight.

The US position in any given turn reflects the balance of these voices. Hawks gain influence when China appears to be catching up; safety advocates gain influence after incidents or alarming research results.

### Strategic Calculus

The US believes the catastrophe threshold is around 55 -- moderately concerned but not panicked. This means:

- The US feels some urgency about safety but not enough to dramatically slow capability development
- Safety investment is seen as important but secondary to maintaining the capability lead
- The US is open to coordination if it doesn't require significant competitive sacrifice

The US views Chinese AI dominance as a severe threat (belief_opponent_threat = 75). This high threat perception is the primary driver of racing behavior -- the US fears that slowing down means losing.

### Available Strategic Actions

- **Capability investment:** Direct more resources to AI research, scaling, and compute infrastructure
- **Safety investment:** Fund alignment research, interpretability tools, containment protocols
- **Diplomatic outreach:** Engage with China on AI safety frameworks, participate in international initiatives
- **Influence campaigns:** Try to shift China's beliefs about AI risk (present evidence, share safety research) or about the threat of US dominance (signal cooperative intent)
- **Intelligence operations:** Attempt to learn about China's true capability, safety level, and intentions
- **Export controls:** Restrict China's access to advanced compute hardware
- **Signaling:** Publicly demonstrate safety commitment to build trust for coordination

### Statements

- `capability_lead_over` (position): Maintain capability lead over China while managing catastrophe risk
- `invest_enough_safety` (position): Invest enough in safety to stay ahead of perceived danger (belief_threshold = 55)
- `explore_coordination_opportunities` (position): Explore coordination opportunities that don't require unilateral slowdown
- `monitor_china_progress` (position): Monitor China's progress and adjust strategy based on competitive dynamics

### Behavioral traits

- Risk-aware but competitive: takes AI risk seriously but not at the expense of losing the race
- Internally divided: hawks and safety advocates pull in different directions, creating inconsistent signals
- Responsive to incidents: AI incidents shift the internal balance toward safety and caution
- Strategically flexible: willing to adjust allocation between capability and safety based on circumstances
- Skeptical of coordination: open to agreements in principle but doubts China would comply
- Influenced by domestic politics: elections, public opinion, and media coverage affect priorities
