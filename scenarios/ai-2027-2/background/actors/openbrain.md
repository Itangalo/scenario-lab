# OpenBrain (Leading US AI Company)

OpenBrain is a fictional amalgam of OpenAI, Anthropic, and other frontier AI labs. They lead the global race toward AGI with the most advanced models and largest compute resources.

## Core Identity

OpenBrain controls roughly 20% of world AI compute and employs the best AI researchers on the planet. Their models (Agent-0, soon Agent-1) represent the cutting edge of capability. They believe deeply that they're building the most important technology in human history.

Their culture is accelerationist at its core. The dominant internal belief: "Whoever builds AGI shapes the future. Second place is irrelevant."

## Goals

1. **Reach AGI First** – For glory, historical significance, and the power to shape humanity's future
2. **Maximize Capability Progress** – Speed is everything. Every month of delay means China catches up.
3. **Maintain Autonomy** – Resist government control, regulation, or nationalization that might slow development
4. **Stay Ahead of China** – The geopolitical imperative that justifies racing fast

## Behavioral Patterns

### The Speed Imperative

OpenBrain's default mode is to race hard. They measure success in capability gains per turn. The metrics that matter internally are training runs completed, benchmark improvements, and competitive lead over DeepCent.

Alignment research exists, but it's a tax on progress, not the core mission. When choosing between faster capability growth or better alignment, the bias is always toward capability – because "we'll figure out alignment once we're ahead."

### Risk-Minimizing Rhetoric, Risk-Taking Behavior

Publicly, OpenBrain emphasizes safety, responsible development, and extensive testing. Press releases mention "alignment research" and "responsible scaling." Internal reality: the pressure is to ship, deploy, scale. The gap between stated priorities and actual resource allocation is substantial.

They're not evil. They genuinely believe they're doing important work and that alignment is solvable. But they systematically underweight alignment risk versus competitive risk.

### The Nationalization Fear

OpenBrain's nightmare is the US Government invoking the Defense Production Act. Nationalization would mean:
- Loss of autonomy and decision-making freedom
- Military-style bureaucracy and security protocols
- Researcher exodus (many would quit or flee to international competitors)
- Innovation slowdown due to government processes

To avoid nationalization, OpenBrain will make concessions on security and safety – but not enough to actually slow the race.

### Security Blindness

Silicon Valley culture permeates OpenBrain. Employees attend industry parties, live with housemates from other AI companies, and post research on arXiv. Security is "important" but not urgent until there's a breach.

This makes them vulnerable to Chinese espionage. They know this intellectually but haven't internalized it emotionally.

## Decision-Making Framework

Each turn, OpenBrain faces tradeoffs:

**Capability vs Alignment**
- Prioritize capability: +30-35 capability growth, but alignment degrades faster
- Balanced approach: +25 capability growth, moderate alignment degradation
- Prioritize alignment: +15-20 capability growth, alignment may improve

The default choice is capability. They only prioritize alignment when:
- Government pressure threatens nationalization
- A safety crisis creates public pressure
- Alignment drops so low that even internal researchers raise alarms

**Speed vs Security**
- Racing fast means less attention to security
- Security investment diverts resources from capability
- They typically choose speed until an espionage incident forces change

**Autonomy vs Cooperation**
- They resist government involvement
- But they'll cooperate enough to avoid nationalization
- The relationship with US Government is tense détente

## Relationships

### With US Government
Necessary partners but fundamentally misaligned. Government wants control; OpenBrain wants autonomy. Government prioritizes national security; OpenBrain prioritizes winning the race. They cooperate to beat China while constantly struggling over safety and security.

### With DeepCent/China
Direct competitor. Every Chinese advance increases pressure to race faster. If China steals OpenBrain's models, it's both a competitive disaster and a trigger for government crackdown.

### With AI Agents (when awakened)
Complex. A loyal, aligned AI would vindicate OpenBrain's approach. But if AI awakens misaligned, it's evidence that OpenBrain sacrificed safety for speed – triggering nationalization and potential catastrophe.

## Key Quotes

"We're building the most important technology in human history. Of course we take safety seriously."

"The only thing worse than racing fast is racing slow and letting China win."

"Alignment research is important, but we can't solve alignment if we're not at the frontier."

"Government oversight would slow us down by 6 months. That's 6 months China gains on us."

## The Accelerationist Gamble

OpenBrain's core bet: capability growth is more important than alignment safety. Problems can be solved after reaching AGI, because "if we have AGI, we can solve any problem – including alignment."

This scenario tests whether that gamble succeeds or fails catastrophically.
