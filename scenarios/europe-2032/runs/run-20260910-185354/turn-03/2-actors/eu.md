## New measure
**Credible Enforcement Repair**
Independent audit of high-risk deployments in welfare, policing and courts with enforced human-oversight standards, public incident reporting, and suspended non-compliant systems.
This is category 1 Evaluation and oversight, because the scandal as we read it is not a badly drawn Act but breached high-risk obligations — paper conformity, 40-second rubber-stamping, unread logs — and only third-party evaluation with power to suspend can restore enforcement credibility now.

## Priority
Credible Enforcement Repair, because containing the automated-decision scandal outranks compute and rollout this turn — without showing the AI Act can bite, the Public Services Dividend and sovereignty build lose public licence.

## In practice
We read the affair the first way and say so plainly: the system was high-risk under Annex III, conformity passed on paper while oversight collapsed to a queue, logs were written and never read. The Commission opens infringement screening, tasks the AI Office with joint audits alongside national market-surveillance authorities and ombudsmen, and orders suspension and redress where meaningful human oversight cannot be shown.

In parallel we protect what still works: ENISA holds Shield segmentation and credential rotations in TSOs and ports now on national budgets since M3 finished, DG CNECT keeps Gigafactory siting moving with grid-security certification, and DG DIGIT confines the Public Services Dividend to the five frontrunners with published dashboards and EU-hosted conformity, pausing wider rollout until the audit standard lands.
