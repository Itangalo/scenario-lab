## New measure
Emergency Resilience Surge  
A time-bound, high-intensity programme to harden critical infrastructure against AI-enabled cyber and bio threats, with binding targets and immediate funding.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect: resilience +25, ai_safety +5 (indirectly via faster detection and containment), public_sentiment +5 (by demonstrating protective action after bio_incident)`  
`Applies to: EU member states, with enforcement through EUCI directives and cohesion funding conditionality`

## Priority  
Emergency Resilience Surge — because the bio_incident and cyber_test_shot prove that harm is no longer theoretical, and our current resilience is insufficient to absorb attacks already in the wild, regardless of our longer-term sovereignty goals.

## In practice  
We are treating the bio_incident and cyber_test_shot as wake-up calls that demand immediate action. This turn, we are launching the Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 122 TFEU for critical infrastructure protection, with the Commission proposing a Council regulation that mandates AI-specific threat modelling and intrusion detection upgrades across energy, water, health, and transport sectors by Q2 2027. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) and the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC) will co-lead technical implementation, with a new Joint AI Threat Response Cell established in Brussels to coordinate cross-domain detection and incident reporting.

Funding will come from a reallocation of €15bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, front-loaded through the EU budget with member states required to match 30% nationally. We are offering fast-track access to InvestAI Gigafactory compute allocations as an incentive for early adopters. Resistance from smaller member states over cost and sovereignty concerns is expected; we are countering with a solidarity clause that caps national contributions at 0.1% of GDP and allows pooling through the EU Civil Protection Mechanism.

The Tech sovereignty package continues on track, with the Commission now negotiating with national energy regulators to fast-track grid access for AI data centres in designated "sovereignty zones" — areas with dual-use potential for public and strategic AI workloads. We are also pressing ASML and national export authorities to formalise a reciprocity framework: access to EUV maintenance and upgrades conditioned on guaranteed compute access for EU public institutions. This is not leverage we wield lightly, but the current level of dependence is untenable.
