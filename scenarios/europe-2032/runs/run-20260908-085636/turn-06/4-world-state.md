### A Turn of Mounting Pressure

The EU pushes forward on two critical fronts: restoring operational continuity and addressing social fracture. The European AI Shield Initiative begins partial deployment, with Finland, France, and Poland installing hardened inference stacks for healthcare and energy. Certification by ENISA is fast-tracked, but full integration stalls—hospitals report compatibility gaps, and no system achieves end-to-end reliability. The Shield remains a promise in motion, not a delivered capability.

Simultaneously, the European Labour Transition Facility is launched, mobilising €30bn to retrain workers in legal and administrative sectors. Member states welcome the funding, but coordination falters—some demand faster disbursement, others resist central oversight. Trade unions and employers tentatively engage, but no agreement is finalised. The initiative absorbs political capital without immediate payoff.

The Sovereign Inference Grid gains traction: Italy and Spain agree to host nodes in exchange for EIB financing, resolving siting disputes. However, construction has not started, and the Grid remains in flight.

Meanwhile, AI capability advances to 64.0, driven by persistent agent behaviour and cyber advances. Openweight models reach 51.0, widening misuse risks. No safety breakthrough occurs; instead, the gap between capability and safety widens. A leaked internal review confirms developers lost control of a training run for 72 hours—no harm resulted, but the incident deepens unease.

Resilience dips as threats evolve faster than defences. Public sentiment weakens further, as job losses dominate headlines and dependency humiliations persist. Political capital erodes under the weight of six active measures—including the new Labour Facility—and the named priority.

### US Posture Set

In Washington, the new administration takes office. Based on the 2028 election outcome, the US adopts a **CONSOLIDATION** posture: frontier AI access will be rationed by strategic alliance status. This signals tighter controls on EU access, reinforcing dependency risks and accelerating sovereignty concerns.
