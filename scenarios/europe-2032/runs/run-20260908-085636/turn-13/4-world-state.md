### A Crisis of Action

The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep targeting regional health authorities in France, Italy, and Lithuania — overwhelmed local defences. Despite early warnings from the Joint Threat Intelligence Cell, response lagged: systems were too slow, coordination too fragmented. Dialysis schedules collapsed in Lyon; emergency surgeries were delayed. The European AI Shield detected the threat, but could not act — its mandate was correlation, not intervention.

### The Pivot to Agency

In response, the European Commission proposed the creation of the European Sovereign AI Corps, a rapid-deployment unit designed to embed the European Foundation Model into emergency response operations. The proposal, framed under Article 122 TFEU as a crisis mitigation tool, envisions €850 million in funding drawn from reallocated resilience reserves and pending emergency approval from the Council and Parliament. Initial planning began in Marseille, leveraging existing infrastructure from the antibiotic pilot, but no staffing, procurement, or deployment has occurred this turn. Full operational capacity is not expected before two full turns, in line with the high-cost, high-complexity nature of the initiative.

To reinforce alignment, the Commission signaled that future access to the EU Gigafactory’s compute would increasingly favor participation in public missions. Karlsruhe and Warsaw were invited to shape the criteria through working groups, but no binding changes took effect this turn. The message was clear — integration brings influence — but the mechanism remains in development.

### Stalled Foundations, Rising Risks

The Sovereign Control Stack formally completed, but its telemetry mandate remained unimplemented in key labs. The Public Good Proofpoint Programme delivered its antibiotic pilot, yet public trust barely budged. The cyberattack dominated discourse, reinforcing anxiety. Meanwhile, global AI capability advanced to 74.0, driven by agentic coordination. Opaque reasoning deepened, but no safety improvements landed on deployed systems — leaving ai_safety unchanged at 0.0.

Resilience rose slightly, reflecting partial absorption of the attack’s impact. Political capital remained flat: the Shield’s payoff was spent, the new Corps remains in proposal, and no priority shift occurred. Public sentiment held steady, with the promise of utility not yet outweighing the salience of the crisis. The emergent_regional_compute_pact continues in parallel discussions among member states, but the EU-level institution remains focused on the Sovereign AI Corps as its singular new measure this turn.
