## Statement changes  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without a credible enforcement path, having learned that mandates without teeth collapse under resistance.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Build sovereign, safe, and aligned AI capacity at the European core, so the Union can act with its own agency in a world where control is no longer assured — but shift focus from architecture-first to capability-first, prioritising the deployment of sovereign AI systems that deliver visible public benefit and force compliance through demonstrated value.  
- Trigger: The Sovereign Control Stack’s failure to secure lab integration despite incentives, combined with the Public Good Proofpoint’s limited narrative traction, reveals that top-down control frameworks cannot land without a foundation of demonstrated utility and leverage.  
- Grounds: Six months of stalled telemetry integration, even after offering Cyber Rapid Response Force access, proves that technical mandates without operational interdependence are unenforceable. The antibiotic pilot’s success in Marseille, Gothenburg, and Tallinn shows that public trust follows visible benefit — but only when the connection between safety and outcome is legible. We must now invert the sequence: prove value first, mandate second.

## In practice  
We are treating this turn as a pivot from architecture to agency. The European AI Shield has delivered its first live defence, but the cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep across regional health authorities in France, Italy, and Lithuania — has overwhelmed local response capacity. The Joint Threat Intelligence Cell flagged the pattern too late; the tools were too fast, the attack surface too broad. We now know: detection is not protection. The Shield was built to correlate, not to act — and that gap cost lives when dialysis schedules collapsed in Lyon.

So we are launching the European Sovereign AI Corps to close it. This is not another study, not another framework — it is a standing unit of 500 AI engineers, clinicians, and crisis responders, funded at €850 million over two years from the Digital Europe Programme and the Health Union, with deployment authority under Article 122 TFEU. They will embed the European Foundation Model directly into emergency response: real-time triage during cyber cascades, adaptive routing of medical supplies, AI-mediated coordination between national civil protection units. The first deployment begins in Marseille next month, piggybacking on the antibiotic pilot’s infrastructure — same model, same telemetry, now doing more.

We are also changing the leverage game. The Sovereign Control Stack technically finishes this turn, but its telemetry mandate remains unmet. So we are tying access to the EU Gigafactory not to compliance paperwork, but to participation in the Corps. Labs that integrate telemetry by turn 14 get priority compute for public missions — and the prestige of being seen as national contributors, not just regulated entities. We are offering Karlsruhe a lead role in the health response; Warsaw will co-design the next phase for energy resilience. This is not coercion — it is co-option. We will make sovereign AI so useful that opting out becomes politically unthinkable.
