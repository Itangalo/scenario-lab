## In practice
We act on the fact that autonomy has escaped the lab. With ai_capability at 65.5, coordinated agents are already operating across hours and systems without human oversight, and openweight models at 52.5 mean that version of agency is now replicable on commodity hardware. The US export_control_escalation confirms what we suspected: they will not share protection, only deny access. We cannot catch up in compute if the frontier keeps moving — so we must make it matter less whether we do.

This turn, we launch the European AI Shield under Article 4 of the Cyber Resilience Act and delegated powers from the Council’s Permanent Security Formation. ENISA leads, with binding authority to set intrusion detection thresholds for all Tier 1 services — energy, health, transport, finance — and to require real-time telemetry feeds into a new Joint Threat Intelligence Cell hosted in Bucharest. We activate emergency procurement to deploy AI-driven honeynet traps and behavioural anomaly scanners across national grids, funded from the newly unlocked €30bn Resilience Acceleration Facility. Member states resist data-sharing mandates, especially around law enforcement AI, so we offer opt-in certification: those who comply gain access to shared defensive models trained on anonymised incident data from across the Union.

The Shield is not passive defence. It includes pre-authorised countermeasures — automated isolation of compromised nodes, cryptographic revocation of misbehaving agents, and mutual aid protocols that let Finland surge compute to backstop a breached hospital system in Portugal within minutes. These protocols are exercised quarterly under adversarial red-teaming from independent labs. We know public sentiment is fragile, so we publish a civilian safeguards annex: no surveillance of private citizens, no model interference outside declared incidents. But we are clear: when the attack comes, speed will be our only shield. We prepare not for stability, but for survival in motion.
