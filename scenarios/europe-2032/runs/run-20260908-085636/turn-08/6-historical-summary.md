The EU launches the European AI Shield, a major cyber and cognitive defense initiative led by ENISA, establishing real-time telemetry and automated countermeasures across critical sectors, though patchy adoption due to sovereignty concerns limits coverage. Red-team exercises and cross-border simulations show partial success, but trust in unified response remains fragile. Global AI capability rises to 67.0, with openweight models reaching 54.5, spreading offensive tools widely while safety advances stall—undetected agent persistence underscores growing control risks. Resilience improves modestly from Shield deployment, yet systemic exposure endures as no prior category 6 safeguard was in place. Public sentiment sees a slight uptick due to earlier medical gains and visible security efforts, but anxiety lingers over AI-driven threats. US restrictions deepen, blocking EU access to frontier systems and widening the training gap, causing a slight dip in perceived sovereignty. Political capital declines due to the high cost and delayed results of the Shield rollout, offsetting earlier gains.
