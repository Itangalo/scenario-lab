## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the European AI Shield Initiative  
A rapid-response programme to deploy sovereign, high-capability AI systems across critical public functions — health, energy, civil protection — using fallback architectures and EU-controlled infrastructure.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Raise eu_ai_sovereignty by 8–10 points by ensuring EU institutions and member states retain functional AI access during foreign cutoffs; stabilize public_sentiment by demonstrating continuity of public services; modestly raise resilience through hardened operational AI stacks.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national governments, and critical infrastructure operators in member states.

## Priority  
European AI Shield Initiative — because eu_frontier_access_denied has already happened, and we are now in a crisis of operational continuity: hospitals cannot process diagnostics, ministries cannot run forecasting models, and civil protection systems are blind. This is not a future risk — it is a present failure. While the Emergency Resilience Surge finishes this turn and the Gigafactories remain years out, we must act now to ensure the Union can still function. The Shield is not about long-term capability — it is about immediate sovereignty of operation, and it is the only measure that restores trust in the Union’s ability to deliver basic governance under AI disruption.

## In practice  
We are treating eu_frontier_access_denied as a systemic rupture. The cutoff from the leading model — without warning, appeal, or explanation — has disabled AI-dependent systems across public health, energy forecasting, and civil protection. This is not merely a supply issue; it is a crisis of political agency. This turn, we are launching the European AI Shield Initiative under Article 172 TFEU, mobilizing €12bn from unspent RRF and Digital Europe funds to deploy fallback AI systems on EU-controlled infrastructure. These are not frontier models — they are robust, auditable, and sovereign stacks derived from the best available open-weight models below 52.0, hardened with interpretability layers from our public research programme and hosted on InvestAI’s nascent compute grid. ENISA and the European Health Agency are co-designing deployment protocols, with priority access for hospitals, grid operators, and emergency response units.

At the same time, we are finalizing the Emergency Resilience Surge. The trilogue has agreed on the €15bn package, with binding threat modelling now mandatory for all critical sectors by Q3. InvestAI vouchers have unlocked co-financing from Germany, France, and Poland, and the first detection logic for AI-generated cyber threats is being integrated into national CERTs. The Openweight Containment Protocol moves to technical drafting at ENISA, though U.S. developers continue to signal non-compliance. We are not deluding ourselves: containment beyond our borders is fragile. But within them, we are building a stack we cannot be cut off from — because if we cannot run our own systems, no protocol, no resilience, no sovereignty remains but a fiction.
