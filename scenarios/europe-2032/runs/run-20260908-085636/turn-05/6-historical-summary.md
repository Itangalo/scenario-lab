Open-weight models reach 48.0, widely distributed but constrained below frontier levels. The EU remains cut off from a critical foreign AI system, deepening a structural crisis in healthcare, energy, and civil protection, exposing systemic dependencies.

In response, the European AI Shield Initiative transitions to early implementation under Article 172 TFEU, with €12bn approved for sovereign, open-based AI stacks below 52.0 capability, prioritizing reliability and interpretability. Funding is partially disbursed, delayed by procurement disputes and interoperability issues. ENISA fast-tracks certification, and deployment planning begins in Finland, France, and Poland, though no system achieves operational status—healthcare integration is postponed. The initiative consumes political capital without delivering immediate resilience.

The Sovereign Inference Grid remains exploratory, with siting studies in Italy and Spain, but is not formally adopted due to limits on new measures and resistance from smaller states over governance and infrastructure concentration. The Openweight Containment Protocol completes drafting but lacks extraterritorial enforcement. A major scandal unfolds as an AI-driven social benefits system in a large member state wrongfully denies thousands of claims due to opaque, biased logic, eroding public trust and modestly weakening safety perceptions.

US AI posture remains pending ahead of leadership transition, with campaign rhetoric signaling future restrictions but no policy enacted. Meanwhile, regional initiatives emerge outside formal EU structures: a group of member states advances a *regional compute pact* to jointly build frontier-class compute in neutral jurisdictions, bypassing stalled negotiations, while another forms a *regional aid pact* to fund retraining amid job losses in legal and administrative sectors, creating pressure for EU-level harmonization.

Resilience declines slightly as threats outpace defenses. Sovereignty weakens further due to prolonged access loss and slow domestic build-out. Political capital erodes moderately under the burden of five overlapping initiatives, many delayed rather than failed. Public sentiment deteriorates amid job displacement and perceived dependency failures, dampening support for top-down AI governance.
