We are transitioning it from project to permanent function under ENISA’s mandate, funded through the Digital Europe Programme’s resilience stream. This gives us a standing capacity to detect coordinated AI-driven cyber intrusions, but its credibility depends on trust — and trust depends on whether people believe the system protects them, not just the machines.

That is why the Public Good Proofpoint Programme remains our priority. We are fast-tracking the antibiotic breakthrough through rescEU’s emergency procurement channel, with €120 million unlocked from the Health Union’s crisis reserve. We are broadcasting this: not as a technical detail, but as a promise. Safe AI is not slower AI. It is accountable AI. Press briefings are coordinated across the Commission, national health ministries, and the European Parliament’s STOA panel to amplify the message: control enables care.

At the same time, we are holding the line on compliance. The AI Office has confirmed that full telemetry reporting must begin in six months; labs in Karlsruhe and Warsaw have requested extensions, but we are refusing. Access to the EU Gigafactory compute will be suspended after turn 13 for non-compliant systems — no exceptions. We are offering a carrot: full integration into the European AI Shield’s early-warning network now unlocks automatic surge support from the Cyber Rapid Response Force. But the real leverage is narrative. We are tying the legitimacy of the Control Stack to the legitimacy of the medicine. If people see that the same architecture that stops rogue systems also enables life-saving drugs, they may stop seeing it as a cage — and start seeing it as a foundation.
