- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The frontier has moved beyond dependence into control uncertainty — capability is now autonomous, open, and persistent, and safety is regressing. Our mandate remains the same, but the path has changed: we can no longer assume that access to capability, even if sovereign, implies control. The next two years must be defined not by catching up, but by mastering what we build, ensuring it remains legible, contained, and aligned — not just to EU values, but to EU agency. This is not a retreat from capability, but a reorientation: sovereignty without safety is vulnerability.

## New measure
European Foundation Model Initiative  
A public-private partnership to train and deploy a frontier-capable, EU-governed foundation model under strict safety and transparency conditions, with weights secured and access limited to vetted institutions and sovereign infrastructure.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`

## Priority
European AI Shield — because the cyber_major_incident this turn proves the threat is no longer hypothetical: automated attacks are landing, defences are fragmented, and resilience remains below the threshold where cascading failure can be ruled out, making Shield completion a matter of operational necessity, not just strategic ambition.

## In practice
We are acting on the fact that the attack has already come. The cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across regional health systems, coordinated by AI agents exploiting a poisoned software dependency — has exposed the limits of our current posture. Detection was delayed, response uneven, and attribution remains unclear. The offensive tools were model-generated, running on hardware no export control can reach. We are no longer ahead of the curve — we are behind it, and the Shield is the only thing standing between disruption and collapse.

This turn, we double down on the Shield’s implementation. ENISA activates the Joint Threat Intelligence Cell in Bucharest, mandating real-time telemetry from all Tier 1 operators. We deploy AI-driven honeynets funded from the Resilience Acceleration Facility, now expanded to €35bn after emergency Council approval under Article 4 of the Cyber Resilience Act. To overcome sovereignty resistance, we refine the opt-in certification: member states that share data gain priority access to defensive models and mutual aid compute, while those that opt out accept full responsibility for their own incident response. Red-teaming intensifies, with Finland and Estonia simulating surge support for southern and eastern members.

Simultaneously, we launch the European Foundation Model Initiative, recognising that dependence is no longer the only risk — loss of control is. We cannot rely on foreign models, nor trust open ones. So we build our own: a sovereign, auditable, and secure foundation model, trained on EU data, secured by state-actor-grade protocols, and governed by a new European AI Foundation. We secure initial compute from the Gigafactories in Finland and France, and offer binding talent incentives to retain researchers at INRIA, Max Planck, and ELLIS. This is not a moonshot — it is a lifeline. We will not catch the frontier by chasing it; we will meet it on ground we control.
