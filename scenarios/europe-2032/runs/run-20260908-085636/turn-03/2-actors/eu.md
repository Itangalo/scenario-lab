A binding EU-wide framework to condition public compute access, research funding, and market access on developers’ adherence to delayed-release windows and safety benchmarks for open-weight models.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`

## Priority  
Emergency Resilience Surge — because the openweight_frontier_release event proves that containment is now breaking at the edges, and our only real buffer against cascading failure in critical systems is the resilience architecture we are mid-stride in building; delaying it risks irreversible exposure.

## In practice  
We are treating the openweight_frontier_release as a systemic breach. The capability is already out, yes — but the precedent cannot stand. This turn, we are drafting the Openweight Containment Protocol under Article 114 TFEU, conditioning access to EU public compute (via InvestAI), Horizon Europe AI funding, and digital market licensing on a developer’s adherence to a 12-month open-release moratorium and independent safety verification. The European Commission is working with ENISA and the AI Office to define technical benchmarks for model transparency, weight security, and misuse resistance — thresholds that must be met before any model above 45.0 can be openly released by an entity operating in or serving the EU. We are not banning open models; we are making responsible release the price of participation.

At the same time, we are accelerating the Emergency Resilience Surge. The Council’s legal service has confirmed the validity of Article 122 TFEU for binding threat-modelling obligations, and we are now in trilogue with Parliament to secure the €15bn reallocation from Digital Europe and RRF funds. To break the deadlock on national co-financing, we are offering InvestAI compute vouchers to member states that ratify their contributions by Q1 2028 — a tangible incentive to move fast. Pilots in Germany (energy grid), France (health emergency response), and Poland (cyber defence) are entering design phase, with ENISA and ECDC jointly overseeing detection logic and escalation protocols. This is not abstract preparation: we are building the reflexes to survive the next incident, and the next, and the next.

Public sentiment remains our fragile advantage. The Danish health AI success is being scaled through the new EU AI Adoption Accelerator (a category 9 measure in preparation), but we are holding back on launching it until political capital stabilises post-resilience vote. For now, we are amplifying stories of public benefit while quietly preparing the flexicurity response — because if we lose the middle class to displacement anxiety, no technical shield will save our legitimacy.
