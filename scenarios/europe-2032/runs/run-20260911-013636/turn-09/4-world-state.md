### The jump
Autumn brought a new model generation that made last spring's roadmaps obsolete. Demonstrations showed long-running agents handling professional work with less supervision, and labs updated their deployment notes within weeks. At almost the same moment, a quarantine around Taiwan stopped advanced chip exports. Every procurement meeting in Europe turned overnight into a security meeting.

A third development cut the other way. An interpretability result that actually worked on shipped systems — behaviour that could be predicted and certified rather than argued over — spread quickly because the big labs wanted it too.

### Wards and hosts
In Europe, hospitals and ministries were still living with the American cut-off. The emergency programme to host vetted open models in European centres became the winter priority. Where grid-isolation kits were already installed, engineers finished the installs, applied the new certification method to clear weights faster, and brought basic triage and ledger tools back online. Referral errors fell where the new checks were used.

Where kits were missing, nothing moved. Municipal objections to new sites and frozen grid connections stalled hosts for months. Clinicians in several regions kept paper appointment books, and local press ran stories of wards running two systems at once.

The small verification cell closed its first mandate, keeping observer seats at the American-Chinese talks and redirecting its patch teams to the fallback sites. It delivered procedure, not bargaining power.

### Leverage on the table
Brussels closed its two-year continuity commitment and opened a new direction: tie continued servicing, spares and licences for European lithography and optics equipment to restored model access and guaranteed chip and compute quotas for essential services. Trade officials, competition staff, anti-coercion lawyers and company engineers were put in one room and sent to the follow-on forum as holders of leverage, not observers.

Washington and Beijing listened politely. No quotas were restored this semester, no cut-off accounts were reopened. With valuations collapsed and private compute gone, the offer was credible but untested, and member states split over how hard to push it while wards were still fragile.
