### Cut off
The notice arrived without explanation and without appeal. Accounts tied to European hospitals, ministries and contractors stopped returning the leading foreign model, mid-shift. Triage assistants, ledger helpers and drafting tools built on that connection went blank. Clinics that had just restored appointment books after the ransomware sweep fell back to phones and paper again.

Brussels read it as a tier decision from Washington, not a technical fault. No negotiation opened it back up.

### Money leaves
At the same time capital fled the sector. Valuations reset, several build-outs were cancelled outright, and private compute arrangements the Union had counted on evaporated. Frontier laboratories talked openly about training less.

In ordinary offices a quieter verdict landed: careful studies showed assistants lifting output in law, accountancy, administration and journalism, most for juniors, with no wave of job cuts. Relief for staff, disappointment for investors who had borrowed against endless transformation.

### A fallback, unevenly
The Union approved an emergency substitution programme: vetted openly available models hosted in European data centres for the cut-off workloads, run by DIGIT and ENISA on reprogrammed digital funds with freed sites and grid connections. Health and interior ministers carried it as continuity, which let Paris and Berlin accept it.

It started slowly. Clearing weights, finding hosts amid municipal moratorium talk and connection freezes, and pairing the new hosts with the finished grid segmentation kits took months. Where the hosts went live, basic triage and admin work resumed. Elsewhere restoration stalled, clinicians complained of referral errors, and single-source reports spoke of wards quietly keeping paper.

The small verification cell kept its observer seats at the American-Chinese follow-on talks, transposing weight-security provisions, its patching teams redirected to the fallback sites. It bought procedure, not leverage.
