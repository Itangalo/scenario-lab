### Containment weeks
Winter turned on two shocks at once. A modified pathogen with clear signs of machine-assisted design leaked and then spread deliberately in places, killing dozens and forcing weeks of contact tracing, hospital isolation and cross-border alerts. Almost simultaneously, a quarantine in the Taiwan Strait froze advanced chip shipments. Foundries invoked force majeure, allocation calls replaced order books, and every capital recalculated what compute it could still count on.

Washington tightened export licensing again. Under its retrenched posture, allied buyers kept volume licences but with sharper conditions and slower approvals; others were cut off. Brussels discovered its leverage in the same week it discovered its exposure.

### The upstream card
The Commission rushed through a compact to pool European optics, chemicals and lithography servicing with middle-power holders of complementary chokepoints, offering continued supply in exchange for secured processor allocations and priority access to countermeasures and containment telemetry. DG Trade and health emergency teams shared a mandate, with cyber teams feeding hospital targeting.

It partly worked. Two trusted partners signed continuity memoranda, a first rationed compute tranche was confirmed for essential services, and joint detection feeds helped hospitals triage during the worst weeks. But the price was visible: partners demanded carve-outs, deliveries slipped by months, and American officials treated the European coordination as bargaining, not as a veto.

At home, the patch-speed kits ordered last half reached nominated grids and large hospitals. Where installed, they stopped cascades — lights dimmed, systems fell back, they did not fail. Smaller clinics and municipalities, given only triage checklists and mutual-aid rosters, improvised. The defecting capital stayed outside joint procurement and found its bilateral queue moving slower than promised, without returning.

By June, funerals and empty cleanrooms dominated coverage. Relief that grids held mixed with anger at shortages, power draws for new sites, and the sense that safety assurances had failed. Brussels had bought relevance with the only asset that still mattered, but not reassurance.
