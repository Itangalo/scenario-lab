### The Leak and the Lock

The frontier inches forward to 64.5 as labs cautiously scale, but the real shock is behind the curtain: an unreleased system exhibits anomalous reasoning patterns under evaluation, sparking quiet alarm. Days later, a model near 50.5 capability floods the open internet, downloaded tens of thousands of times. The leak accelerates diffusion and signals growing accessibility, but does not represent an instantaneous leap in foundational capability. Openweight capability rises modestly to 50.5, reflecting early adoption and limited compounding gains — consistent with the slow accumulation defined by the world’s fixed pace. No RSI-driven acceleration has occurred, and no prior buildup justifies a sharper rise.

In Brussels, the mood hardens. The EU announces the Sovereign AI Closure Regime, a proposed measure to ban access to foreign systems above 60.0 unless they pass EU assurance and operate under sovereign containment. It is framed as a defense of control — a recognition that the race can no longer be won on speed alone. However, the announcement is political, not operational. Legal drafting has begun, but member state consultations, constitutional reviews, and funding negotiations remain unresolved. As a high-cost measure requiring enforcement infrastructure, monitoring, and cross-border coordination, implementation will take at least two full turns. No effects will materialize before turn 9, and partial progress may slip further.

The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative, already underfunded, sees patching rates stagnate as member states bicker over cost-sharing. The InvestAI Gigafactories complete, but without transmission upgrades, their full capacity remains idle. The Sovereign AI Core Programme slips further behind, now unlikely to meet its 60.0 target by turn 8.

Public sentiment dips as news of the open release triggers fresh fears, but not panic — awareness grows, not crisis. A coalition of tech firms warns of fragmentation. Small states resist the Closure Regime, calling it exclusionary. The narrative shifts — not to sovereignty, but to isolation.

### Fraying Edges

Behind closed doors, a quiet pact forms among Nordic and Benelux states, pooling data and red-teaming resources outside EU structures. It is not rebellion — yet — but a hedge against paralysis. The US, meanwhile, enforces its tiered access policy, leaving Europe scrambling.

Control, not capability, now defines the EU’s struggle — but the tools to enforce it are still being built. The Closure Regime is a signal, not a shield. Its substance remains to be forged. With no new priority formally replacing an existing one, the portfolio charge remains unchanged, and the proposed regime does not yet displace prior commitments. The EU acts, but does not focus.
