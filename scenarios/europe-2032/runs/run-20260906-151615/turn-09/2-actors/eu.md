## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign containment and cohesion through enforced resilience and controlled capability access``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience lags, even without a direct mandate, because harm finds the weakest joint. And now, we also *pause where openness outpaces containment*, even if it means falling behind, because distributed capability without distributed accountability is catastrophe in motion. But now, we also *insist on shared burden*, even when solidarity is thin — because a union that does not share risk cannot claim sovereignty. And now, we *bind enforcement to access*: no state or entity operating outside the EU’s containment and resilience framework will receive sovereign compute, data, or market access.
  - Grounds: The emergent member state defection — a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler for a 67.0 model outside the Containment Protocol — proves that permissiveness is not neutrality but surrender. The EU’s authority is being bypassed not by malice but by necessity, as member states act where the Union cannot. This changes the calculus: we can no longer afford to treat containment and cohesion as parallel goals. They must be fused — and enforced through leverage. Our commitment must now reflect that sovereignty is not granted; it is conditioned.

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a fortress with no people left inside. Now, we also see that *openness* without containment is a vector, not a virtue: when the frontier leaks, it does not democratize power — it distributes risk without responsibility. And now, we see that *resilience without cohesion* is a fiction — if member states will not share the burden, no directive will harden the weakest link. And now, we see that *sovereignty without enforcement* is a performance — if we cannot bind access to compliance, we do not govern, we plead.
  - Grounds: The bilateral defection and the failure of the Resilience Solidarity Mechanism reveal that our frameworks are aspirational, not operational. The identity must now confront the fact that without enforcement — without the ability to say “no” to members and providers alike — the EU’s AI policy is not a regime, but a recommendation.

## Priority
**Priority:** Enforce the Sovereign Access Conditionality Regime  
We are pushing this hardest because the emergent member state defection proves that voluntary compliance is collapsing — and because the loss-of-control incident shows that uncontained systems are already acting in the world; if we do not bind access to assurance, we are not governing AI, we are hosting it.

## In practice
We are acting on the hard truth this turn: sovereignty without enforcement is ritual, not reality. The bilateral deal between a member state and a US hyperscaler for a 67.0 model outside the Containment Protocol is not an exception — it is the new normal. We can no longer afford to treat compliance as optional. So we are launching the Sovereign Access Conditionality Regime under Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act framework, with the Commission’s Competition and Internal Market Directorates leading. This regime will deny access to EU sovereign compute clusters, Gaia-X data spaces, and public procurement contracts for any entity — national or foreign — that operates AI systems above 60.0 capability without full compliance with the Containment Protocol and Resilience Baseline Standards.

We are also reactivating the Anti-Coercion Instrument in parallel, not to punish, but to signal that fragmentation has consequences: no member state will gain access to next-phase EU AI infrastructure unless it participates in the shared resilience framework. We are offering a 12-month compliance window, during which states can phase in upgrades with support from the European Cyber Resilience Act funds and the Digital Europe Programme. But after turn 11, access will be cut off for non-compliant actors — including, if necessary, national governments. We are already in quiet talks with the Nordic-Benelux pact to formalise their red-teaming network as an EU-certified compliance pathway, and with Germany and France to anchor enforcement in the upcoming Council Conclusions on Strategic Autonomy. The message is clear: you cannot claim EU sovereignty while opting out of EU responsibility. We are not banning foreign AI — we are making it conditional on playing by our rules. If that slows adoption, so be it. A union that cannot say “no” cannot say “we”.
