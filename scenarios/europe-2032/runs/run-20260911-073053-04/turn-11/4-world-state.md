### The sweep
Autumn began with municipal screens going dark. A largely automated ransomware wave, built with openly available model tooling, swept across city administrations, regional hospitals and mid-size suppliers in half a dozen member states. Backups were corrupted where they existed; where they did not, clerks returned to paper.

Defenders were visibly behind. Power rationing and jammed links from the war slowed patching, and the joint repair battalions guarding cable landings and grid trenches could not guard every server room. Attribution dragged. Emergency procurement of clean backups through the EU telemetry hub helped larger cities restore within weeks, smaller towns waited months.

### Blind models
At the same time the leading labs confirmed what evaluators had feared: the newest systems no longer reason in readable words. Performance was higher, but the intermediate steps were gone. Oversight teams that had relied on reading those steps were left with black-box tests and slow activation probes.

For Europe the news landed hard. University triage tools kept running, but safety assurances around licensed therapy systems thinned. Public coverage fused the two stories — unreadable minds, unopenable files — and trust collapsed further.

### Accession
Brussels answered by plugging into the pooled defence arrangement other hit states had built: real-time telemetry sharing through ENISA, a joint attribution cell in Brussels, conditional safeguards to keep the neutrality line. The wartime energy-compute shield stayed the operational focus — soldiers around repairs, priority power for hospitals and certified medical inference, cross-border failover for data centres.

It kept essentials alive, barely. Lights stayed on, therapy queues moved with surcharges and delays, gigafactory trenches were re-cut only to be guarded again. The middle-power supply and evaluation coalition concluded its work, opening a few alternative parts channels and shared test reports, too small to offset the war's choke.

By December, Europe was better connected to allies, less able to see inside the models it depended on, and still building almost nothing alone.
