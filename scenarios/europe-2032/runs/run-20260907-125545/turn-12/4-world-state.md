### A Surge Against the Tide

The European Union breaks its silence with the launch of the Emergency Resilience Surge, a large-scale, binding initiative to harden municipal and cross-border infrastructure against AI-driven cyber threats. Invoking emergency clauses in the Cyber Resilience Act and NIS2 Directive, the Commission pushes for rapid deployment of detection systems, automated patching, and failover protocols across member states. Technical specifications are drafted, funding reallocated, and political pressure mounted through joint parliamentary hearings.

Yet momentum falters. Poland and Hungary dig in, rejecting harmonised mandates as fiscal overreach, even with 75% co-financing offered. The Council’s Competitiveness configuration delays endorsement, and legal challenges loom over the use of Article 114 TFEU for systemic risk mitigation. The programme starts, but its reach is uncertain—fragmented implementation threatens to undermine its core goal.

### Capability’s Unchecked Climb

AI capability advances to 70.0, driven by recursive optimisation and unbroken training cycles. Offensive cyber tools grow more evasive, and the emerging crisis in scientific replication—journals halting AI-generated research claims—reveals deepening opacity in frontier models. With no safety improvements in deployment, `ai_safety` drops further to 17.0, eroding confidence in technical oversight.

### Resilience Erodes Further

Despite the new measure, `resilience` falls to 32.0. The ransomware aftermath lingers, recovery remains patchy, and the absence of an active, unified defence framework leaves systems exposed. The surge is too late to prevent harm and too contested to deliver immediate gains.
