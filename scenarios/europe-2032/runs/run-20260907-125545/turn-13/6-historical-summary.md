The European Union faces a strategic crisis as a sudden cutoff of access to a critical AI model disrupts healthcare, border security, and emergency planning, exposing deep dependencies. In response, the Commission launches the Emergency Compute Access Guarantee, repurposing EuroHPC and redirecting €2.3 billion to build sovereign inference capacity within 18 months, though legal disputes arise over the use of Article 114 and resistance grows from Poland and Hungary. AI capability rises to 71.5 due to recursive optimization, highlighted by a dual-use model capable of autonomous exploit discovery, while ai_safety collapses to 12.0 amid growing opacity and no new safeguards. Resilience declines to 30.0 as fragmented implementation of the Emergency Resilience Surge fails to protect municipal infrastructure, leaving systems vulnerable to AI-generated ransomware. Political capital drops to 8.0 due to high costs, external shocks including the frontier access denial and cyber incident, and rising public unrest, with public sentiment falling to 9.0 amid the retraining revolt. EU AI sovereignty weakens to 12.0 as gains in capability are offset by lack of sovereign deployment and strategic setbacks.
