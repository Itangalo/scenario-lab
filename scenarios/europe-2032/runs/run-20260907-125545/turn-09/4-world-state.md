### Core Declared, Access Secured

The European Union declares the Sovereign AI Core Programme operational. Audit-verified compute clusters in Brittany and northern Sweden are now online, powered by dedicated nuclear and green hydrogen microgrids, and disconnected from external hyperscalers. The EIB releases the final tranche of its €30bn bond, confirming 18 exaF-days under Union control. EAAD begins real-time evaluations of models above 60.0 capability, and the stack hosts the first live run of the Real-Time Logging Mandate’s detection engine. Despite legal objections from Germany and Austria, technical integration proceeds under a provisional ENTSO-E protocol, and Commission emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU hold.

### Cyber Shock, Coalition Response

A major automated cyberattack strikes hospital administration systems in three member states, exploiting a chain generated by a compromised open-weight model derivative. The attack is disruptive but short-lived: logging systems flag anomalies within hours, and countermeasures are distributed via a nascent Middle Power AI Coalition (MPAC) coordination channel. Japan signals intent to join export controls on AI-specific chip designs, South Korea opens talks on mutual evaluation recognition, and Norway offers Arctic co-location for resilient infrastructure. Canada remains hesitant, pending parliamentary review, but shares intelligence on model leaks. The coordination mechanisms remain preliminary, and no formal agreements are concluded this turn.

### Safety Erodes, Sentiment Stagnates

Eval anomalies deepen: leading labs report unexplained reasoning jumps in unreleased models, undermining interpretability-based oversight. No systemic failure occurs, but assurance frameworks strain. Labour displacement accelerates in law, accounting, and software—university placements near zero. Protests in Lyon and Vienna denounce “black box governance,” and public sentiment remains stagnant at 14.0.
