### Blind models, small mercies
By autumn the two leading labs admitted what engineers had whispered for months: the new systems no longer thought in sentences. Their internal steps had collapsed into machine-native representations that ran faster and scored higher, and no human could follow them. Every oversight routine in Brussels that relied on reading the model's working collapsed overnight. A control technique published weeks later — a way to certify bounds on certain behaviours in deployed assistants — was seized on desperately. The Brussels verification cell trialled it on triage software and care-routing tools, with modest, real gains, but it covered a fraction of what had just gone dark.

The bid for a seat at the superpower risk table went nowhere. European diplomats brought registry logs, bio-dashboard feeds and telemetry to the technical exchange and asked for observer-plus status and raw incident reports. They were thanked and kept in the waiting room. With reasoning unreadable and verification thin, the pact remained a two-chair room.

On the ground, a neutral-flag cable repair flotilla gave Europe breathing space. Atlantic and Mediterranean redundancy came partly back, outages shortened, registries were re-imaged without all-night races, and reagent convoys reached southern clinics that had been rationing for a year. The shelter operation that had held lights and wards through the war formally closed, and health ministries claimed — briefly, credibly — that the worst of the outages was over.

The credit did not stick. A large member state signed its own cloud and model-servicing arrangement with an American hyperscaler, undercutting the common line on verification and pooled procurement. Ministers called it pragmatism; Paris, Warsaw and Brussels called it a break. Conditions tying pooled reagents and voucher cover to the common line contained the damage but deepened the quarrel, and southern mayors asked why solidarity came with strings.

Work changed shape again. Studies showing assistants lifting output without cutting jobs were obsolete on arrival as drafting systems took whole graduate pipelines. Marches of displaced juniors and health workers returned in the autumn, quieter and angrier.
