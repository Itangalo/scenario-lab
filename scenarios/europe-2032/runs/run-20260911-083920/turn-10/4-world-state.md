### A spring of compounding proofs
The winter kits stayed. Municipal depots kept their spares, clinics kept paper folders, grid operators kept the agreed shedding order warm. When demand eased, Brussels declared the emergency pact complete and shifted language from rationing endured to jobs protected.

The respite did not hold.

In March, a largely automated intrusion swept through public-sector networks and a widely used software component at once. Booking systems, payroll and several municipal services locked or leaked, and restoration took weeks. Defenders admitted the tooling had been machine-built and that attribution would take months. Hospitals islanded again, but this time on cyber grounds.

Almost simultaneously, laboratories demonstrated a sharp step forward in code, mathematics and intrusion tooling. Specialists argued over whether it meant anything general; operators noticed that break-ins got faster within weeks. European institutes could verify the results but not reproduce the runs behind them.

The labour data turned at the same moment. Routine coding posts, standard analysis, first-draft writing and entry-level support thinned abruptly. Hiring in those functions did not bounce back. The losses stopped at the edge of checkable work, but for school-leavers and young graduates the edge was where they lived.

Then chip and model licences tightened again. Allied buyers were kept on volume allocations while others were cut off, with paperwork, delays and price to prove it. Seeking pragmatic access, Brussels found itself queuing.

The Commission pushed wage-bridges and fast retraining through employment ministries, funded in part by a levy on firms shedding staff to automation. National job centres began enrolling, but councils haggled over who counted as displaced, employers lobbied the threshold upward, and payments arrived slower than announcements. The new priority did not accelerate delivery; with coffers thin and capitals divided, it added another queue.

By June, power was on and bills were high, services were running and distrusted. Voters saw protection that worked technically and failed politically.
