### Care kept lit, reasoning goes dark
Autumn brought no repeat of the spring freeze, but a near-miss. A fresh wave of AI-assembled intrusion attempts probed clinic booking systems and a water utility in two metros. ENISA-led patching and the offline prescription fallback held — appointments slowed, they did not stop. Health ministers toured wastewater sequencing upgrades in 30 cities and claimed protection was finally visible.

The relief was undercut from the labs. The leading American models stopped showing their work: internal steps that auditors once read were replaced by representations no human can follow. Brussels evaluators admitted overnight that screening rules for lab software and booking patches now rested on black-box tests. Trust in oversight fell further.

At the same time a contested genome-model paper claimed a non-expert could be guided to a viable human-infecting design. Virologists fought over methods, but hospital biosafety officers took notice. The Commission cited it to justify the new shield.

Diplomacy offered a counterweight. The Union joined a middle-power coordination framework on export licences, joint compute bargaining and shared evaluation. No sovereignty was pooled, but procurement officers reported slightly better terms on capped chip licences, and joint screening standards gave HERA cover.

The jobs front barely moved. Wage bridges and short retraining from the finished transition scheme stayed enrolled, placements stayed slow. The automation levy began collection, with SME exemptions still negotiated. Business anger cooled slightly; union anger did not. Voters saw clinics protected and wages paid late — enough to lift mood from zero, not to restore confidence.
