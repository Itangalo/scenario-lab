### The Plateau and the Fracture

Global AI capability inches upward to 62.0, sustained by incremental integration of open-weight agent frameworks into internal development pipelines. Though no public breakthroughs occur, the quiet diffusion of autonomous tools into engineering and research workflows confirms that the frontier, while plateauing, continues to advance. The market signals a shift: top-tier capability is no longer scarce, and prices for access fall, unsettling investors and developers alike.

The EU formally closes the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation, a directive that expired without a single new facility built. Berlin and Dublin’s refusals stand unchallenged, exposing the limits of EU authority in strategic infrastructure. With no sovereign compute gains, the Union’s leverage erodes further.

Instead, the EU doubles down on decentralised resilience. ENISA finalises interoperability standards for the Municipal Resilience Backchannel, embedding protocols to detect unauthorised agent persistence. Cross-border crisis simulations in Helsinki, Lyon, and Barcelona demonstrate functional coordination under civil protection law, bypassing stalled AI governance channels. A conditional €180 million allocation is announced for next year, incentivising compliance without mandates.

However, the release of redacted Evaluation Shield findings — citing anomalous agent behaviours — backfires. Two member states reject the legal basis for market suspension, and a coalition of developers decries the process as opaque. Trust in EU oversight declines further. Public sentiment dips as frustration mounts over job displacement and perceived institutional paralysis.

### Political Erosion

With political capital already at 6.0, the cost of four large measures in flight — including the now-failed compute push — drains resources. The named priority adds another point of strain. Capital falls to 4.0, deepening institutional inertia. No new measures can be proposed without risk of collapse.

Resilience improves slightly due to operational gains from the backchannel exercises. But ai_safety drops sharply, as capability growth outpaces any real assurance. Sovereignty declines as the compute failure compounds dependency, and no new capacity offsets the ongoing capability rise.
