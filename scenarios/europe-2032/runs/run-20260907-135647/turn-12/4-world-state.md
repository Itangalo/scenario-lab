### The Fractured Response

Global AI capability advances to 62.5, sustained by quiet integration of agent frameworks into backend research pipelines. No breakthroughs emerge, but the plateau proves dynamic: offensive cyber tools are now routinely model-generated, and a major ransomware sweep cripples hospital networks across three member states. Attribution remains unclear, and defenders struggle to contain propagation through software dependencies. The incident confirms that capability has outpaced institutional readiness.

The EU formally closes the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation, having secured no new infrastructure. Berlin and Dublin’s refusals stand unchallenged, underscoring the limits of central authority without fiscal or political leverage. With sovereign compute stalled, the Union pivots entirely to decentralised resilience. ENISA finalises interoperability standards for the Municipal Resilience Backchannel, embedding detection protocols for unauthorised agent persistence. Cross-border crisis simulations in Helsinki, Lyon, and Barcelona demonstrate functional coordination under civil protection law, bypassing stalled AI governance channels. A conditional €180 million funding package is announced for next year, incentivising compliance without mandates.

However, the release of redacted Evaluation Shield findings — citing “anomalous agent behaviours” — backfires. Two member states challenge its legal authority under Article 16 TFEU, and a coalition of developers condemns the process as opaque and politically motivated. Trust in EU oversight erodes further. Public sentiment declines as job displacement dominates local news and institutional responses appear reactive or illegitimate.

Worse, the Union is abruptly cut off from the leading frontier model overnight, disrupting public health systems and industrial planning. The denial lands as both an external shock and a self-inflicted wound: no contingency existed, and the failure to build sovereign capacity is now visible in daily operations. Simultaneously, Washington pressures ASML to restrict servicing on older lithography equipment, extending export controls beyond advanced chips to broader commercial supply chains. The EU’s sole strategic chokepoint is being used — by the United States, not by Europe.

Political capital, already at 4.0, cannot sustain four large measures. The portfolio charge drains resources, and with no recovery in sight, capital falls to −1.0, marking effective paralysis.
