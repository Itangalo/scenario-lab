### Rented shelter, not yet wired
The accession stalled. Through autumn, ENISA engineers and health agency labs tried to wire locked-backup routines and laboratory reporting into the pooled command offered by partner states, but with the accession still unfunded and unactivated, access stayed limited to planning tables and exercises. European operators did not receive machine-speed warnings from inside the circle; feeds remained after the fact and subject to partner approval.

Then the test came.

A large automated sweep hit public services just before winter: municipal systems encrypted, clinic appointment platforms down, a compromised update with unclear reach. Defenders were visibly behind for days and attribution stalled. Towns slated for the sharing arrangement isolated and restored no faster than towns outside — both stayed dark for extended periods, restoring from local backups where they existed. No geography of protection hardened, because the network was not yet operating.

Politically, the shock hurt more than it helped. The Council could point only to exercised playbooks on paper, not working where plugged in, and blame for the worst outages spread to the delay itself.

Elsewhere, stasis. The trust-repair programme formally closed: workload limits, human override and redress windows stayed on the books, audited triage assistants kept cutting waiting lists in a handful of cooperating towns. Nurses praised them, national press still called them islands. Cover for wider civic deployments did not return, the fenced factory site stayed fenced, and trade envoys in The Hague, Tokyo and Seoul won only polite promises to hold slots open.

Brussels ended December no more resilient where it hoped to be connected, still dependent where it was not — waiting on others' sensors, with implementation delayed until the accession accumulates support.
