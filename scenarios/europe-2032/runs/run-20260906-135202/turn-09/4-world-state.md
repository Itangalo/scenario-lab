### Democratic Defences Mobilise Amid Grid Strains

The EU closes the Emergency Resilience Reinforcement with 78% compliance among Tier 1 energy operators. Hungary and Poland remain partial holdouts, but the withholding of Cohesion Fund payments forces engagement. During the wave of AI-driven grid instability, red-teaming drills prove decisive: manual overrides activate swiftly, preventing blackouts. The resilience gains are real, though uneven.

Simultaneously, the annulment of a national election over AI-amplified disinformation triggers the Emergency Democratic Integrity Protocol. Fusion cells link ENISA, national electoral commissions and public broadcasters to detect synthetic media and coordinated manipulation. Mandatory disclosure rules for political deepfakes are enforced, and joint alert protocols are tested across six member states. Public broadcasters adopt a unified “trust tag” for verified content, aiming to restore credibility.

Yet the political moment is fragile. The U.S., under its retrenchment posture, maintains silence on EU co-stewardship requests. Export controls continue to block access to frontier models, undermining confidence in transatlantic alignment. The Algorithmic Sovereignty Initiative presses on: sparse model prototypes from ASML and Infineon show promise, achieving 40% efficiency gains in simulations. Early deployment trials begin in grid monitoring and election integrity systems, offering a vision of sovereign, efficient AI.

However, member-state fractures deepen. While Poland engages with the EU’s offer of early access, Hungary refuses, citing its bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler as sufficient. The defection weakens the Union’s leverage and fuels internal criticism.

AI capability climbs to 68.5, driven by self-improving agents in closed labs. Open-weight models reach 54.0, as efficient architectures diffuse despite restrictions. With no safety upgrades landing on deployed systems, ai_safety stagnates at 19.0. The gap between capability and control widens.

Resilience rises to 58.0, reflecting the partial success of both grid and democratic safeguards. But public sentiment remains flat at 30.0 — wary of both disruption and disunity. Political capital erodes under the weight of overlapping crises and a strained portfolio.
