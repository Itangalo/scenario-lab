## In practice  
This turn, we close the **Systemic Integrity Watch** on schedule. Hosted at ENISA and jointly staffed by DG CNECT, DG ENER, and national regulators, it has delivered its first operational protocol: a real-time telemetry framework now live across 61% of designated Tier 1 energy grid operators. The system has already flagged two anomalous coordination events in regional balancing markets — not yet harmful, but structurally novel — triggering emergency reviews under the revised NIS2 framework. We are treating these not as failures but as validation: the nervous system is online, and it is sensing. The cost of €620m from the Digital Europe Programme is fully disbursed, and the function will continue under ENISA’s mandate, no longer drawing from our political capital.

With that milestone reached, we pivot decisively toward sovereignty through innovation. The **Algorithmic Sovereignty Initiative** responds to the Swiss-German efficiency breakthrough — a 40% reduction in compute demand via sparse activation — which threatens to make our ongoing dependence on foreign chip supply irrelevant not in ten years, but now. We are launching the European Institute for Sparse Intelligence as a joint legal entity under Horizon Europe, co-hosted in Munich, Grenoble, and Eindhoven, with €1.8bn in initial funding from the Innovation Fund and EuroHPC. Its mission is dual: to industrialise the new architecture for public-sector use (health diagnostics, climate modelling, transport optimisation) and to seed an open ecosystem of EU-based developers who can replicate frontier work without relying on exascale compute. We are engaging ASML and Infineon not just as suppliers but as co-designers: the next generation of EU-made chips will be optimised not for brute-force training, but for sparse, dynamic inference — turning our semiconductor coordination from a defensive play into an offensive one.

This is not a retreat from hardware sovereignty, but a redefinition of it. We are negotiating with the U.S. under the Trade and Technology Council to exempt EU-developed sparse models from broad export controls, arguing that algorithmic openness with security-by-design should be a third path between retrenchment and recklessness. At the same time, we are offering member states fast-track access to the new models in exchange for aligning national AI strategies with the Initiative’s roadmap. Germany and France have signalled support; Poland and Hungary remain cautious, citing cost. We will absorb the risk: if we wait for consensus, we lose the lead. The measure costs 3 political capital per turn for four turns — a burden, but one we bear to ensure that the EU does not merely react to the future, but shapes it.
