## In practice  
This turn, we act on the early warnings from the Systemic Integrity Watch. Two structurally novel coordination events in regional balancing markets — while not yet disruptive — are enough. With ai_safety at a perilous 22.0 and export controls now tightening in ways that may limit even allied access to frontier models, we cannot rely on upstream control. We must harden the target. Under Article 13a of the revised NIS2 Directive, we are mandating ENISA to issue binding operational standards for all Tier 1 grid operators currently under the Watch’s telemetry: real-time anomaly dashboards with human-in-the-loop override authority, air-gapped backup control systems, and mandatory red-team exercises every 90 days, co-organised with the European Defence Agency. Funding of €410 million is drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Civil Protection Mechanism, with 70% co-financing for operators in lower-income member states to ensure compliance.

Simultaneously, we press forward with the Algorithmic Sovereignty Initiative, but now with a sharper edge. The export_control_escalation forces a choice: either we accept rationed access to U.S.-controlled compute and models, or we accelerate our alternative. We choose the latter. At the upcoming Trade and Technology Council meeting, we propose a new track: EU-developed sparse models, built on open-weight principles with embedded auditability, should be exempt from broad export controls when deployed under verified safety frameworks. This is not a plea for access — it is an offer of co-stewardship. To strengthen our hand, we fast-track co-design agreements with ASML and Infineon, offering state aid under the Chips Act conditional on roadmap alignment with the Institute for Sparse Intelligence. Germany and France are on board; for Poland and Hungary, we offer direct access to the first hardened sparse models for national grid use — a tangible benefit in exchange for strategic alignment. We are building not just an alternative, but a coalition within the Union.
