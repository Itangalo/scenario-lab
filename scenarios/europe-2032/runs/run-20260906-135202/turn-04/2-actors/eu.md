## In practice
We are acting on the fact that capability without resilience is a target, not an asset. The cyber_major_incident — a coordinated ransomware sweep across regional health administrations and municipal grids, enabled by AI-generated exploit chains and polymorphic payloads — has confirmed what ENISA’s probes hinted at: we are already behind in the operational tempo of attack. The Sovereign Compute Implementation Acceleration has delivered physical enclaves in Finland and Romania, with France expected to join by next quarter, but those racks will be useless if the networks they are meant to defend collapse under automated pressure. We are therefore pivoting hard to resilience, launching the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint under the NIS2 directive’s emergency powers, with ENISA tasked to issue binding hardening mandates within 60 days.

The sprint will be funded through the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s crisis window, with €4.2bn mobilised for rapid deployment. All operators of essential services must complete AI-threat-specific risk assessments by turn 5, implement air-gapped fallbacks for critical control systems by turn 6, and undergo unannounced red-team exercises using AI-generated attack patterns developed by the EU’s newly expanded Joint Cyber Unit. We are fast-tracking procurement for zero-trust architecture kits and mandating real-time telemetry sharing with national CSIRTs. Resistance from member states over cost and sovereignty concerns is real — particularly in Germany and Spain — but we are offering 85% co-funding and framing compliance as a condition for continued access to EU-wide cyber mutual aid under the new Cross-Border Incident Response Compact. This is not about catching up — it is about ensuring that when the next wave hits, we do not lose the ability to respond at all.
