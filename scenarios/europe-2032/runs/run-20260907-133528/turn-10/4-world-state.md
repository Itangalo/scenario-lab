### A Crisis of Justice

The EU launched the AI Accountability Tribunal in response to a damning ombudsman report that exposed systemic denials of social benefits due to a flawed algorithm. The move was politically urgent, but structurally fragile. Negotiations with a bloc of six member states delayed final approval, forcing concessions on national opt-outs and enforcement limits. While the Tribunal was formally established, its authority was diluted before operations began. The €10bn Remediation Fund, partly financed by AI Act fines, remains underfunded as enforcement mechanisms lag.

Public sentiment worsened, sinking to 14.0, as protests in Marseille and Lyon dismissed the initiative as “delayed justice.” The Tribunal’s delayed start and compromised mandate failed to quell anger, and the scandal deepened distrust in AI governance across public services.

### Capability Leaps, Safety Stalls

A sudden advance in AI-driven cyber operations — demonstrated in a high-profile intrusion into a European grid operator’s planning system — marked a clear capability jump. Though general competence rose only modestly to 68.5, the jump revealed new offensive autonomy. Open-weight models, benefiting from distributed fine-tuning, reached 56.5, enabling more accessible misuse. With no safety breakthrough and no finished measure improving assurance on deployed systems, ai_safety dropped sharply to 14.0.

Resilience eroded further, down to 38.0, as critical infrastructure operators reported undetected probing consistent with the new capabilities. The Sovereign Cyber Shield, though now complete, had not yet been stress-tested at scale.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

The US-led supply-chain coercion, extending ASML export controls to mature-node lithography tools, struck at Europe’s industrial base. The EU lost leverage at its one potential chokepoint, accelerating compute dependency. With ai_capability rising by 2.0 and no sovereign compute gains, eu_ai_sovereignty fell to 7.0.

Political capital collapsed to 3.0. The new Tribunal, while large and necessary, strained an already overburdened system. The portfolio charge overwhelmed the Union’s capacity, and internal division amplified the cost.
