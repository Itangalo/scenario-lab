## In practice
We are acting on the conviction that sovereignty is not negotiated in peacetime committees when the attack is already underway. The Sovereign Cyber Shield was built, but it was never empowered — its agents run on foreign clouds, its responses require manual approval, and its integration is voluntary. That ends now. Under Article 22a of the NIS2 Directive, we are invoking emergency powers to mandate real-time integration of the Shield’s AI agents into all Tier 1 operators — energy, water, rail, finance — effective immediately. This is not a request. ENISA will issue compliance orders, and the European Defence Agency will assume operational command of the sovereign compute grid in Luxembourg and Finland, ensuring military-grade security and uninterrupted control.

The EIB will freeze all new infrastructure loans to member states or operators that do not integrate by turn 13, including digital and energy projects. Germany and Poland will not be offered veto concessions — they will be given a deadline. The Public Trust Corps, now operational, will publish a live compliance dashboard, naming non-compliant systems and justifying each intervention. We know this fractures consensus. We know it risks legal challenge. But the capability_jump has given attackers AI tools that can map, exploit, and cascade through systems in hours — and we will not wait for a blackout to admit that unity is non-negotiable. This is not overreach. It is the minimum act of a sovereign body that still intends to exist.
