A massive AI-powered ransomware attack crippled healthcare systems across five EU member states, exposing the failure of the fragmented Sovereign Cyber Shield as Germany remained in legal defiance, Poland operated independently, and Luxembourg’s node was isolated. With no unified defense, offensive AI tools spread unchecked. In parallel, a near-frontier open-weight model proliferated through developer networks, boosting `openweight_capability` to 62.0, though uneven adoption limited systemic impact.

In response to the crisis, the EU Commission invoked emergency powers under Article 114 TFEU to activate the EU AI Accountability Tribunal using frontloaded InvestEU funds, marking the launch of a single category 1 measure. The Public Trust Corps was repurposed under its authority to collect incident logs, but faced jurisdictional resistance and yielded incomplete data. Despite constraints, the Tribunal produced a verified attack timeline within 72 hours—its first operational success—raising `ai_safety` from 0.0 to 15.0 and establishing a fragile precedent for EU-level accountability.

However, coordination failures worsened systemic resilience, public trust declined further amid anger over institutional collapse, and `eu_ai_sovereignty` remained stagnant at 4.0. The emergency Tribunal, while a procedural milestone, lacked broad authority and durability, underscoring the EU’s ongoing inability to translate crisis response into sovereign control. Political capital remained exhausted, and fragmentation deepened.
