## In practice
We are acting on the conviction that sovereignty does not reside in declarations, but in the ability to do one thing, once, without permission. The Sovereign Cyber Shield is disarmed, its nodes cut, its authority contested. The EIB freeze has not compelled compliance — it has triggered financial retaliation. Germany has suspended all data sharing under NIS2, Poland has declared its national CERT the sole authority, and the Luxembourg compute node was physically disconnected by national regulators. The cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across public health systems, powered by AI-generated exploits — landed in this vacuum. No alert was raised, no countermeasure deployed, no coordination attempted. The Union did not respond, because it cannot.

So we shift. We abandon the fiction of unified command. Instead, we focus on the only institution left that can still act: the EU AI Accountability Tribunal. We will complete it not as a court of final appeal, but as a crisis cell — a minimal sovereign function. Under Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Services Act’s emergency provisions, we will empower it to issue binding incident holds, compel technical logs from public operators, and disburse remediation funds without national approval. The €10bn fund will be frontloaded from unused InvestEU allocations, and the Tribunal’s first act will be to investigate the current attack and publish a verified timeline — not to assign blame, but to prove that one body in this Union can still speak a fact into the dark.

The Public Trust Corps will support it, not as a transparency exercise, but as a lifeline: they will staff the Tribunal’s rapid-response teams, run its red-line assessments, and publish its findings in real time. We know this is not control. We know it is not unity. But it is not nothing. And in this turn, not nothing may be all we have left.
