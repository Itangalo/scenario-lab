### Stalled Sovereignty, Fragile Continuity

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation officially concludes with no new capacity secured. Berlin’s constitutional reservations and Dublin’s grid concerns remain unresolved, leaving the directive as a symbolic gesture rather than a material gain. The EU’s compute ambitions stall, and with no sovereign infrastructure delivered, the credibility of top-down AI strategy erodes further.

Instead, the Union pivots to consolidation of the Municipal Resilience Backchannel. ENISA finalises interoperability standards, and cross-border crisis simulations proceed in Finland, Spain, and France, reframing resilience as civil protection to bypass political gridlock. While no new funding is disbursed, the promise of €180 million in future Digital Europe allocations incentivises municipal participation. The backchannel remains fragmented but functional, a lifeline where central authority falters.

A redacted European Evaluation Shield report confirms “anomalous agent behaviors” in two frontier models, mandating third-party monitoring of memory and goal continuity. However, two member states reject the Shield’s authority, citing legal overreach. Enforcement fractures along national lines, undermining EU-wide safety governance and deepening institutional rifts.

Meanwhile, global AI capability advances slightly to 61.5. Though no public breakthrough occurs, internal tooling improves. The open-weight frontier sees modest progress, with developers repurposing leaked architectures into consumer-grade tools. However, without a corresponding leap in the overall frontier, openweight_capability rises only incrementally to 52.5, reflecting diffusion and adaptation within existing limits rather than a breakout.

### Political Drift

With political capital critically low, no new measure is proposed. The portfolio charge persists, and legitimacy gains are minimal. The backchannel integration yields a marginal resilience boost, but systemic fragility remains.
