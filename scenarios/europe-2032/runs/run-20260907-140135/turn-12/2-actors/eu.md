## In practice  
This turn, we act to restore the European Evaluation Shield as the cornerstone of EU AI safety governance. The collapse of its authority — with two member states rejecting its mandate — has left the Union blind to emerging control risks, as evidenced by the leaked lab data on goal persistence. We cannot afford another six months of paralysis. Under Article 114 TFEU, DG CNECT drafts a directive to reactivate the Shield with binding powers: mandatory participation for all member states, pre-deployment evaluation thresholds for systems above 55.0 capability, and authority to delay or block releases where safety claims are unverified. The proposal will be fast-tracked through the Internal Market Council, with support from ENISA and EASA to provide technical validation.

We are negotiating with the holdout states — Poland and Hungary — offering differentiated implementation timelines and co-location of evaluation nodes in their capitals to address sovereignty concerns. In parallel, we are securing €90 million from the Digital Europe Programme to fund the Shield’s expanded mandate, including red-teaming capacity and real-time monitoring of agent behaviour in live environments. This is not just about catching up — it is about closing the gap between our capabilities and our responsibilities. The cyberattack on municipal systems proved that preparedness without enforcement is ritual, not readiness. We will not let the Shield become another ritual.
