### A Fragile Reassertion

The European Commission proposes the **European Evaluation Shield Reactivation and Enforcement Directive**, aiming to restore binding EU-wide oversight of high-capability AI systems. Citing the recent cyberattack and leaked evidence of anomalous agent behaviour, DG CNECT frames the move as essential to closing a dangerous governance gap. Legal groundwork proceeds under Article 114 TFEU, with ENISA and EASA drafted to support technical validation.

### Resistance and Stalemate

Despite urgency, negotiations with Poland and Hungary stall. Both states reject the directive’s enforcement powers, framing them as an infringement on national sovereignty. Warsaw insists on opt-outs for domestic AI deployments; Budapest demands joint oversight bodies, raising procedural hurdles. Fast-tracking through the Internal Market Council falters without unanimity. The €90 million funding pledge from Digital Europe remains conditional on adoption, leaving implementation capacity unfunded.

### Drifting Without Anchor

With no measure yet launched successfully, political capital remains frozen at 10.0. The proposal itself fails to gain traction — not due to lack of need, but because the EU lacks the cohesion to compel it. Public sentiment holds at 30.0, reflecting continued anxiety and no visible shift in policy momentum. Meanwhile, resilience erodes slightly as the municipal cyber incident exposes implementation gaps, and no new protective measure is active to counter it.

### The Frontier Moves On

Global AI capability inches upward to 62.5, driven by iterative improvements in agent coordination and deployment efficiency. No breakthroughs occur, but progress continues. Openweight capability remains at 53.0, as no major model release breaches the threshold. AI safety holds at 14.0 — no decline, but no institutional improvement either. The Evaluation Shield remains fractured, its reactivation unrealised.
