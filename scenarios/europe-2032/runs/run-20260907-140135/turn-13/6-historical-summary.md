Global AI capability increases to 63.0 through incremental deployment gains, while open-weight models stagnate at 53.0 and AI safety remains unchanged at 14.0. The EU launches the Sovereign AI Infrastructure Acceleration Initiative, mobilising €12 billion and invoking emergency permitting to fast-track gigafactories in key member states, but faces immediate resistance. Poland and Hungary reject co-investment, with Warsaw finalising a bilateral compute deal with a U.S. hyperscaler that bypasses EU safety oversight, triggering a sovereignty penalty. Legal challenges and lack of coordination stall infrastructure progress, undermining the initiative’s momentum.

With formal governance paralyzed, a coalition of European tech firms and universities establishes an independent AI safety evaluation network, gaining credibility through public benchmarking and anomaly reporting—though its findings are not formally adopted. Political capital declines further amid perceptions of disarray, while public sentiment and municipal resilience remain low, reflecting ongoing systemic fragility and institutional failure.
