### A Fractured Response

The European Commission launches the **Sovereign AI Infrastructure Acceleration Initiative**, framing it as a necessary pivot from stalled governance to foundational power. Citing the recent member state defection and the collapse of the Evaluation Shield, the Commission declares that sovereignty must be built, not negotiated. The plan mobilises €12 billion in blended funding and invokes emergency permitting under the Chips and Net-Zero Industry Acts, aiming to fast-track gigafactories in France, Germany, and the Nordic states.

### Resistance Hardens

Despite high-level outreach, Poland and Hungary reject co-investment offers, accusing Brussels of using strategic infrastructure to enforce compliance. Behind closed doors, Warsaw confirms it is finalising a bilateral compute deal with a U.S. hyperscaler — one that excludes EU safety audits and grants preferential access to American developers. This *de facto* alignment with Washington, outside EU coordination, triggers a sovereignty penalty.

### Grassroots Fill the Void

With official evaluation mechanisms paralysed, a coalition of European tech firms and universities launches an independent AI safety evaluation network. Publishing benchmark results and anomaly reports, the grassroots effort gains credibility among regulators and the press, exposing the EU’s institutional failure. Yet no formal authority adopts its findings.

### Initiative Stalls at Launch

Although announced with fanfare, the Infrastructure Initiative fails to gain immediate traction. No construction begins, and grid integration planning is delayed by legal challenges in two member states. The measure enters the portfolio but yields no early momentum. Political capital, already at rock bottom, erodes further as the narrative shifts from urgency to disarray.

### The Frontier Creeps Forward

Global AI capability rises to 63.0, driven by incremental advances in deployment efficiency. Open-weight models remain stagnant at 53.0. AI safety holds at 14.0 — no deterioration, but no institutional improvement. Resilience dips slightly as municipal systems remain exposed.
