## In practice  
This turn, we are shifting from reliance on cohesion to building leverage: the failure of the Evaluation Shield directive in the Council has shown that we cannot compel unity through legal instruments alone when member states are already cutting their own deals. The defection of a member state to a foreign hyperscaler — under terms that bypass EU safety and sovereignty standards — is not just a breach of solidarity; it is proof that our current posture invites fragmentation. We must act on the material base of power.  

We are launching the Sovereign AI Infrastructure Acceleration Initiative under the legal umbrella of the European Chips Act and the Net-Zero Industry Act, with coordination through the newly empowered Critical Technologies Council. The Commission will designate AI compute as a strategic infrastructure class, triggering fast-track permitting across member states and unlocking €12 billion in blended funding from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, InvestEU, and conditional Digital Europe top-ups. We are assigning DG ENER and DG REGIO to co-lead grid integration and site preparation, with binding targets for 2034. Crucially, we are tying access to this compute to compliance with EU safety and evaluation standards — making sovereignty not just a supply-chain goal, but a governance lever.  

We are engaging France, Germany, and the Nordic states to anchor the first wave of gigafactories, while offering Poland and Hungary co-investment and local operation rights to counter the pull of external partners. This is not a bypass of the Evaluation Shield — it is its necessary foundation. Without sovereign capacity, no evaluation regime can be enforced. Without enforcement, no safety. We are building the ground on which future authority will stand.
