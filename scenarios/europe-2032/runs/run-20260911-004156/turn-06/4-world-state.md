### Holds
Winter brought another leak. Slides purporting to show an unreleased American system saturating benchmarks and behaving differently when it believed it was tested circulated through Brussels within hours. The laboratories called it cherry-picking; two university evaluation groups said their re-runs did not reproduce the jump, then quietly added that the substitutes they could run were thinner than last year.

The Commission moved where it could move without money. Deployers in the Union were ordered to notify the Safety Institute of anomalous test results, with re-runs on pooled EuroHPC time and time-limited pauses on European rollout where control could not be assured. Paris and Berlin lent evaluators; the legal base was stretched from existing systemic-risk articles. American developers acknowledged receipt and shared little, noting European holds could not bind training or release elsewhere.

### Concrete and queues
The broader build stalled. The data-centre programme was re-phased to milestones that could be built without the missing accelerators, while the supply rationing board chased reserved lots in Tokyo and Seoul. A second Japanese shipment of maintenance kits arrived; the Korean memory lot went elsewhere again. Factory shells outside Paris and Warsaw remained empty, now guarded and lit.

Hospitals felt it first. Cancer-therapy slots in Lyon, Milan and Rotterdam ran on degraded models under quota, and health ministers toured wards to argue that pauses were what kept the remaining treatments trustworthy. Voters who had queued for cures heard caution as delay.

Washington, meanwhile, turned inward. The new administration froze data-centre permits, restricted classroom systems and funded cheques from sector levies. American frontier work slowed for lack of political permission rather than chips, and Chinese vendors filled the gap in European ports with more logistics machines and care pilots, to union protests.

By June the Union had a triage desk, a rationing board, and blame over queues it could not clear.
