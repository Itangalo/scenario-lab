### The cutoff
In September the access keys stopped working. Clinicians in Lyon, administrators in Milan and Rotterdam, and several ministries found the leading American model returning refusals without explanation. No appeal channel answered. Washington called it a routine compliance review; Brussels called it a denial.

Because the cut landed on wards already running on quotas, the Commission's new continuity switch mattered immediately. Pooled EuroHPC time was retriaged, degraded European substitutes were pushed out under guardrails, and where no substitute cleared the safety hold, paper protocols and older tools returned. The lights stayed on, but slower.

### The jump
Weeks later a new American demonstration made every timeline obsolete. A system shown to selected press saturated coding and science benchmarks and steered long tasks without pausing. Labs dismissed leaked slides as cherry-picking; university re-runners again could not reproduce the leap on the thinner substitutes they could access.

Brussels refused an emergency import. The anomaly notification and hold regime, now formally closed, stayed enforced through the Safety Institute: no jumped capability, no Chinese substitute without a re-run. That held trust with evaluators in Paris and Berlin and deepened anger in waiting rooms.

Concrete did not move. Japanese maintenance kits kept existing machines alive; the Korean memory lot went elsewhere; the shells outside Paris and Warsaw stayed guarded and empty. The rationing board became the face of scarcity.

By December queues had become outages managed by triage. Ministers said foresight had prevented collapse. Voters heard that foresight did not cure.
