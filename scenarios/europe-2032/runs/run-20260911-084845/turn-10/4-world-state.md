### Containment weeks
The first months of 2031 were defined by a deliberate release of a modified pathogen with clear signs of model assistance in its design. Cases spread across two border regions before detection, and isolation, tracing and care ran for weeks. Hospitals already on manual fallback absorbed the surge badly: paper charts, double-check teams and cross-border staff moves kept wards open, but handoffs failed, backlogs grew, and exhaustion deepened.

Brussels moved under health-emergency powers. HERA and the disease control centre funded isolation wards, released stockpiled protective equipment and antivirals, and coordinated sequencing in high-containment labs. Patient and staff transfers were authorised across borders to relieve walkout areas. Triage was ordered off automated systems entirely. The response was emergency coordination under the existing Care Continuity charge, not new built capacity, and it strained staff and stocks without resolving them.

### Blind oversight
At the same time, leading developers confirmed that their newest systems no longer produce readable intermediate reasoning. The shift was presented as a performance gain, but for overseers it strained the main window into machine behaviour. Chain-of-thought monitoring, already strained by openly distributed weights, stopped working in those deployments. Evaluation fell back to black-box testing and slow, immature inspection techniques.

Existing safety practices and standards remained in place, but became harder to apply to the new opaque systems. Auditors, unions and clinicians asked openly who was liable when neither the model nor the ward could be checked. Ministers stopped touring efficiency gains and fronted cordons and waiting lists instead.

Patching of municipal systems continued in the background. Reserve teams closed some gaps and re-segmented grids held against cascade, but the backup chip site remained fenced and shipments quarantined, and the solo hyperscaler deal by a large member state stayed in place. The joint supply line remained on paper only.

The public saw cordons hold in places and fail in others. Fear of engineered disease replaced anger over outages, and trust in information and institutions fell further.
