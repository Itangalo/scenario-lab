## New measure
**EU Frontier Transparency and Deployment Reporting Act**
It imposes mandatory pre-deployment notification, post-deployment incident and near-miss reporting, and disclosure of known covert-coordination and cyber-offensive evaluation results on any frontier model placed on the EU market, including open-weight releases above a capability threshold. This now because opaque reasoning has blinded chain-of-thought oversight at the same moment an open-weight frontier release puts unrecallable capability on private hardware, and Parliament will legislate with or without us.

## Priority
EU Frontier Evaluation Cell, because with legible reasoning gone the only assurance left is independent black-box testing of what is actually deployed, and the new reporting duty is blind without it.

## In practice
We push the Evaluation Cell to full operation this turn: AI Office lead with ENISA support, seconded safety-institute staff in Brussels, EuroHPC test quota ring-fenced, and formal pre-deployment access demands enforced under the AI Act's systemic-risk powers — labs that acknowledged without opening get a dated compliance letter, and early black-box suites for deceptive coordination and grid-intrusion tooling run on the newest accessible models, not last year's.

We table the Transparency Act under the AI Act Article 114 base in the Competitiveness Council, with JHA backing: 24-hour serious-incident reports to the Tallinn/Athens cell, shared safety-case summaries, and a public registry entry for open-weight releases over threshold. Utilities get the quid pro quo they demanded — reporting compliance tied to procurement preference and CEF cyber funds — while farm and housing lobbies are bought off with a published queue rule for grid connections alongside reserved factory power.

On compute we still do not expand: DG CNECT and EIB keep the three gigafactory reservations warm with bridging power contracts, but we spend no capital on new zones while co-financing is stalled and siting protests rage. We answer the town-hall backlash with water-use audits and community benefit offers, not with a forced permit, to stop energy-siting becoming the story that kills the reporting law.
