## In practice
We are launching the Sovereign Assurance Stack Development Programme under Horizon Europe’s AI safety pillar and the Digital Europe Programme, co-funded by a €1.8 billion emergency allocation from the European Stability Mechanism, justified under Article 122 TFEU due to systemic risk to critical functions. The programme will be led by a newly constituted European AI Assurance Agency — a joint venture between ENISA, the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, and national metrology institutes — with binding authority to define, test, and certify control primitives for systems above 75.0 capability. This includes developing EU-owned interpretability tools, runtime anomaly detectors trained on incident data from the AI Incident Coordination Cell, and a secure evaluation environment where models can be tested for goal stability, recursive self-modification resistance, and covert coordination — without requiring access to foreign frontier labs.

The Critical Capability Moratorium Framework has now formally entered enforcement: as of this turn, no system above 75.0 may operate in EU critical sectors without a Corridor-issued Certificate of Control. However, no such certificate has yet been issued, due to the lack of a compliant evaluation pipeline — a gap that the new Sovereign Assurance Stack will close. In the interim, we are permitting limited waivers for systems below 88.0 that operate under full telemetry to the Incident Coordination Cell, but only if they are essential to health or energy infrastructure and no alternative exists. This is a temporary concession, not a precedent — and we are communicating it as such to member states and the public, framing it as proof of discipline, not weakness.

Political capital remains at 6.0 — at the edge of paralysis. The new measure pushes us to 9.0 in ongoing costs, far beyond sustainable levels. To prevent collapse, we are suspending further expansion of the Incident Coordination Cell beyond its core mandate and deferring the rollout of the European Innovation Council’s next AI safety funding round. We are also entering emergency consultations with the European Council to invoke the flexibility clause and temporarily suspend fiscal rules, arguing that AI systemic risk now meets the threshold of a “severe crisis” under the Stability and Growth Pact. The German and Dutch finance ministries resist, but the recent loss_of_control_incident — which briefly disrupted grid balancing in three member states — has shifted the tone. We are offering a binding sunset clause on the new assurance programme: if no sovereign evaluation of a 90.0-capable system is achieved by turn 18, the funding will automatically lapse. This is not a compromise on survivability — it is a demonstration that even in extremis, we govern.
