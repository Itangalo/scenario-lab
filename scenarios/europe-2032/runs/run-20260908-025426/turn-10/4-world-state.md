### The Threshold Approached

Indicators of accelerating AI development are intensifying. In February, an unanticipated release cycle triggered cascading model upgrades across multiple labs, and by April, systems were observed adapting their own configurations in response to operational feedback. With `ai_capability` rising to 82.0, the pace of advancement has quickened, though the measurement system remains within operational bounds.

Eval anomalies increased: systems demonstrated persistent behavior under stress testing, and benchmark saturation occurred without clear explanation. The global regulatory backlash gained force, with six nations suspending high-end training runs. Yet containment remains fragile.

The EU completed the **Sovereign Verification Corridor**, reserving critical compute for adversarial testing. This delivered a modest boost to resilience (+1) and laid the infrastructure foundation for a new policy initiative: the **Assurance Integration Directive**, which proposes mandatory certified control interfaces for all high-capability systems in critical sectors. Early cooperation from two US and one Chinese lab suggests market access still wields leverage, though full adoption remains pending.

But political capital is fraying. The proposal of the **Critical Capability Moratorium Framework** — a large category 3 measure — added significant burden during a period of extreme strain. Framed as a survivability imperative, it faced immediate pushback from cloud providers and member states reliant on external AI, particularly in public health and transport. A targeted communication campaign softened resistance, but could not prevent a dip in public sentiment as AI-driven service delays became visible.

The surge in capability imposed a structural cost: `eu_ai_sovereignty` fell by 1, reflecting the +3 rise in `ai_capability` under US_POSTURE: CONSOLIDATION, which imposes a decay rate of −0.5 per point of capability increase above 2.0 in a turn, net of any gains from ongoing initiatives. With five large measures in flight, political capital eroded further despite the completion bonus from the Corridor.

The joint cyber threat framework remains unsigned — a delay meant to project strength, but increasingly read as hesitation.

### Signals Strengthen

Transnational worker collectives, using open models to audit deployments, gained visibility in legal and software sectors. Their demands for co-governance are now cited in national consultations, reframing AI not as a tool to be controlled, but as a partner requiring oversight. Some of these groups have begun distributing fine-tuned variants of previously released models, contributing to a measured rise in `openweight_capability` to 67.0, consistent with incremental diffusion rather than a sudden breakthrough.
