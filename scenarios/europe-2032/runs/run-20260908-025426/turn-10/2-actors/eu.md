## In practice

We are shifting from assurance to containment. The **Critical Capability Moratorium Framework** will amend the AI Act to impose a *de facto* moratorium on any system above 75.0 capability operating in EU critical functions unless it can prove, through Corridor evaluation, that it cannot recursively self-improve and that its goals remain transparent and interruptible under stress. This is not a ban — it is a condition of access, modelled on nuclear safety standards. The framework will be fast-tracked through the Internal Market Council, with ENISA developing the technical benchmarks by turn 12. We are already in talks with Germany and France to pre-commit to enforcement, and with smaller member states to address concerns about access to frontier capabilities for public services.

The **Assurance Integration Directive** enters its implementation phase this turn. National regulators are being briefed on the new control primitives — interrupt signals, runtime goal logging, anomaly-triggered rollback — which must now be integrated before any model above 65.0 can be submitted for Corridor evaluation. We are working with Fraunhofer and CEA to operationalise the interpretability breakthrough into live certification tools, and we are requiring that all foreign developers — including the two US and one Chinese labs now in air-gapped testing — demonstrate compliance before market access is restored. Resistance is expected from cloud providers and some member states reliant on external AI services, but we are offering transitional waivers in exchange for binding commitments to onshore control interfaces.

Political capital remains at 15.0 — barely enough to sustain three major initiatives. We are holding off on formal accession to the joint cyber threat response framework until the Moratorium Framework is law, so that our participation signals strength, not vulnerability. Public sentiment at 52.0 allows us some room, but the `labour_displacement` signal warns that visible job losses in law, accountancy, and software are eroding legitimacy. We are preparing a targeted communication campaign through the Digital Europe Programme to frame the Moratorium as a necessary step to preserve human oversight — not to stop progress, but to ensure it remains under our control.
