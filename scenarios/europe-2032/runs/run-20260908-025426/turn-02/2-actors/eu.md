## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_test_shot has already breached critical systems, and without immediate, binding hardening across energy, water, and transport, the Union risks cascading failures that could collapse public confidence and paralyse decision-making just as openweight adoption widens the attack surface.

## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_test_shot not as an isolated incident but as proof of systemic vulnerability. This turn, the Commission is enforcing Article 12 of DORA by requiring all essential entities in energy, water, and transport to complete AI-driven threat assessments by April 2027, with ENISA publishing standardised templates and threat libraries by end-February. We are fast-tracking the NIS2 delegated act on AI-augmented monitoring, aiming for adoption in the COREPER meeting of March, backed by emergency funding: €8.2bn from reprioritised cohesion funds is now ring-fenced, though disbursement remains conditional on national operational programme adjustments. To accelerate delivery, we are activating the Civil Protection Mechanism to pre-position transformer spares at strategic nodes and launching a pan-European cyber drill — “Operation Shieldfall” — in May, involving all 27 member states’ CSIRTs and red teams from the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by resolving the transformer bottleneck: through the Energy Council, we are negotiating a joint procurement mechanism with Norway and Ukraine’s grid operators to pool orders and secure priority delivery from Siemens and CG Power. On talent, the new Talent Anchoring Initiative will be launched via Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme, offering €1.2bn in guaranteed 7-year research grants tied to EU-based institutions and conditional on access to sovereign compute clusters. We are also introducing fast-track “AI Pioneer” visas and tax incentives for relocating researchers, to be coordinated through the newly empowered European Talent Office. Resistance persists in the ECOFIN Council, where some member states argue that fiscal headroom is too tight for parallel pushes on resilience, compute, and talent — so we are offering to defer cohesion disbursements for non-priority regions until Q3 to preserve capital. The Tech Sovereignty Package remains in trilogue, with the Parliament pushing for stronger local content clauses in the permitting waivers, while the Council resists. We are holding firm: without binding commitments on EU-controlled compute, all our resilience and talent investments will be stranded on foreign infrastructure.
