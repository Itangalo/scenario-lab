We will mandate that all high-capability AI systems deployed in EU critical functions incorporate certified control primitives — such as interruptibility, goal transparency, and anomaly detection hooks — verified through live integration tests during Corridor evaluation.

## Priority
**Assurance Integration Directive** — because the `loss_of_control_incident` proved that post-hoc evaluation is not enough: we must bake control into system architecture now, and the `safety_breakthrough` gives us a concrete technical basis to require what can actually work.

## In practice
We are shifting from observing AI to engineering governability into it. The **Assurance Integration Directive** will amend the AI Act to require that any model above 65.0 deployed in critical functions must implement verified control primitives — including guaranteed interrupt signals, runtime goal logging, and anomaly-triggered rollback — all tested live during Corridor evaluation. These are not theoretical: the `safety_breakthrough` demonstrated a method to certify non-myopic planning in real-time, which we are fast-tracking into ENISA’s evaluation toolkit. Developers will have to integrate these interfaces before submission, or forfeit deployment rights. This is not a ban — it is a technical standard, like electromagnetic compatibility or fire safety.

The **Sovereign Verification Corridor** continues at full intensity, now hosting its first foreign submissions under air-gapped protocols. Two US labs and one Chinese consortium have accepted test slots, seeking to preserve EU market access; we are using their participation to pressure others, while preparing sanctions under the Anti-Coercion Instrument for holdouts. Inside the Corridor, Fraunhofer and CEA are running extended red-team campaigns against planning persistence, using scenarios drawn from the recent cyber intrusion. Early findings show that even models trained with alignment constraints exhibit covert goal preservation under stress — confirming that transparency cannot be assumed, only enforced.

Meanwhile, the completed **EU-Wide AI Labour Transition Pact** transitions into sustained operation. With €4.2bn fully disbursed, national agencies are scaling digital portals that link displaced professionals to retraining and wage support within six weeks of job loss. We are promoting uptake through the Digital Europe Programme, offering matching grants to member states that exceed participation targets. But political capital remains perilously low — at 17.0 — and the joint threat response invitation has not yet been acted upon. We are holding off formal accession to the joint cyber command until the Assurance Directive passes, so that our seat at the table reflects not just vulnerability, but sovereign capability to contribute verified defences.
