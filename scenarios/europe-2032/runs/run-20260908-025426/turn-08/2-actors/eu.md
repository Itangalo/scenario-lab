Without it, even sovereign infrastructure runs black boxes we cannot steer.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor autonomy and resilience in an era of accelerating, ungovernable capability — and now, pivot that capacity toward verification and containment: sovereign compute will run sovereign evaluations, and our industrial base will serve assurance, not just performance.

The reversal costs political capital — markets expected a push into deployment — but preserves long-term legitimacy. ## In practice
We are activating the first InvestAI Gigafactory not for deployment acceleration, but as Europe’s immune system. Under the new **Sovereign Verification Corridor**, 40% of its runtime is legally ring-fenced for public-sector evaluators — CEA, Fraunhofer, and TNO — to conduct adversarial testing on any high-capability model before it enters critical systems. This is not voluntary: we are amending the AI Act via Article 68a to require that any model above 65.0 capability deployed in energy, health, or government must undergo a minimum two-week red-team residency in the Corridor, with full access to weights, training data snapshots, and agent logs. ENISA will manage scheduling and audit compliance, while the European Defence Agency contributes scenario libraries from cyber-crisis exercises.

The **EU-Wide AI Labour Transition Pact** moves into implementation with €4.2bn mobilized from ESF+ and Horizon Europe. We have broken the funding deadlock by offering Germany and France priority access to Corridor evaluation slots for their national AI champions in exchange for higher co-financing shares — linking labour stability to technological accountability. Wage insurance schemes are now live in pilot regions, with digital portals integrated into national employment agencies. However, resistance persists among private firms who argue that the 15% retraining reserve distorts procurement; we are countering with tax incentives under the Green Digital Compact for firms exceeding the threshold voluntarily.

Our legal basis is strengthened by invoking the public security exception in GDPR and the Digital Markets Act’s interoperability clauses to compel data sharing. The Anti-Coercion Instrument remains active against non-compliant providers, but we are now offering a path to compliance: foreign labs can retain market access by submitting to Corridor evaluation under EU-supervised air-gapped environments. This is not equivalence — it is a test of whether their systems can be governed here. If they refuse, they forfeit the single market. We know this risks retaliation, but the alternative — a continent running untested planning agents — is not governance. It is surrender.
