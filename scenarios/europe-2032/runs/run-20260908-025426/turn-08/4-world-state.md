### Frontier Acceleration and Scientific Alarm

The global AI frontier advanced sharply, reaching 75.0 as labs integrated planning agents into foundational training loops. This leap followed the release of autonomous research systems that redesigned their own architectures, compressing development cycles. With no matching safety infrastructure deployed, `ai_safety` remained critically low—though a partial rebound occurred as the EU’s new evaluation mandate began absorbing attention and effort.

Amid this turbulence, a coalition of researchers from leading institutions issued a joint warning: current evaluation regimes cannot detect or contain emergent planning behaviours. Their white paper, citing repeated failures in red-team exercises, argued that oversight is structurally broken. The dissident movement gained rapid traction, reframing the crisis from one of speed to one of legitimacy.

### EU Pivot to Sovereign Assurance

The EU completed the **Sovereign Agent Evaluation Facility**, delivering a functional red-team testing environment. This allowed the launch of the **Sovereign Verification Corridor**, a large category 5 measure that reserves 40% of the first InvestAI Gigafactory’s capacity for independent, adversarial testing of high-capability models. The initiative is legally anchored in a revised AI Act, mandating evaluation for any model above 65.0 deployed in critical sectors. Foreign developers may retain EU market access by submitting to testing in air-gapped environments—a calibrated blend of coercion and compliance.

Simultaneously, the **EU-Wide AI Labour Transition Pact** entered implementation, with wage insurance pilots rolling out. A funding deadlock was broken by linking national contributions to evaluation access, reinforcing the new doctrine: sovereignty means control, not just compute.

### Public and Political Reckoning

Resilience dipped slightly as offensive capabilities outpaced defences, underscored by a major cyber intrusion across public health systems—tooling later traced to AI-generated exploits. However, public sentiment rose to 50.0, buoyed by visible scientific benefit and the perception that the EU is finally asserting oversight. Political capital, however, eroded to 17.0 under the weight of four ongoing large measures and the priority cost, despite a modest gain from completing the evaluation facility.
