### Cyber Shock and the EU’s Emergency Response

The discovery of a widespread, stealthy intrusion across critical infrastructure operators — including two EU grid operators — triggered alarm in Brussels. Dubbed a "cyber test shot" by analysts, the breach revealed that existing detection systems were blind to AI-driven attack patterns. The EU responded swiftly, activating emergency powers under DORA and NIS2 to mandate rapid risk assessments and initiate AI-augmented monitoring. ENISA launched a joint task force, and proposals to repurpose cohesion funds were fast-tracked to accelerate defences in energy, water, and transport sectors.

However, the scale of the proposed €8.2 billion in redirected cohesion funding faced immediate procedural hurdles. Emergency provisions allowed expedited review, but full disbursement required Council alignment and adjustments to national operational programmes, delaying implementation beyond the current turn. Technical bottlenecks — especially in transformer supply and skilled personnel — further slowed deployment. While the political intent was clear, execution remained fragmented and partial.

### Open Weights Go Mainstream

Simultaneously, a major open-weight model release landed just months behind the closed frontier, spreading rapidly across private and research hardware. This advancement lifted `openweight_capability`, but not at the pace initially feared. The increase reflects broad adoption and incremental optimization, not a systemic leap. The model remains behind the closed frontier, and while it widens the attack surface, it has not yet outpaced regulatory visibility. Export controls are increasingly strained, but the distribution has not reached the scale or capability level that would render them obsolete.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

Despite ongoing investments in compute infrastructure — the Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package — `eu_ai_sovereignty` declined slightly as global capability advanced faster than the EU could build. The `ai_capability` rise of +2.5 strained assurance, and with no safety measures yet landed on shipped systems, `ai_safety` dropped. The resilience programme is gaining momentum but has not yet delivered measurable gains — only planning and initial contracts.

### Political Costs Mount

The **Emergency Resilience Surge** was formally proposed as a new initiative, but its implementation is staggered and partial within this turn. Given that two major measures (InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package) are already in flight, the Commission has chosen to widen the scope of the existing Tech Sovereignty Package to include emergency monitoring components, rather than launch a fully separate priority. This adaptation avoids exceeding the limit of one new measure per turn. Some political capital was spent to push legal mechanisms and redirect initial funding, but full cohesion across member states has not yet been achieved. Public sentiment held steady — fears of disruption balanced by recognition of the threat — but the window for credible action is narrowing.
