## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_test_shot not as an isolated breach but as a signal that our current preparedness is obsolete. This turn, the Commission activates Article 12 of the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) to mandate immediate risk assessments across all designated essential entities in energy, water, and transport, with a focus on supply-chain and third-party software exposure. We are fast-tracking a delegated act under the NIS2 Directive to impose minimum AI-augmented monitoring requirements on these sectors by Q1 2027, backed by emergency funding from the Crisis Instrument — €8.2bn reprioritised from unused cohesion funds. ENISA will lead a joint task force with national CSIRTs to deploy standardised anomaly-detection agents, trained on synthetic attack patterns generated by our own red-team labs at EUIPO and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking.

At the same time, we are pushing the InvestAI Gigafactories forward by convening a summit of energy ministers and grid operators to pre-clear transmission upgrades for the first two sites — one in Finland, one in Spain — where land acquisition is already complete. The bottleneck now is not political will but transformer availability and skilled labour; we are using the recently activated EU Talent Atlas to redirect Erasmus+ engineering cohorts toward grid certification programmes. Resistance remains in the Council’s Competitiveness configuration, where some member states fear inflationary pressure from simultaneous infrastructure pushes, but we are offering rebalanced cohesion disbursements to offset near-term fiscal strain. The Tech sovereignty package is advancing through trilogue, though delays persist on the permitting waivers — we are now threatening to invoke urgent procedure under Article 240 TFEU if consensus stalls beyond February.
