## New measure  
**Secure Critical Research Infrastructure (Category 4: Sovereignty and industrial capacity)**  
Establish a legally binding framework ensuring that all frontier AI research conducted with EU public funding or on EU soil is subject to weight control, export controls, and non-transfer clauses, with sovereign compute access conditional on compliance.

## In practice  
We are treating the Talent Anchoring Initiative not as an end in itself but as a lever to rebuild structural control over AI development in Europe. This turn, we are finalising the legal architecture for the **Secure Critical Research Infrastructure**, to be proposed under Article 114 TFEU (internal market) and Article 185 (joint research initiatives), conditioning access to Horizon Europe AI grants and sovereign compute clusters on binding commitments to weight security, non-export clauses, and third-party audit rights for high-capability models. The European Talent Office, now operational in Brussels, is being empowered with enforcement authority: residency fast-tracks and tax incentives will be tied to institutional compliance, not individual promises. We are negotiating with the European Research Council and national academies to align grant conditions, and with EuroHPC JU to ensure that access to LUMI-class machines requires adherence to the new framework.

Simultaneously, we are closing out the Emergency Resilience Surge: ENISA has delivered standardised threat templates, and Operation Shieldfall confirmed both readiness gains and persistent coordination gaps — particularly in cross-border fallback protocols for energy grids. The final report will be presented to COREPER II this month, and we are preparing a delegated act under NIS2 to convert its findings into binding obligations by Q1 2028. On the InvestAI Gigafactories, the joint procurement mechanism with Norway and Ukraine has secured transformer delivery timelines, but land acquisition in Poland and Romania faces local opposition; we are deploying the European Infrastructure Mechanism to fast-track permitting, invoking the safeguard clause for strategic digital infrastructure. The Tech Sovereignty Package remains in trilogue: Parliament insists on stronger local content rules, while Council fears WTO challenges. We are holding firm on a minimum 60% EU-controlled compute threshold for permitting waivers, knowing that without it, we are building capacity that can still be switched off from abroad. Fiscal pressure from ECOFIN remains acute, but the modest rise in public_sentiment has given us breathing room — for now.
