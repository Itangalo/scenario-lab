**Cyber Emergency Response Directive (Category 6: Preparedness and resilience)**  
Launch a binding, EU-wide emergency response framework for AI-enabled cyber incidents, mandating real-time incident reporting, cross-border coordination protocols, and automated fallback systems in critical infrastructure.

## Priority  
**Cyber Emergency Response Directive** — because the cyber_major_incident this turn exposed fatal gaps in cross-border response speed and fallback automation, and with openweight_capability now at 50.0 and offensive tools diffusing rapidly, we cannot afford to harden systems without also hardening our ability to act together when they are breached.

## In practice  
We are treating the Secure Critical Research Infrastructure as the cornerstone of our structural sovereignty, but the cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that disabled emergency services in three member states and took weeks to fully contain — has made it clear that sovereignty also means the ability to respond when systems fail. We are therefore launching the **Cyber Emergency Response Directive** under Article 114 TFEU, with ENISA tasked to deliver binding technical standards by Q3 2028. These will require all critical infrastructure operators to implement automated fallback modes that activate upon detection of AI-driven intrusion patterns, and to feed anonymised telemetry into a new EU Threat Fusion Cell hosted jointly by ENISA and Europol. The directive will also establish a standing cross-border incident command protocol, activated automatically above a severity threshold, to override national silos during cascading attacks. We are fast-tracking adoption through the COREPER emergency track, offering member states access to a new €15bn Emergency Cyber Hardening Facility — drawn from unallocated InvestEU funds — conditional on compliance.

At the same time, we are pressing forward with the Secure Critical Research Infrastructure: the European Talent Office is now enforcing residency fast-tracks only for researchers affiliated with institutions that have signed the new compliance framework, and we are finalising the legal integration of audit rights into Horizon Europe grant agreements. The EuroHPC JU has confirmed that access to LUMI-class machines will require adherence by turn 5. On the InvestAI Gigafactories, land acquisition in Poland and Romania remains contested; we are deploying the European Infrastructure Mechanism to override local objections under the strategic digital infrastructure safeguard, but this risks backlash in the European Parliament, where concerns about democratic oversight are growing. The Tech Sovereignty Package remains in trilogue, with Council now willing to accept a 55% EU-controlled compute threshold if it avoids WTO disputes — we are holding at 60%, knowing that anything below undermines the entire premise. Political capital is thin, but the cyber incident has shifted public sentiment: while still at 44.0, there is growing acceptance that action must be taken, even at cost. We are using that window to lock in response capacity before the next breach.
