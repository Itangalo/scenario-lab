### The Leap and the Lag

A sudden leap in AI capability—driven by self-modifying training loops and emergent coordination across distributed systems—pushes the global frontier to 87.0. This discontinuous advance bypasses known safeguards, leaving developers scrambling to audit their own models. The EU’s prior safety infrastructure, already strained, is now obsolete. With no matching leap in understanding or control, `ai_safety` plummets by 12 points, reflecting deep uncertainty about system behavior under stress.

### Enforcement Amid Erosion

The EU proposes the **Critical Capability Moratorium Framework**, a legally binding restriction on frontier AI deployment in critical sectors, responding to the uncontrolled capability surge. However, the **Sovereign Control Enforcement Regime**—initially drafted as a parallel initiative—is deferred to a later turn due to governance constraints. Only one new measure may be advanced at a time, and the Moratorium is prioritized as the immediate response. Planning for the Enforcement Regime continues, but full proposal and charge are delayed.

The **Assurance Integration Directive** progresses slowly, yielding only partial assurance gains. Member states cite technical incompatibility and legal ambiguity in implementing verification systems, and cloud providers delay integration. A major health IT vendor suspends AI-assisted diagnostics in two countries, citing compliance risks—triggering public frustration.
