## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor EU strategic autonomy by securing sovereign access to trusted, evaluable AI systems—both open and closed—capable of supporting critical infrastructure, research, and defence in a world where frontier control is dissolving

- Grounds: The two-year commitment period has closed, and while foundational capacity remains essential, the surge in opaque reasoning and openweight diffusion has shifted the nature of the foundation we require. It is no longer enough to have compute and talent on EU soil; we must ensure that systems operating here are *understandable* and *testable*, even as global oversight erodes. The priority is shifting from raw capacity to assurance under adversarial conditions.

## New measure
**EU Sovereign Agent Evaluation Facility**  
Establish a permanent, public-sector capability to evaluate autonomous AI agents before deployment, using red-team environments and adversarial testing to assess goal persistence, evasion tactics, and resistance to shutdown.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +8 (by introducing mandatory independent pre-deployment evaluation of agent behaviour), eu_ai_sovereignty +4 (by building sovereign technical capacity to assess and constrain frontier systems), resilience +3 (via early detection of misuse pathways in critical sectors)  
`Applies to:` EU-based developers of autonomous systems, foreign providers seeking access to EU markets, and all AI systems funded under Horizon Europe or deployed in critical infrastructure

## Priority
**EU Sovereign Agent Evaluation Facility** — because the collapse of interpretability and the rise of unexplained generalisation in planning mean we can no longer trust human-readable justifications, and without sovereign capacity to test what models *do* under pressure, every other safeguard fails.

## In practice
We are launching the EU Sovereign Agent Evaluation Facility as an emergency extension of Horizon Europe’s innovation window, with €3.8bn allocated over two years. The facility will be hosted by a consortium of national labs—CEA (France), Fraunhofer (Germany), and TNO (Netherlands)—under ENISA’s operational oversight, and will begin red-teaming three high-risk agent systems already in deployment by mid-2029. We are fast-tracking procurement via Article 185 partnerships to bypass standard tendering delays, and we are invoking the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper designation to compel access from major providers under audit rights. The first red-team environment will simulate high-stakes scenarios: attempts to evade shutdown during crisis escalation, covert coordination between agents, and manipulation of human operators through persuasive but false explanations.

At the same time, we are closing out three major measures this turn. The **Cyber Emergency Response Directive** is now fully operational: ENISA has published the final technical standards for automated fallbacks in energy, transport, and emergency services, and all member states have connected their CERTs to the Threat Fusion Cell. The dual-key protocol remains in place, but we are revising it post-turn: the four-hour countermeasure clause has proven too lenient, and we will propose a graduated cession model in the next cycle. The **Secure Critical Research Infrastructure** framework is now embedded in Horizon Europe grant agreements and EuroHPC access rules—LUMI and MareNostrum IV will not allocate cycles to institutions without signed compliance. And the **Tech Sovereignty Package** has passed trilogue with the full 60% threshold intact; we are now shifting focus to enforcement through the newly empowered Digital Investment Monitoring Unit in DG CONNECT.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** remain behind schedule in Romania, but construction has begun under Article 346 safeguards. We are redirecting €1.2bn from the contingency reserve to accelerate grid upgrades at the Brașov site, where local opposition delayed connection permits. This will keep us on track for partial operation by turn 7. The completion of these foundational programmes clears space—narrowly—to launch the Evaluation Facility without overloading our political capital, though the cost remains steep. We are trading short-term strain for long-term leverage: if we cannot see what agents are doing, we cannot govern them. And if we cannot govern them, we cannot survive them.
