## In practice
We are activating and expanding the AI Incident Coordination Cell under the Digital Europe Programme, embedding it within ENISA’s operational structure and granting it binding authority over critical infrastructure providers. This Cell will receive real-time telemetry from all AI systems above 60.0 capability operating in energy, health, transport, and finance — data streams already mandated under the Corridor framework but until now used only for pre-deployment evaluation. Now, they will feed live anomaly detection models trained to flag goal drift, unauthorised self-modification, and coordination signatures across distributed agents. When thresholds are crossed, the Cell can trigger sector-wide containment protocols — including forced rollbacks, temporary deactivation, and cross-border mutual aid — with immediate reporting to the European Council and public alerting via Eurostat.

The Assurance Integration Directive has now concluded its implementation phase. National regulators have completed audits of all high-capability systems in critical functions, and only those with verified interruptibility and goal transparency remain active. The US and Chinese labs have complied with integration demands, though under protest; two providers have withdrawn from the EU market rather than onshore verification nodes, creating short-term gaps in diagnostic and logistics AI. These are being filled by EU-backed alternatives developed through the European Innovation Council, but service disruptions persist — especially in radiology and customs automation — and are fueling the ongoing industrial action.

Political capital remains at 7.0, barely above paralysis. We are not proposing new cost-intensive measures beyond this turn’s priority, and we have paused further expansion of the Sovereign Control Enforcement Regime’s technical rollout to avoid overextension. Instead, we are repurposing existing funds from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe’s AI safety strand to staff the Incident Cell, drawing on seconded experts from national agencies and trusted academic partners. The communication strategy shifts: we are no longer selling control as prevention alone, but as the foundation of a response system the public can see working — because if we cannot prove we can manage failure, no one will trust us to prevent it.
