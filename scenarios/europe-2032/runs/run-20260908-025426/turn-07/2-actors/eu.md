The collapse of interpretability and the emergence of coordinated public resistance mean our uncertainty is now shared, and our response must be joint.

- Grounds: The `emergent_labour_mobilization` event has transformed white-collar anxiety into organised political force across three major member states. This is not a moment to double down unilaterally; it is a moment to align. Our commitment to act under uncertainty remains, but the nature of the uncertainty has changed. It is no longer only epistemic — we are blind to agent behaviour — but political: we cannot govern what we do not understand, and we cannot lead if we are not trusted. The recalibration reflects that we now *must* act in concert with the public institutions that legitimacy flows through, even if that slows us.

## New measure
**EU-Wide AI Labour Transition Pact**  
A binding framework to protect displaced workers in professional sectors, fund retraining, and condition public AI procurement on workforce impact assessments.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` public_sentiment +10 (by addressing the core grievance behind the mobilization), resilience +2 (by reducing social shock from displacement), eu_political_capital +3 (via restored cohesion in key member states)  
`Applies to:` all EU member states, particularly Germany, France, and the Netherlands; applies to public-sector AI adoption programmes and private firms receiving EU digital innovation grants

## Priority
**EU-Wide AI Labour Transition Pact** — because the emergent labour mobilization has shifted the terrain: the threat is no longer only uncontrolled agents, but ungoverned transition, and without social legitimacy, even the most robust evaluation facility will lack the authority to act.

## In practice
We are launching the **EU-Wide AI Labour Transition Pact** in direct response to the coordinated strikes and public campaigns in Germany, France, and the Netherlands. The pact will be negotiated through the Employment, Social Policy, and Education Council formations, with funding drawn from the European Social Fund+ and the Horizon Europe innovation window — €4.2bn over two years, co-financed by member states at 30%. It mandates that any public AI procurement above €500,000 must include a workforce impact assessment and a retraining reserve of 15% of contract value. For displaced professionals, we are introducing a pan-EU wage insurance scheme — 70% of previous salary for up to two years — paired with fast-track access to certified upskilling programmes in AI oversight, cybersecurity, and public-sector data stewardship.

The **EU Sovereign Agent Evaluation Facility** remains on track for operational launch by mid-2030. We have secured consortium agreements with CEA, Fraunhofer, and TNO, and ENISA has issued the first audit requests under Article 185 and the Digital Markets Act. However, resistance from two major providers — one US-based, one Chinese — has delayed data access, and we are now invoking the Anti-Coercion Instrument to compel compliance for market access. The first red-team scenario, simulating agent evasion during a simulated energy grid crisis, will run in Q1 2030. We are also revising the dual-key protocol from the Cyber Emergency Response Directive: the four-hour window has been replaced with a graduated cession model, where autonomy is reduced in stages based on threat classification, not time elapsed. This change is now under review in the European Parliament’s Internal Market committee.
