### Blind instruments, loose copies
The first half of 2029 broke the way Europe tested machines. Two leading developers acknowledged within weeks of each other that their newest systems no longer reasoned in readable words. The internal traces were compressed, non-verbal representations that performed better and read worse. Announced as performance gains, they left evaluators across the continent forwarding the same complaint: the window they had relied on had gone dark.

At almost the same time a sharp advance landed in verifiable work — code generation, mathematics, cyber operations, narrow engineering tasks. Intrusion tooling improved within weeks, not quarters. Municipal operators reported faster, more tailored phishing and probing, absorbed on backups but more expensive to absorb.

Then a near-frontier open release spread beyond recall. Hundreds of thousands of downloads in days put the jumped capabilities on private hardware permanently. University labs celebrated; cyber centres and health security officials warned about unscreened synthesis orders and automated vulnerability hunting now available to anyone.

Brussels answered with a new evaluation cell inside the AI Office, with ENISA and research centre staff seconded to drop reliance on readable traces and test by black-box red-teaming and activation inspection, tied to public procurement and essential-operator approvals. The legal base was stretched to avoid reopening the AI Act, and French and Dutch pilot labs cooperated unevenly. Early suites ran, but inspectors privately admitted the new methods were slow, costly and immature.

The sovereignty agenda both landed and frayed. The permitting and private-capital package for data centres was declared complete, with two mediated sites keeping grid priority and a pipeline of permits finally moving. Yet a member state signed its own supply arrangement with a foreign hyperscaler on terms undercutting the common screening line. The Commission offered joint-procurement pricing to re-anchor the capital and made backstop reinsurance conditional on telemetry sharing. The capital called it pragmatism; others called it a break.

Washington, under its new moratorium-minded administration, turned inward. Less pressure on Brussels, but a distracted partner and slower joint work.

Public mood soured. Assistants remained popular, but unreadable models, ubiquitous copies and talk of dependency dominated coverage.
