### Proof in Europe, pressure in the Strait
The second half of 2029 gave Brussels one thing it could point to and one it could only prepare for.

In a joint effort run through the Ispra labs and the French and Dutch pilot teams, researchers demonstrated AI-generated systems code delivered with machine-checked proof certificates — kernel-level patches for industrial drivers that reviewers could verify in minutes rather than days. Specialists called it a step-change: proof-carrying code moving beyond toy kernels. Municipal security centres began trialling the tooling for defensive patching, though the same advance sharpened intrusion tools elsewhere, and with readable traces gone the new black-box inspection suites remained slow and costly.

The evaluation cell created to replace trace-reading was declared operational. Tied to procurement and essential-operator approvals, it ran its first full red-team campaigns. Inspectors admitted methods were immature, and cooperation from developers was formal rather than forthcoming. With frontier labs withholding weights and methods, open-weight models advanced only incrementally this turn, largely by distilling older releases — no leakage, no coordinated frontier release.

The data-centre build was declared substantially complete in permitting and contracting terms: permits moving, two mediated sites holding grid priority, private capital nominally anchored by joint procurement pricing and telemetry-conditioned reinsurance. Physical construction, however, slipped by weeks as coordinated blockades and grid-connection protests flared, amplified by distrust of unreadable models. The InvestAI Gigafactories therefore remain unfinished this turn; partial capacity is credited but full effect is pushed out, and no completion bonus for sovereignty is taken this turn. With no finishing bonus, ongoing decay and the continued undercut from the separate national hyperscaler deal, sovereignty fell this turn. A separate national hyperscaler deal continued to undercut the common screening line.

Washington stayed inward-looking, slowing joint evaluation work while easing direct pressure.

Then the Strait tightened. Extended exercises around Taiwan, rising shipping insurance, an expulsion — familiar, but harder to dismiss. As its single new measure for the turn, and its current priority, the EU launched the Strait Supply Continuity Buffer: Rotterdam, Hamburg and Piraeus were ordered into rerouting table-tops, customs fast-lanes were mandated through existing shield machinery, and a 90-day buffer of chips and spares was started, funded by reallocation from existing shield and procurement lines with staffing drawn from the current portfolio. Work under the pre-existing Middle-Power Trusted Compute Compact continued with ongoing staffing and coordination costs, alongside the Gigafactories build and Black-Box Assurance Cell operations, all of which continued to draw on political capital this turn, leaving political capital strained lower. The buffer will not protect this winter; stockpiles take time to fill and customs changes require a full turn to take effect. Public mood lifted slightly on the proof success, tempered by supply anxiety.
