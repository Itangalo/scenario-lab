US-China war erupted in February, hitting Europe sideways via cable cuts, jamming, Dresden supplier explosion, airspace and power/data rerouting. Brussels declared non-belligerency, refused basing/targeting use of fabs and infrastructure, dispersed registries and cyber reserves; hospitals and registries held in degraded mode, triage assistants ran locally, but machine halls darkened, hiring collapsed, warehouses automated faster. Spring arrival of foreign tailored cures brought limited treatments in Denmark, France, Portugal under EU hosting/verification rules, but reagents, sequencing and cloud throttling left long waiting lists and resentment. By June fear replaced blame, shelters and bio-detection prioritized. July-December registries restored via cross-border reserve teams by August; triage pilots cut backlogs, Transition Shield top-ups paid late and small. September genome-model biosecurity debate prompted pooled reagents push; EU long-life alloys a bright spot amid empty Paris/Warsaw halls and lag behind US self-tooling agents. By December services visibly improved, blame softened, but hiring frozen, Chinese sorting humanoids spread, bio-detection still seed contracts.
