### Triage Under Two Shocks
January began with the unfinished cable repair still forcing clinics and sequencing hubs onto satellite backup, and Brussels trying to finish what it had started: the bio-detection and trusted-care network rolled out to its last pilot regions, hardened images from the vendor consortium pushed wider, shelters kept on rota.

Then two shocks landed at once.

First, a contested paper claimed a genome model had produced a viable design for a human-infective organism, or that a non-expert could get there with help. Methodologists quarrelled, editors were accused of both hype and handing out a recipe, but health ministries did not wait for the argument to settle. Sequencing hubs tightened screening, pooled reagents were held for verified orders, local-server triage drills ran explicitly for a world where links stay degraded and oversight models stay unreadable.

Second, commercial humanoids moved from pilot to product. Chinese-built machines, running American control stacks, appeared in logistics yards, municipal depots and, within weeks, in military logistics trials that did not stay logistical. For Europe it cut directly into the industrial base it still claims — machine tools, automotive suppliers, warehouse operations — with dexterity improving faster than any retraining plan.

Brussels answered narrowly. A small emergency shield for synthesis screening and bio-detection was launched through HERA and ENISA, reprogramming health and digital funds, no new law. The shelter operation and the patching deployment ground on. On robots, only mapping: market-surveillance teams tasked to trace where machines and models enter, Transition Shield placements stretched to absorb displaced ops staff.

It partly held. Care did not stop, detection nodes stayed up, patching blunted probing on hardened hospitals. But entry hiring collapsed further, restoration-assistant pay slipped late again, and evening news paired nurses keeping wards running offline with warehouses where new machines carried and dug beside men told to retrain with no interval left. Trust thinned further.
