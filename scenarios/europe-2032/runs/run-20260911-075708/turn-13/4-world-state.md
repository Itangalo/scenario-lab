### Holding The Line Offline
The autumn began with wards and sequencing hubs still running on satellite backup and local servers. The cable stayed dark, but the two operations Brussels had ground on for two years finally closed: shelter rotas formalised with municipalities, and the hardened clinical images pushed to the last hospitals and grid operators on the reserve list.

It mattered. When probing swells hit unpatched clinics in November, hardened sites degraded rather than stopped. Nurses triaged offline, registries restored from dispersed copies, power rerouted without blackout. Evening news carried that as competence.

The rest of the semester pulled the other way.

Graduate hiring did not come back. Law, accountancy, software, customer operations — entry posts simply were not reposted. Universities published placement figures that placement officers disowned, ministries argued about measurement, families did not argue: the absence was visible. At the same time Chinese-built humanoids running American stacks moved from logistics yards into supplier plants. Machine-tool makers and auto subcontractors who had survived the power rerouting now faced machines that carried, dug, and within months appeared in armed trials abroad. Retraining vouchers arrived after the jobs they were meant to bridge.

Brussels deliberately opened no second front there — only market-surveillance mapping of where machines and control models enter, and late-paid Transition Shield placements.

The one easing window came from the war itself. Washington and Beijing agreed to let medical reagents, sequencing kits and clinical cloud transit to neutral Europe outside the throttling regime. The Commission moved to land it through joint procurement: a verified-orders channel, stockpiles at sequencing hubs, failover to EU-hosted servers. Synthesis screening under the bio-shield held pooled reagents for verified orders only, drills ran for a degraded-link winter.

Supplies improved, tailored therapies kept running, detection nodes stayed up. But pay for restoration assistants slipped late again, warehouses paired new machines beside men told to retrain with no interval, and trust thinned to open resentment among the young.
