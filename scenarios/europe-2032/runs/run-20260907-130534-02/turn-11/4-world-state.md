The European Union launches the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme, aiming to anchor AI sovereignty in physical infrastructure. Five clusters are slated for deployment across the continent, leveraging RISC-V chips and shielded under a new “AI Critical Infrastructure” status. The initiative is framed as existential: without independent compute, the EU’s regulatory authority is hollow. Yet the political moment is fragile.

The proposal triggers immediate resistance. Germany, despite offers of co-stewardship, raises legal concerns over state aid and market fragmentation. Cloud-reliant member states, including Ireland and the Netherlands, warn of duplication and inefficiency. The Competitiveness Council delays the vote, demanding further impact assessments. No physical construction begins this turn.
