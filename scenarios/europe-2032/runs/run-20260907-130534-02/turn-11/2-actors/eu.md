## In practice  
We are no longer building authority to act — we are rebuilding the ground from which action is possible. The Sovereign AI Core proved we can detect and interrupt threats, but its reach depends on access, and access depends on compute we do not control. This turn, we launch the EU Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme: a binding directive under Article 114 TFEU, co-implemented by the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking and national digital ministries, to deploy five new sovereign AI clusters — in Brittany, northern Sweden, eastern Germany, southern Spain, and the Baltic — within 18 months. These will be powered by next-generation RISC-V-based accelerators, co-developed with European semiconductor firms under the Chips Act, and legally insulated from foreign extraterritorial claims through a new class of “AI Critical Infrastructure” designation.

Financing comes from a reallocation of the Horizon Europe emergency safety window and a 10-billion-euro draw on the SURE mechanism, re-purposed for strategic technological resilience. The Council’s Competitiveness formation will vote under urgency, but resistance is expected from member states reliant on foreign cloud providers and from industry lobbies fearing fragmentation. We are offering Germany co-stewardship of the eastern cluster and full interoperability with its national AI backbone; Poland and the Baltics are being offered regional leadership and guaranteed redundancy for EU crisis coordination systems. This is not a market play — it is a floor beneath our autonomy. Without it, every audit, every intervention, every red line we draw, rests on permission we may not have next year. We are building the substrate of agency, not because it is fast, but because nothing else will last.
