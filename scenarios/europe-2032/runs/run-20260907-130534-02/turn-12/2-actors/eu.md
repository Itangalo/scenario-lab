## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: one to build the foundation of independent power, the other to stop the ground from giving way beneath us. The Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme remains our long-term anchor — but it will not be online before turn 16, and we may not survive until then. This turn, we are therefore launching the Emergency Resilience Hardening Directive under Article 13a of the NIS2 Directive, co-implemented by ENISA, the European Health Emergency Preparedness and Response Authority (HERA), and the European Union Agency for Railways and other sectoral bodies as applicable. This will impose binding, AI-specific security baselines on all operators of essential services: real-time anomaly detection with AI-augmented SOCs, mandatory air-gapped backups for control systems, and red-teaming with sovereign red agents trained to simulate autonomous adversaries. Funding of 12 billion euros comes from an emergency reallocation of the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with 30% reserved for cross-border mutual aid capacity — including mobile cyber-response teams and shared bio-detection grids.

Resistance is expected from member states with underfunded infrastructures and from private operators facing steep compliance costs. We are offering full co-financing for the first 18 months and fast-track access to sovereign compute for resilience-critical AI workloads. To Germany, we propose co-leadership of the central European node; to Ireland and the Netherlands, we offer priority integration with their cloud ecosystems under strict data-locality rules. The bio_uplift_findings cannot be ignored — if a genome model can produce a viable human-infective design, even in contested form, then our biological defences must be as automated and as fast as the threat. This is not precaution — it is survival. We act because the frontier no longer waits, and neither can we.
