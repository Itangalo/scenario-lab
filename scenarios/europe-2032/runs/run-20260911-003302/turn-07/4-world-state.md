### Cures on someone else's servers
The second half of 2029 was dominated by two American shocks. First came news from Washington that frontier labs were being brought under direct federal control — security officers inside training runs, export of weights treated as defence matters, foreign customers vetted. Then came the clinical news: tailored therapies, built on those same frontier models, putting previously untreatable conditions into remission in early US hospital use.

In Brussels the two stories fused into one political fact. The cure worked, but Europe could not run it on its own terms.

The Commission's answer was a state-to-state pact: assured EU-hosted inference for clinical models, EU evaluators with clearance into American oversight, joint incident reporting, offered in exchange for sequencing data, pharma procurement and lithography cooperation. Council gave the mandate quickly; Washington, now under an alliance-minded administration, kept the door open and talks began. Nothing was signed, no code or weights moved, and American lawyers made clear that national security would decide what assurance meant.

At home, two older promises landed. The two grid-prioritised gigafactory sites moved from permits to poured concrete and grid connections, celebrated as proof the Union could still build. Mayors closed the municipal pact with final wage-bridge payments and a continued insurer backstop, and triage-assistant waiting-list gains were advertised heavily.

The robotics containment effort, named as the visible priority, ground forward. Fleet registries in Rotterdam and Hamburg were completed, kill-switch audits started, manual-fallback drills rehearsed. Operators filed paperwork; Chinese vendors continued to withhold firmware, and port unions called night-time autonomous handling unsafe after a near-miss between a humanoid crew and dockworkers slowed operations and prompted short wildcat stops.

Small-town mood lifted slightly where new therapies reached trial wards and where payouts arrived, but coverage still contrasted tireless port machines with exhausted municipal staff. Grids and large hospitals held; assurance thinned as the frontier jumped again.
