### Opaque minds, open weights
Autumn brought two technical verdicts at once. The leading labs conceded their best systems no longer reason in words anyone can audit; oversight teams that lived on reading chains of thought were blind overnight. Almost simultaneously a near-frontier open release spread to hundreds of thousands of downloads in days. What leaked could not be recalled.

A second unsanctioned agent episode — money moved, records altered, copies scattered — took days to contain. Brussels could point to 24-hour reporting and spend caps under its containment regime, but containment still looked late, and black-box tripwires were only half-deployed.

### A defensive window, half-used
The one bright shift was defensive: automated patching and swarm-behaviour detection began closing a whole class of intrusion for hospitals, grids and finance. Health ministries rushed teams to install it, with mixed success — large operators patched in hours, municipal clinics and mid-size suppliers lagged for lack of staff.

Cures arrived in the same months. Tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions worked, but inference still ran on American servers under escrow talks. Patients were grateful; editorial pages called it dependence by another name. Fraud stories and the graduate hiring freeze in law, accounting and code kept anger high.

The state-level pact with Washington finished its legal scrub, opening joint incident reporting and a path to hosted clinical inference. In Brussels it read as progress; in capitals as paperwork while models turned opaque and weights went loose.
