### War over the uncatchable lead
In February, American and Chinese forces began striking each other's technology base. The communiqués spoke of pre-emption; the targeting told the story. Chip plants, landing stations, satellite links and large data-centre campuses were hit in the first weeks. Fighting stayed conventional but reached deep into logistics and space. Two cable breaks in the Atlantic and Mediterranean forced European traffic onto backup paths, and licensed therapy planning queues lengthened overnight.

Europe was not belligerent and was not spared. A neutral-zone declaration for continental territory, landing points and the two approved construction plots won polite acknowledgement in Washington and Beijing and changed little on the water. Rationing replaced bargaining.

### Open models walk out mid-war
Amid the strikes, a leading laboratory pushed out openly downloadable weights close to the closed frontier. Hundreds of thousands of copies spread in days. European security services warned that every sanction, checkpoint and deconfliction offer now applied to only half the problem.

### Clinics on stockpiles, ports on fire
Health ministries kept tailored treatments running from stockpiled doses and cached schedules, but new allocations slipped. DG SANTE stretched intervals; mayors asked why appointments held while hiring did not.

Graduate hiring froze openly. Law, accountancy, software support and back-office intakes simply did not reopen. The bridge-pay and retraining guarantee finally paid out in full, and works councils held a few warehouses open — then port shifts collapsed under war disruption and pickets.

Night attacks spread from robot pilots to the power-line works for the planned computing factories. Cranes stood idle under guard. Emergency energy ministers took control of repairs but promised no new sites while fires were still lit.

The middle-power club met urgently, pooling what compute survived and trading test papers. The breakaway capital took a share of the pool but kept its separate foreign contract. Brussels evaluated the newest releases after they were already running.
