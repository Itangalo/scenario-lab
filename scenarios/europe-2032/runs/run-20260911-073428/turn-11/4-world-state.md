### Ration books and remission letters
The second half of 2031 was defined by two shortages moving in opposite directions: money for machines, and medicine for people.

Investment flight hit first. After three years of frantic build-out, funds walked away from new data-centre and accelerator bets in the autumn. Valuations halved in weeks. Two expansion projects the European computing pool had been counting on — Gulf-backed capacity and a transatlantic spare-compute swap — evaporated. Frontier labs on both sides of the Pacific quietly cut training plans. In Brussels the mood was grim relief: the race was slowing, but Europe's rented share of it was shrinking faster.

Then the clinics broke the gloom. Tailored immune therapies, planned from cached schedules and finally manufactured in quantity in Switzerland and northern Italy, began reaching ordinary hospitals. Remission letters went out in Lyon, Krakow and Porto for conditions that had been death sentences a year before. DG SANTE stretched stockpiles carefully, and mayors who had been shouting about jobs found themselves cutting ribbons in oncology wards.

The contrast did not save the ports. Graduate intakes stayed shut. Dock pickets hardened as shippers tried to run skeleton crews through backup cable routes and damaged rail. Night attacks on power-line works continued; the computing factory plots stayed fenced and floodlit, with no new concrete poured.

Brussels spent what little authority it had left on accession. The Council voted to join the joint cyber command telemetry pool and the binding sample-sharing pact, putting ENISA, CERT-EU and disease control experts forward as operators. Cable-landing data and clinical samples went in; real-time attribution feeds and pooled spares came back. Health and telecom ministers ran failover drills on backup paths, paid for from old shield budgets.

It helped, narrowly. A coordinated intrusion attempt against three grid operators in November was seen and shared in hours rather than weeks. Essential services degraded rather than stopped. But no one mistook shared warnings for owned capacity, and the middle-power pool that carried the deal could promise coordination, not kilowatts.

By December, treatments were working, lights were mostly on, and hiring was still frozen. Gratitude and resentment sat side by side.
