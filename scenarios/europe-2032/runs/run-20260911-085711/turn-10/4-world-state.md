### Containment weeks
January brought what hospitals feared: a deliberate release of a modified pathogen, designed with help from openly available models. Cases spread across two border regions before tracing caught up. Containment ran for weeks — isolation wards, triage tents, countermeasure distribution run through the EU health security apparatus and the disease control centre.

The existing cross-border medical surge, built for backlogs and paper wards, was repurposed overnight to move infectious casualties. No new measure was tabled this turn and the current priority remained suspended with capital below 20. Overtime, double-check teams and transferred staff held lines open, but exhausted wards now carried infection risk on top of chart errors. Backups restored in the autumn kept power and records from cascading, yet did not stop spread. The operation held — it did not expand capacity or build new infrastructure.

Brussels acted only where it could. No new AI law was tabled; ministers conceded frontier oversight was out of reach this turn. Money stayed frozen on factories while shipments remained quarantined and the backup site fenced. Patch teams stayed on municipal systems but were told not to pull doctors off care. Implementation of any broader bio-surge upgrade is delayed until staffing and funding allow.

### Blind models
At the same time the leading labs announced their newest systems no longer reason in readable words. The internal steps that auditors and safety teams had relied on to inspect were gone, presented as a performance gain. Black-box tests and slow activation probes were all that remained.

For Europe the timing was brutal. Tracing and triage guidance leaned on models no one could fully inspect, while the weights that helped design the agent remained permanently downloadable. Safety practice eroded further, trust collapsed further. Outage and casualty coverage dominated news; tours of working clinics barely registered.

By June transmission was slowing, stocks partly replenished, wards still open but poorer. The Union had shown it could keep hospitals running through a live biological emergency, and was credited for holding the line, but the public mood turned darker: the harm had arrived, the tools to prevent the next one looked blinder than before.
