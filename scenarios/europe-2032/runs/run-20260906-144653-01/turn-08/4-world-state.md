### Capability Creeps into the Terminal Zone

AI capability advances to 62.0, edging into the 62–68 terminal zone, as frontier labs extend the autonomy of closed models in chip design and materials optimisation. These systems now manage multi-day workflows with minimal human oversight, though full independence remains out of reach. Open-weight capability remains stagnant at 45.0, constrained by legal and technical barriers, despite underground efforts to repurpose agent architectures.

### Evaluation Shield Holds — For Now

The EU finalises a Council resolution reaffirming the Evaluation Shield’s authority under Article 16 TEU, narrowly securing qualified majority support by linking it to Schengen intelligence-sharing upgrades. This political reinforcement strengthens the mandate ahead of the pending ECJ ruling. Pilot audits with two frontier developers are now live at Ispra, granting limited access to agent telemetry in exchange for liability protections and preferential compute access. However, full transparency remains elusive, and the shadow evaluator network continues publishing unsanctioned safety assessments, gaining traction in technical circles.

### Cyber Shield Gains Ground

ENISA releases standardised red-team scenarios, and a live cross-border exercise involving German, Polish, and French CERTs tests response to AI-driven attacks on energy and health systems. Early adopters like E.ON and Charité gain fast-track access to InvestAI co-funding. Bilateral cyber packages ease resistance in Hungary and Slovakia, though concerns over national sovereignty persist.

### Political Capital Erodes

Despite progress, political capital declines. The ongoing labour unrest among mid-level professionals — protesting AI-driven de-skilling — dampens public sentiment, now at 31.0. The priority status of the Evaluation Shield and continued measure costs drain resources, with no offsetting gains from completed initiatives. Resilience improves slightly due to Cyber Shield momentum.
