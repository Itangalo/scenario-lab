Simultaneously, we secure agreement from the two participating labs to extend telemetry access for six months post-ruling, conditional on continued preferential compute allocation and liability shielding, effectively creating a de facto continuity mechanism regardless of the court’s outcome.

Their integration into the fast-track co-funding stream triggers disbursement of €1.2bn from the InvestAI Resilience Instrument, tied to multi-year maintenance of red-team readiness and information sharing through a new ENISA-operated fusion cell. Hungary and Slovakia remain partially outside the framework, but bilateral packages — including direct funding for national CERT upgrades and access to EU-wide threat intelligence feeds — prevent outright non-compliance. Despite these gains, no new measures are launched: with political capital at 20.0 and public sentiment hovering near revolt, we consolidate rather than expand, preserving what has been built while awaiting the ECJ’s verdict and clearer signals on the alleged capability plateau.
