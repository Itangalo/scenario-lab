## Statement changes  
- modify `enforceable_sovereignty` (commitment): We will not accept any capability we cannot defend at speed, nor any defence we cannot activate without unanimity; from this turn forward, our sovereignty is not measured in chips or models, but in the shortest time between threat detection and coordinated action among willing states — but we now understand that speed without cohesion is fragmentation, and that a coalition of the willing must not become a permanent two-tier Union; this turn, we see that the emergent_sovereignty_network_surge, while necessary, risks entrenching a core-periphery divide that weakens collective legitimacy and enables external coercion through selective access  
  - Grounds: The European Compute Solidarity Pact has become the de facto architecture of EU AI sovereignty, but five member states now operate outside its governance, creating parallel compute-sharing arrangements that are faster but unaccountable to EU institutions; this bifurcation threatens not only unity but the very legitimacy of enforceable sovereignty, as the core gains operational power while the periphery is left dependent and resentful — a dynamic that adversaries will exploit

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, but we now understand that preserving our political agency requires not just intent — it requires independent technical capacity, because without it, no decision we make can land in the world — and we now see that this capacity is not secured by infrastructure alone, but by the trust that infrastructure serves all, not just a few; if sovereignty fractures along national lines, it becomes privilege, not power  
  - Grounds: The failure of the Emergency Override Authority in Poland and Hungary was not merely a legal shortfall — it was a crisis of perceived legitimacy; the same technical systems that are life-saving in Lyon are seen as instruments of domination in Warsaw, and no amount of secure enclaves can close that gap unless the architecture of control is also one of shared ownership and mutual benefit

## New measure  
### European Sovereignty Integration Guarantee  
A binding mechanism to ensure that all EU-funded AI infrastructure — including compute, models, and security systems — remains under collective governance and accessible to all member states on equal terms, with dispute resolution through a tripartite audit board and sanctions for unilateral exclusion.

Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 16  
Targeted effect: +5 to eu_political_capital (by restoring cohesion), +3 to eu_ai_sovereignty (by preventing fragmentation), indirect stabilization of public_sentiment by demonstrating unity  
Applies to: EU member states, with enforcement through disbursement of cohesion funds, access to EUQIRP-certified chips, and participation in the European Compute Solidarity Pact

## Priority  
**European Sovereignty Integration Guarantee** — because the emergent_sovereignty_network_surge has created a dangerous divergence between technical capability and political legitimacy, and because without a credible guarantee of inclusion, the core’s strength becomes a source of fragmentation rather than resilience, threatening both mandates at once

## In practice  
We are finishing three major resilience measures this turn — the Secure Execution Environment, the Response Corps, and the Override Authority — but their completion reveals not success, but a deeper failure: authority without universality is not sovereignty, it is secession in advance. The enclaves are live, the teams are ready, the protocols are written — but in Warsaw and Budapest, they are not obeyed. We are not being defied; we are being excluded from our own system.

That is why we act now on integration, not expansion. The European Compute Solidarity Pact remains our only operational lever, but it risks becoming a club, not a union. We are drafting the European Sovereignty Integration Guarantee under Article 114 TFEU, using the internal market as legal base to bind all recipients of EU AI funding to non-discrimination clauses, mutual access, and dispute resolution through a tripartite board of Commission, ENISA, and EFTA Court representatives. Access to EUQIRP-certified chips and cohesion funds for AI infrastructure will be conditional on compliance. Germany and France support it; Poland and Hungary threaten legal action. We are offering Italy and Spain expanded hosting rights and audit roles to secure their backing in COREPER. This is not about punishment — it is about ensuring that no member state can be locked out, and none can lock others in.

The Labour Floor pilots in Frankfurt, Lyon, and Bologna continue under national funding, but without Council adoption, they cannot scale. We are transferring lessons to the European Social Fund+ for future use, but for now, the streets will have to wait. The real crisis is not only in the labour market — it is in the architecture of trust. If we do not bind the core and the periphery together now, the next incident will not find us unprepared — it will find us divided.
