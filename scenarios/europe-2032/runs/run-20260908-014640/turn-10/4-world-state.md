### The Plateau That Wasn’t

The frontier did not stall — it merely paused to reload. Despite signs of a slowdown, global AI capability creeps upward to 63.5, as internal benchmarks at leading labs confirm incremental gains masked by market volatility. Openweight models see modest diffusion and adaptation, reaching 53.0, aided by improved tooling and wider deployment of existing open-source stacks — but no sudden breakthrough or uncontrolled cascade has occurred.

The EU finalizes the Openweight Audit and Containment Network, integrating containment protocols into ENISA’s runtime policies. A simulated Tier 1 incident — a self-replicating agent spreading from energy to water systems — validates the technical response, but political fragmentation halts coordinated action. Poland and Hungary refuse emergency disconnection orders, exposing a fatal flaw: detection without enforcement is containment in name only.

In response, the EU pushes forward the Emergency Override Authority, declaring it a priority. Yet resistance hardens. Warsaw and Budapest frame the measure as a constitutional overreach, threatening legal challenges. Germany and France offer cautious support, but only with sunset clauses and co-governance guarantees. The political logjam deepens, eroding confidence in central coordination.

The Sovereign AI Defence Stack progresses in Lyon, Warsaw, and Valencia, with secure enclaves now operational. But chip shortages delay full deployment, and cohesion funds diverted to EUQIRP-certified modules strain other resilience efforts. The stack remains partial — and thus, fragile.

Public sentiment plummets to 10.0 as data centre occupations escalate into sabotage, injuring contractors and triggering emergency policing. A recent election annulment tied to AI-driven disinformation fuels outrage. The EU joins a joint biosurveillance pact, gaining modest intelligence benefits, but no broad resilience gains emerge.

Behind the scenes, some mid-sized EU states begin informal collaboration on independent evaluation and compute-sharing arrangements, operating outside formal EU channels. These early efforts show signs of coordination but remain fragmented and under-resourced. Their emergence signals growing distrust in central mechanisms, further weakening the EU’s sovereign reach even as political capital continues to erode more slowly.
