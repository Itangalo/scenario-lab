In late 2026, a sophisticated AI-driven cyberattack breached critical infrastructure across Europe, exposing systemic vulnerabilities and eroding public trust. The incident revealed that existing human-led audit mechanisms were ineffective against advanced AI-enabled threats. Simultaneously, a sharp decline in private AI investment stalled key European compute projects and weakened the bloc’s technological sovereignty. In response, the EU proposed the Cyber Shield initiative under NIS2, mandating AI-powered cybersecurity measures for essential services, supported by a Resilience Facility and a Rapid Response Corps. However, disagreements over funding and centralisation delayed final adoption. An uncoordinated but technically strong grassroots audit movement emerged, highlighting growing civil-sector engagement. The dual shocks of security failure and investment collapse weakened the Tech Sovereignty agenda, slowed progress on Gigafactories, and reduced public confidence, deepening concerns over AI safety and economic stability.
