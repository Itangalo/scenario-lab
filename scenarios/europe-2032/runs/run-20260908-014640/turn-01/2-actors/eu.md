## New measure
Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure  
A binding directive requiring real-time intrusion detection, automatic failover, and AI-assisted red-teaming for all operators of essential services in energy, water, and transport.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)

## Priority
Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure — because the cyber_test_shot proves our defences are blind to advanced AI-driven intrusions, and without resilience, sovereignty collapses no matter how much compute we build.

## In practice
We are acting on the cyber_test_shot by launching a binding directive under the NIS2 legal base, to be negotiated in the Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formation. The directive will mandate AI-powered intrusion detection systems with autonomous red-teaming capabilities for all designated operators of essential services, starting with electricity transmission, water utilities, and major port authorities. ENISA will be tasked with drafting technical standards by Q1 2027, and we will propose a new €12bn Resilience Facility under the Multiannual Financial Framework to co-fund implementation, especially in member states with weaker cyber capacity.

The cyber incident revealed that detection based on known signatures and human-led audits is obsolete. We are therefore aligning with Germany and the Nordic states to push for real-time behavioural monitoring using AI agents trained to simulate adversarial tactics — but under strict EU oversight to prevent mission creep. Resistance is expected from some southern and eastern member states over cost and sovereignty concerns, so we are pairing the directive with a compensation mechanism: access to pooled EU cyber talent via a new Rapid Response Corps, staffed by seconded experts from national CERTs and Europol’s EC3, and funded through the same facility.

This measure does not replace our industrial push — the Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain central — but it reframes them: without hardened infrastructure, any domestic AI capacity we build will be vulnerable the moment it goes live. We are treating resilience not as a follow-on, but as the foundational layer of sovereignty in an AI-contested world.
