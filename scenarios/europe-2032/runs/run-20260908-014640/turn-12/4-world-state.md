### The Weight of Completion

The EU formally completes three major resilience measures: the Secure Execution Environment, the Joint AI Incident Response Corps, and the Emergency Override Authority. Technically, they are operational. But in Warsaw and Budapest, override protocols are blocked by local gatekeepers. The Response Corps, though certified, cannot cross borders without host-state consent. The Authority exists in law but not in effect — a directive without enforcement. The gap between design and obedience widens.

### A Labour Crisis Ignites

Eurostat confirms a sharp rise in long-term youth unemployment, concentrated in cities where AI has reshaped legal, administrative, and financial work. University career offices report near-zero graduate hiring in affected sectors. Protests erupt in Paris, Milan, and Frankfurt. The European Parliament convenes an emergency session, demanding immediate action. The Commission proposes the Emergency Labour Transition Floor — a large category 7 measure, automatically triggered by regional thresholds, funded by a 0.5% levy on AI-driven productivity gains in finance.

Germany agrees to a pilot in Frankfurt, France in Lyon, Italy in Bologna. But fiscal hawks resist the levy, and the European Compute Solidarity Pact — already strained by Poland’s absence — now faces demands for broader social guarantees before expansion. Public sentiment, rather than rising, drops further — the perception is not of decisive action, but of delay and fragmentation.

### The Sovereignty Gap Deepens

Despite finishing three measures, resilience gains only +3: partial deployment limits systemic impact. The category 6 finishes yield +3 to resilience. But with `ai_capability` rising by 0.5, and no safety measure landing, `ai_safety` drops by −5. `openweight_capability` remains flat, trailing the frontier. The EU’s compute pact advances, but no sovereignty gain is realized this turn — the mechanism is still in negotiation, not execution.

Political capital barely shifts: +5 from completed measures, but −9 ongoing costs (three large measures still charging, plus new large measure and priority), and no proposal bonus — the Labour Floor responds to a crisis now evident, but not one that occurred in the prior three turns. Sentiment falls further due to the labour crisis, outweighing legitimacy lending.
