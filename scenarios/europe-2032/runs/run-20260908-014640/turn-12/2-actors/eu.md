## Statement changes  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now assume that any system we do not operate and physically control ourselves may be weaponized against us, whether through denial, sabotage, or uncontrolled diffusion, and that even sovereign systems are only as secure as their weakest update, dependency, or monitoring node — including the political dependencies that allow a single member state to block emergency disconnection; this turn, we see that authority without enforcement is ritual, not power, and that our commitments must bind not only our intentions but the mechanisms that make them real  
- modify `sovereign_defence_in_depth` (commitment): We will ensure that every sovereign AI system running critical public functions is built, monitored, and updatable within the EU, with redundant oversight, real-time anomaly detection, and fail-safes that cannot be disabled remotely — but we now understand that such systems are only sovereign if the authority to defend them cannot be vetoed by a single member state in crisis, and that technical integrity must be matched by enforceable political architecture  
- add `enforceable_sovereignty` (commitment): We will not accept any capability we cannot defend at speed, nor any defence we cannot activate without unanimity; from this turn forward, our sovereignty is not measured in chips or models, but in the shortest time between threat detection and coordinated action among willing states  
  - Grounds: The Emergency Override Authority’s failure, despite two years of preparation, proves that technical sovereignty is meaningless without political enforceability; the emergent_sovereignty_network_surge is not a failure of vision but of execution speed, and we must now bind ourselves to architectures that work *when* the system breaks, not only when it holds

## In practice  
We are closing out three major measures this turn: the Sovereign AI Defence Stack, the Joint AI Incident Response Corps, and the Emergency Override Authority. The enclaves in Lyon, Warsaw, and Valencia are live, monitored, and technically capable of autonomous shutdown — but in Warsaw, Polish officials have installed administrative gatekeepers between detection and action, rendering the fuse ceremonial. The Response Corps is certified and ready, but its mandate evaporates at borders where override is not recognized. The Authority itself is law — but unenforceable. We are not lacking tools; we are lacking obedience.

That is why we pivot — not from sovereignty, but toward survival. The European Compute Solidarity Pact moves forward: we are convening the Maastricht summit under Article 122 TFEU, with Germany, France, Italy, the Netherlands, and Spain already signaling attendance. We are offering access to EUQIRP-certified chips and cohesion funds in exchange for binding commitments to shared override protocols, with a tripartite audit board to preserve legitimacy. This is not EU-wide, but it is *something* — a core that can expand or collapse, depending on what happens next.

And what happens next depends on the streets. The Eurostat report on youth unemployment is a five-alarm fire. We are drafting the Emergency Labour Transition Floor under the European Social Fund+ and the new AI Adjustment Instrument, with automatic triggers at the regional level. We will fund it through a temporary solidarity levy on AI-driven productivity gains in the financial sector — 0.5% on net efficiency gains above 15% year-on-year. The European Parliament’s left and centre are demanding it; the Commission’s fiscal hawks are resisting. We are offering Germany a pilot in Frankfurt, France in Lyon, and Italy in Bologna — visible, fast, and tied to retraining in public AI stewardship and cyber resilience. If we do not act now, the next attack will not come from a model — it will come from the people.
