**Resilience Facility & Rapid Response Corps**  
A dedicated €12bn EU facility to co-finance AI-driven cyber defences in member states, paired with a standing corps of 500 seconded cyber experts for rapid deployment during incidents.

## Priority  
**Resilience Facility & Rapid Response Corps** — because the emergent_regional_aid_surge shows member states are already moving, and without a unified, funded EU mechanism, fragmentation will deepen, undermining both resilience and cohesion.

## In practice  
We are converting the political momentum from the emergent_regional_aid_surge into a binding EU-wide instrument: the Resilience Facility, to be adopted under the Internal Security Fund’s legal base with a new regulation tabled in Q1 2027. The Facility will co-finance up to 70% of AI-powered detection systems for operators of essential services in member states scoring below 50 on the EU Cybersecurity Maturity Index, with priority to those that experienced the cyber_test_shot breach. ENISA will administer the fund and verify technical compliance with the Cyber Shield directive’s standards, ensuring interoperability and preventing a patchwork of incompatible systems.

Simultaneously, we are standing up the Rapid Response Corps under Article 8 of the Schengen Borders Code, leveraging existing secondment mechanisms from Europol and national CERTs. The Corps will begin with 500 full-time equivalents drawn from member states on a rotating basis, trained in AI-enabled threat hunting and autonomous red-teaming. Germany, France, and the Netherlands have pledged initial personnel; we are offering southern and eastern states guaranteed access to the Corps in exchange for accepting the Facility’s conditions. This is not just about capability — it’s about solidarity: no member state should face an AI-driven attack alone.

The Cyber Shield directive remains on track for adoption in turn 4, but without the Facility and Corps, it would be a mandate without means. Together, they close the loop: the directive sets the standard, the Facility funds it, and the Corps ensures it works in practice. We are also monitoring the eval_anomaly_reports and openweight_frontier_release with growing alarm — if capability is advancing faster than we can verify, our sovereignty is not just technological, but epistemic. But we cannot act on that yet — not without breaking cohesion or bankrupting capital. For now, resilience is the only floor we can still lay before the storm hits.
