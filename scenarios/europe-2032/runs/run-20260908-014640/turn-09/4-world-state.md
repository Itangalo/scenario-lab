### A Capability Plateau, and a Political Cliff

Signs emerge that the breakneck advance of AI may be slowing. A major release from a leading lab fails to meet internal benchmarks, and the market price for top-tier capability drops — a signal, analysts whisper, that the frontier is no longer accelerating. Yet even a plateau at 63.0 is high enough: openweight models now reach 52.0, enabling persistent, automated cyber intrusions and raising the stakes for containment.

The EU finalizes its Openweight Audit and Containment Network. Austrian and Finnish teams publish protocols to block self-replicating agents, and ENISA prepares a live drill. But detection is not defence. When a simulated grid intrusion spreads across three nations in a test, response falters — not from technical failure, but because Poland and Hungary refuse to grant emergency override authority. The Joint AI Incident Response Corps exists in name only.

In response, the EU proposes the Emergency Override Authority, a bold directive to bypass national objections during Tier 1 incidents. Backed by Article 173 TFEU and NIS2, it would give ENISA-Europol the power to isolate compromised systems unilaterally. Germany and France signal cautious support, but Warsaw and Budapest dig in, calling it a constitutional overreach. Talks stall.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign AI Defence Stack advances slowly. Secure enclaves in Lyon, Warsaw, and Valencia near completion, but chip shortages delay full deployment. Cohesion funds are redirected, but not enough to close the gap.

Public sentiment remains frozen at 15.0. Data centre occupations continue, and a high-profile election annulment in a member state — officially tied to AI-driven disinformation — deepens distrust. The EU joins a new joint threat response pact on biosurveillance, gaining intelligence access and modest resilience gains.

Yet sovereignty slips to 15.0, weighed down by capability growth and the unresolved fragmentation crisis. Political capital falls to 6.0, crushed by a portfolio of four large measures and a priority, with no offsetting gains. The EU acts — but not yet as one.
