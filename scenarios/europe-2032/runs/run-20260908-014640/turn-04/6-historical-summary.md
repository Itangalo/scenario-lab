AI capability advances as open-weight models achieve near-parity with frontier systems, enabling autonomous cyber-planning on consumer hardware. The Taiwan blockade triggers a global compute crisis, prompting the EU to invoke emergency powers under Article 357 TFEU and establish a Semiconductor Allocation Board, prioritizing sovereign labs and critical infrastructure amid rising private costs and public unrest. Germany and France support strict controls; Poland and Hungary abstain but secure preferential cyber defence access. The open-weight release spreads globally, evading EU audit jurisdiction and fueling misuse concerns, while red-teaming efforts lag due to jurisdictional limits. Cyber Shield and the Resilience Facility become operational, with ENISA deploying cyber experts for AI-enhanced ransomware drills, though hardware shortages threaten readiness. Public sentiment sours over job losses and perceived state overreach, undermining trust. Despite stalled control measures and legal disputes delaying full implementation, emergency actions and resilience gains provide partial legitimacy, resulting in a modest net gain in EU AI sovereignty, though political capital continues to erode amid internal and external pressures.
