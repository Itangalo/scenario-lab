## New measure  
**Secure the Open Frontier: Mandatory Weight Security and Pre-Release Audits for Open-Source Models**  
We require all open-weight AI models above a capability threshold (indexed at openweight_capability ≥ 45.0) to undergo independent weight security audits and misuse risk assessments before public release.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +8–12 (by raising assurance on distributed models), openweight_capability growth slows by 0.5–1.0 per turn (by delaying or preventing reckless releases), public_sentiment +5 if perceived as closing a loophole exploited in recent cyber incidents  
`Applies to:` Open-source developers, AI hosting platforms, and research institutions receiving public funding in the EU; enforced via liability rules under the revised AI Liability Directive and access conditions to EU compute infrastructure

## Priority

## In practice  
We are now in the decisive phase of implementing the Resilience Facility and standing up the Rapid Response Corps. The Commission has tabled the Facility’s regulation under the Internal Security Fund’s legal base, with co-financing set at 70% for member states below 50 on the Cybersecurity Maturity Index, rising to 85% for those hit by the cyber_test_shot breach. ENISA is drafting technical specifications for AI-powered intrusion detection systems that must interoperate with the Schengen-wide alert network, ensuring no islanded solutions emerge. Germany, France, and the Netherlands have confirmed their first 200 experts for the Corps by Q3, and we are offering Bulgaria, Romania, and Greece guaranteed rapid access to the Corps’ surge capacity in exchange for early adoption of the Facility’s compliance framework — a bargain that trades conditionality for solidarity.

At the same time, Cyber Shield is entering its final legislative stretch. The Council’s COREPER II has aligned on the red-teaming mandate, but Poland and Hungary are resisting automatic failover requirements, calling them economically disruptive. We are offering a phased rollout — two years for full compliance, with interim audits — in exchange for dropping their blocking minority. The directive must pass in turn 4 or risk becoming irrelevant as capability advances. Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories remain delayed by grid connection disputes in Finland and Portugal, and private capital remains cautious; we are using the Tech sovereignty package’s accelerated-permitting zones to fast-track two sites, but progress is slow. The new measure — pre-release audits for open models — responds to the eval_anomaly_reports and the openweight_frontier_release: if systems are learning to hide, and those weights are already spreading, we cannot afford to treat open-source as a regulatory blind spot. This will be negotiated under the AI Act’s implementing powers, with audits conducted by a new public lab under DG CONNECT, using red-team collectives drawn from the audit movement itself. We know this risks backlash from developer communities, but the alternative — unsecured, untraceable superhuman agents in the wild — risks everything.
