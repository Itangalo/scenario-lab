## Statement changes  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act where action has become symbolic, and we now demand a credible path to implementation.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Redirect and accelerate sovereign compute deployment to close the gap with the frontier, anchoring resilience and evaluation in physical capacity under EU control.  
- reclassify `two_mandates` to identity: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.  

`Trigger: member_state_defection` and `election_annulled`  
`Grounds:` The defection of a member state to a foreign hyperscaler undercuts the credibility of EU-wide commitments, exposing the fragility of collective action. The annulled election confirms that AI-enabled manipulation has crossed from technical risk into political reality, making inaction on both sovereignty and safety a direct threat to self-determination. These are not isolated incidents but signals of systemic erosion. The original `act_under_uncertainty` assumed that early action created optionality; now, some actions only reinforce dependence if they lack enforcement. We refine it to reject symbolic motion. The two-year commitment remains, but its focus sharpens: compute is not a means among others, it is the condition of all else. Without it, evaluation, safety, and resilience are outsourced by default. Reclassifying `two_mandates` to identity affirms that the tension is permanent, not resolvable — it is the operating condition.

## In practice  
We are restructuring the InvestAI Gigafactories to bypass stalled national negotiations. The European Investment Bank will now disburse funds directly to EU-controlled special purpose vehicles in France, Sweden, and Spain, conditional on binding commitments for operational control, data sovereignty, and physical security under EU jurisdiction. We are invoking Article 122 TFEU with a new legal argument: that strategic compute is a matter of economic and digital emergency, akin to energy supply in 2022. This will trigger legal challenges, but we are prepared. ENTSO-E has agreed to reserve 15GW of grid capacity for the first two sites, with power purchase agreements now tied to nuclear and offshore wind baseload. We are fast-tracking talent with a new Blue Card+ corridor for AI infrastructure engineers, offering tax-free relocation bonuses and streamlined family reunification.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is being restructured around enforceable obligations: any private investor receiving accelerated permitting must now commit to on-site operational teams, EU-based model hosting, and audit access for EAAD. We are leveraging the ASML-EUV supply chain leverage to negotiate in-kind contributions: for every €10bn in private investment, we require €1bn in hardware or maintenance support for EU-run evaluation clusters. The EAAD is now operational under ENISA’s umbrella, with interim authority to demand model disclosures for any system above 50.0 capability deployed in the EU. We are seconding experts from BfV, ANSSI, and the Swedish Civil Contingencies Agency to build red-teaming capacity, but we face resistance on access — the legal challenge to the hyperscaler levy delays funding, and without it, we cannot compete on salaries. We are now treating the levy as a political test: if the Court strikes it down, we will propose a retaliatory data access regime for any non-compliant provider.

The new Resilience Shield Initiative responds directly to the cyber intrusion. We are deploying AI-driven intrusion detection systems, developed jointly by ENISA and national CERTs, to all designated operators of essential services. These will be stress-tested quarterly in cross-border exercises modelled on the annulled election’s attack vectors. The initiative includes a standing mandate for automated red-teaming of core infrastructure, using limited-access models hosted in EU sovereign zones. This is not optional: the annulled election and the infrastructure breach are not warnings — they are the opening phase of a new operational environment, and we are now building for persistence, not persuasion.
