`Trigger: ai_investment_collapse`  
`Grounds:` The sudden evaporation of private AI investment resets the global landscape: the frontier labs can no longer afford the largest training runs, and the race is no longer one of unchecked scaling. This creates a narrow window in which strategic control — not raw capacity — becomes the decisive edge. Our previous two-year commitment assumed a trajectory of exponential growth we could only hope to partially match; now, the bottleneck is not money but access, security, and the ability to *act* on insight. We shift focus: instead of racing to replicate hyperscaler-scale compute, we consolidate control over the nodes that govern risk — evaluation, red-teaming, and model access. The refinement to `act_under_uncertainty` reflects this recalibration: we are still acting before the path is clear, but we are no longer pretending that building more of what we cannot secure is action at all. It is theatre.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Establish the EU Red-Team Access Framework  
Create a legally protected, sovereign-controlled mechanism for granting vetted public researchers and security agencies access to high-capability models — both open and closed — for the purpose of probing, stress-testing, and exposing emergent risks.

Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 5  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by 8–12 points by enabling discovery of unreported capabilities; strengthen resilience by revealing systemic vulnerabilities in critical infrastructure; increase eu_ai_sovereignty by creating a sovereign capability to audit systems no vendor can opt out of.  
Applies to: EU member states, EAAD, ENISA, national intelligence and cyber agencies, and vetted academic consortia under EU oversight

## Priority  
**Priority:** Establish the European AI Assurance Directorate  
This measure is now our leverage: as private investment collapses and the frontier stutters, the EAAD becomes the gatekeeper of legitimacy — the only body with legal authority to delay releases, and now the anchor of our new red-team access framework. Without it, we have no standing to demand access; with it, we can shape the global standard.

## In practice  
We are retooling the Gigafactories initiative around strategic density, not just scale. The EIB is now conditioning disbursements not only on grid and security commitments but on co-location of sovereign evaluation clusters within each site — these will host the red-team infrastructure and serve as the physical anchor for model access under the new framework. We are renegotiating with the three selected sites (France, Sweden, Spain) to reserve 20% of compute for public assurance missions, legally ring-fenced from commercial use. ENTSO-E has confirmed 15GW baseload, but we are now prioritising nuclear-backed nodes to ensure uninterrupted operation during geopolitical shocks.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is being reframed as a leverage play: with private capital fleeing, we are offering emergency liquidity through EIB guarantees in exchange for binding commitments on data sovereignty, model hosting, and audit access — but only for firms that accept EAAD oversight as a condition. We are using our position in the ASML-EUV supply chain to extract side deals: TSMC and Samsung have been told that continued access to EU markets depends on cooperation with red-team validation of AI-optimised chip design tools. This is not aid — it is renegotiation under changed conditions.

The EAAD, though still under legal challenge, is now operational in shadow mode. We have seconded 47 experts from national agencies and are standing up a secure enclave at the Joint Research Centre in Ispra. This month, we issued our first informal advisory: a capability alert on autonomous phishing agents detected in open models at 44.0, which we shared with CERTs and financial regulators. We are preparing our first formal delay request on a model above 55.0 capability — a test case we know will be challenged, but one we are ready to defend in the ECJ if needed. The Resilience Shield is deploying detection stacks to 127 essential operators, but integration is patchy; we are assigning ENISA task forces to the slowest adopters and threatening to withhold cohesion funds if compliance lags beyond Q1. This is no longer about prevention — it is about survival.
