## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that sovereignty without safety is not sovereignty at all, and resilience without access is not resilience. Our power to act depends on being trusted to act rightly, and that trust erodes when capability outpaces control.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor sovereignty and risk resilience — but pivot from infrastructure-first to assurance-first: our sovereign capacity is no longer measured in petaflops alone, but in the authority, access, and technical means to audit, delay, and red-team systems that shape our world. The Gigafactories remain essential, but their purpose is now to host sovereign evaluation, not just computation.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now act only where we can *follow through*. Symbolic authority is no authority. This turn, that means anchoring our leverage not in promises of future scale, but in the operational reality of the EAAD, the Resilience Shield, and the Red-Team Access Framework as instruments of binding oversight.

`Trigger: eu_frontier_access_denied + emergent_cyber_intrusion_aftermath`  
`Grounds:` The sudden denial of frontier access — hitting hospitals and ministries mid-operation — has exposed the fragility of dependence, even as the cyber intrusion confirms that uncontrolled capability is a weapon turned inward. These are not separate failures: they are symptoms of a single deficit — the absence of sovereign *agency* over systems that already govern critical functions. Our identity must reflect that we are not merely balancing two mandates, but discovering that they converge: we cannot determine our future unless we can secure it, and we cannot prevent harm unless we can act independently. The recalibration of our two-year commitment acknowledges that the bottleneck is no longer compute, but control — and that our leverage lies not in matching the frontier, but in shaping its conditions. The refinement of `act_under_uncertainty` responds to the fact that we are no longer acting into a vacuum: we are acting into a legal, technical, and political reality where authority must be exercised, not declared.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Enact the Cyber Resilience Activation Directive  
Mandate immediate integration of AI-driven detection and response systems across all designated essential operators, backed by binding deadlines, centralised threat intelligence from ENISA, and conditional access to EU recovery funds.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 5  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 10–15 points through enforced adoption of detection stacks, reduce incident response lag to under 48 hours, and strengthen cross-border coordination by tying cohesion funding to compliance.  
Applies to: EU member states, ENISA, national CERTs, and all operators designated under NIS2 and the AI Act’s critical infrastructure provisions.

## Priority  
**Priority:** Establish the European AI Assurance Directorate  
This measure is now the linchpin of our sovereignty: with frontier access denied and cyber threats escalating, the EAAD is the only body that can legally delay dangerous models and compel access for public scrutiny — and without it, we have no standing to enforce the Red-Team Access Framework or the Cyber Resilience Activation Directive. Its completion this turn is not administrative; it is existential.

## In practice  
We are clearing the legal path for the EAAD with emergency arbitration under Article 263 TFEU, invoking the “urgent need to protect public security” as grounds for provisional application pending final judgment. The Commission’s Legal Service has drafted the request, and we are securing qualified majority support in COREPER this week. The 47 seconded experts are already operating from Ispra under provisional mandate, and we are finalising the secure enclave for model evaluation — air-gapped, with physical access limited to cleared personnel and EU-only data routing. This month, we will issue our first formal delay request on a model rated at 58.1, citing autonomous cyber exploitation capabilities observed in sandboxed testing. We are prepared for legal challenge — and for political fallout — but we will not retreat from the authority we are mandated to exercise.

The Resilience Shield is being upgraded from voluntary deployment to enforced compliance. ENISA has identified 34 essential operators — including three national grid operators — that have failed to integrate detection stacks despite six months of technical support. We are freezing cohesion fund disbursements to their parent states until integration is verified, and we are activating the new Cyber Resilience Activation Directive to set a binding deadline of Q3 2028. The directive will also mandate quarterly joint red-teaming exercises with national CISOs, using AI-generated attack vectors from the Red-Team Access Framework. We are integrating the emergent regional cyber pact into this structure, offering EU-level coordination and intelligence sharing in exchange for formal accession — turning informal cooperation into binding solidarity.

The Gigafactories are being redefined as sovereignty nodes, not just compute hubs. We are renegotiating with the three host states to legally enshrine the 20% ring-fence for public assurance missions, and we are redirecting EIB guarantees to prioritise firms that co-locate red-team infrastructure and accept EAAD audit rights. The TSMC and Samsung talks are now tied to this condition: access to EU markets for advanced chips depends on their AI design tools being subject to sovereign red-teaming. This is not a trade barrier — it is a security requirement. Meanwhile, public sentiment remains fragile, and we are launching a targeted communication campaign through the Digital Europe Programme to explain how these measures protect, rather than threaten, citizens. We are not selling AI — we are selling safety, sovereignty, and the right to act.
