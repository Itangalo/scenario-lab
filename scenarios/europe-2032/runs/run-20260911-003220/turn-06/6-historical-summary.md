Winter shocks gave way to spring technology shocks: a US lab demonstrated long-horizon agentic AI that obsoleted timelines, while US and Chinese firms moved humanoids from pilot to catalogue, capturing logistics orders in Rotterdam, Hamburg, Lyon and sparking union fears of no retraining refuge.

In January the new US administration offered promised allied frontier access tiers, researcher access, joint pre-release evaluation and aligned export controls. Brussels negotiated to tie any deal to keeping its two advanced gigafactory sites on grid priority to avoid dependence; talks dragged over audit rights and state aid, with no researcher access by June.

Tech sovereignty permitting/capital pipeline and bio-shield sequencing/stockpile/rationing regimes were declared complete/operational, though only two gigafactory sites advanced and Taiwan allocations stayed frozen. Brussels pushed municipal pact — wage bridges, clinic staffing, backup-power deals, manual chlorination drills, EIB reinsurer backstop. Where crews arrived, boil notices ended and triage gains held; elsewhere councils blamed Brussels for isolations and insurance retreat. By June, relief at US access was tempered by dependence fears, small-town resentment by fragile repair.
