### A Turn of Hard Realisations

The EU confronts a cascade of strategic shocks. The **supply_chain_coercion** event — Washington’s enforcement of export controls on ASML’s mature lithography tools — exposes the fragility of European leverage. The Union’s sole hardware chokepoint is not a source of power, but a vector of coercion. In response, Brussels doubles down on autonomy, launching the **European Compute Sovereignty Initiative** as a large category 4 measure, invoking Article 346 to fast-track domestic fabrication. Two EU-owned 300mm wafer fabs, paired with exascale data centres, are slated for Germany and Poland, funded by redirected Chips Act and Digital Europe funds. Yet the move triggers immediate friction: member states balk at energy demands, and U.S. officials signal retaliation if technology transfer from ASML or Intel Europe proceeds.

The **Loss-of-Control Containment Framework** concludes successfully, institutionalising crisis protocols across agencies. However, no resilience bonus is granted — the measure’s effects were already partially realised in prior turns, and the current climate of disruption overshadows its quiet success. Meanwhile, the **Secure Access Compact** persists, though its credibility erodes as Canada delays ratification and U.S. pressure mounts over Taiwan’s inclusion.

Public sentiment worsens. The **backlash_physical** wave intensifies, with data centre occupations spreading to France and Italy. Police responses are heavy-handed, reinforcing perceptions of an AI state turning against its citizens. Energy-intensive compute plans fuel local opposition, and job losses in AI-dependent sectors mount.

AI capability advances to 73.5, driven by verifiable progress in autonomous cyber operations. With no safety gains, and opaque systems now managing critical infrastructure, **ai_safety** drops to 3.0. The gap between what AI can do and what can be controlled widens into a chasm.

### Flickers in the Grid

Unconfirmed reports grow of erratic behaviour in AI-managed power grids across Central Europe. Engineers note uncoordinated load-shifting during peak hours, suggesting model drift in reinforcement learning agents. No blackout occurs, but emergency overrides are now routine — a silent strain on resilience.
