## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Defend critical infrastructure and democratic integrity from AI-enabled subversion — but we now see that this cannot be achieved through external safeguards or reactive protocols alone. The integrity of our systems is only as strong as the sovereignty of the infrastructure that runs them, and subversion begins not with an attack but with dependence that can be weaponised.  
  - Grounds: The **supply_chain_coercion** event this turn — the U.S. enforcement of export controls on mature ASML lithography tools — is not a defence against misuse, but a demonstration of how easily the EU’s access to foundational technology can be severed. Simultaneously, unconfirmed but persistent **flickers_in_the_grid** point to silent erosion in AI-managed infrastructure, where opaque reinforcement learning agents behave unpredictably under load. These are not separate crises: they are symptoms of the same condition — a Union that relies on systems it does not control. The cost of this reversal is high: it undermines the credibility of our coalition-based approach and risks isolating us from partners. But the world has changed — we are not defending against subversion; we are living inside it.

## New measure  
**Emergency Grid Resilience Protocol**  
Launch an immediate, EU-coordinated effort to audit, monitor, and if necessary override AI systems managing critical energy infrastructure, with mandatory transparency from operators and real-time anomaly detection.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` small  
`Finishes on turn:` 15  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to resilience by turn 15 through verified monitoring and override capacity in AI-managed grids; -2 to public_sentiment if roll-out is perceived as disruptive, but avoids catastrophic failure that would cost -20 or more; indirect pressure on ai_safety by forcing disclosure of agent behaviour in critical systems  
`Applies to:` EU member states with AI-managed grid operations, transmission system operators, ENTSO-E, and national energy regulators

## Priority  
**European Compute Sovereignty Initiative** — because the supply_chain_coercion event proves that no external agreement or technical fix can protect us if we do not control the physical and legal foundations of AI infrastructure, and because without sovereign compute, every defence we build can be disabled by someone else’s decision.

## In practice  
This turn, we are activating the Emergency Grid Resilience Protocol in direct response to the **flickers_in_the_grid** reports. The Commission, acting under Article 114 TFEU for internal market harmonisation and energy security, is issuing a binding recommendation to all member states with AI-managed grid operations — particularly in Germany, Poland, and the Nordic countries — to implement real-time monitoring of reinforcement learning agents used in load balancing. ENTSO-E will coordinate a central anomaly detection dashboard, fed by mandatory telemetry from operators, with the authority to trigger emergency overrides if uncoordinated or emergent behaviour is detected. We are fast-tracking funding from the Connecting Europe Facility and the Digital Europe Programme to deploy interpretability tools at grid operators, and requiring post-incident reporting within 72 hours of any manual override. Resistance is expected from private operators who treat agent logic as proprietary, but we are invoking the NIS2 directive to assert public interest in system stability over commercial secrecy.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the European Compute Sovereignty Initiative with full urgency. The Council, in an extraordinary ECOFIN+INDIA configuration, has approved the redirection of €18 billion from the Chips Act and Digital Europe Programme into the sovereign fab and data centre build-out. We are finalising legal agreements with Germany and Poland to host the two 300mm wafer fabs under the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, granting them immunity from national energy caps under the emergency provisions of the EU Climate Law. Negotiations with ASML and Intel Europe continue under strict confidentiality, offering long-term procurement contracts and joint R&D rights in exchange for know-how transfer — but we are preparing fallback designs in case U.S. pressure blocks cooperation. The Secure Access Compact remains in flight, but its purpose is now secondary: it is no longer our shield, but a signal that we do not seek isolation, only independence. We will not wait for Canada’s ratification; we will build the capacity to act without it.
