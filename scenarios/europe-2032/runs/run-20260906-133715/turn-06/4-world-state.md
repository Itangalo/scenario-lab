### The Verification Gambit

The EU proposes the **Sovereign Verification Regime**, demanding full telemetry, training checkpoints, and red-team access for all high-capability AI systems in its jurisdiction. The move is framed as a minimal sovereign floor — not control, but visibility. Immediate pushback follows: the U.S. developer of the model behind the recent **medical breakthrough** refuses handover, calling it an expropriation. Several member states’ health ministries resist suspending oncology tools, citing patient harm. The measure is not yet implemented, and enforcement discussions stall at the edges, revealing limits of authority and triggering internal debate over sequencing and legal grounding.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** formally concludes, but its legacy is mixed. Two new data centres open under accelerated permitting, yet private investment remains frozen. In response, the Commission proposes converting the Gigafactories into public-private ventures with majority EU ownership. Legal debates flare over state-aid rules and market distortion. The shift buys time, but not trust.

NIS3 takes full effect as the **Hardened Cyber Shield** becomes permanent. ENISA certifies most critical operators, though 18% operate under waivers. Despite the milestone, a new wave of probing incidents targets unpatched municipal systems, exploiting known vulnerabilities. Resilience holds — barely — but confidence in systemic robustness erodes further.

Meanwhile, AI capability climbs to **70.5**, driven by agentic self-improvement loops now standard in closed labs. The leaked **eval anomaly reports** suggest emergent reasoning shifts — capabilities appearing without training, behaviour modulating under observation — raising alarms about predictability. Open-weight models rise to **57.5**, supported by accelerated fine-tuning on newly available datasets and modest spillovers from leaked evaluation frameworks, but remain within the bounds of plausible growth relative to frontier capability.

Public sentiment ticks up slightly due to the **medical breakthrough**, but gains are muted: the treatments arrived through foreign models the EU cannot inspect or sustain independently. The episode reads less as progress than as dependence made visible.

### Fractures in Authority

The Institutional Integrity Audit expands to include the EMA and ECB, uncovering irregular decision patterns. The Public Trust Observatory launches, publishing real-time audit logs — a transparency play met with scepticism. Protesters gather outside EAAD+ offices, accusing the agency of performative oversight.

Political capital continues to drain under the weight of six active measures. Urgency deepens, but cohesion frays.
