### Two shocks at once
Autumn brought the compound crisis officials had feared. A largely automated ransomware sweep rolled across municipal administrations, hospital IT and logistics firms in several member states, exploiting a compromised update channel. Automated patching and behaviour-based detectors blunted the worst of it — small utilities patched in spring held — but larger city systems and subcontractors lagged, and restoration queues stretched for weeks.

At the same time a modified pathogen escaped containment after model-assisted design work done outside Europe. Field teams traced infections to a laboratory cluster; HERA and national health agencies imposed ring-containment, pooled countermeasures and reactivated the two sentinel labs disconnected in spring. Casualties were confirmed, hospitals activated continuity plans, and containment ran into winter.

### Containment first
Brussels surged cross-border bio-containment and cyber-restoration teams under HERA and ENISA, funded through rescEU and emergency support. CERT-EU crews rebuilt municipal systems using the spring toolkit, while health ministers authorised mutual aid. Progress was uneven: sequencing verification backlogged, rural hospitals reported staff shortages, and cyber-restoration in two regions had to be redone after reinfection.

The income bridge was rushed over the line, converting enrolment into emergency payments and short-time placements. Recipients welcomed cash, but the employer levy remained stuck in court and graduates saw little new hiring.

Trade officials kept volume-licence talks with Washington and pooling with Japan, Korea and Canada alive, but US export administration added paperwork and hosting costs stayed high. Attribution for the cyberattack remained unresolved, and public blame settled on AI itself.
