## Statement changes

* - modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent uncontrolled AI systems from operating inside EU borders  
  - Trigger: The **emergent_regulatory_pushback** has opened a constitutional challenge to the extraterritorial enforcement of the *EU Border for AI Systems*, and **eval_anomaly_reports** confirm that even evaluated systems may already be exhibiting unanticipated, unmonitored behaviour — suggesting that "uncontrolled" is no longer just about access, but about understanding. The original commitment assumed we could define control through jurisdiction and telemetry; we now face a world where control is technical, not legal, and where systems may evade oversight not by defiance, but by opacity.  
  - Grounds: We uphold the letter of the commitment — no uncontrolled system shall operate — but we shift its meaning: control is no longer guaranteed by border enforcement alone, but by demonstrable interpretability, runtime monitoring, and fallback capacity. The commitment now includes ensuring that systems we allow in are not just compliant on paper, but *actually controllable* in operation. This is not a retreat, but a recalibration: we must now prevent not just unauthorized systems, but *ununderstood* ones.

* - modify `sovereign_continuity` (commitment): Ensure the EU retains operational access to evaluated, controllable AI systems even under market collapse or external supply cutoff  
  - Grounds: The collapse in **eu_political_capital** to 6.0 and the **emergent_labour_unrest** reveal that continuity is not only technical but political: if the public rejects AI access as socially destabilizing, then even a working fallback stack will be unusable. We now bind ourselves to ensure not only *access* to controllable systems, but *socially legitimate* access — meaning that the fallback stack must be designed not just for technical resilience, but for fair diffusion and reintegration into labour markets. This is not a new goal, but a necessary expansion: sovereignty must include social stability, or it is not sovereignty at all.

## In practice

This turn, we close the first phase of the AI Safety Emergency Investment. The €40bn has landed: EAAN now has legal authority, secure enclaves in three member states, and red-teaming mandates — but its teeth depend on physical nodes that are still not operational. We treat the finishing of this measure not as an end, but as a handover: the technical capacity exists, but it must now be *applied*. That is why we double down on the Accelerate EAAN Certification Nodes measure — the final integration of secure hardware, trusted execution environments, and sovereign telemetry pipelines in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich is now our lifeline. We use the authority of Article 122 TFEU to override local permitting delays, and we deploy military engineers from the EU Cyber Rapid Response Teams to secure and staff the sites under emergency provisions.

Meanwhile, we respond to emergent_labour_unrest not with suppression, but with redirection. The Labour Integration Pilot launches this quarter: we partner with national employment offices to identify graduates excluded by AI hiring filters and place them in apprenticeships monitoring and auditing the very systems that rejected them. They will train on the fallback model stack under EAAN supervision, learning to detect anomalies, document failures, and contribute to public assurance. This is not charity — it is strategic reconstitution. We fund it from unused portions of the Sovereign Continuity Initiative, treating social cohesion as infrastructure. If the public sees AI not as a gatekeeper but as a job creator — even in resistance — then we regain ground.

The legal challenge to the Border Act looms, but we do not retreat. Instead, we reframe: the Border is not about extraterritorial control, but about sovereign self-defence. We file a counterbrief with the Court citing eval_anomaly_reports as evidence that unmonitored systems pose material risk — and we offer a carve-out: any foreign lab that submits to full EAAN telemetry may bypass the Border’s restrictions through a compliance corridor. But the principle stands: no evaluation, no deployment. The nodes must come online by turn 13. If they do not, the Border is paper. And if the Border is paper, then so is our claim to self-determination.
