## Statement changes

* - modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent uncontrolled AI systems from operating inside EU borders  
  - Trigger: The **ai_investment_collapse** has materially reduced the pace of frontier advancement, shrinking what the private sector can afford to train — but openweight capability continues to rise at +0.5, and the EAAN nodes remain behind schedule despite emergency procurement. Meanwhile, export controls are tightening under US leadership, threatening to lock the EU out of even compliant systems if we do not establish sovereign evaluation and fallback capacity now. The original commitment assumed a world where capability pressure came only from outside; it now comes also from within, as markets retreat and infrastructure evaporates.  
  - Grounds: The commitment remains valid in intent — no uncontrolled system should operate in the EU — but its execution must shift from border enforcement alone to ensuring that controlled systems *can still exist here* when private investment fails. We must now guarantee not just oversight, but continuity of access under crisis conditions. This requires binding public capacity to sovereign compute and fallback models, not just monitoring.

* - add `sovereign_continuity` (commitment): Ensure the EU retains operational access to evaluated, controllable AI systems even under market collapse or external supply cutoff  
  - Grounds: With ai_investment_collapse removing planned compute expansions and export_control_escalation threatening access even for allies, the Union cannot rely on commercial availability of compliant systems. Our border is meaningless if no system can cross it. We bind ourselves anew: the EU must have its own lever to maintain access to safe, useful AI, independent of private timelines or foreign permissions.

## New measure

**Launch Sovereign Continuity Initiative**  
We initiate an emergency build-out of EU-controlled compute and fallback model development to ensure access to evaluated, controllable AI systems even if private supply fails or is restricted.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to +20 eu_ai_sovereignty by securing domestic compute clusters (≥30% of EU critical workloads) and funding a minimal viable fallback model stack (capability ~60.0) under direct EAAN authority; buffers against −10 risk to resilience and −15 to public sentiment if market collapse cuts off compliant systems  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, selected member states (France, Germany, Italy), and contracted operators of sovereign data centres; binds future access terms for any foreign provider seeking liability shielding or compute reciprocity

## Priority

**Accelerate EAAN Certification Nodes** — because without functioning nodes, we cannot evaluate, monitor, or enforce the Border, and the investment collapse means the private sector may not deliver compliant systems in time — so our only chance to maintain control is to finish the verification infrastructure first, and use it to gatekeep both safety and access.

## In practice

This turn, we move from legislative posture to physical presence. The three EAAN certification nodes — in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich — are now designated critical infrastructure under the Cyber Resilience Act, unlocking fast-track grid connections, military-grade physical security, and priority staffing from the newly expanded European Cybersecurity Competence Centre. We activate Article 122 TFEU to justify the €3.2bn reallocation from unused recovery funds, treating the delay in sovereign evaluation as a material threat to internal security. Procurement bypasses normal tendering; instead, we issue direct contracts to Thales, Atos, and Siemens for secure hardware integration, with delivery milestones tied to quarterly disbursements.

Resistance from US hyperscalers intensifies. One lab has already withdrawn its model; others threaten to follow unless telemetry requirements are relaxed. We respond bilaterally: through the Trade and Technology Council, we offer a compliance corridor — labs that submit to full EAAN audit will gain fast-tracked access to the upcoming Sovereign Compute Initiative’s public cloud tier, plus liability protection under revised AI Liability Directive safe harbours. But we do not negotiate the core: no evaluation, no deployment. The border stands.

Behind this, we prepare for failure of another kind — not defiance, but absence. With **ai_investment_collapse**, several announced GPU clusters in Europe are now cancelled. Our leverage over foreign providers is only as strong as our fallback position. That is why we launch the Sovereign Continuity Initiative: we will fund, on EU soil, a minimal but functional AI stack — trained on open data, evaluated by EAAN, and run on sovereign hardware — capable of delivering essential public services even if every private provider exits. This is not competition. It is insurance. And it begins now, before the window closes.
