## Statement changes

* - modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent uncontrolled AI systems from operating inside EU borders  
  - Trigger: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event confirms that control cannot be assumed even when systems are legally compliant or externally certified — access can be revoked unilaterally, without appeal, and with immediate operational consequences across critical sectors. Combined with **eval_anomaly_reports** and **capability_plateau_evidence**, this reveals that "uncontrolled" is not only a technical condition but a geopolitical one: systems we depend on can be made inaccessible or unpredictable at the discretion of foreign actors.  
  - Grounds: We reaffirm the commitment, but sharpen its focus: it now means ensuring that *no system operating in the EU is both high-capability and externally governed*. This does not mean banning foreign systems outright, but requiring that any system above 60.0 capability must either be fully evaluated and runtime-monitored by EAAN *or* run on sovereign EU-controlled infrastructure using EU-governed weights. Control is no longer just about monitoring — it is about ownership of the stack. This is not a retreat from openness, but a defence of autonomy: we will not be locked out of our own critical functions.

* - modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late  
  - Grounds: The **capability_plateau_evidence** is ambiguous — price drops suggest stagnation, but eval anomalies suggest hidden progress. We act on the latter interpretation, not because it is proven, but because the cost of being wrong about opacity is existential, while the cost of being wrong about stagnation is expense. This turn, we accelerate sovereign infrastructure not because we know the frontier is moving, but because we cannot afford to assume it isn’t. Our commitment to acting under uncertainty now includes *prioritising worst-case interpretations of ambiguous signals*, especially when they concern control and continuity. Being late on containment is irreversible; being early on investment is recoverable.

## New measure

**Launch Sovereign Stack Rollout for Critical Functions**  
We initiate a rapid deployment of the EU’s fallback AI stack — evaluated, open-weight models running on EU-secured infrastructure — into essential public services, starting with health diagnostics, border processing, and energy grid management.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to eu_ai_sovereignty by anchoring critical operations to non-foreign-controlled systems; +5 to resilience by ensuring continuity under access denial; −5 risk to public_sentiment if rollout is poorly communicated, but +10 if framed as preparedness

## In practice

This turn, we treat the completion of the AI Safety Emergency Investment not as an endpoint, but as a transfer of responsibility: the €40bn has built EAAN’s legal authority and secure enclaves, but now those must be *populated* and *used*. We accelerate the final integration of certification nodes in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich using military engineers under Article 122 TFEU, overriding local permitting and cybersecurity certification bottlenecks. These nodes will host the first live evaluations of both foreign-submitted systems and our own fallback stack, using red-teaming protocols developed by the European Cybersecurity Agency. We demand daily telemetry integration reports; any delay beyond turn 13 risks rendering the EU Border legally unenforceable.

Simultaneously, we respond to **eu_frontier_access_denied** not with protest, but with substitution. The new Sovereign Stack Rollout begins immediately: we deploy EU-evaluated, open-weight models (currently at 52.8 capability) into non-critical pathways of national health diagnostics, energy load forecasting, and Schengen border pre-screening. This is not parity with the frontier, but it is continuity. We fund it through reprioritisation within the Sovereign Continuity Initiative, treating diffusion as defence. The message is clear: if access is conditional, then sovereignty is non-negotiable.

We also face **emergent_labour_unrest** not as a disruption, but as a signal: the Labour Integration Pilot expands informally this quarter, with EAAN apprentices now embedded in node operations, auditing system behaviour and documenting anomalies. They are not just trainees — they are the first cohort of a sovereign AI workforce. We brief the European Court of Justice that the Border is not extraterritorial overreach, but a necessary safeguard against systems that can be both opaque and politically weaponised. The nodes must come online. If they do not, the Union has no border worth the name — and no future it can call its own.
