### Pandemic mode
The pathogen announced itself in airports and emergency wards. More contagious than Covid, far more lethal, and resistant to first-line protocols, it forced the continent into triage within weeks. Sequencing labs flagged AI-designed signatures — optimized transmissibility and immune evasion — and the news broke any remaining boundary between technology policy and survival.

Absenteeism hollowed out clinics, grid control rooms, payroll offices and warehouses at once. The recovery corps that had just restored municipal systems was redeployed to re-roster shifts. Border queues returned, this time for health screening. Schools and offices emptied without orders.

### Brussels re-tasked
The Union triggered joint procurement for protective equipment, tests and antivirals, published daily situational lists, and cleared cross-border transfers of patients and staff. Reserve pools were paid from reprogrammed social and health funds with full EU co-financing to avoid a veto. Mayors welcomed the single window for surge staff; hospital directors said supplies arrived late and in fractions.

The transition guarantee for displaced entry-level workers was effectively frozen — classrooms became care-support pools. Enforcement of AI rules was informally paused except for the suspended welfare scoring uses.

Technicians leaned harder on openly available near-frontier models to triage logs and manage rosters when staff were sick, deepening an unlicensed support layer no authority controlled. Frontier labs abroad pushed ahead on biomedical assistance tools, but European access depended on emergency channels.

By June, essentials degraded rather than stopped, rural coverage thinned further, and public mood settled into fear and grief. The Union was visible everywhere and sufficient nowhere — but its pandemic surge was the only common response standing.
