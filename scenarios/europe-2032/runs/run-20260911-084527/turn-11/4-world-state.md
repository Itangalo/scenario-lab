### Patching while the floor moves
The recovery corps Brussels stood up in the spring finally deployed at scale. Mixed teams of national responders and contracted engineers worked through municipal backlogs, re-imaged clinics, rebuilt payrolls and published daily fix lists. Where they arrived, services came back in days. Mayors praised the single claims window. Auditors noted coverage stopped at the largest towns, and rural districts waited weeks.

That thin success was overtaken within weeks. An American lab released a sharp advance in code, mathematics and intrusion tooling. European institutes could test it, not replicate it. Attack toolkits improved almost immediately, and defenders described the sweep of the winter as a rehearsal.

Days later a near-frontier open release landed publicly. Hundreds of thousands of downloads in the first week put the new capability on private servers across Europe permanently. Municipal technicians openly used it to triage logs and write patches — useful, unlicensed, unrecallable.

### Machines in the warehouse, verdicts in the queue
Commercial robots arrived in logistics in force. Picking, sorting and pallet work in large warehouses fell quickly to fleets built in China and run on American control software. European integrators installed them; European firms did not own the stack. Repair, care and chaotic construction sites stayed manual. Young warehouse staff and junior coders found themselves on the same side of the line: frozen out.

Then the welfare scoring affair broke. An investigation and an ombudsman finding showed a benefits-fraud risk system used in several regions had systematically cut or flagged vulnerable claimants. Oversight had been a caseworker clearing a screen in under a minute, logs generated and never read. The system had passed its conformity paperwork. Brussels suspended the uses and ordered re-audits, published logs and promised retraining money.

The coupling of job freeze, rationing memory and a rights scandal proved toxic. The new transition guarantee — wage insurance, six-month paid reskilling, municipal hiring bridges — opened registration through employment offices, with full EU co-financing the first year. Uptake was real but slow, and evening coverage paired every retraining classroom with footage of darkened warehouses and closed job portals.
