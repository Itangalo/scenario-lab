### Restoration and reckoning
Autumn brought two shocks at once. A largely automated ransomware sweep, built with machine-written intrusion tooling, moved through a compromised software dependency into municipal administrations in several member states. Registries, appointment systems and local payment desks went dark for days. Defenders were visibly behind for the first week; attribution will take months.

Almost simultaneously, a national ombudsman and then a court found that an AI-supported benefits and policing support system had systematically wronged claimants for over a year. Caseworkers had approved flagged files in under a minute, logs that recorded the pattern were never read, and conformity papers were in order. Brussels framed it as an enforcement failure, not a badly drawn law, and ordered re-audits of similar high-risk systems, suspension of queue-style approvals, and opening of logs to ombudsmen, with EU money for case review.

The Commission's new pact paired the two responses: joint restoration teams, clean backups and continuity payouts for hit municipalities, plus mandatory incident reporting and human-review redress. Daily technical bulletins showed slow progress; a newly published interpretability technique helped operators predict some malicious behaviours on deployed systems and was quickly copied by vendors. A sharp advance in code and intrusion tooling demonstrated by an unreleased system made the catch-up partial — attackers moved within weeks, defenders within months.

Capacity politics hardened. Washington tightened chip and model export controls again, with allies rationed alongside others on volume licences. In Brussels this was used to argue that cheap foreign hyperscaler deals could not substitute for anchored capacity. The first gigafactory sites finally reached energisation after years of permitting and court fights, celebrated as proof the build was real, though three further sites remain stalled.

Protests around two gigafactory grid connections grew louder, with night-time blockades and a brief sabotage attempt noted by local press. Public mood soured: helpful assistants shared front pages with closed counters and wrongful benefit cuts.
