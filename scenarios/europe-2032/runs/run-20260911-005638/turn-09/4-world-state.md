### Counters still dark
Through autumn the municipal ransomware grind continued. The compromised management component was patched, but reinfection via cloned images and slow backup restores kept registries and payment desks closed in rotation. Standing joint teams from the health emergency authority and the EU cyber agency cleared backlogs town by town, and hospitals and grids stayed ring-fenced. Citizens saw crews arriving, but also queued again.

The new bio and recovery surge was voted as the semester's priority. Sequencing and wastewater pilots in France and the Netherlands were extended, screening duties on DNA providers tightened by implementing act, and restoration teams put on permanent funding. Money moved slowly: procurement, hiring of bioinformaticians and municipal IT staff stretched into winter. Laboratories noted the first upgrades landing; town halls did not yet feel them.

### A paper on what should not be easy
In October a contested pre-print claimed a genome model had helped produce a viable design for a human-infecting organism, or that a non-expert could get there with assistance. Methodologists attacked the result, others accused the authors of publishing too much. Inside the biosecurity community it landed as a step-change: stronger than anything published before. The Commission cited it to justify the surge; opponents cited the dispute to call the surge alarmist spending.

### Concrete blocked
The automation shield paid out. Wage insurance and retraining vouchers reached warehouse and parcel workers displaced by American software on Chinese machines. It slowed anger without reversing it.

At the same time, coordinated protests and night-time sabotage attempts hit grid connections for the gigafactory expansion in two countries. Police guarded substations, courts heard challenges to permits, and two links stayed blocked. Defenders of the first energised site held in court, but no new concrete was poured. The Union proved it could restore counters and pay continuity cheques; it did not prove it could build.
