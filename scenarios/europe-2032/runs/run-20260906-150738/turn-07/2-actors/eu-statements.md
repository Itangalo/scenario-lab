# Statements: The European Union (turn 7)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse
- `new_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent catastrophic misuse by enforcing strict limits on all systems above 60.0 capability, whether sovereign or foreign, deployed or in development, with binding containment, real-time monitoring, and public accountability — even if it means halting our own flagship programme.

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural

  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without social legitimacy is a fortress without a people. However, legitimacy cannot be built on broken promises: we must now accept that **sovereign capability cannot be delivered on time**, and that **preventing catastrophic misuse requires us to constrain what we cannot yet control**.

Our flagship Sovereign AI Core Programme, already delayed by export controls, will not reach 60.0 by turn 8. We are exposed. The two mandates now demand a shift: from building sovereign capability *first* to *limiting uncontrolled capability everywhere*, including our own. We are no longer just behind; we are outmaneuvered. This is a categorical shift — not in values, but in what is possible.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and we will now pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; nor will we assume that control justifies silence, and we will couple every containment measure with a public explanation of its purpose and limits. **Now, we add: we will not promise what we cannot deliver, and we will not fund what we cannot finish.** Where timelines slip and capital vanishes, we will constrain rather than pretend.
  - Grounds: The *ai_investment_collapse* has invalidated the financial assumptions underpinning the InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package. The *cyber_major_incident* demonstrates that the threat is not future but present. Our commitment to act must now include the discipline of restraint: we cannot build our way out of this crisis in time, so we must govern what exists. The cost of reversal is high — markets will see retreat, and member states may defect further — but the cost of overreach is collapse.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **retire** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **add** `new_two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Prevent catastrophic misuse by enforcing strict limits on all systems above 60.0 capability, whether sovereign or foreign, deployed or in development, with binding containment, real-time monitoring, and public accountability — even if it means halting our own flagship programme.
  - Grounds: The Sovereign AI Core Programme will not reach 60.0 by turn 8. US labs have already surpassed it. The *cyber_major_incident* was model-generated and automated at scale. Our previous commitment assumed we could build sovereign control fast enough to matter. That assumption is now invalid. The new commitment reflects a shift from *achieving control* to *exercising control*, regardless of origin. This is not surrender — it is realism. The Union must now govern capability, not just produce it.
