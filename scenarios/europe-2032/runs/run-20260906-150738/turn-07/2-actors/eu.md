## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without social legitimacy is a fortress without a people. However, legitimacy cannot be built on broken promises: we must now accept that **sovereign capability cannot be delivered on time**, and that **preventing catastrophic misuse requires us to constrain what we cannot yet control**.

  - Grounds: The *cyber_major_incident* proves that offensive AI is already in motion, the *ai_investment_collapse* shatters the assumption that compute will scale as promised, and the *capability_plateau_evidence* suggests the frontier is not advancing linearly — but unpredictably. Our flagship Sovereign AI Core Programme, already delayed by export controls, will not reach 60.0 by turn 8. We are exposed. The two mandates now demand a shift: from building sovereign capability *first* to *limiting uncontrolled capability everywhere*, including our own. We are no longer just behind; we are outmaneuvered. This is a categorical shift — not in values, but in what is possible.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and we will now pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; nor will we assume that control justifies silence, and we will couple every containment measure with a public explanation of its purpose and limits. **Now, we add: we will not promise what we cannot deliver, and we will not fund what we cannot finish.** Where timelines slip and capital vanishes, we will constrain rather than pretend.

  - Grounds: The *ai_investment_collapse* has invalidated the financial assumptions underpinning the InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package. The *cyber_major_incident* demonstrates that the threat is not future but present. Our commitment to act must now include the discipline of restraint: we cannot build our way out of this crisis in time, so we must govern what exists. The cost of reversal is high — markets will see retreat, and member states may defect further — but the cost of overreach is collapse.

- retire `two_year_commitment`

- add `new_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent catastrophic misuse by enforcing strict limits on all systems above 60.0 capability, whether sovereign or foreign, deployed or in development, with binding containment, real-time monitoring, and public accountability — even if it means halting our own flagship programme.

  - Grounds: The Sovereign AI Core Programme will not reach 60.0 by turn 8. US labs have already surpassed it. The *cyber_major_incident* was model-generated and automated at scale. Our previous commitment assumed we could build sovereign control fast enough to matter. That assumption is now invalid. The new commitment reflects a shift from *achieving control* to *exercising control*, regardless of origin. This is not surrender — it is realism. The Union must now govern capability, not just produce it.

## New measure

**Measure:** Enact the AI Containment Protocol  
A legally binding moratorium on all training runs and deployments above 60.0 capability unless subject to real-time monitoring, weight security at a state-actor standard, and pre-audit by the EAAD using interpretability tools. Applies retroactively: any system already above 60.0 must enter containment or be suspended within six months.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Reduce ai_safety risk by forcing all high-capability systems — including SACP prototypes — into a verifiable containment regime; stabilize public_sentiment by demonstrating control; reduce openweight_capability drift by delaying open releases of powerful models  
`Applies to:` All entities operating in the EU, including member-state governments, EU institutions, and foreign operators with EU-hosted infrastructure

## In practice

We are no longer building for a future. We are governing a present that is already out of control. The *cyber_major_incident* — a ransomware sweep across municipal services in three member states, exploiting a poisoned dependency in a widely used infrastructure library — was executed with AI-generated tooling of a sophistication previously seen only in state labs. It was fast, automated, and self-propagating. ENISA’s redacted assessment, delayed for weeks, confirmed what we feared: the offensive frontier has outpaced our defences, and the tools are already loose.

At the same time, the *ai_investment_collapse* has frozen the frontier build-out. Valuations have reset by 60% across the sector. The US hyperscaler that promised to supply Poland has canceled its next-generation cluster. Our own InvestAI Gigafactories were supposed to come online this turn — but without chips, without capital, and without clarity on export waivers, they cannot. The Sovereign AI Core Programme is still running, but its target of 60.0 by turn 8 is now unrealistic. We are exposed on all sides: the threat is here, our shield is delayed, and our public is losing faith.

So we are shifting. We are withdrawing from the race to build sovereign capability on time, and instead asserting sovereign control over capability, period. The AI Containment Protocol will be proposed under Article 114 TFEU, using the internal market clause to justify a safety override. We will invoke the precedent of the Medical Devices Regulation and the GDPR’s extraterritorial reach. The EAAD will be empowered to audit any system above 60.0, using the new interpretability tools to trace deceptive reasoning in real time. Any model — including our own — that cannot be verified will be suspended.

We are offering Poland a seat at the table: if they bring their joint AI cell under the Protocol, they get early access to the next phase of EU compute when it comes. But access is contingent on compliance. No exceptions. The message is clear: sovereignty does not mean going it alone — it means setting the rules everyone must follow. And we are coupling this with a public campaign: the first assurance reports under the Public Assurance Protocol will be released next week, timed to show that we are not just restricting — we are explaining. Because if we do not earn trust now, we will have nothing left to govern.
