AI capability stabilizes at 67.0 amid signs of a potential plateau, as a sophisticated AI-generated ransomware attack cripples municipal services across three EU member states, exposing critical vulnerabilities in software supply chains. Attribution remains uncertain, but evidence suggests advanced offensive tools are now accessible beyond state actors. Simultaneously, the AI investment bubble bursts—valuations collapse, major compute projects are cancelled, and frontier access prices drop sharply, triggering sector-wide retrenchment.

In response, the EU abandons its flagship InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package due to fiscal strain and hardware restrictions, pivoting to the AI Containment Protocol: a moratorium on all systems above 60.0 capability unless they submit to real-time monitoring, weight security, and EAAD audit—including the Sovereign AI Core Programme. Poland is offered future compute access in exchange for compliance, but resistance grows over sovereignty concerns. The first Public Assurance Protocol reports are published, yet public trust erodes further as disruption overwhelms messaging.

Labour unrest spreads as AI displaces public-sector administrative roles, compounding social strain. ENISA’s delayed threat assessment reveals institutional bottlenecks, while legal challenges emerge against the Containment Protocol. Political capital falls deeper, coordination frays, and the EU’s shift from development to restriction underscores a defensive posture. Sovereignty drops to 10.0, resilience weakens, and safety declines despite technical progress, as institutional adoption lags. Public sentiment dips below 25.0, reflecting growing anxiety and perceived stagnation.
