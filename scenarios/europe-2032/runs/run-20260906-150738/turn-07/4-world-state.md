### A Crisis of Control

The EU confronts a cascading crisis. A sophisticated AI-generated ransomware attack cripples municipal services across three member states, exploiting a poisoned software dependency. Attribution remains murky, but the scale and automation point to advanced offensive tooling now in circulation. Simultaneously, the AI sector reels from a sudden investment collapse: valuations plummet, major compute expansions are scrapped, and confidence evaporates. A much-hyped model release underperforms, while prices for top-tier access drop sharply—signs the frontier may be plateauing, though labs dispute it.

The EU pivots hard. It abandons the InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package, citing vanished capital and blocked hardware. Instead, it launches the AI Containment Protocol—a sweeping moratorium on systems above 60.0 capability unless they submit to real-time monitoring, weight security, and EAAD audit. The rule applies to all, including the EU’s own Sovereign AI Core Programme. Poland is offered inclusion in future compute in exchange for compliance, but resistance simmers.

Publicly, the Commission releases the first Public Assurance Protocol reports, aiming to rebuild trust. But the narrative is overwhelmed by disruption. The containment push is seen as reactive, not visionary. Labour unrest grows as AI displaces mid-level administrative roles in the public sector, and the cyberattack fuels anxiety.

Behind the scenes, coordination frays. ENISA’s delayed threat assessment underscores institutional strain. The AI Containment Protocol faces legal challenges and scepticism from member states wary of ceding control. The shift from building to restricting erodes already thin political capital.

### Fractures Deepen

Sovereignty slips further as promised domestic capacity fails to materialize. Resilience declines under the weight of unpatched vulnerabilities. Safety erodes despite interpretability gains, as capability advances outpace institutional adoption. Public sentiment dips on rising disruption and perceived stagnation. The EU acts, but the momentum is defensive, fragmented, and costly.
