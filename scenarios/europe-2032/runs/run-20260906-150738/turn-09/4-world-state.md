### A Fragile Pause

Global AI capability inches forward at a reduced pace, as signs emerge that the frontier is plateauing — major labs underdeliver on promises, and high-end model access becomes cheaper, suggesting last year’s breakthroughs are now commoditized. Yet the gap between capability and control remains vast.

The EU declares the Sovereign AI Core Programme complete, but the system delivered falls short of operational readiness and operates only in isolated test environments. No sovereign stack reaches deployment, deepening dependence on foreign infrastructure just as export controls tighten. In response to the leak, the Commission expands enforcement of the AI Containment Protocol, targeting hobbyist networks running derivatives of the compromised architecture. Emergency suspension orders are issued, but detection remains spotty and legal challenges mount over overreach. The expansion of the Containment Protocol is maintained as the EU’s sole priority measure.

Meanwhile, Poland finalizes a bilateral agreement with a U.S. hyperscaler for priority AI access, bypassing EU coordination — a clear act of member-state defection. Other mid-sized states quietly expand their informal AI Preparedness Pact, pooling defensive tools and red-teaming resources outside Union structures.

The EU announces the Emergency AI Resilience Corps, aiming to build rapid-response capacity across healthcare, energy, and emergency services. Training sites are designated in Lyon and Tallinn, but recruitment lags and funding disputes delay full mobilization. The initiative is proposed but not adopted as a priority, and implementation remains partial and slow.

Public sentiment holds at rock bottom. Media spotlight the irony of regulating leaked domestic technology while failing to secure it in the first place. Labour protests grow in cities where AI-driven layoffs coincide with the crisis.

Despite a global cyber defence breakthrough that improves automated patching and swarm detection, the EU fails to fully integrate the tools in time to bolster confidence. Resilience sees minor gains from early preparations, but systemic fragility persists.

### Sovereignty Erodes Further

With no sovereign capability delivered, rising global capability, and a member state defecting, the Union’s AI sovereignty continues to decline. Open-weight capability sees a minimal rise, reflecting limited spillover from decommissioned test models and partial open-sourcing of non-core components from the failed Sovereign AI Core — not a structural leap, but a marginal technical creep.

Political capital erodes sharply under the weight of multiple active measures: the ongoing expansion of the AI Containment Protocol (−3), the new Emergency AI Resilience Corps (−3), and the continued designation of a priority measure (−1), for a total charge of −7. With no offsetting legitimacy gains, political capital falls from 8.0 to 1.0, reflecting the Union’s stretched capacity and weakening credibility.
