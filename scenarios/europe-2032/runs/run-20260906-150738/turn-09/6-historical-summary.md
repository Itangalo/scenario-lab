A fragile stabilization emerges as global AI advancement slows, with frontier capabilities plateauing and high-end models becoming commoditized, though the gap between technological progress and regulatory control remains wide. The EU declares the Sovereign AI Core Programme complete, but the resulting system is non-operational, confined to test environments, and fails to deliver any sovereign AI stack, reinforcing reliance on foreign infrastructure amid tightening global export controls.

In response to the earlier lab breach, the EU intensifies enforcement of the AI Containment Protocol, extending it to hobbyist and decentralized networks using derivatives of the leaked architecture. Emergency suspension orders are issued, but inconsistent detection and mounting legal challenges undermine legitimacy. The Protocol remains the Union’s sole priority measure, consuming dwindling political capital.

Poland formalizes a bilateral AI access deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, breaking from EU coordination and signaling open defection. Other mid-sized member states strengthen the informal AI Preparedness Pact, pooling defensive AI resources and red-teaming capabilities outside official Union channels, reflecting deepening fragmentation.

The EU launches the Emergency AI Resilience Corps to bolster critical sectors, designating training hubs in Lyon and Tallinn, but recruitment shortfalls and funding disputes delay full activation. Despite a global breakthrough in cyber defense—improving automated patching and swarm detection—the EU fails to integrate these tools effectively, leaving systemic vulnerabilities exposed.

Public trust remains at historic lows, fueled by irony over regulating AI the EU itself failed to secure. Labor unrest spreads as AI-driven job displacement accelerates. Political capital, drained by the Protocol's expansion (−3), the Resilience Corps (−3), and ongoing priority designation (−1) without offsetting gains, collapses from 8.0 to 1.0, marking a severe erosion of institutional credibility and capacity. Open-weight AI advances only marginally, through limited releases from the failed Sovereign AI Core, indicating no structural progress—only technical creep.
