## In practice
We are acting on three fronts: containment, credibility, and continuity. On containment, we are enforcing the expanded AI Containment Protocol despite legal challenges and uneven compliance. The EAAD has issued its first emergency suspension orders targeting hobbyist clusters running agentic models derived from the leaked architecture. ENISA is deploying detection signatures to national CERTs by Q4, funded through the Digital Europe Programme’s crisis envelope. We are pressing Poland and others to align, warning that bilateral deals with hyperscalers will not shield them from cross-border attacks enabled by uncontrolled code.

On credibility, we are refusing to hide. The lab responsible for the leak will receive conditional immunity only if it fully cooperates with forensic red-teaming and assists in tracking derivatives. We are publishing a special Public Assurance Report detailing what failed, how it was exploited, and what we are doing — not to assign blame, but to show that we are learning. This is not spin: it is survival. If the public believes we govern only to protect institutions, not people, then every future measure will face resistance.

On continuity, we are launching the Emergency AI Resilience Corps. Under the Civil Protection Mechanism and co-funded by the Recovery and Resilience Facility, it will integrate cybersecurity reservists, public health IT teams, and energy grid engineers into a standing coalition. Training begins next quarter in Lyon and Tallinn, with live exercises scheduled before year-end. This is not another study group — it is boots on the ground for a new kind of crisis. When the next ransomware swarm hits hospital systems, we will have teams ready to isolate, restore, and keep care running. This is how sovereignty survives: not by perfect prevention, but by proven endurance.
