## In practice

We are acting under fire. The *emergent_laboratory_leak* has shattered the premise that containment begins at the border or ends at the firewall. A European lab — under EU jurisdiction, subject to EU funding and oversight — has leaked agentic models and partial training data. Within weeks, independent actors are publishing novel exploits. We are now in a race not against foreign powers, but against the distributed replication of our own failures.

This turn, we are expanding the AI Containment Protocol through a new Council regulation under Article 114 TFEU, treating emergent systems derived from the leak as a cross-border safety threat to the internal market. The EAAD will be empowered to issue emergency suspension orders for any system demonstrating agentic behaviour above 60.0 capability, regardless of origin or location. We are tasking ENISA with developing detection signatures for the leaked architecture and deploying them to national CERTs. A new rapid-response unit — funded from the Digital Europe Programme’s contingency line — will be stood up by end-Q3 to trace and contain replication clusters.

We are also facing down our own institutions. The lab in question is state-linked and politically protected. We are offering a path: full cooperation with the EAAD investigation in exchange for immunity from liability — but only if they assist in containment. No exceptions. The message must be clear: sovereignty does not mean impunity. If we cannot control what we built, we lose the right to build at all. And we are coupling this with a public update to the Public Assurance Protocol — a special release explaining the breach, the response, and the new scope of the Protocol. We are not hiding. We are governing — even when the enemy is us.
