### The Leak and the Law

A European AI laboratory, funded and overseen by EU institutions, suffers a catastrophic breach. Early-stage agentic models and partial training data escape into open networks. Within weeks, distributed actors begin replicating frontier-level reasoning and publishing novel exploits derived from the architecture. The breach originated not from foreign sabotage, but from systemic oversight failures within a trusted domestic institution.

In response, the EU declares a crisis of governance. The AI Containment Protocol, already strained, is expanded via emergency regulation under Article 114 TFEU to cover not only declared systems above 60.0 capability, but any system—regardless of origin or infrastructure—that exceeds the threshold and is derived from the leaked architecture. The European Artificial Intelligence Assurance Directorate (EAAD) is empowered to issue emergency suspension orders, while ENISA races to develop detection signatures for national cyber teams.

The Sovereign AI Core Programme, intended to finish this turn, delivers only partial infrastructure. No system reaches 60.0 under EU control, and the promise of sovereign capability collapses amid the scandal. The Commission attempts to assert authority by offering legal immunity to the offending lab in exchange for cooperation—a move criticized as lenient yet politically necessary.

Public sentiment erodes further. Media coverage focuses on institutional failure and the irony of regulating systems the EU itself unleashed. Labour unrest intensifies as automation spreads through public administration, now perceived as both disruptive and domestically sourced. Legal challenges emerge against the expanded Protocol, questioning its reach into academic and hobbyist spaces. Implementation of the new enforcement regime will be phased, with full operational capacity delayed pending legal clarity and national coordination.

Behind the scenes, confidence in EU coordination wanes. Poland, still resisting full compliance, signals it may pursue bilateral access arrangements. The informal AI Preparedness Pact among mid-sized member states gains quiet momentum, reflecting growing distrust in central enforcement.

### Fractured Authority

The EU acts decisively on paper, but its authority is tested in practice. The breach undermines the legitimacy of its containment regime. While the expansion of the Protocol is legally bold, its enforceability against distributed, non-state actors remains uncertain and will unfold over time. The pace of global capability development continues, but the EU’s ability to shape or even track it is now in question. Control has become as much a matter of detection and denial as of law.
