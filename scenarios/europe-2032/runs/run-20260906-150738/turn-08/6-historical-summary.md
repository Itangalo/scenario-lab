A catastrophic breach at an EU-funded AI laboratory leaks early-stage agentic models and training data, enabling distributed actors to replicate frontier-level systems and develop novel exploits. The leak—caused by domestic oversight failures, not foreign attack—triggers a crisis of governance. The EU expands the AI Containment Protocol under emergency powers to cover any system exceeding 60.0 capability derived from the leaked architecture, regardless of origin, empowering EAAD to issue suspension orders and ENISA to develop detection tools. 

The Sovereign AI Core Programme fails to deliver, shattering hopes of sovereign AI capability. Legal immunity offered to the offending lab draws criticism as public trust plummets amid perceptions of institutional failure and irony in regulating technology the EU itself unleashed. Public sentiment falls further, labor unrest intensifies with automation now seen as both disruptive and homegrown, and legal challenges arise over the Protocol’s reach into academic and hobbyist domains.

Enforcement is delayed due to legal and coordination hurdles, while Poland resists full compliance and explores bilateral deals. An informal AI Preparedness Pact among mid-sized states gains traction, signaling declining confidence in central EU authority. Despite decisive regulatory moves, the EU’s ability to control, detect, or track advancing AI capabilities is now seriously in doubt, marking a shift toward reactive containment and fragmented implementation.
