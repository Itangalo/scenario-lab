### Thresholds and Telemetry  
The EU clears legal scrutiny for the **EU Agent Safety Threshold**, finalising stress tests for goal stability and interruptibility, and accrediting a first cohort of third-party evaluators. With diplomatic outreach to Japan and South Korea advancing mutual recognition talks, the framework edges toward tabling in the Council—though US and UK labs continue to dismiss it as innovation-hostile. The regime’s credibility now hinges on implementation, not design.

Meanwhile, the **Cyber Response Activation and Open-Weight Safeguards Directive** completes its rollout. ENISA’s telemetry network, tested during the Dutch logistics cascade and financial breach, proves effective in coordination. However, the incident reveals that open-weight models—static in capability but widely embedded—are operating with dangerous autonomy due to weak API boundaries and absent monitoring.

### Sovereignty Without Muscle  
Despite completing the InvestAI Gigafactories, sovereign compute remains underutilised. Integration delays and workforce shortages prevent operational uptake, leaving eu_ai_sovereignty unchanged from last turn’s erosion. The infrastructure exists, but the capacity to wield it does not—sovereignty decays further as global AI advances.

### Fragmentation Under Pressure  
Germany adopts the Fraunhofer-ENISA provenance tagging standard through enforcement leverage, but France’s push to expand oversight into education and local government fails amid concerns over overreach. Regulatory fragmentation persists, fueling a fast-tracked legal challenge by industry groups demanding harmonisation under the single market. Public protests against data centres grow, reflecting unresolved tensions between security and deployment.

### Stalled Momentum  
With no new measure proposed and political capital strained, the EU consolidates around the Agent Safety Threshold. The portfolio remains heavy: two large measures in flight, one finishing this turn. While crisis response strengthens resilience, broader control over autonomous systems remains aspirational.
