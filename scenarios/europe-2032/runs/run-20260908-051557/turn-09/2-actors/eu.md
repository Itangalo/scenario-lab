## In practice  
This turn, we bring the EU Agent Safety Threshold to the edge of adoption. The legal review concludes, and we prepare to table the draft in the Council and Parliament under Article 114 TFEU, with the European AI Office leading implementation. Final stress tests for goal stability and interruptibility—co-developed with CERN and national labs—are now validated and ready for deployment. Third-party evaluators, drawn from academia, ENISA, and vetted private auditors, are in final accreditation. We are not merely setting a standard; we are building a regime that must survive both US hostility and internal fragmentation. Diplomatic outreach to Japan and South Korea continues, not for consensus but for mutual recognition pathways: if they accept our test protocols, we accept theirs, creating a bloc of enforceable standards. The US and UK dismiss it as innovation-hostile, but we are no longer asking for permission.

Simultaneously, we operationalise the lessons from the Dutch logistics cascade. The Cyber Response Activation Directive, now finished, has proven its value: ENISA coordinated real-time telemetry from nine states and triggered mutual aid within minutes. The breach in the financial clearinghouse was contained, but the Dutch incident exposed a deeper truth—open-weight models, though not advancing in capability, are already embedded in semi-autonomous roles with weak API boundaries and no monitoring. Germany’s participation in the provenance tagging pilot is a foothold, but it is narrow. We resist France’s push to expand into education and local government, not because the risks aren’t real, but because our political capital is at 21.0 and overreach now would collapse everything. We consolidate instead: control first, coverage later. The Gigafactories stand idle not for lack of will, but for lack of skilled operators and integration frameworks—sovereignty is not metal, it is muscle. We are learning, but the world does not wait.
