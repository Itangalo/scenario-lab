## In practice
This turn, we lock in the Union’s place within the new joint cyber command, turning an invitation into operational integration. The Commission tables a decision under Article 17(6) TEU for a Council Implementing Decision that binds ENISA and national CERTs into the shared telemetry and response framework established by the joint_threat_response. We fast-track it through the Permanent Representatives Committee (COREPER II) and the Competitiveness Council, leveraging the cyber_defence_breakthrough as proof that coordinated defence now has teeth. The legal basis is Article 4(2) TEU — collective security — and funding flows from the Internal Security Fund, with a €1.2bn allocation for integration of national systems into the shared platform by turn 3. This is not a standalone capability, but force multiplication: we gain access to detection models trained on global intrusion patterns, and in return, we contribute EU-wide telemetry, raising the cost of attacks on any member.

At the same time, we press forward on the Resilience Hardening Directive, now in trilogue between the Commission, Parliament (IMCO and ENVI), and the Council. Resistance from smaller member states persists, centred on cost and sovereignty concerns. To break the deadlock, we announce an upfront disbursement of €15bn from the Modernisation Fund, conditioned on early transposition, and link compliance to continued access to REPowerEU grants. The Commission’s Legal Service confirms that Article 114 TFEU suffices for harmonisation on systemic cyber risk, and we begin drafting delegated acts specifying minimum patching cadence, agent-detection thresholds, and third-party audit requirements.

The two industrial measures — InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech sovereignty package — continue on track. Site selection for the Gigafactories is down to five candidates; we prioritise those with existing semiconductor clusters in Germany, France, and Poland, using the 2025 Crisis Response Instrument to override regional objections on grid allocation. For the Tech sovereignty package, we monitor national transposition of accelerated permitting, offering fast-grid-access vouchers from the Digital Europe Programme to the first three member states that complete it. Talent retention remains a quiet crisis: we begin consultations on a EU-wide golden visa for AI engineers, but this waits for a future measure. For now, we build the ground on which sovereign capacity can stand — not just compute, but cohesion, credibility, and collective response.
