## In practice
This turn, we act on two fronts: one anticipatory, one reactive. On the sovereign capacity front, we finalise binding memoranda with Poland and Finland for the first two InvestAI Gigafactories, leveraging the Crisis Response Instrument to override remaining permitting delays and guarantee grid prioritisation. The €50bn disbursement under InvestEU is activated, with EuroHPC JU assuming operational oversight of compute allocation. We enforce conditions: full audit rights, sovereign control of model weights, and embedded red-teaming units with live access to training and inference pipelines. France and Germany commit matching national funds, but Dutch academic resistance persists — we counter by decentralising research hosting while centralising compute access, ensuring unity without overreach. This is not just about hardware; it is about making sure that the EU’s compute cannot be remotely disabled, compromised, or withheld.

Simultaneously, we respond to the **cyber_major_incident** — a large-scale ransomware sweep, model-generated and targeting public health and municipal services across three member states, exploiting a poisoned software dependency with a blast radius still being mapped. Attribution is unclear, but the tooling bears the hallmarks of agent-level planning. In response, we launch the **Cyber Response Activation and Open-Weight Safeguards Directive**, a new category 6 measure that mandates real-time incident sharing through ENISA, activates cross-border mutual aid protocols, and imposes minimum security thresholds on open-weight models used in critical infrastructure. This includes mandatory watermarking, weight hardening, and audit trails for any model above 45.0 capability deployed in regulated sectors. We tie compliance to access to EU funding and digital procurement eligibility, giving it teeth.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** formally concludes this turn, with Ireland, the Netherlands, and Germany activating fast-grid vouchers that bind private hyperscalers to EU oversight in exchange for accelerated deployment. These agreements, structured under the Digital Markets Act, now lock in redundancy and audit rights for €42bn of private investment. The Resilience Hardening Directive also finishes, with ENISA launching compliance assessments in 18 member states — we link continued Modernisation Fund disbursements to audit outcomes, ensuring enforcement has bite. The US election brings promise of access, but we do not stand down: sovereignty is not a backup plan. We consolidate what we have built, and we harden it against the world as it is — not as we hope it to be.
