## In practice
This turn, we close the Resilience Hardening Directive with decisive action in the Council’s Internal Market and Consumer Protection formation. The Commission leverages REPowerEU grant conditionality and the €15bn Modernisation Fund disbursement to secure final adoption of delegated acts on patching cadence, agent-detection thresholds, and third-party audits. Member states resistant to compliance—particularly smaller economies fearing cost—are offered co-financing through cohesion instruments and technical assistance from the newly operationalised Cyber Resilience Task Force. Legal certainty is cemented by the publication of the Commission’s Article 114 TFEU opinion, which preempts legal challenges and enables immediate transposition. By mid-June, 18 member states have submitted national implementation plans, and ENISA begins its first round of compliance assessments under the new mandate.

Simultaneously, the Sovereign AI Talent Accelerator launches as a flagship initiative under the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe coordination. EURES activates a dedicated placement cell for frontier talent, working with EuroHPC JU to guarantee compute access for recruited researchers within six months of arrival. Golden visas are fast-tracked through a new Schengen-wide priority lane, backed by Frontex and national immigration authorities, while housing allowances are drawn from the Social Climate Fund’s mobility allocation. The first cohort targets 1,200 engineers, with emphasis on agent safety, red-teaming, and secure deployment—skills that feed directly into both sovereign capability and public-sector resilience. Germany, France, and the Netherlands commit national matching funds, turning this into a signal that Europe is not just building compute, but deciding who operates it.

The InvestAI Gigafactories continue site selection under crisis permitting, with grid access prioritised via the Crisis Response Instrument. Poland emerges as a dark horse candidate due to available baseload and political willingness to host dual-use infrastructure. Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package accelerates: Ireland, Finland, and the Netherlands activate their fast-grid-access vouchers, unlocking sub-18-month deployment timelines for private hyperscalers that agree to EU-controlled redundancy and audit rights. These efforts collectively sustain pressure on industrial delivery—but talent remains the bottleneck, and this turn, we begin to break it.
