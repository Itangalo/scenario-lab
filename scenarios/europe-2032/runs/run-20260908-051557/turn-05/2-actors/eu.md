## In practice
This turn, we lock in the first two InvestAI Gigafactory sites through binding memoranda with host member states, leveraging the Crisis Response Instrument to override local permitting bottlenecks and guarantee grid prioritisation. Poland and Finland emerge as lead candidates: Poland offers baseload redundancy and political willingness to host dual-use infrastructure, while Finland provides cooling efficiency and proximity to Arctic fibre routes. The Commission activates the €50bn first disbursement tranche under the InvestEU guarantee, with EuroHPC JU assuming operational oversight of compute allocation. We condition access on audit rights, sovereign control of weights, and mandatory red-teaming capacity embedded in each site’s governance — turning raw compute into enforceable strategic autonomy.

Simultaneously, the Tech Sovereignty Package accelerates as Ireland, the Netherlands, and Germany activate fast-grid-access vouchers, enabling private hyperscalers to deploy within 18 months in exchange for EU-controlled redundancy and audit rights. These agreements are structured through the Digital Markets Act enforcement framework, turning compliance into leverage. The Sovereign AI Talent Accelerator moves from announcement to operation: EURES launches a dedicated placement cell, Frontex clears golden visa lanes within 48 hours, and the Social Climate Fund disburses housing allowances for the first 300 recruits. France and Germany commit matching national funds, but academic resistance in the Netherlands persists — we respond by decentralising research placement while centralising compute access, preserving unity without overreach.

The Resilience Hardening Directive formally closes with ENISA launching compliance assessments in 18 member states. We tie continued Modernisation Fund disbursements to audit outcomes, ensuring enforcement has teeth. The cyber defence breakthrough — automated patching at attack speed — is rapidly integrated into the directive’s technical standards, upgrading the baseline for all critical operators. This, combined with the US election alliance, shifts the immediate risk calculus: access to frontier capability improves, but dependence deepens. We resist the temptation to stand down. The relief is real, but sovereignty cannot be outsourced — not even to allies. The next two years must turn infrastructure into agency.
