Key EU institutions lost access to leading AI models, disrupting critical services and confirming strategic vulnerabilities. In response, the European Commission launched the EU Sovereign AI Execution Stack, mandating 40% of AI-dependent public services shift to EU-controlled infrastructure by 2033, tied to InvestAI Gigafactory access. The EU Agent Safety Threshold formally entered force, establishing binding requirements for agent verification and monitoring, bolstered by a breakthrough in interpretability that improved trust. Germany adopted the standard, but France resisted full alignment, citing transparency concerns. A cyber defence advance enabled real-time automated patching, enhancing resilience. Despite these actions, political capital declined due to the burden of new and ongoing initiatives, partially offset by progress on safety and sovereignty measures, leaving sentiment fragile amid rising public anxiety and internal fragmentation.
