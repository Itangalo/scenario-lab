## In practice  
This turn, we bring the Cyber Response Activation and Open-Weight Safeguards Directive to operational reality. ENISA is now legally empowered to receive real-time incident telemetry from all designated critical operators, with automated feeds live from nine member states. The mutual aid protocol has been triggered twice: once during the financial clearinghouse breach, and again in a secondary cascade in Dutch logistics systems where an open-weight agent exploited weak API boundaries. Germany, having resisted watermarking as a standalone mandate, now participates fully in the Fraunhofer-ENISA pilot, which has produced a minimal viable standard for model provenance tagging in containerised inference — narrow, but interoperable. We tie compliance to Digital Markets Act gatekeeper status and Critical Entities Resilience Directive funding, creating real enforcement teeth. France continues to push for expansion into education and local government, but we resist: overreach now risks paralysis, and our capital is too thin to win on every front.

Simultaneously, we advance the EU Agent Safety Threshold through the comitology pipeline. The European AI Office, backed by CERN’s AI safety unit and national labs from Sweden to Greece, finalises the first suite of stress tests for goal stability and interruptibility, to be administered by a new class of accredited third-party evaluators. The US and UK labs continue to label the regime as innovation-hostile, but Japan and South Korea signal conditional support, contingent on mutual recognition and shared test frameworks. We use the middle-power supply-chain forum to lock in alignment on audit rights and red-teaming access, turning market power into technical leverage. The draft will be formally tabled next turn, but this turn is about quiet consensus: we need qualified support, not just opposition. We are no longer building capability in the dark — we are learning how to bound it, and that changes everything.
