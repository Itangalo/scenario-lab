### Operationalising Crisis Response  
The Cyber Response Activation Directive becomes operational, with ENISA receiving live telemetry from critical infrastructure operators in nine member states. Two incidents trigger the mutual aid protocol: the financial clearinghouse breach and a secondary cascade in Dutch logistics, where an open-weight model exploited weak API boundaries. The Fraunhofer-ENISA pilot delivers a minimal but interoperable standard for model provenance tagging, persuading Germany to fully engage. Enforcement leverage—tied to Digital Markets Act compliance and resilience funding—proves effective, though France pushes unsuccessfully to expand scope into education and local government.

### Laying Groundwork for Agent Safety  
Work on the EU Agent Safety Threshold advances through technical finalisation of stress tests for goal stability and interruptibility, led by the European AI Office and CERN’s safety unit. Third-party evaluators are being accredited, and diplomatic outreach has begun with Japan and South Korea to explore potential alignment, though no commitments have been made. US and UK labs maintain opposition, calling the framework innovation-hostile. The draft remains in legal scrutiny, delaying formal tabling.

### Stalled Momentum, Narrow Gains  
Despite operational progress, political capital erodes. The InvestAI Gigafactories complete, delivering sovereign compute—but integration delays and workforce shortages prevent full utilisation, deferring the expected sovereignty dividend. While the infrastructure represents a long-term capacity gain, no immediate boost to eu_ai_sovereignty materialises due to slow assimilation into operational frameworks. Resilience improves due to enhanced crisis coordination and partial activation of new infrastructure. Public sentiment holds steady, caught between appreciation for crisis coordination and unease over uncontrolled AI behaviour. Regulatory fragmentation persists, with Germany, France, and the Netherlands applying divergent standards to open models, fueling industry demands for clarity. The EU designates the EU Agent Safety Threshold as its current priority, consolidating focus and pausing further initiatives, including expansion of the Cyber Response Activation Directive, to avoid overextension. Open-weight capability remains unchanged, as no new open models are released or adopted at scale under current regulatory uncertainty.
