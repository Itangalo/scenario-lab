## New measure
Resilience Hardening Directive  
A binding directive requiring cyber hardening of all critical infrastructure operators across energy, water, transport and health sectors, with mandatory penetration testing and incident response plans.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`

## Priority

## In practice
This turn, we act on the fact that the frontier has already breached us. The cyber_test_shot was not an attack but a test — and it passed. We now know that current detection regimes failed, and that agents can persist undetected in industrial systems at scale. Our first move is not to build more walls at the border, but to make the territory behind them harder to conquer. Under Article 114 TFEU, the Commission will table a Resilience Hardening Directive, co-legislated with the European Parliament in the Internal Market and Consumer Protection (IMCO) and Environment, Public Health and Food Safety (ENVI) committees. The Council will take it up in the Competitiveness formation, where resistance from smaller member states fearing cost must be met with upfront disbursement from the Modernisation Fund and conditional access to REPowerEU grants.

We are advancing both inherited measures in parallel. For InvestAI Gigafactories, the Commission’s Investment Task Force is negotiating site selection with seven member states, prioritising locations with grid headroom and existing semiconductor clusters. The legal basis is Regulation (EU) 2021/241, as amended by the 2025 Crisis Response Instrument, allowing fast-tracking of projects tied to strategic autonomy. Resistance from regional authorities over energy allocation is being offset by guaranteed local employment clauses and direct revenue-sharing from compute leasing. Meanwhile, the Tech sovereignty package is moving through national parliaments to transpose accelerated permitting into law; we are offering fast-grid-access vouchers to early adopters, funded from the Digital Europe Programme.

The emergent_safety_consortium_formed is not yet a policy lever, but we are engaging its leads through the European Research Executive Agency to align their open-source evaluation framework with upcoming conformity assessments under the AI Act. This is groundwork, not action — but if it converges with our own technical agencies by turn 3, it could become the foundation of a Category 5 capability. For now, we focus on what only the Union can do: bind member states to common standards of operational resilience, using fiscal leverage and legal integration to close gaps no single country can afford to fix alone.
