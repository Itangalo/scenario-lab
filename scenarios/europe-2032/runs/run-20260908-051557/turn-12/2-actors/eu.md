## Priority
**EU Sovereign AI Execution Stack and Public-Use Deployment Mandate** — because the access denial event was not an anomaly but a structural exposure, and without binding demand-side leverage, our sovereign compute remains idle and our talent untested—this turn we shift from readiness to real use, or lose sovereignty by default.

## In practice
This turn, we act on the shock of being locked out. Hospitals in three member states lost access to diagnostic models overnight. Ministries could not run planning simulations. Firms built on foreign APIs faced sudden collapse. This was not a failure of safety or foresight—it was a failure of *presence*. We were cut off because we were not *there*. We had compute plans, not systems. Talent pipelines, not teams. Frameworks, not functions. No more.

We now bind public-sector AI adoption directly to our sovereign stack. Under Article 114 TFEU, we launch the EU Sovereign AI Execution Stack mandate, requiring EU institutions and member states to shift 40% of AI-reliant public services onto EU-operated infrastructure by 2023. The European AI Office, backed by the Digital Europe Programme and Cohesion Fund conditionality, will enforce compliance. We tie access to InvestAI Gigafactories in Poland and Finland to participation—no sovereign use, no sovereign compute. The train-to-deploy initiative from our previous measure becomes the core workforce: ten thousand red-teamers, integrators, and system monitors, fast-tracked through EIT Digital and defence corps secondments.

Germany agrees to pilot the stack in federal health AI, but France warns that mandating use before full safety certification risks public trust. We listen—but we act. The Agent Safety Threshold is now law, and its verification protocols are embedded into the stack’s deployment layer. This is not recklessness. It is *sovereign responsibility*: we will not wait for permission to exist. The offence-defence balance has shifted with the cyber_defence_breakthrough; we seize the moment to harden not just systems, but our right to run them. We are not building capacity to admire it. We are building it to *use*. And this turn, we begin.
