### The Momentum of Necessity

The European Commission, reeling from the sudden cutoff of foreign AI systems, doubled down on sovereignty. The **EU Sovereign AI Execution Stack** mandate entered force, compelling member states to shift 40% of AI-dependent public services—health, planning, legal—onto EU-controlled infrastructure by 2033. Access to InvestAI’s high-end compute became conditional on compliance, creating a powerful demand-side lever. Germany embraced the shift, launching federal health pilots. France, however, remained hesitant, warning that rushed deployment could undermine public trust despite the new Agent Safety Threshold.

### Building Safety from the Ground Up

In parallel, the EU launched a **pan-European autonomous system evaluation network**, a large category 5 measure aimed at boosting *ai_safety*. Designed to centralise testing and monitoring, it would integrate national agencies into a rapid-response unit with access to high-fidelity simulation environments. Though still in early stages, the initiative was framed as essential to verifying agent behaviour under the new safety law, especially as open models proliferated.

### Strain on the Surface

Despite the urgency, political capital eroded. The new large measure cost −3, the existing portfolio carried a −8 charge, and the named priority added another −1. Only a modest +2 from finishing the Agent Safety Threshold offset the drain, leaving *eu_political_capital* at 14.0. Public sentiment held steady—neither rising nor falling—as protests against data centres simmered and regulatory fragmentation deepened. Industry pressure for harmonisation grew louder, with legal challenges looming.

Meanwhile, *ai_capability* rose by 0.5, consistent with the slowing growth rate above 60. *openweight_capability* edged up to 54.0, as older models approached the closed frontier of three turns prior. The interpretability breakthrough and cyber defence gains helped *ai_safety* tick up slightly, but systemic risks remained.
