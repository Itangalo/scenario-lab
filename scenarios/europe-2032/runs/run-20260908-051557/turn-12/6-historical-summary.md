The EU accelerated its push for AI sovereignty following the disruption caused by foreign AI access losses, enforcing the EU Sovereign AI Execution Stack mandate requiring 40% of AI-dependent public services to transition to EU-controlled infrastructure by 2033, with access to InvestAI compute tied to compliance. Germany advanced federal health pilots in support, while France expressed caution over deployment speed and trust. To strengthen safety, the EU launched a pan-European autonomous system evaluation network—a major initiative to centralize testing and monitoring—amid rising open-weight model capabilities and ongoing safety improvements from interpretability advances and cyber defence upgrades. However, political capital declined to 14.0 due to high implementation costs, offset only slightly by completion of the Agent Safety Threshold. Public sentiment remained stagnant amid protests, regulatory fragmentation, and growing industry demands for harmonization, with legal challenges anticipated. AI capability and open-weight model performance continued rising gradually, narrowing the gap with closed models.
