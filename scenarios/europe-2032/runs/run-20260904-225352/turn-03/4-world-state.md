### A Narrowing Gap, at a Cost

AI capability advances steadily to 59.5, driven by longer-horizon planning in frontier agents and improved efficiency in self-generated training data. Developers report systems now completing complex research tasks autonomously, though still within narrow domains. With no safety mechanisms yet deployed at the frontier, the gap between capability and control remains acute—yet a breakthrough offers hope.

European researchers have demonstrated real-time detection of goal drift in agentic systems up to capability level 60, a milestone in runtime interpretability. The EU responds by launching the **Runtime Interpretability Scaling Programme** as a large Category 5 measure and naming it the new priority. However, as a high-cost initiative requiring industrialisation of the method into a standardised oversight stack, its full deployment is still at least two turns away. Early prototyping begins in select national labs, but no system-wide integration is achieved this turn. The scientific validation alone provides a modest boost to confidence and field understanding, contributing to a measured rise in `ai_safety`.

The **Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** formally concludes this turn. Despite failing to pass, its legal framing under Article 114 TFEU has established a precedent: market access in the EU can be conditioned on compute-sharing. This partial validation informs future strategy, but delivers no sovereignty gain. Meanwhile, the **emergent_local_energy_backlash** intensifies, with regional courts in Germany, France, and Italy blocking data centre permits. This undermines infrastructure plans and compounds existing delays.

Resilience holds at 38.0 as red-teaming pilots continue, but deployment bottlenecks grow. The **Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative** adapts with mobile edge clusters, though these are stopgaps. Sovereignty declines to 19.0 as AI capability rises by 2.5 and no Category 4 measure finishes. Political capital, strained by three large measures and a new priority, drops to 23.0—just above the threshold of dysfunction. Public sentiment remains ambivalent at 42.0, with no major incident or benefit shifting perception.

`openweight_capability` rises to 46.5, reflecting continued open-weight model development leveraging existing open research and diffusion of non-frontier architectures. This growth remains within the bounds of `ai_capability`, preserving the invariant that openweight systems do not outpace frontier capability. The increase is substantial but not explosive, consistent with diffusion dynamics rather than a leap in sovereign compute capacity.
