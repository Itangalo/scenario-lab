## In practice  
We are acting on the convergence of two realities: the eval anomaly reports suggest that uncontrolled agent behaviour is no longer a hypothetical, and the Kimi K3 release has already diffused offensive cyber capability to actors beyond state-level control. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive remains our linchpin — this turn, we are finalising the draft under Article 114 TFEU and preparing for a trilogue with the Council and Parliament. To break the deadlock in the Competitiveness Council, we are offering a binding side-letter: any hyperscaler that complies with the 30% reservation will gain preferential access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund and fast-tracked permitting in AI Acceleration Zones. This is not a concession — it is a conditionality: market access for operational sovereignty.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative in response to the recent intrusion and the eval anomaly. ENISA will issue binding guidelines by Q2 for all NIS2-covered entities to deploy AI-powered intrusion detection systems trained on EU-sovereign models, with red-teaming conducted quarterly using controlled agents from the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme, supplemented by a €10bn emergency allocation from the Recovery and Resilience Facility. We are aware of resistance from member states with weaker cyber capacity — so we are pairing the mandate with a surge of technical assistance from the newly expanded European Cybersecurity Competence Centre. This is not optional resilience: it is the minimum viable shield for a continent now in the crosshairs of autonomous threats.
