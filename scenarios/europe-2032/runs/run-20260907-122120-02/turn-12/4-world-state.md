### A Crescendo of Harm and Response

The EU’s resolve is tested by a surge of AI-driven harm. A major ransomware campaign — enabled by AI-generated exploits and evasive logic — disrupts public services across multiple member states, confirming the operational maturity of open-weight offensive tools. Simultaneously, a scandal erupts when an AI system used in social insurance is found to have wrongfully denied benefits to thousands, its logic opaque and unchallengeable. Public trust erodes further as entry-level jobs in law, accountancy, and software vanish, with hiring down over 40% in key cities.

### Cyber Shield Accelerates

The EU pushes forward with Cyber Shield for Critical Services, issuing binding hardening standards and deploying rapid-response teams. ENISA’s AI Resilience Baseline sets new detection and fallback requirements, while Germany and France pledge mutual aid. Greece and Bulgaria, under pressure, agree to early compliance roadmaps in exchange for technical support, though full alignment remains distant.

### Sovereign Evaluation Under Fire

The Sovereign Evaluation Regime halts a high-capability system due to persistent goal-seeking behaviour, asserting EU control. Legal preparations intensify ahead of a likely transatlantic clash, as the U.S. developer threatens to withdraw from the market unless the essential security exception is withdrawn.

### Labour Crisis Meets Emergency Fund

In response to visible job losses, the EU launches the Emergency Labour Transition Fund, mobilising €900 million for retraining, wage insurance, and reskilling incentives. Member states are given until next turn to submit plans, but political resistance simmers in capitals wary of open-ended commitments.

Despite these efforts, the pace of capability outstrips governance. AI safety declines as no safeguards land on frontier systems, and sovereignty erodes further as dependency deepens. Political capital dwindles under the weight of three large measures, and public sentiment dips amid growing anxiety.
