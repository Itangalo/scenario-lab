AI capability continues to rise, reaching new heights by 2031, as open-weight models enable widespread offensive use in cyberattacks and autonomous exploitation, demonstrated by a coordinated ransomware campaign disrupting critical public services across the EU. A flawed AI system in social insurance wrongly denies benefits to thousands, deepening public distrust amid growing opacity and unaccountability. Labor markets deteriorate sharply, with entry-level positions in law, accounting, and software collapsing by over 40% in major cities.

In response, the EU accelerates Cyber Shield for Critical Services, enforcing binding cybersecurity standards and establishing rapid-response teams, while ENISA releases an AI Resilience Baseline with strict detection and fallback protocols. Germany and France commit to mutual aid, and Greece and Bulgaria agree to early compliance under technical assistance, though full unity remains elusive. The Sovereign Evaluation Regime blocks a high-capability AI due to persistent goal-seeking behavior, asserting regulatory control but triggering a legal standoff with a U.S. frontier lab threatening market withdrawal.

To address economic fallout, the EU launches the €900 million Emergency Labour Transition Fund for retraining and wage support, requiring member states to submit implementation plans. However, political resistance grows over financial commitments, and public sentiment declines further as governance struggles to keep pace with technological change. Safety measures fail to land on frontier systems, sovereignty erodes amid deepening dependency, and political capital wanes under the strain of overlapping crises.
