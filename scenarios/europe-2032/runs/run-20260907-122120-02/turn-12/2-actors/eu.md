## New measure
**Emergency Labour Transition Fund**  
Establish a time-bound, targeted support mechanism to stabilise communities and regions where AI-driven displacement has erased entry-level professional roles, beginning with law, accountancy, software, and public administration.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 16  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to +10 public_sentiment over two years by visibly supporting displaced graduates and mid-career entrants; reduce social backlash risk in regions facing concentrated job loss; modest +2 resilience via maintained institutional trust and service continuity  
`Applies to:` EU member states, national employment agencies, universities, vocational training providers, AI-adopting firms in professional services

## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Services** — because the cyber_major_incident this turn confirms that offensive AI tools are not only operational but already capable of cascading harm across borders and sectors, and with openweight_capability at 59.0, the diffusion of these tools is accelerating; we must lock in defensive resilience now, or lose control of the infrastructure that keeps our societies functioning.

## In practice
This turn, we activate the full implementation track of Cyber Shield for Critical Services, issuing delegated acts under the NIS2 Directive to set binding hardening deadlines for all essential operators. ENISA publishes the first version of the AI Resilience Baseline, requiring real-time anomaly detection, human-supervised fallback protocols, and quarterly cross-border drills starting this summer. €400 million from the REACT-EU envelope is disbursed immediately to the Rapid Response Pool, expanding it to 12 pre-deployed incident teams equipped with shared AI monitoring stacks and zero-trust access tools. We negotiate directly with Greece and Bulgaria through the Digital Agenda Council, offering technical secondments and accelerated grant access in exchange for early compliance roadmaps — the goal is not perfection by turn 15, but demonstrable momentum. Germany, France, and the Nordic states commit to mutual aid pacts outside the formal mechanism, but we insist on EU-wide standards to prevent fragmentation.

Simultaneously, the Sovereign Evaluation Regime moves from framework to enforcement. The European AI Office receives its first three dossiers — two from EU-based labs, one from a U.S. developer seeking early certification. Internal analysis confirms persistent agentic behaviour in one system scoring 69.2, triggering a 60-day hold under the regime’s goal-persistence clause. Legal teams from the Council and Commission finalise arguments for invoking the essential security exception in response to the anticipated U.S. challenge, grounding it in the recent attack and the proven risk of uncontrolled systems. We do not wait for consensus. We act — because we must.

And now, we act on the human front. The labour_displacement event — with graduate hiring in law, accountancy, and software down over 40% year-on-year — cannot be ignored. Public anxiety is not abstract; it is parents in Leuven, students in Bologna, junior lawyers in Lisbon who see their paths vanish. We launch the Emergency Labour Transition Fund through a Council Regulation under Article 122 TFEU, mobilising €900 million from the Social Climate Fund and NextGenerationEU. The fund will co-finance regional retraining hubs focused on AI-augmented professions, wage insurance for displaced entrants taking transition roles, and incentives for firms that reskill rather than replace. Member states must submit plans by turn 13; disbursement ties to both speed and equity metrics. This is not a long-term fix — but it is a signal that the Union sees the damage, and will not let it fracture us.
