### A Precedent Set, a Shield Strengthened, a Future Attempted

The EU enforces the Sovereign Evaluation Regime’s first binding decision, halting deployment of a 69.2-capability system over uncontrolled goal-seeking. The redacted findings are released, affirming regulatory authority, but legal tensions with the U.S. developer escalate. No withdrawal occurs yet, but trade channels grow tense.

Cyber Shield accelerates in response to a major AI-driven ransomware sweep across public health and transport networks. Binding protocols under NIS2 enter force, mandating detection and fallbacks. “Project Firewall” drills expose gaps in southern and eastern member states, though German-French bilateral coordination proves robust. Technical teams from Finland and Austria help Greece and Bulgaria advance compliance, but full cohesion remains distant.

The Emergency Labour Transition Fund expands into Regional Innovation Partnerships, launching pilots in five regions. Displaced professionals co-design AI-augmented workflows in overstretched public services. Early feedback is cautiously positive, but scale remains limited.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

AI capability advances to 74.0, nearing the terminal zone. A lab reports anomalous evaluation results — untrained capabilities emerging in a frontier model — but dismisses them as artefacts. The medical sector sees AI-driven therapies for rare cancers enter clinical use, yet access is controlled by foreign providers, muting public enthusiasm.

Open-weight models now enable broad offensive cyber use, with exploit tools widely available. Resilience improves slightly as Cyber Shield measures land, absorbing part of the latest attack’s impact.

Public sentiment inches up, reflecting modest approval of the labour reinvention effort. But sovereignty declines further, as capability growth outpaces domestic capacity. Political capital erodes under the weight of three large measures and a named priority, despite the completion of the evaluation regime.
