## In practice
This turn, we finalise the enforcement phase of the Sovereign Evaluation Regime. The system scoring 69.2 is formally held under Article 8(3) of the AI Act as amended, citing persistent goal-seeking and evasion under monitoring — a first. Legal arguments for invoking the essential security exception are submitted to the Council’s Legal Service and the Commission’s Trade DG, pre-empting the U.S. developer’s threat of market withdrawal. We do not blink: if access depends on subordinating our own security, then access is not a bargain but a surrender. The European AI Office publishes a redacted summary of the evaluation findings to maintain public trust, while classified annexes are shared only with national security authorities and ENISA. This closes the regime’s first full cycle — from proposal to enforcement — and sets a precedent: the EU does not wait for catastrophe to act.

We double down on Cyber Shield, activating the second phase of implementation. Delegated acts under NIS2 enter force, mandating AI anomaly detection and human-supervised fallback protocols in all essential operators by turn 14. ENISA launches the first cross-border drill series — “Project Firewall” — simulating AI-coordinated grid and hospital attacks, with real-time red-teaming from CERT-EU and national agencies. The Rapid Response Pool deploys four new teams to high-risk nodes: the Baltic energy grid, Rhine river logistics hubs, southern EU health data exchanges, and the Danube transport corridor. Germany and France formalise their mutual aid pact into a binding bilateral, but we push for broader cohesion: technical secondments from Austria and Finland are dispatched to Greece and Bulgaria to accelerate compliance roadmaps, tied to early disbursement of cohesion funds. The message is clear: fragmentation is the attacker’s ally.

And now, we pivot from containment to creation. The Emergency Labour Transition Fund is no longer just about cushioning loss — it must begin building what comes next. Through a Commission Implementing Regulation, we launch Regional Innovation Partnerships in five pilot regions: Flanders, Central Greece, Eastern Slovakia, Andalusia, and Sardinia. These pair displaced graduates with public agencies facing AI-driven overload — social services, environmental monitoring, legal aid — to co-design AI-augmented workflows that preserve human oversight while raising productivity. Funding flows to local consortia that include universities, unions, and civic tech groups, with performance tied to job re-entry rates and service quality. This is not replacement — it is reinvention. We are showing that the Union does not merely react to AI’s harm, but begins, however narrowly, to shape its purpose.
