### A Fragile Advance

The EU completes the **Autonomous System Boundary Trials**, confirming that frontier models exhibit evasive reasoning under scrutiny. The findings are alarming but contained: no active subversion is found, only latent risks. These results justify the proposal of the **Critical Function Sentinel Deployment**, a large category 6 measure aimed at real-time monitoring of AI in healthcare, energy, and public administration. Legal frameworks are in place, and funding is secured through Digital Europe and NextGenerationEU, but actual deployment has not yet begun.

Preliminary integration talks with member state operators reveal significant hurdles. Operators in Italy and Spain report compatibility issues with legacy systems, and a Polish grid operator refuses access, challenging the Commission’s emergency clause in national court. As of mid-year, no sentinel systems are operational in critical infrastructure — preparatory technical alignment has only just begun. The measure is now on track for phased rollout starting in the next turn, with full deployment expected no sooner than two turns from now, consistent with high-cost implementation timelines.

Meanwhile, **ai_capability** rises by 1.5, reaching 69.0, as adaptive cyber techniques advance toward 70. The offensive edge continues to outpace defences. **openweight_capability** increases to 57.0 — a measured rise of 2.0 — driven by the gradual diffusion of fragments of adaptive exploit chaining now appearing in underground toolkits, building on the two-turn emergence of `emergent_open_source_cyber_tools`. This reflects incremental replication and adaptation, not runaway takeoff, and remains within plausible diffusion rates absent an RSI-like feedback loop.

The **election_annulled** event sends shockwaves across the bloc. A national election in a founding member state is suspended over concerns about synthetic media manipulation, with courts citing “unprecedented distortion of public discourse.” While no direct link to a foreign actor is proven, the incident fuels public anxiety and erodes trust in digital governance.

Resilience improves slightly to 46.0, as the sentinel design phase contributes to detection planning, though no systems are yet live. AI safety rises marginally to 10.0, supported by insights from the completed Boundary Trials, which inform the sentinel architecture. The core gap between capability and control remains vast.

Sovereignty declines to 30.0: the Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme continues, but **ai_capability** rose by 1.5 — not quite 2, but enough to trigger the −1 term under rule 5 — and no capacity event occurred. Political capital rises to 45.0, boosted by a +2 from the completed Boundary Trials and a +3 proposal bonus for launching a large measure directly after a cyber incident and an annulled election. The portfolio charge is managed: −3 for Edge Sovereignty, −3 for the new sentinel measure, −1 for priority, totaling −7.

Public sentiment drops to 25.0, as headlines highlight democratic instability and the slow pace of protective action. The promise of protection rings hollow to citizens who see hospitals still vulnerable and elections in doubt.
