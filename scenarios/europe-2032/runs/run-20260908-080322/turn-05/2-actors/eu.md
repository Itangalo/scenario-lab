## In practice
This turn, we are acting from the edge of breakdown. The **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation** has entered its make-or-break phase. In Lyon, French authorities have accepted co-ownership of the Saint-Genis-Pouilly site under condition of shared operational command and guaranteed energy allocation from RTE. We are deploying Commission task forces to Frankfurt and Warsaw with full Article 122 backing to override permitting delays, but resistance hardens: Bavarian officials warn that forced grid integration risks blackouts, while Polish courts have issued a temporary injunction citing unresolved land-use disputes. We are offering both governments equity stakes in the resulting infrastructure and guaranteed access to 15% of output for national use—concessions designed to convert opposition into partnership. If these negotiations fail by turn end, the initiative collapses into protracted litigation, and our sovereignty score stays frozen at 18.0.

Meanwhile, the **Public-Private Compute Assurance Compact** has formally concluded—but not as intended. Ireland withdrew at the final Council vote, demanding tax exemptions incompatible with state aid rules, and Finland signed only a non-binding memorandum. The compact is now marked **finished**, having delivered interpretability cooperation with Nordic operators but no enforceable capacity guarantees. Its partial legacy lives on in bilateral side agreements, but it failed the core test: securing reserved compute under EU law. With **InvestAI Gigafactories** still years from delivery and private investment evaporating, we cannot rely on market-led solutions. That is why we are launching the **Sovereign Talent Anchoring Initiative**: without people who can build and operate next-generation systems, even seized hardware is inert. Through Horizon Europe and the newly empowered European AI Board, we are creating 1,200 permanent research positions across six hubs—from Munich to Stockholm—with priority given to those working on alignment, agent safety, and open-weight verification. National governments will be required to streamline visa processes and provide housing allowances, funded jointly by the Recovery and Resilience Facility and new levies on high-margin AI deployments. This is not just recruitment—it is a bid to make the EU a jurisdiction where frontier work *wants* to happen, not just where it’s allowed.
