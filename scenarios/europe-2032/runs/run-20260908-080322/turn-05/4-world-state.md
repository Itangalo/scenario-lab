### The Fracture Point

A sudden leap in closed-model performance—driven by a breakthrough in recursive self-improvement—catapults global AI capability to 68.0. The release renders prior planning obsolete, triggering a **capability_jump** and exposing deep fragilities. With no matching safety advance, assurance plummets as developers scramble to understand systems that now outpace human oversight in reasoning speed and strategic depth.

Simultaneously, the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event lands with force: EU hospitals, research institutes, and public agencies lose access to the leading AI model overnight. No explanation is given. The cutoff fractures operations across critical sectors, from drug discovery to infrastructure planning. In Brussels, the move is seen not as a surprise but as an inevitability—yet one that the Union failed to prepare for. The **Public-Private Compute Assurance Compact**, once hailed as a shield, collapses into irrelevance: Ireland abstains from the final vote, Finland signs only a memorandum, and no binding capacity guarantees are secured. The initiative is marked complete—but as a partial failure.

The **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation** limps forward. Lyon’s site sees limited activation under joint French-Commission control, but in Germany and Poland, resistance hardens. Bavarian officials warn of grid instability; Polish courts issue a temporary injunction. Offers of equity and energy shares fail to break the deadlock. The effort consumes political capital without delivering capacity.

Worse, **backlash_physical** erupts: protesters occupy a data centre site near Munich, cutting power lines and injuring two officers. The incident fuels public anxiety, while **labour_displacement** accelerates—entry-level roles in law, accounting, and software vanish, with graduates locked out of the job market. Trust in AI erodes further.

In response, the European Commission begins drafting the **Sovereign Talent Anchoring Initiative**, a category 4 measure aimed at countering brain drain. However, with sentiment collapsing and political capital strained, the proposal remains in early consultation and will not be formally launched until conditions allow. No new measure is initiated this turn.

Meanwhile, the **middle_power_coalition** forms quietly—coordinating export controls and evaluation standards among EU, Japan, and South Korea—offering a sliver of leverage.

US voters elect a new president. The administration will take office next turn, and its posture will be determined then.
