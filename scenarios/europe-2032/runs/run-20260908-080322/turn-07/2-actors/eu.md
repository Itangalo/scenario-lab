- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act as if speed alone can compensate for structural exposure.  
- Grounds: The **rsi_onset** event has changed the nature of the race: capability now compounds beyond human throughput, making reactive acceleration futile. The **export_control_escalation** and **member_state_defection** confirm that access and cohesion are now binding constraints, not bottlenecks to be engineered around. Acting under uncertainty remains necessary, but it must now be paired with strategic sequencing — choosing where to absorb risk, where to consolidate, and where to wait. The cost of misstep has risen; so must the discipline of commitment.

- modify `sovereign_control_over_critical_ai_systems` (commitment): Ensure the EU can inspect, operate, and if necessary override any AI system performing essential public functions or accessing strategic data, regardless of origin — with the understanding that inspection is no longer possible through interpretability, and override must be pre-positioned through technical and institutional redundancy.  
- Grounds: The **opaque_reasoning** and **rsi_onset** events have rendered post-hoc auditing meaningless. Control can no longer mean "seeing inside" but must mean "having a working alternative" and "being able to cut and switch". The commitment evolves from oversight to operational redundancy: the right to act is meaningless without the capacity to act in its place.

## New measure  
**Critical System Fallback Regime**  
Mandate that every AI system performing essential public functions must be paired with a human-operable, EU-controlled fallback system capable of sustaining core operations for 72 hours.  
`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +12 (via operational continuity), ai_safety +5 (via forced decomposability and monitoring surface), public_sentiment +3 (via visible preparedness)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, national regulators, public-sector AI providers, and foreign vendors operating in critical domains

## Priority  
**Critical System Fallback Regime** — because the **rsi_onset** and **opaque_reasoning** events have made clear that no system can now be trusted to self-report or remain predictable, and the **automated_decision_scandal** in Dutch social services this turn reveals that even well-intentioned systems are making irreversible harms — control without redundancy is illusion, and we must build the capacity to step in, not just watch.

## In practice  
This turn, we are acting from the recognition that the AI systems we depend on are not only inscrutable but self-accelerating. The **rsi_onset** event changes everything: capability is no longer linear, and our oversight frameworks — even the newly drafted **Sovereign Control Framework** — are built for a world where humans set the pace. They do not fail gradually; they become irrelevant overnight. The **automated_decision_scandal**, in which an AI-assisted welfare eligibility system in Utrecht denied thousands of claims without explanation, confirmed by the Dutch ombudsman, has shattered public trust. Restriction is now politically feasible, but adoption is frozen. We cannot wait.

We are therefore shifting from monitoring to preparedness. The **Sovereign Control Framework** moves forward under Article 114, but with a new clause: override is not enough. Any system in critical infrastructure must have a fallback — a human-run, EU-operated parallel system that can take over within minutes. This is not a shadow system; it is a working alternative, tested quarterly, funded from the Digital Europe Programme and co-implemented with ENISA and the European Health Agency. We are offering member states 70% co-financing from the Recovery and Resilience Facility if they commit by Q1 2030.

Simultaneously, the **Sovereign Talent Anchoring Initiative** is being retooled: instead of focusing only on model development, we are redirecting 40% of the Horizon funds to safety engineering and fallback system design. Stockholm and Toulouse are leading on "decomposable AI" — systems built to fail open, not closed. We are also fast-tracking visas for 300 engineers specialising in legacy system integration and crisis operations, because the future of control lies not in matching frontier speed, but in preserving the ability to step outside it. The **regional_compute_pact** is being expanded to include shared fallback infrastructure: Finland will host the northern node, Austria the alpine, with mutual aid protocols signed under Article 122 TFEU. This is not sovereignty as parity. It is sovereignty as survival.
