### The Ground Shifts

The EU’s Emergency Resilience Surge concluded successfully, mandating intrusion detection and automated failover across 60% of critical infrastructure operators. While uneven, the baseline hardening has taken root, and resilience climbs as incident response drills begin. Yet the broader landscape fractures. A wave of capital flight — the **ai_investment_collapse** — gutted private AI investment, freezing data centre projects in Germany, Poland, and France. Promised build-outs evaporated, and with them, hopes for organic growth in EU compute capacity.

Simultaneously, tightening export controls — now applied so strictly that even allied developers face rationing — severed assumed access to frontier training runs. The **export_control_escalation** exposed the fragility of reliance on external supply chains, as even permitted transfers became bottlenecked by volume caps and compliance delays.

Amid this, the **campaign_backlash** gained electoral traction, with parties across the spectrum adopting AI skepticism. Workers displaced by automation, parents concerned about school bans, and activists targeting data centres found common ground. Public sentiment dipped further, as the AI-driven job losses and opaque decision-making in border control — recently exposed by ENISA’s pilot — fueled distrust.

In response, the EU pivoted. With private capital gone and access uncertain, it launched the **Public-Private Compute Assurance Compact**, targeting the few remaining viable hyperscalers. The deal offered regulatory stability and procurement priority in exchange for binding commitments: 20% of future compute reserved for sovereign workloads, data jurisdiction guarantees, and audit rights for safety enforcement. But early negotiations revealed resistance — operators demanded concessions on energy subsidies and liability shields, and member states clashed over enforcement scope.

The Accelerated Public Evaluation Programme continued, with interpretability tools uncovering hidden biases in asylum risk models. Still, this progress was overshadowed by the collapse in confidence. The Tech Sovereignty Package remained stalled, and sovereignty eroded further as capability outpaced infrastructure.

### A Narrowing Path

The Union acted, but not without cost. Political capital drained under sustained pressure, as the new compact struggled to gain traction and public sentiment soured. The pivot was necessary — but not yet sufficient.
