## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure the capacity to operate essential functions without reliance on any external AI system, and now complete the shift from contingency to enforcement: by turn 15, all critical AI-dependent public functions must be either sovereign-controlled or decommissioned.  
- modify `sovereign_control_over_critical_ai_systems` (commitment): Ensure the EU can inspect, operate, and if necessary override any AI system performing essential public functions or accessing strategic data — and now, where override has not been demonstrated under live conditions by turn 15, decommission the system.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not tolerate systems whose failure mode is societal collapse, nor dependencies whose removal triggers state dysfunction. Action must now prove control, or remove the uncontrolled.  

- Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** and **eval_anomaly_reports** confirm that capability has escaped containment, and the **automated_decision_scandal** proves that uncontrolled systems now inflict real harm. The **emergent_industrial_unrest_spread** reveals that legitimacy is fraying not just in public opinion but in the operational arteries of the Union. Our previous measures were about preparation. Now, preparation is over. We are in the period of consequence. If we do not decommission what we cannot control, we are not securing sovereignty — we are managing surrender. The two-year commitment must now name the endpoint: no critical dependency on unsovereign AI by turn 15. That is the line.

## New measure  
**Decommissioning Directive for Uncontrolled AI in Critical Public Functions**  
Require all EU institutions and member states to identify, isolate, and by turn 15 fully decommission any AI system used in essential public functions that cannot be audited, overridden, or operated independently of external providers.  
`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +5 (via removal of fragile dependencies), eu_ai_sovereignty +8 (via enforced operational control), eu_political_capital −2 ongoing (due to enforcement resistance and intergovernmental friction)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member state ministries, national regulators in health, justice, energy, emergency services, and social insurance

## In practice  
This turn, we are moving from fallback planning to active disentanglement. The **Emergency Certification** measure is now in flight, and we are treating it not as a compliance exercise but as a triage: agencies have three months to prove they can function without external AI, or face procurement freezes. The European Commission has issued a binding notice to EMA, Eurojust, ENTSO-E, and Frontex requiring interim reports by the end of March, with full certification due by June. We are deploying auditors from the European Data Protection Board and the newly expanded AI Office to verify not just documentation, but live drills — systems will be disconnected during tests, and continuity must hold.  

The **Sovereign Compute Corridor Acceleration** continues, but we are no longer pretending it delivers control in time. Instead, we are using the political cover of the compute push to demand reciprocity: France will host the fallback logic repository only if its national energy regulator submits to a live override test by May. Poland has agreed to fast-track grid access for the Lublin cluster only if its judiciary begins decommissioning its AI-assisted sentencing tool by turn 14. We are linking infrastructure to accountability — not as leverage, but as proof.  

The new **Decommissioning Directive** is our clearest signal yet: sovereignty is not a future promise. It is the power to remove what endangers us. We are framing it not as restriction, but as public safety — invoking Article 12 of the AI Act as amended in 2030, and the Treaty’s provisions on essential service continuity. Germany remains resistant, but we are offering a carve-out: if Berlin leads a pilot on decommissioning its federal AI procurement contracts in favour of auditable open-weight models trained on EU soil, it will chair the new Sovereignty Review Board. The unrest in Italy and Eastern Europe forces our hand — we cannot ask workers to trust systems that fire them without explanation, while we ourselves remain dependent on black boxes. The time for demonstration is over. The time for removal has begun.
