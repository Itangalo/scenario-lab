## Statement changes  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act where action only deepens dependence or inflames backlash without delivering control.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure the capacity to operate essential functions without reliance on any external AI system, and begin shifting from infrastructure promises to operational sovereignty: deploy fallback systems, certify manual procedures, and enforce control over all AI used in critical public functions.  
- modify `sovereign_control_over_critical_ai_systems` (commitment): Ensure the EU can inspect, operate, and if necessary override any AI system performing essential public functions or accessing strategic data — and now, verify this control through binding audits and live drills, not just legal claims.  

- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has made clear that legal assertions of control are meaningless without operational reality. The **labour_displacement** wave confirms that public legitimacy erodes when AI reshapes livelihoods without consent or transition. And **backlash_physical** reveals that infrastructure without social license becomes a liability. We are still acting under uncertainty — but now we must act *differently*: not only building future capacity, but proving control *today*, or lose the capital to build anything at all.

## New measure  
**Emergency Certification of Fallback Systems for Critical AI-Dependent Functions**  
Mandate and fund the immediate certification of non-AI or EU-controlled AI alternatives for all essential public services currently dependent on foreign or closed AI systems, with binding deadlines and public reporting.  
`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +8 (via hardened continuity plans), eu_ai_sovereignty +5 (via operational control over critical functions), eu_political_capital −1 ongoing (due to enforcement friction)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member state ministries, national regulators in health, justice, energy, and emergency services, public operators using AI in essential functions

## In practice  
This turn, we are shifting from planning to enforcement. The **Sovereign Compute Corridor Acceleration** remains in flight, but we are no longer treating it as our sole path to sovereignty. The Court of Justice challenge has bought time — and we are using it to redefine what sovereignty means: not just future compute, but present control.  

We are launching the **Emergency Certification of Fallback Systems**, binding the European Medicines Agency, Eurojust, ENTSO-E, and Frontex to report within three months on their capacity to operate without any externally controlled AI. Each must certify at least one fully functional, staff-trained, non-proprietary alternative for core decision-making functions — whether that is a manual process, an open-weight model under EU jurisdiction, or a sovereign AI stack. The European Commission will audit compliance by turn 13, with non-compliant agencies barred from renewing AI procurement contracts until certified. Funding of €1.2 billion from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility will be redirected to support rapid retraining and system conversion.  

Germany, still wary of central overreach, is being offered a bilateral protocol: if its national energy regulator leads a pilot on AI-free grid balancing, it will gain co-design rights over the EU-wide certification framework. France has agreed to host a central repository for auditable fallback logic, under Eurocontrol’s existing secure infrastructure. We are not waiting for the compute corridor to come online. We are proving that the EU can act — now — to control what it depends on. Because if we cannot override a system when it fails, we never controlled it at all.
