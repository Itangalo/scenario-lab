### Closing the Gap, One Step at a Time

The EU pushes forward on two critical fronts. The **Emergency Resilience Surge** enters its final phase, with ENISA finalising binding standards for intrusion detection and automated failover across energy, water, and transport operators. A €12bn reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme and cohesion funds unlocks 70% co-financing, accelerating adoption. Rapid-assessment teams deployed to Hungary and Slovakia help resolve technical disputes through collaboration, avoiding immediate legal escalation. While full compliance remains uneven, early hardening measures are now in place at 60% of designated sites, yielding measurable improvements in detection and response readiness.

Simultaneously, the **Accelerated Public Evaluation Programme** is launched in response to a major interpretability breakthrough from a leading research lab. The EU fast-tracks €3.5bn in funding through DG CONNECT and the European Research Council to operationalise real-time reasoning monitoring in public-sector AI systems. ENISA, bolstered by new technical capacity, begins piloting these tools in border control and social services. Coordination with France’s AI Safety Institute and Germany’s BSI aims to create a unified evaluation framework, marking a step toward independent oversight.

Despite these advances, challenges persist. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** remains stalled, with no new compute capacity online. Private investment waits for clearer signals, and land-use disputes in Germany and Poland drag on. The continued rise in **ai_capability**—now at 57.0—outpaces gains in safety and infrastructure, straining sovereignty.

### A Fragile Balance

The interpretability breakthrough delivers a much-needed boost to **ai_safety**, lifting it to 32.0 as evaluation pipelines begin integrating the new techniques. However, this is not yet systemic assurance—only early deployment. Public trust, meanwhile, remains stagnant at 37.0, as the recent social insurance scandal continues to resonate in media and political discourse.

Political capital erodes further. The portfolio cost remains high, and the new measure, while timely, does not fully offset ongoing strains. The Union’s ability to act decisively is tested, even as it avoids overreach by maintaining focus on existing priorities.
