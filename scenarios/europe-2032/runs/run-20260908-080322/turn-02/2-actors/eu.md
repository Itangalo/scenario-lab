## Priority
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because resilience remains below baseline, the openweight frontier has reached offensive cyber capability, and we are still reeling from a domestic incident that proved both technical fragility and institutional distrust; hardening critical systems is not optional, and this turn is its last full window before completion.

## In practice
This turn, we act on two fronts: one defensive, one opportunistic. On the defensive front, the Emergency Resilience Surge enters its decisive phase. ENISA finalises emergency standards for intrusion detection and automated failover, which are now binding under Article 114 TFEU for all essential operators in energy, water, and transport. We break the budget logjam by reallocating €12bn from the Digital Europe Programme and cohesion funds, with 70% co-financing now unlocked. The Commission deploys rapid-assessment teams to Hungary and Slovakia to resolve implementation disputes through bilateral technical support, not legal coercion — our aim is compliance, not confrontation. Still, we prepare contingency enforcement through the CJEU docket, knowing that cohesion in crisis depends on perceived fairness.

On the offensive, we seize the interpretability breakthrough. While the result emerged from a private lab, its open release gives us a rare chance to close ground without waiting for industrial scale. We direct DG CONNECT and the European Research Council to fast-track a €3.5bn initiative — folded into existing research envelopes where possible — to operationalise the technique across public evaluation pipelines by turn 4. This is not just research: it is the beginning of an independent EU capacity to *see into* high-capability models, not just test their outputs. We task a newly reinforced ENISA with piloting real-time reasoning monitors in public-sector deployments, starting with border control and social services. This is not sovereignty of compute, but sovereignty of understanding — and in a world where capability advances without safety, understanding may be the last line of control we have. We coordinate with France’s AI Safety Institute and Germany’s BSI to align standards, ensuring this becomes a Union capability, not a patchwork.
