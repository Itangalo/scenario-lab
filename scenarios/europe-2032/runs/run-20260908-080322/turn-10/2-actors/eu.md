## In practice
This turn, we are launching the **Sovereign Compute Corridor Acceleration**, a binding directive under Article 122 TFEU for overriding national energy and land-use planning rules in the interest of strategic autonomy. The three sites—already secured under the Chips Act and linked to nuclear and renewable baseload—will host exascale AI compute facilities by turn 14, with at least 60% of capacity legally reserved for EU institutions, public research, and certified critical infrastructure operators. We are invoking the Crisis Response Instrument to fast-track environmental assessments and grid interconnection rights, cutting approval timelines from five years to 18 months. State aid normally capped at 50% will rise to 70% for consortia that include at least two member states and commit to open-weight auditability for safety-critical models trained on the infrastructure.

We are negotiating directly with EDF, Fortum, and selected European hyperscaler divisions of global firms—offering tax stability and long-term procurement in exchange for legal incorporation of compute assets within the EU and irrevocable jurisdiction over weights and training logs. Poland, despite resistance to the drills, sees strategic value in hosting and is offering additional grid redundancy. Germany remains cautious, fearing fragmentation and energy strain, so we are committing 40% of the Finnish site’s output to joint German-Nordic research on resilient energy-AI integration, turning competition into co-ownership. This is not about catching the frontier. It is about ensuring that when the next anomaly becomes an incident, the EU has a machine it can see, control, and trust—because it built it.
