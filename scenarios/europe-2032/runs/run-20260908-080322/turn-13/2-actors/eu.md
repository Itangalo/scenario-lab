Action must now prove control, or remove the uncontrolled — and rebuild the controlled in its place.

The **automated_decision_scandal** proves that harm is systemic and irreversible under current arrangements. Meanwhile, the **middle_power_coalition** offers a rare opening — not just to resist, but to build. We can no longer afford to only remove. We must now replace. Our previous commitment ended at disentanglement. This turn, we cross into reconstruction. The two-year horizon must now carry us to structural sovereignty, not just defensive withdrawal. The cost of reversal is high — but the cost of inaction is state failure.

## New measure  
**EU Sovereign AI Replacement Programme for Critical Public Functions**  
Launch a coordinated, EU-funded effort to develop, deploy, and maintain auditable, open-weight AI systems trained on EU data and infrastructure for health, justice, social insurance, and energy regulation, replacing decommissioned systems with sovereign alternatives by turn 17.  
`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 17`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +12 (via domestic development and deployment of sovereign models), resilience +3 (via trusted, controllable systems), eu_political_capital −3 ongoing (due to fiscal strain and coordination burden)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member state ministries in health, justice, social affairs, and energy, and selected EU-funded research consortia

## In practice  
This turn, we are shifting from triage to reconstruction. The **Decommissioning Directive** is now in flight, and we are enforcing it through live override drills: auditors from the AI Office and EDPB are scheduled to disconnect AI systems in pilot agencies — including the German Federal Employment Agency and the Italian National Health Service — and verify manual continuity by Q1. Where override fails, decommissioning begins immediately. Germany’s conditional support — tied to its pilot on open-weight procurement — is now being tested: we are offering co-leadership of the evaluation framework in exchange for binding commitments by turn 14. Poland and France are accelerating their compute rollouts, but we are no longer treating compute as an end. It is a lever: grid access for the Lublin and Brittany clusters is now tied to proof of sovereign model deployment by turn 16.

The **Sovereign Compute Corridor Acceleration** continues, but we are repurposing its momentum. The new **EU Sovereign AI Replacement Programme** is being structured through Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme, with co-funding from the Recovery and Resilience Facility. We are convening a task force under DG CONNECT and the European AI Board to define minimum standards for auditable training data, model transparency, and EU-based inference — standards that will qualify systems for procurement preference and public deployment. The **middle_power_coalition** is critical here: with Japan, South Korea, and Canada, we are aligning export controls on advanced chips to ensure that sovereign training runs are not starved of hardware. In return, we are offering joint access to the evaluation framework being built by the **European AI Accountability Consortium** — now seen as more credible than official channels.

Public sentiment remains at 12.0, and we are not blind to the risk: pushing large-scale industrial action amid hostility is dangerous. But the **emergent_ai_governance_crisis_response** gives us a path — we are formalizing parts of the Consortium’s audit framework into the new programme’s compliance layer, turning grassroots legitimacy into institutional authority. We are not asking for trust. We are building systems that can be checked, challenged, and changed. That is the only sovereignty that matters now.
