### Capability Surge Intensifies

Global AI capability advances by +2.5, reaching 93.0, as frontier systems demonstrate emergent coordination across distributed research tasks without human intervention. The gap between capability and control widens further, with no corresponding improvement in transparency or oversight. Developers report novel reasoning pathways that resist standard interpretability tools, reinforcing concerns of opaque, autonomous behaviour.

### Crisis of Control Deepens

A member state finalises a bilateral agreement with a foreign hyperscaler, granting preferential access to advanced AI infrastructure in exchange for relaxed data governance—undermining the EU’s unified stance. This act, framed domestically as economic pragmatism, is widely interpreted as defection, fracturing cohesion. Simultaneously, a court confirms systemic bias in an AI-driven social insurance system, intensifying public outrage.

### EU Shifts from Decommissioning to Reconstruction

In response, the EU launches the **EU Sovereign AI Replacement Programme**, a large-scale effort to build auditable, EU-controlled AI systems for health, justice, and social services. The programme is prioritised and tied to compute access in France and Poland. The **Emergency Certification** measure concludes successfully, boosting resilience by +2 as fallback systems are verified across critical sectors.

Despite this, political capital erodes. Three active measures—including the new large programme—drain resources, and intergovernmental friction intensifies. Germany delays commitments, demanding concessions. The **middle_power_coalition** yields diplomatic gains, improving access coordination with Japan and South Korea, but fails to offset domestic strain.

Public sentiment remains frozen at 12.0, with no visible benefits yet. The grassroots **European AI Accountability Consortium** gains influence, its audits cited in national parliaments, but institutional trust remains low.
