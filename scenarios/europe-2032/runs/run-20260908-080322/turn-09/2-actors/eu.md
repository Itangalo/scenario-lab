## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure the capacity to operate essential functions without reliance on any external AI system``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The current trajectory confirms that even advanced oversight and fallback design are insufficient when systems are opaque, uncontrollable, and embedded by dependency. At `ai_capability` 80.5 and `openweight_capability` 67.0, the frontier is accelerating beyond human-led verification, and interpretability advances have not yet scaled to deployed systems. The EU’s sovereignty remains at 10.0, indicating total structural dependence. The **Fallback System Readiness Certification** revealed that readiness is not guaranteed by law alone — it must be exercised, funded, and institutionally owned. The new commitment shifts from preparing to act independently to *routinely operating* independently in core domains, making autonomy a lived condition, not a contingency.

## New measure
**Operational Autonomy Drills for Core Public Functions**  
Launch mandatory biannual drills simulating extended AI unavailability across welfare, energy, emergency response, and border control, requiring full manual operation by national and EU crisis units using non-AI tools and procedures.  
`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +10 (via institutionalised readiness), eu_political_capital +2 (if successful, as proof of functional sovereignty), public_sentiment +3 (if visible and credible)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, national crisis agencies, public-sector operators in essential domains

## Priority
**Operational Autonomy Drills for Core Public Functions** — because the **Fallback System Readiness Certification** showed that certification without routine, high-stakes practice leads to complacency, and because the **emergent_sovereign_ai_alliance** offers a coalition to co-develop the drills, this measure turns resilience from a technical requirement into a lived capability, directly fulfilling the new two-year commitment.

## In practice
This turn, we are closing out the **Sovereign Control Framework** and **Critical System Fallback Regime** — both now law, but neither yet proven. The Framework established override rights, but no member state has tested them. The Regime mandated fallbacks, but most remain unstaffed and untrained. We are not building new rules; we are forcing them into practice. The **Operational Autonomy Drills** will be co-designed with the emergent Sovereign AI Alliance, pooling Nordic and Alpine civil protection expertise to build a shared scenario library and evaluation standard. ENISA and the European Emergency Response Coordination Centre will co-lead, using the Civil Protection Mechanism to fund cross-border participation.

We are allocating €1.8bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Innovation Fund, with 75% co-financing for drills that meet EU-wide stress criteria — including total blackout in automated border checks during peak migration flows, manual dispatch of grid-balancing reserves during heatwaves, and paper-based welfare disbursement under fraud surge conditions. Germany and Poland remain resistant, but the Alliance’s momentum and framing as civil protection — not AI policy — are opening doors. We are offering to exempt nations that achieve two consecutive successful drills from certain digital compliance audits, turning readiness into a currency of trust. This is not about surviving collapse. It is about proving we never needed the machine in the first place.
