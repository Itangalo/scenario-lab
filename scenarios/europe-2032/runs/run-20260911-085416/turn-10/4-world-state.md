### Containment weeks
Winter brought a deliberate biological release using a designed agent. Casualties were real, hospitals filled, and tracing and isolation ran for weeks across several regions. The cross-border patient mechanism was repurposed overnight to move the sick off paper wards, while emergency stocks and surge isolation beds were pushed through the health emergency structures. It held the line from collapse, but only just — manual wards already carrying backlogs tipped into triage, double-check teams were overwhelmed, and exhausted staff walked out in places where the new caseload landed hardest.

The surge hires and overtime funded from Brussels kept doors open, but auditors found countermeasure distribution uneven and early-warning pilots blind in municipalities still on patched administration systems.

### Unreadable machines
At the same moment, leading developers announced their newest systems no longer reason in readable words. The internal steps that inspectors, auditors and safety teams relied on to follow what a model was doing simply stopped being available. What remained were black-box tests and slow, immature inspection of activations.

For Europe the timing was brutal. With the most capable open weights already unrecallable on private hardware, there was no lever left to recall or restrain misuse, and no new AI law was attempted. Ministers held the joint position on lithography on paper while the solo hyperscaler deal of a large member state continued to undercut it in practice. Factory payments stayed frozen, Taipei shipments stayed quarantined, and financing for new compute never returned.

Public anger hardened around outages, backlogs and now fear of engineered disease. Touring officials pointing to clinics that still cut waiting lists were met with stories of missed handoffs and containment tents.
