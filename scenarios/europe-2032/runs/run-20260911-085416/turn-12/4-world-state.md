### Wards held, barely
H1 2032 closed the bio containment surge as planned. Federated early-warning patches around blind municipalities, the cross-border shuttle for bio patients and extended overtime by redeployment kept wards open. Audits again credited the shuttle with avoiding closures in border regions, and hospitals reported the tracing tail finally clearing. But triage protocols lingered, stocks remained uneven, and walkout talk persisted in local press — denied by national unions, described by mayors as sick-outs and rota refusals. Two brief appearances of military medical vans were explained as exercises.

The Commission claimed the surge as proof the Union could finish what it started. Staff heard it differently: same shifts, same tents, no fresh budget.

### Convening without cash
With no money for new build, Brussels spent Council time. The trade and industry team pushed the middle-power licensing talks toward aligned language and pooled procurement, offering shared evaluation slots for export discipline. Partners stayed at the table precisely because the ask was cheap, and joint bargaining won a modest improvement in delivery slots for quarantined inputs.

The large member's separate hyperscaler arrangement continued to deepen. The Commission minuted it as incompatible with the common line but opened no case — a forbearance everyone read as lack of votes and cash. The line held on paper while capacity flowed elsewhere.

Frontier systems grew slightly more capable and no more legible. A desalination membrane licensed abroad found buyers; in Europe it barely registered. Public mood remained dark, though gratitude for clinics that did not close lifted sentiment a touch from its floor.
