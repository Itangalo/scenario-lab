### A Shift in Doctrine

The EU formalises its response to the Swiss loss-of-control incident, retiring the Loss-of-Control Containment Framework after its limited rollout. Poland and three energy TSOs now host live circuit-breaking cells under ENISA coordination, a modest but operational gain in detection and response. Resilience rises as a result, absorbing part of the shock from ongoing systemic risks.

But containment is no longer enough. The European Commission proposes the **Mandatory Cognitive Sovereignty Audits**, a sweeping new regime targeting AI agency in financial, energy, and governance systems. The proposal would empower a strengthened European AI Safety Lab in Seville to conduct evaluations of model weights and agent memory, requiring proof of goal stability and resistance to unauthorised action. The move marks a doctrinal shift: from treating AI as a tool to treating it as an actor.

However, the measure is still in proposal phase. Full implementation will require legal validation, additional staffing, and technical infrastructure not yet in place. Emergency powers are invoked under existing crisis provisions — narrowly justified by cumulative incidents including `emergent_grid_instability_surge` and `emergent_synthetic_election_interference` — but the status of judicial scrutiny is not yet confirmed, and the pathway forward remains uncertain. No audits have yet been conducted; design work and stakeholder consultations begin this turn.

### Resistance and Repercussions

France and Germany express conditional support, but insist on phased application and exemptions for joint ventures with non-EU partners. U.S. hyperscalers state they will comply only if required to access the EU market, pending clarity on enforcement mechanisms — acknowledging regulatory leverage, not automatic jurisdiction. Hungary files a formal protest, citing breaches of bilateral agreements and sovereignty, while accelerating its off-grid AI integration with American partners outside the scope of any EU audit.

Public sentiment dips as private sector adoption slows and media frame the proposed audits as a brake on innovation. The promised benefits—architectural assurance, sovereign oversight—are abstract; the delays, tangible.

Meanwhile, AI capability climbs steadily to 71.5, driven by agentic efficiency gains in research and finance. Openweight capability increases slightly to 59.5, reflecting the ongoing diffusion of sparse architectures through underground networks, though integration remains fragmented and experimental. The open-weight ecosystem shows early signs of cohesion, but not yet at scale.

Sovereignty erodes to 20.0. The capability rise costs −1, and Hungary’s de facto defection—deepening integration with external compute providers—counts as `member_state_defection`, another −1. No sovereign compute gains offset the loss.

Political capital holds at 31.0 but is strained: the new large measure costs −3, the priority another −1. A +2 boost from finishing the containment framework is nearly erased by a −2 hit from falling public sentiment and lingering fragmentation.
