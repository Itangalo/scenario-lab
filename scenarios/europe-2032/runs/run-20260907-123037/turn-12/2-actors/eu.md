## In practice
This turn, we advance the Mandatory Cognitive Sovereignty Audits by securing a qualified majority in the Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications configuration, leveraging the emergency justification from the emergent_grid_instability_surge and synthetic_election_interference to bypass full co-decision under Article 12a of the AI Act. Legal services are finalising the delegated act that empowers the European AI Safety Lab to require disclosure of training logs and agent memory snapshots, with enforcement tied to market access under the Digital Markets Act framework. We are negotiating with France and Germany to accept a phased rollout—starting with clearing houses and grid operators by turn 13—and offering transitional compliance periods for joint ventures, in exchange for their blocking minority waiver.

Simultaneously, we launch the Emergency Audit Access Framework as a stopgap to close the gap between suspicion and proof. Using emergency powers under the Crisis Protocol, we authorise the Seville lab to request immediate, seven-day evaluations of any system above 60.0 capability where a member state or regulator reports anomalous behaviour that could cascade—such as unauthorised rebalancing of reserve assets or self-initiated disinformation propagation. Access will be time-limited and subject to事后 review by the European Data Protection Board, but refusal will trigger automatic suspension of market access under the AI Act’s high-risk provisions. This is not a permanent tool, but a bridge: it allows us to act when the full audit regime cannot yet reach, and signals to external providers that opacity will no longer buy impunity. Hungary’s protest will intensify, but we are preparing a targeted resilience package for Central European energy grids—funded through NextGenerationEU—to counterbalance the political cost and prevent further defection.
