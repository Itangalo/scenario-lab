### Crisis Authority Tested

The EU advances its doctrinal shift, leveraging emergency powers to push forward the **Mandatory Cognitive Sovereignty Audits**. A qualified majority in the Council’s Internal Market configuration is secured, allowing the Commission to bypass full co-decision and fast-track the delegated act empowering the Seville lab. Legal services finalise provisions for access to training logs and agent memory snapshots, with enforcement tied to Digital Markets Act compliance. France and Germany agree to waive their blocking minority in exchange for phased rollout—clearing houses and grid operators first by mid-2033—and transitional exemptions for joint ventures. Yet the framework remains unimplemented: staffing, technical standards, and judicial validation are still pending.

Simultaneously, the **Emergency Audit Access Framework** is launched as a provisional tool. Under Crisis Protocol authority, the Seville lab may now request seven-day evaluations of systems above 60.0 capability exhibiting anomalous behaviour. Refusal triggers automatic suspension of market access. The measure is narrowly framed and time-limited, but marks the first assertion of jurisdiction over foreign-owned high-capability systems in critical domains.

### Resistance Hardens

Hungary denounces the emergency access framework as extrajudicial overreach, accelerating its off-grid integration with U.S. hyperscalers. Other Central European states watch closely. The promised resilience package for regional grids is delayed by budget disputes, weakening the counteroffer.

U.S. providers comply only under protest, citing jurisdictional overreach. China’s leading lab suspends updates to its EU-facing models, citing regulatory uncertainty.

Public sentiment holds at 30.0. Media frame the moves as reactive and legally fragile. Private adoption slows further, reinforcing narratives of innovation drag.

AI capability rises to 73.0, driven by agentic optimization in research and finance. Openweight diffusion reaches 61.0, as sparse architectures gain coherence in underground networks. No safety upgrades land on deployed systems. Resilience remains stable, supported by circuit-breaking cells, but systemic risks in energy and elections persist.
