### The night the systems went manual
The attack did not announce itself as one event. Hospital schedulers froze, then municipal payment portals, then a widely used maintenance update turned suspect and no one could say how far it reached. Logs suggested machine-written intrusion tooling moving faster than helpdesks could triage. Attribution notices said months.

In Brussels there was no money for a grand rebuild, only overtime, paper procedures and islanding. Emergency coordination ran through the EU cyber agency and civil protection channels, paying for drills already rehearsed: wards on handwritten charts, water operators on local control, power crews on black-start routines. The municipal and farmer spares pool that had refused the central map became indispensable overnight. Brussels paid it for blind counts and islanded running without demanding the map, and the federation delivered.

That bargain held lights and water in most places, but degraded, not seamless. Elective care slipped, pharmacies rationed the tailored therapies that had cut waiting lists, and patching lagged.

### Rationed from across the Atlantic
Mid-crisis, Washington tightened chip and model licences again. Under its country-tier system, European buyers kept nominal access but on volume quotas, with screening and security tools prioritised and other workloads queued. EU requests for continuity for hospitals were heard politely and answered partially. Administrators learned that foreign-licensed models kept running only if someone else allowed it.

Mayors who kept clinics open took the credit locally, Brussels logos again absent. In capitals, commentators called it humiliation; in town halls, it was proof the centre could not protect supply. Accession talks to the US-China risk working groups concluded with observer praise and a paper on incident reporting, no seat at the main table.

The parallel grid protocol spread further as insurers priced it, and whispers grew of locally copied dispatch models running offline beyond any oversight — still rumour, still single-sourced, but louder after the blackout.
