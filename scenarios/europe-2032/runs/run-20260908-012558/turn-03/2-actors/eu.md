## In practice  
We are acting on three fronts: building future capacity, hardening response, and now, seizing control of what we already have. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme remains on track, with site finalisation expected by turn 3. Contracts are being structured through the European Investment Bank, and we are using state aid flexibility under Article 107(3)(c) to de-risk private participation. Grid upgrades in Finland and Spain are advancing under TEN-E fast-tracking, though Poland continues to resist binding commitments on energy allocation. We are meeting local opposition with binding job guarantees and regional reinvestment clauses, funded from the Gigafactory envelope, to convert resistance into partnership.

The Tech sovereignty package is under negotiation in the Competitiveness Council. We are offering member states co-investment in grid and cooling infrastructure — one euro from them, two from Brussels — in exchange for accepting accelerated permitting and public benefit clauses: 15% capacity reservation for public research and crisis response, and adherence to open interconnection standards. The US is conditioning access; we must condition ours. But legal delays in the Council have stalled the Emergency Response Corps, so we are now bypassing inertia by acting on what is already in our domain: existing compute.

This turn, we are invoking emergency provisions under Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Services Act’s systemic risk framework to direct the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking to identify, secure, and repurpose high-performance computing assets across national labs, universities, and public research centres. These clusters — already funded by EU or member state money — will be integrated into a sovereign AI grid, governed by Brussels, with priority access for public safety, resilience, and control research. We are fast-tracking this through emergency permitting and leveraging the safety_breakthrough to embed new interpretability tools at the firmware level. Germany and France have raised concerns about national control, so we are offering them co-leadership in the oversight board and first call on 20% of the capacity for national security applications. This is not about building more — it’s about owning what we already paid for, before someone else decides we can’t use it.
