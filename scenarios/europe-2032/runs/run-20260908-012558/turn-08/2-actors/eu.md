## In practice  
We are invoking Article 22 for the first time in the Union’s history, not for war or pandemic, but for uncontrolled intelligence. The cyber attack on public services — a model-generated ransomware sweep exploiting zero-day logic flaws — exposed the limits of reactive resilience. The bio uplift findings, though contested, confirm that the barrier to human-targeting design is dissolving. And the annulled election in Georgia, where deepfaked judicial testimony led to a court-ordered rerun, proves that the integrity of institutions is now on the line. We cannot wait for consensus.

We are drafting the directive under Commission emergency powers, with legal basis in Article 114 (internal market) and Article 222 (security of energy supply), extended by analogy to AI as a systemic risk to societal function. The European AI Office will be empowered to require pre-deployment evaluations by independent auditors for any model above 65.0 capability, with a right to block deployment pending safety certification. We are fast-tracking the accreditation of three EU-based evaluation labs — in Paris, Leuven, and Warsaw — with access to sovereign clusters and model weights under strict non-disclosure. We are also mandating real-time anomaly reporting from all certified systems, feeding into the Emergency Response Corps’ live monitoring.

Resistance is fierce. The Internal Market Council, led by Ireland and Sweden, argues that unilateral restrictions will isolate EU markets and provoke retaliation. US providers threaten to withdraw from EU compliance frameworks. But we have secured support from France, Germany, and the Nordic states by linking the directive to access: only systems that submit to EU evaluation will be allowed to interface with public infrastructure, healthcare, and energy grids. Poland and the Baltics have joined, citing national security. We are offering transitional waivers for non-military sectors if providers join a mutual recognition pilot with Japan and Canada — a diplomatic hedge. This is not a trade measure. It is a survival measure. We are no longer building the future. We are defending the present.
