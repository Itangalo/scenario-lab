### A Turning Point in Crisis

Global AI capability advances to 73.5, driven by private-sector momentum despite the US RETRENCHMENT posture slowing its own trajectory. The frontier continues to advance rapidly, with systems demonstrating increasingly broad cognitive capabilities.

A wave of AI-enabled shocks hits simultaneously. A coordinated ransomware campaign, powered by AI-generated exploits, cripples public services in three member states. A controversial study demonstrates that genome design tools, when paired with accessible models, can produce plausible human-infective pathways—sparking alarm in biosecurity circles. And in Georgia, an election is annulled after courts rule that synthetic media manipulated judicial proceedings, raising fears of democratic erosion.

In response, the EU invokes Article 22 for the first time in its history, launching the AI Safety Emergency Directive as a large category 1 measure, prioritised and set to finish in turn 9. The proposal mandates pre-deployment evaluations for high-capability models and outlines new intervention powers for the European AI Office. Emergency labs in Paris, Leuven, and Warsaw are designated for future accreditation, and plans for real-time anomaly reporting are announced for certified systems. However, no operational capabilities are yet active—implementation will require full staffing, legal validation, and interagency coordination over the coming months.

Resistance is immediate. Ireland and Sweden warn of market fragmentation. US tech firms suspend EU compliance updates, citing overreach. While the directive passes politically, its implementation faces legal challenges and diplomatic friction, limiting immediate safety gains.

Resilience improves slightly to 52.0, as the Emergency Response Corps successfully contains the cyberattack’s spread, demonstrating value under fire. Public sentiment ticks up to 28.0—citizens welcome decisive action, even if flawed.

Sovereignty holds at 39.0: the completion of two major category 4 measures last turn delivers no new bonus this turn, and the +1 momentum effect has expired. A −1 penalty applies due to the 3.5-point rise in ai_capability. No capacity event occurs.

Political capital falls to 23.0. The portfolio charge is heavy: three large measures in flight (−9), plus the priority penalty (−1). The new safety directive earns a +3 proposal bonus for directly addressing the cyber incident and bio uplift. No sentiment-led boost applies.

Openweight capability rises to 58.0, reflecting continued diffusion of last turn’s model releases and modest optimisations in inference efficiency—supporting wider deployment of agent-like workflows, though not yet reaching fully autonomous multi-day task completion.
