## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: building sovereign capacity and defending against the now. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme is being structured through the European Investment Bank, with a first call for proposals issued under Article 107(3)(c) TFEU for state aid in strategic sectors. Site selection is underway in four member states — Finland, Spain, France, and Poland — with grid upgrades already fast-tracked under the Trans-European Networks for Energy (TEN-E) revision. Resistance from regional planning authorities is being met with binding compensation packages and local job guarantees, funded from the Gigafactory envelope itself. The goal is not just compute, but compute under EU legal jurisdiction, with data sovereignty, energy sourcing, and access governed by Brussels, not boardrooms.

The Tech sovereignty package is advancing through the Competitiveness Council, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable public benefit clauses: any data centre receiving fast-track status must reserve 15% of its capacity for public research and crisis response, and commit to open interconnection standards. This is not just about speed — it’s about anchoring infrastructure. The US and China are already conditioning access; we must condition ours. The package is fragile in the Council, where some member states fear ceding permitting power, so we are pairing it with national co-investment offers: for every euro a member state contributes to local grid or cooling infrastructure, the EU doubles it.

But we cannot wait five years for resilience to land. The cyber_test_shot exposed a fatal gap: no EU body has the mandate or tools to move fast when AI-driven attacks evade detection. So this turn, we are launching the EU AI Emergency Response Corps under the authority of the Health and Digital Executive Agency (HaDEA), with operational command co-located at ENISA. It will integrate national cyber units, critical infrastructure operators, and academic AI forensics teams into a single standing force, with pre-authorised access protocols and live simulations every quarter. Legal bases are being drafted under Article 16 TFEU (internal security) and Article 114 (internal market), with a proposal for a Council Regulation to be tabled by December. The cost is high, but the alternative — another near-miss discovered by accident — is unacceptable. This is not preparation for war, but for a world where AI makes crisis response a race measured in minutes.
