## New measure  
**Launch EU-wide AI Labour Transition Guarantee**  
A legally binding promise to every worker displaced by AI in the public or regulated sectors: six months of full wage replacement, access to retraining in AI-augmented professions, and guaranteed public-sector placement if no match is found within a year.

## Priority  
Priority: Accelerate Sovereign Compute Access via Repurposed Hardware and Emergency Permitting  
Because we are running out of time to control the infrastructure we already fund — and because every point of rising capability without sovereign oversight is a point of strategic surrender — this remains the only lever that secures both our autonomy and our ability to act at all.

## In practice  
We are acting on three fronts: building future capacity, hardening response, and now, seizing control of what we already have — while finally answering the human cost of the transition. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme remains on track, with site finalisation expected by turn 5. Contracts are being structured through the European Investment Bank, and we are using state aid flexibility under Article 107(3)(c) to de-risk private participation. Grid upgrades in Finland and Spain are advancing under TEN-E fast-tracking, though Poland continues to resist binding commitments on energy allocation. We are meeting local opposition with binding job guarantees and regional reinvestment clauses, funded from the Gigafactory envelope, to convert resistance into partnership.

The Tech sovereignty package is under negotiation in the Competitiveness Council. We are offering member states co-investment in grid and cooling infrastructure — one euro from them, two from Brussels — in exchange for accepting accelerated permitting and public benefit clauses: 15% capacity reservation for public research and crisis response, and adherence to open interconnection standards. The US is conditioning access; we must condition ours. The EU AI Emergency Response Corps has now been formally established and is undergoing its first cross-border exercise in Q2, hosted by the Netherlands and involving critical energy and health infrastructure in Belgium, Germany, and Denmark. Legal authorities are in place, and we are integrating early interpretability tools into its forensic toolkit.

This turn, we are invoking emergency provisions under Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Services Act’s systemic risk framework to direct the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking to identify, secure, and repurpose high-performance computing assets across national labs, universities, and public research centres. These clusters — already funded by EU or member state money — will be integrated into a sovereign AI grid, governed by Brussels, with priority access for public safety, resilience, and control research. We are fast-tracking this through emergency permitting and leveraging the safety_breakthrough to embed new interpretability tools at the firmware level. Germany and France have raised concerns about national control, so we are offering them co-leadership in the oversight board and first call on 20% of the capacity for national security applications. This is not about building more — it’s about owning what we already paid for, before someone else decides we can’t use it.

At the same time, we are launching the EU-wide AI Labour Transition Guarantee, a direct response to the growing unrest. This will be implemented through a new regulation under Article 122 TFEU, co-financed 70% by the EU and 30% by member states, with disbursement tied to verified displacement events in legal, administrative, and public IT roles. The European Social Fund+ will be redirected to cover training in AI-augmented public service delivery, and we are partnering with universities and public hospitals to create 120,000 new roles over the next 18 months. This is not charity — it is strategic stabilisation. Without it, public sentiment will keep falling, and our political capital will evaporate before we can finish what we’ve started.
