### US Posture Confirmed: RETRENCHMENT
The new US administration takes office, formally adopting a RETRENCHMENT posture. Frontier AI development slows under domestic political constraints, reducing the global capability growth rate slightly — though the effect is masked by strong private-sector momentum elsewhere. The EU gains breathing room but loses a key partner in joint governance efforts.

### Sovereignty Gains from Completed Build
The EU finalizes its sovereign compute integration under emergency powers, completing the repurposing of national HPC clusters. Despite delays, the grid is now operational across core states, delivering a tangible boost to autonomy. The Tech Sovereignty Package also concludes, unlocking €180bn in private data centre investment commitments, though final permitting remains uneven.

### Resilience Rises — But Labour Policy Falters
The AI Emergency Response Corps becomes fully operational, enhancing cross-border cyber defence and incident coordination. This, combined with live monitoring of critical systems, lifts resilience. However, the Labour Transition Guarantee continues to stumble. Wage verification delays and fragmented retraining pathways spark protests in Brussels and Lisbon, feeding public frustration.

### Opaque Reasoning Triggers Safety Crisis
With leading AI systems no longer producing human-readable reasoning, existing interpretability tools fail. The EU launches a €12bn emergency research drive to decode non-linguistic model representations. While ambitious, the effort starts from near-zero and will take years to yield deployable safeguards.

### Political Capital Erodes
Four large measures remain in flight, including the prioritised labour guarantee. The new safety research programme adds further strain. Though the Tech Sovereignty Package and Compute Access finish, their benefits are outweighed by ongoing costs and the labour policy’s visible failure. Public sentiment dips further as job displacement becomes a daily reality.
