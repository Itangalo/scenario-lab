## In practice  
We are completing the integration of the sovereign HPC grid under the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking, with firmware-secured nodes now operational in Germany, France, and Sweden. Legal instruments under Article 114 TFEU have enabled rapid repurposing, and interpretability probes are embedded at the hardware-software interface, though their utility is now in question due to the opaque_reasoning event. Poland and Hungary remain partially compliant, bound by energy-for-access swaps that tie grid priority to audit transparency — a fragile compromise, but one that holds for now. The completion of this measure frees up legal and administrative bandwidth, but not political capital, which remains strained.

The InvestAI Gigafactories are moving into construction phase, with Finland and Spain breaking ground under TEN-E fast-tracking. However, the conditionality linking each exaflop to 200MW of new renewable capacity is causing delays in Poland, where local opposition to wind projects has stalled transmission upgrades. We are working through the Commission’s Energy Directorate to offer hydrogen co-investment as a bridge, but this risks inflating costs. Meanwhile, the Labour Transition Guarantee is under acute pressure: the central verification portal is behind schedule, and displaced workers in Belgium and Portugal are waiting eight weeks for wage replacement. We are diverting Digital Europe Programme funds to accelerate deployment, and the Social Affairs Council will convene an emergency session in April to align national verification standards.

The new measure responds to the opaque_reasoning shock: we are launching a €12bn emergency call through Horizon Europe, co-managed by EASA-AI and the European Laboratory for Learning and Intelligent Systems (ELLIS), to reverse-engineer the latent spaces of current models and develop non-linguistic interpretability tools. The first phase will fund 20 high-risk teams across 12 member states, with results required to be open-access and auditable. This is not about catching up — it is about ensuring that, even if the world’s leading developers no longer understand their systems, the EU still can.
