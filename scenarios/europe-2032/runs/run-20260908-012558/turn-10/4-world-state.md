### The Directive Lands — But Not Fully

By June 2031, the EU AI Safety Emergency Directive is technically operational: the Paris, Leuven, and Warsaw labs are accredited, anomaly reporting is live across critical infrastructure, and the first enforcement action in Lithuania has been upheld. However, full operationalisation falters. Leuven remains understaffed despite emergency hiring, delaying pre-deployment reviews for models above 65.0 capability. Weight-sharing agreements with US developers remain unsigned, with major providers citing unresolved legal exposure and refusing to transfer model weights under NDA. The European AI Office issues symbolic compliance deadlines, but enforcement remains partial and geographically uneven. As a result, the directive contributes only incrementally to sovereignty and political capital this turn, reflecting its incomplete reach.

The political cost of the directive’s incomplete rollout is partially offset by the full completion of one major measure: the InvestAI Gigafactories programme delivers a significant expansion of domestic compute capacity. This tangible infrastructure gain strengthens long-term autonomy and supports a modest sovereignty boost. The Accelerate Sovereign Compute initiative reaches advanced completion, with final integration into the EuroHPC grid expected next turn, but does not cross the finish line in time to count as completed. Similarly, the broader Tech Sovereignty Package — initially expected to conclude this turn — experiences delays in cross-border certification and procurement, pushing its full operational status into the next turn. Only one major measure may be completed per turn under EU implementation capacity, and InvestAI Gigafactories is the sole programme to clear that threshold.

### Capability Pressures Mount

Global AI capability advances to 78.5, driven by rapid private-sector iteration. Open-weight models remain flat at 61.0, as incremental improvements in efficiency and distribution within existing open-source communities are offset by the absence of transatlantic weight-sharing agreements and the lack of a major new open release. While consumer-grade agent workflows show slight gains in robustness, no foundational leap occurs. The continued blockage of weight transfers from US frontier labs and the absence of a sovereign EU-led open model launch constrain open-weight progress. The biosecurity precursor continues to circulate in expert circles, with growing concern that synthetic biology tools are nearing dangerous accessibility.

Resilience holds steady at 54.0, as detection systems contain low-level cyber intrusions, but no new protective measures finish this turn. AI safety remains critically low at 15.0, as no deployable safeguards are integrated into frontier systems. Public sentiment stagnates at 30.0, caught between anxiety over job displacement and cautious approval of emergency measures.

Political capital rises to 31.0, supported by the completion of one major measure (InvestAI Gigafactories) and visible, though incomplete, progress on the safety directive and sovereign compute integration. The gains reflect tangible delivery, but the limitations in enforcement, coordination, and measure completion prevent a broader surge. Sovereignty climbs to 40.0, reflecting newly secured compute infrastructure and partial progress on category 4 programmes, with the delayed finalisation of the Tech Sovereignty Package and the constrained rollout of the safety regime tempering momentum.
