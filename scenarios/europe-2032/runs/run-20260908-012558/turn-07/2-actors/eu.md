### New measure
**Launch EU-wide AI Labour Transition Guarantee**  
We prioritise the urgent repair and acceleration of the EU-wide labour guarantee, diverting emergency funds and political bandwidth to stabilise its delivery mechanisms before public legitimacy collapses.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Stabilises public_sentiment by visibly delivering on protection promises; increases resilience by ensuring displaced workers are absorbed rather than alienated; indirectly supports eu_political_capital by preventing backlash that would paralyse further action.  
`Applies to:` All EU member states, with binding targets for wage replacement timelines and retraining throughput.

### Priority
Priority: Launch EU-wide AI Labour Transition Guarantee  
Because the failure of this measure to deliver in time is now the single largest threat to our political capital and cohesion — not the pace of AI development, not the safety crisis, but the erosion of trust that we can manage its consequences — and because without that capital, no other commitment can be kept.

### In practice  
We are in damage control mode on labour transition. The central verification portal, delayed by interoperability disputes between Belgium and Germany, is now being rebuilt using a simplified, GDPR-compliant schema co-developed with the European Data Protection Board. We are fast-tracking deployment via the Digital Europe Programme’s emergency clause, diverting €1.8bn to stand up regional processing hubs in Lisbon, Marseille, and Bratislava by turn 8. The Social Affairs Council has agreed to mutual recognition of retraining credentials across eight member states, breaking a key bottleneck. In return, Poland and Hungary have dropped their resistance to audit transparency in exchange for priority access to retraining slots for coal-sector workers — a concession that keeps the energy-for-access swaps intact.

Meanwhile, the €12bn interpretability research push is underway, but we are not waiting for results to act. EASA-AI has deployed interim monitoring protocols based on behavioural consistency checks and anomaly detection at inference time, using hardware-embedded probes on sovereign clusters. These are crude, but they buy time. We are also pressing the Commission to classify non-linguistic reasoning systems above capability 65.0 as high-risk under a revised AI Act Annex III, using the eval_anomaly_reports as justification — a move that would require post-deployment audits and restrict deployment in critical infrastructure. That proposal faces resistance in the Internal Market Council, where some states fear retaliation from US providers, but we are building a blocking minority with Nordic and Eastern members. The sovereignty grid is now online, but its value hinges on whether we can govern what runs on it — and right now, we cannot see clearly enough to do so.
