The EU faces cascading crises as the leading AI model becomes inaccessible, disrupting critical services and exposing strategic dependencies. Private AI investment collapses, stalling compute expansion, while the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme (SCAP) fails to become operational due to unresolved legal and infrastructure hurdles. In response, the Commission launches the Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme under Article 122 TFEU, committing 5GW of low-carbon power to sovereign AI by turn 6, with nuclear and geothermal projects prioritized in Brittany and northern Sweden. Germany and Austria object on grounds of energy market distortion, and ENTSO-E resists binding grid allocations without intergovernmental consensus.

The Emergency AI Accountability and Redress Mechanism (EAAD) advances with mandatory pre-deployment certification powered by new interpretability tools, but real-time logging for critical systems faces member-state opposition—Italy and Hungary continue to block audits, weakening enforcement. Public trust erodes further amid rising job losses in professional sectors, protests against data centres, and perceptions of institutional failure, exacerbated by SCAP’s missed deadline.

Despite a safety breakthrough improving model oversight, rapid progress in autonomous code generation widens the gap between AI capability and control. A cyber intrusion exploiting open-weight models causes localized outages, highlighting persistent vulnerabilities. No resilience measures are completed, and systemic fragility deepens, leaving the EU strategically exposed and politically weakened.
