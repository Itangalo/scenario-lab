### The Shield Before the Storm

The EU scrambles to respond to a cascading cyber intrusion that exploited a poisoned software dependency, disrupting health data systems and grid coordination across three member states. With human-led defences overwhelmed, the Union invokes Article 122 TFEU to fast-track the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative — a large-scale deployment of AI-hardened defence stacks to Tier 1 operators. ENISA begins rolling out standardised tools for automated patching and swarm detection, though full integration remains years away.

Despite the urgency, progress is uneven. Germany and Austria reject the Commission’s attempt to override ENTSO-E’s grid allocation rules via Article 194 TFEU, warning of legal overreach. The 5GW energy-backed compute goal, though officially “completed,” exists only in pilot form in Brittany and northern Sweden. The InvestAI Gigafactories press on, but integration with the Cyber Shield lags due to technical misalignment and member-state resistance to centralised monitoring.

Public sentiment worsens as media spotlight failures in auditability and job displacement. A new EIB transition window for displaced professionals launches quietly, but the messaging campaign around the Cyber Shield fails to gain traction, seen as reactive and technocratic. Protests grow in Paris and Rome, targeting both AI-driven layoffs and the perceived erosion of national sovereignty in infrastructure decisions.

AI capability advances to 62.0, driven by improved autonomous code generation and model self-debugging. Open-weight models reach 50.0, aided by leaked tooling from the recent attack, which circulates in underground developer forums. Safety erodes further — the gap between capability and control widens — as no new safeguards land on frontier systems.

Resilience sees a marginal uptick from early deployment signals, but systemic fragility remains. Political capital falls under the weight of four large measures in flight, a named priority, and the fallout from unmet commitments. The US administration takes office next turn, its posture still unknown but widely expected to reshape global AI access.
