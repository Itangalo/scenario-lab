A major cyberattack exploiting poisoned software dependencies severely disrupts public services across three EU member states, exposing systemic fragility beyond hardened infrastructure. In response, the Commission fast-tracks a new regulation under Article 109 TFEU mandating real-time model logging, energy-source tracing, and remote audit access, making the final €200bn InvestAI disbursement conditional on Cyber Shield integration. However, a member state bypasses EU rules by striking a private deal with a US hyperscaler, exempting it from EAAD oversight and undermining bloc-wide unity.

Despite regulatory escalation, enforcement falters: the EAAD lacks political backing, Germany and Austria block grid overrides, and fast-track arbitration fails. The rebranded "Cyber Resilience Corps" draws public distrust, echoing broader skepticism toward top-down AI governance. InvestAI Gigafactories and Secure Energy-Backed Compute conclude with fragmented outputs; the promised 5GW of monitored compute remains limited to pilot sites. Protests in Paris and Rome intensify as displaced workers reject measures perceived as control, not protection. With four major initiatives straining capacity, political capital erodes further—sovereignty is asserted but not consolidated.
