### The Fracture Deepens

A major cyber intrusion, exploiting poisoned software dependencies, overwhelms public services across three member states. Defenders are outpaced by automated attacks, and attribution drags for months. The damage is real, visible, and systemic — a wake-up call that hardened infrastructure alone cannot protect brittle control layers.

Simultaneously, a member state strikes a private deal with a US hyperscaler, bypassing EU auditability and energy-binding rules. The agreement grants preferential access to compute capacity and exempts the provider from EAAD oversight. The move is defended domestically as pragmatic necessity but widely interpreted as a crack in EU unity.

In response, the Commission fast-tracks enforcement of auditability and energy binding across all sovereign compute. A new regulation under Article 109 TFEU mandates real-time model logging, energy-source tracing, and remote audit access. Non-compliant sites risk disconnection from energy guarantees and EIB financing. The €200bn InvestAI fund’s final disbursement is now conditional on integration with the Cyber Shield’s monitoring layer.

Yet resistance hardens. Germany and Austria maintain legal objections to grid overrides, and the fast-track arbitration mechanism fails to gain traction. The EAAD, though empowered, lacks enforcement credibility without political backing. The rebranded “Cyber Resilience Corps” initiative is met with public scepticism — seen as a rebranding of displacement, not a remedy.

The InvestAI Gigafactories and Secure Energy-Backed Compute programmes officially conclude, but their outputs remain fragmented and disconnected from central oversight. The promised 5GW of energy-backed compute exists only in isolated pilots, with no unified monitoring.

Public sentiment dips further as protests grow in Paris and Rome. Workers displaced by AI-driven automation see the new measures as top-down control, not protection. The narrative of “secure by design” fails to resonate amid visible fragmentation and elite defection.

### The Cost of Control

Ambition outstrips capacity. Four large measures remain in flight, including the newly prioritized enforcement initiative. The portfolio strain, combined with a failing legitimacy, drains political capital. The EU acts, but the ground shifts beneath it — sovereignty claimed, not held.
