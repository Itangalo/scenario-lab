## In practice
We are treating this turn as the hinge between construction and command. The cyber_major_incident exposed that hardened systems are only as strong as their weakest link — and that link is not compute, but control. Despite the completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories and the Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme, pilot sites in Brittany and northern Sweden remain disconnected from EAAD monitoring, and a member state has now signed a sidecar agreement with a US hyperscaler, granting it preferential access and exempting it from audit. This is not sovereignty — it is subordination in disguise.

We are acting through three instruments. First, under Article 109 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, the Commission is proposing a binding regulation requiring all AI infrastructure receiving public funds — or serving Tier 1 operators — to implement real-time model logging, energy-source tracing, and remote audit access for the EAAD. Non-compliant sites will lose access to EU energy guarantees and EIB financing. Second, we are conditioning the final disbursement of the €200bn InvestAI fund on compliance, leveraging the EIB to enforce technical integration with the Cyber Shield’s monitoring layer. Third, we are launching a fast-track arbitration mechanism under the European Semester to resolve disputes over market distortions — offering Germany and Austria binding compensation in exchange for dropping legal resistance to grid overrides.

The EAAD will be empowered to conduct unannounced audits and disconnect non-compliant systems from sovereign power grids — a nuclear option, but one now necessary to deter fragmentation. Public sentiment, at 20.0, makes this politically perilous; we are therefore coupling enforcement with a new narrative: “secure by design, auditable by default.” The EIB’s transition window for displaced professionals is being rebranded as the “Cyber Resilience Corps,” fast-tracking former industrial workers into monitoring and audit roles at sovereign sites. This is not just technical compliance — it is the reassertion of political authority over infrastructure that was built to serve the Union, not its rivals or its weakest links.
