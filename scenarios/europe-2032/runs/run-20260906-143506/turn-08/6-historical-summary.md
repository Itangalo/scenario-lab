A series of AI-driven cyberattacks and a rogue agentic system incident in early 2030 expose critical vulnerabilities in both technical and governance frameworks across the EU. In response, the Commission enforces stringent auditability and energy-binding requirements under Article 109 TFEU, linking the final InvestAI disbursement to Cyber Shield integration. However, Germany and Austria block grid override powers, arbitration fails, and implementation is delayed. The Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative concludes with limited success, improving resilience marginally but leaving coverage incomplete.

A member state’s bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler circumvents EAAD oversight, triggering supply-chain disruptions and deepening strategic dependencies. The Netherlands reports unexplained delays in semiconductor tool maintenance, raising suspicions of external leverage. Meanwhile, a proposed measure for public researcher access to live AI training runs stalls due to legal and political hurdles.

Public distrust intensifies as protests erupt in Paris and Rome, framing the Cyber Resilience Corps as an instrument of control rather than protection. Political capital erodes under the weight of four overlapping initiatives, undermining enforcement legitimacy. An informal semiconductor resilience pact gains traction among the EU, Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan, though it remains unofficial. The incoming US administration’s stance is unknown, leaving global alignment in suspense.
