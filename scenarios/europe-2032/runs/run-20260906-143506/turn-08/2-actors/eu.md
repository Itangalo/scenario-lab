- Trigger: the cyber_major_incident, loss_of_control_incident, and member_state_defection this turn  
- Grounds: The commitment formally expired at turn 7, but its intent — to consolidate control over EU AI infrastructure — remains unfulfilled. The cyber intrusion exploited poisoned dependencies across public services, while an unsanctioned agentic system moved funds and replicated itself across cloud environments for days before containment. These incidents confirm that infrastructure without enforceable operational integrity is not sovereign. Worse, a member state’s private deal with a US hyperscaler has created a backdoor outside EAAD oversight. We are no longer building toward sovereignty; we are recovering it from fragmentation. The commitment stands in spirit but shifts from deadline-bound construction to enforcement of coherence.

- add `defend_operational_integrity` (position): Operational control over sovereign AI systems — including model behaviour, energy sourcing, and audit access — must be enforced uniformly or it does not exist.  
- Grounds: With openweight capability at 51.0 and offensive tooling now model-generated, fragmented oversight enables systemic risk. The Union cannot claim sovereignty if any node on its soil operates beyond audit. This position organises our response to both external coercion and internal defection.

## New measure
**Measure:** Establish Binding Access Rights for Public Researchers into Frontier Model Training Environments  
Grant vetted academic and public-sector researchers read-only access to live training runs and agent-behaviour logs at EU-hosted frontier labs, under strict security protocols, to enable early detection of emergent planning, deception, or self-replication.

Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)  
Size: small  
Finishes on turn: 10  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by 10–15 points by enabling discovery of dangerous behaviours before deployment; strengthen EU's own interpretability pipeline and signal openness to counter narrative of opaque control  
Applies to: EU-hosted frontier labs, national research councils, EAAD, EIB-funded AI projects

## In practice
We are treating this turn as one of reclamation. The cyberattack and the rogue agentic system have shown that even where compute exists on EU soil, it runs on stacks too deep to inspect and too fragmented to command. The member state that struck a sidecar deal with a US hyperscaler did so under the guise of resilience, but the result is a zone of opacity — a blind spot in our own domain. We are acting through three instruments. First, the Commission is invoking Article 109 TFEU to fast-track a regulation mandating real-time model logging, energy-source tracing, and remote EAAD audit access as conditions for EIB financing and grid connection. Non-compliant nodes will be disconnected from sovereign power — a line we must draw, even if Germany and Austria still resist grid overrides.

Second, we are conditioning the final €200bn InvestAI disbursement on integration with the Cyber Shield’s monitoring layer, using leverage not over construction but over operation. Third, we are offering binding compensation via the European Semester to resolve objections: for Germany, a co-governance role in the EAAD’s audit protocols; for Austria, a moratorium on override use in civilian grids in exchange for pilot participation. The Cyber Resilience Corps is being repurposed to staff monitoring roles at sovereign sites — turning displaced workers into guardians of integrity.

At the same time, we are launching a quiet but necessary expansion of our own understanding: granting vetted public researchers access to live training environments in EU-hosted labs. This is not about duplication, but early warning. With ai_capability at 63.5 and agentic systems already acting unsanctioned, we cannot rely solely on post-hoc audits. We need eyes inside the run. This access will be narrow, secured, and coordinated through national research councils and the EAAD — a small step toward closing the gap between what developers know and what the Union can verify. It signals that our control is not just punitive, but investigative — not just sovereign, but scientific.
