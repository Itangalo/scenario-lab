### Restoration under fire
The first half of 2029 was defined by a sprawling automated intrusion. A compromised update swept through municipal administrations, clinics and port logistics in several member states, encrypting records and forcing services back to paper. ENISA-led teams worked from borrowed offices, restoring backups town by town. Operators contained the worst cascades, but recovery was slow and uneven, and evening news carried queues at town halls.

Brussels answered with a joint repair cell built from existing emergency teams and reprogrammed funds. With no budget for anything larger, the cell could triage but not transform: worst-hit municipalities got help, others waited. The effort was visible enough to be noticed, too thin to reassure.

### A solo deal breaks the line
At the same moment, one capital broke ranks. Facing power bills and waiting lists for foreign models under Washington's new tiered-access doctrine, its government signed a direct arrangement for dedicated capacity and investment, on terms that undercut the common bargaining position agreed with lens, chemical and packaging partners.

Paris and The Hague scrambled to hold the joint licence and testing line, offering the defector shared access and warning that solidarity aid would follow common rules. The deal stayed. Editorials in the east called it pragmatism; elsewhere it read as proof the Union could not hold.

The American administration took office promising to treat advanced models as a national asset. No new terms were published yet, but export reviews tightened and European officials returned from Washington with slower timelines.

On the five reserved factory sites, nothing rose. Permits were extended, fences maintained, power-price protests continued. A sovereignty package of private-capital targets and fast-permit zones was declared complete, adding paper capacity more than power.
