### US Posture Set: Alliance Confirmed

The new US administration takes office, formally adopting a posture of **ALLIANCE**. Early cooperation agreements are announced on joint AI incident reporting and evaluation standards, offering a modest uplift in shared assurance. However, access to frontier models remains tightly controlled, with European developers still excluded from the most advanced training runs.

### Cyber Shield Launched Amid Rising Tensions

The EU proposes the **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield**, a large category 6 measure aimed at hardening essential services against AI-driven attacks. Drafted under Article 114 TFEU, it advances to technical committees, with ENISA publishing preliminary red-team scenarios based on the hospital ransomware campaign. Germany and Poland signal cautious support, conditioned on liability protections and co-funding mechanisms.

Despite momentum, early adopters remain hesitant. Utilities and transport operators warn of implementation costs, while some member states question enforcement feasibility. The directive is not adopted this turn, remaining in negotiation for turn 7.

### Legal Challenge Deepens Evaluation Crisis

The legal challenge against the **European Evaluation Shield** gains traction, with foreign governments formally backing tech firms in the ECJ case. The Commission responds with a Council resolution affirming the public interest in pre-deployment audits, but judicial uncertainty stalls expansion beyond pilot audits. No new developer joins the programme this turn.

### Sovereignty Gains Offset by Strategic Drift

While the **SEAL** initiative concludes successfully, no further sovereignty gains materialise. The **Tech sovereignty package** finishes this turn but delivers below expectations due to lukewarm private investment signals. With `ai_capability` crossing 60.0, the gap between closed and open capabilities widens, and offensive tooling proliferates.

Public sentiment declines further, driven by job displacement fears and perceptions of institutional overreach. A high-profile election in a major member state is annulled over allegations of AI-generated disinformation, triggering political turmoil and eroding trust in digital governance.

Political capital erodes under sustained portfolio pressure and growing backlash, despite the proposal bonus for the Cyber Shield. Resilience sees minor improvement from planning activity, but systemic vulnerabilities persist.
