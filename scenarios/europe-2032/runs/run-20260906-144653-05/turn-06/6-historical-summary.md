The EU launches the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield proposal under Article 114 TFEU, advancing it to technical committees with support from Germany and Poland, conditional on liability and funding safeguards; however, adoption is delayed due to industry cost concerns and enforcement doubts. The European Evaluation Shield faces deepening legal challenges backed by foreign governments, stalling expansion despite a Council resolution affirming its public interest, leaving audits in pilot phase. The successful completion of the SEAL initiative bolsters semiconductor sovereignty, but broader tech sovereignty goals fall short due to weak private investment. A major election in a key member state is annulled over AI-generated disinformation, fueling public distrust. Public sentiment drops further amid job displacement fears and perceived institutional overreach, while political capital erodes under mounting pressure. Resilience sees minor gains from planning, but systemic cyber vulnerabilities persist.
