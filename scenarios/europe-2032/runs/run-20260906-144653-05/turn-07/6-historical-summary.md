The Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield is formally adopted, establishing the EU’s first binding mandate for AI-resistant cyber defences, with Germany and Poland leading implementation and cross-border simulations showing improved detection despite coordination gaps. Emergency trilogues launch to develop the Joint Resilience Integration Protocol with the US, but negotiations stall over data sovereignty concerns, leaving real-time telemetry sharing unrealized. Despite completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package, private investment remains insufficient, failing to close the widening AI capability gap or bring new compute online. Public distrust deepens due to the annulled election, synthetic media protests, and activist attacks on data centres, though services remain intact. Political capital recovers modestly from policy delivery on process, but systemic vulnerabilities and low sentiment limit gains. Open-weight AI capability remains flat at 47.0, with no justified increase due to lack of anchoring investments or spillover effects.
