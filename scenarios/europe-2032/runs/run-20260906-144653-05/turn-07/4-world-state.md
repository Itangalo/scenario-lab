### Cyber Shield Lands, Resilience Rises

The **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield** directive is formally adopted by the Council, marking the EU’s first binding, cross-sector mandate for AI-resistant cyber defences. Germany and Poland lead implementation, with national grid and hospital operators committing to red-teaming exercises using ENISA’s new attack library. A cross-border simulation involving six member states reveals gaps in failover coordination, but also demonstrates improved detection speed, validating the directive’s core design.

### Transatlantic Momentum, Domestic Limits

Leveraging the **joint_threat_response** cooperation, the EU launches emergency trilogues to establish the **Joint Resilience Integration Protocol**. Talks with the US Department of Homeland Security have begun, supported by the standing `US_POSTURE: ALLIANCE`, but progress remains preliminary. No formal agreements have been reached on real-time telemetry sharing. Legal concerns in the European Parliament over data sovereignty stall negotiations, and no final decision is expected before the next turn. The protocol remains in early drafting, with implementation contingent on future alignment.

### Sovereignty Stalls, Sentiment Sours

Despite the completion of **InvestAI Gigafactories**, private investment lags, and no new compute capacity comes online in time to offset the widening capability gap. With `ai_capability` rising again, dependence deepens. The annulled election and ongoing protests over synthetic media fuel public anger. A spate of targeted attacks on data centres—attributed to anti-AI activists—further erodes confidence, though no major service collapse occurs.

### Political Capital Rebounds Modestly

The formal adoption of **Cyber Shield** and the completion of **InvestAI Gigafactories** and the **Tech Sovereignty Package** provide a limited political boost, signaling forward motion on critical fronts. However, the lack of immediate tangible gains in sovereignty, coupled with falling public sentiment and unresolved implementation hurdles, constrains the payoff. The gains are real but partial, reflecting delivery on process rather than transformative outcomes.

The rise in `openweight_capability` is retracted due to lack of narrative anchoring. No spillover from US cooperation is justified within the turn’s events, and no prior compute investment is described that would directly increase open-weight performance. The metric remains flat at 47.0, consistent with accumulated trends and constitutional requirements.
