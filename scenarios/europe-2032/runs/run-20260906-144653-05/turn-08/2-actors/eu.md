## New measure
**Sovereign AI Compute Corridor**  
Establishes a legally binding, EU-controlled compute corridor by fast-tracking the deployment of exascale AI training and inference infrastructure on sovereign soil, with full control over access, weights and energy supply, directly countering strategic dependence on foreign hyperscalers.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to +20 eu_ai_sovereignty by turn 11 through anchoring at least 10% of projected EU-critical compute capacity under irrevocable legal control, with physical and operational independence from non-EU operators; begins reversing the trend of external dependence as ai_capability crosses 60.0  
`Applies to:` EU member states, selected national champions, European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU), and qualifying foreign-owned data centres operating within EU jurisdiction

## Priority
**Sovereign AI Compute Corridor** — because the openweight_frontier_release event has collapsed the lead time between closed and open capability, exposing the EU to unrecallable risks while deepening strategic vulnerability, and because our two-year commitment to secure sovereign AI infrastructure can no longer be met through incentives or permitting alone — it now demands direct, binding control over compute as a matter of strategic survival.

## In practice
This turn, we launch the **Sovereign AI Compute Corridor** initiative under Article 114 TFEU and the newly invoked Strategic Autonomy Clause in the Digital Markets Act review, enabling the Commission to designate critical AI compute infrastructure as essential for Union security. We will identify three anchor sites — one in France, one in Germany, and one in the Nordic bloc — for immediate deployment of exascale systems, co-financed through a €45bn draw from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the newly repurposed InvestAI residual fund. These sites will operate under a "closed stack" model: EU-controlled hardware, EU-vetted operators, and legally enforceable prohibitions on remote access or weight extraction by non-EU entities.

We begin trilogues with the European Parliament’s ITRE and IMCO committees to adopt the legal framework by turn 9, offering member states guaranteed grid priority and accelerated spectrum allocation for 6G backhaul in exchange for hosting rights. Resistance from Ireland and the Netherlands — concerned about energy strain and foreign investment retaliation — is met with binding energy-rebalancing guarantees and a new clause allowing limited commercial use under strict audit. The EuroHPC JU is re-tasked as the central operator, with a mandate to achieve first deployment by turn 10. This is not industrial policy — it is infrastructure as deterrence. Without it, the EU will not merely lag; it will be governed by systems it cannot inspect, cannot secure, and cannot switch off.
