### The Teeth Behind the Mandate

The EU pushes forward with the Sovereign Verification Regime, demanding telemetry and checkpoints from high-capability foreign AI systems. While France and Italy comply, Germany and Sweden resist, questioning legal proportionality. The European AI Assurance Agency (EAAD+) issues warnings but cannot verify submissions — it lacks the tools and staff. Verification remains symbolic, not operational.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme officially concludes, securing two sites. With private investment evaporated, the Commission shifts to public ownership for the next phase. The move is framed as strategic necessity, but sparks debate over state overreach. The Council delays the vote, citing fiscal concerns.

Meanwhile, NIS3’s Hardened Cyber Shield reaches full institutional status, though 18% of critical healthcare operators remain non-compliant, relying on fragile proxy defences. ENISA stresses that system-level threats cannot be stopped without model-level control — a gap that widens as AI capability advances.

AI capability climbs to 74.0, driven by global momentum. Benchmark anomalies grow more frequent, but labs dismiss them. No safety improvements land on deployed systems. The EU’s new **EAAD+ Technical Capacity Acceleration** launches with €18bn, aiming to build sovereign verification expertise — but it will take years. For now, the Union demands accountability it cannot enforce.

Public sentiment inches up, buoyed slightly by oncology advances and transparency signals from the Public Trust Observatory. Yet distrust persists. Protests flare outside data centres, and media narratives deepen the split: AI as saviour, AI as silent ruler.

Political capital erodes further. Ambitious measures multiply, but no major initiative finishes to offset the cost. Legal challenges mount, cohesion frays, and the priority regime yields no quick wins.

### Faint Signals in the Fog

Rumours grow of a municipal oversight network using open tools to audit public AI — a grassroots counterweight to opaque systems. And whispers of a European efficiency breakthrough suggest compute gaps may narrow. But neither has yet changed the balance.
