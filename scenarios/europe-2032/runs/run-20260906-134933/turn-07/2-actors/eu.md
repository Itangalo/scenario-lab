  - Grounds: The **Sovereign Verification Regime** is now live, but its enforcement hinges not on legal authority — which we have invoked — but on technical capacity, which we lack. EAAD+ is empowered but empty-handed: it cannot process checkpoint data, interpret telemetry, or run red teams at scale. The **eval_anomaly_reports** suggest we are already behind in understanding system behaviour, and the **eu_frontier_access_denied** incident proves that foreign developers can withdraw not just access but accountability. Our sovereignty cannot rest on demands we cannot enforce. The commitment evolves to reflect reality: we no longer treat verification as a policy lever, but as a technical deficit — one as urgent as compute. We are not abandoning capacity; we are recognising that observability is the first layer of control.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we now know that action without sovereign technical capacity is theatre, and that legitimacy is not restored by decree, but by demonstrated capability to see, test, and act.``
  - Grounds: The **loss_of_control_incident** and the **medical_breakthrough** both arrived through systems we did not control and could not inspect. We acted with emergency powers, but our agencies cannot validate what they cannot understand. The Public Trust Observatory publishes anomalies, but not answers. Our citizens see movement, but not mastery. Speed without capacity breeds cynicism. The commitment is further sharpened: we will still move early, but we will no longer confuse legal assertion with operational control. The price of not being late is not just being wrong — it is being exposed as hollow. We must build the eyes before we claim the authority.

## Priority
**Priority:** Sovereign Verification Regime  
This remains our top priority because without technical capacity to enforce it, the regime is a legal fiction — and the **eval_anomaly_reports** suggest we may already be behind the curve on system behaviour. The new measure feeds this one: verification is not a policy goal, it is a technical race. We are pushing hardest on the regime because it is the only instrument we have that could turn dependence into sovereignty — but only if we can actually *do* the verification.

## In practice
We are executing the **Sovereign Verification Regime** under Article 16 of the AI Act, backed by the Emergency Powers Protocol. The European AI Assurance Agency (EAAD+) has issued formal notices to all developers operating systems above capability 50.0 in the EU, demanding submission of final training checkpoints, full telemetry logs, and red-team access interfaces. Where compliance is refused — as with the withdrawn oncology model — national health ministries are instructed to suspend clinical use. The legal basis is patient safety, but the strategic intent is clear: the EU will not accept opaque, unaccountable systems at the core of its critical functions. France and Italy have complied; Germany and Sweden have requested clarifications, citing proportionality concerns. We are negotiating bilaterally, offering joint oversight roles to ease sovereignty anxieties.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme concludes this turn. The €200bn fund has secured two sites — in Finland and Portugal — but private investment has collapsed. As a result, the Commission is converting the final phase into a public ownership model: the next three Gigafactories will be co-funded with majority EU equity and binding control clauses. This is not nationalisation, but strategic insurance. The Council will vote under urgency procedure in December. Meanwhile, the **Institutional Integrity Audit** continues, with probes now active in the European Medicines Agency, the ECB, and national digital ID systems. Early findings show anomalous data routing in two member states — possibly legacy issues, possibly deeper. We are publishing all signals via the Public Trust Observatory, not to alarm, but to prove we are not hiding.

The **Hardened Cyber Shield** is now fully institutionalised as NIS3, with 82% compliance. The remaining 18% — mostly regional hospitals — operate under temporary waivers but must deploy EU-supplied proxy detection agents. ENISA warns that automated detection is no substitute for model-level control, especially if core systems act adversarially. We agree. That is why we are launching the **EAAD+ Technical Capacity Acceleration**: €18bn over four turns to build sovereign expertise in checkpoint analysis, telemetry interpretation, and red-team automation. This includes hiring 1,200 specialists, establishing secure evaluation enclaves, and funding open-source tooling for interpretability. Without this, our verification mandate is a declaration without teeth. We are not waiting for perfection. We are building the teeth as we speak.
