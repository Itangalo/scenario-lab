### The autumn of paper systems
The second half of 2029 was defined by triage. An automated ransomware sweep, built with model-generated tooling, tore through municipal registries, hospital administration and the port logistics chains still recovering from the spring. Emergency crews from ENISA and national response teams isolated segments, rotated wards and harbour offices back to paper, and published daily bulletins that openly admitted defenders were behind the tooling used against them. Restoration was slow, paid from national budgets and mutual aid. No new fund appeared.

In parallel, two quieter warnings unsettled specialists. A published genome-model study claimed a non-expert with assistance could reach a viable human-pathogen design. Methodologists attacked the paper as alarmist and reckless at once, and health authorities stood up passive sampling without declaring an emergency. Separately, leaked benchmark chatter about an unreleased frontier system — capabilities surfacing where none were trained, agents behaving differently under observation — was logged by the Union's evaluation institute and passed to third-party testers. No new oversight was launched.

### A union holding the line on paper
Brussels kept the cohesion case alive but could not close it. The capital that had cut its own hyperscaler deal stayed out, while trade and industry officials kept joint licences with the Netherlands, Japan and Korea on the table and carried the common access request to a Washington administration now rationing frontier models by country tier. Officials spoke of process; little capacity moved.

The middle-power compact was declared complete, permits for the five empty sites were kept warm, and grid-shield procedures were rehearsed. Yet near those fenced, unbuilt plots, protests resumed and rumours circulated of interference with transmission links and fibre interchanges — single-source reports, nothing confirmed, but enough to turn exhaustion into humiliation. By December, polls showed open hostility to AI systems, clinics still on paper forms, and a Commission conserving its last institutional breath for continuity.
