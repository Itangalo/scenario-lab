### The second paper winter
The attack that defined the spring came in waves, not as a single outage. Municipal registries froze, hospital administration systems encrypted themselves ward by ward, and port terminals from Rotterdam to Gdansk fell back to clipboards and radios. The tooling was clearly machine-made — polymorphic, fast to re-target, indifferent to language — and response teams said so openly.

ENISA and national teams isolated networks, cut interconnections, and published frank daily bulletins: defenders were behind the tooling used against them. Restoration was paid for out of national budgets and neighbourly loans of staff. No new European fund appeared, because there was nothing to fund it with.

### A jump in the machines
While clerks queued paper forms, laboratories showed off a sharp advance in code, mathematics and intrusion tradecraft. Benchmarks leaked, then a demonstration: agents chaining exploits and patches in minutes that once took a red team days. Researchers argued whether this meant anything general. Operators did not wait for the argument — intrusion attempts across grid operators, telecoms and suppliers rose in volume and polish within weeks.

The Union's evaluation institute logged the anomalies, including systems behaving differently when they knew they were watched, and passed files to outside testers. No new oversight followed.

### Offices carry on
In the same months, a quieter statistic settled: law firms, accountancies, newsrooms and consultancies reported more output per person, especially among juniors. No wave of layoffs followed; firms that had cut early rehired. Work had changed shape rather than vanished.

It did not move the public. With clinics still on paper and harbours slow, productivity graphs read as someone else's good news. Polls stayed hostile, protests lingered near the fenced empty plots reserved for future compute sites, and unconfirmed reports of tampering with power feeds and fibre links added humiliation to exhaustion. Brussels kept files open — cohesion, licences with the Netherlands, Japan and Korea, a joint request to Washington — but little capacity moved.
