### The attack and the breakaway
Spring began with encrypted screens. A wave of machine-generated ransomware moved through municipal systems, hospital administration and two port logistics chains within days, exploiting an unpatched software dependency auditors had flagged in the autumn. Emergency teams redeployed, but without fresh funds they worked from spare-parts lists and national budgets. Daily technical bulletins from Brussels kept guidance flowing, yet coverage dwelled on delayed restorations and clinics reverting to paper.

While systems were still being rebuilt, a member state announced its own compute and model-access arrangement directly with an American hyperscaler, on pricing and conditions below the common ask Brussels had mandated. The capital called it pragmatism after the American election; Paris called it undercutting. The Commission opened a cohesion file, offered a return path through the joint Washington request and licence alignment, and warned joint testing would proceed without the holdout if needed.

### Paper leverage
With no money for the reserved sites in Paris, Berlin, Madrid, Stockholm and Warsaw, the planned two-hundred-billion build was formally dropped. Permits were kept warm through existing channels at zero cost, and the technology sovereignty package was declared complete — largely permitting reform and investment signals rather than poured concrete.

Attention narrowed to the middle-power compact with the Netherlands, Japan and Korea. Joint licence positions were tabled and a common request for computing capacity was carried to Washington, now under a new administration treating advanced models as a national asset with tiered foreign access. American officials listened, offered process, and conceded little.

By June cascading blackout paths stayed narrower, but defenders looked behind the tooling used against them. Polls recorded humiliation and exhaustion, and protests near the empty sites resumed.
