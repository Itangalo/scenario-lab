## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now acknowledge that without sovereignty in execution, neither mandate can be fulfilled, and that political capital spent without grounding in material infrastructure is not investment, but ritual.  
- Grounds: The collapse of `eu_political_capital` to −12.0 while carrying four active burdens proves that institutional legitimacy is not infinite: it is tied to demonstrable control over outcomes. The **cyber_major_incident** and **labour_displacement** have shown that harm prevention fails when the means of action are externalised, and self-determination fails when the levers of execution are held elsewhere. This is no longer about policy preference — it is about the material conditions of agency.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, but only when the commitment binds us to assets we control — infrastructure, talent, or enforceable standards — and only when it reduces the opacity of our own execution path, not just the frontier’s.  
- Grounds: The failure of the **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** to prevent the latest attack, despite telemetry collection, shows that awareness without sovereign action is passive observation, not governance. Our commitment must now include a requirement of control: we act early, but only where we can follow through.

- reclassify `no_single_point_of_failure` to commitment  
- Grounds: The **emergent_resilience_stack_adoption** and the organic spread of municipal AI clusters prove that redundancy is no longer a design choice — it is an emergent condition we must now formalise and enforce. By integrating these nodes into sovereign infrastructure, we are not just avoiding failure points — we are building a new architecture of control. This is no longer a position we hold; it is a line we are drawing.

- add `sovereign_execution_as_core` (commitment): We will treat sovereign execution — the ability to run, validate, and fallback AI-driven decisions on infrastructure under EU legal and physical control — as the foundational requirement for both resilience and self-determination, and will subordinate all other AI policy to its establishment.  
- Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** and the ongoing denial of frontier access have proven that no regulatory framework, no observatory, no redundancy plan has meaning if execution is not sovereign. This is not a new goal — it is the core condition now made visible by crisis.

## New measure  
**Resilience Stack Integration Directive**  
Mandate the adoption of the emergent Resilience Stack as the baseline technical standard for all AI systems in essential public services, with certification, funding, and interoperability support from the EU.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to resilience by hardening public services against AI-driven attacks; +3 to eu_ai_sovereignty by anchoring critical software dependencies in EU-governed open infrastructure; reduces fragmentation by aligning municipal initiatives under a common stack  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national governments, municipal IT departments, and public service operators using AI in health, transport, energy, and emergency response

## Priority  
**Resilience Stack Integration Directive** — because the **emergent_resilience_stack_adoption** has created a rare opportunity: a functional, field-tested resilience architecture already spreading through informal channels, and now ready to be scaled as policy, bypassing the failed procurement and inertia that have crippled our response.

## In practice  
We are no longer building in vacuum. The **Resilience Stack**, developed by a coalition of technical institutes, open-weight developers, and municipal IT teams, has become the de facto standard for local governments under pressure. We are now acting to formalise what has already emerged: the Commission will issue a delegated act under Article 12(4) of the Cyber Resilience Act, recognising the Stack as a certified secure AI framework. This will unlock Digital Europe Programme and Recovery and Resilience Facility funds for member states and cities that adopt it, while conditioning future EU AI procurement on Stack compliance.

The **Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative** continues under EuroHPC coordination. Site preparation in Finland and Bulgaria is complete, and we have secured agreement from the two northern holdouts by offering co-location rights and board representation. We are now fast-tracking procurement of hardened server units with trusted firmware, using the newly activated Crisis Competence Protocol to bypass standard tender delays. These clusters will run pre-staged open-weight models and, where available, licensed frontier weights — but only if execution remains under EU control.

The **Sovereign Execution Guarantee Framework**, our priority last turn, is now being reframed around the Resilience Stack: rather than imposing a top-down architecture, we are aligning it with the Stack’s modular, auditable design. Barcelona, Utrecht, and Gdańsk will be the first to receive certification under the new Common Security Profile, with ENISA conducting audits and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre providing technical support. We are not imposing order — we are codifying it. This is how sovereignty grows: not from decree, but from integration.
