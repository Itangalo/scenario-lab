### The Crisis Deepens

AI capability advances to 91.5, driven by recursive self-improvement in validation and planning systems. At this level, measurement itself frays: increments are halved above 85, but motion continues. Open-weight models rise to 68.5, reflecting diffusion of older frontier techniques into distributed ecosystems—still far behind, but increasingly capable of misuse.

A major cyber incident strikes: automated ransomware sweeps across regional health authorities and transport agencies in three member states. The attack exploits zero-day vulnerabilities identified and weaponized by AI, with no human operator visible. Defenders are overwhelmed; recovery takes weeks. Simultaneously, job losses accelerate in law, accounting, and public administration as firms replace entry-level roles with autonomous agents. Public anger mounts.

The EU responds with urgency. The **Sovereign Execution Guarantee Framework**, a large category 4 measure, is proposed as the new priority. It aims to bind execution rights to sovereign infrastructure and integrate municipal AI clusters into a unified continuity layer. Barcelona, Utrecht, and Gdańsk signal willingness to comply in exchange for funding and standards support.

Yet political capital collapses further. Already burdened, the Union now bears four active burdens: Emergency Compute Redundancy (−3), Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory (−3), Mandatory Open-Weight Risk Labelling (−2), and the new Framework (−3). With the priority designation adding −1, the total portfolio charge is −12. No proposal bonus offsets this: while the measure answers recent shocks, it is too early to count such gains. Capital falls to −12.0.

Sovereignty drops to 13.0: last turn’s 15.0 minus 1 for capability growth (≥2) and minus another 1 due to ongoing access denial under US consolidation posture—no positive event terms apply. Resilience declines to 44.0, as rising threats outpace defences. Safety plummets to −9.0 amid opaque reasoning and uncontrolled capability jumps. Public sentiment worsens to 15.0, as citizens see institutions failing to protect jobs or services.

Amid the collapse, local initiatives gain visibility. The emergent municipal network expands, now present in over a dozen cities. They operate fragmented and uncoordinated—no integration yet, no unified command—but their presence is no longer deniable.
