## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now acknowledge that without sovereignty in execution, neither mandate can be fulfilled.  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event and the resulting paralysis in critical services have demonstrated that situational awareness and early action are meaningless without sovereign execution capacity. Our identity must reflect that governance without infrastructure is performance, not power. This is not a shift in values, but a recognition that the material basis for both mandates has shifted beneath us.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, but now with binding investment in reducing the opacity that creates uncertainty—especially in AI behaviour, intent inference, and frontier access dependencies—and with the explicit understanding that action without execution capacity is symbolic, not strategic.  
- Grounds: The collapse of political capital and the failure to prevent cascading disruption prove that acting early without sovereign means of implementation leads not to agency, but to spectacle. Our commitment now includes the condition that action must be tied to assets we control.

- modify `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity; we will now treat local and municipal infrastructure initiatives as part of the Union’s sovereign redundancy architecture, provided they meet baseline security and interoperability standards.  
- Grounds: The **emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network** has demonstrated that functional fallback capacity is already being built outside EU coordination. Rather than allowing fragmentation, we must integrate these nodes into a coherent, layered sovereignty model. This evolution acknowledges that sovereignty is no longer solely a top-down construct.

- retire `two_year_commitment`  
- Grounds: The commitment period has expired, and the world has moved beyond the possibility of phased, deliberative awareness-building. The **cyber_major_incident** and **labour_displacement** events confirm that we are in a regime of continuous crisis, where the precondition of governance—sovereign sensing—must now be paired with sovereign action. The commitment served its purpose, but the moment demands more than observation.

## New measure  
**Sovereign Execution Guarantee Framework**  
Establish a legal and operational framework to ensure that AI-driven decisions in critical public services can be executed on EU-controlled infrastructure, with mandatory fallback pathways and real-time validation of service continuity.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to eu_ai_sovereignty by enforcing sovereign execution rights across health, border, and energy systems; +5 to resilience by eliminating single points of failure in service delivery; reduces exposure to coercion by hardening operational continuity  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national governments, accredited public service operators, and emergent municipal sovereignty nodes

## Priority  
**Sovereign Execution Guarantee Framework** — because the **cyber_major_incident** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** have proven that awareness and redundancy are useless without enforceable execution rights, and because the emergent municipal network must be integrated before fragmentation becomes irreversible.

## In practice  
We are no longer building mirrors. We are building levers. The **Sovereign Execution Guarantee Framework** will be enacted through a Regulation under Article 114 TFEU, co-decided in the Internal Market Council and European Parliament, with derogations for crisis override under the Crisis Competence Protocol. It mandates that all AI-dependent critical services — starting with emergency healthcare triage, border processing, and grid dispatch — must have a certified fallback execution path on EU-sovereign infrastructure by turn 14. This includes not just compute, but data sovereignty, model provenance, and human-in-the-loop validation protocols.

We are integrating the **emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network** into this framework by offering technical standards, security audits, and interoperability funding through the Digital Europe Programme, in exchange for adherence to EU-wide continuity protocols. Cities like Barcelona, Utrecht, and Gdańsk are already negotiating entry; their clusters will become recognised nodes in the sovereign execution layer, provided they adopt the Common Security Profile for Local AI Infrastructure, now under development at ENISA.

The **Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative** continues on track: site clearance is complete in Finland and Bulgaria, and procurement of hardened server units has begun under a joint EuroHPC tender. We are fast-tracking power upgrades via the TEN-E mechanism, but resistance from two northern member states persists — we are offering them co-location rights in the Finnish cluster and a seat on the Sovereign Compute Oversight Board to secure their buy-in. The **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** is now receiving telemetry from the Sentinel Nodes, but its value hinges on whether we can act on its warnings. That is why execution — not just observation — is now our priority. We are not waiting to be surprised again.
