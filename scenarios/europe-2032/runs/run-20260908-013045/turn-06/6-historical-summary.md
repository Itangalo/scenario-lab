In late 2028, the EU finalized the legal framework of the Tech Sovereignty Package but faced ongoing delays in Gigafactory development due to funding reallocations. The Sovereign AI Access Guarantee became operational, yet its effectiveness was undermined by Italy and Hungary’s expanding bilateral AI corridor reliant on non-EU cloud infrastructure, highlighting persistent East-West divisions. A cyberattack exploiting a compromised open-source dependency crippled health systems across three member states, exposing coordination failures and prompting the launch of the €30bn Resilience Surge to accelerate AI-driven cyber defences and zero-trust architectures. Legal disputes over procurement waivers slowed deployment, while the Cyber Reserve was mobilized amid growing strain on institutional capacity.

Mounting white-collar job displacement triggered cross-border labor unrest, met with emergency retraining hubs dismissed by unions as insufficient. Public trust in EU governance continued to decline, exacerbated by perceptions of reactive and fragmented leadership. The incoming U.S. administration classified the EU as a tier-two AI partner, restricting access to frontier models and reinforcing European strategic dependency. Despite urgent initiatives, political capital fell to 9.5, ai_safety remained stagnant at 19.0, and the Legibility Standard lacked binding enforcement, leaving EU sovereignty efforts fragmented and under pressure.
