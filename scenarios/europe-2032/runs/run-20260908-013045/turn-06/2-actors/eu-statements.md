# Statements: The European Union (turn 6)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI capability to anchor European strategic autonomy, with binding oversight, legibility, and operational resilience as the core conditions of sovereignty — ensuring systems remain under democratic control, technically auditable, and socially sustainable

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure independent AI capability to anchor European strategic autonomy, with binding oversight, legibility, and operational resilience as the core conditions of sovereignty — ensuring systems remain under democratic control, technically auditable, and socially sustainable
  - Trigger: the emergent_labour_displacement_backlash and cyber_major_incident this turn
  - Grounds: The original commitment to legibility and oversight was necessary but insufficient. Two further developments this turn — a major cyber incident driven by model-generated tooling and a cross-border labour backlash — prove that sovereignty now depends not only on technical control but on social endurance and systemic resilience. Without addressing the human and infrastructural dimensions, even legible systems will collapse under external or internal stress. This revision expands the conditions of sovereignty to include resilience and social stability, not as secondary concerns but as constitutive elements of strategic autonomy.
  - Evidence quoted: emergent_labour_displacement_backlash (emergent event): Widespread strikes and civil disobedience erupt across multiple EU member states, triggered by accelerating white-collar job losses attributed to AI adoption in legal, administrative, and financial sectors. Unions demand binding EU-wide impact assessments and moratoriums on public-sector AI deployment until retraining infrastructure is scaled. The movement gains cross-party support in several parliaments, forcing emergency debates.
cyber_major_incident: A large, largely automated attack lands. Its form is chosen at the time – a ransomware sweep across public services, a compromised software dependency with an unknown blast radius, or disruption of infrastructure directly – and severity scales with how far offensive capability has outrun defence. Damage is real and public, defenders are visibly behind, and attribution takes months. Whoever ran it, the tooling was model-generated.
  - Reason: The cyber incident exposes technical fragility; the labour backlash reveals social instability—both directly challenge the EU's sovereignty conditions, making resilience and social sustainability necessary for strategic autonomy.
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now anchor those commitments in observable system failures, societal breakdowns, and active threats, not just technical risks, to preserve legitimacy amid collapsing public trust
  - Grounds: The original modification tied action to observable technical failures. This turn, with both a cyber incident and a mass social backlash materialising, the threshold for credible commitment has risen: the Union must now respond to *compound* failures — technical, social, and institutional — or lose all claim to agency. This update reflects a deepening crisis in which public trust erodes not from risk alone, but from visible harm and perceived abandonment.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
