## In practice

This turn, we are closing two major measures: the Tech Sovereignty Package and the Sovereign AI Access Guarantee. The former delivered its legislative framework — accelerated permitting zones and private capital incentives — but its implementation is uneven, and the diversion of funds to audit tooling has delayed Gigafactory siting. The latter is now operational: the EU fallback capability exists, but its credibility is strained by Italy and Hungary’s parallel corridor and by the lack of enforceable conditions on access. That is why we are doubling down on the Sovereign Inference Legibility Standard — now not just a technical requirement, but a gatekeeper to sovereignty itself. Certification under the standard will be mandatory for any member state or agency seeking access to the fallback infrastructure. The European Interoperability Board, backed by ENISA and the AI Office, will finalise the draft by June, with pilot audits in France and Estonia by autumn.

But the cyber_major_incident changes everything. This was not a drill: AI-generated ransomware swept regional health networks in three member states, exploiting a poisoned dependency in a widely used open-source billing library. Defenders were blind until it was too late. That is why we are launching the EU Resilience Surge: a €30bn emergency programme under the Digital Europe Programme and the Connecting Europe Facility, co-managed by ENISA and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre. It will deploy AI-powered intrusion detection, automated patching agents, and zero-trust rollouts across all Tier 1 and Tier 2 operators, with binding deadlines and Commission oversight. We are fast-tracking procurement through the Crisis Response Protocol, bypassing standard tendering, and mobilising the newly formed Cyber Reserve — a pan-EU corps of ethical hackers and incident responders trained under the Digital Skills Compact.

We are also responding to the emergent_labour_displacement_backlash. While the Resilience Surge addresses technical fragility, we cannot ignore social fracture. We are redirecting €5bn from the stalled Gigafactories fund to launch emergency retraining hubs in cities hardest hit by white-collar automation — Milan, Warsaw, Marseille — in partnership with trade unions and universities. These will not replace a full flexicurity reform, but they are a signal: we are not abandoning those left behind. The political cost is immense, and our capital is at 12.0 — barely enough to breathe, let alone act. But if we do not act now, with coherence and force, there will be no Union left to save.
