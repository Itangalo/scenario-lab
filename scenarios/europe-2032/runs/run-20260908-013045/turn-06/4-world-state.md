### The Surge That Breaks the Bank

The EU closes two flagship programmes: the Tech Sovereignty Package delivers its legal framework, but delays in Gigafactory siting persist due to diverted funds. The Sovereign AI Access Guarantee is declared operational, yet its authority is undermined as Italy and Hungary deepen their bilateral AI corridor, sourcing models from non-EU clouds. The Commission’s attempt to tie fallback access to the new Legibility Standard fails to gain Council consensus, exposing enduring fractures.

A major AI-driven cyberattack cripples health networks in three member states, exploiting a poisoned open-source dependency. Response is slow, coordination fragmented. In the aftermath, the EU launches the €30bn Resilience Surge, fast-tracking AI-powered defences and zero-trust rollouts across critical infrastructure. The Cyber Reserve mobilises, but procurement waivers spark legal challenges from smaller member states, delaying deployment.

Simultaneously, accelerating white-collar job losses ignite cross-border strikes. Emergency retraining hubs launch in Milan, Warsaw, and Marseille, but union leaders dismiss them as tokenistic. Public discourse frames the EU as reactive and under-resourced. Trust in AI governance plummets further.

The American administration takes office, adopting a posture of strategic consolidation. Washington announces tighter access controls on frontier AI, categorising the EU as a tier-two partner—eligible for limited cooperation, but excluded from core development loops. The move hardens European dependence.

Despite urgent action, political capital erodes. The Resilience Surge, while necessary, strains an already overloaded agenda. Public sentiment, battered by disruption and perceived mismanagement, slips into deeper hostility.

### A Fragile Front

The Legibility Standard drafting continues, but without binding enforcement, its reach remains uncertain. The Union acts with urgency, but cohesion and credibility are fraying—sovereignty is not just about infrastructure, but the capacity to hold the line.
