## In practice

This turn, we are finalising the Resilience Surge. ENISA has delivered the core intrusion detection suite to all Tier 1 operators, and zero-trust migration is at 60% completion across healthcare and energy. The Cyber Reserve is fully staffed and operational, with rotational deployments in progress. Legal challenges from smaller states over procurement waivers are being resolved through a Council mediation track under the Digital Agenda Presidency, with partial fund ring-fencing offered in exchange for expedited approval. The Surge will conclude this turn, but its defensive posture cannot compensate for strategic exposure: when the frontier cuts us off, we cannot even audit what we are missing.

The Legibility Standard has formally entered into force, with certification pilots launching in France and Estonia. However, enforcement remains limited to EU-funded systems—less than 15% of critical AI use. National digital agencies lack the technical capacity to scale audits, and private providers are not bound. The standard is a proof of concept, not a regime.

The Emergency Social Stability Compact is active, with wage insurance disbursed to 12,000 displaced professionals and transition centres in Milan, Warsaw, and Marseille operational. Union co-governance has held, but public sentiment remains at 20.0—only marginally improved. The physical backlash against data centres in Bavaria and Wallonia has exposed the limits of reactive redistribution: people are not only losing jobs, they feel the infrastructure of displacement is being forced upon them without consent.

Hence the Sovereign Compute Emergency Mobilisation. We are invoking Article 222 TFEU (security of energy supply) and the Crisis Coordination Mechanism to fast-track three sovereign compute clusters: one in Finland (leveraging existing hyperscaler infrastructure under new public control), one in Spain (repurposing stalled Gigafactory funds), and one in Romania (as a cohesion and security investment). The European Chips Act will be amended to mandate that 40% of next-gen lithography output is reserved for these sites. National governments will be required to designate them as critical infrastructure, with military-grade physical and cyber protection. The European Investment Bank will provide €50bn in crisis financing, drawn from unused cohesion and recovery funds. This is not industrial policy—it is strategic salvage. Without it, every other measure we have ever passed rests on foreign foundations.
