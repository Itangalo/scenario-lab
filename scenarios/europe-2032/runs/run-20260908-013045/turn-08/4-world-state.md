### Strategic Salvage Amid Collapse

The EU invokes crisis powers to launch the Sovereign Compute Emergency Mobilisation, aiming to deploy three sovereign compute clusters in Finland, Spain, and Romania within 18 months. The plan repurposes stalled Gigafactory funds, mandates national contributions, and reserves 40% of next-gen chip output. However, immediate execution falters: Spain’s government hesitates, citing energy strain; Finland resists military-style control; and Romania lacks grid readiness. Legal challenges mount, and the European Court of Justice opens a fast-track review on whether Article 222 TFEU applies to AI infrastructure.

Meanwhile, the frontier access cutoff paralyzes hospitals relying on advanced diagnostic AI, exposing total dependency. The US offers no explanation, deepening strategic humiliation. Simultaneously, physical protests escalate—data centre construction in Bavaria and Wallonia is halted by blockades, with transformers sabotaged. Police respond forcefully, inflaming tensions. The backlash is not just anti-AI, but anti-procedure: citizens reject top-down infrastructure imposed without consent.

The Resilience Surge completes its rollout: intrusion detection systems are deployed across critical sectors, and zero-trust architecture reaches 60% adoption. ENISA declares systems more robust, but resilience gains are offset by new vulnerabilities—legal fragmentation and public resistance now pose systemic risks.

The Legibility Standard enters force, but only covers EU-funded systems, leaving most high-risk AI unregulated. Audit capacity remains weak, and private firms ignore it. The Emergency Social Stability Compact supports 12,000 workers, yet job losses accelerate, and unions denounce it as symbolic. Public sentiment dips as displacement and disruption outpace relief.

### A Union in Freefall

Political capital erodes further. The portfolio remains overloaded, and the new mobilisation fails to gain traction. With capital below 12, the EU risks losing control of its agenda. The priority yields no acceleration, and the measure’s ambition clashes with institutional fragility. Sovereignty, already hollow, declines as global AI advances and no domestic capability emerges.
