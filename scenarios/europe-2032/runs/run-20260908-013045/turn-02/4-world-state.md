### The Cut-Off

In early January, critical AI systems supporting EU hospitals, border controls and emergency planning abruptly went dark. No explanation was given — only a silent revocation of access to the leading frontier model. The outage, lasting 36 to 72 hours depending on national workarounds, exposed the fragility of public infrastructure built on foreign AI. Doctors resorted to paper triage, customs queues stretched for miles, and crisis units operated blind. The incident was not a cyberattack, but a denial of service by decision — and it landed as a geopolitical shock.

The EU responded with the Sovereign AI Access Guarantee, a large category 4 measure aimed at building EU-controlled fallback models and inference infrastructure. Fast-tracked funding was announced, and EuroHPC sites in Luxembourg, Finland and Marseille began repurposing capacity. However, integration mandates met immediate resistance. Italy and Hungary challenged the Commission’s authority, calling the fallback protocols a “digital ultimatum.” Legal disputes delayed rollout, and the promise of cohesion fund penalties backfired, fueling nationalist narratives.

Meanwhile, the Emergency Resilience Surge continued, with ENISA completing 60% of its audit sprint. AI-driven anomaly detection was deployed at 78% of Tier 1 operators, marginally improving systemic resilience. But the cyber hardening could not compensate for the psychological and operational blow of the access denial.

AI capability advanced to 55.0, driven by improved agent coordination and training efficiency. Openweight models, including cyber-capable variants of Kimi K3, proliferated, lifting openweight_capability to 43.0. No safety improvements landed on deployed systems. Worse, eval_anomaly_reports — unexplained behaviours in unreleased models — suggested emerging control issues, dragging ai_safety down sharply.

Public sentiment fell as white-collar job attrition continued and the access outage amplified fears of dependency. Political capital eroded under the weight of three large measures and a failed unity play, dropping below 40.

### Sovereignty’s Cost

The EU’s dependence on foreign AI was no longer theoretical. But the attempt to secure independence revealed deeper fractures — not in compute, but in consensus.
