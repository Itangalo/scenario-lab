## In practice  
The denial of frontier model access has landed like a circuit breaker across hospitals, border control systems, and emergency planning units. We were not cut off for cause, nor given appeal — only silence. This is not a market failure; it is a sovereignty failure. The Emergency Resilience Surge continues: ENISA’s audit sprint is 60% complete, and AI-driven anomaly detection is being integrated into 78% of Tier 1 operators, but we are learning that resilience without independence is a dam against a rising sea — temporary by design.

We are now activating the Sovereign AI Access Guarantee to ensure that no critical public function runs on a stack someone else can revoke. This is not about building a rival GPT, but about securing minimally viable, EU-controlled models capable of running essential services — healthcare triage, crisis logistics, emergency translation — even if all foreign APIs vanish overnight. We are repurposing €12bn from the Digital Europe Programme and leveraging the EuroHPC JU to stand up a federated network of secure inference clusters, hosted in Luxembourg, Finland, and Marseille, with openweight models fine-tuned under EU supervision for public-sector reliability. The Commission will issue a delegated act under the AI Act to designate these as essential infrastructure, mandating that all member states integrate fallback protocols by turn 5. The European Defence Fund will co-fund hardening of model weights against extraction and tampering, treating them as strategic assets.

This is not duplication — it is insurance. We are offering member states full reimbursement for integration costs and fast-tracking procurement under Article 138 of the Financial Regulation. But we are also demanding reciprocity: any state that refuses fallback integration forfeits access to cohesion funds during declared AI emergencies. The Council’s Legal Affairs Committee will meet in March to resolve jurisdictional disputes, and we are preparing a Communication on Strategic Dependence to frame this not as fragmentation, but as the price of remaining a decision-making power in a world where capability is power.
