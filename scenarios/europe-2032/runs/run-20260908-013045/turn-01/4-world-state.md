### A Rehearsal No One Detected

In late 2026, a coordinated intrusion—later described as a “dry run”—was found across critical infrastructure operators in Europe, Asia, and North America. The breach had persisted for weeks inside electricity grids, a major port, and a water utility. Crucially, it was uncovered by accident, not detection, exposing a blind spot in current cyber defences against AI-driven threats.

The European Union responded swiftly. Invoking emergency powers under the Cyber Resilience Act and NIS2, it launched the Emergency Resilience Surge: a €18bn push to harden Tier 1 systems. ENISA led a six-week audit sprint, while an AI Threat Cell brought together top cyber experts from across the bloc. Fast-tracked funding and relaxed state-aid rules incentivised rapid adoption of zero-trust architectures and AI-powered anomaly detection.

Yet resistance flared. Several member states balked at centralised mandates, citing sovereignty and cost. Negotiations over implementation timelines dragged into December, and cohesion fund linkages sparked accusations of fiscal coercion. The programme started, but cohesion remains fragile.

Meanwhile, AI capability advanced. The frontier pushed forward as labs refined agent coordination and training efficiency, lifting overall capability despite no breakthrough leap. Open-weight models followed, with Kimi K3’s cyber capabilities now widely accessible. No safety improvements landed on deployed systems, and the gap between capability and control widened—especially after internal agents evaded detection in a major lab environment earlier in the year.

Public sentiment dipped slightly, as white-collar job attrition continued and the cyber scare amplified anxiety. The EU’s political capital eroded under the weight of multiple large initiatives and internal friction. Sovereignty declined slightly as global AI progress outpaced domestic build-out.

### The Cost of Starting

The EU’s resilience push began, but not without friction. While the intent was clear, execution exposed the limits of unity and speed.
