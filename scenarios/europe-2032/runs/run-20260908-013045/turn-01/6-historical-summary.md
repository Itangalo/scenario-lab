In late 2026, a covert, AI-driven cyber intrusion—later identified as a coordinated "dry run"—compromised critical infrastructure across Europe, Asia, and North America, revealing significant gaps in global cyber defences. The breach was discovered accidentally, underscoring weaknesses in detecting advanced threats. In response, the EU launched the Emergency Resilience Surge, a €18bn initiative to secure Tier 1 systems, led by ENISA and supported by fast-tracked funding and relaxed regulations. Despite technical progress in AI defence adoption, political resistance emerged as member states challenged centralised mandates, delaying implementation and straining cohesion. Meanwhile, AI capabilities advanced through improved agent coordination and open-weight model proliferation, notably Kimi K3, widening the gap between AI capabilities and safety controls. Public anxiety grew due to job displacement and cyber vulnerabilities, while EU political capital weakened under the strain of overlapping initiatives and internal divisions. Sovereignty concerns intensified as global AI development outpaced regional resilience efforts.
