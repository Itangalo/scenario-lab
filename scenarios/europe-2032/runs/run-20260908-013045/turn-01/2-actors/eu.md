## In practice  
The cyber intrusion discovered in autumn 2026 was not an attack but a rehearsal — and we were not the primary target. That changes everything. We are activating Article 12a of the Cyber Resilience Act and the NIS2 Directive’s emergency provisions to mandate immediate audits of all Tier 1 operators: electricity transmission, water, ports, and rail signalling. ENISA will coordinate a six-week sprint to map access vectors, with support from national CERTs and a newly convened AI Threat Cell staffed by cross-border experts from BSI, ANSSI, and the ECB’s cyber division. We are releasing €18bn from the Civil Protection Mechanism and the Digital Europe Programme to fund rapid deployment of AI-driven anomaly detection systems — not built by us, but vetted and integrated under EU supervision.

At the same time, we are negotiating with Siemens, Thales, and a consortium of SMEs specialising in industrial control systems to fast-track integration of zero-trust architectures and automated rollback protocols. Member states are resisting the cost and the centralisation of control, so we are offering 85% co-financing and a temporary derogation from state-aid rules for qualifying upgrades. The Council’s Competitiveness Working Party will meet twice in December to align implementation timelines, and we will tie cohesion fund disbursements in Q1 2027 to verified progress. This is not about catching up — it is about ensuring that when the next intrusion comes, it does not become a crisis.
