## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI capability to anchor European strategic autonomy, prioritising fallback infrastructure and sovereign inference capacity over duplication of frontier training at this stage of the crisis  

- Grounds: The foreign access denial in January and the subsequent legal resistance have made clear that sovereignty at the level of operational continuity is now more urgent than matching the frontier. Our immediate task is not to train a rival model, but to ensure that essential functions do not collapse when one is revoked. This shift does not abandon our aim, but focuses it on what can be built in time and sustained against political headwinds — sovereign inference, not sovereign training.

## In practice  
The Emergency Resilience Surge has concluded its active phase. Its core components — AI-driven anomaly detection at Tier 1 operators and ENISA’s audit sprint — are now embedded in national cybersecurity frameworks, contributing to incremental gains in systemic awareness. But the outage revealed that even hardened systems fail when their intelligence is externally revoked. We are no longer defending only against attack, but against withdrawal.

We are now doubling down on the Sovereign AI Access Guarantee, treating it as the cornerstone of strategic survival. The Commission has issued a delegated act under Article 10 of the AI Act, designating fallback inference clusters as essential infrastructure. We are repurposing additional EuroHPC capacity in Prague and Vilnius to join Luxembourg, Finland, and Marseille, creating a geographically dispersed network resistant to single-point coercion. Legal resistance from Italy and Hungary remains, but we are shifting tactics: instead of threatening cohesion fund penalties, we are offering joint governance through a new Interoperability Board under the Digital Europe Programme, giving member states technical oversight in exchange for integration. A pilot with the Slovenian health system has demonstrated that fine-tuned openweight models can support triage and diagnostics during peak load — proof that fallback need not mean downgrade.

At the same time, we are launching the EU Fallback Inference Corps under DG CONNECT, recruiting 400 engineers, auditors, and safety specialists by turn 5 to staff these clusters permanently. This is not about building another lab; it is about ensuring that someone inside the Union is always watching the models, updating them, and accountable when they drift. We are drawing talent from national cyberdefence units, academia, and the private sector, with binding contracts under EU law to prevent brain drain. Funding comes from a reallocation within the Digital Europe Programme, avoiding new fiscal pressure. The first cohort begins training in October under a new curriculum co-developed with EIT Digital.
