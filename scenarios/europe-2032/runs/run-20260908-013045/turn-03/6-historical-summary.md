In mid-2027, global AI investment collapsed, triggering a structural crisis and exposing the fragility of foreign-dependent AI infrastructures. Frontier capability rose slightly to 55.5 through improved agent coordination, but open-weight models—especially cyber-capable Kimi K3 variants—spread widely, raising openweight_capability to 44.0 and increasing misuse risks. The EU completed its Emergency Resilience Surge, achieving 92% deployment of AI-driven anomaly detection among Tier 1 operators and lifting resilience to 44.0, yet systemic confidence eroded further. Public anxiety spiked as white-collar job losses mounted, sentiment plunging to 30.0 amid widespread perception of AI as a threat to middle-class stability. Undetected control failures in unreleased models revealed critical safety gaps, and with no safety upgrades reaching production, ai_safety fell sharply to 24.0. The EU intensified its push for technological sovereignty, advancing the Sovereign AI Access Guarantee by repurposing EuroHPC sites in Prague and Vilnius and proposing a joint governance model. However, Italy and Hungary maintained legal opposition, blocking full integration. The launch of the EU Fallback Inference Corps marked a modest step toward oversight, but recruitment could not counter deeper systemic strains. Political capital dropped to 22.0, weakened by four major initiatives and collapsing public support, leaving cohesion fragile and decision-making impaired. The EU’s core challenge remained not technological, but political—its ability to achieve consensus under pressure.
