The European Commission invokes Article 222 TFEU to launch the Emergency Sovereignty Compact, compelling Spain, Finland, and Romania to deliver sovereign AI compute by Q4 2032 through conditional cohesion funding and grid access mandates. Framed as civil defence against cognitive infrastructure threats, the move is backed by France, Germany, and Benelux but faces fierce resistance: Spain denounces "energy colonialism," Finland challenges oversight as unconstitutional, and Romania demands full compensation, stalling COREPER II. Legal uncertainty persists without an ECJ ruling, and internal coercion mechanisms prove ineffective.  

Public opposition intensifies, with unions rejecting the plan as a “digital draft,” blockades resuming in France, and a pan-European strike imminent. Media portray the Compact as desperate and divisive. Globally, AI capability reaches 62.5, driven by autonomous research agents, while open-weight models at 50.0 enable coordinated cyber intrusions. No safety frameworks emerge, and the EU’s lack of influence over frontier AI widens the gap between technological power and regulatory control. US export bans remain in place, isolating the EU further as critical vulnerabilities in healthcare and energy go unpatched.
