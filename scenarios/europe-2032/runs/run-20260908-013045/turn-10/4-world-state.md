### The Last Stand for Sovereignty

The European Commission launches the Emergency Sovereignty Compact, invoking Article 222 TFEU to bind Spain, Finland, and Romania to deliver sovereign AI compute by Q4 2032. Framed as civil defence, the plan ties cohesion funds to grid access, civilian oversight, and infrastructure upgrades. France, Germany, and Benelux back the move, while legal teams argue that US denials of frontier AI for healthcare and rising open-weight cyber threats meet the threshold for mutual defence in cognitive infrastructure.

Yet resistance hardens. Madrid calls the draft “energy colonialism,” refusing grid prioritisation. Helsinki rejects Commission oversight as unconstitutional, threatening legal action. Bucharest demands full compensation for delayed grid works, calling the pre-financed bonds “fiscal coercion.” COREPER II stalls, unable to reach consensus. The Anti-Coercion Instrument, designed for external threats, rings hollow when turned inward. With no ECJ ruling yet, the legal foundation remains contested.

Public messaging fails to shift sentiment. Unions denounce the plan as a “digital draft” diverting funds from job protection. Sabotage resumes near data sites in France. The pan-European strike looms. Media frame the Compact as desperation, not defence.

### Capability Without Control

Global AI capability climbs to 62.5 as autonomous agents accelerate materials science. Open-weight models reach 50.0, now enabling coordinated cyber intrusions. No safety improvements land. With no EU levers on frontier development, the gap between power and oversight widens. Resilience erodes as unpatched vulnerabilities grow in healthcare and energy.

The US maintains its frontier AI export ban. No dialogue opens. The EU’s isolation deepens.
