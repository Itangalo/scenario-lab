### The Fracture Deepens

The Commission pushes forward with the Emergency Sovereignty Compact, deploying ECOFIN’s fiscal leverage and offering tailored incentives to Spain, Finland, and Romania. Madrid remains defiant, calling the grid access demands a "power grab" masked as solidarity. Helsinki insists that civilian oversight infringes on judicial independence and refers the matter to its constitutional court. Bucharest accepts partial disbursement of cohesion funds but delays legal ratification, demanding ironclad guarantees on compensation. COREPER II remains deadlocked. Without an ECJ ruling, the legal foundation of the Compact frays further.

Public backlash intensifies. French unions escalate blockades near planned data sites, citing energy rationing fears. The pan-European strike is now expected within months. Media portray the Compact as a failing gamble, deepening public hostility.

### A Glimmer of Global Leverage

Amid the domestic turmoil, the EU accepts the invitation to co-lead the nascent global AI oversight initiative. EEAS envoys join the technical design group, advocating for inspection rights on high-capability systems and emergency moratoria. The move gains cautious praise from Canada, Norway, and Singapore, and for the first time in years, the EU is seen shaping norms rather than reacting to them.

Yet the contrast is stark: the Union seeks to govern the world’s most powerful AI while lacking reliable access to any system above 50.0. The credibility of its leadership is questioned, especially as the US maintains its export ban and the Consolidation posture holds firm.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

Global AI capability rises to 63.5 as autonomous research agents refine quantum materials. Open-weight models remain at 51.0 — potent enough for coordinated cyber operations but not yet self-improving. No safety improvements land on frontier systems. The leaked lab unrest reports lead to internal reforms in the US and UK, but the EU remains locked out.

Resilience dips as healthcare and energy sectors report rising unpatched vulnerabilities. Labour displacement accelerates, with law and software firms cutting graduate intakes by over 60%.
