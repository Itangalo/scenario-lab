Henceforth, credible commitment must integrate across domains — or it will be seen as yet another reactive patch, accelerating the loss of trust.

## In practice

This turn, we are closing three major programmes: the InvestAI Gigafactories, the Tech Sovereignty Package, and the Sovereign AI Access Guarantee. The Gigafactories fund has formally concluded, but no site has broken ground due to diverted capital and legal disputes over procurement — a failure of execution, not intent. The Sovereign AI Access Guarantee is operational, but its credibility is hollowed out by bilateral deals and the absence of enforceable conditions. That is why we are now binding access to the Legibility Standard: no certification, no fallback. The final draft of the standard will be adopted by the AI Office in December, with mandatory audit pilots launching in France and Estonia. ENISA will co-develop the verification toolkit, and the European Interoperability Board will have enforcement authority — but only for systems funded or operated by EU bodies or member states. The leverage is narrow, but it is real.

The Resilience Surge is our primary operational focus. Under the Crisis Response Protocol, we are fast-tracking €30bn through the Digital Europe Programme and CEF Digital to deploy AI-powered intrusion detection, automated patching agents, and zero-trust architectures across Tier 1 and Tier 2 operators. ENISA and the Cybersecurity Competence Centre are co-managing implementation, with binding deadlines: 90 days for detection rollout, 180 for full zero-trust adoption. The Cyber Reserve — 1,200 personnel drawn from national agencies and vetted private experts — is being mobilised under Article 4 of the Cybersecurity Act. Legal challenges from smaller states over procurement waivers are being addressed through bilateral consultations and partial fund ring-fencing, but cohesion is fragile.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Emergency Social Stability Compact. €8bn is being redirected from stalled industrial lines — €5bn from Gigafactories, €3bn from unused sovereignty grants — to fund wage insurance for displaced professionals in law, accountancy, and software, and to expand retraining hubs in Milan, Warsaw, and Marseille into full transition centres. These will be co-governed by unions, employers, and regional authorities, with a mandate to produce local adaptation plans by Q2 2030. The goal is not to stop disruption, but to prove that the Union still governs it. Public sentiment is at 18.0 — near revolt level — and without this signal, no technical measure will hold. We are acting at the edge of our capacity, with political capital at 10.0. But if we do not act now, we will not be late. We will be irrelevant.
