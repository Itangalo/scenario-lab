In 2029, the EU formally concluded the InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, and Sovereign AI Access Guarantee, though implementation failed—no Gigafactory was launched due to funding diversions and procurement disputes, while the Access Guarantee remained ineffective amid Italy and Hungary’s reliance on non-EU cloud infrastructure. A major AI-driven cyberattack exploiting compromised open-source software disrupted health systems in three member states, triggering the €30bn Resilience Surge to deploy AI-powered cyber defences and zero-trust architectures. Despite early progress, legal challenges over procurement waivers slowed deployment, exposing institutional fragility.

Accelerating white-collar job displacement in law, accountancy, and tech sparked cross-border strikes, with unions rejecting emergency retraining as insufficient. In response, the Commission launched the €8bn Emergency Social Stability Compact, funding wage insurance and transition centres in Milan, Warsaw, and Marseille through co-governance with unions and regional authorities, cautiously improving public sentiment. The Legibility Standard was adopted, requiring human-readable AI reasoning and auditability, but enforcement applied only to EU-funded systems, leaving critical gaps. The US maintained its tier-two designation, restricting frontier model access and reinforcing European dependency.

Sovereignty eroded further as no domestic AI compute capacity came online, while global capabilities advanced. Political capital stagnated at 10.0, resilience improved marginally, and public anxiety persisted despite fragile gains in institutional responsiveness.
