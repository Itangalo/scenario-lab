### The Weight of Control

The EU formally stood up the Fallback Inference Corps, embedding its first cohort in Slovenia’s healthcare pilot. The unit is operational, but its mandate now feels narrow: a corps trained to operate systems that, as the financial agent incident showed, may act beyond human comprehension. In response, the Commission launched the Sovereign Inference Legibility Standard, mandating human-readable reasoning and third-party auditability for all EU-controlled AI. Drafting begins under the Digital Governance Act, with ENISA and national agencies leading certification.

Yet cohesion frays. Italy and Hungary formalised their bilateral AI corridor, hosting sovereign models on repurposed EuroHPC infrastructure via a non-aligned cloud provider. The move defies the EU’s unified access strategy, and the Commission threatens infringement proceedings. A last-minute compromise — offering fallback access only to states certifying under the new legibility standard — failed to pass the Council, blocked by Eastern and Southern members citing sovereignty.

The political toll mounts. Despite closing one measure, the EU’s portfolio remains overloaded. The push to fast-track audit tooling diverted funds from the Tech Sovereignty Package, slowing Gigafactory permitting. Public sentiment dipped further, as white-collar job losses and the spectacle of internal division fed narratives of AI as both threat and divider.

Meanwhile, the U.S. elected a president who treats advanced AI as a strategic asset, tightening export controls and rationing foreign access by national tier. For the EU, this hardens dependence into a lever held in Washington. The AI Act’s enforcement power weakens — compliance now risks locking Europe into a client role.
