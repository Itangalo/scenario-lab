## In practice
This turn, we are in damage control. The Resilience Surge has technically concluded: ENISA delivered the final intrusion detection modules to Tier 1 operators, and zero-trust architecture is at 60% adoption across healthcare and energy. But legal fragmentation persists — Poland and Hungary have not implemented the co-developed protocols, citing sovereignty concerns — and the cyber reserve, though staffed, cannot operate across borders without bilateral agreements that do not yet exist. The Surge hardened systems, but not the Union.

The Legibility Standard is law, but not practice. Certification pilots in France and Estonia show that human-readable reasoning is technically feasible for narrow diagnostic systems, but audit capacity remains at 18% of what is needed. National digital agencies lack both tools and personnel. Worse, the standard applies only to EU-funded AI — meaning the vast majority of high-risk systems, especially in finance and private healthcare, are untouched. We have a rulebook with no inspectors.

The Emergency Social Stability Compact has disbursed wage insurance to 12,000 workers, but displacement is accelerating: 47,000 jobs lost in AI-driven automation this quarter alone. Transition centres in Milan, Warsaw, and Marseille are overwhelmed. Unions now demand sectoral moratoria on AI adoption, not just compensation. The backlash is no longer just about jobs — it is about consent. The sabotage in Bavaria and Wallonia was not random vandalism; it was targeted, coordinated, and met with tacit public sympathy.

Hence our full focus on salvaging the Sovereign Compute Emergency Mobilisation. We are in emergency talks with Spain, Finland, and Romania to renegotiate the terms: offering Spain a 15-year energy price cap in exchange for grid priority; proposing a civilian governance board for the Finnish site to address military control concerns; and fast-tracking EU infrastructure bonds for Romania’s grid upgrade. The European Chips Act amendment is before the Council, but the US is lobbying hard against it, warning of “fragmentation of the global semiconductor ecosystem.” We are invoking the Anti-Coercion Instrument in parallel, but legal services warn it may not apply retroactively.

We have no capital to launch a new measure. At 8.0 political capital and falling, we are one legal defeat or protest wave from total paralysis. Our only leverage now is time — and the hope that the Court upholds Article 222 TFEU’s applicability to AI infrastructure. If it does not, we will have no basis to override national resistance, and the Union’s strategic autonomy will be legally, technically, and socially unmoored.
