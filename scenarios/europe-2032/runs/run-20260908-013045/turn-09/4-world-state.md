### Strategic Paralysis

The EU’s emergency efforts to salvage the Sovereign Compute Emergency Mobilisation falter further. Despite renegotiation offers—energy guarantees for Spain, civilian oversight in Finland, and fast-tracked bonds for Romania—national resistance hardens. Madrid demands veto power over workload allocation; Helsinki insists on full operational independence; Bucharest ties participation to prior grid upgrades that won’t be ready for 18 months. The European Court of Justice’s fast-track review on Article 222 TFEU now appears unlikely to deliver a ruling before mid-2031, stripping the Commission of legal leverage.

With no new measures launched, the portfolio winds down. The Resilience Surge formally ends, but its gains are hollowed out by patchy implementation—Poland and Hungary refuse cross-border cyber protocols, and the cyber reserve remains siloed. The Legibility Standard, though law, is unenforced beyond pilot zones; audit capacity covers less than one-fifth of required systems. Private AI providers ignore it, citing jurisdictional overreach.

Public backlash intensifies. The sabotage of transformers in Bavaria and Wallonia is followed by coordinated blockades at two planned data centre sites in southern France. Unions declare AI-driven displacement a “structural coup” and call for a pan-European strike in January. Media coverage frames the Sovereign Compute push as a top-down gamble failing on all fronts: legally, technically, and socially.

The US maintains its frontier AI export restrictions, leaving EU hospitals without critical diagnostic tools. Washington offers no dialogue, citing “reciprocity gaps” in AI governance. With no sovereign compute online, the EU’s access remains contingent—and denied.

### Capability Marches On

Global AI capability advances to 61.5 as frontier labs deploy autonomous research agents that optimise quantum materials. Open-weight models reach 50.0, with a leaked version of a major lab’s agent framework enabling distributed cyber-operations. No safety or resilience measures land to offset the rise. The gap between capability and control widens.
