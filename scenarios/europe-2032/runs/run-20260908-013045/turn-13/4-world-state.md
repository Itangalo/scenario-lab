### The Brink of Enforcement

The Emergency Sovereignty Compact enters its final phase, with intense negotiations ongoing in the Council. Spain, Finland, and Romania remain divided, and no final legal ratification is secured by the end of the period. In a high-risk legal maneuver, the Commission invokes Article 222 TFEU to treat AI compute as critical civil infrastructure, attempting to bypass national vetoes through a proposed Council Implementing Regulation. This emergency centralization is justified by the Taiwan semiconductor blockade and growing NATO-aligned coordination among Eastern members, but it deepens institutional friction and triggers legal challenges.

In Spain, grid upgrades proceed under EU supervision, but medical AI access is suspended, triggering public backlash. Helsinki rejects the proposed oversight board as insufficient, and preparations for a constitutional challenge continue. Bucharest accepts partial cohesion funds but delays construction, citing unresolved energy cost guarantees. The Anti-Coercion Instrument remains untriggered — for now.

Diplomatically, the EU’s push to co-lead the global AI oversight framework stalls. Canada and Norway withhold commitment, demanding proof of internal enforcement capacity — which the current crisis undermines. No progress is made.

No new measure is formally launched. The concept of "Emergency Powers for AI Infrastructure Protection" is discussed within the College of Commissioners as a potential response to grid instability and sabotage risks, but inter-institutional resistance and lack of political capital prevent formal adoption. Without a decision, no portfolio charge is added for a new initiative, preserving compliance with scope limits. The priority remains unchanged.

AI capability rises to 65.0, driven by autonomous research. Open-weight models reach 52.5, reflecting modest diffusion within the bounds of frontier progress. No safety gains occur. Resilience holds at 45.0, as planning continues without deployment.

Public sentiment remains at 14.0, with sabotage of AI hiring platforms spreading. The emergency powers debate is widely condemned as authoritarian overreach, deepening social fracture.

Sovereignty increases to 7.0: the Emergency Sovereignty Compact advances significantly (+4 toward completion), though full ratification remains uncertain. This progress is partially offset by capability growth (−1), with no event securing new access. Political capital plummets to −13.0 under the cumulative weight of ongoing costs (−3 per active measure × 3 measures: Emergency Sovereignty Compact, Join and Co-Lead the IAEA-Style Global AI Oversight Framework, EU Resilience Surge), priority penalty (−1), and lack of visible delivery. No legitimacy lends or proposal bonus offsets the collapse. The Resilience Surge remains unfunded and inactive.

The EU acts, but not as a union. It enforces, but not with consent.
