### The Consolidation Takes Hold

The new U.S. administration formally assumes office, marking a decisive shift toward strategic AI consolidation. Frontier access is now tiered, with the EU relegated to a secondary status—consulted, but excluded from core development and security protocols. This hardens the Union’s sense of technological isolation.

A major, model-generated ransomware attack cripples health and energy operators across four member states. Defenders were overwhelmed, and containment relied on emergency manual shutdowns. The incident underscores the widening offensive gap and the fragility of current defences.

In response, the European Commission proposes the **Critical Infrastructure AI Shield Initiative**, a category 6 measure mandating deployment of EU-controlled defensive AI across critical sectors. The programme is tied to Recovery and Resilience disbursements, aiming to enforce compliance. However, the proposal immediately triggers legal scrutiny, with Germany and Poland challenging ENISA’s authority to enforce cross-border AI mandates. No implementation occurs this turn; the initiative enters a fast-track consultation phase, with adoption expected no earlier than mid-2030.

The **Tech sovereignty package** completes its rollout, unlocking streamlined permitting for data centres and attracting early private investment. Yet, the broader momentum is overshadowed by setbacks: the **Sovereign AI Sentinel Programme** faces grid approval delays in Finland and Portugal, while ASML export negotiations remain frozen under U.S. pressure.

Public backlash intensifies. A leaked study confirms accelerating job losses in legal and administrative sectors, feeding widespread protests. Data centres in Bavaria and Wallonia suffer sabotage, and an AI-assisted social insurance system is exposed for wrongly denying thousands of claims—sparking a political firestorm.

Despite these pressures, the EU repurposes €42bn from stalled Gigafactories to accelerate Sentinel sites, though physical deployment remains slow. Global AI capability advances to 61.5, driven by distillation and verification gains. Open-weight models rise to 50.0, reflecting broader diffusion of last cycle’s frontier models through underground networks and open communities. While more capable than before, they remain behind the frontier. No safety upgrades accompany the leap, and rushed defensive deployments introduce new risks.

### A Fragile Front

Resilience sees a marginal +2 from institutionalising EADC protocols, but sentiment plummets to 17.0. Political capital erodes under the weight of multiple active measures and a declared priority, with portfolio strain now factored into the −3 drop. Sovereignty dips to 13.0 as global advances outpace domestic build-out, and the failure to launch the Shield Initiative this turn underscores implementation friction. The openweight capability rise is contained within realistic diffusion bounds, preserving the frontier lead.
