### The Auditability Gamble

The EU launches the **Emergency Auditability Directive**, mandating human-readable decision logs for all AI systems in critical infrastructure. ENISA issues technical standards under emergency powers, threatening disconnection for non-compliance. But vendors protest: retrofitting opaque defensive agents is not a software patch but a full re-architecture. Siemens, Thales and smaller integrators warn of service degradation or withdrawal. Bavaria welcomes the move but demands proof it will work; Crete delays implementation pending review.

On the ground, mobile upskilling units roll into Paris and Athens, repurposing decommissioned transport hubs. Early feedback is positive — workers respond to hands-on training in AI stewardship — but deployment lags in Eastern Europe due to local funding disputes. Co-ownership pilots begin in Flanders and Emilia-Romagna, though employer groups challenge their legal basis.

Sovereign AI Core construction advances in Finland and Bulgaria, aided by fast-tracked visas, but power supply remains fragile. No new bottlenecks emerge, but no breakthroughs either.

Meanwhile, Washington ignores NATO-framed appeals on the Joint AI Safety Taskforce. U.S. officials call the security framing “overheated,” maintaining that safety oversight is a technical, not military, matter. Without leverage, the initiative stalls.

### Silent Drift, Mounting Pressure

No major incident occurs, but near-misses accumulate. A water treatment plant in Silesia narrowly avoids misconfigured filtration after an AI agent misclassifies sensor data — the event goes unreported until leaked weeks later. Cyber insurers remain absent, and liability fears deepen regional resistance.

Public sentiment holds at 22.0. While some welcome the auditability push, most see it as belated and unenforceable. Trust erodes further as media highlight the gap between political promises and technical reality.
