## In practice
We are closing the Sovereign AI Sentinel Programme with formal certification from INRIA and ENISA: the defensive agents are now verified for red-team resilience, cryptographic control, and fail-safe rollback under EU jurisdiction. The Kittilä and Sines nodes are operational, though grid instability in northern Finland still limits full throughput—we are invoking Article 122 TFEU to fast-track substation reinforcements, treating energy access as a security-of-supply issue. These agents are being handed off to the three certified integrators for deployment under the Shield Initiative, but only if national resistance can be overcome.

On the Shield Initiative, we are finalising the co-governance model: the joint oversight board will include ENISA, national CERTs, and parliamentary observers from the ECON and ITRE committees, with sunset review at turn 10. The telemetry trials with Japan and South Korea are now live, and Taiwan’s CERT participates informally through Singaporean relays. But Germany still resists mandatory integration, citing federal competence; we are offering them a pilot phase with opt-in SOC augmentation, paired with access to the Data Soil platform as an incentive for alignment.

Meanwhile, the Emergency Social Transition Compact is being implemented under urgency procedure through Article 153 TFEU. The levy mechanism is live: Eurostat has identified 47 firms exceeding the €10m threshold, and disbursements to the European Social Fund+ are beginning. We are fast-tracking retraining consortia in Poland and southern Italy, where displacement is acute, and worker cooperatives in retrofitting are already bidding on municipal contracts. The Social Partners’ Council will meet next month to audit compliance. This is not just compensation—it is the beginning of a new social bargain, one where AI does not extract consent but must earn it anew in every region it transforms.
