### A Surge in the Shadows

Global AI capability climbs to 65.5 as closed labs refine agent coordination, though signs of strain persist—model pricing remains depressed, and internal reports hint at verification bottlenecks. Offensive cyber tools derived from frontier models enable a **major cyber incident**, disrupting public health systems in three member states and exposing critical infrastructure SOC stacks as unprepared. The attack’s sophistication confirms that offensive capability is outpacing defence, despite EU efforts.

Open-weight capability rises to 53.0, reflecting gradual improvements in model efficiency and wider availability of training infrastructure. Underground replication of mid-tier models continues on consumer hardware, with leaked inference weights enabling narrow autonomous functions in cyber operations. While no formal release occurred, diffusion has accelerated modestly due to accumulated know-how over prior turns—consistent with the steady pace of progress—but not enough to support sudden leaps. The level remains below the threshold where broad autonomy becomes robust or general-purpose.

The **Critical Infrastructure AI Shield Initiative** reaches its finishing turn. While not universally adopted, integration proceeds in 14 of 27 member states, with ENISA’s threat feed now operational across energy and transport sectors. Joint telemetry with Japan and South Korea proves vital during the cyber incident, enabling partial containment. Resilience rises by +3, reaching 49.0.

The **Emergency Social Transition Compact** continues under urgency procedure. Wage insurance disbursements begin, and worker cooperatives gain traction in retrofitting and care sectors. Public sentiment improves to 27.0, buoyed by visible support measures and early signals that the labour transition is being acknowledged politically. Some local governments express interest in repurposing AI for regional challenges, citing ongoing pilot discussions around agricultural resilience and carbon monitoring—prefiguring what may come from future programmes, but not yet delivering results.

The EU launches the **Data Soil Expansion and Rural AI Integration Programme**, a large category 9 measure. Implementation will require at least two full turns before effects emerge; no societal feedback loops are triggered this turn.

The Union held—but barely. Defences absorbed a blow, workers saw relief, and tentative steps toward equitable AI narratives have begun. Yet expectations rise faster than delivery, and the window for credible action narrows.
