## Priority  
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)** — because the ransomware attacks on health and transport systems have proven that resilience is not a complement to sovereignty but its foundation, and without operationalised standards, even the most capable AI defences cannot be deployed in time.

## In practice  
We are forcing the operationalisation of CS-CO through a dual track: legal enforcement and financial inducement. The Commission has triggered infringement proceedings against the two member states blocking procurement streamlining, arguing that their delay violates the spirit of Article 114 TFEU by undermining the internal market’s security baseline. Simultaneously, we are releasing the first tranche of the €15bn Recovery and Resilience Facility disbursement — €3.2bn — to six member states that have committed to full ENISA standard adoption by Q1 2028. This is not optional: funds are tied to the deployment of AI-driven intrusion detection systems certified by the EU Cybersecurity Centre for AI, a new node within ENISA staffed by seconded experts from frontline operators.

Behind the scenes, we are negotiating with national procurement agencies to accept pre-vetted vendor pools and modular contracts that allow rapid patching and agent deployment. The goal is to ensure that by the end of this turn, at least 60% of designated critical operators are under active monitoring with automated threat triage. We are also expanding the classified threat-sharing protocol to include municipal-level operators in healthcare and transport, reversing the previous exclusion that left regional systems blind. This is not just about compliance — it is about creating a live, adaptive shield that learns from each intrusion attempt, because the next wave will not wait for us to finish debating.
