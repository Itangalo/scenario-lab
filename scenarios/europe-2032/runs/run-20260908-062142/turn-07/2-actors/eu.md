## In practice  
We are executing the Sovereign AI Sentinel Programme under the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking, finalizing the security architecture for the Kittilä and Sines facilities, where construction has now begun despite local opposition. The €42bn repurposed from the Gigafactories fund is committed, but grid delays persist: in Finland, we are fast-tracking a regulatory override under the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Directive to bypass municipal objections; in Portugal, we are offering regional development packages tied to workforce integration. The model training consortium—INRIA, Fraunhofer, TNO—has begun synthetic data generation under strict redaction protocols, and we are finalizing cryptographic control schemes with ENISA and national intelligence coordinators to ensure no single party can override safeguards.

Meanwhile, the Shield Initiative is stalled in legal debate, but we are not waiting: we have issued a delegated act under NIS2, conditioning Recovery and Resilience disbursements on verified deployment of Sentinel-derived agents by mid-2030. ENISA is building a centralised threat feed, and we are fast-tracking certification for three EU-based AI security integrators to avoid foreign dependency. Resistance in Germany and Poland remains, so we are offering co-located red teams and a sunset clause on emergency powers, reviewed annually by Parliament. Public sentiment is collapsing, but we cannot afford delay—the ransomware attack proved we are already at war, and the only question is whether we will fight with tools we control. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that when the next attack comes, we have one line that holds—because it answers to no one else.
