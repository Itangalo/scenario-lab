## In practice  
We are acting on the cyber_major_incident with operational urgency. The attack — a ransomware sweep across regional health systems and transport hubs in three member states — was enabled by AI-generated exploit chains targeting legacy systems. Attribution remains uncertain, but the tooling bears hallmarks of frontier model outputs. This proves that defensive preparedness is now a strategic bottleneck. We are fast-tracking the CS-CO regulation through COREPER II and the LIBE committee, using Article 114 TFEU to justify harmonisation across internal market vulnerabilities. ENISA has been tasked with delivering draft technical standards by June, drawing on classified feeds from national intelligence and the frontline defenders at the affected sites.

Resistance persists, particularly from member states citing fiscal strain and implementation delays. To break the deadlock, we are offering immediate disbursement from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s digital tranche — up to €15bn in co-funding — conditional on adoption of AI-driven intrusion detection systems and participation in the EU-wide threat-sharing network. We are also streamlining procurement under the Digital Europe Programme to reduce delivery timelines from 18 to 6 months. The goal is not just compliance, but cohesion: a live, cross-border defence layer that degrades the advantage of automated, persistent threats. Without this, no amount of upstream control will protect the Union when the next wave hits.
