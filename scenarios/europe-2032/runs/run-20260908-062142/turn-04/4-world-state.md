### The Shield That Failed, the Cell That Rises

The long-anticipated Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO) officially concluded this term—but not as a success. Legal gridlock over national procurement sovereignty prevented full deployment, and the promised mandatory baselines remain patchy. When a major AI-driven cyberattack crippled public health systems in three member states, manual interventions barely contained the damage. Attribution remains unclear, but forensic analysis points to model-generated malware, underscoring the widening gap between offensive capability and institutional readiness.

In response, the EU activated emergency powers under the NIS2 Directive to launch the Emergency AI Defence Cell (EADC), a small but fast-moving unit embedded within ENISA. Hosted in Tallinn and staffed by seconded national experts, the EADC is now operational, granted temporary cross-border authority to deploy defensive AI agents during active threats. Fast-tracked procurement waivers under Article 114 TFEU sidestep national delays, though legal challenges from two member states loom. The move is framed as a necessary bypass, not a permanent shift in sovereignty.

### Capability Creeps, Trust Erodes

Global AI capability rose to 58.5, driven by distillation and efficiency gains. Open-weight models now reach 46.5, enabling non-state actors to deploy increasingly sophisticated cyber tools. AI safety dipped to 25.0 as unregulated defensive AI deployments introduced new failure modes, and no systemic safeguards landed on frontier systems.

Resilience improved slightly to 38.0, as the EADC’s creation signals a shift toward operational readiness, absorbing some of the incident’s impact. However, public sentiment fell to 28.0 amid growing anxiety over job losses and perceived institutional failure. Protests intensified outside data centres in France and Germany, and regional councils passed motions rejecting AI integration in public services.

EU AI sovereignty declined to 15.0, as global capability advanced and no sovereign compute milestones were reached. Political capital held at 27.0 despite the new measure, weighed down by ongoing costs and the CS-CO closure without decisive gain.
