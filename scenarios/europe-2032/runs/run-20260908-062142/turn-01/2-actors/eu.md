## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)** — because the cyber_test_shot proves existing detection is blind to advanced persistent threats, and without minimum hardening across critical sectors, the Union cannot absorb or respond to AI-enabled attacks, regardless of upstream controls.

## In practice
We are acting on the cyber_test_shot with urgency. The intrusion pattern — deep, silent, and spanning jurisdictions — exposes a fatal gap: our critical systems are not hardened to a common standard, and information sharing is voluntary, slow, and fragmented. This turn, the Commission will draft a Council Regulation under Article 114 TFEU to establish binding cyber resilience requirements for designated operators of essential services, expanding the scope of NIS2 with AI-specific threat models. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will be mandated to issue technical standards within six months, based on classified threat feeds from member states and frontline defenders.

We are already negotiating with the Council’s COREPER II and the European Parliament’s LIBE and IMCO committees to secure agreement by early 2027. Resistance is expected from member states with under-resourced national CERTs and from port and energy operators citing cost and operational burden. To overcome this, we are pairing the mandate with immediate access to the EU Recovery and Resilience Facility’s digital tranche — up to €15bn in co-funding for qualifying upgrades — and fast-tracking procurement for AI-driven intrusion detection systems under the new Digital Europe Programme framework. The goal is not just compliance, but operational cohesion: a live, cross-border threat-sharing network that degrades the advantage of AI-scaled intrusions. This is not a standalone defence, but the floor beneath everything else. Without it, sovereignty means nothing when the lights go out.
