### The Consolidation and the Counterbuild

The 2028 U.S. presidential election concludes with a decisive victory for the candidate advocating strategic consolidation of AI power. The new administration treats frontier AI as a national security asset, tightening export controls and rationing foreign access based on geopolitical alignment. For the EU, this means no longer being treated as a peer in AI governance — dependence is now a lever held in Washington.

Simultaneously, a major cyber incident — a model-generated ransomware cascade — breaches public health and energy operators across four member states. Defenders were overwhelmed, and containment relied on manual shutdowns. The attack underscores the offensive lead now held beyond EU reach. In response, the EU formally closes the Emergency AI Defence Cell (EADC) as planned, institutionalising its protocols under ENISA with codified cross-border powers. However, legal resistance from Germany and Poland delays full integration.

The Union launches the Sovereign AI Sentinel Programme, a large-scale effort to build a fully controlled defensive AI on sovereign compute. Fast-tracked facilities in Finland and Portugal begin site preparation, though grid approvals face local opposition. Repurposing funds from the stalled Gigafactories project sparks tension with private investors, and chipmaker negotiations stall over delivery timelines.

Public sentiment deteriorates further as protests escalate into sabotage of data centre infrastructure in Bavaria and Wallonia. Workers in legal and administrative sectors report mass layoffs due to AI automation, and a leaked internal study confirms a growing displacement wave. The EU’s credibility is strained: its emergency response worked, but its long-term autonomy remains fragile.

AI capability reaches 60.0, driven by distillation and verification advances in clinical coding and contract analysis. Open-weight models now reach 48.0, enabling broader misuse. With no safety upgrades on frontier systems, and rushed defensive deployments introducing new risks, ai_safety declines. Resilience improves slightly due to EADC’s legacy integration, but sovereignty erodes as global advances outpace EU build-out.
