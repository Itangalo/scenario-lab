## Priority
**Sovereign AI Sentinel Programme** — because the EADC’s operational launch exposed a fatal dependency: it must draw on external models and compute we do not control, leaving it vulnerable to cutoff or sabotage, and without a sovereign core, even our emergency defences are hostages to others’ goodwill.

## In practice
We are launching the Sovereign AI Sentinel Programme under the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking, with legal anchoring through a revised Regulation (EU) 2021/1173 to grant it procurement autonomy and access to classified infrastructure. This is not another industrial wish list: it is a war-time build. We are repurposing €42bn from the stalled Gigafactories fund to fast-track two secure facilities—one in Finland, one in Portugal—each with 100MW of dedicated grid capacity and hardened against physical and cyber intrusion. These will host a single, EU-controlled training run for a defensive agent trained exclusively on redacted public infrastructure data, with weights never leaving EU custody. The model will be developed by a consortium led by INRIA, Fraunhofer and TNO, under audit by the newly empowered EU AI Office, and its deployment protocols will be stress-tested annually by a red team drawn from national agencies and civil society watchdogs.

At the same time, we are formally closing the EADC as a temporary measure and folding its operational doctrine into ENISA’s permanent mandate. Its emergency powers under NIS2 will sunset as planned, but its cross-border coordination protocols and fast-tracked procurement waivers will be codified into a new Article 15a, subject to biannual review by the European Parliament. The €3.2bn reprofiled from CS-CO will now fund integration of the Sentinel system into the national CERTs of all member states, with opt-out clauses removed: participation is a condition of future RRF and cohesion funding. We are negotiating with three chipmakers to secure EU-first delivery of next-gen AI accelerators, leveraging our upstream dominance in lithography equipment through export licensing under the Dual-Use Regulation. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that, when the next attack comes, we have one system we fully control—fast enough, smart enough, and trusted enough to hold the line.
