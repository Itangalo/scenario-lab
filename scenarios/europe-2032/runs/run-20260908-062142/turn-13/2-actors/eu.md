## In practice
We are responding to the attack as it unfolds. The ransomware wave — generated by model-optimized exploit chains, self-propagating across municipal networks — hit health registries in Valencia, school systems in Bucharest, and transit scheduling in Antwerp within hours. No single system was unprepared, but none were prepared *together*. We are now activating the Civil Protection Mechanism under Article 8 of Decision 1313/2013/EU, mobilising a surge capacity of 120 cyber responders from the EU Cyber Rapid Response Teams, with priority access to backup infrastructure stored in Luxembourg and Helsinki. These are not consultants — they carry temporary authority to override local IT decisions under crisis protocols ratified last year in the Cross-Border Incident Accord.

The Sovereign AI Core is no longer theoretical: we are booting its first cluster in Kittilä *now*, not at completion, to host recovery agents trained on pre-compromise configurations. This is not ideal — power stability is still marginal, and we are burning precious early uptime — but if we wait for perfection, there will be nothing left to defend. We are authorising ENISA to deploy these agents directly into affected systems under emergency override, bypassing national procurement objections. To Sweden and Finland, we offer immediate grid stabilisation support from the Innovation Fund; to Romania and Spain, we attach mobile upskilling units to recovery sites, turning repair into retraining. This is triage — but triage done right becomes precedent.

Meanwhile, the Emergency Auditability Directive moves forward under fire. Vendors still resist, but the attack has shifted the ground: even Siemens now concedes that opaque systems cannot be trusted in recovery. We are revising the standards to prioritise *incident reconstruction* over general interpretability — focusing logs on what changed, when, and who approved it. We are also accelerating the Joint AI Safety Taskforce through NATO’s CEP Committee, framing refusal to share model provenance as a failure of alliance solidarity. Washington may dismiss safety as technical, but it cannot dismiss sabotage — and if this attack traces back to a proliferated model, then the absence of controls becomes a geopolitical liability they cannot ignore. We are not waiting for permission. We are acting — because if we do not, no one else will.
