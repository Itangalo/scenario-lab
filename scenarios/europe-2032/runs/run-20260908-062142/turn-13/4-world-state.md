### The Fire This Time

A wave of AI-optimized ransomware cripples public services across Valencia, Bucharest, and Antwerp, encrypting health registries, school records, and transit control systems. The attack exploits self-evolving exploit chains, propagating faster than human teams can respond. Attribution is unclear, but forensic analysis points to model-generated tooling — a capability now in reach of non-state actors.

The EU activates its Civil Protection Mechanism, deploying 120 cyber responders from the Rapid Response Teams. ENISA overrides national objections to install recovery agents on compromised systems, leveraging pre-positioned infrastructure in Luxembourg and Helsinki. The first Sovereign AI Core cluster in Kittilä is booted early, despite power instability, to host recovery models — a high-risk move that strains the nascent system but enables faster restoration.

### Response Under Fire

The **Rapid Cyber Response Activation for Public Services** is launched as an emergency measure, integrating mobile upskilling units into recovery operations to convert repair efforts into workforce retraining. Sweden and Finland receive grid support; Romania and Spain get emergency technical aid. The operation remains chaotic but contained — critical systems degrade rather than collapse, and partial recovery is achieved within days. Full deployment is delayed by logistical bottlenecks and local resistance, limiting immediate impact.

Public sentiment remains stagnant. While the response is technically competent, it underscores systemic fragility. Media highlight delayed coordination and patchwork defences, eroding confidence further.

### Auditability in the Aftermath

The **Emergency Auditability Directive**, proposed in a prior turn, gains traction in the wake of the attacks. Vendors, including Siemens, reluctantly acknowledge that opaque systems failed under pressure. Regulatory standards begin shifting toward incident reconstruction, with new logging requirements prioritizing change tracking over general interpretability. However, full compliance remains distant, and implementation proceeds slowly due to industry pushback and fragmented enforcement.

The **Joint AI Safety Taskforce** is advanced through NATO channels, reframing AI safety as alliance security. Washington remains skeptical under the Consolidation posture, but pressure grows for coordinated action if the attack is linked to uncontrolled model proliferation.
