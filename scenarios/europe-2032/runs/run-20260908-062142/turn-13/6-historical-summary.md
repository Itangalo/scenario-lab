A wave of AI-driven ransomware paralyzes critical infrastructure in Valencia, Bucharest, and Antwerp, exposing systemic vulnerabilities and triggering the EU’s Civil Protection Mechanism. ENISA deploys cyber responders and enforces emergency recovery measures, overriding national resistance, while the first Sovereign AI Core in Kittilä is activated prematurely to support restoration efforts despite instability. The crisis forces rapid integration of mobile upskilling units into recovery operations, turning repair into retraining.

The **Emergency Auditability Directive** gains momentum as vendors like Siemens concede that opaque AI systems exacerbated the failures, shifting regulatory focus toward incident reconstruction and change tracking, though compliance remains partial and enforcement fragmented. Amid growing evidence of non-state actors using model-generated attack tools, AI safety is reframed as a security priority, with NATO advancing a **Joint AI Safety Taskforce** despite U.S. skepticism. Public sentiment stagnates as the technically effective but chaotic response highlights deep-seated fragility, eroding trust further and underscoring the gap between policy and resilience.
