### The Openweight Surge and the Sovereignty Response

An open-weight model release, matching the frontier at 65.0 capability, spreads globally within days, downloaded hundreds of thousands of times onto private hardware. Overnight, the EU’s lead-time advantage collapses: what was once a year-long buffer between closed and open capabilities is gone. The release, unrecallable and widely distributed, fuels underground AI operations and raises alarms across critical infrastructure sectors.

In response, the EU declares model access a core strategic asset. The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** concludes its audit phase, confirming deep dependencies on U.S.-controlled orchestration layers in European clusters. While enforcement mechanisms are now codified under the Chips Act and AI Act, actual control remains fragile. Building on this foundation, planning advances for the **Sovereign Access to Foundational Models** programme, with €900m tentatively allocated to federated EuroHPC sites in France, Germany, and Italy. Legal authority is asserted under Article 114 TFEU and emergency energy powers to compel telemetry sharing from U.S. cloud providers — but under the current U.S. posture of RETRENCHMENT, cooperation remains limited and enforcement is patchy. No immediate operational capability is achieved; the initiative remains in early development, facing delays in cross-border coordination, technical integration, and provider compliance.

The **Secure Model Repository Expansion** was considered this turn as a small category 4 measure to host models above 60.0 capability with hardened controls. However, due to governance constraints and the limit of one new measure per turn, it cannot be formally launched alongside Sovereign Access to Foundational Models. As a result, no proposal bonus or portfolio charge is incurred, and the idea remains in internal consultation, pending consolidation of political support and clarification of jurisdictional boundaries. Member state resistance over duplication and oversight persists, slowing momentum.

### Safety and Resilience Under Pressure

With no safety measures landing and capability surging, **ai_safety** drops further to 15.0. The gap between capability and control widens, enabling more sophisticated misuse. **Resilience** declines to 48.0 as detection systems struggle to keep pace with AI-driven cyber intrusions, particularly in energy and municipal networks, where adversarial feedback loops re-emerge.

The openweight release triggers a surge in distributed offensive capability, straining governance. While the EU advances planning on sovereignty, its political capital erodes under the weight of overlapping initiatives and unresolved public distrust. **eu_ai_sovereignty** remains at 29.0: +1 from the continued momentum of the Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative is offset by −1 from rising capability, with no additional event penalties or new measure bonuses. The absence of a second new measure prevents fragmentation of effort, preserving the constitutional limit on initiative density. The net effect is stagnation, reflecting delayed implementation and constrained bandwidth.

Emerging developments continue to unfold:  
- `emergent_grid_stability_resolution` remains active. Early detection techniques developed by European energy operators and AI safety labs show promise in isolating adversarial feedback loops in grid-control systems. While confidence is beginning to stabilize, full deployment of countermeasures remains months away, and the voluntary pause in automation upgrades continues in several countries. No systemic improvement in resilience has yet materialized.  
- `emergent_sovereign_compute_pool` continues to develop as a quiet coalition of mid-sized EU member states repurposes industrial supercomputing capacity into a shared sovereign cloud initiative. Though still nascent, it represents a growing appetite for non-U.S.-dependent AI infrastructure.  
- `emergent_open_source_cyber_tools` persists, with evolving evasion techniques challenging municipal defenses.  
- `emergent_synthetic_election_interference` continues to strain platform liability debates ahead of regional elections.
