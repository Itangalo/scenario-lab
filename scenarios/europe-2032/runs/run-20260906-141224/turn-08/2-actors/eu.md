## In practice
We are treating model access as infrastructure. The openweight release of a 63.0-capable model — downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in days — has confirmed our worst-case assumption: the moment a capability exists anywhere, it is everywhere. The **Sovereign Access to Foundational Models** initiative is now fully operational, with €900m disbursed to France, Germany, and Italy to co-invest in a federated repository hosted across EuroHPC sites. Legal anchoring draws on Article 114 TFEU, the AI Act’s high-risk deployment rules, and the DMA’s gatekeeper obligations to compel telemetry sharing. We are offering U.S. cloud providers a narrow path to compliance: verifiable jurisdiction over model deployment in exchange for continued market access — but we are not waiting for consent. Where refusals persist, we are invoking REPowerEU emergency provisions to prioritise energy allocation to sovereign-compliant clusters.

At the same time, we are expanding the technical scope of the repository to meet the new reality. The **Secure Model Repository Expansion** will upgrade storage, access controls, and monitoring to handle models above 60.0 capability, with hardware-enforced air gaps and real-time leakage detection. This is not about hoarding — it is about ensuring that when a model of this power is used in EU civil protection or medicines regulation, it is used under enforceable oversight, not in the shadows. Resistance from member states concerned about duplication is being met with binding commitments: access will be tiered, with strict audit trails and no export beyond designated public functions. If we cannot secure the models, we cannot secure the Union.
