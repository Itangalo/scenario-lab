### The Semiconductor Shock

The blockade around Taiwan brings the global AI race to a halt — not through code or policy, but through silicon. Advanced semiconductor exports freeze overnight, halting production for nearly all frontier AI systems. The EU, heavily dependent on foreign chip supplies, faces a crisis of access. Suddenly, compute is not just an economic question but a matter of survival. The Union’s leverage in upstream manufacturing — photolithography, materials, and precision engineering — becomes both its most valuable asset and its most dangerous exposure.

### Resilience Tested

Despite the shock, the **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** concludes this turn with measurable gains. Forty-two percent of Tier 1 operators now have fallback systems, and red-teaming has exposed critical flaws in grid automation protocols. These efforts absorb the initial wave of AI-driven cyber incidents, pushing **resilience** to 50.0. However, the success is tempered by rising strain: a coordinated cyber probe exploiting newly discovered evasion techniques disrupts two mid-sized municipal networks, underscoring that hardened systems remain the exception, not the norm.

### Sovereignty in Crisis

With **ai_capability** rising to 62.5 — driven by self-improving agents despite hardware constraints — **ai_safety** plummets to 20.0. No systemic safeguards have been implemented, and the gap between capability and control widens dangerously. The EU responds with the **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative**, a small category 4 measure aimed at securing existing compute clusters under enforceable jurisdiction. Though met with resistance from U.S.-linked hyperscalers, the measure passes under emergency powers, with audits mandated by mid-2030.

### Political Calculus

The **Tech sovereignty package** and the now-completed **Resilience Sprint** continue to drain political capital, but the completion bonus delivers +4, offsetting costs. The new initiative earns a modest proposal bonus due to the severity and recency of the Taiwan crisis. **Public sentiment** dips to 30.0 as fears of technological isolation and infrastructure fragility spread. The US administration takes office, confirming **US_POSTURE: RETRENCHMENT**, slowing global AI momentum — but offering little comfort to a Europe now fighting to preserve its own access.
