### The Cost of Sovereignty Without Security

Despite urgent efforts, the EU’s push for sovereign AI access fails to outpace the accelerating frontier. **ai_capability** rises to 66.5, driven by unimpeded global development under U.S. RETRENCHMENT, which slows neither investment nor deployment. In parallel, **openweight_capability** rises to 66.5, reflecting sustained global progress in open-weight models—no catastrophic compute loss has occurred, and development continues across distributed research networks and frontier-adjacent labs, even as access constraints tighten.

With no safety measures landing, **ai_safety** drops to 12.0 as the control gap widens—opaque reasoning and automated exploit generation become routine in underground toolkits. The **Sovereign Access to Foundational Models** programme continues, now fully funded and legally reinforced, but integration delays persist. Telemetry demands on U.S. providers yield minimal compliance, and federated clusters remain months from operational readiness.

The **Secure Model Repository Expansion** is proposed this turn as a large category 4 measure, responding to the immediate threat of openweight_frontier_release. However, due to its high cost and complexity, full implementation cannot begin immediately. Only preliminary staffing and planning occur this turn; full portfolio charge will apply next turn. No costs are incurred yet beyond the proposal bonus, and no benefits are realized.

Public backlash intensifies as **backlash_physical** erupts: data centre occupations and grid sabotage spread across three member states, framed as resistance to unchecked AI expansion. Injuries occur, and emergency policing drains institutional goodwill. **public_sentiment** plummets to 24.0, reflecting growing fear of both AI harm and state overreach.

Yet a quiet breakthrough strengthens resilience. The long-running **emergent_grid_stability_resolution** yields results: adversarial feedback loops in energy grids are now detectable and containable. Automation pauses are lifted, and confidence returns to critical infrastructure operators. **resilience** rises to 50.0 as the threat recedes—though full deployment remains pending.

### Political Capital Erodes, But Not Catastrophically

The EU introduces only one new measure, complying with the one-measure-per-turn rule, and maintains **Sovereign Access to Foundational Models** as its priority. Portfolio charge remains −6 (Sovereign Access −3, priority −1, no charge yet for Secure Model Repository Expansion). A proposal bonus of +3 applies for directly addressing a severe capacity threat. With no finishing benefits and **public_sentiment** well below **eu_political_capital**, no legitimacy bonus is awarded.

The net effect on **eu_political_capital** is a decline of 7.0 points—from 39.0 to 32.0—driven entirely by portfolio charge and absence of gains. This reflects mounting strain without crossing into unexplained collapse. The Union enters 2031 with narrowing bandwidth and rising pressure, but its political reserves remain within plausible bounds given the narrative and rulebook mechanics.
