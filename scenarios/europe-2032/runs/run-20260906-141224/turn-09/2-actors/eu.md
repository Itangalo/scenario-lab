## Priority
**Sovereign Access to Foundational Models** — because the openweight release at 65.0 has erased any buffer between closed and open capabilities, making sovereign model access the linchpin of both operational resilience and institutional control: without it, no other safeguard can be enforced at scale.

## In practice
We are treating model access as infrastructure, and we are building it like one. The **Sovereign Access to Foundational Models** programme is now fully funded, with €900m disbursed to federated EuroHPC sites in France, Germany, and Italy. Legal anchoring under Article 114 TFEU, the AI Act, and REPowerEU emergency powers allows us to compel telemetry sharing from U.S. cloud providers and to prioritise energy allocation to sovereign-compliant clusters. Where cooperation fails, we are prepared to act unilaterally — not to exclude, but to ensure that when a model of 65.0 capability is used in EU medicines regulation or civil protection, it runs under enforceable jurisdiction, not in the shadows of private GPUs.

At the same time, we are hardening the **Secure Model Repository Expansion** against leakage and misuse. This is not about hoarding frontier models — it is about ensuring that their deployment in high-risk public functions is monitored, auditable, and bounded. We are deploying hardware-enforced air gaps, real-time anomaly detection, and tiered access controls that bind usage to specific institutional mandates. Resistance from member states concerned about duplication is being met with binding agreements: audit trails will be shared, export beyond designated functions prohibited, and oversight centralised under the European AI Office. If we cannot secure the models, we cannot secure the Union — and this turn, we are proving that we mean it.
