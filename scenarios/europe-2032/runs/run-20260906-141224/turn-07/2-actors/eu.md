## New measure
**Sovereign Access to Foundational Models**  
Establish a legally and technically sovereign EU access point to foundational AI models at or above 50.0 capability, ensuring that public agencies, auditors, and critical infrastructure operators can operate independently of foreign-controlled cloud or API dependencies.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size:` small  
`Finishes on turn:` 10  
`Targeted effect:` +6 to +9 eu_ai_sovereignty by turn 10 by ensuring that EU institutions and essential services can access, inspect, and operate high-capability models without reliance on foreign providers; indirect pressure on openweight_capability via downstream control of deployment ecosystems  
`Applies to:` EuroHPC, national AI agencies, EU regulatory bodies, and designated public-sector operators in energy, health, and emergency response

## Priority
**Sovereign Access to Foundational Models** — because the Taiwan blockade and U.S. supply chain coercion have revealed that compute sovereignty is meaningless without model sovereignty: if the EU cannot run its own systems on its own infrastructure, all resilience and oversight efforts depend on permissions we no longer control.

## In practice
We are acting on the fact that sovereignty is not just infrastructure or chips — it is full-stack agency. The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** is now active: audits under the European Chips Act safeguard clause are underway at four major hyperscaler sites and three EuroHPC nodes. We are offering U.S. operators a clear trade — verifiable EU jurisdiction in exchange for recognition of equivalent U.S. assurances — but we are not waiting for consent. Where legal anchoring is refused, we are invoking REPowerEU emergency provisions to redirect energy allocations and prioritise sovereign-compliant clusters. The €1.8bn is disbursed in tranches tied to failover readiness and telemetry transparency.

At the same time, we are launching **Sovereign Access to Foundational Models** to close the gap that the current crisis has exposed: even where we have compute, we lack models we can operate independently. This is not about building a GPT-7 equivalent — not yet. It is about ensuring that the European Medicines Agency can run drug discovery agents without querying a Silicon Valley API, that ENISA can red-team critical systems with locally hosted models, and that national civil protection agencies can coordinate disaster response without relying on foreign inference. We are repurposing €900m from the stalled Tech Sovereignty Package’s incentive fund to co-invest with France, Germany, and Italy in a distributed, federated model repository hosted across EuroHPC sites, with strict no-export controls and EU-only access for designated public functions. The legal base is Article 114 TFEU for internal market harmonisation, supported by the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper obligations and the AI Act’s high-risk deployment rules. Resistance is expected from U.S. cloud providers and some member states wary of duplication, but we are framing this as continuity of government — not competition. If we cannot think for ourselves, we cannot decide for ourselves.
