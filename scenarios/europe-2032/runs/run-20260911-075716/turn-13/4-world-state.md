### Holding the line
Brussels chose not to launch anything new, and for once restraint helped. The recovery cell kept its emergency mandate through autumn, pushing clean backups from ENISA's pool out to the smaller councils that had waited longest in the spring. Grid operators held reduced pace for weeks longer than planned, keeping therapy wards ring-fenced while queues cleared elsewhere. Supervised reconnection checks replaced forced orders where crews had left manual switches in. It prevented a third lock, but left a patchwork of half-digital town halls.

That fragile hold was then hit from two sides.

A fresh largely automated assault swept public services again in September, exploiting the same compromised dependency chain. This time defenders diagnosed faster with allied telemetry, but the tooling was again machine-made and attribution again drifted. Services degraded rather than collapsed — a measure of the finished recovery work — yet images of closed counters and paper prescriptions returned.

At the same time, export controls tightened further. With Washington turned inward, licences were drawn tight even for allies: European labs found volume access rationed, replacement commercial compute unobtainable, cancelled data-centre builds unrevived. EuroHPC and banked Gigafactory time went to rebuilds, not training. Dependence, long abstract, became freight costs and queue times.

Against this came two counter-currents. Laboratories adopted a new interpretability check that made deployed-system behaviour more predictable — welcomed by operators, though far from closing the gap with capability. And clinics delivered tailored cures that did reach ordinary wards. Patients walked out, but polls tied the cures to foreign models Europe could not run itself. Gratitude arrived laced with humiliation, and did little to lift a public that linked AI to outages and jobless months.

Wage-insurance and short courses continued, triage hardening as queues lengthened. By December, essential services functioned, thinly and manually where needed — sustained, not rebuilt.
