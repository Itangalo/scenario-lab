### The second sweep
The compromised dependency was not finished. In February a second wave of largely automated extortion moved through the same councils and hospitals, this time locking backups that had been reconnected too quickly. Town halls that had just reopened closed again. Prescription paper came back. Grid operators stayed on reduced pace for weeks, ring-fencing therapy wards while everything else queued.

The Brussels recovery cell, still on emergency powers, held the line but could not get ahead. Clean backups pooled through ENISA and national teams helped larger cities; smaller municipalities waited. Partner telemetry showed the same intrusion elsewhere, which helped diagnosis and hurt morale — defenders were visibly behind, and the tooling was machine-made. Attribution dragged, as before.

### No money to rebuild with
Restoration collided with the finance reset. Cancelled data-centre builds stayed cancelled. Commercial compute European labs had rented vanished. Frontier labs abroad talked of training less, and with Washington turned inward, no one offered replacement capacity. Brussels did not re-bid. Officials banked the completed Gigafactory capacity and spent EuroHPC time on rebuilds.

For graduates, wage-insurance cheques and short courses kept flowing through ministries and universities. Queues lengthened and triage hardened. Cures still left the wards, but in polls they sat beside dark offices and jobless months and did not lift the mood.

In a few exhausted towns, crews left manual switches in place after restoration. Single-source press reports spoke of refusals to reconnect. No order for forced reconnection came; instead money for supervised checks, to avoid a third sweep.

Abroad, extended exercises around Taiwan pushed shipping insurance up and produced an expulsion. In Brussels it registered as freight costs and a quiet memo about supply chains.
