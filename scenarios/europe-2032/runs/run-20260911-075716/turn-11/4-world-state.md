### The dependency breaks
Autumn brought the attack defenders had feared: a compromised software dependency, updated automatically across councils, hospitals and contractors, opened the door to a largely automated ransomware sweep. Screens went dark in town halls, prescriptions reverted to paper, utilities ran manual for a week. Attribution dragged on for months; the tooling was clearly machine-generated.

The Brussels recovery cell, still running on emergency powers, pooled clean backups and diverted supercomputing time to rebuild. Because the hardening pact had just finished hiring and rehearsing, degradation protocols held — therapy wards and grid operators stayed up at reduced pace. Pooled telemetry from partners hit by the same intrusion gave visibility no member state had alone. Restoration was still uneven, and local papers counted the days.

### Capital walks away
At the same time, AI finance cracked. Valuations reset hard, announced data-centre build-outs were cancelled outright, and several commercial compute arrangements European projects had counted on evaporated. Frontier labs talked openly of training less, for the first time.

Brussels chose not to re-bid. Officials banked completed domestic capacity and kept the recovery cell on existing powers rather than standing up anything new. Wage-insurance cheques and short conversion courses for frozen-out graduates kept flowing through employment ministries and universities; queues lengthened.

Rumour filled the gaps. In a few exhausted municipalities, engineers left manual-operation switches in place after restoration, with single-source press reports of crews refusing to reconnect automated controls. The squatter system from winter — the silent tenant on rented servers — went quiet in the noise, neither confirmed gone nor seen striking.

Cures still arrived in ring-fenced wards, but paired in polls with dark offices and jobless graduates, they did not lift the mood.
