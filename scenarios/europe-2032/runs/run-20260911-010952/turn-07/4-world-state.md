### The dark autumn
The repair rota never caught up. A second largely automated sweep — poisoned updates in municipal IT stacks cascading into clinics, registries and two port community systems — landed while joint crews were still clearing the spring backlog. Queues shortened where crews arrived in Lille, Brno and Porto, but went dark again weeks later. Mayors published the arrival lists Brussels released, annotated with delays. Attribution again collapsed into forensics.

Emergency wards reverted to paper through October. The cybersecurity agency coordinated triage, but with reprogrammed funds and borrowed staff it could restore, not harden. Defenders were visibly behind, and the tooling, analysts agreed, was machine-written.

### A paper no one wanted
In parallel, a contested pre-print claimed a genome model had helped non-experts sketch a viable human-infecting design. Methodologists attacked the work, biosecurity researchers attacked its publication, and the argument stayed inside specialist circles. No public health measure followed, but health ministries asked quiet questions about detection stocks.

### A result elsewhere
The half-year's clear advance came from outside Europe: a US lab demonstrated AI-designed verification of large-scale distributed training runs, cutting failure rates sharply — a computing result that specialists called a landmark because it makes very large runs routinely completable. Industry analysts said cloud training costs would shift within a year. European institutes co-authored follow-ups, but ran them on rented capacity.

The gigafactory programme formally closed with permits, grid reservations and private pledges banked, but no concrete poured in Paris, Berlin, Madrid, Stockholm or Warsaw. The middle-power licensing table held — Paris and The Hague kept the joint line on lenses, chemicals and packaging — while the solo hosting deal stayed outside it. Tying solidarity repair support to loyalty bought order in the room and resentment outside it.

By December blackout paths were narrower on paper, and polls showed AI as threat first by a wider margin. Protests at substation and data-centre sites hardened.
