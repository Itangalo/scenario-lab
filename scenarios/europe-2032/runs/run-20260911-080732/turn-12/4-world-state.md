### The night the backups went dark
In February the sweep found a new gear. A wave of machine-built ransomware moved almost simultaneously through municipal billing suites and the logistics platforms behind clinics and city utilities. Screens froze at reception desks from the Ruhr to Lombardy; backup servers encrypted where isolation gates were still half-installed. ENISA later called it largely automated, with tooling assembled too fast for signature defences. Attribution, officials said, would take months.

Defenders were visibly behind for weeks. Hospitals reverted to paper triage, two cities paused utility billing, and evening news ran long queues alongside maps of dark districts.

### Strained repair under existing orders
Brussels answered through the existing joint response cell, with no new measure this turn beyond the ongoing EU Certified Assurance Rollout pilot trials. The existing isolation orders held in audited districts, which restored from clean copies in days and stayed running behind the gates. Elsewhere recovery stretched to weeks.

The attempted extension of French-Dutch crews to the backlog districts was only partially staffed. Inter-state secondments and audit teams could not be scaled on short notice while systems were down, and telemetry integration waited on personnel who were already deployed. Recovery funds from the already-budgeted isolation programme were released slowly, and the proposed audit conditions and human-approval switches for health and utility software remained in technical talks, not yet enforced. Council halls filled with shouting over delayed payments and dark screens.

The completed isolation programme and the completed EU Sovereign Fallback Stack blunted the worst cascade — essential services degraded rather than stopped — but did not prevent real, public damage. Certification work stayed in the lab: pilot trials on essential operators and European-hosted models showed mixed results and limited coverage, and two large providers stayed in talks without agreeing to deploy.

Courts quietly froze more contractor payments in the procurement-assistant cases, and talk of a public inquiry grew louder. Single-source reports spoke of coordinated municipal claims, but nothing was confirmed.
