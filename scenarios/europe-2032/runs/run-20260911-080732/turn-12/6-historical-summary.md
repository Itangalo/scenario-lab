Early containment collapsed in February into a machine-built ransomware wave hitting municipal billing and clinic/city-utility logistics from the Ruhr to Lombardy, encrypting backups where isolation gates were incomplete. ENISA described it as largely automated; attribution pending months. Hospitals reverted to paper triage, cities paused utility billing.

No new EU measure was taken beyond ongoing Certified Assurance pilot trials. Audited isolated districts restored in days; others took weeks amid protests over payments and outages. French-Dutch repair extension was only partially staffed, secondments unscalable, telemetry and funds slow, and audit conditions/human-approval switches remained in talks. The completed isolation programme and Sovereign Fallback Stack prevented total stoppage but not public damage. Certification pilots showed mixed results, limited coverage, with large providers refusing deployment. Procurement-assistant lawsuits advanced with frozen payments and inquiry talk; reports of coordinated municipal claims unconfirmed.
