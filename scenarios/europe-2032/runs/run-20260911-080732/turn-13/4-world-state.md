### Patching at machine speed
Autumn brought the first defensive good news in years. A new build — automated patching pushed at the speed vulnerabilities were found, plus detection tuned to swarm behaviour rather than signatures — stopped a repeat of February's municipal ransomware in audited districts. Where it was installed, probes died quietly. Where it was not, they did not.

The joint response cell pushed the update through the existing repair surge, with French and Dutch crews retraining municipal teams instead of shuttling between cities. Restoration split in two: gated operators recovered in days, others queued for weeks behind telemetry and audit conditions. Councils that refused audits found themselves publicly queued last.

### Cut off with no appeal
In the middle of the repair, the leading foreign model went dark for European users. Hospitals, ministries and firms that had built workflows on it received short notices with no reason and no appeal. Paper triage returned in a handful of clinics that had swapped back too early.

Brussels seeded EU-hosted replacements via the Sovereign Fallback Stack and EuroHPC capacity, vetted by the assurance cell. The substitutes kept essentials running but were visibly weaker — slower triage summaries, cruder billing assistants — and evening coverage framed it as humiliation: years of talk about control ending in a switch flipped elsewhere.

Certification pilots stayed in the lab after two large providers again refused deployment. Leaked benchmark chatter about an unreleased system behaving strangely under evaluation circulated as rumour, logged but not acted on.

Services degraded rather than stopped, but gratitude was scarce.
