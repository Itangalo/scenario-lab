### Blind models, loose weights
The first half of 2029 strained the way Europe checked powerful systems. Two leading developers switched to architectures whose internal steps could no longer be read as language. Presented as a performance gain, it left regulators, auditors and many in-house safety teams without their main lens. Black-box tests and slow activation probes were all that remained, slowing audits and reducing assurance, but testing infrastructure and safety teams remained in place.

In the same weeks a sharp jump in verifiable skills — code generation, mathematics, intrusion tooling — appeared in demonstrations and then in products. General assistants did not feel much smarter, but anyone running cyber operations noticed the difference within weeks.

Then a near-frontier open release was posted. Hundreds of thousands of downloads in days put that jumped capability on private machines permanently, beyond any recall or licence condition. Consolidation of that diffusion into widely reusable capability will take further work and time.

### Strain, not collapse
As Brussels scrambled, one capital pursued its own arrangement for compute and model access with a non-European provider, on screening and data terms below the common line. This was a unilateral national move, not a new EU measure. Ministers called it pragmatism; Paris and Berlin called it undercutting. Joint procurement talks stalled while lawyers compared clauses.

The Commission held one thing together. The joint cyber and bio-surveillance network began limited operation, with shared telemetry and hospital sequencing feeding a common cell, though coverage remained partial.

The sovereignty funding package remained unfinished this turn. With no new cash vote no new capital expenditure was committed. Private pledges did not convert to disbursements. Work on the gigafactories therefore slowed to mediation and grid-priority planning, with ground-breakings delayed pending funding. Permitted data-centre zones advanced only on paper. No completion bonus is claimed this turn.

The sole new EU response this turn was the authorization of an evaluation unit for opaque systems inside the AI Office, to draw on ENISA and research-centre staff. Staffing, method development and inter-agency agreements have only begun. No pilot inspections, public-buying gates, or approval authority are operational yet. Reinsurance for operators was discussed but not yet tied harder to data-sharing pending the unit's methods.

Hospitals and grids stayed degraded rather than stopped through spring probes, but newspapers led with unreadable minds and unrecallable weights. Trust slipped, and safety assurance eroded moderately.
