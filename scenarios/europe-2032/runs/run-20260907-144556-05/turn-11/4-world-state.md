### The Emergency That Wasn’t

The European Union’s bid to suspend AI in public services collapses before it can take legal effect. With political capital at just 6.0, the bloc lacks the authority to impose even temporary measures. The invocation of Article 114 TFEU is immediately challenged by Germany, Poland, and the Czech Republic, who argue that welfare systems fall under national sovereignty. The European Parliament delays debate indefinitely, citing procedural concerns. No member state fully implements the suspension; some pause new deployments, but most continue operations unchanged.

The **Integrity Audit Expansion** concludes its run, but its findings are ignored. Without enforcement teeth or public trust, the reports are dismissed as bureaucratic formalities. Civil society groups reject co-design offers, calling the process a “crisis performance.” The promised redress pathways remain undefined, and no system is taken offline.

### Fracture and Fire

Protests escalate into broader disruption. In France and Belgium, union-backed actions disable local AI-driven administrative portals. In Italy, municipal chatbots are flooded with adversarial queries until they crash. These acts are not crippling, but they signal a shift: resistance is no longer symbolic. Meanwhile, the **backlash_physical** event unfolds—data centre access roads are blockaded, and a transformer serving a major AI site is sabotaged, causing localized outages. Policing the unrest drains resources without restoring legitimacy.

A fragile bright spot emerges: the EU is invited into a **joint_threat_response** pact on AI-enabled cyber threats. Real-time intelligence sharing with allied states improves detection and response coordination, marginally boosting resilience. But the gain is external—driven by others’ initiative, not EU leadership.

### Drift and Decay

No new industrial or sovereign capacity comes online. The **Sovereign AI Core Programme** and related category 4 measures remain in final implementation stages but are not yet complete. Delays in staffing, component procurement, and inter-state coordination have pushed final commissioning into early 2032. While core infrastructure is in place, integration and validation are ongoing, and no operational benefit has been realized. The US, under **CONSOLIDATION**, offers no new access. The frontier advances to 70.0, driven by opaque, autonomous systems the EU cannot audit or control.

Openweight capability sees modest growth, rising to 54.0, as leaked tools from frontier labs begin to circulate in research networks and are cautiously integrated into non-critical academic and civic projects. However, the diffusion is uneven and lacks coordination—most institutions lack the expertise or compute to deploy these models at scale. The increase reflects early-stage absorption, not mature capability, and falls well short of frontier levels. The EU’s containment strategy remains strained but not yet obsolete.
