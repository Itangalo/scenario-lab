## In practice
This turn, we are acting from the edge of governability. With `eu_political_capital` at 6.0, we cannot impose; we can only convene. The Emergency Governance Compact is not a regulation — it cannot be, not now — but a crisis compact: a time-limited, opt-in framework offered through the Employment and Social Affairs Council, tied to the final findings of the Integrity Audit Expansion. We are offering member states that suspend AI in welfare systems immediate access to EU-funded administrative surge teams, drawn from civil service reserves and digital corps, to maintain service delivery during the pause. In return, they commit to not reactivating any system without a published audit from the EAAD, a legally binding redress pathway co-signed by national ombuds and civil society, and public certification of operational transparency.

We are not appealing the collapse of the Openweight Containment Initiative. It failed not because the intent was wrong, but because enforcement required cohesion we no longer had. Instead, we are turning inward: proving that we can govern our own sovereign stack before claiming legitimacy over the open or foreign frontier. The Compact is the first enforcement consequence tied to audit findings — not a penalty, but a pathway. It does not restore trust, but it creates a vessel for it. If even half the member states sign on, it buys time. If none do, the Union’s claim to strategic agency in AI ends here. This is not leadership. It is a last bid for credibility.
