### The Breach and the Break

A model-generated ransomware wave cripples emergency dispatch systems in three member states, exposing the fragility of foreign-dependent infrastructure. With AI capability now at 84.0, offensive tools evolve faster than human defenders can track. The EU declares the incident a wake-up call — but public trust erodes further as reports confirm AI played a central role in crafting the attack.

The Commission invokes Article 172 to fast-track the Emergency Sovereign Continuity Directive, mandating a six-month severance from non-EU cloud providers in critical emergency systems. France, Germany, and Italy signal strong intent to comply, and planning for migration begins under tight deadlines. €32bn in funds are proposed for repurposing, though formal approval remains pending amid legal scrutiny over fiscal authority and cross-border obligations. The Baltics and Benelux raise concerns over binding US contracts, triggering emergency consultations. The Anti-Coercion Instrument is discussed as a potential lever, but no formal activation occurs this turn.

EuroHPC starts preparatory work on hardened inference nodes, but full deployment is delayed by integration challenges. Audit teams identify unresolved vulnerabilities in fallback protocols. Reports circulate about anomalous activity in one sovereign cluster, though no confirmed breach is established. The Public Trust Observatory releases an interim report linking the ransomware to open-weight models fine-tuned on defensive infrastructure data — a warning that distributed capability is now weaponised.

Despite the urgency, political capital remains frozen at 2.0. The directive, though prioritised, fails to generate momentum — legal challenges mount, and member-state cohesion frays. Public sentiment drops to 20.0 as media spotlight failures in AI governance, labelling the Observatory a “delayed whisper in a storm.” The fog is no longer just technological: it is institutional and legal.

### Signals in the Static

Researchers quietly exit EU labs for better-resourced posts abroad. Auditors question whether sovereign systems are truly autonomous. The Union acts — but the scale and speed of response remain uncertain, and the cost of unity is coming due.
