Global AI capability rises to 84.0, enabling highly adaptive offensive cyber-tools that exploit weaknesses in critical infrastructure. A coordinated AI-generated ransomware attack disrupts emergency dispatch systems in three EU member states, exposing systemic fragility and deepening reliance on foreign cloud providers. In response, the EU invokes Article 172 to launch the Emergency Sovereign Continuity Directive, mandating a six-month severance from non-EU cloud services in critical systems. France, Germany, and Italy commit to rapid migration, backed by a proposed €32bn fund, though legal disputes over fiscal authority and binding US contracts stall approval, particularly in the Baltics and Benelux. The Anti-Coercion Instrument is considered but not activated.

EuroHPC begins prep work on hardened inference nodes, but integration delays and unresolved fallback vulnerabilities persist. Anomalous activity is detected in one sovereign cluster, raising alarms about potential compromise. The Public Trust Observatory issues an interim report linking the attack to fine-tuned open-weight models, confirming that distributed AI capability is now weaponized. Despite heightened urgency, political capital remains stagnant at 2.0 due to institutional inertia and legal fragmentation. Public sentiment plunges to 20.0 amid widespread condemnation of failed AI governance and the perceived ineffectuality of oversight mechanisms. Sovereignty improves slightly as autonomy efforts accelerate, but institutional weakness, talent outflow, and audit doubts about true system independence reveal ongoing fragility. Resilience holds at 50.0, yet the gap between threat evolution and defensive capacity widens.
