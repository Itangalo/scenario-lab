## Priority
**Priority:** Sovereign Resilience Backbone  
This is our top priority because the Emergency Sovereign Continuity Directive exposed a deeper truth: sovereignty without resilience is a shell — and trust without verification is propaganda. We acted to cut foreign dependencies, but we did not act fast enough to make our own systems trustworthy. With anomalous activity detected in a sovereign cluster and ai_safety at 1.0, we can no longer assume our defences are secure simply because they are ours. This measure ensures that independence is not just legal but operational, and that resilience is not just technical but political.

## In practice
We are launching the Sovereign Resilience Backbone under Article 172 TFEU’s emergency clause, with co-funding from the repurposed InvestAI envelope and military infrastructure budgets under PESCO coordination. The programme will deploy four new hardened inference clusters — two inland, two coastal — with full geographic and jurisdictional separation, each capable of sustaining emergency dispatch, civil protection coordination, and basic public administration during total external disconnection. These nodes will run on openweight-derived models fine-tuned for defensive integrity, with real-time interpretability feeds monitored by ENISA and cross-verified by the Public Trust Observatory. Weekly red-teaming exercises will be conducted by a newly empowered Joint Cyber Unit, with results published in anonymised form to maintain public confidence.

Implementation will be led by EuroHPC in coordination with national CERTs and the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium. The first cluster will be operational by turn 12, the second by turn 13, and full redundancy achieved by turn 14. Transitional funding of 75% will be offered to member states that complete fallback integration by turn 12, with full liability assumed by the Union for service disruption — a critical incentive for the Baltics and Benelux, where legal exposure from broken US contracts remains a barrier. We are also establishing a new audit protocol — the Sovereign Integrity Standard — which will require continuous proof of independence, including supply-chain provenance for hardware, runtime isolation, and cryptographic attestation of model provenance. This standard will be enforced by ENISA and made public through the Observatory, turning technical assurance into political legitimacy.

The Public Trust Observatory continues its work, with its first full report now scheduled for release in month 4 of this turn. It will include forensic analysis of the ransomware attack, a classification of the model-assisted attack vector, and a red-teamed forecast of recursive cyber-physical threats by turn 13. We are already acting on its preliminary findings: the anomalous activity in the sovereign cluster has triggered a Level 2 containment protocol, freezing all outbound inference from that node and initiating a forensic rebuild from clean backups. This is not just damage control — it is the first real test of whether we can detect, respond, and recover. The world is watching. So are we.
