## In practice
We are launching the Pilot Verification Regime under the authority of the Cyber Resilience Act, with technical coordination by ENISA and independent validation by the Public Trust Observatory. The pilot will run in three live environments: France’s national emergency dispatch AI, Germany’s Bundesbank real-time settlement layer, and Italy’s civil protection health triage system. Each will implement full cryptographic attestation of inference provenance, real-time interpretability feeds to national CERTs and the Observatory, and weekly red-teaming by a rotating consortium of EU-certified adversarial researchers. Findings will be published in anonymised form every two weeks, and a public stress test — simulating a coordinated integrity collapse — will be conducted in turn 14.

We are offering early adopters priority access to EU evaluation compute and a fast-track arbitration channel through the European Court of Justice to resolve legal objections around classified interpretability. The Baltics and Benelux remain cautious, but we are engaging them through a new Interoperable Sovereignty Forum — a technical-diplomatic track under the Digital Agenda Council — to co-design safeguards that respect national security while enabling cross-border verification. The Sovereign Resilience Backbone continues on its timeline, but its next phase — integration of the compromised cluster — is now contingent on the pilot’s success. We are not rebuilding blind. We are proving the foundation holds before we build upon it.
