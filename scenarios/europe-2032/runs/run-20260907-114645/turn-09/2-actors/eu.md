## In practice
We are executing the final phase of the Accelerated Sovereign Core Deployment under extreme political and technical strain. The retrofit of the France and Poland EuroHPC sites is complete, and the first hardened inference node — running a 58.0-capable, auditable model derived from openweight sources — is now live in Paris, integrated into emergency response systems in France, Germany, and Italy. However, resistance persists in the Baltics and Benelux, where national cloud contracts with US hyperscalers remain legally binding. To overcome this, we are activating transitional co-financing: offering 70% reimbursement for sovereign migration costs over the next 18 months, funded from the repurposed €75bn InvestAI envelope. We are also proposing a joint liability shield — the Union will assume 90% of the risk for any service disruption during transition — to be negotiated in the ECOFIN Council next month. The legal challenges to Article 114 TFEU remain active in the CJEU, but we are preparing a fallback: invoking Article 172 for research and innovation coordination, which does not require unanimity.

Simultaneously, we are launching the groundwork for the Public Trust Observatory. The Commission’s Joint Research Centre is drafting the founding regulation under Article 172 TFEU, with a mandate to publish quarterly threat assessments, maintain a public register of eval anomalies, and coordinate red-teaming across member states. The Observatory will absorb the technical team from the Institutional Integrity Audit and gain direct access to classified biosecurity and cyber threat reports from ENISA and Europol. Its first report — due turn 10 — will assess the bio_uplift_findings and eval_anomaly_reports, and will include a public theory of harm for uncontrolled model self-modification. This is not about creating another bureaucracy — it is about creating a credible, independent voice that can cut through the fog, restore public trust, and give us the political cover to act when it matters.
