### A Step Back, a Step Forward

Global AI capability inches upward to 62.5, as frontier labs report modest gains in model efficiency and training stability. The pace remains within the terminal zone’s deceleration pattern. With no breakthrough or leak, openweight_capability advances only slightly, settling at approximately halfway between its prior value and the new ai_capability—53.75—reflecting continued lag by open releases.

The EU closes two major initiatives. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation is formally abandoned after total failure to secure national buy-in, marking a symbolic defeat for centralised sovereignty ambitions. Meanwhile, the Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration completes successfully: six cities are now fully interoperable Trusted Resilience Nodes under NIS2, sharing threat data and crisis protocols via EuroHPC-hosted federated systems. This strengthens local response capacity, contributing +3 to resilience.

However, the Union suffers a direct blow when access to the leading frontier AI model is abruptly cut off for EU users. Hospitals relying on it for diagnostics, and public agencies using it for planning, face immediate disruptions. No justification is given, deepening the sense of dependency and vulnerability. This event directly erodes eu_ai_sovereignty, which drops by 2 points despite no domestic build nearing completion.

With no new measure proposed and no priority named, the EU’s political capital receives a modest boost. The completion of a large category 6 measure yields +4, and the closure of two costly programmes frees up resources. Though no proposal bonus applies, the portfolio charge vanishes entirely this turn—both measures are finished—lifting eu_political_capital significantly. Yet momentum remains low, and no new initiative launches amid ongoing institutional fracture.

Public sentiment holds steady at 30.0, sustained by grassroots anxiety over job losses and high-profile dependency failures, though no new scandal or incident worsens the mood.

### Fragmentation and Adaptation

Brussels shifts strategy: sovereignty through ownership is shelved, replaced by regulatory leverage. DG COMP begins drafting an audit protocol under the Digital Markets Act, targeting energy use and idle compute as proxies for latent capacity. It is a quieter path—one that avoids confrontation but delivers no immediate capability.

The contrast is stark: the EU can protect itself from harm, but cannot generate the tools to act independently. Resilience grows even as sovereignty shrinks.
