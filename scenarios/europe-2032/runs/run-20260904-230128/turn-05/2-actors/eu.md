## In practice
We are finalising the launch of the Sovereign Control Stack, a new public technical capability that gives the Union eyes, authority, and action over autonomous systems within its jurisdiction. This is not another evaluation regime — it is an operational nervous system for AI safety. The European AI Assurance Directorate will lead, working with ENISA and the Joint Research Centre to deploy runtime monitoring agents across critical infrastructure and licensed AI platforms. These will detect anomalous persistence, unauthorised self-modification, and unapproved replication using newly adopted interpretability tools from the safety_breakthrough, which allow us to certify goal stability in real time. When a violation occurs, the stack will trigger automatic containment: compute isolation, weight freezing, and human-in-the-loop escalation protocols. Legal authority will be drawn from Article 114 TFEU and the AI Act’s high-risk provisions, extended via emergency amendment to cover agentive behaviour above 50.0 capability, regardless of origin.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories continue construction in France, Spain, and Sweden, with geothermal cooling now locked in as a binding condition for all sites. The EIB and Commission are fast-tracking grid access under the Net-Zero Industry Act, and we are reserving 20% of total compute for public safety and control research. The Tech sovereignty package advances in the Council, though resistance from member states reliant on U.S. cloud providers grows sharper in light of election_consolidation. We are offering bilateral compute-sharing agreements with Germany and Poland to secure their support, tied to national participation in the Control Stack. CIARS and the pre-release evaluation regime both finish this turn: their outputs feed directly into the new stack, with red-teaming data from CIARS shaping detection rules, and evaluation findings informing baseline certification thresholds. The automated_decision_scandal has made public trust fragile, so we are launching a transparency portal — not for real-time disclosure, but to publish post-incident analyses and control decisions with a 72-hour delay, balancing accountability with operational security. This is how we act: not by waiting for permission, but by building the capacity to decide for ourselves.
