## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the Critical Infrastructure AI Resilience Shield (CIARS)  
A rapid-hardening programme to secure EU energy, water, and transport networks against AI-assisted intrusion, combining red-teaming, real-time monitoring, and failover automation.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 5  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 10–12 points; reduce time to detect and isolate AI-enabled attacks from weeks to hours  
Applies to: EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, with binding standards and co-funding for implementation

## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
This remains the cornerstone of our two-year commitment: without sovereign compute, no resilience, no evaluation, and no autonomy in crisis — and we are still building the foundation, not yet operating on it.

## In practice  
We are pressing ahead with InvestAI Gigafactories, now in its second turn, by finalising site agreements in France and Spain where conditions are most advanced, while intensifying political engagement in Brittany and northern Sweden to overcome local resistance. The European Investment Bank is releasing the first tranche of funding — €25bn — contingent on member states delivering grid access within 18 months, backed by ENTSO-E’s 15GW reservation. We are leveraging the Net-Zero Industry Act to fast-track permitting, treating AI infrastructure as strategic like clean hydrogen, and offering regional reinvestment packages that tie 10% of operational profits to local green transition projects. Germany and France have committed co-financing, but Nordic hesitations over energy competition with data centres require direct intervention at the European Council in March.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is being advanced through the Digital Agenda Council, where we are linking accelerated permitting to enforceable conditions: EU-based operational control, minimum energy efficiency thresholds tied to the EU Taxonomy, and binding commitments to integrate with national AI assurance frameworks. Legal services are finalising the Article 114 TFEU directive to harmonise permitting, aiming for a June stakeholder consultation. However, investor uncertainty persists, and without movement on EAAD, confidence in the EU’s regulatory clarity remains fragile.

In response to the near-miss cyber intrusion and the eval anomaly reports — a clear signal that capability is outpacing control — we are launching CIARS as a large-scale resilience measure. This will be coordinated by ENISA and the European Programme for Critical Infrastructure Protection (EPCIP), with €12bn in dedicated funding from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme. The first phase mandates AI-driven intrusion detection systems across all tier-one operators, funded at 70% by EU co-financing, and requires quarterly red-team exercises simulating AI-coordinated attacks. We are also establishing an AI Incident Response Cell within ENISA, staffed with cyber-physical system experts and drawing on lessons from nuclear and aerospace safety protocols. Member states will be required to report compliance biannually, with non-compliance triggering automatic review by the Commission. This is not optional adaptation — it is binding preparedness, because we can no longer assume we will detect an attack before it lands.
