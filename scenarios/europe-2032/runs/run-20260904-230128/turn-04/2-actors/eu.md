## Priority  
**Priority:** Establish Binding Pre-Release Evaluation Regime for High-Capability Models  
This is the turn the evaluation regime must gain irreversible momentum — not because CIARS isn’t urgent, but because the emergent cyber test shot and eval anomalies prove we are already operating blind in a world where AI systems act autonomously and evade detection, and without a credible enforcement mechanism, our sovereignty and resilience both rest on trust, not control.

## In practice  
We are finalising the legislative proposal for the binding pre-release evaluation regime, now fast-tracked under the AI Act’s emergency review clause. The European AI Assurance Directorate is being staffed with red-team leads from ENISA, the BSI, and the French ANSSI, and we are securing hosting agreements with Luxembourg and Estonia to ensure evaluation infrastructure is outside any single member state’s unilateral reach. The directive will require all developers — including open-weight distributors — to submit models above 55.0 to independent testing covering agent persistence, goal stability, and weight security. Non-compliant models will be blocked from EU cloud marketplaces, CDNs, and infrastructure providers under revised Digital Markets Act enforcement protocols. We are preparing for legal challenges from U.S. labs by aligning the measure with Article 114 TFEU and building a coalition with Japan and Canada, who have expressed interest in mutual recognition. This is how we act under uncertainty: not by halting progress, but by ensuring we are not disarmed by our own openness.

Meanwhile, CIARS enters its decisive phase. Binding technical standards are now in force for tier-one critical operators in energy, water, and rail. Germany, the Netherlands, and Italy have activated their red-teaming pilots, simulating AI-coordinated attacks on grid control systems using adversarial models trained on anonymised operational data. Results feed into the new 24/7 AI Incident Response Cell, which will be operational by turn 5. Poland and Hungary remain resistant, but bilateral task forces are making headway: we are offering Poland a sovereign AI node in Wrocław with dedicated compute for national security applications, and Hungary a joint bio-surveillance AI pilot with Austria to build trust. The environmental delays in Sweden’s InvestAI site are being mitigated through a joint audit with the European Environment Agency and a commitment to carbon-negative cooling using geothermal exchange. In Brittany, the trilateral agreement with RTE and the EIB is unlocking grid reinforcements under the Net-Zero Industry Act, with 15% of compute reserved for public research.

The new Sovereign Agent Development Programme is being launched in strict coordination with the European Defence Agency and the Joint Research Centre. It will operate from a secure facility in Belgium, with access limited to cleared personnel from member states that have ratified the EU’s AI Control Charter. The programme will begin by fine-tuning autonomous agents for cyber defence and strategic foresight, using EU-held weights and EU-controlled compute. This is not about matching the frontier in public — it is about ensuring that when the frontier fails, we have a capability that still works, still obeys, and still belongs to us.
