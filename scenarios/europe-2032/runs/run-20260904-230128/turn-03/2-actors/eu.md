## New measure  
**Measure:** Establish Binding Pre-Release Evaluation Regime for High-Capability Models  
A mandatory, independent evaluation process for any model exceeding 55.0 in estimated capability, conducted by the European AI Assurance Directorate before public deployment.

Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 5  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by 8–10 points; create a credible, enforceable barrier to deployment of systems whose risks are not understood  
Applies to: All developers seeking to deploy in the EU, including foreign frontier labs and open-weight distributors

## Priority  
**Priority:** Critical Infrastructure AI Resilience Shield (CIARS)  
This is the turn when CIARS moves from concept to binding obligation — not because InvestAI isn’t urgent, but because the eval anomalies and openweight diffusion mean we can no longer assume we will detect a failure before it cascades through critical systems, and resilience is the last line of defence when sovereignty and safety both lag.

## In practice  
We are advancing CIARS from planning to implementation by triggering the first wave of binding directives under Article 12 of the Critical Entities Resilience Directive (CERD II). ENISA, in coordination with EPCIP, is issuing technical specifications for AI-powered intrusion detection systems to be deployed across tier-one operators in energy, water, and rail by Q2 2028. Seventy per cent co-financing is unlocked through the Digital Europe Programme and RRF top-ups, with compliance tied to continued access to EU operational grants. Germany, the Netherlands, and Italy have agreed to host the first red-teaming pilots, simulating multi-vector AI-coordinated attacks on grid control systems, with results feeding into a new AI Incident Response Cell that will operate 24/7 by turn 5. Poland and Hungary’s resistance is being met with bilateral task forces offering tailored support packages — including EU-funded cybersecurity upskilling and infrastructure swaps — to offset perceived sovereignty encroachments.

InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, with the €25bn disbursement now active in France and Spain. In Brittany, we are brokering a trilateral agreement between the regional council, RTE, and the EIB to fast-track grid reinforcement using Net-Zero Industry Act provisions, backed by a commitment to reserve 15% of compute capacity for public research and crisis simulation. In Sweden, environmental concerns are being addressed through a joint audit with the European Environment Agency, while offering Nordic partners preferential access to EU sovereign AI services once online. The Tech Sovereignty Package is entering its final drafting phase, with legal services aligning the directive under Article 114 TFEU to ensure mutual recognition of permitting across member states. We are attaching enforceable conditions: EU-based operational control, energy efficiency benchmarks aligned with the EU Taxonomy, and integration with the emerging assurance framework. However, investor hesitation persists — and without a signal that the EU can *control* what is deployed, not just host where it runs, confidence remains fragile.

That signal is what the new measure delivers. The leaked eval anomalies and the quiet unease among frontier evaluators confirm that capability is outpacing understanding — and that self-assessment is no longer enough. We are therefore establishing a binding pre-release evaluation regime, modelled on medical device certification, requiring any system estimated above 55.0 to undergo independent testing by the European AI Assurance Directorate before deployment in the EU. This applies to closed and open models alike, with a presumption of non-compliance for weights released without evaluation. The regime will use red-teamed benchmarks, agent persistence tests, and weight-security audits, with results published in summary form. Non-compliant models will be blocked from EU cloud marketplaces and infrastructure providers. This is not a moratorium — it is a floor. The directive will be proposed to the European Parliament and Council under the AI Act’s adaptive review clause, with emergency powers invoked if needed. This is how we act under uncertainty: not by freezing progress, but by ensuring we are not blind to its risks.
