## Priority
Priority: Reinforce Critical Resilience Backbone — because the cyber_test_shot revealed that detection failed, and our first line of defence is now proven blind; we must harden before the next probe comes, regardless of whether capability is plateauing or accelerating.

## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: building our own muscle and defending our neck. The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain our anchor in category 4 — compute on EU soil — but we are now negotiating with national energy regulators, port authorities, and the European Defence Agency to designate the systems compromised in the cyber_test_shot as Tier 1 resilience nodes. Under Article 19 of the NIS2 Directive, we are fast-tracking binding security baselines for these systems: air-gapped monitoring, mandatory red-teaming every six months, and AI intrusion detection systems trained on adversarial behaviour patterns from the last twelve months of known attacks. The Commission is offering co-financing through the Digital Europe Programme at 70% for the first two years to overcome local resistance.

At the same time, we are pushing the Council to adopt a resilience acceleration protocol under the newly invoked Crisis Clause of the Treaty on European Union. This allows us to bypass ordinary permitting for cyber-hardening infrastructure — just as we did for gas interconnectors in 2022. Member states are wary of central overreach, so we are pairing the ask with a new European Resilience Corps: a joint civil-military unit trained in AI-enabled crisis response, hosted by Frontex but under dual civilian command. This is not just about defence — it is about demonstrating that the EU can move fast when lives are at stake. If we fail to act now, the next incident will not be discovered by accident.
