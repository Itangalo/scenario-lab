## In practice  
We are doubling down on the ASML negotiation, treating it as the hinge of our sovereignty. The Commission has drafted a regulation under Article 346 TFEU to establish a Union interest in critical dual-use technologies, which would legally ground the export control framework. We are working through the European Council to secure a qualified majority, leveraging Franco-German alignment and offering smaller states side guarantees on access to lithography services in exchange for support. The €15bn equity instrument is being structured through the European Innovation Council and the Dutch Ministry of Finance, with a non-controlling but blocking minority designed to prevent unilateral decisions by either foreign shareholders or national governments. Behind the scenes, we are engaging Japan and South Korea via the Chip 4 dialogue to frame this not as decoupling but as multilateral risk-sharing — the goal is not to block exports, but to ensure they follow coordinated strategic guardrails.

On resilience, we are finalising the Tier 1 node designations under NIS2, with ENISA now conducting red-teaming pilots in three energy grid operators. The air-gapped monitoring specs are complete, and we are pushing for adoption by Q1 2028. The European Investment Bank has approved the first disbursements, though uptake remains slow in member states where national cybersecurity agencies resist external oversight. We are linking co-financing levels to compliance with EU-wide incident reporting timelines to create incentive alignment.

On compute, the Gigafactories remain on track, though no construction has started. The permitting accelerators are stuck in legal challenges in two of the three test states, so we are preparing a revised directive to override national delays under the grounds of strategic infrastructure. The unified semiconductor energy allocation proposal is gaining traction in the Energy Council, but faces resistance from countries with tight grids. We are offering priority access to future AI compute capacity in exchange for grid priority now — a trade that could break the logjam, but risks being seen as favouring digital over physical infrastructure.
