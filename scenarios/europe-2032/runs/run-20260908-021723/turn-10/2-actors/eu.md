## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that sovereignty is not just about access to compute or models — it is about control over use. With the Anti-Coercion Instrument now concluded and no meaningful shift in U.S. access policy, we are pivoting to where we *can* set the terms: the point of deployment in public services. We are drafting a Regulation under Article 168 and Article 114 TFEU, to be adopted by qualified majority, that mandates pre-deployment evaluation by EU-certified bodies for any AI system used in healthcare, legal aid, or social welfare. The evaluation will assess robustness, explainability, misuse potential, and failure modes, with results published in a public safety case. Systems that fail to meet binding thresholds will be blocked from deployment, and citizens will have a clear right to redress if harm occurs.

This measure builds on the Joint Deployment Oversight Board compromise, now repurposing its mandate into a standing evaluation authority with real teeth. We are assigning lead drafting responsibility to DG SANTE and DG JUST, with technical support from the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium and the newly operational AI Incident Coordination Cell. France and Germany remain cautious, fearing regulatory overreach and competitive disadvantage for their national champions. To address this, we are offering co-location of the first evaluation hub in Paris and rotating technical leadership between national agencies — ANSSI, BfArM, and RIVM — to ensure shared ownership. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme and repurposed cohesion funds, avoiding new appropriations.

Behind the scenes, we are coordinating with ENISA and HERA to align evaluation criteria with the resilience framework now in flight, ensuring that bio and cyber risk indicators are embedded in the assessment templates. This is not just oversight — it is the foundation of a credible, sovereign AI order: the ability to say not just *how* AI is used, but *whether* it is used at all in the systems that shape people’s lives. In a world where capability is escaping control, this is the line we can still hold.
