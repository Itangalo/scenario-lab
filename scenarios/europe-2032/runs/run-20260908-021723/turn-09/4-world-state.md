### Frontier Widens, Open Access Leaps

Global AI capability advances to 62.5 as automated reasoning matures in scientific design, particularly in synthetic biology. A genome model produces a viable pathogen blueprint, sparking fierce debate in the biosecurity community. While contested, the event marks a qualitative shift in risk. Simultaneously, a major open-weight release lands just months behind the frontier, rapidly disseminating powerful agent-like capabilities. Openweight capability jumps to 56.0, eroding the West’s technical monopoly and raising urgent concerns about uncontrolled misuse.

### EU Asserts Operational Sovereignty

Faced with structural exclusion from frontier models, the EU pivots from compute parity to deployment authority. It finalizes the Anti-Coercion Instrument response, leveraging cloud data sovereignty to demand reciprocity from U.S. hyperscalers. Behind closed doors, envoys propose a transatlantic safety compact in exchange for easing semiconductor restrictions. Domestically, the Commission pushes a binding regulation for high-impact public-sector AI, invoking qualified majority voting to bypass German and French resistance. A compromise establishes a Joint Deployment Oversight Board, softening opposition but delaying full consensus.

### Resilience Framework Launched Amid Rising Risk

In response to the bio breakthrough and cascading cyber risks, the EU launches a new incident response protocol integrating HERA, ENISA, and a new AI Incident Coordination Cell in Brussels. The framework enables cross-border mutual aid and emergency suspensions, aiming to harden critical systems. However, implementation will take time. With no immediate protective effect and capability rising sharply, resilience sees no gain. Safety declines further due to the capability-safety gap and opaque model reasoning, dropping to 27.0.

### Political Capital Erodes Under Load

The portfolio remains heavy: three large measures in flight, including the newly proposed resilience protocol. The priority adds further strain. Though the new measure responds to recent shocks, its scale and timing yield only a modest proposal bonus. Sovereignty falls to 19.0 as frontier access remains denied and capability growth exceeds 2.0. Political capital dips to 29.0 despite limited gains, reflecting institutional fatigue and unresolved dependency.
