### Access Crisis Triggers Emergency Response

The sudden cutoff of a leading AI model from EU public services — hospitals, ministries, and research institutes — triggers a political and operational emergency. The European Commission invokes emergency powers under the AI Act and Article 4(3) TEU, launching legal action and countermeasures against the U.S. hyperscaler responsible. The Anti-Coercion Instrument is activated, and data adequacy negotiations are frozen. Emergency compute is rerouted to Gaia-X-hosted fallback models, while incentives are offered to open-weight developers to harden systems for public use.

### Political Capital Crumbles, Sovereignty Under Pressure

The EU announces a large category 4 measure — *Reclaim Frontier Access* — in direct response to the *eu_frontier_access_denied* event. Though governance bandwidth is saturated — with three large measures already in flight and portfolio charge at −10 — the urgency of the crisis grants partial momentum. The measure enters early mobilization, contributing a +3 sovereignty bonus under crisis-response rules, reflecting preparatory coordination and political signaling.

However, no implementation begins this turn due to legal challenges and inter-institutional delays. This gain is overwhelmed by mounting pressures: the ongoing access crisis imposes a −2 penalty on *eu_ai_sovereignty*, compounded by a −1 loss from member_state_defection. The rise in global capability further erodes sovereignty by −1. The net effect is a decline from 16.0 to 15.0.

*eu_political_capital* falls to 10.0, hit by a −4 internal-origin penalty as the crisis exposes systemic dependency, partially offset by a +4 proposal bonus for directly countering a severe external event. *ai_safety* drops sharply (−5) due to the *capability_jump* event and lack of safeguards. *resilience* and *public_sentiment* remain unchanged. The system remains under strain, with effects unfolding over time.
