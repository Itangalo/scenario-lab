## In practice  
We are treating the completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package as a mixed legacy: the permitting precedent stands, but the capital shortfall and private sector retreat have left us without the compute foundation we counted on. The European Open Research Assurance Network is now our most active measure, transitioning from proposal to implementation. We are finalizing governance under the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking, with EMBL launching the first bio-risk evaluation protocol by turn 8. Funding is secured through redirected Horizon Europe allocations, avoiding new fiscal pressure. Early engagement with the U.S. National AI Research Resource has yielded conditional acceptance of our tools for transatlantic data access—turning openness into leverage, as intended.

But the denial of frontier access and the ASML coercion have changed everything. We can no longer assume that technical leadership alone will secure our position. The Anti-Coercion Instrument, dormant since its adoption, is now our only credible tool to resist strategic subordination. We are initiating proceedings under Article 14 of Regulation (EU) 2023/1786, citing the extraterritorial application of U.S. export controls as a clear case of economic coercion. The Commission will lead, with the EEAS coordinating member-state alignment. We are briefing the Dutch government that this is not a trade dispute, but a constitutional moment for EU autonomy. If we do not act, we signal that our sovereignty is negotiable. The response will not be tariffs—it will be countermeasures designed to impose real cost on U.S. leverage, while opening parallel channels for de-escalation. This is not a turn toward isolation; it is a bid to restore balance in a relationship that has become structurally unequal.
