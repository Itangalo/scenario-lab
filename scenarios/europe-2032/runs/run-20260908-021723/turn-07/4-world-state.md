### Frontier Crossed, Access Cut

Global AI capability reaches 60.0 as the first systems complete multi-day professional projects autonomously, displacing junior roles across engineering and legal analysis. This milestone triggers broader deployment, even as safety lags. The EU’s Open Research Assurance Network begins integration at EMBL and INRIA, but its tools are not yet applied to in-development systems—limiting real-world assurance.

### Sovereignty Under Siege

The U.S. abruptly restricts EU access to the latest frontier model, disrupting AI-dependent public health and infrastructure planning. Simultaneously, Washington pressures the Netherlands to extend ASML export limits to mature-node lithography, crippling not only Chinese production but also European automotive and medical device suppliers reliant on stable chip flows. In response, the European Commission initiates activation of the Anti-Coercion Instrument—a legally significant step—but full implementation is delayed pending legal review and member state consultations. No countermeasures are yet imposed. The Commission signals intent to propose targeted restrictions on U.S. cloud and data services operating in Europe, aiming to raise the cost of unilateral control, but the measure remains in drafting.

### Mixed Gains, Mounting Costs

The *InvestAI Gigafactories* programme officially concludes—though only two sites are operational, short of the target. Still, sovereign compute capacity expands, yielding a partial boost. However, the *Tech Sovereignty Package* ends with unmet investment goals, and the denial of frontier access undercuts confidence. Political capital erodes under the strain of three large measures in flight, including the new priority. Public sentiment dips as healthcare AI scandals amplify fears of opaque systems, while the ASML crisis fuels narratives of dependency.

### Transatlantic Tensions Harden

Initial U.S. reactions to the EU’s invocation of the Anti-Coercion Instrument are dismissive, framing export controls as national security imperatives. Behind closed doors, however, some officials signal openness to talks if the EU de-escalates. The move strengthens internal cohesion among member states wary of strategic subordination, but risks isolating Europe from future technical coordination. The proposed cloud service countermeasures remain under debate, with no consensus expected before the next turn.
