### US Election Reshapes Access
The United States elects a president who treats advanced AI as a strategic asset, not a commodity. Export controls tighten, frontier model access is now tiered by national alignment, and foreign partners are formally treated as clients. The EU, despite its regulatory influence, finds its leverage hollowed out—compliance no longer guarantees access. The new posture signals a shift: compute is power, and Washington will decide who gets it.

### EU Responds with Sovereign Compute Push
In response, the EU launches the *Foundational Compute Initiative*, a €48bn public programme to build and operate sovereign AI training infrastructure across France, Germany, and Finland. Governed by a new Joint European Compute Authority and powered by dedicated energy, it aims to ensure the Union can train high-capability models independently. The project is framed as defensive necessity, not technological rivalry. Public sentiment shifts upward as the move is seen as restoring agency.

While not full control, it creates a firewall against unilateral foreign pressure. The middle-power coalition with Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan becomes operational, launching red-team exercises and sharing evaluation protocols.
