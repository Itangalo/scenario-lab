## In practice
We are treating the completion of the ASML autonomy measure as a partial victory: the export control framework has passed the Council under Article 346 TFEU, with the Netherlands accepting EU-level authority in exchange for guaranteed access to legacy tools and a €15bn equity structure that gives the Union a blocking minority. This is not full control, but it is a firewall against unilateral coercion — and it is the anchor the middle-power coalition needs to trust us as a node, not a conduit. The coalition framework is now active: the joint statement on strategic interdependence has been signed, and red-team exercises on AI-optimised supply chain attacks will begin in Q1 2029. We are already sharing evaluation templates with Tokyo and Seoul, and Taiwan has agreed to early warning on model leakage in exchange for lab access.

But the U.S. election outcome changes everything. With frontier access now explicitly tiered and security-reviewed, we can no longer assume that participation in global AI development is a given. The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track, but they are private-sector levers — they build capacity, but not control. This is why we are launching the EU Foundational Compute Initiative now: a €48bn sovereign compute programme to be sited across three member states (France, Germany, and Finland), powered by dedicated grid allocations and governed by a new Joint European Compute Authority hosted under CERN’s legal model. We are approaching the European Investment Bank for 70% financing, with the remainder split between member state co-investment and redirected Digital Europe funds. The first machine will be operational by turn 8, capable of training models up to 62.0 in closed conditions — not to race the frontier, but to ensure that when access is denied, we can still act.

We are not accelerating broader adoption or diffusion — public sentiment remains too fragile, and the resilience backbone, while improved, is not yet extended to healthcare or transport. We are instead focusing on credibility: showing that the EU can build, own, and defend its own stack. This is not isolation — it is insurance. And it is the only way to ensure that our two mandates — self-determination and harm prevention — remain in our own hands.
