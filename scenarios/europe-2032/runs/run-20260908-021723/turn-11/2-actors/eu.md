## New measure  
**Propose:** *Establish binding minimum cyber and bio-resilience baselines for all AI systems in critical infrastructure, with mandatory stress-testing and real-time monitoring*  
A regulation that sets enforceable technical and operational resilience standards for AI used in energy, healthcare, water, and transport systems, requiring live monitoring, automatic failover protocols, and quarterly adversarial testing against simulated AI-driven threats.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 15  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `resilience` by 10–12 points by hardening critical infrastructure against AI-enabled cyber and bio attacks; reduce incident severity by ensuring systems degrade gracefully and response protocols are exercised; indirectly support `ai_safety` by creating feedback loops between operational failure and pre-deployment design.  
`Applies to:` All AI systems operating in or interacting with EU-designated critical infrastructure, including privately operated utilities and cross-border networks; enforced by ENISA in coordination with national regulators and the newly formed Bio-Defense AI Consortium.

## Priority  
Priority: Establish binding minimum cyber and bio-resilience baselines — because the openweight_frontier_release and automated_decision_scandal prove that dangerous capability is now embedded in the wild, and because deployment control alone cannot stop cascading harm if our core systems lack the capacity to absorb impact.

## In practice  
We are acting on the recognition that sovereignty is not only about who evaluates or deploys AI — it is about whether our society holds when AI is used against us, or fails within us. The automated_decision_scandal has shattered public trust, and the openweight_frontier_release means that whatever is dangerous is already distributed. We can no longer assume that containment at the frontier or oversight at deployment is enough. The Transnational AI Accountability Forum and the Bio-Defense AI Consortium are emerging as real instruments, but they are not yet connected to the systems they must protect.  

This turn, we are drafting a Regulation under Article 114 TFEU to establish binding resilience baselines for AI in critical infrastructure. The measure will mandate that all AI systems in energy grids, hospitals, water treatment, and rail networks meet minimum standards for adversarial robustness, real-time anomaly detection, and automatic containment. These will be stress-tested quarterly using red-team simulations drawn from the Bio-Defense AI Consortium’s threat library and ENISA’s cyber playbook. Monitoring data will be shared in near-real time with the EU AI Incident Coordination Cell, creating a live picture of systemic exposure. We are assigning lead drafting to DG ENER and DG SANTE, with technical input from ENISA, HERA, and the European Defence Agency. Resistance is expected from industry, particularly German industrial AI providers and French energy operators, who see compliance as costly and disruptive. To secure buy-in, we are offering co-financing from the Connecting Europe Facility and fast-track access to EU sovereign compute for firms that exceed the baseline. This is not just hardening — it is the beginning of a reflexive infrastructure: systems that detect, resist, and report. In a world where the open frontier moves with the speed of code, our survival depends not on perfect control, but on enduring failure.
