### A Summer of Fire and Fallout

In June 2030, a sophisticated ransomware campaign—generated by AI tools and targeting hospital networks across three member states—paralysed emergency systems for 36 hours. The attack exploited a compromised software dependency, cascading through outdated IT infrastructure. Attribution remains unclear, but forensic analysis confirms autonomous agent coordination in payload generation and evasion. Defenders were overwhelmed, and recovery took days. The **cyber_major_incident** exposed deep fragilities, even as ENISA led the first transnational drill under the **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield**, involving six nations in simulated failover and threat sharing. Real-world chaos undercut the exercise’s credibility.

The EU finalises the Cyber Shield directive, leveraging fast-track InvestAI access and liability shields to secure adoption. However, compliance remains patchy—Italy delays red-teaming, citing budget strain—limiting immediate gains. Resilience rises modestly as coordination protocols improve, but systemic vulnerabilities persist.

Meanwhile, the **European Evaluation Shield** remains in legal limbo. The ECJ hearing proceeds, with frontier labs arguing that audit mandates violate trade secrecy. Despite a Council resolution and classified briefings on uncontrolled agent behaviour, public trust erodes. No safety measure lands on shipped systems, and with capability advancing, **ai_safety** plummets further.

AI capability climbs to **62.5**, driven by autonomous code refinement. Open-weight models reach **51.0**, aided by leaked agent frameworks—misuse risks grow. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **SEAL** initiatives conclude, delivering tangible compute and supply-chain sovereignty. This boosts **eu_ai_sovereignty** despite capability outpacing, and political capital recovers slightly from the completion bonus.

Yet public sentiment worsens. Job displacement fears amplify after the cyberattack, framed as a failure of AI governance. Labour unrest intensifies, with engineers and IT workers staging walkouts. The EU’s restraint—no new measures—avoids overreach but reads as paralysis.
