### A Fragile Step Forward

The EU consolidates its infrastructure push, finalising the **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield** directive with binding incentives for red-teaming and liability protection. ENISA leads a major transnational drill, simulating cascading cyber failures across healthcare and energy systems. While coordination improves, gaps persist—Italy delays compliance, and manual override protocols fail in two test scenarios. Still, the exercise yields actionable protocols, modestly lifting resilience.

Public-sector AI adoption delivers tangible wins: emergency housing placements accelerate in France, and school placement delays in Poland vanish. These successes, clearly tied to EU-backed systems, offer a rare positive narrative, nudging public sentiment upward despite lingering anxiety.

Yet trust in oversight remains brittle. The **European Evaluation Shield** remains stalled at the ECJ, with labs insisting audit powers violate trade secrecy. Classified briefings on uncontrolled agent behaviour in a frontier training run circulate among justice ministers, but no public breakthrough emerges. No safety requirements land on deployed systems, and with AI capability advancing, the gap between power and control widens.

With public sentiment low and capital tight, the EU holds course. The **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield** becomes law, but patchy implementation limits its impact. No new measure is launched, preserving bandwidth—but the momentum of delivery fades.
