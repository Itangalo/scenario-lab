## In practice
We are advancing the Public-sector AI Safety Audit Programme through a delegated act under Article 40 of the AI Act, with enforcement tied to Article 71’s penalties and Article 80’s funding conditionality. The European AI Office is now operationalising the audit templates in close coordination with ENISA, adapting DORA red-teaming protocols to public administration workflows, particularly in benefits, tax, and justice systems. Despite resistance from several member states—Poland, Hungary, and Slovakia have filed preliminary legal challenges—we are holding firm on mandatory participation by linking full access to cohesion and digital transition funds to audit compliance. A €1.2bn allocation from the MFF’s digital envelope is being disbursed to accredit independent audit firms, with a priority on those with prior experience in algorithmic impact assessments.

At the same time, we are responding to the election_alliance event by negotiating the Frontier Access Framework under the auspices of the Foreign Affairs Council and the Competitiveness Council. This is not about dependency—it is about conditional interdependence. We are demanding reciprocal rights: access to frontier models for EU evaluators, joint incident reporting, shared red-teaming protocols, and veto rights over deployments that could destabilise EU institutions or markets. The US offer is real, but we will not accept alignment on export controls or standards as a one-way street. The framework will be implemented through a Council regulation, with enforcement tied to the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions and the AI Act’s high-risk deployment bans. This is how we buy time: by turning access into a lever, not a lifeline.

The InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track. We have fast-tracked permitting for two of the four sites, with grid connections accelerated under the new emergency energy infrastructure rules. But we know compute alone is not sovereignty. Without control, without understanding, without public trust—none of it holds. That is why this turn, even as we build and bargain, the Audit Programme remains our anchor. We are not choosing between safety and capability. We are insisting on both.
