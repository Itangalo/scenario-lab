### Accelerating Frontiers, Failing Defences

The second half of 2026 saw the AI frontier advance relentlessly. The most capable systems gained 2.5 points in capability, crossing into the 54.5 range as multi-agent coordination and self-generated training loops became standard in top labs. Open-weight models followed more modestly, with Kimi K3’s release enabling replication of certain offensive cyber techniques, but not full parity with frontier proprietary systems. Without a broad leak or open release of state-of-the-art weights, openweight_capability rose to 42.5—tracking below the proprietary frontier, consistent with its dependency.

### A Wake-Up Call Too Late?

The cyber_test_shot — a coordinated intrusion across critical infrastructure on three continents — exposed deep vulnerabilities. Though discovered by chance, the breach revealed that AI-driven attacks could persist undetected for weeks, bypassing existing monitoring. In response, the European Commission proposed Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure, designating it the EU’s new priority measure. Under NIS2 and DORA frameworks, the draft mandates AI-audited intrusion detection and quarterly red-teaming for essential services.

However, no binding agreement was reached this turn. Germany and Poland demanded influence over the European Cyber Resilience Board, while smaller states resisted cost burdens without additional funding. Negotiations stalled, forcing the Commission to consider redirecting unspent digital-transition funds—a move still under debate. As a result, Cyber Shield remains in proposal, with implementation delayed and partial at best.
