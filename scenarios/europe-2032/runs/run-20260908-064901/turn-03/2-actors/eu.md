## In practice
We are in final consultations with the Council’s juridical service to resolve outstanding legal objections to the Cyber Shield directive, particularly around liability for automated isolation decisions and the enforcement powers of the European Cyber Resilience Board. The compromise on financing — 70% co-funding from MFF digital-transition funds — holds, but legal certainty is now the bottleneck. We are working through COREPER II to amend the draft act’s Article 12 to clarify that national authorities retain ultimate decision authority in crisis escalation, while the Board coordinates threat intelligence and simulation readiness. This is not a retreat from centralised capability but a necessary calibration: without legal legitimacy, the directive collapses under its own weight.

Parallel to this, ENISA is operationalising the first phase of AI-audited monitoring under the DORA delegated act, with pilot deployments in three major financial market infrastructures by September. The templates for red-teaming and continuous monitoring are being stress-tested against Kimi K3-derived exploitation patterns, and we are feeding findings into the *Iron Firewall* after-action review. The exercise exposed that national CERTs still operate in silos when AI forces decisions in minutes — but it also proved that integrated simulation is possible. We are now building fallback protocols that assume partial compromise, not perfect defence, because the frontier at 57.0 can already plan across domains and time horizons beyond human reflex. That changes everything — not just the tools, but the assumptions.
