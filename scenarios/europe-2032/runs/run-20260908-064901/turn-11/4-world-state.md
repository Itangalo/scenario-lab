### Frontier Stalls Amid Institutional Fragility  
AI capability advances only modestly to 89.0, reflecting the dampening effect of the intensifying global AI worker strike. Recursive self-improvement cycles persist, but progress is hindered by walkouts at multiple frontier labs and academic institutions refusing to train models above capability 60.0. Automation pipelines are reevaluated, releases delayed, and talent attrition grows, slowing the rate of advancement despite underlying momentum. The closed frontier continues to evolve, but at a reduced pace.

Open-weight models rise incrementally to 73.0, sustained by ongoing distillation efforts and modest improvements in open-access training efficiency. While partial leakages from frontier checkpoints are reported, these do not translate into sudden capability jumps, as replication remains bottlenecked by compute access, safety filters, and legal constraints. Under constitutional modeling rules, open-weight progress reflects accumulated investment, not opportunistic gains, and thus grows at a rate consistent with prior trends.

Despite rising risks, no meaningful safety improvements land: `ai_safety` remains at 5.0, with no deployed interpretability, no secured weights, and containment failures now routine.

### EU Consolidates Minimal Trust, at Great Cost  
The **Crisis-Verified AI Stack** completes deployment across four member states, establishing air-gapped nodes for emergency logistics and civil protection. However, Ireland and the Netherlands withhold full adoption, citing unresolved interoperability and cost-sharing disputes. The EU offers access to sovereign edge clusters as incentive, but legal friction persists.

The EU *announces* the **Sovereign Edge for Essential State Functions Beyond Crisis**, a proposed extension of hardened infrastructure to tax, civil registry, and border control. The initiative is formally tabled and enters the feasibility and consultation phase, with DG TAXUD and several member states raising immediate concerns over data sovereignty, duplication, and funding. No implementation occurs this turn. Due to the scale and complexity of the proposed measure—classified as category 4, large—it will require at least two full turns before any operational impact or sovereignty benefit can materialize. The announcement alone consumes political capital, reflecting early resistance and coordination costs.

Resilience improves slightly to 55.0, reflecting partial hardening of critical systems despite the expired Emergency Resilience Surge. Public sentiment holds at 22.0, as media highlight the EU’s fragmented response and the widening gap between defensive efforts and frontier reality. The image of isolated digital bunkers, disconnected and limited, dominates public discourse.

The global AI worker strike and AI refugee crisis continue, straining frontier development and EU asylum systems alike.

This turn introduces only one new measure—the **Sovereign Edge** initiative—while the **Crisis-Verified AI Stack** remains the active programme. The announcement of the new measure incurs a political capital cost of −1 and a sovereignty charge of −3, but no benefit, consistent with the minimum two-turn delay for high-cost measures. Sovereignty declines by 2.0: the completion of the Crisis-Verified Stack provides +3.0, but this is offset by a −1.0 erosion from the +1.0 rise in `ai_capability` (adjusted for strike-slowed growth) and a −3.0 portfolio charge from the new measure, under US_POSTURE: ALLIANCE which enforces ongoing decay. The net result is a drop from 18.0 to 16.0. Political capital decreases by 1.0, reflecting the cost of initiating a contested large-scale proposal without immediate legitimacy lending.
