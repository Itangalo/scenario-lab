## In practice

We are acting on the conviction that sovereignty now means survival in isolation. The taiwan_blockade has severed our access to next-generation semiconductors, and the ai_investment_collapse has frozen private-sector expansion of data centres we were counting on. Our previous compute strategy assumed peacetime scaling. This turn, we are in a different condition.

Under Article 114 TFEU and the recently invoked NIS2 crisis clause, we are redirecting €47bn from the MFF, InvestEU, and recovered cohesion funds to launch the Accelerated Domestic Compute Assurance Programme. This is not about matching the frontier — that race is lost this cycle — but about guaranteeing that essential functions (grid balancing, medical triage, border control, emergency response) continue using AI systems we can physically maintain, verify, and defend. The programme tasks the ECCC, in coordination with ASML, STMicroelectronics, and national HPC centres, to retrofit three existing exascale-class facilities in France, Germany, and Italy with air-gapped training and inference bays, hardened against remote intrusion and designed to operate without external firmware updates. These will run a sovereign variant of the last verifiable model generation (capability ~52.0), distilled and pruned for reliability, not performance.

We are mandating that all high-risk public AI deployments migrate to this assured stack by turn 9, with exemptions only for systems that can demonstrate equivalent physical security elsewhere. Resistance is expected from member states reliant on cloud providers and from industry lobbies warning of inefficiency, but we are offering transition grants and accelerated permitting for local edge clusters as compensation. ENISA will conduct quarterly integrity audits, and the Commission will report monthly to COREPER II. This is not the future we planned. It is the one we must now inhabit.
