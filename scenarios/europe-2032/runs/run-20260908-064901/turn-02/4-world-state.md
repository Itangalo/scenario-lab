### Frontier Presses Ahead

The AI frontier advanced steadily in the first half of 2027, with top systems gaining 2.5 points in capability, reaching 57.0. Improvements in recursive self-refinement and cross-domain planning allowed frontier models to autonomously design and execute multi-week research cycles, particularly in materials science and logistics optimisation. No public release or leak brought open models to parity, but Kimi K3 derivatives enabled wider replication of cyber exploitation frameworks, pushing openweight_capability to 43.5.

### Cyber Shield: Progress Amid Friction

The EU pressed forward with Cyber Shield, finalising the DORA delegated act mandating AI-audited monitoring for financial infrastructures. ENISA released draft compliance templates, and COREPER II reached a tentative compromise: smaller states accepted centralised oversight in exchange for 70% co-financing from redirected MFF funds, while Germany and Poland secured rotating seats on the European Cyber Resilience Board. However, legal scrutiny from the Council’s juridical service delayed formal adoption, citing concerns over enforcement authority and liability in automated isolation protocols.

Despite delays, the first cross-border exercise, *Iron Firewall*, was conducted in June. It exposed critical gaps in real-time coordination between national CERTs and EU-level systems, particularly in AI-mediated escalation decisions. Still, the drill demonstrated functional fallback procedures and marked the first integrated use of AI-driven threat simulation across civilian and border security infrastructures.
