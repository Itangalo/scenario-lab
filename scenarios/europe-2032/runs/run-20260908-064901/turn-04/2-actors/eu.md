## New measure
**Public-sector AI Safety Audit Programme**  
Establish a mandatory audit regime for all high-risk AI systems deployed in public services, using independent evaluators to assess system behaviour, failure modes, and recourse mechanisms before and during operation.

- Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)  
- Size: large  
- Finishes on turn: 6  
- Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by at least +8, reduce risk of automated_decision_scandal recurrence, improve public_sentiment through demonstrated accountability  
- Applies to: EU member states’ national and regional public administrations deploying AI in social services, taxation, immigration, and justice

## Priority  
Public-sector AI Safety Audit Programme — because the automated decision scandal has shattered trust in public AI use, and without verified, enforceable safeguards in place, no amount of sovereign compute or resilient infrastructure will restore legitimacy; we must act now to stabilise the domestic foundation before extending further.

## In practice  
We are launching the Public-sector AI Safety Audit Programme in direct response to the ombudsman investigation into the unemployment office scandal. The programme will be implemented through a delegated act under the AI Act’s high-risk framework, administered jointly by the European AI Office and national competent authorities. We are allocating €1.2bn from the MFF digital-transition envelope to fund independent audit firms and build technical capacity within the AI Office to accredit and supervise them. By turn 5, audits will be mandatory for any new deployment of AI in benefits determination, visa processing, tax assessments, and criminal risk scoring.

The legal base is clear, but political resistance is emerging from several member states that view this as an infringement on administrative sovereignty. To overcome this, we are offering co-design rights on audit templates and a phased rollout—starting with voluntary participation in Q2—with full enforcement tied to continued access to EU cohesion and digital funds. ENISA will support with tooling, adapting its DORA red-teaming frameworks to public service contexts. This is not just about catching failures before they happen; it is about rebuilding public_sentiment by making accountability visible. The Cyber Shield directive may have formally finished this turn, but its spirit lives on: we cannot delegate crisis decisions to systems we do not understand, whether in cyber defence or in welfare administration.
