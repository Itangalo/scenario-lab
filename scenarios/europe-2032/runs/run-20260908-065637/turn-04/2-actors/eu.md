## Priority  
**Sovereign Model Initiative (SMI)** — because the member state defection and capability plateau together signal that the window to lock in irreversible EU access is narrowing, and delaying structural anchoring now risks permanent dependency, regardless of technical progress elsewhere.

## In practice  
We are doubling down on the Sovereign Model Initiative, but under a new legal and political strategy. Legal services have confirmed that Article 58a remains vulnerable to TFEU Article 310 challenges, so we are formalising the pivot to the Multiannual Financial Framework’s solidarity clause as the legal basis for the €18bn transfer from RRF funds. This avoids treaty reform but requires enhanced cooperation from 15 member states. We are circulating a draft Council decision under Article 48(7) TEU to COREPER, backed by a joint letter from the Commission and High Representative stressing SMI as a matter of joint resilience. To secure Italy and Spain, we are offering early access to SMI-powered public health analytics under the EU4Health umbrella, with dedicated compute slices. For smaller states, we are deploying the European External Action Service to brief capitals directly on the risks of extraterritorial model control — particularly on judicial reasoning, migration decisions, and electoral integrity.

At the same time, the Cyber Shield Enforcement Mechanism is now in full legal motion. We have issued implementing acts under NIS2, conditioning access to Digital Europe Programme grants and early SMI service deployment rights on ETSI-compliant detection stack deployment. Poland and Hungary have filed for interim measures at the ECJ, but we are not pausing: disbursements to non-compliant regions are suspended, and ENISA is conducting remote verification. The election annulment event has hardened our resolve — we cannot afford cascading institutional fragility. The new audit measure is a direct response: we are mandating the Joint Research Centre, assisted by national audit offices, to map all critical dependencies on foreign AI systems by September. This is not a delay — it is a condition for rational prioritisation. If the audit reveals that key judiciary or health systems run on US-controlled agents, we will have the justification to fast-track SMI integration into public services, even at the cost of further friction.
