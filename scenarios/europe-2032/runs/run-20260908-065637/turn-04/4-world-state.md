### The Plateau That Wasn’t

Despite signs of a plateau in frontier AI—underwhelming releases and falling prices—global capability continues its steady climb to 55.5, driven by incremental automation in legal and biomedical domains. Yet no corresponding safety upgrades land, and the gap between power and control widens. The EU’s push to consolidate sovereignty hits turbulence.

The **Cyber Shield for Critical Operators** completes its rollout, delivering modest resilience gains. Detection upgrades in energy and port systems are now live in nine member states, though patchy compliance in the east and south limits systemic impact. Resilience rises to 42.5, but vulnerabilities persist.

The **Sovereign Model Initiative (SMI)**, now the EU’s priority, faces fierce political headwinds. The legal pivot to the solidarity clause avoids a treaty challenge, but securing enhanced cooperation from 15 states proves elusive. Italy and Spain remain cautious, demanding data sovereignty guarantees beyond health analytics. Smaller states resist perceived tiered access, and the European External Action Service’s briefings only deepen suspicions. No new compute is pooled, and Article 58a remains unratified.

A **member state** bypasses EU coordination to strike a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler for cloud-based AI services in public health and judiciary systems—undermining SMI’s legitimacy and triggering a −2 sovereignty penalty. The Commission labels it “pragmatic fragmentation,” but the damage is done.

The new **Emergency Audit of Frontier Access Dependencies** launches, mandating a sweeping review of foreign AI use in critical functions. While framed as urgent due diligence, it sparks backlash. Critics accuse the Commission of overreach, and public sentiment dips to 38.0 amid fears of disruption and job losses in AI-dependent sectors.

Political capital erodes to 31.0 under the weight of five large measures, the priority cost, and the audit’s short-term scrutiny. The portfolio charge is steep, and no finishing bonus offsets it. The audit earns a modest proposal bonus for responding to recent dependency risks, but cohesion is fraying.

Openweight capability rises to 45.0, lifted by informal spillover from national labs and anticipation of SMI’s open evaluation framework—though no weights are released. Safety, however, drops to 25.0 as governance gaps widen and no safeguards land.

The election annulment in a major democracy, linked to AI-driven disinformation, hardens resolve but deepens public anxiety. The EU’s response is seen as reactive, not preventative.

### Fractures Within

The Union’s identity evolves: it now formally acknowledges the tension between capability and cohesion, between harm prevention and legitimacy. But unity remains aspirational. The audit may clarify dependencies—but it cannot yet heal divisions.
