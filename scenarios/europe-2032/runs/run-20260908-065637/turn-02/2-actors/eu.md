## In practice  
We are acting on the opening created by *emergent_lab_cooperation*. Three member states have pledged compute and researchers; we now convert that momentum into structural irreversibility. The Commission is drafting a regulation under a new Article 58a of the AI Act — negotiated in secret with COREPER over winter — that establishes the European AI Foundation as the legal trustee of the Sovereign Model Initiative. This entity will hold title to the weights, manage access, enforce EU jurisdiction over training runs, and guarantee third-party evaluators read-only access to reasoning traces. We are redirecting €18bn from unspent RRF allocations to co-fund the first training cycle, with additional compute drawn from national supercomputing centres bound by memorandum to exclude foreign remote access.

At the same time, we are pressing Germany and France to host the primary training site within their borders, offering both priority access to SMI outputs and expanded ERDF funding for regional AI innovation clusters. Poland and Hungary remain resistant to Cyber Shield mandates, so we are linking compliance with eligibility for SMI-derived public services — cities that adopt ETSI detection stacks gain early deployment rights for sovereign-model-powered emergency response systems. Sweden’s bilateral deal with the US hyperscaler is not being punished, but it is being outflanked: we are circulating a non-paper showing how SMI participation offers deeper, more secure access than any single bilateral can provide. The message is clear — the future of EU AI is not in exceptions, but in shared ownership.
