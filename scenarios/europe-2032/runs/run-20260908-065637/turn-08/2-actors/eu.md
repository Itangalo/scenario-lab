## New measure

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`

## Priority
**Sovereign Evaluation Corps (SEC)** — because the openweight release at 50.5 and the denial of frontier access this turn prove that sovereignty can no longer be outsourced: we must have our own eyes on the weights, our own engineers in the loop, and our own authority to say what runs in our critical systems. Without SEC, every other measure becomes a ritual of compliance without control.

## In practice
We are treating SEC as the cornerstone of post-access sovereignty. With the denial of frontier model access this turn — no appeal, no explanation — the fiction of reliable external supply is over. The Commission has fast-tracked the establishment of DG-AI, and we are activating the solidarity clause in the MFF to reallocate €7.2bn from cohesion funds toward staffing and secure evaluation infrastructure. Recruitment begins immediately from national cybersecurity agencies (ANSSI, BSI, CNC), academic partners in the ELISE network, and secondments from compliant private labs under a new Article 59b AI Act provision for public service obligation rotations.

ENISA and the JRC are drafting the first evaluation protocol suite by turn 9, to be stress-tested against the initial SMI training run. Access to weights will be non-negotiable for market entry: we are amending the AI Act’s conformity assessment to require secure enclaves for EU evaluators, modeled on ITAR-style handling. The first red team will focus on inference-time optimization anomalies and emergent goal fidelity — the very signals now appearing in audit logs. This is not about catching lies; it is about closing the gap between capability and understanding, which is now the Union’s most urgent vulnerability. We are offering hyperscalers a trade: full evaluation access in exchange for fast-tracked market renewal and liability shielding under crisis conditions. If they refuse, we build around them — and SEC ensures we can tell when we must.
