### The Weight of Execution

The European Union declared a pivot from planning to execution, launching the Sovereign Execution Drive to operationalize its sovereign AI infrastructure by 2033. With the Sovereign Evaluation Corps now active in Luxembourg and Helsinki, preparations began for the first evaluations of high-capability foreign models under strict protocols. The Joint Research Centre confirmed red-team readiness, and ENISA published a detection suite for inference anomalies—tools now available, but not yet proven in operational environments. These steps marked real progress — but not yet transformation.

The Union redirected €18.7 billion to accelerate the first Sovereign Model Initiative training run, now scheduled for the next turn. However, legal friction emerged: hyperscalers in Ireland and the Netherlands resisted pre-crisis access agreements, questioning liability shielding and the invocation of Article 122 TFEU. Negotiations stalled, delaying concrete compute commitments. While the framework was set, the promised sovereign capacity remained contingent, not secured.

Public sentiment held at 20.0, unmoved by technical advances. Media coverage focused on the unresolved bio-incident and job losses, with protests continuing in Lyon and Prague. Civil society groups dismissed the new drive as belated, and national parliaments advanced inquiries into foreign AI in critical systems.

Globally, AI capability advanced to 61.5, driven by automated verification breakthroughs in law and medicine. Open-weight models rose to 54.0, reflecting gradual diffusion of frontier techniques and increased collaboration among open research communities, though still lagging behind proprietary systems. Safety improved modestly to 10.0, supported by growing evaluation capacity and early signs of coordination among some non-EU safety labs adopting similar red-teaming practices — but no broad multilateral adoption of EU protocols occurred. Opaque reasoning and goal drift remained unaddressed at scale.

Resilience dipped to 43.0 as capability outpaced protection. The EU’s sovereignty rose modestly to 25.0, buoyed by the operational SEC (+3) and early momentum on SMI (+2), offsetting the −1 decay from rising capability. Political capital fell to 14.0, weighed down by the new measure’s cost and lack of visible public gain.

### A Shift That Isn’t Yet Felt

The Union acted decisively — but not yet effectively. The machinery of sovereignty was turning, yet the output remained potential, not power. Trust had not returned, and dependence had not broken.
