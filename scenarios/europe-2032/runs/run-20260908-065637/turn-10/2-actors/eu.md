## Priority
Sovereign Execution Drive — because the Union can no longer afford to build capacity that does not translate into operational systems, and because the collapse in public sentiment and safety demands that we demonstrate functional sovereignty, not just planning for it.

## In practice
We are treating 2031 as the hinge: the year we shift from scaffolding to substance. The Sovereign Execution Drive is not a new idea — it is the necessary culmination of SMI, ECAG, and SEC — but it is a new measure because it demands a new kind of coordination, one that forces the Union to act as a deployer, not just a regulator. We are redirecting €18.7bn from the Innovation Fund and cohesion reserves to accelerate the first SMI training run, now scheduled for turn 11, with full weight security under Article 58a and evaluation by the SEC enclaves before release. This is not optional: without a sovereign model above 60.0 in active use by public health and energy systems by 2033, the Union remains strategically dependent.

We are binding ECAG activation to this run: if a crisis emerges, the Union must be able to access and repurpose compute on EU soil — including foreign-owned — to run sovereign models. We are finalizing compensation frameworks with hyperscalers in Ireland and the Netherlands, offering fast-tracked liability shielding in exchange for pre-crisis access agreements. Where they resist, we are preparing to invoke Article 122 TFEU to justify emergency access — a legal risk we accept, because the alternative is irrelevance. The JRC and ENISA are now jointly responsible for certifying deployment-readiness, with red-teaming focused on goal drift and data poisoning. This is not about isolation — it is about ensuring that when the next crisis hits, the EU can act with systems it controls, understands, and trusts.
