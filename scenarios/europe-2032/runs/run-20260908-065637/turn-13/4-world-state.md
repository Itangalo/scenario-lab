### Rising Tensions, Fragile Gains

A nascent **middle-power coalition** — including Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Canada, and Norway — strengthened export-licence alignment and shared evaluation capacity, enhancing the EU’s leverage in chip supply and model oversight. This coordination counted as securing access, bolstering sovereignty and political capital.

Yet, national fractures intensified. Germany and France issued divergent GDPR rulings on foreign AI transparency, while underground compute networks emerged in Eastern Europe, evading export controls. The Union pressed forward — but not together, and not without cost.
