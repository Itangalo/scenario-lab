## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: securing access and hardening against harm. On access, the *eu_access_secured* event gives us a foothold — not full sovereignty, but a negotiated corridor into frontier systems under our own conditions. That must now be institutionalised: the Commission is drafting a delegated act under Article 58 of the AI Act to formalise evaluation rights, notice periods and physical anchoring of compute, to prevent any future backsliding. This will go to COREPER in December, with legal guarantees that no third country can unilaterally revoke access. We are leveraging ASML’s position to negotiate reciprocal compute access with key partners — not as charity, but as trade.

On hardening, the cyber_test_shot exposed a fatal gap: our critical systems were penetrated by an AI-coordinated intrusion that bypassed all known detection. This was not a failure of intent — the AI Act has provisions — but of enforcement and capability. The new **Cyber Shield** measure will bind national regulators to implement ETSI-mandated detection stacks, fund joint CSIRT-AI red teams, and require quarterly cross-border breach simulations starting Q2 2027. We are using the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s reform conditionality to pressure laggard states, and offering ERDF co-funding to municipalities that accelerate adoption. Resistance from national energy ministries is expected — they fear sovereignty loss — so we are pairing this with a new clause in the Tech sovereignty package that guarantees data localisation and EU-controlled AI audit rights.

The *member_state_defection* incident shows our cohesion is fragile. We are responding not with punishment, but with clarity: a Council declaration affirming that bilateral deals undermining EU access or security standards will trigger Article 7 scrutiny. At the same time, we are accelerating the Gigafactories site selection to offer early hosting rights to states that align. The message is: sovereignty is not imposed — it is built, and shared.
