### The Shield and the Foothold

The EU takes a cautious step forward with the proposal of the **Cyber Shield for Critical Operators**, a new measure aimed at strengthening defences of energy, water, and port infrastructure against AI-driven cyber threats. The initiative would mandate ETSI-compliant detection systems and quarterly red-teaming exercises, leveraging Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF) conditionality as a potential enforcement tool. However, no binding adoption occurs this turn. Germany and the Netherlands express early support, but Poland and Hungary voice strong objections, citing national sovereignty. Legal services begin drafting the framework, but consensus is not yet secured—implementation remains contingent on future negotiations.

Simultaneously, the **eu_access_secured** condition persists as an informal corridor of influence. The Commission initiates preparatory work for a potential delegated act under the AI Act that could formalize evaluation rights and physical anchoring of frontier AI systems. Yet no legislative text is adopted, and no new powers are exercised. This remains a latent option, not an active policy.

Cohesion weakens. A **member_state_defection** occurs as Sweden finalizes a bilateral compute-sharing agreement with a US hyperscaler, undermining the bloc’s effort to present a unified posture. Brussels issues a Council declaration warning of possible Article 7 proceedings for future violations, but the political signal is diluted by lack of immediate consequences.

Executive bandwidth is strained. The **Cyber Shield** is designated the EU’s current priority, absorbing political capital and slowing progress on other fronts. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech Sovereignty Package** continue at reduced pace, with no major advances. The narrative of multiple concurrent measures is corrected: only one new measure is introduced, and only one is prioritized.

The EU’s open-weight stagnation reflects the absence of a coordinated public model programme or open infrastructure deployment—growth in this stock must be earned through deliberate action, not assumed as a passive follower of global trends.
