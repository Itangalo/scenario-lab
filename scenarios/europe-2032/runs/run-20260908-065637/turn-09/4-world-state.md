### The Weight of Unseen Harm

A biological agent, designed with AI assistance, escaped containment in a northern lab. The release was deliberate, though responsibility remains unclaimed. Weeks of containment followed, with real casualties. The incident shattered the assumption that AI risk was abstract or digital: it was now in the air, in the blood, in the headlines.

Verification also widened — a breakthrough in automated contract review and clinical coding proved that domains once thought to require human judgment now admit fast, reliable checks. This shifted the frontier: capability in law and medicine leapt forward globally, accelerating the pace of displacement and deepening institutional dependence.

The EU advanced the Sovereign Evaluation Corps, formally standing up DG-AI and securing enclave sites in Luxembourg and Helsinki. Recruitment began, and protocols were drafted. But no evaluations ran. The corps remained a skeleton — capable in design, inert in practice. Without operational assessments, the Union could not yet condition market access with teeth.

Three measures concluded their preparatory phases: the Sovereign Model Initiative unlocked pooled compute among six states, the Critical Dependency Shield became law, and the Emergency Compute Access Guarantee completed drafting. Yet implementation lagged. The SMI’s first training run was delayed, and ECAG remained in standby. Without active deployment, the gains in compute coordination and emergency planning remained potential, not realized.
