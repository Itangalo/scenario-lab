## New measure
**Critical Dependency Shield**
A binding directive requiring all public-sector AI deployments in health, judiciary, elections, and energy to undergo sovereign fallback assessment — ensuring that no system lacks a domestically controlled alternative within 12 months of deployment.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +8 (by mandating fallbacks), eu_ai_sovereignty +5 (by forcing sovereign capability into critical paths), public_sentiment -3 (short-term disruption fears)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member state governments, and public operators using AI in critical functions (health, energy, judiciary, elections)

## Priority
**Sovereign Model Initiative (SMI)** — because without a functioning EU-governed model, there is no fallback, and without fallback, the Critical Dependency Shield is empty. SMI remains the only measure that can deliver the sovereign capability that resilience now depends on, and the audit findings make its success non-negotiable.

## In practice
We are treating the Sovereign Model Initiative as the cornerstone of systemic survival, not just technological ambition. Legal services have confirmed that the solidarity clause under the MFF can fund the €18bn transfer without treaty change, but only if 15 member states sign on under enhanced cooperation. We are now in full diplomatic motion: the High Representative is leading a rolling trilateral with Italy, Spain, and France to resolve data sovereignty concerns, offering dedicated SMI compute slices for EU4Health and Eurojust under strict data residency rules. For smaller states, the EEAS is conducting secure briefings on extraterritorial model control, using redacted case studies from the Emergency Audit — including a judiciary system in a northern member state that relies on a U.S.-hosted reasoning engine for asylum decisions.

At the same time, the Critical Dependency Shield is being drafted as a Commission proposal under Article 114 TFEU, to be fast-tracked through COREPER and the Internal Market Council. It will require every critical AI deployment to submit a sovereign fallback plan within six months of go-live, with ENISA verifying capability within 12. This is not a ban — it is a forcing function. The Taiwan blockade and supply chain coercion have made it clear: we cannot depend on systems that can be cut off. The U.S. election retrenchment only confirms that even allies may withdraw support without warning. We are not waiting for consensus — we are building the capacity to act without it.
