### The Fracture Widens

Global AI capability edges to 56.0, driven by automated contract and clinical coding systems enabled by new verification techniques. Yet no safety upgrades land—governance gaps widen as labs race ahead—and `ai_safety` drops to 23.0. Open models rise to 48.0, lifted by informal spillover from national labs and anticipation of SMI’s evaluation framework, though no weights are released.

The EU responds with urgency. The **Critical Dependency Shield** is proposed, mandating sovereign fallbacks for all critical AI deployments in health, judiciary, elections, and energy. Framed as a resilience imperative, it triggers fierce debate: member states warn of disruption, vendors cite cost, and civil servants fear implementation chaos. Public sentiment dips to 35.0 amid fears of service delays and job losses.

Diplomatic efforts to salvage the **Sovereign Model Initiative (SMI)** intensify. France, Italy, and Spain reach a draft accord on data residency, but smaller states remain wary of asymmetric access. No breakthrough on Article 58a ratification occurs, and no compute is pooled. SMI remains in flight—but not yet irreversible.

Meanwhile, external shocks compound dependence. The **Taiwan blockade** disrupts global chip supply, while **supply_chain_coercion** sees Washington force Dutch export limits beyond frontier tools—hitting EU industrial capacity. The **election_retrenchment** in the U.S. confirms a strategic withdrawal: American frontier progress slows, but so does cooperation. The EU gains breathing room, but loses a key partner.

Resilience rises slightly to 44.5, boosted by partial enforcement of the Cyber Shield and early planning under the new Directive. But systemic fragility persists.

Political capital erodes to 27.0 under the weight of six large measures—including the new priority—no finishing bonuses, and growing friction. The Emergency Audit yields insights but deepens distrust. Sovereignty falls to 22.0 as capability advances and access risks mount, despite no formal exclusion.

The 2028 U.S. election is decided: retrenchment wins. The administration takes office next turn.
