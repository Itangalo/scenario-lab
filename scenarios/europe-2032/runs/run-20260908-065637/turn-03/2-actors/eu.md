## Priority  
**Sovereign Model Initiative (SMI)** — because it remains the only instrument capable of locking in irreversible EU access to frontier AI capability before external integration or internal fragmentation fractures the emergent_lab_cooperation, and because delaying structural anchoring risks losing both sovereignty and safety to de facto extraterritorial control.

## In practice  
We are pressing forward on the Sovereign Model Initiative, but the cracks are widening. Legal services in the Commission continue to flag Article 58a as vulnerable to challenge under Treaty on the Functioning of the EU (TFEU) Article 310, which limits autonomous spending powers. To counter this, we are shifting strategy: instead of relying solely on new treaty law, we are repurposing the Multiannual Financial Framework’s solidarity clause to justify the €18bn transfer from RRF funds, framing SMI as a joint resilience effort. This avoids a full treaty amendment fight, but requires buy-in from at least 15 member states under enhanced cooperation rules. We are offering Italy and Spain early access to SMI-powered public health analytics in exchange for their support in COREPER, while quietly warning smaller states that US or Chinese-controlled models will not be configured to protect EU legal norms.

At the same time, the lack of cyber resilience is becoming a liability we can no longer defer. With global ai_capability at 53.0 and ai_safety collapsing to 30.0, a single breach in a critical operator could cascade into systemic failure. The Cyber Shield remains unadopted, and two turns of prioritization have yielded only pledges. So we are now acting to enforce it: we are drafting a regulation under the NIS2 directive that conditions access to EU digital infrastructure grants and early deployment rights for SMI-derived public services on compliance with ETSI-compliant detection stacks. This applies to energy, water, and port operators in all member states. Poland and Hungary are resisting, but we are not waiting — we are suspending disbursements from the Digital Europe Programme to non-compliant regions until verification is in place. It will cost us cohesion, but the alternative — a preventable catastrophe — would cost far more.
