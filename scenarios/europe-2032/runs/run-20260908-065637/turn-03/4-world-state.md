### Cracks in the Shield

The EU presses forward with the **Cyber Shield Enforcement Mechanism**, attempting to bind compliance to digital funding and access to future AI services. However, the move triggers immediate backlash. Poland and Hungary file a preliminary reference to the European Court of Justice, arguing coercion violates subsidiarity. Disbursement suspensions stall in legal limbo, and several regional operators delay upgrades, citing budget uncertainty. The measure lands partially: detection protocols advance in Germany and the Benelux, but southern and eastern states drag their feet. Resilience improves slightly due to incremental gains, but systemic fragility persists.

Meanwhile, the **Sovereign Model Initiative (SMI)** faces deeper headwinds. The Commission’s pivot to the Multiannual Financial Framework’s solidarity clause avoids a treaty challenge — for now — but securing enhanced cooperation from 15 states proves harder than expected. Italy and Spain show interest in health analytics, but demand concessions on data governance. Smaller member states grow wary of conditional access deals, fearing permanent second-tier status. The emergent_lab_cooperation shows signs of strain as national labs recalibrate expectations. No new compute is pooled, and legal anchoring under Article 58a remains incomplete.

Globally, **ai_capability** climbs to **54.5**, driven by further automation in verification for legal contracts and clinical trial design. Yet **ai_safety** erodes further, dropping to **27.0**, as no new safeguards are implemented and oversight gaps widen. The gap between power and control grows visibly.

**openweight_capability** rises to **43.0**, lifted by anticipation of SMI’s open evaluation framework and informal spillover from lab collaborations, though no formal release occurs. Resilience edges up to **41.5** on partial cyber upgrades. Sovereignty dips to **25.0** as capability growth outpaces tangible gains and internal friction mounts. Political capital falls to **36.0**, burdened by four large measures and rising dissent. Public sentiment sours slightly to **40.0**, as enforcement is seen as heavy-handed and benefits remain abstract.
