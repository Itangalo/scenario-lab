## New measure
**Emergency Compute Access Guarantee (ECAG)**  
Establish a legally binding, crisis-triggered right of access to all compute on EU soil — including foreign-owned data centres — for the purpose of running sovereign AI systems during declared AI or infrastructure emergencies.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +7 (by guaranteeing access to physical infrastructure regardless of ownership), resilience +3 (by enabling rapid rerouting of critical AI workloads during disruption), public_sentiment -2 (due to perceived overreach and fears of nationalisation)  
`Applies to:` All data centres with more than 1 exaFLOP of AI-optimised compute located within EU territory, including those operated by non-EU entities

## Priority
**Sovereign Model Initiative (SMI)** — because without a functioning EU-governed model, every fallback, every shield, every audit is theoretical. The Critical Dependency Shield demands sovereign capability; ECAG secures the means to run it; but only SMI delivers the capability itself. With Article 58a still unratified and compute pooling stalled, this remains the linchpin of survival.

## In practice
We are treating SMI not as a project but as a condition of existence. The High Representative, backed by the Commission’s Legal Service, is now convening an Article 48(7) enhanced cooperation procedure to bypass the Article 58a deadlock, with France, Italy, Spain, Belgium, and Finland already signalling support. Funding will be drawn from the MFF solidarity clause, conditional on full data residency guarantees and compute mirroring for all participants. We are offering smaller states a two-tier access model: full voting rights in SMI governance tied to compute contributions, but guaranteed emergency access to model outputs regardless — a sovereign utility, not a club.

At the same time, the Emergency Compute Access Guarantee is being drafted under Article 122 TFEU (economic and social cohesion) and Article 222 TFEU (energy union and security of supply), with emergency activation thresholds tied to ENISA and JRC risk assessments. We are engaging directly with hyperscaler country managers in Ireland, the Netherlands, and Germany, offering compensation frameworks and liability shields in exchange for pre-negotiated access protocols. The message is clear: if your infrastructure sits in our jurisdiction, it serves our continuity when crisis hits. This is not nationalisation — it is preparedness.

The Critical Dependency Shield moves through COREPER II this month. We are amending the draft to allow phased fallback timelines for systems with high integration costs, but only if they commit to full data and agent logging under ENISA supervision. The goal is not perfection — it is visibility. We must know what we depend on, and we must be able to replace it. The Taiwan blockade proved that supply chains break. The U.S. election retrenchment proved that alliances shift. We are no longer building for competition. We are building for isolation.
