### A Turn of Hard Choices

Global AI capability advances to 57.0, driven by increasingly autonomous research agents in biotech and materials science. No safety upgrades land on deployed systems; with labs racing ahead and no coordinated evaluation regime in place, `ai_safety` drops to 21.0. Open-weight models rise to 49.0, lifted by informal spillover and anticipation of SMI’s evaluation framework—though still short of a true release.

The EU pushes forward on sovereignty. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** completes its formal lifecycle this turn, unlocking €42bn in private investment and fast-tracking permits across seven states. However, grid bottlenecks in Germany and Poland delay physical deployment, limiting real-world impact. Meanwhile, the new **Emergency Compute Access Guarantee (ECAG)** is proposed under emergency treaty clauses, aiming to secure access to all compute on EU soil during crises. While legally defensible, it triggers fierce resistance from U.S. hyperscalers, who warn of capital flight and operational withdrawal.

Diplomatically, efforts to break the SMI deadlock intensify. An enhanced cooperation procedure under Article 48(7) gains traction among six member states, but smaller nations remain wary of exclusion. A two-tier access model is floated—voting rights tied to compute contributions, universal emergency access otherwise—but consensus remains fragile.

The **Critical Dependency Shield** advances through COREPER II with phased compliance timelines, trading speed for buy-in. ENISA supervision will ensure visibility into foreign-controlled systems, even if full fallbacks take longer.

Public sentiment dips to 33.0 as fears grow over state overreach and job losses. Media spotlight falls on planned data centre seizures under ECAG, framed as “digital conscription.”

In Washington, the new administration takes office under a **RETRENCHMENT** posture. American frontier progress slows, but so does cooperation. Export controls tighten further: allied buyers now face rationing, not just adversaries. This directly undermines EU compute ambitions.

Resilience improves slightly to 46.5, aided by completed enforcement mechanisms and partial Cyber Shield rollout. Sovereignty inches up to 23.0—ECAG signals resolve, and SMI momentum builds—but political capital falls to 20.0 under the weight of six large measures and rising friction.
