The EU's public AI infrastructure suffered severe cyberattacks, disrupting healthcare and emergency services in multiple member states, exposing critical dependencies on foreign cloud providers and open-weight models weaponized through coordinated updates. A constitutional court annulled regional elections in Central Europe due to widespread synthetic media manipulation, intensifying fears over democratic erosion. The EU Public Trust Corps’ real-time dashboard, intended to bolster transparency, was taken offline by a U.S. export compliance action, underscoring systemic lack of technological sovereignty.

In response, the Commission launched the Secure and Sovereign Compute Initiative to establish EU-controlled data centres, but political capital fell below zero, stalling progress. France and Germany offered energy support, yet Poland blocked the regulation in COREPER demanding project exemptions, while Spain conditioned support on hosting a facility domestically. Italy opposed diverting InvestEU funds from green initiatives, leaving financing unresolved.

Public sentiment deteriorated further, with media framing AI as uncontrollable and governance efforts as ineffective without autonomy. Despite a modest resilience boost from joint cyber threat responses by European, Japanese, and Canadian agencies, the gap between rising AI capabilities (+1.0) and collapsing AI safety continues to widen, deepening the crisis of control.
