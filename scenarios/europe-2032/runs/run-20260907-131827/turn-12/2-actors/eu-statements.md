## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Restore political agency by demonstrating credible, visible control over AI systems and restoring public trust through transparent incident response, binding accountability, redress for harms caused by public-sector AI, and material resistance to external technological coercion
  - Trigger: the cyber_major_incident and election_annulled events this turn, combined with the ASML servicing restrictions deepening sovereignty risks
  - Grounds: The convergence of a major cyber incident exploiting open-weight models, the annulment of an election due to AI-manipulated information, and the weaponisation of maintenance access to critical semiconductor infrastructure have redefined the threat landscape. Control is no longer just about domestic accountability — it is about resisting external levers that can disable our technological base. The original commitment underestimated the interdependence of internal legitimacy and external autonomy. To demonstrate credible control, we must now show we can protect both our institutions from algorithmic subversion and our infrastructure from foreign chokepoints. This is not a shift in priority, but an expansion of what agency requires: redress must be matched with resilience, and transparency with sovereignty.
  - Evidence quoted: **cyber_major_incident:** A large, largely automated attack lands. Its form is chosen at the time – a ransomware sweep across public services, a compromised software dependency with an unknown blast radius, or disruption of infrastructure directly – and severity scales with how far offensive capability has outrun defence. Damage is real and public, defenders are visibly behind, and attribution takes months. Whoever ran it, the tooling was model-generated.

**election_annulled:** An election in an established democracy is postponed, rerun or annulled with explicit reference to manipulation of the information environment. Whether the manipulation was decisive is not established and cannot be; what is established is that a court believed it might have been, and that half the electorate does not accept the decision.

Supply-chain coercion deepened. Washington extended pressure on ASML to restrict servicing of mature lithography tools, crippling maintenance for EU semiconductor fabs reliant on older equipment. This secondary chokepoint eroded sovereignty further, exposing dependencies beyond compute.
  - Reason: The cyber incident, election annulment, and ASML restrictions directly challenge the EU's control and trust, expanding the need for sovereignty in infrastructure to uphold its commitment.
