## Priority  
**Priority:** Secure and Sovereign Compute Initiative  
This is now our dominant effort because the ASML servicing ban has exposed a fatal vulnerability: we cannot run independent oversight, redress, or incident response if the infrastructure enabling it can be disabled from abroad. The Trust Corps may be finishing, but without sovereign compute, its legacy tools — dashboards, audits, bias alerts — become untrustworthy the moment foreign providers withdraw support. This initiative is not about catching the frontier; it is about ensuring we can act *at all* when the frontier moves against us.

## In practice  
The Trust Corps reaches its scheduled end this turn, and we are treating it as a partial success: it established the first real-time public AI incident dashboard, piloted bias alerts in France and Finland, and embedded redress coordination into national ombudsman systems. But its dependence on foreign cloud infrastructure became a liability when U.S.-based providers delayed access during the recent cyber incident, citing export compliance. That delay cost hours — hours in which harm spread. As the Corps finishes, we are transferring its redress functions into the Accountability Boards and its dashboard into a new, hardened infrastructure track. The lesson is clear: trust requires not just transparency, but autonomy.

The Public Algorithmic Accountability Boards are now in active negotiation in COREPER, with a draft Regulation under Article 114 TFEU on the table. Poland’s CJEU challenge has slowed adoption, but we are offering a compromise: phased implementation with technical assistance from the European Data Protection Supervisor and front-loaded access to Redress Fund resources for early adopters. Spain remains hesitant, but informal talks suggest conditional support if we link Board mandates to national digital sovereignty grants. We are prepared to make that trade.

Now, we act on the deeper flaw: the EU has no compute we can rely on when external actors choose to restrict access. The Secure and Sovereign Compute Initiative will repurpose €18bn from the InvestEU and Digital Europe Programmes, co-financing with member states to build three sovereign data centres — in Finland, Portugal, and Romania — powered by EU-sourced renewables and governed by a new European AI Infrastructure Authority. These will host the Accountability Boards’ investigation environments, run safety evaluations for public-sector AI, and support biosecurity and cyber resilience modelling. We are already in talks with ASML and IMEC to ensure toolchain independence, and with France and Germany to secure long-term energy commitments. This is not a subsidy — it is a strategic necessity. Without it, every accountability measure we pass is a promise we cannot keep.
