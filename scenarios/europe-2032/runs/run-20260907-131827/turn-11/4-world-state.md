### A Turn of Narrow Gains Amid Deep Fractures

The EU launched the **Redress and Reintegration Fund**, its first major initiative in this crisis cycle, aiming to repair public trust through targeted redress for algorithmic harms and support for displaced workers. However, with political capital stagnant at 2.0, the fund remained unfunded in practice — no levy was imposed, and member states treated it as aspirational. France and Finland began planning pilot redress units under the nascent Public Trust Corps framework, but Poland challenged its legal basis before the CJEU, arguing overreach. Spain delayed participation, citing fiscal strain.

Instead of advancing multiple new measures, the Commission consolidated its focus on the fund as the single new initiative this turn, pausing formal advancement of the Public Trust Corps and Public Algorithmic Accountability Boards as standalone mandates. Behind the scenes, draft regulations for Algorithmic Accountability Boards were prepared, but not formally proposed — their publication delayed to preserve bandwidth and comply with implementation limits.

Abroad, the landscape shifted. An unexpected plateau in frontier AI progress slowed capability growth to +1.0 this turn, though doubts linger about how long it will hold. A significant open-weight model release did occur, accelerating distributed capability — but not instantaneously. Openweight capability rose to 60.0, reflecting early diffusion on consumer hardware, with further gains expected as adoption spreads. It remains below the frontier, but momentum is building.

Supply-chain coercion deepened. Washington extended pressure on ASML to restrict servicing of mature lithography tools, crippling maintenance for EU semiconductor fabs reliant on older equipment. This secondary chokepoint eroded sovereignty further, exposing dependencies beyond compute.

A rare bright spot emerged: a joint threat response among European, Japanese, and Canadian cyber agencies improved detection and containment of coordinated intrusions. The Union participated, gaining intelligence access it could not have built alone. Resilience edged up as a result.

Public sentiment saw modest uplift. Visible improvements in public-sector AI — faster processing of disability claims in Italy, adaptive tutoring in Portuguese schools — were credited to EU-led deployment. Still, job losses in law and accountancy firms dominated headlines, tempering goodwill.

The middle-power coalition quietly formalised, aligning export controls on dual-use AI components. While not yet decisive, it marked the first coordinated leverage the EU could exercise collectively.

### Signals in the Fog

Rumours grew of informal cyber mobilisation among northern member states, testing AI-driven counter-intrusion tools. Open-source biosecurity toolkits spread rapidly through academic labs, suggesting grassroots preparedness where institutions lagged.
