## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore political agency by demonstrating credible, visible control over AI systems and restoring public trust through transparent incident response, binding accountability, redress for harms caused by public-sector AI, material resistance to external technological coercion, and the sovereign infrastructure necessary to sustain all three  
- Trigger: the EU Public Trust Corps dashboard being taken offline by a U.S. export compliance action during a live crisis, despite its public mandate  
- Grounds: The incapacitation of a core transparency mechanism at the moment of highest need proves that jurisdiction without infrastructure is illusion. Accountability, redress, and incident response are not acts of policy alone—they are technical capabilities that fail if hosted beyond our control. This turn, we learned that sovereignty is not a precondition for governance—it *is* governance. The original commitment assumed we could build oversight first and secure the stack later. We were wrong. To restore trust, we must first restore the ground it stands on.

## Priority  
**Priority:** Secure and Sovereign Compute Initiative  
This remains our dominant effort because without sovereign infrastructure, every accountability mechanism we pass is a promise written on foreign paper — revocable at a border, a firewall, or a compliance notice. The Trust Corps’ silencing during crisis was not a glitch — it was a warning. We are now rebuilding not just capacity, but the substrate of trust itself.

## In practice  
The Secure and Sovereign Compute Initiative is now legally anchored under Article 114 TFEU, with co-financing secured through a repurposing of €12bn from the Digital Europe Programme and €6bn from InvestEU, subject to member-state matching for energy and land. We are negotiating trilateral hosting agreements with Finland, Portugal, and Romania, each offering grid resilience, geological stability, and legal clarity. France and Germany have committed long-term renewable energy contracts, but Poland continues to resist the draft regulation, demanding exemptions for national AI research clusters. We are offering a carve-out for non-distributed systems under 1 exaflop, conditional on full audit access and no foreign cloud dependencies — a trade that may unblock COREPER by turn 14.

The Public Algorithmic Accountability Boards are entering final trilogue negotiations. Spain’s demand for co-location of a data centre has been absorbed into the hosting talks, creating a political linkage: support for the Boards will be matched with hosting preference. Italy remains hesitant on funding, but we are exploring a green AI certification that would allow diverted InvestEU funds to count toward climate targets if used for energy-efficient sovereign compute. This could break the logjam without weakening the green transition.

Public sentiment remains fragile. The Commission has launched a quiet rebranding of the initiative — not as a technological project, but as the “Foundations of Trust”: a narrative campaign linking secure infrastructure to the right to vote, to healthcare, and to truth. We are briefing ombudsman offices to prepare for a wave of redress claims following the election annulment, using the Accountability Boards as a conduit. The goal is not to restore confidence overnight, but to prove that when systems fail, the EU can still act — and that the next dashboard will not go dark.
