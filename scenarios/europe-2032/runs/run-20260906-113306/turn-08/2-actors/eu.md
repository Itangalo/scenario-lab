## In practice  
We are acting on a truth the crisis forced upon us: resilience without sovereignty is performance. The European AI Emergency Response Corps finishes this turn — its cells are now embedded, its legal override tested, its compute access provisioned. But as the forensic report confirms, every defensive AI tool currently deployed across our grids, hospitals, and dispatch centres was licensed from a U.S. provider operating under a classified export regime. When the attack came, we could not patch, adapt, or inspect — only request. That ends now.

We are launching the Sovereign AI Core Activation to build, within 18 months, a minimal but operational stack: a secure cluster of 10,000 GPUs on EU soil, legally anchored under the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions and powered by repurposed Gigafactory grid commitments. On it, we will run a defensively focused agent model — not frontier, but auditable, upgradable, and under EU command. This will be trained using defensive telemetry from the CyberShield programme and the Emergency Corps’ incident logs, with interpretability baked in from day one. The model will not be open, but it will be ours.

To make this happen, we are redirecting €4.2bn from the unspent InvestAI reserve and €1.8bn from the Military Mobility budget under the EU’s civil emergency clause. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking will lead integration, with delivery milestones tied to the European Cyber Resilience Board’s threat assessments. Resistance is expected from member states reliant on U.S. cloud partnerships and from industry lobbies warning of duplication — we are offering them early access and joint governance in return for non-obstruction. This is not about catching the frontier. It is about not being left in the dark when the lights go out.
