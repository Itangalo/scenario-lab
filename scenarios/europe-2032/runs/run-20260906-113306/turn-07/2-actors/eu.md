## In practice  
We are acting in the wake of breach. The cyber_major_incident — a self-propagating ransomware sweep that disabled emergency dispatch systems in three capitals and disrupted power distribution across two grids — has exposed how far offensive autonomy has outpaced our defences. The tooling was AI-generated, adaptive, and exploited zero-day vulnerabilities at machine speed. Human-led response was too slow, too fragmented, and too siloed. This is not a failure of will but of architecture: we built plans, but not reflexes.

We are now forcing execution on the European AI Emergency Response Corps, which has been stuck in design since turn 6. Under Article 122a TFEU, we are bypassing stalled ratification by activating its operational core through a binding Commission decision, co-signed by ENISA and ECDC, with immediate effect. Nine pre-positioned response cells — each composed of cyber, bio, and systems engineers with cross-border deployment rights — are being activated this turn. They will be embedded in national CERTs and public health agencies by turn 8, with direct access to sovereign compute at Barcelona, Bologna, and LUMI. To break federalist resistance, we are offering full cost coverage for integration and a sunset clause: intervention authority expires automatically after 72 hours unless renewed by the Council. This is not a power grab — it is a triage protocol.

Simultaneously, we are launching CyberShield as the next layer of preparedness. Drawing on the €3.8bn unspent from the Resilience Surge Protocol and €2.1bn from the emergency reserve of the Digital Europe Programme, we are deploying AI-driven intrusion detection and automated patching systems across Tier 1 critical infrastructure. These will operate under strict human-supervised escalation: autonomous only in detection and isolation, never in retaliation. The European Cyber Resilience Board will oversee deployment, with real-time reporting to the European Parliament. This is not perfect defence — nothing is — but it is the first time we are building systems that can move at the same speed as the threat.
