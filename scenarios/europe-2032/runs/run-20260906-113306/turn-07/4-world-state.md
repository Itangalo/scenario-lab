### The Fire This Time

A self-propagating ransomware strain, generated and deployed by AI, cripples emergency dispatch systems in three EU capitals and disrupts power grids across two member states. The attack exploits zero-day vulnerabilities at machine speed, overwhelming human-led defences. Attribution remains unclear, but forensic analysis confirms AI-generated tooling — a sign that offensive autonomy has decisively outpaced defence.

In response, the EU activates the core of the European AI Emergency Response Corps via a binding Commission decision, bypassing stalled national ratification. Nine cross-border response cells — combining cyber, bio, and systems engineers — are established and begin integration with national CERTs and public health agencies. Sovereign compute hubs in Barcelona, Bologna, and LUMI are provisioned for emergency access. The move draws legal challenges from two member states, but the Commission invokes Article 122a as a crisis override.

The CyberShield Activation and Integration programme is launched this turn, initiating deployment of AI-driven intrusion detection and automated patching across Tier 1 infrastructure. Energy grids, air traffic control, and central banking systems are prioritised, though full integration will take time. Human-supervised escalation protocols limit autonomy to detection and isolation. The European Cyber Resilience Board assumes oversight, reporting directly to Parliament. Due to its high cost and complexity, full implementation is expected no sooner than two turns from proposal.

The Resilience Surge Protocol completes this turn, delivering planning frameworks now operationalised in response to the attack. However, the InvestAI Gigafactories do not complete; while funding is finalised and sites selected, construction has not yet begun. As a high-cost infrastructure measure, it remains in early implementation and does not qualify for completion bonus this turn. BioShield Rapid Detection Network is not proposed; instead, planning begins on a potential future extension, but no formal proposal is advanced, preserving the single-measure limit.

AI capability rises to 78.5, driven by continued self-improving agent development. The opacity of these systems deepens, and with no safety measure yet embedded in deployed models, ai_safety declines to 10.0 — a moderate drop reflecting growing misalignment risks, but not a collapse, as defensive coordination exerts partial stabilising pressure. Openweight capability advances to 59.0, trailing the frontier. Resilience increases to 45.0, reflecting the full effect of the Resilience Surge Protocol and partial activation of emergency response systems, but not premature credit for ongoing initiatives. Sovereignty dips to 14.0 due to capability-driven decay. Political capital rises to 13.0, gaining only the completion bonus from Resilience Surge Protocol and crisis-response momentum, offset by portfolio strain. Public sentiment improves to 40.0 on visible crisis response.
