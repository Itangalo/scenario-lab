## In practice
The **Tech sovereignty package** concludes this turn with mixed momentum. While the €200bn private investment target remains formally on track, only €42bn has been committed to date, concentrated in Finland, Ireland, and Poland. Investor hesitation reflects both global supply chain instability and uncertainty around U.S. export policy. To counter this, we are activating state-backed loan guarantees under the InvestEU programme, targeting first-mover projects with binding commitments on local hiring and grid integration by turn 9. The European Commission, working through the Competitiveness Council, is fast-tracking permitting for three new data centre zones under the revised TEN-E regulation, but legal challenges from environmental NGOs in Germany and Belgium threaten delays.

The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** moves into full implementation. Under the safeguard clause of the European Chips Act, we have issued binding audit requirements to all operators of clusters exceeding 1 exaFLOP/s, mandating hardware provenance verification, failover planning, and jurisdictional anchoring by turn 8. The European Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) provides enforcement teeth, allowing fines of up to 6% of turnover for non-compliance. Resistance from U.S.-linked hyperscalers is firm but contained: we are offering mutual recognition of U.S. Department of Energy and Japanese NICT certifications as a quid pro quo, reducing friction while preserving control. Funding of €1.8bn is drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and REPowerEU’s emergency clause, with disbursement tied to quarterly verification milestones by ENISA and EuroHPC.

Simultaneously, the emergence of **emergent_open_source_cyber_tools** forces a shift in defensive posture. The successful evasion of signature-based detection in two municipal networks — one in Rotterdam, one in Marseille — confirms that open-weight models are now enabling non-state actors to conduct sophisticated, adaptive attacks. Our new **Open-Source Cyber Threat Monitoring and Containment Framework** will integrate AI-driven anomaly detection into national CERTs, feed real-time telemetry to ENISA’s new Threat Intelligence Hub, and establish legal protocols for rapid takedown of malicious repositories under the DSA’s crisis mechanism. We are also piloting automated code-signing and provenance tracking for high-risk open-source projects, funded through Horizon Europe, to disrupt replication pathways before they weaponise. This is not about stopping open source — it is about making it survivable.
