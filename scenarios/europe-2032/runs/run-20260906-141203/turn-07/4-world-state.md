### A Fragile Leap Forward

Despite mounting pressures, the EU advances on multiple fronts — but not without cost. The **Tech sovereignty package** formally concludes, delivering symbolic momentum but uneven results. While the €200bn investment goal remains, actual commitments stand at just €42bn, concentrated in northern and eastern member states. Legal challenges in Germany and Belgium delay permitting for two of the three fast-tracked data centre zones, undermining the perception of unity.

The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** accelerates, with ENISA conducting emergency audits of high-performance clusters. Binding jurisdictional anchoring requirements are met by most operators, though U.S. hyperscalers push back on hardware provenance rules. A compromise allows partial recognition of American and Japanese certifications, preserving operational continuity but raising concerns about long-term control.

In cyber defence, the **Open-Source Cyber Threat Monitoring and Containment Framework** launches in response to successful AI-powered intrusions in Rotterdam and Marseille. National CERTs integrate new anomaly-detection systems, and ENISA activates its Threat Intelligence Hub. Early results reduce dwell time in test environments, but full deployment across municipal networks lags due to staffing shortages.

Globally, a rare **safety_breakthrough** in model interpretability improves assurance across leading labs, lifting `ai_safety`. Simultaneously, a surprising **us_china_agreement** on biological design tool controls briefly slows frontier momentum. The EU is consulted only after the fact, highlighting its exclusion from core strategic dialogues.

A **middle_power_coalition** forms among semiconductor, tooling, and rare earth suppliers — including the EU, Japan, and South Korea — establishing joint evaluation protocols and export alignment. This bolsters diplomatic leverage and is judged as securing access under rule 5.

However, **backlash_physical** erupts in rural France and northern Italy, where activists disrupt power feeds to data centres, citing environmental and sovereignty concerns. Policing is effective but politically costly.
