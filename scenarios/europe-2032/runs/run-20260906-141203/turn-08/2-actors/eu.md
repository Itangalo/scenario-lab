## In practice
The **Open-Source Cyber Threat Monitoring and Containment Framework** enters full deployment this turn. ENISA activates its Threat Intelligence Hub with real-time telemetry from national CERTs in ten member states, using AI-driven anomaly detection models trained on synthetic attack patterns generated by the Joint Research Centre. We are mandating integration into all critical infrastructure operators covered by the NIS2 Directive, with compliance tied to Digital Europe Programme funding. To address staffing gaps, we are fast-tracking a temporary EU-wide cyber reserve corps under the Civil Protection Mechanism, drawing from public-sector IT specialists and vetted private-sector volunteers. Legal protocols under the DSA’s crisis clause allow for rapid takedown of repositories distributing weaponised exploit chains, while Horizon Europe funds pilots in automated code-signing for high-risk open-source projects — not to stifle innovation, but to create friction where it matters.

Simultaneously, we are acting on the **joint_threat_response** opportunity. The new **AI Incident Escalation Protocol** will be negotiated through the Permanent Council of the European Union, with legal basis in Article 88 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU (civil protection) and Article 114 (internal market harmonisation). The protocol establishes thresholds for AI-enabled incidents — Category 3 (cross-border disruption), Category 4 (strategic infrastructure compromise), Category 5 (loss of control in autonomous systems) — each triggering automatic information sharing, joint forensic analysis via Europol’s EC3, and, at Category 4+, activation of the joint cyber command. We are offering member states mutual access to classified threat feeds and liability shielding for good-faith reporting, in exchange for real-time data sharing. This is not about centralising control — it is about making collective response faster than the attack cycle. The first exercise, *CyberShield 2030*, is scheduled for turn 9, simulating a coordinated AI-powered grid disruption across the Benelux.
