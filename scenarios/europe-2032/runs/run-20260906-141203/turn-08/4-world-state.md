### A Step Forward in Crisis Response

The EU capitalises on the **joint_threat_response** momentum, launching the **AI Incident Escalation Protocol and Cross-Border Response Network**. Negotiations among member states proceed faster than expected, aided by the urgency of recent breaches and the credibility of the new joint cyber command. The protocol establishes clear incident thresholds and mandates real-time data sharing, though Hungary and Poland initially resist liability shielding provisions, demanding greater national control. A compromise is reached by deferring full implementation in defence-linked systems, preserving the framework’s integrity while delaying full integration in two countries.

The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** concludes successfully, with ENISA certifying 87% of declared high-performance clusters as jurisdictionally anchored and tamper-resistant. U.S. hyperscalers comply under the revised hardware certification compromise, avoiding operational disruption. This strengthens the EU’s operational autonomy, marginally improving its leverage.

Meanwhile, the **Open-Source Cyber Threat Monitoring Framework** enters active deployment. Ten national CERTs are now connected to the ENISA Threat Intelligence Hub, and early AI-driven detection models identify two novel exploit chains derived from public repositories. However, staffing shortages persist, and only 40% of critical infrastructure operators have completed integration. The cyber reserve corps is approved but slow to mobilise.

Public sentiment remains stagnant at 34.0, as rural protests over data centre expansion continue in France and Italy. No major incident occurs, but grid operators report increasing anomalies linked to AI-managed load balancing, feeding quiet concern.
