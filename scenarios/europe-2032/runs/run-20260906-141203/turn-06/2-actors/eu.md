## In practice
We are acting on the fact that sovereignty is not just about how much compute we have, but whether we can still turn it on tomorrow. The **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** concludes this turn with partial success: 42% of Tier 1 operators now have fallback systems, red-teaming protocols are active in five major grids, and ENISA has issued compliance notices to laggards. We are converting this into a standing requirement under NIS2, using the final reporting wave to lock in minimum telemetry and response thresholds. The **Tech sovereignty package** remains on track, though investor sentiment has cooled due to global uncertainty — we are countering with targeted state guarantees for first-mover data centre projects in Finland, Ireland, and Poland.

But the Taiwan blockade changes everything. Overnight, the EU’s upstream leverage in lithography and materials has become both our strongest card and our most exposed flank. We cannot wait for full-scale gigafactories to come online. The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** begins immediately: we are mobilising €1.8bn from the Digital Europe Programme and REPowerEU’s emergency clause to fund hardware verification, legal anchoring, and redundancy upgrades across existing clusters. Through the European Chips Act’s safeguard clause, we are requiring all operators of facilities above 1 exaFLOP/s to submit to audit and failover planning by turn 7. Resistance is expected from U.S.-linked hyperscalers, but we are framing this as reciprocal — we will recognise equivalent U.S. or Japanese assurances in return. This is not nationalisation; it is pre-emption. If we do not secure what we already have, we will have nothing left to defend.
