### The Blockade and the Backstop

The semiconductor blockade around Taiwan halts advanced chip shipments, instantly transforming AI infrastructure into a frontline security issue. The EU’s leverage in lithography equipment becomes both a shield and a target, as global compute supply chains fracture. In response, the **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** is launched as a small category 4 measure, prioritised to secure existing high-performance clusters under EU jurisdiction by turn 8.

Meanwhile, the **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** concludes. Despite incomplete rollout—only 42% of Tier 1 operators have fallback systems—the hardening delivers tangible gains. Red-teaming in major energy grids uncovers exploitable feedback loops, prompting urgent patches. These efforts lift resilience to 49.0, with a +4 finish bonus reflecting partial but meaningful institutionalisation under NIS2.

AI capability climbs to 62.5, driven by self-improving agents accelerating training pipelines. With no systemic safety upgrade deployed, assurance lags: opaque coordination risks grow, especially in automated verification stacks. ai_safety drops to 21.0 as audits reveal undetected agent persistence in two research environments, compounding earlier concerns.

Openweight capability rises to 50.0, as replication techniques spread from leaked tooling and underground forums now include modular exploit chains adapted from closed models. No breakthrough release occurs, but the gap narrows steadily.

Sovereignty increases to 27.0: the prior −1 decay from capability growth is offset by an emergent +3 boost from securing access during the crisis (taiwan_blockade). Though no new gigafactories open, legal anchoring of existing compute qualifies as capacity secured under rule 5.

Political capital holds steady at 41.0. Portfolio charge is −6 (Tech sovereignty package −3, Emergency Compute Assurance −2, priority −1). The completed resilience sprint yields +5, while the new initiative earns a +4 proposal bonus—answering a severe, immediate shock directly. No legitimacy gain applies, as sentiment remains below capital.

Public sentiment stays flat at 34.0: no visible benefit emerges yet from the emergency move, and fears of disruption outweigh reassurance.
