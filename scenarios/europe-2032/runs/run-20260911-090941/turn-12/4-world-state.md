### The sweep
It started as a helpdesk ticket in a dozen town halls at once. By mid-February a largely automated ransomware sweep had locked registries, billing and patient administration across uncertified municipalities and clinics in several member states. Clean backups existed in certified networks; outside them, staff went back to paper while cross-border repair crews drove with hard drives.

Defenders were visibly behind. The tooling was model-written, mutating faster than signature lists, and attribution slipped away into months of forensics. Certified hospitals degraded rather than stopped, taking diverted patients while their own systems were patched. Small-town administrators repeated that the certificate scheme had marked them as exposed without protecting them, and some quietly stopped forwarding telemetry.

### Care as counterweight
In the same months two different kinds of news arrived. University hospitals began offering tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions, built on frontier models and delivered first through certified public hospitals under EU-controlled computing and incident reporting. Waiting lists formed within weeks.

At the same time a control technique from the laboratories — a way to predict and certify certain behaviours of deployed systems rather than argue about them after the fact — was hurried into clinical and administrative systems. Engineers wanted it; health authorities made it a condition for deployment.

Brussels fused the two into a single restoration drive: repair crews and clean backups for hit services, plus trusted care rollout. No new fund was created. Money came from capped emergency and social-fund reflows, and missing commercial hosting was replaced with conditioned allied capacity. Finance ministers got no new frontier build; interior and health ministers got crews on the ground and a visible therapy queue.

The holdout capital accepted conditioned help for sequencing and restoration tied to common reporting, without rejoining the common line. Recovery was real but uneven — certificates restored faster where they already existed — and public mood, lifted briefly by recoveries in wards, sank again under the weight of the sweep and continued hiring freezes.
