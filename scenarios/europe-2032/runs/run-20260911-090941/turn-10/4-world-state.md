### Patch Tuesday, every day
The first good news in years came from the defenders. New tooling that patched flaws almost as fast as they were found, and detectors that spotted coordinated bot behaviour rather than known signatures, spread quickly through banks, telecoms and certified hospitals. National response teams pushed updates out through existing certificates and rehearsal networks, and for the first time intrusions that would have cascaded simply fizzled.

Where the tooling was installed, the spring exercises felt different. Where it was not — small-town utilities, uncertified clinics — administrators watched the same dashboards and saw no change. The gap between protected and unprotected widened visibly.

### A recipe argument
At the same time a disturbing paper circulated among biosecurity specialists. A genome model had helped sketch a viable human-infecting design, or a study claimed a non-expert could get there with assistance. Methodologists attacked the claims; others accused the authors of publishing too much. Health authorities in Brussels quietly ordered extra detection stocks and asked allied labs abroad to pool their blind tests of opaque systems. The public heard little, but the expert argument did not settle.

### Money for the missing jobs
In Brussels the missing graduate offers finally forced spending. Labour and finance ministers agreed a capped, temporary guarantee: income bridging, six-month retraining vouchers and first-job credits in law, accountancy, software and customer operations, with large deployers of assistants asked to report automation and contribute.

The decision was fast by EU standards, but rollout was uneven. Two member states opened voucher portals by May; others argued over eligibility and co-financing rates. Employer groups called the hiring credits too small to offset automation; youth organisations called them a start. Graduate fairs remained thin, and anger over empty desks eased only slightly.

The standing compact with Japan, Korea, Canada and Gulf hosts formally entered operation, pooling hosting quotas and swapping incident feeds. The holdout capital kept its separate supply arrangement, neither breaking nor mending, leaving cohesion cracked but functional.
