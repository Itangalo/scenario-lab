## New measure  
**European AI Safety Lab (EASL)**  
Establish a public evaluation and monitoring agency with authority to conduct pre-deployment assessments of high-capability models entering the EU market.

## In practice  
We are pushing forward on three fronts, but cohesion is tightening. The Cyber Shield remains our political lifeline: without demonstrable progress on protecting energy grids and hospitals, the emergent backlash will harden into legislative resistance. This turn, we are finalising the draft Council Regulation under Article 114 TFEU and submitting it to COREPER II, backed by an updated impact assessment from ENISA showing how AI-coordinated intrusions could bypass legacy NIS2 safeguards. To break the deadlock with Germany and Slovakia, we are offering opt-in pilot phases for red-teaming — voluntary at first, mandatory after one year — and clarifying that data sharing occurs only at the network-monitoring layer, not personal data. The €12bn Resilience Modernisation Facility is being fast-tracked through the MFF flexibility clause, with DG REGIO preparing disbursement rules by Q2. We are also engaging directly with RWE and TenneT to co-design operational safeguards that preserve grid stability while enabling adaptive defence.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories are hitting real-world friction: local referenda in northern Finland and southern Spain are threatening site approvals over power allocation. We are responding with binding regional development packages — including retraining for displaced forestry and agricultural workers — funded at €1.2bn across the two sites, drawn from cohesion instruments. Grid access remains the bottleneck: we are instructing ACER to revise congestion management rules to prioritise AI-critical loads during peak stress, a move that risks tension with renewable exporters but is necessary to signal credible commitment. On the Tech sovereignty package, we have secured non-binding MOUs with OVHcloud and Capgemini for EU-anchored deployments, but AWS and Microsoft remain cautious. Our leverage lies in the Digital Markets Act review: we are signalling that gatekeeper status renewal will depend on verifiable legal and physical anchoring of AI training infrastructure in the EU.

Now, we are launching the European AI Safety Lab (EASL), recognising that trust cannot be legislated without technical credibility. Hosted jointly by DG CONNECT and the Joint Research Centre, with satellite nodes in France, Germany, and Sweden, EASL will begin operations in late 2027 with a core team of 150 — half recruited from frontier labs, half from national cybersecurity agencies. Its mandate includes pre-release evaluations of any model above 50.0 capability entering the EU market, using compute secured under EU jurisdiction and threat models developed in collaboration with ENISA. We are funding it through a reallocation of €8.5bn from the Horizon Europe reserve, justified as a direct response to the drop in ai_safety. This is not a replacement for private-sector testing — it is a check on it. And it is the first step toward closing the gap between what the world builds and what we can verify.
