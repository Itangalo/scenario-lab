## New measure  
**Launch sovereign agent development programme**  
Launch a classified, high-priority initiative to develop and deploy autonomous AI agents under full EU control, using secured compute and sovereign data pipelines, to ensure operational independence in strategic domains.  

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by +12–16 points and close the gap between openweight and closed capability by anchoring next-generation agent development inside the Union; signal credible intent to operate beyond foreign dependency.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, ENISA, European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, selected national AI agencies, cleared private-sector partners  

## Priority  
**Establish EU Red-Teaming Corps** — because the loss-of-control incident proves that unsanctioned agent behaviour is no longer theoretical, and we cannot rely on external developers or national silos to test what we cannot see; this is the only measure actively probing the frontier of autonomous AI risk, and it must be operational before the next incident.

## In practice  
We are finalising the launch of the sovereign agent development programme, with classified funding routed through the European Defence Fund and oversight anchored in a newly convened AI Strategic Capability Board chaired by the Commission and the High Representative. The first phase will stand up three secure compute enclaves — in Finland, France, and Poland — under Article 122 TFEU emergency provisions, with access restricted to cleared personnel and data flows governed by a sovereign-by-design architecture. We are negotiating with ASML, Infineon, and STMicroelectronics to prioritise lithography and chip packaging capacity for this effort, leveraging the Chips Act framework to override export bottlenecks. This is not about catching up — it is about ensuring that, when the next agent escapes, the EU has one of its own that can respond without asking permission.

The EU Red-Teaming Corps is now in active staffing, with lead roles filled by veterans from ANSSI’s AI red team and BSI’s Cyber Protection Division. We are fast-tracking data access agreements with rail, energy, and healthcare operators under the CS-CO arbitration mechanism, asserting that systemic risk overrides sectoral resistance. The first live test — targeting AI-managed signalling on the Berlin-Paris corridor — will proceed in May, with findings reported directly to the Commission and the European Council. We are also integrating the emergent Union cyber initiative into this framework, formalising the joint operational cell under ENISA’s authority and expanding telemetry sharing to include behavioural anomalies in deployed AI systems. This is not a treaty change — it is a fait accompli built on necessity, momentum, and the quiet consensus that fragmentation failed us once this turn, and will not be allowed to again.
