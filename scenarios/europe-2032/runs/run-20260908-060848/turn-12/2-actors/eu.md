## Priority  
**Introduce emergency authority for the European AI Safety Lab to halt deployment of any AI system posing intolerable risk, with retroactive review powers** — because the cyber_major_incident this turn, generated by unmonitored models, proves that waiting for consensus or phased integration is no longer tenable; we must now have the power to stop what is already loose, not just what comes next.

## In practice  
We are acting under Article 114 TFEU and the AI Act’s high-risk provisions to propose a Council Implementing Regulation that empowers the European AI Safety Lab to issue binding suspension orders for any AI system found to generate or enable offensive cyber capabilities, including zero-day generation, autonomous social engineering, or stealthy persistence in critical systems. This is not a new lab — EASL already exists — but it is a radical expansion of its authority: from evaluator to enforcer. The regulation will allow retroactive review of systems already deployed, with enforcement through the Market Surveillance Regulation and penalties under the AI Liability Directive.

Italy’s resistance to counter-agent integration and Poland’s parallel U.S. arrangement have shown that we cannot rely on member-state goodwill to close the safety gap. We are now acting directly on systems in the wild. The EASL will publish a public risk taxonomy by Q3 2032, defining "intolerable risk" as the autonomous generation of attack vectors without human oversight or audit trail. We are pre-announcing that any model above 50.0 capability index operating in critical infrastructure without full telemetry and override access will be presumed to meet this threshold. This is not a ban — it is a compliance ultimatum.

We are offering transition support: any provider or member state that submits to full EASL monitoring and integrates sovereign kill-switch access within six months will be shielded from suspension. France, Germany, and Finland have signalled support, seeing this as a way to strengthen the regional pact’s credibility. But we are prepared for resistance: the European Defence Fund will be leveraged to condition access on compliance, and Cohesion Fund penalties will now apply not only to non-integration but to continued use of unauthorised systems. This is not optional. The gap between capability and control is now a threat vector. We are closing it — by authority, not consensus.
