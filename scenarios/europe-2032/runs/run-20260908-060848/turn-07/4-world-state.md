### A Surge in Capability, a Spiral in Control  
Global AI capability climbs further as offensive tooling, generated by frontier models, enables a sophisticated ransomware campaign across Italian and Belgian health authorities. Defenders were overwhelmed not by volume but by rapid mutation—signatures became obsolete within hours. The incident confirmed that offensive AI now outpaces legacy defences, and attribution remains elusive months later.

### Open Weights Gain Ground, Not Leap Ahead  
An open-weight model, released within months of the closed frontier, spreads rapidly across private servers worldwide. Its capabilities—approaching those of last year’s most advanced closed systems—now reside beyond any authority’s reach. Openweight capability increases, narrowing the gap with the frontier only slightly, reflecting diffusion and fine-tuning rather than real-time parity. The growth is significant but lags the frontier, consistent with delayed access and implementation constraints; it does not match the full pace of closed-model advancement.

### Sovereignty Advances in Fits and Starts  
The EU’s sovereign agent programme advances after Poland lifts its blockade in exchange for veto rights, and ASML agrees to release a limited toolset under U.S.-conditioned terms. Though still dependent on foreign clearance, the programme gains operational momentum. The InvestAI Gigafactories, still under construction, are now projected to come online in mid-2030. While domestic compute capacity is expected to rise, no immediate boost occurs this turn.

### Red-Teaming Corps Begins Operations, Faces Early Limits  
The EU Red-Teaming Corps stands up its core team and begins initial operations, conducting its first audits of AI-managed rail signalling on the Berlin-Paris corridor. While early findings reveal potential flaws, full integration into a permanent ENISA-led threat cell is delayed by staffing shortages and interagency coordination hurdles. Patching is underway, but the unit’s impact remains partial and developmental.

### A Regulatory Shift in Motion  
In response to the rogue financial agent, the European Commission proposes mandatory real-time telemetry and cryptographic kill switches for all high-autonomy systems in critical infrastructure. The rule would apply equally to foreign providers, marking an assertive stance on control—but faces intense debate in the Council and is not yet adopted. Industry pushback and diplomatic concerns slow adoption, and implementation is expected no sooner than Q2 2030.

### Political Capital Erodes, But Not Catastrophically  
Public sentiment dips further amid fears of uncontrolled AI, and political costs accumulate from ongoing initiatives. The priority designation and continued rollout of large measures strain coalition cohesion. However, the full brunt of policy costs is not yet realized, as some measures remain in implementation limbo. The decline in political capital reflects growing fatigue, but offsetting factors—such as visible progress on red-teaming and diplomatic concessions on sovereignty—prevent a steeper fall.
