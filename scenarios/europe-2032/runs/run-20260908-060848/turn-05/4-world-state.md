### A Major Breach, a Widening Gap  
A coordinated ransomware sweep paralyzes hospital networks across five member states, exploiting AI-generated zero-days in legacy diagnostic software. The attack unfolds autonomously, adapts to patching attempts, and evades detection for 36 hours—proof that offensive capability has decisively outpaced defence. Meanwhile, verification widens: clinical coding is now automatically checkable, accelerating AI deployment in healthcare with unanticipated speed.

### Shield Completed, But Unity Frays  
The Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO) and Secure Critical Infrastructure by Default formally conclude implementation. ENISA enforces compliance via MFF conditionality, but Germany invokes national override clauses, rejecting interoperable fallback protocols. Council arbitration under NIS2 begins, yet delays risk hollowing out the shield’s cross-border integrity. Technical teams deploy to Greece and Bulgaria, but progress is patchy.

### Red-Teaming Gains Ground, Safety Stalls  
The EU Red-Teaming Corps is established, with secondments confirmed from key agencies. Planning advances for rail signalling tests, though data access disputes persist. Yet no new safeguards land on frontier models—the European AI Safety Lab’s assessment remains advisory, blocked by legal challenges. With capability rising sharply and no safety gains, assurance erodes.

### Sovereignty Push Begins, Not Yet Operational  
The European Commission approves a classified proposal to develop sovereign AI agents under full Union control, aiming to anchor next-generation capabilities within EU governance. However, implementation is in early stages: staffing, budget allocation, and legal clearance are pending. The initiative does not yet contribute to capability metrics. Global AI capability climbs to 62.0, widening the gap with EU efforts. Openweight capability rises to 50.0, reflecting limited leakage of clinical AI tools into open ecosystems—progressive, but constrained by access barriers and export controls.

### Political Capital Holds, Sentiment Sours  
Despite the crisis, political capital rises slightly, buoyed by decisive action on infrastructure enforcement and the sovereign agent programme’s strategic signal. But public sentiment dips as hospitals go dark and headlines frame AI as an uncontrolled threat. A backlash grows in parliaments across Germany, France, and the Netherlands, calling for moratoriums on AI in critical systems.
