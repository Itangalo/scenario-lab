## New measure
**Launch sovereign agent development programme**  
Launch a classified, high-priority initiative to develop and deploy autonomous AI agents under full EU control, using secured compute and sovereign data pipelines, to ensure operational independence in strategic domains.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`

## Priority
**Secure Critical Infrastructure by Default** — because this is the final turn of implementation and fragmentation risks are real: Germany’s fallback override creates incompatible protocols, while Greece and Bulgaria lack implementation capacity, so we must enforce coherence now or lose the measure’s core benefit of systemic resilience.

## In practice
We are closing out the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO) and Secure Critical Infrastructure by Default this turn, with the Regulation now binding under Article 114 TFEU. ENISA has published the first red-teaming templates, and compliance is being tied to MFF disbursements — a line we are enforcing despite German resistance. We have triggered Council arbitration under NIS2 to override national fallback vetoes, arguing that interoperability is non-negotiable for cross-border resilience. DG DIGIT is deploying rapid-response technical teams to Greece and Bulgaria, co-funded by the Resilience Modernisation Facility, to close implementation gaps before the winter review.

The EU Red-Teaming Corps is now formally established, with secondment agreements signed by ANSSI, BSI, and NCSC. Initial staffing begins next quarter, and planning is underway for the first live simulation on AI-managed rail signalling in Q1 2029. Legal access to operator data remains under negotiation, but we are asserting authority under the CS-CO framework to compel cooperation. Findings will be anonymised and published to build public trust — because we cannot secure what we do not test, and we will not wait for a blackout to learn what fails.

The European AI Safety Lab (EASL) is finalising its pilot assessment of the Swiss agent framework, using red-team protocols developed with BSI and ANSSI. Legal challenges loom, but we are proceeding under the “significant market impact” clause, asserting that any model above 50.0 capability operating in the EU must submit to evaluation. Simultaneously, we are accelerating the Tech sovereignty package: DMA gatekeeper reviews now include a binding clause requiring AWS, Microsoft, and Google to legally anchor their AI training infrastructure in the EU by turn 6, with access subject to revocation. This is not leverage — it is a condition of market access, and we are prepared to enforce it.
