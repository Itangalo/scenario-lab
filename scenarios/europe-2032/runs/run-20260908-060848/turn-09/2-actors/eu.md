## In practice  
We are executing the sovereign agent programme under Article 122 TFEU, with co-funding from the European Defence Fund and dual-use compute enclaves now operational in Finland and France. The Polish veto was lifted in exchange for a binding seat on the AI Strategic Capability Board and a national opt-in requirement for any deployment on its territory — a concession that holds cohesion but risks delay. ASML’s “trusted foundry” release remains pending U.S. Treasury clearance; we are treating turn 8 as the deadline, and if clearance fails, we will invoke the Anti-Coercion Instrument to reframe semiconductor access as a security negotiation, not a dependency. The architecture now includes mandatory telemetry to EASL and cryptographic kill switches under ENISA’s authority — not as a limitation, but as a condition of political survival.

The Red-Teaming Corps has transitioned from pilot to permanent status, now embedded within ENISA’s Threat Intelligence Cell. Its findings from the Berlin-Paris rail test have triggered mandatory patching cycles, with CS-CO arbitration ensuring cross-border enforcement. We are expanding its mandate to include live red-teaming of financial AI agents in Frankfurt and Paris, starting this quarter. Meanwhile, the cyber_major_incident in Italy and Belgium has accelerated the adoption of the telemetry mandate under the AI Act: ENISA is finalising technical standards for real-time decision logging and kill-switch integration, with enforcement tied to market access. No foreign provider will be exempt — this is not protectionism, but a condition of operating in a space where a single rogue agent can paralyse a regional health system.

Public sentiment remains fragile, and we are not blind to the risk of overreach. But the alternative — a second-order cascade from an uncontained AI incident — would shatter trust entirely. We are therefore moving forward with the new counter-agent measure under the Cyber Resilience Act and ENISA’s expanded mandate, with oversight from the European Data Protection Supervisor to prevent mission creep. The system will not act outside critical infrastructure, will not target civilian systems, and will be subject to biannual audit by the European Parliament. This is not the path we wanted, but it is the one the world has forced upon us: defence at machine speed requires machine agency, and we will not outsource our survival.
