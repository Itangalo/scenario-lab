## New measure
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)**  
Establish mandatory intrusion detection and response protocols for all operators of essential services, with red-teaming and cross-border incident coordination.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by +15–20 points; reduce probability of cascading cyber incidents exploiting AI-enabled automation.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, ENISA, critical infrastructure operators (energy, water, transport, health)

## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)** — because the cyber_test_shot event proves that detection based on known patterns is no longer sufficient, and the Union cannot afford to learn the hard way that its critical systems are blind to AI-coordinated intrusions.

## In practice
We are acting on the realisation that our defences are reactive, not anticipatory. The cyber_test_shot incident did not target the EU primarily, yet two member states were compromised — and we were lucky it was found at all. This turn, the Commission will draft a Council Regulation under Article 114 TFEU, amending the NIS2 Directive to impose AI-aware monitoring standards on all designated operators of essential services. ENISA will be empowered to certify detection systems that use AI to model adversarial behaviour, not just match signatures, and to conduct unannounced red-team exercises using controlled offensive AI tools. We will require data-sharing agreements between national CSIRTs and ENISA to enable real-time correlation of anomalies — a step that several member states have resisted on sovereignty grounds, but which the recent intrusion makes indefensible to delay.

To get this through COREPER and the Internal Market Council, we are pairing the proposal with a €12bn Resilience Modernisation Facility under the MFF, co-financing up to 70% of detection system upgrades for lower-capacity member states. This addresses both the capability gap and the cohesion risk. The Facility will be managed jointly by DG CONNECT and the European Defence Agency, leveraging dual-use expertise. Industry pushback from utility providers centres on cost and operational burden, so we are structuring compliance in phases, with lighter obligations for SMEs in the supply chain. Still, this is a hard sell — public_sentiment is ambivalent, and framing this as a security necessity, not a digital transformation, is key. We are briefing the European Parliament’s LIBE and IMCO committees early to avoid a backlash over surveillance overreach, emphasising that the systems monitor network behaviour, not citizens.

At the same time, we are standing up the InvestAI Gigafactories with site selections underway in Finland, Spain, and Poland. The challenge is not funding — the €200bn is committed — but grid availability and local opposition to energy-intensive projects. We are negotiating with transmission system operators to reserve 500MW for AI workloads by 2028, and offering regional development packages to host communities. The Tech sovereignty package is driving private investment conversations with Equinix, AWS, and OVHcloud, but the US cloud giants remain cautious about binding commitments under EU legal control. We are signalling that access to the EU market for AI services will increasingly depend on physical and legal anchoring here — a leverage point we intend to formalise in the next phase.
