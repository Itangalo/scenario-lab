### The Sovereignty Pivot

The EU executes a dramatic reversal: repurposing the stalled InvestAI Gigafactories into sovereign emergency compute clusters to counter sudden frontier access denial. Under Article 122 TFEU, three of five completed sites are legally ring-fenced for public-use AI workloads in healthcare, energy, and civil protection. France and Germany back the move; Italy cautiously supports the health cluster, while Poland remains aloof but accepts integration with its national emergency network.

Despite swift legal action, implementation faces friction. ENISA audits reveal two sites lack secure telemetry integration, delaying operational readiness. Industry partners protest the override of commercial access, and a leaked memo questioning the fallback models’ effectiveness fuels media doubt. No major incident occurs, but the perception of scrambling persists.

### Access Cut, Leverage Broken

In parallel, the U.S., under its CONSOLIDATION posture, enforces strict tiered access, cutting EU institutions from the latest frontier model without explanation. Hospitals using AI diagnostics report system failures, and transport ministries lose predictive maintenance capabilities. Simultaneously, Washington pressures the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s servicing of mature-node lithography tools—hitting not only China but also Eastern European chipmakers reliant on legacy equipment. The EU’s supply-chain leverage proves unexercisable when sovereignty over execution is absent.

### Stalled Authority, Sinking Capital

The EASL’s emergency enforcement powers remain mired in legal challenges. Poland’s constitutional court agrees to hear a case on jurisdictional overreach, freezing enforcement actions involving U.S.-hosted systems. With no safety improvements realized and public trust eroding, sentiment holds flat despite the new redundancy effort. Resilience is unchanged—existing counter-agents function, but no new capacity lands.

Political capital declines further under the weight of multiple large measures and a failing priority. The pivot to sovereign compute earns no immediate bonus, as it responds to an ongoing crisis rather than closing a past one.
