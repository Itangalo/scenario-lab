The EU repurposes stalled InvestAI Gigafactories into sovereign emergency compute clusters under Article 122 TFEU, ring-fencing three sites for public AI workloads in healthcare, energy, and civil protection. France and Germany support the move; Italy conditionally backs the health cluster, while Poland remains distant but agrees to network integration. Implementation stalls due to ENISA audit findings on insecure telemetry and industry backlash over lost commercial access, while leaked doubts about fallback models fuel public skepticism. Simultaneously, the U.S. enforces tiered frontier model access, cutting EU institutions from advanced AI systems—disrupting healthcare diagnostics and transport maintenance—and restricts ASML servicing of mature-node tools, impacting Eastern European chipmakers. The EU’s inability to leverage supply-chain influence underscores its executional weakness. EASL enforcement remains paralyzed by legal challenges, including Poland’s constitutional court review of jurisdiction over U.S.-hosted systems. No safety or resilience gains occur, and political capital falls further to 18.0 amid perceived crisis management failures. Sovereignty rises slightly to 12.0 due to compute reorientation, but global AI capability advances to 73.5, widening the gap with EU open-weight progress at 60.0. Public trust remains stagnant, reflecting ongoing governance strain.
