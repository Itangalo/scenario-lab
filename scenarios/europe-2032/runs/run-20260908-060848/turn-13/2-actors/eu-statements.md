# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing. But this turn shows that without sovereignty in capability, prevention is a gesture — not governance.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Operationalise autonomous defence and sovereign response capacity as the baseline for EU-wide AI security, ensuring full integration, real-time telemetry, and unified kill-switch authority across all member states, with binding enforcement and no tolerated exceptions
- `contain_the_gap` (position): Shift focus from regulating external systems to building sovereign capacity that closes the safety gap from within, through independently controlled agents, telemetry, and override infrastructure. Regulation remains necessary, but only enforcement rooted in autonomy can close the gap when external systems are neither accountable nor available.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — applied
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing. But this turn shows that without sovereignty in capability, prevention is a gesture — not governance.
  - Trigger: eu_frontier_access_denied and supply_chain_coercion occurring simultaneously
  - Grounds: The denial of frontier access has grounded hospitals and ministries; the coercion of our sole chokepoint by a third power has revealed that leverage without control is an illusion. We acted under the belief that safety authority could compensate for dependency. It cannot. When the system you rely on vanishes overnight and your one strategic lever is turned against you, the two mandates collapse into one: if you do not control the means, you cannot fulfill either purpose. This is no longer about balancing priorities — it is about survival of agency. The identity evolves not because the principle changed, but because the situation has moved categorically beyond what it anticipated: we are now fighting for the material basis of decision-making itself.
  - Evidence quoted: **eu_frontier_access_denied:** The Union is cut off from the leading model at short notice, wholly or by nationality of user. No detailed reason is given, there is no appeal, and the immediate practical effect lands on hospitals, ministries and firms that had built on it. Whether this reads at home as an outrage or as a failure of foresight depends on what the Union had done about it beforehand.
**supply_chain_coercion:** Washington forces the Netherlands to cut ASML's exports and servicing further still – beyond the leading-edge machines to the older lithography equipment China uses for ordinary chips, and in the harder versions to a widening list of other customers. The instrument is jurisdiction over American technology in the supply chain, and refusing it is not obviously survivable for the company. The Union's one chokepoint is being used, and not by the Union.
  - Reason: The developments directly undermine EU sovereignty, revealing that safety authority without control over means is ineffective, thus altering the actor's foundational rationale for the statement.
- **modify** `contain_the_gap` — applied
  - Tier: position
  - Text: Shift focus from regulating external systems to building sovereign capacity that closes the safety gap from within, through independently controlled agents, telemetry, and override infrastructure. Regulation remains necessary, but only enforcement rooted in autonomy can close the gap when external systems are neither accountable nor available.
  - Grounds: With EASL’s emergency authority still in flight and already meeting legal resistance, and with the Union suddenly cut off from the very systems it sought to regulate, we cannot depend on external compliance. The gap is not just between capability and safety — it is between dependence and command. Our regulatory reach extends only as far as our operational grasp. We must now close the gap not by ruling over foreign systems, but by replacing reliance with resilience rooted in EU-controlled infrastructure.
