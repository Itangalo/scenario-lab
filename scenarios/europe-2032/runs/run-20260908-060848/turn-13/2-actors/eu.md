- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing. But this turn shows that without sovereignty in capability, prevention is a gesture — not governance.  
  - Trigger: eu_frontier_access_denied and supply_chain_coercion occurring simultaneously  
  - Grounds: The denial of frontier access has grounded hospitals and ministries; the coercion of our sole chokepoint by a third power has revealed that leverage without control is an illusion. We acted under the belief that safety authority could compensate for dependency. It cannot. When the system you rely on vanishes overnight and your one strategic lever is turned against you, the two mandates collapse into one: if you do not control the means, you cannot fulfill either purpose. This is no longer about balancing priorities — it is about survival of agency. The identity evolves not because the principle changed, but because the situation has moved categorically beyond what it anticipated: we are now fighting for the material basis of decision-making itself.

- modify `contain_the_gap` (position): Shift focus from regulating external systems to building sovereign capacity that closes the safety gap from within, through independently controlled agents, telemetry, and override infrastructure. Regulation remains necessary, but only enforcement rooted in autonomy can close the gap when external systems are neither accountable nor available.  
  - Grounds: With EASL’s emergency authority still in flight and already meeting legal resistance, and with the Union suddenly cut off from the very systems it sought to regulate, we cannot depend on external compliance. The gap is not just between capability and safety — it is between dependence and command. Our regulatory reach extends only as far as our operational grasp. We must now close the gap not by ruling over foreign systems, but by replacing reliance with resilience rooted in EU-controlled infrastructure.

## Priority  
**Re-purpose the InvestAI Gigafactories framework to rapidly deploy sovereign compute clusters dedicated to emergency retraining and hosting of fallback AI agents** — because eu_frontier_access_denied has created an immediate operational vacuum in essential services, and without sovereign compute, even our most aggressive safety measures are unenforceable; this turn demands not new rules, but new roots.

## In practice  
We are acting under Article 122 TFEU (emergency economic measures) and the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s crisis clause to re-task the InvestAI Gigafactories programme. The sites are built, the grid connections exist, but the original intent — commercial-scale AI training for private actors — lies stalled due to investment uncertainty. We are now converting three of the five sites into sovereign emergency clusters: legally ring-fenced, powered by EU-supervised operators, and reserved for retraining and hosting of EU-controlled agents in healthcare diagnostics, energy grid management, and civil emergency coordination. These will run fallback models derived from last-known-safe open-weight versions, fine-tuned on EU data under strict telemetry and human-in-the-loop protocols.

The European Commission is convening a special COREPER session to fast-track the legal designation of these clusters as critical infrastructure under the NIS2 Directive, ensuring priority access during energy shortages and immunity from export diversion. We are offering transition partnerships to firms previously slated for Gigafactory access: in exchange for ceding initial capacity claims, they gain guaranteed access to sovereign inference APIs and co-development rights on non-strategic modules. France and Germany support the shift; Italy, though still resistant to EASL enforcement, sees value in medical redundancy and may back the health-focused cluster. Poland remains skeptical, but we are offering direct linkage to its national emergency network as a bridge.

This is not a long-term replacement for frontier access. But it is a line in the sand: the EU will not be left blind and idle when external providers withdraw. We are building operational continuity where regulation cannot reach. Sovereignty is no longer a project — it is a necessity we begin today.
