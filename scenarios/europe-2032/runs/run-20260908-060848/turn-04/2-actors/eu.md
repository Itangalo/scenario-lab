## New measure  
**Establish EU Red-Teaming Corps**  
Create a standing, cross-border unit of offensive AI security specialists authorised to conduct unannounced penetration testing on high-risk AI systems and critical infrastructure, with powers to trigger emergency mitigation protocols.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by +6–10 points and ai_safety by +4–7 by institutionalising proactive threat discovery and forcing systemic fixes before incidents occur; reduce lag between capability growth and defensive adaptation.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, operators of essential services, high-impact AI deployers, ENISA, national cybersecurity agencies

## Priority  
**Secure Critical Infrastructure by Default** — because this is the second turn of a two-turn rollout, and national implementation is fracturing: Germany’s demand for unilateral fallback control risks creating a patchwork of incompatible protocols, while Greece and Bulgaria lack the technical staff to implement templates without central support, so we must act now to enforce coherence or lose the measure’s core benefit.

## In practice  
We are finalising the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO) this turn, with the Regulation now adopted under Article 114 TFEU and red-teaming formally mandated for all operators of essential services. ENISA will coordinate the first pan-European red-team exercise in Q3, funded through the €12bn Resilience Modernisation Facility, and non-compliance will be tied to MFF conditionality — a line we are holding despite German resistance. The measure is now law, and its delivery shifts to enforcement and capacity-building, particularly in Southern and Eastern member states.  

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track for turn 7 completion, but we are accelerating grid access in Finland and Spain through binding ACER rulings and national co-investment. We are also tightening the Tech sovereignty package: DMA gatekeeper reviews will now include a binding clause requiring AWS, Microsoft, and Google to legally anchor their AI training infrastructure in the EU by turn 6, with compute access subject to revocation if unmet. This is not mere leverage — it is a condition of market access, and we are prepared to enforce it.  

The European AI Safety Lab (EASL) is now conducting its first pilot assessment of the Swiss agent framework, using EU-secured compute and a red-team protocol developed with BSI and ANSSI. Legal challenges are expected, but we are proceeding under the “significant market impact” clause, asserting that any model above 50.0 capability operating in the EU must submit to evaluation. Simultaneously, we are scaling the Secure Critical Infrastructure by Default rollout: DG DIGIT is deploying rapid-response technical teams to Greece and Bulgaria, and we are overriding Germany’s fallback veto through a Council arbitration mechanism under the NIS2 Directive, arguing that interoperability is a condition of cross-border resilience.  

We are launching the EU Red-Teaming Corps to close the gap between detection and action. Hosted under ENISA but staffed with seconded specialists from national agencies and the private sector, it will conduct live offensive simulations on AI-driven infrastructure, with authority to trigger emergency protocols. This is not optional: at 58.0 capability, self-improving agents are outpacing human-led audits. We are funding it through a reallocation from the Resilience Modernisation Facility and drawing initial personnel from France’s ANSSI, Germany’s BSI, and the Netherlands’ NCSC. The corps will report directly to the Commission and publish anonymised findings to build public trust — because we cannot harden what we do not understand, and we will not wait for another hospital to go dark to learn that lesson.
