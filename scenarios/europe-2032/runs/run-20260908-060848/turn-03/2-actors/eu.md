## New measure  
**Secure Critical Infrastructure by Default**  
Mandate AI-hardened configurations in all new public-sector IT procurement and essential service infrastructure upgrades from 2028 onward, with pre-approved templates and automatic fallbacks in case of anomaly.  

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by +8–12 points through systemic hardening of essential services; reduce blast radius of AI-generated attacks by limiting exploitable surface and enabling faster recovery.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, national procurement agencies, operators of essential services, EU-funded infrastructure projects

## In practice  
We are executing on four measures in flight, each under pressure but converging on a single truth: sovereignty without resilience is performance, not power. The Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO) is now in its final turn of development, and we are forcing resolution in COREPER II this month. The draft Regulation under Article 114 TFEU will be amended to include mandatory red-teaming after a one-year pilot, with penalties for non-compliance tied to EU funding conditionality. We are presenting the recent cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep across hospital networks in Belgium and Bavaria — as the decisive moment: this is not a hypothetical threat. ENISA has issued a flash alert, and we are fast-tracking the €12bn Resilience Modernisation Facility through the MFF, with disbursement now tied to CS-CO compliance. Germany and Slovakia will get their operational safeguards, but not their veto.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track despite local resistance. We have finalised binding regional development packages worth €1.2bn across Finland and Spain, combining retraining for displaced workers with grid modernisation co-funded by national governments. ACER is revising congestion rules to prioritise AI-critical loads during peak stress, effective immediately under Article 9 of Regulation (EU) 2019/943. This is not popular in the Nordic Council, but the alternative — delayed compute sovereignty — is worse. On the Tech sovereignty package, we are finalising binding agreements with OVHcloud and Capgemini, and we are invoking DMA review timelines to pressure AWS and Microsoft: gatekeeper renewal will require verifiable legal anchoring of AI training infrastructure in the EU by turn 6 at the latest.

The European AI Safety Lab (EASL) is now operational in pilot form. DG CONNECT and the JRC have established interim leadership, and recruitment has begun for the core 150, with priority given to personnel from frontier labs and national cybersecurity agencies. The lab’s first mandate is to evaluate any model above 50.0 capability seeking deployment in the EU, using compute secured under EU jurisdiction. Legal authority over non-EU developers remains contested, but we are testing jurisdiction through a pilot assessment of a Swiss-developed agent framework under the newly invoked “significant market impact” clause in the AI Act. This is risky, but necessary: trust without verification is propaganda. The adoption_success this turn — a Danish public health AI cutting cancer diagnosis wait times by 40% — proves that we can deliver benefit without surrendering control. We are amplifying that story across member states to counter the moratorium momentum, but we will not let optimism blind us to the attack that just landed. Resilience is now the front line.
