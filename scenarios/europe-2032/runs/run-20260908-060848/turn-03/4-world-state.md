### The Attack That Changed Nothing — And Everything  
A coordinated ransomware campaign, powered by AI-generated exploits, crippled hospital networks in Belgium and Bavaria. The attackers bypassed legacy defences using synthetic credentials and adaptive lateral movement, disabling critical systems for over 72 hours. Public outrage was immediate. ENISA issued its first-ever flash alert, and the European Commission fast-tracked the Resilience Modernisation Facility, tying €12bn in funding to compliance with the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO). Despite resistance, COREPER II approved mandatory red-teaming for essential operators, ending the pilot phase.

### Hardening Amid Headwinds  
The new **Secure Critical Infrastructure by Default** measure mandates AI-resilient configurations in all public IT procurements from 2028. Pre-approved templates and automatic fallbacks will limit exploitability, but implementation varies. Germany insists on national sovereignty over fallback triggers, while Greece lacks the technical capacity to adopt templates without EU support. The measure begins its two-turn rollout, contributing partial resilience gains.

### Sovereignty on Shaky Ground  
InvestAI Gigafactories remain delayed in Finland and Spain, though binding regional packages and ACER’s new congestion rules stabilise the timeline. However, reliance on external compute during the gap deepens dependency. The Tech sovereignty package progresses with OVHcloud and Capgemini, but DMA pressure on AWS and Microsoft yields only vague commitments. EU access to frontier models remains contingent, and no new sovereign capacity comes online.

### Safety Without Authority  
The European AI Safety Lab (EASL) begins pilot operations, evaluating a Swiss agent framework under the “significant market impact” clause. But without legal recognition from non-EU developers, its authority is untested. Meanwhile, AI capability advances to 58.0 as self-improving agents accelerate development cycles — yet no safety gains land on deployed systems. Interpretability remains shallow, and control gaps widen.

### A Fragile Mandate  
Public sentiment rises slightly as Denmark’s AI-driven cancer diagnosis success goes viral, countering moratorium narratives. But the cyberattack’s shadow lingers. Political capital erodes under the weight of four large measures and a named priority. The Commission pushes forward, but cohesion frays at the edges.
