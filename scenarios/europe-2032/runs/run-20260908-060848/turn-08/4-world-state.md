### A Crisis of Control  
The EU scrambles to respond as a wave of AI-generated ransomware paralyzes health systems in Italy and Belgium, exposing the limits of human-mediated defence. The attack’s speed and mutation outpace traditional incident response, forcing ENISA to invoke emergency protocols. In Brussels, the Commission fast-tracks a new offensive-defensive counter-agent programme, authorizing autonomous systems to detect and neutralize threats in real time across energy, health, and transport networks.

### Sovereignty on the Brink  
The InvestAI Gigafactories officially come online, marking a symbolic leap in domestic compute. Yet, ASML’s critical tooling remains blocked by unresolved U.S. Treasury clearance, nullifying expected gains in sovereign production. With the Polish compromise straining coordination and no breakthrough in access, the EU’s AI sovereignty stagnates. Frustration mounts as the U.S. posture of consolidation tightens supply-chain pressure, and no alternative path to frontier hardware emerges.

### Red-Teaming Goes Live  
The EU Red-Teaming Corps achieves full operational status under ENISA, conducting its first live audits on financial AI agents in Frankfurt. Early findings reveal vulnerabilities in algorithmic trading systems, triggering mandatory patching. The European AI Safety Lab also launches, gaining authority to assess high-capability models—though its reach is limited to post-hoc evaluations, not real-time control.

### Political Costs Mount  
Public anxiety deepens as headlines spotlight job losses and cyber chaos. The new counter-agent initiative, while framed as defensive, draws criticism from civil society and emerging AI worker unions. Political capital erodes further under the weight of multiple large measures and a faltering public mood, despite the urgency of the moment.
