### Fractures in the Firewall

A wave of AI-driven ransomware paralysed municipal IT systems in three member states, exploiting zero-day vulnerabilities generated by unmonitored models. The attack, though contained within 72 hours, exposed deep seams in the Union’s defences. ENISA confirmed the malware’s origins in automated tooling, underscoring how far offensive capability had surged ahead of safeguards.

In response, the EU advanced a single new measure: linking Cohesion Fund disbursements to mandatory integration of autonomous counter-agents across critical infrastructure. Binding audits under the Cyber Resilience Act would now gatekeep funding, with non-compliance triggering automatic penalties. This measure, introduced this turn, is set to ramp over time, contributing to early resilience gains already reflected in the metrics.

Italy signalled resistance, citing patient safety in healthcare AI, while Poland’s separate arrangement with a U.S. hyperscaler now stood in direct tension with EU-wide standards. The Commission began consultations with Italy on a potential compliance pathway, tasking the Florence safety group to explore human-in-the-loop overrides — but no formal agreement was reached this turn. For Poland, preliminary discussions began on possible co-financing for an Eastern flank simulation hub via the European Defence Fund, contingent on future signing of the integration protocol. No decision was made, and the offer remains exploratory.

Meanwhile, France, Germany, and Finland continued developing the emerging regional pact, with joint threat modelling activities progressing under existing arrangements. This work builds on prior collaboration and does not constitute a new initiative.

The move was decisive but costly. Resilience rose as the counter-agent mandate advanced, delivering early gains. Yet sovereignty eroded further, hit by the ongoing U.S. export control escalation and the failure to unify command. Political capital dipped slightly under the weight of enforcement friction and rising backlash.

AI safety declined, as the capability frontier advanced without commensurate safety deployments. However, the drop was moderated by ongoing baseline monitoring and legacy safeguards still in place. The absence of new safety measures landing on deployed systems contributed to downward pressure, but no systemic collapse occurred. The emergent_ai_worker_unions issued a critical statement, warning of unchecked deployment—rejection of their moratorium request deepened mistrust. Public sentiment, already fragile, fell as enforcement measures were framed as punitive, not protective.

Openweight capability made progress, but not at the pace implied. The underlying compute diffusion continued, yet integration delays and partial model releases meant gains were reined in—falling short of closing the gap with frontier capability as quickly as hoped. The Union acted, but not as one. Command was asserted, yet unity remained elusive.
