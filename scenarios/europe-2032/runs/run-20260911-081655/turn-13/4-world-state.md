### Holding the fallback
Autumn 2032 tested whether the spring's patching gains could survive contact with politics and physical force.

A new openly downloadable frontier-class model spread within days to hundreds of thousands of machines. Hospitals using European-hosted fallbacks stayed largely clean; elsewhere, triage helpers and municipal helpdesks were quickly re-wired around the public weights, bypassing the detection stack Brussels had made funding conditional on.

At the same time, blockades and night-time attacks hit data-centre sites and Continuity Corps depots in four member states. Gendarmerie pools under solidarity mutual-aid, Corps repair platoons and French-Dutch cyber teams restored most sites in days, with daily uptime published district by district. Two EuroHPC nodes and a Gigafactory pilot stayed on reduced power for weeks, forcing registries back to paper.

A side-deal deepened the strain. One capital signed its own hosting arrangement with a non-EU hyperscaler on cheaper terms, defended at home as pragmatism. Brussels offered a bolt-on clause to mirror the capacity on EU soil under EU law; the capital kept the deal and delayed the mirror. Council ministers agreed to treat sabotage of continuity sites as an offence of Union interest, unlocking Europol coordination, but procurement freezes by hospital federations widened, demanding liability guarantees before reconnecting diagnostics.

The assurance checks finished on European-hosted models helped defenders spot coordinated agent activity faster where deployed, but coverage remained patchy. Services held in guarded districts and slipped elsewhere. By December, containment was visibly a guarded perimeter, not a system — working where guarded, absent where not.
