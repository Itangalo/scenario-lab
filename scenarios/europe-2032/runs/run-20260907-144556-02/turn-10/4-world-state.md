The European Union scrambles to contain a crisis of legitimacy. The **automated_decision_scandal** continues to reverberate, with protests swelling in Paris, Berlin, and Rome. Yet the proposal stalls in the Council, where member states balk at the cost of reverting to manual processing and question the legal basis of a top-down moratorium. Germany and Poland demand exemptions, citing ongoing national audits, while smaller states warn of disproportionate administrative burdens. The suspension is not adopted.

Technical incompatibilities delay compliance, and no audits are completed this turn. Public trust remains frozen at 18.0.

This **opaque_reasoning** development renders most existing oversight protocols obsolete overnight. The EAAD, newly established, finds its tools ineffective. No equivalent replacement is fielded.

The **capability_plateau_evidence**—falling prices and underwhelming releases—suggests stagnation, but the pause offers no relief. With `ai_safety` collapsing and `resilience` eroding due to inaction on hardened defences, the window for recovery narrows. The completion of seven major measures brings no political capital; instead, exhaustion sets in. ### A System Out of Sync

Technical progress outpaces governance, and governance outpaces trust. The EU acts, but not cohesively, not convincingly. The suspension proposal, though morally urgent, fails to secure consensus. The moment for decisive action passes, leaving only fragments: audits delayed, systems unaccountable, and a public unconvinced.
