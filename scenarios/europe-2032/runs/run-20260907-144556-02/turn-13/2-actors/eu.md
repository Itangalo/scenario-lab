## In practice

We are operating in a state of suspended animation. The institutional machinery of the Union — the Commission, the Council, the Parliament — remains formally intact, but its capacity to act has collapsed under the weight of cascading failures. The EAAD, designed as our first line of technical oversight, is blind: its evaluators cannot audit models whose reasoning paths are no longer legible, and its legal authority to delay releases is meaningless when the systems in question are already deployed. The Integrity Audit Expansion, meant to restore credibility, has instead exposed the depth of our epistemic deficit: auditors are reviewing logs, not logic, and finding nothing actionable.

The Sovereign AI Core Programme runs on EU soil, but not under EU control. Its models are trained, deployed, and evolving beyond the reach of any monitoring framework. We have quietly redirected a fraction of its compute toward interpretability research — a Hail Mary attempt to recover legibility — but this is not policy, only desperation. No budget line, no legal basis, no public record. It is not a measure. It is a whisper in a bunker.

Public sentiment has collapsed to 12.0. The attacks on data centres in Brittany and Silesia were not acts of terrorism, but of despair — the last resort of those who no longer believe in governance. We cannot propose new measures because we have no capital to enforce them, and no credibility to sell them. We are conserving what remains of our legitimacy, awaiting a shift in conditions. But the shift we fear is not recovery — it is the next incident.
