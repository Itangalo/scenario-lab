### The Trust Deficit

The European Union scrambles to restore credibility after a cascade of systemic failures. The **automated_decision_scandal**—a ruling that an AI-assisted welfare system unlawfully denied benefits to thousands—ignites public fury and forces a reckoning within the bloc. The scandal, rooted in a sovereign system meant to exemplify control and accountability, exposes a fatal gap: hardening infrastructure does not harden trust. Public sentiment plummets as protests erupt in multiple capitals, with demonstrators targeting data centres and demanding a moratorium on AI in public services.

In response, the EU accelerates the **Sovereign Stack Hardening Initiative**, redirecting funds from the stalled Polish compute cluster to enforce zero-trust architecture across the Sovereign AI Core Programme. However, implementation falters. ENISA audit teams face resistance in Wrocław, where local authorities claim Brussels overreach, and in Marseille, technical incompatibilities delay compliance. The initiative, intended as a shield, becomes a symbol of fragmentation.

Meanwhile, the **Integrity Audit Expansion** is launched as a small category 1 measure, aiming to restore confidence through adversarial review. Yet its scope is narrow, its timeline stretched to turn 11, and its authority contested by member states wary of external scrutiny. The European Commission negotiates hard for buy-in, offering co-governance roles, but the momentum is defensive, not transformative.

The **Openweight Containment Initiative** remains mired in legal challenges. Cloud providers argue that remote deactivation mandates breach digital rights, and the European Court of Justice fast-tracks a preliminary reference, casting doubt on enforcement. With no breakthrough in openweight control and closed capability inching forward, the EU’s leverage erodes.

Multiple large measures—Gigafactories, the Tech Sovereignty Package, EAAD, CIRS, SMAF, and the Public Assurance Protocol—conclude this turn. But their completion brings no political capital. The mood is not one of achievement, but of exhaustion. The bloc’s foundational assumption—that sovereign control ensures safety—has been shattered from within.

### A Sovereignty of Paper

Sovereignty, once measured in compute, now rests on brittle institutional claims. The US maintains its **CONSOLIDATION** posture, rationing frontier access and deepening the EU’s dependency. With no new capacity coming online and political capital collapsing, the Union’s ability to act decisively is slipping away.
