### The Calm After Collapse

The European Union enters 2032 in reactive paralysis. With no new measures proposed and the Emergency AI Governance Suspension formally abandoned, institutional momentum stalls entirely. The portfolio of completed initiatives — from sovereign compute to audit frameworks — stands as a monument to effort without effect. None of the seven finished programmes delivered their intended assurance, and political capital remains frozen at 1.0, too low to even name a priority or initiate triage.

### Blind to the Threat

A major cyber intrusion sweeps through regional health networks and municipal services across three member states. The attack leverages model-generated malware that adapts in real time, evading signature-based defences. Initial response is fragmented, with national CERTs overwhelmed and no coordinated EU-level countermeasures activated. Resilience, already weak, erodes further as recovery timelines stretch into months. The breach is not contained quickly, and public anger mounts.

Simultaneously, physical protests against AI infrastructure escalate. In Brittany, activists occupy a data centre under construction, severing power links; in Silesia, a transformer station serving a cloud facility is sabotaged, causing localized blackouts. No fatalities, but the symbolic rupture is clear: critical infrastructure is now a target, and policing costs exceed repair bills.

Public sentiment plummets to 12.0 as trust collapses. Media coverage frames AI not as a tool, but as an ungoverned force enabled by a distant, ineffective Union. Job losses in administrative sectors accelerate under silent automation, while high-profile system failures dominate headlines.

Behind closed doors, the Commission concedes that its core oversight bodies are blind. The EAAD cannot audit opaque models, and the Integrity Audit Expansion has yielded zero actionable findings. Sovereign systems run on EU soil but are no longer under EU understanding. A quiet repurposing of Sovereign AI Core compute toward interpretability research begins — not a policy, but a last-ditch technical gamble.

Meanwhile, frontier AI capability advances again, reaching 69.5, driven by architectural refinements invisible to external observers. With no safety counterpart, ai_safety drops sharply to 7.0. Openweight capability holds at 53.0 — still trailing, but now sufficient for dangerous misuse. The gap between capability and control widens into a chasm.
