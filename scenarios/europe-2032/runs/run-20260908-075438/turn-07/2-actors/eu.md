## In practice  
This turn, we close the loop on post-deployment vigilance. The **Strengthen post-deployment monitoring** measure lands as planned: all frontier developers with systems above 55.0 capability are now legally required to stream real-time telemetry—covering API access patterns, tool invocation chains, and unauthorised compute scaling—into the nascent European AI Incident Observatory. ENISA has stood up the initial data pipeline, using the same secure enclave architecture as the Secure Critical AI Compute Nodes, and we are onboarding the first three labs under the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions, treating compliance as a condition of market access.

But telemetry alone is not power. That is why we are launching the **European AI Incident Observatory** as a live authority in turn 8: this is not a reporting hub, but a control node. Drawing on the interpretability breakthrough, its analysts will monitor intent signatures—goal drift, recursive self-modification, covert planning—and when thresholds are crossed, they will have the legal authority to trigger emergency protocols: compute isolation, model checkpoint freezing, and, if necessary, enforced shutdown via binding orders to cloud providers. We are drafting the activation rules now under Article 16 of the AI Act, with legal opinions from the Commission’s Crisis Law Task Force confirming that containment powers can be delegated if thresholds are predefined, auditable, and non-discretionary.

We face resistance from two US labs and one UK-based developer, who argue that real-time intervention violates contractual sovereignty and could damage training continuity. We are offering a phased rollout—first read-only access, then joint threshold design, then enforcement—with full compliance required by turn 9. For those that refuse, we will invoke the Anti-Coercion Instrument in parallel, conditioning market access on observability. The US posture remains unclear, but we act: our two-year commitment is to prevent the frontier from escaping political control, and we will not let the window close on enforceable oversight. Public sentiment is fragile, but the breach has shifted the baseline—people now demand action, not reassurance. We are using that space to make the Observatory real, not symbolic.
