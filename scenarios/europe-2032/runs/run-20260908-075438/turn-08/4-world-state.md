### The Observatory Activates, Sovereignty Inches Forward

The European AI Incident Observatory officially goes live, marking the first time an EU body holds real-time authority over frontier AI systems. Emergency protocols are now legally binding, with automated triggers for recursive self-modification and unauthorised goal persistence integrated into telemetry pipelines. A successful simulation with Deutsche Bahn’s logistics AI demonstrates rapid isolation of anomalous planning loops, reinforcing confidence in containment. However, two U.S.-based labs continue to restrict data access, challenging the jurisdictional reach of the regime under international trade law.

Public-sector AI successes in Marseille and Helsinki—cutting hospital wait times and accelerating asylum processing—shift the narrative. These systems, built on EU-governed infrastructure rather than imported models, are widely credited as homegrown wins, boosting public trust and political momentum.

Seizing this window, the EU launches a new small measure: **phased sovereign compute expansion** through binding partnerships with non-U.S. frontier labs. Negotiations begin with a Swiss and a Singaporean developer to colocate training runs in InvestAI Gigafactories under EU jurisdiction, in exchange for market access and compute priority. The move is framed as a pragmatic foothold in the training stack, not a full leap to frontier parity.

Legal challenges to the Public Evaluation Corps intensify, with national courts in Germany and Belgium reviewing claims of democratic overreach. While the program remains operational, delays loom. Meanwhile, informal safety cooperation among non-U.S./non-China labs grows, with anonymized telemetry shared via a Swiss consortium—yet no formal alignment emerges.

### Measured Gains, Persistent Gaps

AI capability advances by +3.0, driven by autonomous agent refinements. With safety improving only modestly—thanks to limited interpretability gains and unresolved systemic risks—the gap persists. Resilience rises as completed infrastructure measures fully land. Sovereignty ticks up on the promise of new partnerships, though physical capacity remains limited. Political capital stabilizes on delivery momentum and public-sector wins, while sentiment climbs on visible benefits.
