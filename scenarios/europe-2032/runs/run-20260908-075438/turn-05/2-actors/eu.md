## In practice
We are consolidating gains from the Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive as it reaches implementation: the legal gridlock in Bavaria has broken with the EIB co-financing package unlocking grid access, and Madrid has withdrawn its constitutional challenge in exchange for leadership of the Iberian AI Resilience Cluster. The ten Secure Critical AI Compute Nodes are now legally designated under NIS2+, with physical hardening contracts signed and incident response protocols submitted to ENISA. Power purchase agreements with Iberdrola and Ørsted secure 78% green baseload by 2029, and we are now conditioning access to the European Public Evaluation Corps’ sandbox environments on hosting within these nodes — making them not just symbols but operational necessities.

Simultaneously, the European Public Evaluation Corps enters its first operational phase: recruitment through EURES is on track to deliver 150 red-team engineers and interpretability researchers by turn 6, and ENISA has finalized access protocols under the newly adopted DMA-based regulation. The first sandbox environments will be hosted at the Paris and Helsinki nodes by turn 6, with initial testing focused on agent persistence and goal-hacking behaviours. We are treating the leaked eval anomaly reports as a wake-up call: though dismissed as measurement artefacts, the unexplained capability jumps open a control gate that could widen fast. Our position remains that we act under uncertainty — hence pushing testing even before the full picture is clear. However, with member state defection in the east undermining cohesion, and the US election alliance offering tempting shortcuts, we are holding firm: sovereignty is not cheaper if outsourced. The Tech Sovereignty Package and InvestAI Gigafactories continue on track, with ECOFIN now tying digital grant allocations to permitting speed, ensuring that national foot-dragging comes at a fiscal cost.
