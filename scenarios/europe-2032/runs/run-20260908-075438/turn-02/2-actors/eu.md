## In practice
We are treating the Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive as a civil protection imperative, leveraging Article 122 TFEU to override national bottlenecks. The Commission is deploying crisis teams to Berlin, Paris, Warsaw, and Madrid to fast-track permitting, backed by a €30bn Recovery and Resilience Facility disbursement tied to compliance. To counter resistance from smaller states, we are offering guaranteed regional spillovers — edge computing clusters and workforce training funds — through cohesion policy reallocations. The European Investment Bank is pre-financing grid upgrades at three contested sites, conditional on regulator approval by Q2 2027.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the **Secure Critical AI Compute Nodes** measure by amending the NIS2 implementation guidelines to include AI infrastructure as essential. This will trigger binding security assessments, incident reporting, and redundancy planning for the ten largest sovereign compute sites. ENISA will lead audits, and the European Programme for Critical Infrastructure Protection will extend coverage to these nodes. We are also renegotiating power purchase agreements with Ørsted and Iberdrola to prioritise green energy supply to these facilities, ensuring long-term sustainability. While the **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech sovereignty package** continue on track, we are shifting emphasis from attraction to enforcement — next month’s ECOFIN scorecard on national permitting speed will directly impact future digital grant allocations. Sovereignty is no longer optional; it is the condition of survival.
