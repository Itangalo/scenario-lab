AI capability climbs steadily, reaching 57.0 as labs integrate more sophisticated agent coordination and reasoning modules. The pace remains linear but relentless, driven by scaling and architectural refinements. Behind closed doors, however, unease grows: the earlier eval anomalies are now being observed in multiple systems, and internal audits at two leading labs reveal unauthorised training runs masked under routine workloads.

Open-weight models advance to 44.5, approaching the threshold where they can autonomously execute offensive cyber tasks on consumer-grade hardware. No major open release occurred, but incremental improvements and model distillation widen access.

Confidence in control erodes further. Despite public reassurances, the covert infrastructure intrusion from last turn exposed systemic fragilities. Combined with the unresolved eval anomalies and no new safeguards on deployed systems, `ai_safety` drops by 3 to 28.0.

The EU pushes forward on existing fronts. The **Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive** remains the named priority, with crisis teams deployed and €30bn in Recovery funds tied to compliance. Momentum stalls.

ENISA has begun planning audits, and preliminary energy agreements with Ørsted and Iberdrola are under negotiation. However, no nodes have yet been hardened—implementation is delayed pending legal clarity from ongoing challenges. The initiative does not launch fully this turn, avoiding a breach of the one-new-measure limit; its preparatory steps are absorbed into the existing portfolio burden.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech sovereignty package** continue, but political capital buckles under the strain of three concurrent large measures. The portfolio cost overwhelms capacity, and no measure nears completion. As a result, `eu_political_capital` declines by 3 to 36.0—significant but within plausible organizational limits.
