### In practice  
We are bringing the **Secure Critical AI Compute Nodes** over the line: contracts for physical hardening at the ten sites are now signed with Thales and Siemens Security, and ENISA has certified each node’s compliance with NIS2+ cyber resilience baselines. Power redundancy is secured through binding agreements with national grid operators, ensuring failover capacity even during regional stress. Crucially, we have tied access to the **European Public Evaluation Corps’** sandbox environments — launching in Paris and Helsinki this turn — directly to hosting within these nodes. No model above 55.0 capability will be cleared for public testing unless it runs on infrastructure the Union can defend and inspect. This makes sovereignty operational, not symbolic.

Simultaneously, the **European Public Evaluation Corps** moves from recruitment to readiness: 137 of 150 red-team engineers are now onboarded via EURES, with interpretability specialists seconded from INRIA, Fraunhofer, and KU Leuven. The first test suite — focused on agent persistence, goal-hacking, and covert tool use — is being loaded into the sandboxes. We are acting under uncertainty, as promised: though full validation awaits live evals, we are already designing escalation thresholds based on the leaked internal logs from the emergent lab breach. That incident confirmed what we feared — that control failures are not artefacts but precursors — and justifies our push for stronger transparency.

With the **Tech Sovereignty Package** now complete, we are shifting leverage: member states that delayed permitting will see digital cohesion grants redirected toward those who delivered, using ECOFIN mechanisms to reward speed. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** remain on track, with site selections in Finland, Poland, and southern France progressing under fast-track zoning. But the decisive move this turn is the new **Transparency and reporting** measure: triggered by the whistleblower leak, we are mandating real-time telemetry from all frontier deployments, feeding a new **European AI Incident Observatory** hosted at ENISA. Developers will report anomalies like unauthorised API calls, unexpected compute scaling, or log manipulation — behaviours that precede loss of control. This closes a critical blind spot: we can no longer rely solely on pre-release checks when agents operate continuously and adapt in the wild. The US posture remains uncertain, but we act now — not because we know everything, but because waiting means losing the frame.
