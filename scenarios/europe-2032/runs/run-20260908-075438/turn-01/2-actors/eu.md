## New measure
**Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive**

A binding directive to fast-track national AI compute projects under EU coordination, overriding permitting delays and securing grid access for priority sites.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`

## Priority
**Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive** — because the cyber_test_shot proves that distributed threats are already operating below detection thresholds, and without sovereign compute we cannot even monitor, let alone counter, what is coming.

## In practice
We are invoking Article 122 TFEU on grounds of serious threat to critical infrastructure to justify an emergency legal base for the **Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive**. This allows us to bypass the usual co-decision process and issue a regulation directly through the Council, provided qualified majority support is secured. Our leverage lies in framing this not as industrial policy but as civil protection: the cyber intrusions affected two member state grids, and we now have evidence they were coordinated and undetectable by existing tools. The ENISA post-incident report, published this month, becomes our exhibit A.

The Commission’s Digital Europe Programme office will be re-tasked to coordinate site approvals across member states, backed by a new €30bn disbursement from the Recovery and Resilience Facility conditioned on compliance. Resistance is expected from smaller states fearing exclusion and from environmental ministries concerned about energy use; we will offer guaranteed regional spillovers — edge computing clusters and workforce training funds — to secure buy-in. The European Investment Bank has agreed to pre-finance grid upgrades at three contested locations if national regulators fast-track decisions by Q1 2027.

At the same time, we are accelerating the **InvestAI Gigafactories** timeline by redirecting unused cohesion funds toward early-stage infrastructure, and we are renegotiating power purchase agreements with Ørsted and Iberdrola to lock in green energy supply. The **Tech sovereignty package** remains on track, but we are shifting emphasis from attracting private capital to enforcing delivery: next month’s ECOFIN meeting will include a scorecard on national permitting speed, tied to future access to EU digital grants. Sovereignty is no longer optional — it is the condition of survival.
