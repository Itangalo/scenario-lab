## In practice
We are treating the Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive as a crisis of strategic access. The Commission has activated Article 122 TFEU to override national bottlenecks, deploying crisis teams to Berlin, Paris, Warsaw, and Madrid with mandates to cut through permitting inertia. €30bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility is now tied directly to compliance timelines, with disbursements suspended in Bavaria pending resolution of the grid strain dispute. To defuse legal challenges from smaller states, we are offering guaranteed edge computing clusters and workforce academies funded through cohesion policy reallocations—tangible spillovers in exchange for solidarity. The European Investment Bank is advancing €8bn in pre-financing for grid upgrades at three contested sites, conditional on regulator approval by Q4 2027.

Simultaneously, we are launching the **Secure Critical AI Compute Nodes** initiative in full: ENISA has issued the first audit templates under the amended NIS2 framework, and the ten designated nodes are now required to report incident response plans and redundancy architectures by January. We are finalising power purchase agreements with Ørsted and Iberdrola to secure green baseload supply, and the European Programme for Critical Infrastructure Protection now includes AI infrastructure in its scope. On the evaluation front, we are standing up the **European Public Evaluation Corps** through a new regulation under the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions—frontier developers must grant access or face market restrictions. Recruitment begins immediately via EURES, targeting red-team specialists and interpretability researchers, with initial testing capacity to be operational by turn 5. While the **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech sovereignty package** continue, we are shifting enforcement leverage: next month’s ECOFIN scorecard will link national digital grant allocations to permitting speed and grid cooperation—sovereignty is no longer aspirational, it is enforced.
