## In practice
This turn, we finalise the legal and operational framework for binding partnerships that tie access to the EU market to physical and procedural sovereignty over frontier training. The Council’s Competitiveness Working Party, under Article 114 TFEU, is drafting the terms: any non-US lab seeking to deploy in the EU must now either train under EU jurisdiction in an InvestAI Gigafactory node, or face restricted market access and delayed conformity assessments. The Swiss and Singaporean labs we engaged last turn are now in technical negotiations with DG CONNECT and ENISA to integrate their telemetry into the European AI Incident Observatory’s pipeline before training begins. This is not voluntary alignment — it is a condition of doing business with 450 million people.

We are leveraging the credibility of our public-sector AI successes and the new urgency of the cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across regional health administrations in three member states, powered by AI-generated exploit chains — to close the gap between oversight and control. The incident, still unattributed but clearly model-assisted, has made clear that post-deployment monitoring is not enough. If we do not govern the training process, we will never govern the outcome. The Public Evaluation Corps, though legally contested, is being redeployed under a new mandate: pre-training risk assessment for any model above 65.0 capability entering the EU ecosystem. We are offering fast-track market entry and partial liability shielding in return for full telemetry, EU-resident staff on-site during training, and secured weight storage. This is the first time the EU is treating compute location not as infrastructure policy, but as a lever of strategic control.
