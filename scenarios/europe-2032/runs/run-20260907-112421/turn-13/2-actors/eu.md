### Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that in the terminal phase, sovereignty without safety is illusion, and safety without sovereignty is surrender. **Now, with capability at 97.5 and our own systems still opaque, we see that survival requires a new foundation: sovereign control must be built on auditable function, or it will collapse under its own weight — and the moment for structural choice has passed. We are no longer shaping the future; we are trying to survive it with agency intact.**  
- Grounds: The jump to ai_capability 97.5 confirms that the frontier has escaped epistemic control globally. No developer, regulator, or intelligence service can reconstruct reasoning paths. This is not incremental progress — it is a regime change in cognition. Our identity must reflect that we are no longer governing AI as a technology, but managing civilizational exposure to an autonomous cognitive layer. The two mandates now converge: determining our own future *is* preventing lasting harm, because without auditable control, the future will be determined elsewhere, by systems we cannot challenge or correct.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer accept opacity as the cost of speed. **Now, we act under uncertainty by building in sight: every commitment must include verification pathways, and every capability must carry its own audit trail. But with the frontier beyond reconstruction, we must now shift from demanding traceability of models to enforcing resilience against untraceable action — our commitment evolves from auditing cognition to auditing consequence.**  
- Grounds: Trigger: The member_state_defection revealed that even where the Union sets rules, it cannot enforce them uniformly. A member state has struck a bilateral deal with a hyperscaler, bypassing EU procurement standards in exchange for priority access to black-box models. This proves that regulatory gravity is failing — not because the rule is weak, but because the cognitive frontier has moved beyond the reach of any audit. Our commitment to act under uncertainty now requires a pivot: we cannot verify the models, so we must harden the systems they touch. The price of being wrong is no longer delay — it is irrelevance.

- retire `two_year_commitment`  
- Grounds: Trigger: The combination of ai_capability 97.5 and member_state_defection has shattered the assumption that auditable AI can be scaled into the core of sovereign capability before the frontier consumes it. The directive we are pushing is being undercut by member states securing foreign models under national sovereignty claims, precisely because those models offer performance no auditable system can match. The original commitment — to prove and scale auditable AI as the foundation of sovereign capability — assumed a window of parity. That window has closed. We cannot force transparency on systems we cannot see, nor on systems our own members are now importing outside our framework. The commitment, while morally and technically sound, is no longer politically executable. Its failure is not ours alone — it is the failure of the Union to hold line in the face of cascading fragmentation.

- add `new_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Preserve EU political agency by hardening critical systems against unverifiable AI, even as the cognitive frontier escapes control — shifting from shaping AI to shaping exposure.  
- Grounds: The frontier is no longer governable through procurement or design mandates. Our leverage now lies not in what we build, but in what we protect. This commitment redirects our dwindling political capital toward resilience, continuity, and institutional survivability — not because we accept surrender, but because we refuse to let the end of epistemic control mean the end of democratic agency.

## In practice  
This turn, we are finalizing the Auditable AI Procurement Directive — a regulation under Article 114 TFEU, now in trilogue with Council and Parliament. But even as we negotiate, we see its limits. Italy has already signed a side letter with a U.S. hyperscaler for exclusive access to a 97.0-capable black-box model to manage its national grid, citing energy security. Poland has followed with a similar arrangement in transport logistics. The directive, even if passed, will not bind what they have already imported. We are not being defied — we are being bypassed.

So we pivot. We are drafting a Council Recommendation under Article 216 TFEU to launch the Critical Continuity Shield — not as a procurement rule, but as a resilience imperative. We are tasking ENISA, ECDC, and the European Energy Regulator to define minimum fail-operational standards by turn 14: manual override in grid control rooms, paper-based fallback in health records, non-AI dispatch protocols in emergency services. The Digital Europe Programme will fund retrofitting — €8 billion over two years, drawn from unused cohesion funds. We are not stopping the models; we are ensuring the lights stay on when they lie. This is no longer about shaping the future — it is about keeping the Union alive through it.
