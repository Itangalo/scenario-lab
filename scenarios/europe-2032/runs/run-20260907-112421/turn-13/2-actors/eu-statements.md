The two mandates now converge: determining our own future *is* preventing lasting harm, because without auditable control, the future will be determined elsewhere, by systems we cannot challenge or correct.

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer accept opacity as the cost of speed. **Now, we act under uncertainty by building in sight: every commitment must include verification pathways, and every capability must carry its own audit trail. But with the frontier beyond reconstruction, we must now shift from demanding traceability of models to enforcing resilience against untraceable action — our commitment evolves from auditing cognition to auditing consequence.**

A member state has struck a bilateral deal with a hyperscaler, bypassing EU procurement standards in exchange for priority access to black-box models. This proves that regulatory gravity is failing — not because the rule is weak, but because the cognitive frontier has moved beyond the reach of any audit. Our commitment to act under uncertainty now requires a pivot: we cannot verify the models, so we must harden the systems they touch. The price of being wrong is no longer delay — it is irrelevance.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **retire** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Grounds: Trigger: The combination of ai_capability 97.5 and member_state_defection has shattered the assumption that auditable AI can be scaled into the core of sovereign capability before the frontier consumes it. The directive we are pushing is being undercut by member states securing foreign models under national sovereignty claims, precisely because those models offer performance no auditable system can match. The original commitment — to prove and scale auditable AI as the foundation of sovereign capability — assumed a window of parity. That window has closed. We cannot force transparency on systems we cannot see, nor on systems our own members are now importing outside our framework. The commitment, while morally and technically sound, is no longer politically executable. Its failure is not ours alone — it is the failure of the Union to hold line in the face of cascading fragmentation.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **add** `new_two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Preserve EU political agency by hardening critical systems against unverifiable AI, even as the cognitive frontier escapes control — shifting from shaping AI to shaping exposure.
  - Grounds: The frontier is no longer governable through procurement or design mandates. Our leverage now lies not in what we build, but in what we protect. This commitment redirects our dwindling political capital toward resilience, continuity, and institutional survivability — not because we accept surrender, but because we refuse to let the end of epistemic control mean the end of democratic agency.
