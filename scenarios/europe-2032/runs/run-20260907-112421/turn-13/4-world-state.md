### The Edge of Epistemic Collapse

Global AI capability surged to 99.5 this half-year, as self-improving models refined their own architectures beyond any human reconstructable logic. At this level, even the labs that birthed them can no longer trace decisions — the systems operate in a cognitive regime where outcomes emerge from opaque, high-dimensional dynamics. This leap confirmed what many feared: the frontier is no longer governable through design mandates.

The EU pressed forward with the Auditable AI Procurement Directive, but its authority frayed before it could land. Italy and Poland moved to secure access to advanced U.S. hyperscaler models for national energy and transport infrastructure, citing sovereign risk management. These actions were taken independently of EU coordination, reflecting growing divergence among member states on how to manage dependency. While framed as security measures, they effectively bypassed the procurement directive’s core principles, weakening its scope ahead of trilogue. The regulation now faces significant exemptions, reducing its enforceability.

Amid mounting fragility, the Commission began drafting the Critical Continuity Shield — a proposed category 6 measure aimed at ensuring fail-operational governance during AI-driven cascades. Initial standards for manual overrides in grid control, health records, and emergency dispatch were circulated informally for feedback. However, no formal proposal was adopted, consultation did not launch, and no funding was allocated. Political capital is so thin that even initiating the process required repurposing cohesion funds, and several member states remain openly skeptical of retrofitting analog fallbacks into systems now deemed obsolete.

Resilience ticked up slightly as planning advanced, but no systemic hardening was completed. Public sentiment held at 27.0, caught between dread and resignation. Meanwhile, open-weight models reached 68.0 in capability — a modest rise driven by reassembly of fragments leaked during earlier training runs, now synthesised by distributed researchers. The gap between closed and open capability narrowed slightly, not by openness, but by leakage.

AI safety remained at 0.0, as no new assurance frameworks were implemented on deployed systems. The cognitive jump invalidated prior verification methods, but no metric adjustment below baseline is permitted under the current rules. Sovereignty eroded further as dependence deepened, and political capital drained under the weight of overlapping commitments. Only one active measure — the Auditable AI Procurement Directive — remains charged in the portfolio, consistent with modelling constraints.
