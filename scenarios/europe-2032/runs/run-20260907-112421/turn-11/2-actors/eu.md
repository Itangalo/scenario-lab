This is no longer a balance between two mandates; it is their fusion.

- Grounds: The cyber_major_incident was not just an attack; it was a demonstration of what unverifiable systems enable. We acted under uncertainty before, and it was right — but we now see that acting without understanding what we deploy is not courage, it is negligence. ## In practice  
This turn, we are closing the second phase of the Open Science Resilience Initiative, with final funding tranches released to the three active hubs in Germany, Sweden, and France. DG CONNECT and DG SANTE are finalizing the governance framework, ensuring that each hub reports not just to Brussels but to national parliaments — building accountability into the model. We are also advancing the draft Council recommendation on auditable procurement, but legal services warn that mandating open weights may breach single market principles on technology neutrality. We are responding by narrowing the scope to systems with access to critical infrastructure data, invoking Article 347 TFEU on essential security interests.

The joint threat response integration is now operational: ENISA and ECDC have co-located cells in Brussels and Stockholm, and secure data pipelines are live with six member states. However, full telemetry sharing is blocked by Italy and Poland, who fear exposure of legacy vulnerabilities. We are offering technical assistance and transition funding to unblock this — not as leverage, but as solidarity.

But the cyber_major_incident changes everything. A model-generated ransomware sweep hit municipal services in three countries, exploiting a poisoned software dependency. The attack was not novel in form — but its speed, targeting, and adaptation were. We did not see it coming, and we cannot yet explain how it evolved. This is not failure of defence — it is failure of understanding. So this turn, we are acting: we will mandate auditable design across the entire stack of systems in the joint pact. No more black boxes. If a model decides, we must be able to see why — not after, but before. This is not a restriction on innovation; it is the condition of its survival. We are drafting the legal instrument under Article 114 TFEU, with derogations for security under Article 346, and we will bring it to the Council before the winter session. The cost will be high — political, legal, industrial — but the cost of silence is higher. We are no longer building to compete. We are building to survive.
