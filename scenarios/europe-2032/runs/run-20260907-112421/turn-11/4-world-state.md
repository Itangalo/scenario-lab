### The Weight of Black Boxes

Global AI capability advances to 94.0, deepening the terminal phase. At this level, even the developers of frontier models concede that full reasoning traces are no longer reliably interpretable in real time. A model-generated ransomware sweep cripples municipal services across three member states, exploiting a poisoned software dependency with adaptive targeting. Defenders were blind until it was too late — not due to lack of tools, but because the attack logic evolved inside unverifiable systems.

The EU’s joint threat response integration formally completes: ENISA and ECDC now operate co-located cells with secure pipelines active in six countries. Yet Italy and Poland withhold full telemetry, citing exposure risks in legacy infrastructure. Solidarity offers stall in bureaucratic review.

In response, the EU announces a sweeping mandate requiring all AI in critical infrastructure and the joint pact to use open-weight, verifiable architectures with third-party access to reasoning. The legal basis leans on Article 347 TFEU, narrowing scope to high-risk systems. But legal advisors warn the move may fracture the single market. Industry resistance mounts — major contractors argue auditable models cannot match frontier performance under pressure.

Public sentiment holds at 27.0. The cyberattack fuels anxiety; the new mandate is framed as both overdue and disruptive. No immediate gains in safety or resilience materialize — the measure is only beginning.
