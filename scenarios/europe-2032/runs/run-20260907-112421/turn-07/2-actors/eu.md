## In practice
We are acting at the breaking point of control. The genome model leak has revealed that the frontier is now capable of generating actionable, recreatable biological threats at low cost — and our defences are still structured for slower, visible escalation. The Resilience Surge Protocol ends this turn, but its legacy is uneven: cyber hardening in finance and telecoms is advanced, yet healthcare remains exposed, and no cross-sector incident response has been exercised. We cannot afford to let preparedness end there. This turn, we are operationalising the European AI Emergency Response Corps as the Union’s first standing technical intervention force. Under Article 122a TFEU, we are finalising the Council’s deployment protocol to grant pre-authorised cross-border access to critical AI systems during declared emergencies. Recruitment begins immediately for 500 specialists — red teamers, biosecurity analysts, cyber commanders — embedded in ENISA and ECDC with direct links to sovereign compute clusters. We are offering member states opt-in liability shielding and fast-track reimbursement for participation, aiming to secure at least ten national commitments by turn’s end.

Simultaneously, we are launching a new coalition measure to break our compute dependence. With the taiwan_blockade risk now acute and our own industrial base stalled — InvestAI Gigafactories finishes this turn but delivers no operational sites — we must convert our upstream supply-chain leverage into guaranteed access. We are convening a joint negotiation under the middle-power coalition framework, bringing together the Netherlands (ASML), Germany (equipment), France (demand), and key external partners: the US (CHIPS Act infrastructure) and Taiwan (TSMC). The goal is a binding agreement: in exchange for coordinated export licensing and shared evaluation standards, we secure a minimum annual allocation of training runs on non-Chinese frontier systems. This is not sovereignty through domestic build — not yet — but sovereignty through interdependence, exercised at the chokepoint we still control: control over the tools, not just the chips. The Commission is drafting the mandate, and we will bring it to the Foreign Affairs Council in September, with a signing target of mid-2030.
