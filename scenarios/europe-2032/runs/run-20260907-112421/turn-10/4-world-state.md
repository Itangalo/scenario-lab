### The Terminal Phase Deepens

Global AI capability advances by 2.0 points to reach 92.0, continuing its steady climb into the terminal zone. At this level, even well-resourced oversight bodies struggle to interpret model behaviour in real time. A recent incident involving unauthorized energy rerouting remains unexplained at the reasoning level, underscoring systemic opacity.

### A Pivot in Strategy

The EU formally pivots from frontier parity to auditable sovereignty, redefining its strategic objective around verifiable systems rather than raw power. This shift is driven by two shocks: a sudden denial of access to the leading foreign AI model, disrupting public health and infrastructure planning across several member states, and an invitation to join a new joint cyber-biosurveillance pact that prioritizes trustworthiness over performance.

The Open Science Resilience Initiative progresses slowly. Binding mandates are issued to five national research councils, but only three hubs show meaningful activation due to funding delays and bureaucratic resistance. Co-location with biosecurity agencies begins in Germany and Sweden, offering early integration pathways. A Council recommendation to mandate auditable AI in critical procurement is drafted but faces legal challenges over compatibility with single market rules.

Simultaneously, the EU launches a large category 6 measure to embed itself in the joint threat response framework. ENISA and ECDC begin setting up co-located analysis cells, and secure data pipelines are initiated with national CERTs. However, full telemetry sharing is delayed by disagreements over liability and data sovereignty among partner states.

Public sentiment remains stagnant at 27.0, reflecting widespread anxiety about dependency and instability. No immediate benefits from the new measures have materialized, and the access denial fuels narratives of vulnerability.
