The Sovereign AI Core becomes operational and transitions into an active enforcement role, with the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) launching intervention-capable monitoring teams in Brittany and northern Sweden. Empowered under Article 114 TFEU, these teams can freeze, fork, and audit high-risk AI models, particularly foreign systems derived from weaponised open-weight models. The Middle Power AI Coalition (MPAC) formally establishes itself, with Japan and South Korea aligning on export controls, while Norway and Finland contribute red-team resources; however, Canada remains outside pending review, and a parallel Supply Chain Pact emerges, fragmenting coordination. EAAD suspends two high-risk models, marking early safety successes amid rising anomalies in frontier AIs. Despite improved resilience, public sentiment stagnates at 14.0 due to protests in Lyon and Vienna, and concerns over centralisation grow, with Germany and Austria warning against technocratic overreach.
