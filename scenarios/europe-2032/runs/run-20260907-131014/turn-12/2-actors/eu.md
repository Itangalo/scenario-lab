## In practice  
The cyber_major_incident this quarter was not an outlier — it was a signal. It was executed with model-generated tooling, and its target was not just disruption but erosion: of trust, of continuity, of our ability to respond coherently. We now know what we face — not a single adversary, but an ecosystem of capability in motion, and the open-weight fork is the first sign that the tide might be turned. But only if we act.  

We are repurposing €400 million from the stalled AI Safety Assurance budget — funds originally meant for internal monitoring — to build a distributed red-team access network anchored in the existing MPAC framework. This will not wait for consensus. We are invoking Article 114 TFEU again, this time to establish a temporary evaluation corridor: secure, sovereign, and interoperable with the hardened fork now gaining traction in Helsinki and Lyon. The French and Finnish collectives that released the model will be invited to join as technical stewards, but access will be gated by a joint board including ENISA, BfDI, and Norway’s NISMA. Germany remains resistant, fearing fragmentation, but we are offering them leadership of the technical node in Bavaria — not control, but co-responsibility. Austria will host the legal registry for evaluation requests, ensuring compliance with GDPR and the AI Act. This is not a replacement for sovereign compute — that remains our foundation — but it is how we make that compute *mattering*: by ensuring it can see, test, and break what is loose in the world. We are no longer chasing the frontier. We are learning how to meet it.
