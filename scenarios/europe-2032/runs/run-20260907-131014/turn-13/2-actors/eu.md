## In practice  
The ransomware cascade this summer did not just exploit flaws — it weaponised our slowness. It moved faster than human response, bypassed legacy segmentation, and targeted not just data but continuity: the quiet assumption that someone, somewhere, will still be able to read a scan, dispatch a repair, authorise a payment. We were not ready. The red-team pipeline we launched last turn is necessary, but it is not sufficient. We are still learning to see the threat. We must now act as if we already know it.

This turn, we are mobilising €1.2 billion from the Civil Protection Mechanism and redirecting €300 million from the stalled Safety Assurance budget into an emergency resilience surge. ENISA will lead, with binding authority to mandate patching, segmentation, and failover protocols in all designated critical operators. We are invoking Article 122 TFEU for the first time in an AI context, treating cyber collapse as equivalent to supply disruption. The first nodes — Brittany, Upper Austria, and Utrecht — will receive hardened microgrids, AI-resistant triage systems, and live red-teaming from the MPAC pipeline already coming online. Germany resists central mandates, but we are offering them leadership of the technical integration cell in Munich — not veto power, but responsibility. France has agreed to host a parallel biological detection pilot, piggybacking on the same hardened infrastructure. This is not sovereignty through compute alone, but sovereignty through continuity: the right to keep functioning when everything else fails. We are no longer building for the future. We are defending the present.
