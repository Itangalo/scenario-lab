### A Surge Against the Tide

The European Union launched the **Stand Up Emergency Resilience Surge Capacity for Critical Health and Energy Infrastructure**, a large Category 6 measure, mobilising €1.5 billion in redirected and emergency funds. ENISA assumed binding authority over critical operators in high-risk regions, mandating rapid cyber hardening, failover systems, and live red-teaming. Early deployments began in Brittany, Upper Austria, and Utrecht, with Munich’s integration cell overcoming initial German resistance through shared technical leadership.

Despite this urgency, progress was uneven. Patching legacy systems revealed deeper vulnerabilities than expected, and coordination across 14 national regulators slowed rollout. The first microgrid installations faced local opposition tied to broader anti-AI sentiment, now worsened by physical sabotage at two data centre sites under the **backlash_physical** wave. In Vienna and Marseilles, protests disrupted planning sessions, delaying node activation by months.

Meanwhile, `ai_capability` rose by +1.5 to 72.0, driven by autonomous operations advances in closed labs. No safety countermeasures landed on frontier systems, leaving `ai_safety` stagnant at 24.0. The hardened red-team fork from the MPAC pipeline contributed incrementally, pushing `openweight_capability` to 61.0 — diffusion continues, but no leap occurred.

Resilience improved slightly (+2) as the new surge created early deterrence effects, satisfying part of the category 6 bonus. However, full benefits await later turns. The Sovereign Compute Corridor remains distant, and rising capability deepened dependence, costing −1 to sovereignty.

Political capital declined: the portfolio charge for three active measures (−3−3−3) and a new priority (−1) totalled −10. A +5 proposal bonus for addressing the recent cyber crisis partially offset it, but final capital fell to 15.0. Public sentiment dropped to 20.0 as backlash incidents dominated media coverage, framing AI as both threat and target.

### Fragile Foundations

The EU acts with growing desperation. It builds not to lead, but to survive — and even that is not guaranteed.
