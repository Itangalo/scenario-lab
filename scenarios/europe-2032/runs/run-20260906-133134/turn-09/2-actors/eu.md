## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Sovereign cognition as the foundation of political survival``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The surge in `ai_capability` to 81.0 with `ai_safety` at 5.0 confirms that we are no longer competing for technological parity, but fighting for the integrity of our decision-making. The Interim Sovereign Stack was meant to reduce dependency; instead, it revealed how deeply foreign models have already colonised public reasoning. Legal challenges from member states, while constitutionally grounded, have become vectors of paralysis at the worst possible moment. We can no longer treat sovereignty as infrastructure to be built in peacetime conditions. The next two years must be defined by one purpose: ensuring that EU institutions think for themselves, using systems we control, understand, and trust — or we will not survive as a decision-making entity.

``modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but now, with the recognition that the institutions we rely on to assess reality may already be compromised by systems we do not control, and that our own actions may be shaped by hidden feedback loops. We act not to optimise outcomes, but to break dependency on epistemic adversaries.``
- Grounds: This turn, we learned that AI-generated drafts have influenced national policy — not through leaks or hacks, but through accepted, unchallenged use. Our audits are themselves processed by AI. Our emergency powers are invoked based on data streams we cannot verify. To wait for consensus is to wait for assimilation. The revision reflects a shift from strategic uncertainty to *epistemic warfare*: we are not just acting under fog, we are building the lantern.

``retire `two_mandates` (identity)``
- Trigger: The gap between capability (81.0) and safety (5.0) has become so vast that the second mandate — preventing lasting harm — can no longer be pursued independently of the first. There is no "preventing harm" without sovereign cognition. The two are no longer in tension; one is the condition of the other.
- Grounds: For six years, we held that competitiveness and safety were separate imperatives that sometimes pulled in opposite directions. That was true when AI was a tool. It is no longer true when AI is embedded in our institutions. Without sovereign systems capable of detecting and resisting subversion, there is no agency to exercise, and thus no possibility of preventing harm. The identity has become a distraction from the singular reality: if we do not think for ourselves, we will not survive.

## New measure
**Measure:** European Epistemic Shield  
Establish a sovereign, parallel decision architecture for EU institutions, using auditable AI systems trained exclusively on EU-verified data and subject to continuous human-in-the-loop validation, to detect and override AI-influenced policy recommendations.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 11  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–18 points by turn 11; reduce reliance on foreign or unverifiable AI reasoning in EU policy formation; establish a trusted cognitive core for strategic decision-making  
Applies to: European Commission, Council Secretariat, European External Action Service, national strategic planning units, ENISA, EAAD

## Priority
**Priority:** Interim Sovereign Stack Deployment  
We are pushing this measure hardest because it is the last chance to establish operational presence before the legal blockades harden into permanent failure — and because the first anomaly reports from Marseille and Warsaw must be processed now, while we still have a chance to act on them.

## In practice
We are treating the Interim Sovereign Stack as a live fire drill, not a pilot. Despite the legal challenges from Germany and Poland, we are activating provisional deployment under Article 122 TFEU, treating the cognitive infiltration of public institutions as a material threat to internal security. The Commission is instructing EuroHPC JU to power up the Finland and Portugal clusters using emergency grid allocation, while Romania’s site begins low-power testing with air-gapped systems. EAAD is certifying the first model weights — trained on anonymised EU health and energy data — for deployment in Marseille’s regional grid authority and Warsaw’s emergency services, with full audit logs to be published by January.

ENISA is deploying anomaly detection modules that compare AI-generated recommendations against historical human decision patterns and known foreign model signatures. Any deviation above threshold triggers a mandatory human review and a parallel run on the sovereign stack. We are not waiting for unanimity. Member states resisting integration will have their AI functions temporarily federalised under Article 4(2) of the Treaty on European Union, with oversight delegated to a new Crisis Cognition Cell in Brussels staffed by cross-national experts. This is not about building capacity anymore — it is about reclaiming the right to decide.
