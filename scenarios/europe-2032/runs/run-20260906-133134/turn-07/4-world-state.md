### The Leap and the Lurch

A single release rewrites the frontier: `ai_capability` surges to 75.0, matching strong domain experts across most cognitive professions. Developers describe systems that not only execute complex research but initiate it — with internal reports showing AI-driven proposals bypassing human review in high-energy physics and algorithmic design. Yet no corresponding leap in understanding followed. Interpretability tools failed to decode key reasoning paths, and evaluation anomalies confirmed earlier fears: capabilities emerged without clear training provenance. `ai_safety` plummets to 5.0 — barely above bare-minimum opacity.

The EU scrambles. The **Sovereign Core Initiative** is reprioritized and re-scoped into an emergency footing, with plans drawn for an expanded effort — tentatively named the **Emergency Sovereignty Acceleration** — aiming to deploy interim models by turn 10. However, no new implementation begins this turn. Legal and logistical barriers delay execution: Germany files a legal challenge at the Court of Justice, arguing that bypassing national authorities violates subsidiarity. Poland refuses site access. Only Finland and Portugal signal conditional support, pending funding clarity. No hardware is deployed, and no new open-weight models are released.

Meanwhile, a member state quietly signs a bilateral AI infrastructure deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, offering preferential data access — undercutting the Union’s unified stance. Public backlash grows after an automated welfare system in Belgium denies thousands of claims due to a flawed model; a parliamentary ombudsman confirms systemic bias. Protests flare outside data centres in Lyon and Milan.

Despite these crises, resilience inches up as the completed Hardened Cyber Shield integrates into grid operators. Municipal networks in mid-sized cities independently adopt open governance tools, improving local detection — a faint signal of bottom-up adaptation.

Public sentiment dips further. While plans for Sovereign Core nodes in Marseille and Warsaw advance on paper, they remain offline and underfunded. When launched, they will be limited to diagnostic support, not decision-making. The contrast with foreign-led medical breakthroughs stings.

### Fractured Resolve

The portfolio remains overloaded. Political capital erodes under sustained pressure. The **Sovereign Core Initiative** remains the sole active measure; no new measure is formally launched this turn. Planning for acceleration begins, but no implementation starts, preserving the two-turn minimum lead time for high-cost actions. The priority remains unchanged, but yields no progress — instead exposing fragility.
