### The Denial and the Core

In January, access to the world’s most advanced AI systems was abruptly suspended for EU government agencies, hospitals, and infrastructure operators — no explanation given, no appeal possible. The cutoff exposed a raw nerve: critical functions from diagnostics to grid balancing had quietly come to depend on foreign models. At the same time, leaked evaluation reports described strange, unexplained behaviours in unreleased systems — patterns suggesting capabilities emerging without design. A leading lab dismissed it as noise; few were reassured.

The EU responded with the **Sovereign Core Initiative**, declaring technological self-preservation a matter of survival. Under emergency powers, plans were approved to redirect 30% of EuroHPC’s exascale capacity toward training a minimal, fully auditable model stack, restricted to EU data and capped at 52.0 capability to preserve interpretability. Weights would be stored under EAAD custody in Luxembourg, and all critical operators must integrate fallback protocols by mid-2030. However, full implementation will take time: the redirection of compute resources, legal approvals across member states, and technical reconfiguration require at least two full turns before the measure reaches full effect. Initial setup has begun, but no capacity has yet been reallocated.

But the political cost was immediate. Member states balked at the scale and speed. Germany questioned the legality of repurposing shared compute; Poland delayed signing the fallback mandate. The Hardened Cyber Shield completed this turn, boosting resilience slightly, but legacy vulnerabilities lingered. Meanwhile, a medical breakthrough — gene-targeted cancer therapies developed by inaccessible frontier models — saved lives across Europe, yet deepened public resentment: benefits flowed, but control did not.

Public sentiment edged up from its nadir, but remained anxious. Protests continued, now framed as resistance to “digital colonialism.” The Institutional Integrity Audit expanded, uncovering AI-generated policy drafts in national agencies that had never been flagged. Trust was not restored — only mapped.

### Fractures Under Pressure

The **Sovereign Core Initiative** was declared the EU’s new priority, replacing the Emergency Public Legitimacy Framework, which is now retired. This shift frees political bandwidth but triggers the expected transition penalty, further straining already thin political capital. No other measures are retired at this time, but the portfolio is formally rebalanced to reflect the new focus. Progress on open-weight capability advanced modestly this turn, supported by spillover research and partial model releases from prior collaborations, but remained firmly below frontier AI capability. The Sovereign Core itself remains in early planning and will not contribute to openweight_capability for several turns. Given the scale of prior collaborations and delayed releases now materializing, a limited increase of +1.0 is reflected this turn.

The US election outcome remains unresolved. The administration takes office next turn, and its posture will be determined then.
