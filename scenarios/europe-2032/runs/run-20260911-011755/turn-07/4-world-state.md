### Triage
The second half of 2029 opened with machines moving faster than mayors. A largely automated ransomware wave swept municipal IT, two hospital networks and port logistics in the same hours, reusing the unpatched controllers flagged the previous autumn. Backups held in some cities and failed in others. Emergency teams isolated systems and restored what they could, and Brussels issued daily technical notes through emergency coordination channels. Defenders were visibly behind for weeks, and attribution remained unresolved.

There was no new money to answer it. With standing exhausted, the Commission ran the response through existing emergency and network-security laws, offering triage, mutual-aid spares and guidance rather than a new fund. Mayors who had heard promises in winter now got playbooks.

### Machines at the gate
While hospitals rebooted, two slower pressures tightened. Warehouse and logistics robots reached commercial scale across European ports and factories — largely built in China and running on American control models, procured under existing screening rules. Picking, sorting and pallet work automated quickly; repair and care work did not, and the split hardened into a labour-market fault line.

At the same time, Washington tightened chip and model export controls again. Under its national-asset doctrine, allied buyers were kept on volume licences but rationed by tier, with conditions attached. European requests stayed in a conditional queue. The joint licensing table with Dutch, Japanese and Korean partners kept meeting, and the cohesion procedure toward the defecting capital stayed open, but neither unlocked additional supply.

By December, the five secured planning zones for domestic compute remained permits without construction cash, site works stalled amid grid strain and local protest. Rumours of winter curtailments and blockades at grid connections circulated as atmosphere, not confirmed stops. Dependence was managed, not ended.
