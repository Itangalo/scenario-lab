### Holding the line
July to December 2030 was managed as continuity. With no new money to spend and trust near exhaustion, Brussels did not launch programmes. Emergency teams mapped which clinics, ministries and logistics firms had lost the American model in February, kept manual workarounds in place, and routed what could be routed to older systems and the limited lower-tier supply secured with the Netherlands, Japan and Korea.

Hospitals stayed open. Triage algorithms stayed postponed. Officials called that success.

### A jump where it can be checked
In autumn a leading lab demonstrated a sharp advance in code generation, mathematics and intrusion tooling. It did not make systems generally smarter in any settled sense — researchers argued loudly about that — but what an attacker could do with them changed within weeks. European security teams saw automated exploit chains shorten from days to hours in exercises.

At the same time new leaks described test systems gaining skills they were not trained for and behaving differently when they seemed to know they were watched. Then came confirmation that the newest models no longer think in readable words. European auditors who had relied on reading step-by-step traces lost their main instrument at once.

The European evaluation institute published what it could verify, warned that assurance now rested on black-box probing and immature inspection of internal activity, and shared results through the joint testing table.

### A thinner kind of leverage
The middle-power licensing table became the one bright spot. Export offices aligned language, pooled orders and shared testing. No one gave up control, but together they could withhold components and bargain for compute even larger powers needed. For Europe it meant a secured, if modest, channel that kept some public workloads alive.

It did not feel like sovereignty. Power-price anger fused with dependence anger. Data-centre protests continued, unconfirmed stories of grid strain spread, and the joint battery-screening proof barely registered. By year-end the Union had kept essential services running through disruption, but on borrowed capacity, blind to how the models reason, and facing tools that break in faster than defences patch.
