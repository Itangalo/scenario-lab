### The sweep
The automated ransomware wave that had been feared since winter arrived in late summer, moving through the same shared booking and payment software that had been hit before. This time the payload rewrote itself per network, slipping past signature filters and opening helpdesk tickets in its own defence. In half a dozen member states, municipal counters, local clinics' appointment desks and regional payment offices went dark.

Where isolation gates and human sign-off had been fitted, operators cut the connection within hours and rebuilt from clean images. Where they had not, systems stayed down for weeks. No hospital lost power, but paper triage returned to town halls that had just recovered, and evening news ran queues alongside maps of the outage.

Attribution lagged for months. Forensics pointed to tooling assembled largely by models, but whose hands directed it remained contested.

### An invitation answered
In the middle of the sweep came an offer from states hit by the same class of intrusion: a pooled command for attribution, real-time telemetry sharing and joint playbooks, with a seat for the Union if it wired in its own defences.

With no budget for a solo rebuild and political room at its thinnest, Brussels took it. Justice and home affairs ministers mandated the EU agency for cybersecurity as the single node, feeding gate telemetry from councils receiving recovery money into the joint feed. French and Dutch seconded teams became the European contribution, moving from council to council with clean images and a condition: take the money, share the feed.

Accession talks consumed almost all legal and staffing bandwidth. No new compute or outside supply instrument was opened; the two hospital pilot systems on European supercomputers continued on existing funds, and the middle-power supply pact formally closed without binding capacity beyond guarantees for Europe-anchored hosting.

### Two ledgers
By December the joint feed was live but thin. Connected districts recovered in days and shared signatures outward; uncovered municipalities queued to join, resenting the conditionality even as they signed. Insurers still refused new municipal cover.

Offices told the other story: law firms, accountants and administrations again reported assistants lifting output without layoffs. The Commission showed the figures quietly to mayors. It did not move public mood, which stayed hostile and exhausted.
