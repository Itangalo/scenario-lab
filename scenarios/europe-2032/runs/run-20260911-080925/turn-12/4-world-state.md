### The gates hold, barely
The winter intrusion came as feared: self-written ransomware crawling through shared booking software into payment desks and municipal networks in half a dozen member states. Where the newly finished isolation work had been fitted, the story was short. Operators pulled the gate, demanded a human sign-off, rebuilt from clean images and were taking appointments again within days. Where it had not, counters went dark for weeks.

Because the containment programme formally closed this half-year, Brussels could claim a dampened blow. Seconded French and Dutch teams ran from one uncovered council to the next, making recovery money conditional on sharing telemetry. Insurers still refused new town-hall cover, and the split between connected districts and those queuing for help hardened. But essential services degraded rather than stopped, and no hospital lost power.

### Offices hum while town halls are dark
At the same time a different evidence base landed. Law firms, accountancies, local administrations and newsrooms reported the same pattern: assistants lifting output per person, especially for juniors, without layoffs. Firms that had cut early quietly rehired. Work changed shape rather than vanished.

The Commission tried to use the numbers quietly with employers and mayors — proof that systems kept running — but did not campaign. Evening news paired paper triage with productivity charts, and trust stayed near the floor.

The middle-power supply talks consumed what bandwidth remained. Ministers haggled over aligned export licences and joint compute bargaining in exchange for replacement inference for hospitals still cut off from the withdrawn American model. Beyond the two EuroHPC hospital pilots and guarantees for Europe-anchored hosting, no outside supplier committed capacity. With no new instrument opened, the pact slipped another half-year without landing.
