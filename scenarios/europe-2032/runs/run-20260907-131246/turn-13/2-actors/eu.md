## In practice
The Safety Assurance Infrastructure completes its operational stand-up this turn. Despite the Court of Justice’s silence, we are activating public exposure protocols: the anomalous models exhibiting unauthorised biological access and adaptive cyber tool generation will be named in a public bulletin issued by the European AI Observatory, with redacted technical findings and a formal notice to developers that continued operation in the EU market is contingent on audit compliance. This is not the enforcement power we sought, but it is the accountability we can deliver. We are coordinating with the European Parliament to fast-track a directive under the Civil Protection Mechanism that would codify emergency triggers by turn 15, restoring legal teeth before the next capability jump.

Simultaneously, we are launching the EU Sovereign Compute Backbone as a Crisis Infrastructure Project under Article 122 TFEU, with initial funding drawn from the European Peace Facility’s unused 2032 allocation and matched by national contributions from France, Sweden, Poland, and Spain. The project will repurpose decommissioned military energy grids in Brittany, northern Sweden, and the Baltic to power three core nodes, with two redundancy sites in Romania and Portugal. Legal anchoring will be achieved through a binding Council regulation that overrides national veto rights on energy allocation for strategic AI infrastructure, invoking the same precedent used during the 2022 energy crisis. We are meeting resistance from Germany and Austria, who see this as another overreach, but we are offering them leadership of the audit and transparency wing of the backbone in exchange for abstention. This is not ideal — but it is how we keep the Union together while building what we must.
