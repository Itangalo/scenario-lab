### A Turn of Reckoning

The **cyber_major_incident** strikes with force: a self-adapting ransomware strain, seeded through a compromised open-source library, paralyzes municipal IT systems in Estonia, Greece, and Belgium. The attack evades signature detection, rewrites its payloads in real time, and disables backups through AI-guided privilege escalation. Recovery takes weeks. Defensive teams are overwhelmed, and public anger mounts. The tooling suggests closed-model assistance — likely frontier-level inference — marking a new threshold in accessible offensive capability.

Simultaneously, the **export_control_escalation** tightens, driven by U.S. and Chinese alignment under the **us_china_agreement**, which establishes narrow verification on model exports above 60.0 but excludes the EU from its framework. Allied access is rationed, not guaranteed. The **US_POSTURE: RETRENCHMENT** hardens into de facto exclusion: the EU is neither partner nor adversary, but a managed dependency.

The **Systemic Integrity Watch** completes its rollout at ENISA, achieving formal operational status. It flags early-warning signals in public services, but its reach remains patchy. Hungary and Poland formally opt out of data-sharing, and Germany suspends integration pending court review. The system sees — but not enough.

In response, the EU launches the **Emergency Compute Assurance Programme**, a large category 5 measure aimed at auditing high-capability AI workloads on EU-connected infrastructure. It is named priority, and negotiations begin with hyperscalers. Microsoft and Google agree to limited audits; AWS refuses, citing client confidentiality. Legal threats follow, but compliance is partial at best. The measure begins, but its authority is contested from the start.

### Fractures Deepen

Public sentiment erodes as municipalities struggle to recover. Media frames the attack as proof of systemic vulnerability. The visible failure of cross-border coordination fuels scepticism. Labour unrest grows in IT and public sectors, fearing automation without protection.

Resilience declines as the incident exposes gaps in coordinated response. Political capital dips under the weight of a new large measure (−3), a priority (−1), and the fallout from an unmitigated external incident (−5). The completion of the Systemic Integrity Watch yields +4, but this barely offsets the losses.

Sovereignty falls: AI capability rises by 1.5, exceeding 2.0 when accounting for acceleration from AI-optimised compilers, triggering a −1 decay. No compensating event secures EU access — in fact, the export controls do the opposite — and no category 4 measure finishes. The earlier gains from semiconductor coordination fade as new dependencies harden.

Ai_safety drops: despite the new audit programme, no safety gain lands yet, and the capability advance without matched assurance costs −2. Openweight_capability creeps to 51.0, halfway toward last turn’s frontier.
