**Emergency Compute Assurance Programme** — because the cyber_major_incident this turn confirms that offensive AI tooling is already in circulation, and with openweight_capability at 50.0 and closed systems at 62.5, the risk that EU infrastructure is being used to train or run dangerous systems without oversight has become intolerable — we must know what is being run on the compute we depend on, even when it is not ours.

## In practice  
This turn, we close the **Systemic Integrity Watch** on schedule. The new unit is now operational at ENISA, with live telemetry feeds from 18 national grid operators, five major hospital networks, and digital civic platforms in seven member states. It has already flagged two anomalous automation feedback loops in public benefits processing — one in Belgium, one in Italy — both now under technical review. But the system’s authority remains contested: Poland and Hungary have refused to connect their energy regulators, and Germany has delayed full integration pending a constitutional review of intervention thresholds. The nervous system is live, but not yet whole.

The closure of that measure frees political capital, but the **cyber_major_incident** — a model-generated ransomware sweep that crippled municipal services in three member states — forces a pivot. The attack exploited AI-optimised obfuscation and adaptive lateral movement, suggesting tooling at or near 55.0 capability. It was not run from open weights, but the method could be replicated by any actor with access to high-end inference. With the **export_control_escalation** now cutting off most non-allied access to advanced chips, and the **us_china_agreement** excluding us from its limited verification framework, we cannot rely on external restraint. The frontier is accelerating, and our leverage is local: the compute, the energy, the jurisdiction.

So we launch the **Emergency Compute Assurance Programme**. Using powers under the Cyber Resilience Act and the Digital Markets Act, we require all hyperscalers operating in the EU to submit to real-time auditing of high-capability AI training and inference workloads. We fund it with €1.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, and staff it through a new taskforce at ENISA with embedded experts from national labs and EuroHPC. The goal is not to control foreign models, but to ensure that no system above 50.0 capability trains or runs at scale on EU infrastructure without detectable safeguards. We are negotiating access now with Microsoft and Google; AWS has pushed back, citing client confidentiality. We will not yield: if they cannot assure us, they cannot assume our trust. This is not protectionism — it is self-preservation.
