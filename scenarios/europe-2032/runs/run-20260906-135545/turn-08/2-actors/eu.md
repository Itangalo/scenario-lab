**Joint Semiconductor Assurance Framework**  
Establish a binding coordination mechanism with Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan to jointly monitor, license, and if necessary restrict exports of AI-relevant semiconductor equipment and advanced chips, with shared early-warning protocols and mutual recognition of strategic bottlenecks.

## In practice  
This turn, we are advancing the **Emergency Compute Assurance Programme** under intense pressure. ENISA’s audit taskforce is now operational, with 120 staff drawn from national cybersecurity agencies and EuroHPC, and we have secured provisional data access from Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud for inference workloads above 50.0 capability. But AWS continues to resist, citing contractual obligations and U.S. CLOUD Act supremacy. We are responding through the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions, preparing a formal infringement procedure that could trigger fines of up to 6% of global turnover — a move that risks escalation but signals we will not accept opaque compute as a fait accompli. Legal teams are finalising the case, and we are lobbying the Competitiveness Council to back a unified position by June. The cyber_major_incident has hardened public and political will, but the gap between legal authority and technical reach remains wide: we can demand logs, but we cannot yet interpret model behaviour at scale.

Simultaneously, we are moving fast on the **Joint Semiconductor Assurance Framework**, capitalising on the emergent_semiconductor_coalition. The Taiwan Strait blockade has created urgency: Japan and South Korea fear supply disruption, and Taiwan seeks political cover for deeper coordination. We are convening a ministerial-level forum under the Foreign Affairs Council, using the EU-Japan Digital Partnership and existing trade frameworks as legal scaffolding. The goal is a tripartite+1 agreement on shared export licensing thresholds for EUV-related tools, advanced packaging equipment, and AI-optimised chips above 5nm. We are offering access to EU demand forecasting, joint R&D via Horizon Europe, and mutual stockpile guarantees in return for veto rights on dual-use exports to high-risk jurisdictions. The Netherlands and Germany are supportive; France is cautious, fearing U.S. retaliation. But with sovereignty at 24.0 and no path to independent compute at scale, this is our best chance to turn supply-chain interdependence into strategic autonomy. We are allocating €300 million from the European Defence Fund and the Chips Act to fund monitoring infrastructure and secure communications between national control agencies. If we lock this in by turn 11, we will have a real lever — not just a plea.
