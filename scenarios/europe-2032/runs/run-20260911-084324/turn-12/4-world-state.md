### The sweep
It started as slow logins in February and became a continent-wide lockout by March. A largely automated ransomware sweep, assembled with model-generated tooling, moved through municipal portals, clinic systems and a shared records dependency. Screens went to ransom notes in three languages. Two hospital groups reverted to paper triage; several cities paused non-urgent care. Defenders said publicly they were behind. Attribution will take months.

Brussels did not pass a new law. It plugged in. ENISA and CERT-EU began feeding real-time telemetry into a joint cyber command stood up with partners hit by the same class of intrusion, and HERA and the disease centre signed binding sample-sharing into a standing biosurveillance pact. The telemetry helped stop a second wave in April. The clinics still closed wards in the first wave.

### Weights that will not come back
While servers were being rebuilt, a new openly released model family landed within months of the closed frontier. Hundreds of thousands of downloads in the first week. Copycats tuned for intrusion support were circulating within days on private hardware beyond any recall. European police bulletins noted the tooling matched fragments seen in the spring sweep.

### Checkable work ends
By May the employment offices confirmed what graduates already knew: routine coding, standardised analysis, first-draft documentation and tier-one support desks were not rehiring at entry level. The losses stopped at the edge of verifiable work, but for that edge there was no recovery. Forecasts of a wider wave were quietly pushed to next year.

The wage-bridge and retraining fund approved years earlier finally pushed late payments through emergency social-fund advances, and checker courses filled — still unrecognised by most employers. Detection kits and screened configurations from the municipal bio-cyber programme reached more clinics, and wastewater flags triggered two precautionary closures later cleared by labs.

Voters saw protection arriving after harm, jobs lost before alternatives, and dependence unchanged. Anger at infrastructure hardened into blockades at two data-centre sites, quickly cleared but widely filmed.
