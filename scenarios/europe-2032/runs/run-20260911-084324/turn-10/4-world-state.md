### The patch that owned the grid
Spring began with clinics locked out of their own records. A compromised update in a widely used IT management tool opened the door, and automated ransomware walked through it — municipal portals, appointment systems, two regional hospital groups, a parts supplier for the grid spares stock. The code had been probed, packed and mutated with machine help. Defenders found the backdoor weeks after it was already inside.

The islanding kits held, barely. Paper folders came back out, wastewater sampling continued, power stayed on. But care degraded to queues and handwritten prescriptions, and attribution notices said only that investigation would take months. Evening news ran the map of dark portals alongside the winter's map of dark suburbs.

### The jump in the verifiable
While incident teams rebuilt servers, an American lab demonstrated a new coding and operations agent that wrote, tested and broke into hardened targets far faster than its predecessor. Specialists argued about generality; security teams did not. Within weeks, copycat tooling distilled from near-frontier open weights circulated on private hardware, weeks behind the frontier and beyond recall.

Brussels felt the squeeze from the other side as well. Washington tightened chip and model licences again, and this time European buyers were rationed alongside everyone else. Volume-licence pleas bought meetings, not wafers. Data-centre curtailments from the winter pact suddenly looked like a permanent condition.

### Checkable work disappears
The layoffs were not a wave but a cut. Junior developer posts, standard analyst roles, first-draft documentation, tier-one support desks — hiring froze and did not thaw. Universities reported graduates with clean grades and no offers. Forecasts of a broader collapse were quietly pushed to next year; the narrow collapse was enough.

The Commission answered with a wage-bridge and fast retraining fund, routed through employment ministries and paid in part by a levy on firms cutting staff through automation. The employment council fought the levy for three months before assenting to a diluted version. Payments began late, enrolment was thin, and the message shift from rationing endured to jobs protected did not land. Bills remained high, portals remained slow, and voters who had tolerated two winters of triage now treated Brussels as the manager of decline.
