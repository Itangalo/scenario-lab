### The sweep
Autumn began with alarms that did not stop. A largely automated wave — ransomware locking municipal administrations and hospitals, a poisoned software component buried in maintenance tools, and probing that tipped into disruption at water and mid-sized energy suppliers — spread across a dozen member states within days. Transmission grids held, islanding as drilled in spring. The edge did not.

City IT desks, clinics and contractors found backups encrypted or patch servers serving the attackers' code. Cleanup itself caused outages. Attribution blurred immediately; investigators agreed only that the tooling had been generated by frontier models, adapted in hours.

Brussels put its crisis networks on war footing. The machine-speed patching and swarm-detection stack built for the high-voltage network was pushed down to municipalities, with emergency triage teams, funded resets and mandatory segmentation orders. A small cell was set up to map the compromised component, issuing disclosure demands to vendors. Money moved fast; implementation did not always. Mayors complained teams arrived after systems were already rebuilt from scratch.

The evaluation facility tried to claim access to the model systems behind the attack, but vendors stalled over what counted as the system, and seconded staff were still arriving.

### Hands that work
While servers were being rebuilt, another shock arrived quietly in warehouses. Commercial humanoid robots, largely built in China on American control software, began appearing in European logistics pilots — carrying, picking, then with dexterity visibly improving month to month. Industry federations warned the industrial base Europe still led in could be undercut from below, with defence uses already discussed abroad.

The Commission ordered a mapping of exposure and promised a transition instrument later, deliberately refusing a second front while the cyber cleanup consumed attention. Unions called it delay; manufacturers called it realism.

Public mood soured. Relief that lights stayed on gave way to anger at cancelled operations, late benefits and ransom debates. Gigafactory and technology-zone works continued, but only where grid connections were certified hardened, slowing several sites.
