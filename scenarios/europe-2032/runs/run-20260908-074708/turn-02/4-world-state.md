### A Sovereignty Surge Meets Hard Resistance

The European Union launched the EU Sovereign Access Guarantee in response to the sudden denial of access to the leading global AI model, which had disrupted hospital diagnostics and energy grid coordination. Invoking emergency powers, the Commission repurposed EuroHPC JU to deploy a sovereign model stack trained on EU-controlled compute. However, legal challenges emerged immediately: Germany and the Netherlands questioned the override of existing cloud contracts, while industry groups warned of fragmentation and performance gaps.

Despite fast-tracking permits, the Finland and Portugal Gigafactory sites remain months behind on power infrastructure. The redirected cohesion funds helped, but talent shortages slowed model fine-tuning. No sovereign capability has yet been fielded at scale; early prototypes remain below target performance, and deployment to critical sectors is uncertain and delayed.
