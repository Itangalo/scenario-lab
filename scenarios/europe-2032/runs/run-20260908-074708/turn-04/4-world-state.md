### The Weight of Ambition

The EU pressed forward on multiple fronts, but momentum faltered under the strain of overlapping commitments. The launch of the European Bio-Risk Early Warning System was met with cautious optimism, framed as a necessary step in light of growing concern over AI-enabled bio-risks. However, the initiative faced immediate coordination hurdles between national biosecurity agencies and the ECDC, delaying integration. While red-team grants were announced, operational deployment remained distant, limiting near-term impact.

### Talent and Turf Wars

The Sovereign Talent Acceleration Programme, invoked under emergency powers, triggered a backlash. Several member states challenged the use of cohesion funds and raised legal objections over binding contracts, citing interference with national education and labour markets. Recruitment stalled, with only a fraction of targeted specialists onboarded. Industry groups decried the move as a state-led talent grab, further fracturing consensus.

### Infrastructure and Access

Efforts to unblock the InvestAI Gigafactories through accelerated permitting made limited progress. Power constraints in Finland and Portugal persisted, and supply_chain_coercion continued to undermine confidence in long-term compute access. The EU Sovereign Access Guarantee, still the named priority, slipped further behind schedule, its credibility eroding.

### Political and Public Strain

Public sentiment dipped as campaign_backlash intensified, with growing political momentum behind data centre moratoriums and worker protections. The narrative around AI shifted toward risk and dependency, undercutting support for large-scale infrastructure. Meanwhile, campaign_security_hawk in the US reinforced a posture of technological containment, foreshadowing tighter export controls.
