## In practice
This turn, we are driving the final negotiation of the EU Compute Resilience Directive in COREPER II, with the Council Presidency scheduling a special session in April to break the deadlock between net contributors and net recipients. The Commission has circulated a revised annex that allows Germany and the Netherlands to count existing hybrid-cloud investments toward compliance if they include EU-controlled failover nodes, easing interoperability concerns. For Poland and other lower-readiness states, we have unlocked an additional €900 million from the Digital Europe Programme’s reserve tranche, conditional on national action plans submitted by June. The European Defence Agency is conducting parallel tabletop exercises with ENISA to stress-test proposed architectures, feeding technical validation into the legal drafting.

At the same time, we are managing dual crises: the cyber_major_incident has hit municipal services in three member states, exploiting an AI-generated exploit chain in a widely used open-source dependency. While not yet attributed, forensic analysis confirms the attack used agent-designed payloads indistinguishable from human-crafted code. Simultaneously, backlash_physical has escalated in rural France and Spain, where protestors have cut power feeds to two US-operated data centres hosting critical public AI services. We are deploying civil protection funds under Article 12 of the Civil Protection Mechanism to restore services, but the political cost mounts. Our response remains focused: complete the directive, absorb the shocks through existing resilience scaffolding, and preserve capital for a coordinated countermeasure package next turn — but only once the scale of harm and public reaction are fully mapped.
