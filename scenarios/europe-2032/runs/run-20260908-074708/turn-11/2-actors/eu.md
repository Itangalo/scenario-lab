## New measure  
**Software Supply Chain Resilience Framework**  
Establish a mandatory verification and attestation regime for open-source software dependencies used in essential AI systems, with EU-run mirror repositories and sandboxed integration testing.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to `resilience`, +3 to `ai_safety` by hardening critical software supply chains and reducing attack surface from poisoned dependencies  
`Applies to:` EU member states, open-source maintainers receiving public funding, and providers of AI systems used in essential functions

## Priority  
**Software Supply Chain Resilience Framework** — because the ransomware attack via a compromised library proves that infrastructure sovereignty means nothing if the software stack is porous, and because the middle_power_coalition now offers leverage to set upstream standards that can be mirrored across partners.

## In practice  
This turn, we are treating the fallout from the cyber_major_incident not as a one-off breach but as proof of systemic fragility: AI-generated exploit chains in a trusted open-source dependency bypassed human review and automated checks alike. We are launching the Software Supply Chain Resilience Framework under the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) legal base, with ENISA tasked to stand up pilot mirror repositories by Q1 2032. The framework will require all software dependencies in AI systems supporting essential functions to be attested through a new EU Open Source Attestation Body, funded at €420 million from the Digital Europe Programme and co-staffed by national cybersecurity agencies. We are engaging GitHub, GitLab, and the Open Source Initiative to align on signing and provenance standards, leveraging the newly formed middle_power_coalition to ensure common requirements across Japan, South Korea, and Canada.

Simultaneously, we are finalizing the EU Compute Resilience Directive in COREPER II, where the recent grid disconnections in France and Spain have shifted the debate: Germany and the Netherlands now accept the need for failover nodes on EU soil, and Poland’s demand for co-investment has been met with the unlocked €900 million tranche. The Council Presidency has scheduled a vote for late November, and we expect adoption by turn 12. The directive’s completion will free up political capital, but only if we can show a follow-on resilience measure that answers both digital dependency and physical disruption. That is the Framework: it is large, it is urgent, and it is the only way to close the gap between having compute and being able to trust what runs on it.
