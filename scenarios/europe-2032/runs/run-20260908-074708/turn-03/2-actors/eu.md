## In practice
We are acting on the understanding that compute without competence is inert. The Gigafactories in Finland and Portugal remain power-constrained and understaffed, but the deeper failure is human: we have not yet built the sovereign team to run what we are building. The Sovereign Access Guarantee cannot succeed if the models are underperforming and the fine-tuning pipeline is stalled. We are therefore launching the Sovereign Talent Acceleration Programme under Article 122 TFEU’s emergency competence, using cohesion fund flexibility to offer binding five-year public service contracts with competitive compensation packages—indexed to private-sector frontier roles—for 1,200 AI researchers, engineers, and safety specialists. Priority will go to EU nationals currently working abroad and to third-country experts in strategic fields, with fast-tracked Schengen Blue Cards and housing support coordinated through the European Labour Authority.

We are embedding these personnel directly into EuroHPC JU’s sovereign stack teams and linking them to national cyber and critical infrastructure agencies to ensure operational cohesion. Resistance is expected from member states wary of brain drain from their own universities and from industry groups arguing for open labour markets, but we are framing this as a temporary wartime footing: these are not permanent extractions, but a surge to secure continuity in essential functions. We are also using the Tech Sovereignty Package’s accelerated-permitting zones to unlock grid connections in Finland and Portugal, pairing physical and human infrastructure in a single push. The supply_chain_coercion event confirms that our chokepoint is being used against us; we will not wait for the next cutoff to realise that talent is just as strategic as lithography.
