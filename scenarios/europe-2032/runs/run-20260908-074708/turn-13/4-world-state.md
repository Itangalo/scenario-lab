### The Weight of Control

AI capability creeps upward to 63.0, sustained by closed-loop research automation despite commercial stagnation. The open-weight frontier advances to 59.0, as the latest powerful release spreads rapidly, now confirmed to enable advanced bio-design tasks outside controlled environments. Intelligence agencies note its use in crafting stable viral vectors with human cell targeting—still in silico and early lab validation, but the path to misuse is clearer.

The EU pivots hard toward trusted infrastructure, launching the **Sovereign Inference Corridor**, a large category 4 initiative to ensure essential public services run only on verified, EU-controlled models and hardware. The plan repurposes stalled compute capacity, focusing not on raw power but on safety and legitimacy. Finland, Austria, and Estonia will host the first certified clusters, with strict model audits enforced via a new Public Model Assurance Framework. The move is framed as a response to both bio-risk and public distrust.

However, momentum falters. The **Open-Weight Containment Protocol** faces stiff resistance in the Council, with tech-forward member states demanding exemptions and questioning enforcement. Japan and South Korea offer only conditional support, waiting for operational proof. The **Software Supply Chain Resilience Framework** continues in pilot, but delivers no new resilience gain this turn.

Public sentiment remains frozen at 25.0—fear of AI-enabled bio-risks rises, but so does scepticism that the EU can deliver on its promises. Political capital erodes further as the cost of three large measures mounts. The new Sovereign Inference Corridor is named priority, but no sentiment boost follows, and backlash continues to simmer.

Sovereignty declines to 9.0, as `ai_capability` rises and no domestic capacity is secured. The US posture of **CONSOLIDATION** continues to constrain access, and the Union’s leverage remains thin.

### A Strategy Reforged in Doubt

The EU’s shift from capacity to control is conceptually sound, but politically strained. The vision of safe, sovereign inference is clear—but the path is narrow, underfunded, and contested.
