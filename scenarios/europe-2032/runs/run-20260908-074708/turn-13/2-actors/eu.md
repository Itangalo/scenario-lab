## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI infrastructure for essential public functions — but we now narrow this to focus on **sovereign access to safe, verifiable inference capacity**, after the emergent_bio_uplift_signal proves that capability without control is a vector of harm, not a strategic asset, and after backlash_physical reveals that infrastructure without legitimacy cannot stand.  
- Grounds: The bio-uplift signal demonstrates that open-weight models at 58.0 are already enabling high-consequence dual-use research outside any oversight regime. At the same time, public attacks on data centres show that our infrastructure is politically vulnerable where it lacks perceived legitimacy. This changes the nature of sovereignty: it is not enough to own compute if unsafe models run on it, and not enough to secure models if the public sees the infrastructure itself as a threat. We must now tie sovereignty to safety and acceptance — not just access. This does not abandon the commitment, but redefines its core: we are no longer building capacity for capacity’s sake, but for *controlled, trusted use* in essential functions.

## New measure  
**Sovereign Inference Corridor**  
Establish a protected, audited pathway for running essential public AI services on EU-controlled infrastructure using only models that have undergone independent safety verification and weight-provenance checks.

## Priority  
**Sovereign Inference Corridor** — because the emergent_bio_uplift_signal proves that capability without containment is already enabling dangerous applications, and because our sovereignty means nothing if we cannot guarantee that the AI running our essential services is both under our control and safe to run.

## In practice  
This turn, we are shifting the meaning of sovereignty: not just access to compute, but access to *safe and legitimate* compute. The emergent_bio_uplift_signal is the wake-up call — open models at 58.0 are now being used to design viral vectors with human tropism, not in a lab under review, but in distributed, unmonitored settings. We cannot stop every download, but we can ensure that the EU’s own systems run only on models we can verify, on hardware we control, and under conditions that the public can trust. We are launching the Sovereign Inference Corridor under the Digital Markets Act and the revised AI Act’s high-risk provisions, with the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) as lead coordinator. The plan is to repurpose 30% of the stalled Compute Resilience Directive’s planned capacity — now that we cannot build fast enough to compete at the frontier, we focus on building *right* for core functions.

We are working with DG CONNECT, the European AI Office, and national CIOs to define the first wave of essential services: pandemic early-warning systems, energy grid balancing, and emergency dispatch coordination. These will be migrated to a new class of EU-certified inference clusters, physically located in member states with strong rule of law and energy resilience (Finland, Austria, Estonia), and legally insulated from foreign service or data demands. The models they run must pass a new Public Model Assurance Framework — developed under category 5 but now operationalised — which requires third-party interpretability audits, weight-provenance tracing, and misuse resistance testing. We are offering compute priority and procurement guarantees to developers who comply, and we are preparing to exclude any model from public use that cannot demonstrate verifiable safety — even if it is technically capable.

At the same time, we are advancing the Open-Weight Containment Protocol through the Council’s Internal Market configuration, where resistance remains strong from member states with large tech sectors. We are offering a compromise: enforcement will be phased, with lighter obligations for models between 55.0 and 60.0, and only full controls for those above. We are also linking progress to the middle_power_coalition, where Japan and South Korea have expressed conditional support if the EU can show operational utility. And we are accelerating the Software Supply Chain Resilience Framework’s pilot phase, integrating the new bio-risk detection modules developed by the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control. But the truth is clear: resilience and containment are no longer enough. We must now *demonstrate* that sovereign AI is safer AI — or lose both public trust and strategic ground.
