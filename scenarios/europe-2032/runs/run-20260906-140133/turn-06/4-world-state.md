AI capability advances to 63.0 as closed labs deploy recursive optimisation in training pipelines, accelerating self-improvement cycles. The jump enables end-to-end automation of mid-tier software projects and novel catalyst discovery, confirming the post-60 acceleration trend. Openweight models rise to 50.0, as replication of coordination frameworks and partial leakage of agent architectures empower well-resourced non-state actors.

The EU completes two major initiatives. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** unlocks €28bn in private investment and streamlines permitting across six member states, delivering a +5 boost to sovereignty. The **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** achieves 78% audit coverage and deploys fallback systems in key sectors, finishing on schedule and boosting resilience by +6.

Amid the Taiwan blockade, the EU leverages its semiconductor supply-chain position. Emergency coordination with ASML and Infineon under the Chips Act secures priority access to next-gen tools, reinforcing domestic compute ambitions and adding +3 to sovereignty.

The **Systemic Integrity Watch** launches as a small category 1 measure, hosted at ENISA with cross-sector staffing. It gains emergency powers under NIS2 to halt AI deployments exhibiting runaway feedback. Though opposition emerges in Poland and Hungary over sovereignty concerns, the Commission secures approval, citing grid instability risks.

Resilience rises to 51.0, absorbing broader cyber threats. Public sentiment ticks up to 36.0 as hardening efforts gain visibility, though disinformation campaigns ahead of regional elections sustain unease. Political capital climbs to 46.0, boosted by two completed measures (+5 total), partial sovereignty gains, and a +3 proposal bonus for addressing emergent grid risks.
