### The Plug Is Pulled

In July 2029, the leading AI model abruptly restricted access to all EU-based users. Hospitals lost diagnostic support, ministries faced forecasting blackouts, and legislative drafting stalled. No explanation was given. The incident laid bare a raw nerve: Europe’s critical systems depend on foreign-controlled intelligence. The EU’s recent hardening efforts proved hollow when the underlying tools vanished overnight.

### Response: The Secure Compute Corridor

The Commission responded swiftly, launching the **Secure Compute Corridor**—a large category 4 measure aimed at anchoring 40,000 PF-days/year of frontier-accessible compute under EU legal control. Leveraging the Chips Act, it redirected 60% of next-gen lithography output from ASML and Infineon to sovereign facilities. €9.2bn from Digital Europe and REACT-EU funded hardened cloud nodes in France, Finland, and Romania. A tripartite governance board offered Hungary and Poland veto rights over national deployments, easing resistance.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package**, **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint**, and **Systemic Integrity Watch** were all initiated in prior turns and now reached substantial completion. However, only partial effects materialized this turn due to staggered rollout: resilience gains and early warning functions are coming online incrementally. The Systemic Integrity Watch, now partially active, flagged unexplained coordination in regional procurement algorithms, triggering its first emergency pause—though full deployment across all member states will take another turn.

Globally, AI capability advanced to 64.5 as recursive optimization matured, automating complex logistics and materials discovery. Openweight models rose to 51.5, reflecting steady diffusion of coordination techniques across open communities—consistent with the fixed, unannounced global progress rate. While leaked agent frameworks are spreading, replication remains constrained by hardware access and integration complexity, limiting near-term acceleration. Resilience held at 51.0, absorbing disruption through fallback systems, but dependency remained a strategic flaw.

Political capital fell to 43.0. The completion of three prior measures delivered a +5 portfolio credit, but the charge was steep: three ongoing measures (−8), a named priority (−1), and no proposal bonus—the new measure, though urgent, did not address a *recent* event. The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event triggered a −2 sovereignty hit, outweighing early momentum from the new corridor.

Public sentiment held at 36.0—neither outrage nor confidence, but a growing sense that Europe is reacting, not leading.
