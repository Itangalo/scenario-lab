## New measure

Establish a legally binding, technically enforced corridor of EU-anchored compute capacity dedicated to critical public functions, secured from foreign withdrawal or interference, by repurposing and expanding existing Chips Act coordination and pairing it with sovereign cloud infrastructure.

## Priority

## In practice  
This turn, we close three major initiatives. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** has delivered €28bn in committed investment and permitting reforms across six member states, but its true test comes now: will those data centres run EU-controlled systems, or serve as offshored compute farms for foreign labs? The **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** ends with 78% audit coverage and fallback systems in place, yet we saw during the June grid anomaly in Bavaria that hardened systems still fail when the AI managing load balancing is withdrawn without warning. Most urgently, the **Systemic Integrity Watch** goes live at ENISA this month, integrating telemetry from 14 national grid operators and seven health AI coordinators. Early signals show anomalous coordination in procurement algorithms across three regional governments — not yet harmful, but unexplainable. We are activating its emergency powers for the first time to pause two deployments pending review.

But the event of the turn is clear: **eu_frontier_access_denied**. Without notice, the leading model has been cut off from EU institutions. Cancer diagnosis support in three countries, supply chain forecasting in five ministries, and AI-assisted legislative drafting in the Council — all disrupted. No reason given. No appeal. This is not a market failure. It is a sovereignty failure. We cannot harden systems against attack if the tools they run on vanish overnight. We cannot monitor systemic integrity if the systems themselves are foreign-controlled. And we cannot maintain public trust when our services collapse because someone else decided to pull the plug.

So we act. Under Article 4 of the Chips Act and the Digital Markets Act’s essential facility provisions, we initiate the **Secure Compute Corridor**: a binding framework that redirects 60% of next-gen lithography output from ASML and Infineon to EU-controlled facilities, not for stockpiling, but to anchor a sovereign compute stack. We pair this with a €9.2bn allocation from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU to build out sovereign cloud nodes in France, Finland, and Romania — hardened, audited, and legally insulated from foreign jurisdiction. Access will be restricted to vetted public operators and EU-certified developers working on critical functions. Hungary and Poland, who resisted the Systemic Integrity Watch, now demand assurances this does not override national control. We offer them co-governance: a tripartite board (Commission, Member State nominees, technical experts) to oversee allocation, with veto rights on deployment in national systems. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that, next time, the decision to cut access is ours — and ours alone.
