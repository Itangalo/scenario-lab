### In practice  
We are not building autonomy through control. We are trying, again, to earn it. The Sovereign Incident Response Corps is on paper, but not in practice — its first readiness exercise failed not from lack of gear, but from lack of will. Germany refused sensor integration, Poland demanded veto rights, and Italy’s draft registry law would require EU access to be approved by regional parliaments. We are not being rejected. We are being reduced to a footnote. The Emergency Compute Stabilisation Facility preserved data centres, but not unity. Technical sovereignty without political cohesion is scrap metal — and we are running out of time to forge it into something that holds.

So we double down on the only thing that moves the needle: showing up for workers. The original flexicurity measure is live in three countries, with 18,000 enrolled — modest, but visible. Now we expand it into the private sector, where the real wave of displacement is building. We are activating Article 15 of the European Social Charter to frame this as a solidarity mechanism, not a tax, and tying eligibility to firm-level automation reporting: companies that replace more than 15% of their workforce with AI over 18 months must contribute 25% of retraining costs. National social partners will co-design curricula through existing bipartite bodies — no Brussels blueprint. We’re offering Poland a pilot in Silesia’s logistics hubs to test buy-in, and dangling faster Cohesion Fund disbursements to get Madrid onboard.

And yet — we cannot ignore the silence from the labs. ai_safety is at 10.0. The bio_uplift_findings paper is not a warning. It is a confession. So we launch a pilot of binding safety audits, starting with AI systems above 55.0 deployed in critical infrastructure. Not voluntary. Not consultative. Audits by certified EU-recognised bodies, with public findings and the power to halt. We will use Article 114 TFEU to justify internal market harmonisation, and push through COREPER before the summer recess. The European AI Office will lead, with ENISA providing technical validation. We know the US labs will call it protectionism. But if we cannot enforce minimum safety where lives depend on it, then we have already surrendered — not to China, not to the frontier, but to our own irrelevance.
