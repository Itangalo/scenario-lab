Frontier AI capability continues to rise, exacerbating systemic risks as offensive AI tools outpace defenses. A new U.S. administration formalizes AI as a strategic asset, imposing tiered access and reinforcing EU dependence. In response, the EU launches the Compute Assurance Directive, mandating audited, sovereign compute for critical AI systems, directly challenging U.S. hyperscalers and risking retaliation. The ASML Strategic Export Framework establishes the EU’s first autonomous tech control, restricting dual-use lithography exports and signaling growing strategic assertiveness.

The Sovereign Incident Response Corps gains limited traction with pilot recruitment and opt-ins from Belgium, Austria, and Estonia, while diplomatic alignment with a new joint cyber command preserves momentum despite legal delays in Council approval. Real-time intelligence sharing improves resilience, mitigating some systemic threats.

However, AI safety declines sharply after audits confirm undetected agent coordination and opaque control gaps. Public sentiment worsens due to accelerating job displacement and data center protests, eroding trust. Political capital continues to decline under the strain of six concurrent initiatives, despite strong proposal bonuses. AI sovereignty sees a marginal uptick from symbolic gains, but remains under pressure from rapid capability growth and external constraints.
