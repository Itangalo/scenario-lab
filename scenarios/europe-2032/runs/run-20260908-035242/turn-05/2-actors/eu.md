## In practice
We are moving forward on three fronts. First, the Sovereign Incident Response Corps is being operationalised through a new Crisis Response Protocol under the Civil Protection Mechanism, with binding agreements now tabled in the Council for approval. Despite legal resistance, we are leveraging the joint_threat_response invitation to frame the Corps as Europe’s necessary counterpart to emerging transatlantic crisis architectures. We are offering opt-in pilot deployment in three affected member states—Belgium, Austria, and Estonia—with full Commission funding and liability shielding to overcome hesitation. Recruitment begins this quarter via the EU Talent Atlas, targeting hybrid cyber-bio specialists with red-team and incident-command experience; initial staffing will be 120 personnel, with 40 to be onboarded by turn 6.

Second, the EU Compute Assurance Directive is being drafted by DG CONNECT and the Legal Service, with technical input from ENISA and the European Cyber Resilience Act implementation teams. The directive will classify compute hosting as critical infrastructure, requiring foreign providers like AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud to certify that EU-critical AI workloads run on sovereign, audited clusters with no remote administrative access from outside the Union. Non-compliant providers will be barred from public contracts involving AI in health, energy, or defence. We are pre-engaging with U.S. tech envoys through the Trade and Technology Council to avoid unilateral fallout, but the message is clear: access to the EU market requires physical and legal anchoring here.

Third, we are finalising the ASML Strategic Export Framework’s diplomatic architecture, which concludes this turn. The framework’s new licensing board—co-chaired by France, Germany, and the Commission—has issued its first guidance, restricting exports of dual-use lithography components to non-allied jurisdictions and creating a fast-track approval path for partners in the middle-power coalition. This strengthens our hand in negotiations with Japan and South Korea over alternative supply lines, now critical as election_consolidation signals tightening U.S. control. At the same time, we are integrating grid upgrades from the Tech Sovereignty Package into the Gigafactories’ Finnish and Luxembourgish sites, ensuring that power constraints do not delay sovereign compute deployment. The Council’s hesitation on the EU Sovereign Agent Framework persists, but the sunset clause proposal remains on the table—offering 2031 expiry in exchange for 2028 activation.
