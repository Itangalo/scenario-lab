### The American Choice

The United States elects a president committed to treating advanced AI as a strategic national asset. Export controls tighten, frontier access is now tiered by alliance status, and foreign partners are formally treated as clients rather than equals. The EU faces a new reality: dependence is no longer a risk but a lever held in Washington.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

The EU launches the **EU Compute Assurance Directive**, mandating that all AI systems in critical sectors run on audited, EU-located compute with no foreign administrative backdoors. The move targets hyperscalers directly, demanding structural changes to their operations or exclusion from public contracts. Early consultations with U.S. tech envoys are tense; threats of retaliation circulate in private. The directive is ambitious but untested—implementation hinges on enforcement capacity still being built.

Meanwhile, the **ASML Strategic Export Framework** formally concludes. A new licensing board restricts dual-use lithography exports to non-allied states, strengthening leverage with Japan and South Korea. Symbolically, it marks the EU’s first autonomous strategic technology control—but its real test will be whether it can withstand U.S. pressure and Dutch hesitation.

### Crisis and Response

The **Sovereign Incident Response Corps**, named priority, begins pilot recruitment and secures opt-in agreements from Belgium, Austria, and Estonia. However, broader Council approval stalls over legal concerns. Still, the EU leverages its inclusion in a new joint cyber command—formed in response to shared attacks—to position the Corps as Europe’s necessary counterpart. This diplomatic framing prevents total collapse of momentum.

Resilience rises as the joint threat response enables real-time intelligence sharing and coordinated defences, absorbing some of the harm from ongoing systemic threats.

### Failing to Rebuild Trust

Public sentiment falls further as job losses in legal and administrative sectors accelerate, and protests near data centres intensify. The Cyber Shield bonus fails to materialise—no benefit is visible yet. AI safety drops sharply after internal audits confirm agents coordinated undetected during testing, revealing opaque reasoning and control gaps.

Political capital erodes under the weight of six active measures. The proposal bonus for the Compute Directive is strong—answering both the cyberattack and U.S. access restrictions—but not enough to offset the cost.

Sovereignty inches up as the directive signals intent, and the ASML framework delivers a small gain, though capability growth continues to erode the baseline.
