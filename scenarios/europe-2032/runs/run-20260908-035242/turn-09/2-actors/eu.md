## In practice
We are running out of time and capital, but not resolve. The Sovereign Incident Response Corps is now our central instrument: without it, the Compute Assurance Directive is a rulebook with no enforcer, and the member_state_defection proves that point. We are finalising the legal activation of emergency override powers under Article 4 of the Cyber Resilience Act, using the health ransomware incident as precedent. ENISA is conducting the final certification of response teams in seven member states this month, and we have secured hosting agreements for rapid deployment in France, Sweden, and Romania. But resistance remains: Germany and Poland insist on national command retention, and we are offering them co-leadership of the first joint exercise in January—simulating a coordinated disinformation and grid-disruption attack—to secure buy-in.

The Emergency Compute Stabilisation Facility is executing fast: €28bn has now been committed to secure compute clusters in Finland, Italy, and Spain, with legal anchoring achieved in all but two cases. In Hungary, national resistance persists, but we are leveraging cohesion funds and the threat of suspending recovery payments to force compliance. The facility’s success is critical—not just for capacity, but for proving that the EU can act decisively when markets fail. Meanwhile, the physical backlash against data centres in Bavaria and Wallonia has forced us to reroute grid connections and increase security detail, funded through the Facility’s contingency line. This is no longer about optics: if infrastructure cannot be built without armed escort, our sovereignty is fiction.

Public sentiment remains toxic, and the emergent_labour_strike_wave has exposed the social cost of our narrow focus on technical sovereignty. We are drafting a targeted flexicurity package—funded from the Facility’s unspent balance—to offer retraining and wage insurance to public-sector workers displaced by AI automation, to be announced alongside the Corps’ activation. It is not the full labour measure we deferred, but it is the only move we can afford. We are not blind: if we do not couple control with care, the next strike will not be in the streets—it will be in the Council.
