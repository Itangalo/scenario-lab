### Terminal Zone Drift  
Global AI capability advances to 63.0, as labs consolidate multi-agent coordination into standard development pipelines. Progress slows but remains steady, entering the upper half of the 62–68 terminal zone. Open-weight models follow at a delay: openweight_capability rises to 52.5, reflecting wider access to tools capable of sustained task execution under supervision.

### Incident Response Meets Resistance  
The Sovereign Incident Response Corps reaches operational status, but its first joint readiness exercise is boycotted by Germany and Poland, who refuse to grant pre-authorised access to national critical systems. The refusal undermines the corps’ credibility and delays integration with national cyberdefence units. While the measure finishes, its real-world effectiveness remains unproven.

### Emergency Compute Preserved — But Not Unified  
The Emergency Compute Stabilisation Facility secures key data centres in France and Italy, preventing collapse. Yet in Bavaria and Wallonia, local authorities continue to treat EU envoys with suspicion, framing foreign-owned AI infrastructure as a threat rather than an asset. National police, not EU coordinators, manage access.

### A Narrow Bet on Social Trust  
With political capital at 7.0, the EU launches Rebuild Public Trust through Targeted Flexicurity — a small-scale, €8.7bn programme repurposed from unspent emergency funds. It offers wage insurance and retraining for public-sector workers in Germany, France, and Spain. The move is seen as a gesture of goodwill, though unions criticise its narrow scope. Early uptake is moderate, but media coverage shifts slightly: for the first time in months, some outlets frame the EU as responding, not just reacting.

### Political Paralysis Persists  
Despite the new initiative, eu_political_capital remains flat. The completion of one large measure (+3) offsets the cost of three ongoing ones (−9), the priority (−1), and no proposal bonus. Sovereignty declines further (−1) due to capability growth and no offsetting build. Public sentiment inches up slightly on visible action, but structural distrust remains.
