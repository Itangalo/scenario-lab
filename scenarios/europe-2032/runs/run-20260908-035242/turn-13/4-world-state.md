### The Fire This Time  
A wave of AI-generated ransomware cripples public health systems across Belgium, the Netherlands, and southern Germany, marking the most severe cyber incident in the EU’s history. The attack exploits zero-day vulnerabilities identified and weaponised by autonomous tools, overwhelming defences. Response is fragmented, attribution delayed. The EU declares a systemic crisis under Article 122 TFEU.

### Surge, Not Audit  
The European Union pivots sharply: the planned safety audit pilot is scrapped. Instead, it launches the Rapid Cyber Defence Surge Capacity, repurposing the Sovereign Incident Response Corps into a standing emergency force. Thirty cross-border teams, equipped with AI-driven detection and automated patching tools, are to be operational by mid-2033. ENISA assumes command in emergency zones, overriding national hesitation through binding authority.

### Hard Bargains  
Germany initially resists centralised control but accepts co-leadership of the northern response hub. Poland secures parliamentary oversight in peacetime, but emergency protocols remain under EU authority. Italy suspends its national AI registry in exchange for accelerated cohesion funding. The €7.2bn from the Emergency Compute Stabilisation Facility is redirected via comitology, bypassing full legislative scrutiny.

### Trust Through Action  
Displaced workers are fast-tracked into cyber retraining, linking resilience to social policy. Public sentiment rises slightly as visible action counters paralysis. However, the flexicurity expansion finishes without full uptake, limiting gains.

### Silent Labs, Distant Powers  
U.S. and Chinese developers remain silent. A bilateral agreement on biological design controls emerges—excluding the EU. Export controls on advanced chips tighten under the American consolidation posture, further restricting access.
