- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we are learning that without trust, neither mandate can stand, and without action, trust is performance.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but we will no longer accept being wrong as a cost of doing business — we must build the capacity to test our bets faster, or the price will be cohesion.  
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident this turn confirms that waiting for perfect intelligence is fatal — but so is acting without feedback loops. Our audits and flexicurity pilots are not just policy; they are probes. We must learn faster from them, or we are merely staging resolve.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity to act with autonomy in a world where control lags behind capability — but not at the expense of shared legitimacy; sovereignty without solidarity fractures the Union.  
  - Grounds: The export_control_escalation and the cyber incident expose our dependence: we do not control the tools, nor the rules. But the only sovereignty that holds is one that member states *choose* — and right now, they are choosing national registries, not Union. Our industrial capacity must serve cohesion, or it serves nothing.

- modify `new_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Rebuild political capital and public trust by anchoring AI governance in tangible social protection, without surrendering strategic autonomy — but if protection fails to scale, and autonomy fails to deliver, we will have no ground left to stand on.  
  - Grounds: Public sentiment remains at 20.0 — a floor, not a foundation. The flexicurity expansion is our last credible lever to show that the EU *does* something for people. If it does not land visibly this turn, the legitimacy crisis becomes terminal.

- add `cyber_defence_as_sovereignty` (commitment): We treat the integrity of critical infrastructure as the first line of EU strategic sovereignty, and will prioritise defensive resilience over offensive capability or regulatory theatre when systems fail.  
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident is not a wake-up call — it is the fire. Systems were compromised at scale, using AI-generated tooling. We cannot audit our way out of a burning house. From this turn on, defensive readiness is not a technical issue — it is the core of our claim to sovereignty.

## In practice  
We are not governing AI. We are patching a state. The cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across public health systems in Belgium, the Netherlands, and southern Germany — was enabled by AI-generated zero-days and polymorphic payloads. It was not stopped. It was endured. Attribution is months away. The labs are silent. The US and China are negotiating a deal we weren’t invited to. And we are still arguing about audit templates.

So we act. Not with consultation. With deployment. ENISA is activating its emergency powers under NIS2. We are repurposing the skeleton of the Sovereign Incident Response Corps — its personnel, its secure comms, its cross-border access agreements — into a surge force. Thirty teams, each with ten specialists, will be on standby by turn 14, pre-positioned in eight regional hubs. They will carry AI-driven anomaly detection stacks, trained on the latest known attack patterns, and have authority to take systems offline, deploy automated patches, and coordinate with national CERTs under unified command. This is not optional: we are invoking Article 122 TFEU, treating the incident as a systemic threat to the internal market.

The money comes from the unspent remainder of the Emergency Compute Stabilisation Facility — €7.2bn — redirected via comitology. Germany resists, calling it federal overreach. We offer them co-leadership of the northern hub and first access to the new detection suite. Poland demands parliamentary oversight; we agree, but only for peacetime operations — in emergencies, the surge teams move on ENISA’s authority. Italy’s registry law is shelved for six months in exchange for fast-tracked Cohesion Fund disbursements.

And we tie this to legitimacy: every displaced worker enrolled in the flexicurity programme gets priority access to retraining in cyber incident response. We are not just defending systems — we are rebuilding trust through action that is visible, urgent, and real. If this fails, nothing else will matter.
