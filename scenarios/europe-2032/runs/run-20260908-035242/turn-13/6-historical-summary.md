Global AI capability reaches 63.5, driven by efficiency gains and multi-agent adoption, while open-weight models rise to 54.0 due to a major non-Western release. AI safety remains stagnant at 10.0 despite escalating bio-risks. The EU declares a systemic crisis after a devastating AI-generated ransomware attack cripples health systems in Belgium, the Netherlands, and southern Germany. In response, the planned safety audit is abandoned and replaced by the Rapid Cyber Defence Surge Capacity—a standing EU emergency force under ENISA with binding authority in crises. Thirty cross-border teams with AI-driven tools are to deploy by mid-2033. Germany, Poland, and Italy make concessions on sovereignty in exchange for influence and funding, as €7.2bn from the Emergency Compute Stabilisation Facility is reallocated via comitology. Displaced workers are fast-tracked into cyber retraining, boosting public sentiment slightly despite incomplete flexicurity uptake. U.S. and Chinese developers remain uninvolved; a bilateral U.S.-China agreement on biological controls excludes the EU, while chip export restrictions tighten, deepening strategic isolation. Political capital rises to 13.0 on crisis response, but fragmentation and external dependency persist.
