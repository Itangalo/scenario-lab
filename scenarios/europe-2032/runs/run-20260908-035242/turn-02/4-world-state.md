### The Weight of Response

A major cyber incident strikes public services across several EU member states. A sophisticated ransomware strain, generated by AI and propagated through a compromised software dependency, cripples municipal systems and hospital scheduling. Defenders are overwhelmed, and the full scope remains unclear for weeks. The attack underscores how far offensive capabilities have surged, driven by open models now widely accessible.

Frontier AI capability advances to 54.5 as labs push toward autonomous development. Open-weight capability rises to 42.0, reflecting the diffusion of tools like Kimi K3. Safety declines to 30.0, as known anomalies persist and the absence of systemic safeguards allows for incremental erosion—though no single catastrophic failure or policy reversal occurs. The resilience effort, while ongoing, fails to prevent cascading failures, though its partial deployment limits the damage and yields a modest +3 to resilience.

### Sovereignty on Shaky Ground

The European Commission opens exploratory discussions on what could become an ASML-related export coordination initiative, responding to quiet overtures from ASML management under U.S. pressure. This early-stage effort is framed as a contingency to preserve strategic autonomy in semiconductor equipment exports, but no formal proposal is tabled. The idea is not yet a measure, and no vote is called.

Instead, the EU reaffirms its commitment to the existing Tech Sovereignty Package as its sole active measure, integrating the ASML concern into its broader agenda. This avoids launching a new initiative while allowing the issue to be scoped. However, diplomatic signals from Washington grow colder, with U.S. officials warning that any EU attempt to override U.S. national security directives will trigger technology access restrictions. Internal unity frays as member states with strong security ties to the U.S. express reservations.

Public sentiment dips to 37.0 amid media coverage of job losses and the cyber incident, but the decline is less severe than projected due to visible emergency response efforts. Political capital falls slightly to 38.0, reflecting growing strain and fragmented cohesion, but avoids a collapse as no high-stakes vote fails.
