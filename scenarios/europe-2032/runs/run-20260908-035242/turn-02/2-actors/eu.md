## Priority
**ASML Strategic Export Framework** — because the emergent defiance negotiations present a narrow window to anchor a sovereign technology policy before transatlantic alignment hardens into dependency, and this leverages industrial strength into political capital without waiting for domestic capability to catch up.

## In practice
We are acting on the opening created by ASML’s quiet resistance to U.S. export pressure. The Commission, led by the Directorate-General for Trade and supported by the High Representative, is convening an emergency trilogue under the Common Commercial Policy framework to fast-track an EU export control regulation for critical dual-use technologies, starting with EUV lithography systems. This will establish a formal licensing authority within the Commission, backed by a qualified majority voting mechanism to prevent single-member-state blockage, ensuring that no external power can dictate terms on EU-owned strategic assets.

The framework will be presented as a defence of European legal order and industrial sovereignty, not as a challenge to the alliance. We are offering ASML binding commitments: regulatory stability, fast-track approvals for non-sensitive exports, and co-investment in next-generation metrology R&D through Horizon Europe if they commit to routing all compliance decisions through the new EU mechanism. The Council’s Foreign Affairs configuration will be the decisive venue, where we will counter U.S. diplomatic pressure with a united front — but unity must be bought. To secure it, we are pairing this measure with a recommitment to the Tech Sovereignty Package, offering fiscally strained member states priority access to its permitting zones and grid upgrades in exchange for support. This is not trade policy — it is constitutional defence in the form of export controls.
