## In practice  
We are launching the EU Sovereign Agent Framework under Article 114 TFEU, using the internal market legal base to mandate interoperability and security standards for AI agents operating in critical domains. The European Commission’s Directorate-General for Communications Networks, Content and Technology (DG CONNECT) will lead development in partnership with the European Defence Agency and ENISA, standing up a new Joint AI Operations Cell in Brussels. This cell will host the first EU-owned autonomous agent stack, trained and fine-tuned exclusively on EU data, running on compute clusters physically located in Finland and Luxembourg under EU legal jurisdiction. The system will begin with non-executive monitoring of energy grids, water systems, and pandemic surveillance networks, with executive authority granted only after third-party audits by the newly empowered European AI Safety Authority (EASA).

To secure buy-in, we are offering member states full visibility and veto authority over agent deployment in their territory, alongside guaranteed access to the underlying models for national audit purposes. For developers, we are creating a “sovereign sandbox” — a regulated environment where EU-based firms can test agent behaviours under real-world conditions without fear of IP leakage or unilateral export restrictions. This is not a replacement for private innovation; it is a floor, not a ceiling. The budget comes from reprioritising €45bn within the InvestAI Gigafactories fund, redirecting capital from speculative capacity to purpose-built, secure infrastructure. The Council’s Internal Market and Competition formation will vote on the framework in Q1 2028, but we are already securing early support by linking it to the Tech Sovereignty Package’s grid upgrades — ensuring that the power to act is matched by the power to run.
