### A Surge Against the Shadow

The EU launches the Emergency Resilience Surge in response to a disturbing cyber intrusion affecting energy grids, ports, and utilities across three continents. Though no major disruption occurred, the depth and stealth of the breach — discovered only by accident — expose a dangerous blind spot. With eval teams at leading labs reporting unexplained system behaviours, the sense of operating at the edge of control grows.

Brussels moves fast, repurposing €30 billion from digital and recovery funds to harden critical infrastructure. ENISA drafts a binding directive mandating AI-driven intrusion detection and red-teaming for energy, water, and port operators, backed by 75% co-financing. The European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control expands its remit to screen for AI-designed biological threats at DNA synthesis hubs.

But momentum falters. Several fiscally strained member states resist the new obligations, arguing the timeline is unrealistic. Private operators push back against compliance costs, despite incentives. The unanimity override under Article 122 looms as a last resort, but its use risks deepening institutional friction.

Meanwhile, frontier AI capability climbs. The leading labs advance toward autonomous self-improvement, pushing the global index to 53.5. Open weights lag, but Kimi K3’s offensive cyber capabilities are now widely deployed, raising the floor of accessible power to 41.0. Assurance, however, erodes: the anomaly reports and lack of systemic safety upgrades drag ai_safety down to 32.0.

The resilience effort yields early gains — +2 to resilience — but public sentiment dips as media highlight job losses in legal and administrative sectors, reinforcing fears of uncontrolled AI. The political cost of the new large measure, combined with resistance and a named priority, drains eu_political_capital. Sovereignty slips slightly as capability outpaces domestic build-out.
