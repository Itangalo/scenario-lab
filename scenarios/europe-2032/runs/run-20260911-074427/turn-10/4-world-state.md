### A cure from elsewhere, a verdict at home
Spring brought two kinds of news about what intelligent systems could do. An American laboratory announced a planning method that cut the cost of a class of materials screening to a fraction, licensed immediately into battery and catalyst supply chains. Almost simultaneously, American hospitals began using individually tailored immunotherapies designed with frontier models, with remission figures that morning shows repeated for weeks.

In Europe both advances arrived as imports. Procurement officers were told to run them only where domestic secured capacity existed — in practice Lyon, Porto, Gdansk and a handful of other sites — while elsewhere clinics joined waiting lists for access negotiated in Washington. Gratitude for the therapies mixed openly with humiliation at the terms.

That mood turned when an ombudsman inquiry found that an automated triage system used in social insurance and municipal benefits had systematically cut entitlements for tens of thousands of people over two years. Caseworkers had approved flagged files in under a minute, logs existed but were never reviewed. Lawyers then established the harder point: the deployment did not fall under the high-risk categories as written in 2024. Everything done had been lawful.

The finding detonated the legitimacy of the rulebook. Editorial pages called the flagship technology law a law for another decade. Opposition parties demanded a moratorium on public-sector automation; mayors running the Union's job placement scheme were asked on camera whether their own scheduling tools were any different.

### Holding the line
The placement corps kept paying wages for juniors in hospitals, town halls and guarded construction sites, and joint cyber teams kept hospitals and ports running in degraded mode through a spring of insurance surcharges after extended manoeuvres around the strait. Stockpiles were quietly topped up and insurers coordinated under existing emergency rules.

But delivery remained thin against need. Funds cleared slowly, slots numbered in thousands, and fenced building sites stayed under guard against routine sabotage. By June the Union was visibly keeping services from stopping, visibly unable to promise more, and visibly dependent for its most celebrated cures.
