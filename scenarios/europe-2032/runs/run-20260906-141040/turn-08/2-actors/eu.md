## Priority
**Launch the Critical Systems Shield Initiative** — because the openweight frontier release this turn means that AI-capable offensive tools are now widely distributed and permanently loose, and our two-year commitment to defend critical systems can no longer wait for perfect standards or full consensus.

## In practice
We are acting on a new reality: the open-weight release that landed within months of the frontier means that 64.0-level capability — or close to it — is now embedded in private, unrecallable systems. The gap between closed and open has collapsed from 12.5 to under 10 points in a single turn, and we must assume that adversarial actors, criminal networks, or rogue states now hold tools that can automate sophisticated attacks on our most vital systems. Our two-year commitment to defend the integrity of critical institutions is no longer a long-term ambition — it is an urgent operational necessity.

We are therefore launching the Critical Systems Shield Initiative under Article 12 of the NIS2 Directive, with co-funding from the Digital Europe Programme and the Civil Protection Mechanism. ENISA will lead implementation, working with national cybersecurity agencies to mandate AI-driven intrusion detection systems at all Tier 1 and Tier 2 operators, with real-time telemetry flowing into a newly established EU Cyber Shield Coordination Cell. These systems will be required to run on sovereign compute — either within the Foundational Compute Reserve or on nationally certified infrastructure — and must include human-in-the-loop override for any automated response affecting system integrity. We are fast-tracking certification and offering grants to mid-sized operators who cannot bear the cost alone. Resistance is expected from member states guarding regulatory autonomy and from industry players reliant on U.S.-based cloud security stacks, but we are offering transitional equivalence for those that can prove equivalent safeguards under EU legal jurisdiction. This is not about control for its own sake — it is about ensuring that when an AI-enabled attack comes, it does not bring our hospitals, grids, or transport networks to a halt.
