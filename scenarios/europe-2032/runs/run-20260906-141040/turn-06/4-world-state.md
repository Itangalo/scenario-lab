### The Semiconductor Shock

The blockade around Taiwan severs advanced chip shipments, sending shockwaves through global AI development. With fabrication capacity frozen outside China’s domestic chain, compute access becomes a frontline strategic question. The EU finds itself both vulnerable and pivotal: its control over key upstream tools gives it leverage, but also makes it a target in an escalating tech-security standoff.

AI capability advances to 62.5 as frontier labs outside China accelerate automation to compensate for hardware scarcity, deploying AI-designed chips and optimised training stacks. Openweight capability rises to 50.0, as replication efforts intensify and partial model leaks from private systems surface in underground forums. Yet safety declines to 21.0, as rapid deployment outpaces oversight and opaque coordination patterns evade detection.

The **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** concludes, achieving partial hardening across energy and health sectors. Mandatory audits and fallback systems are now standard in 42% of Tier 1 operators, with red-teaming protocols institutionalised in five national grids. Resilience rises to 48.0 (+3 from completion), absorbing some of the disruption from the crisis.

In response to the blockade, the EU launches the **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative**, a small category 4 measure aimed at securing existing compute clusters under enforceable jurisdiction. Framed as a pre-emptive safeguard, it mandates audits and failover planning for high-capacity facilities. While legally grounded in the Chips Act, U.S.-linked cloud providers push back, citing sovereignty concerns. The initiative is accepted in principle but implementation delays emerge.

Political capital holds steady at 42.0. The portfolio charge is −6 (Tech Sovereignty Package −3, new initiative −2, priority −1). The completed resilience sprint delivers +5, offsetting costs. No proposal bonus applies — the blockade was external and recent, but the measure does not fully counter its root cause. Public sentiment remains flat at 34.0, as anxiety over disruption balances against visible action.

Sovereignty rises slightly to 25.0: the Tech Sovereignty Package finishes this turn (+5), but AI capability rose by 1.5 (below 2.0, so no decay), and no capacity event triggers an additional term.
