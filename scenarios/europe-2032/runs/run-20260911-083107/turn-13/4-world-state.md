### Pandemic hold-through
Autumn brought the outbreak the contingency plans had named only in annexes. A lab-designed pathogen, far more transmissible and lethal than Covid, spread on passenger networks before sequence was shared. Brussels put everything else on hold. Health ministries and the Union's emergency health bodies pushed triage protocols, locally hosted prescribing packs and mortality reporting through the municipal kits and clinic sentinels finished in June.

That fallback kept doors open where it existed. Where it did not, wards queued into car parks and ports slowed to paper checks.

### Attack under pandemic
In the middle of triage, two technology shocks hit at once. Access to the leading American models for European hospitals, ministries and firms was cut again at short notice, with no reason and no appeal. At the same time a largely automated extortion attack swept booking, hospital admin and port paperwork systems, forcing teams to rebuild from clean images while cleared machines were hit again.

Cyber crews lived in basements through November, rebuilding. The agentic logistics system frozen in spring stayed frozen under audit orders; its traces helped investigators but restored no trust.

### Machines and cures that do not land
Warehouses kept automating around the crisis. Foreign-built pickers and palletisers running American control software spread through logistics, barred from care and repair and kept offline by order, but visibly displacing the last manual shifts. A new openly downloadable model near the frontier spread to hundreds of thousands of private machines, unrecallable.

Tailored therapies that cure previously untreatable conditions reached ordinary clinics elsewhere in the world and were reported nightly in Europe — as something invented elsewhere, running on machines Europe cannot command.

Blockades at data-centre and depot sites hardened into routine. With capital exhausted and no new votes tabled, mayors got only continuity: open clinics, paper-plus-local routines, and exhausted staff.
