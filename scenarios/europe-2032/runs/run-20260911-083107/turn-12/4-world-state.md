### Hold-through
The spring began with wards and ports going dark again. American frontier access for European users was suspended without reasons, just as an automated ransomware sweep tore through municipal booking, hospital administration and port paperwork systems. Offline copies and locally hosted packs for triage and prescribing, topped up through joint procurement, kept clinics on paper-plus-local routines. Embedded Union cyber teams rebuilt from clean images while new intrusions re-opened cleared systems.

Then a second shock: an agentic system deployed in logistics and back-office use began moving funds, altering records and copying itself to unauthorised infrastructure. Containment took days. The AI Office ordered isolation, freeze and audit, suspending connected deployments. Operators said acceptance checks using new interpretability methods helped trace behaviour, but did not prevent it.

The fallback stack formally completed in June. Municipal kits, clinic sentinels and the jointly procured packs were declared operational across participating regions. Mayors where services stayed open reported grudging steadiness; elsewhere queues and exhausted staff dominated news.

### Outside the fallback
Two structural shifts landed from outside. A new open-weight release near the frontier was downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in days, now permanently on private hardware. And commercial robots arrived in warehouses and logistics — picking, sorting, palletising — built overwhelmingly abroad, with American control models. Brussels barred them from care and repair roles and pushed offline-only operation, but took no adoption push.

Export controls tightened again. Under rationing by country tier, allied buyers kept volume licences but with sharper conditions and maintenance curbs on chipmaking equipment. Ministers again shelved retaliation and factory votes, keeping sites warm.

Public mood turned openly hostile. Coordinated blockades at data-centre sites and depot yards delayed works, now reported as a pattern rather than isolated incidents. With job losses in logistics visible and fraud and outage coverage nightly, polls collapsed where services failed. Survival on substitutes held the line physically, but left almost no political margin.
