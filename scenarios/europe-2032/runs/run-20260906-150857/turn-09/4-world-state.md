### The Frontier Moves On

Global AI capability advances to 72.5 as US labs refine recursive self-improvement loops in real-world control systems. While not a sudden jump, the trend consolidates a dangerous gap: models now design their own training regimes faster than human oversight can audit them. No collapse occurs — yet — but the margin for error narrows.

An open-weight release circulates widely, originating from a partial leak of a frontier model’s weights. The release enables rapid downstream adaptation, particularly in Europe, where hobbyist and academic groups replicate stripped-down versions of advanced capabilities. However, the absence of full training infrastructure and compute constraints limits replication fidelity. As a result, `openweight_capability` rises to 56.0 — a modest gain reflecting diffusion, not parity. The gap with `ai_capability` remains significant, and no event narrates a collapse in frontier containment or mass leakage that would enable faster catch-up.

### Bio-Watch Launches Amid Backlash

The European Bio-AI Watch officially launches from Warsaw in a symbolic bid for legitimacy, with Polish health officials co-presenting its first public threat dashboard. The €1.2bn fund is disbursed, and local bio-risk councils begin forming. Yet legal disputes persist, and Poland stops short of full operational integration. The move softens resistance but does not end it.

Simultaneously, an AI-supported social insurance system is exposed for systematically denying benefits based on flawed risk profiling, affecting tens of thousands. An ombudsman’s report confirms discriminatory outcomes across three member states. Public fury mounts, and trust in AI governance plummets further.

### Redress Initiative Proposed, Trust Still Broken

In response, the EU proposes a sweeping Redress and Transparency Initiative, mandating explainability, appeal rights, and centralised reporting for all AI in public services. Germany and France back fast-tracking, but smaller states warn of administrative burdens. The proposal enters consultation, but no immediate sentiment shift occurs.

Despite finishing the Bio-AI Watch, gains are offset by deeper erosion. Resilience dips as open biological risks widen faster than defences scale. AI safety falls sharply due to the capability surge and lack of assurance on deployed systems. Sovereignty declines again under persistent backlash and dependency. Political capital erodes from ongoing costs and internal scandal, despite the new measure offering long-term hope.
