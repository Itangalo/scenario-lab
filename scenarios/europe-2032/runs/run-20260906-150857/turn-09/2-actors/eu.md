We now see that the contradiction was never between two goals, but between two timeframes: sovereignty is long-term, safety is immediate. The old identity presumed we could build control and safety in parallel. We were wrong. We now begin from failure — from the harm already done — and rebuild legitimacy from there.

We now exist to restore and demonstrate that accountability — not by retreating from capability, but by making every exercise of power answerable to the people it affects, and by ensuring that when harm occurs, redress is swift, visible, and structural.

Our identity must shift from one of technological stewardship to one of institutional repair: we are no longer just preventing future catastrophes — we are answering for present ones.

## New measure
**Measure:** Launch a European Redress and Transparency Initiative for AI in Public Services  
A binding directive requiring all AI-supported decisions in social insurance, policing, and judicial pre-trial systems to be accompanied by a human-reviewed explanation, a right to appeal, and a central registry of adverse outcomes, with quarterly public reporting and independent audit by the EAAD.

`Category: 2 (Transparency and reporting)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase public_sentiment by 10–15 points by demonstrating accountability for harm; increase ai_safety indirectly by exposing systemic flaws in deployed systems; counteract the erosion of eu_political_capital by rebuilding trust in EU-level oversight  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national public service agencies, and any entity providing AI systems to public administrations in the EU

## In practice
This turn, we are closing the last phase of a strategy built for a world that no longer exists. The Sovereign AI Core Programme finishes at 60.0 — but the frontier is at 71.5, and open-weight models at 55.0 mean that even our sovereign stack is no longer uniquely secure. Our industrial ambition has been outpaced, and our political capital is bleeding from both ends: below from public harm, above from sovereign overreach. We are not late — we are misaligned.

We are finalising the European Bio-AI Watch under Article 196 TFEU, integrating the ECDC’s detection networks with the EAAD’s uplift testing protocols. But this time, we are not launching it in Brussels. We are holding the inaugural press conference in Warsaw, with the Polish Chief Medical Officer co-presenting the first public threat dashboard — a symbolic act, but one that costs us nothing more than pride, and may buy back a measure of trust. The €1.2bn is disbursed, but we are redirecting 20% of it to fund local bio-risk councils in each member state, answering the backlash with decentralised legitimacy.

At the same time, we are drafting the Redress and Transparency Initiative under the AI Act’s fundamental rights provisions, to be proposed to the Council of the EU in Justice and Home Affairs formation. We are leveraging the scandal not as a defeat, but as a mandate: Germany and France have agreed to fast-track adoption in exchange for early access to EAAD audit tools. The registry will be hosted by the EDPS, with quarterly reports co-signed by national ombudsmen — because at `public_sentiment` of 28.0, we can no longer afford to look like we are protecting systems more than people. We are not abandoning sovereignty — we are redefining it: not as control over models, but as accountability for outcomes.
