## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without social legitimacy is a fortress without a people. The moment has passed for control alone to suffice: we are now in a phase where the frontier moves faster than institutions can absorb it, and our legitimacy must grow not from containment, but from demonstrated stewardship — protecting not only systems, but the public we serve.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* to 67.0 has created a structural lag: the EU’s sovereign stack (SACP) is still targeting 60.0 by turn 8, while the world has already vaulted beyond. At the same time, the *bio_uplift_findings* — though still contested — signal that the next threshold may not be cryptanalysis or materials, but biology. Our identity must now encompass not just dual mandates, but dual speeds: we are building slowly, while the frontier explodes. This demands not just control, but credibility — and credibility flows from visible protection of life, not just of systems.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and we will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; nor will we assume that control justifies silence, and we will now couple every containment measure with a public explanation of its purpose and limits. We now add: we will also act where harm is probable, not only where it is proven, and we will treat uplifted biological risk as a trigger for sovereign pre-emption, even without consensus.

  - Grounds: The *bio_uplift_findings* represent a categorically stronger signal than prior warnings. While not yet a release, they demonstrate a credible path to engineered human pathogens via model assistance. Given the lag in our sovereign capability and the US posture of rationing, we cannot rely on external restraint. The duty to act under uncertainty now includes acting *before* the gate opens — especially when the gate leads to bioweapons-scale risk.

- retire `two_year_commitment`
- add `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent catastrophic misuse through sovereign control of high-capability AI systems, with enforceable containment, real-time monitoring, public accountability for all systems above 55.0 capability, and now, a dedicated biological threat shield: sovereign red-teaming of genome models, mandatory uplift testing for all systems above 60.0, and a European Bio-AI Watch to detect and respond to precursor signals.

  - Grounds: Trigger: the *bio_uplift_findings* and the *capability_jump* together create a new risk regime. The former proves that non-experts are nearing dangerous capabilities; the latter proves that the frontier is accelerating beyond sovereign reach. Our commitment must evolve from general containment to targeted pre-emption — especially in biotechnology, where the cost of failure is irreversible. This is not a shift in direction, but a necessary deepening: we cannot control the frontier, so we must harden the most vulnerable thresholds.

## In practice

This turn, we are pivoting from general assurance to targeted survival. The *capability_jump* to 67.0 has shattered our timeline: the SACP’s target of 60.0 by turn 8 is no longer sufficient, and the *member_state_defection* proves that trust in centralised control is fraying. But the *bio_uplift_findings* — a genome model producing a viable human-infective design — have shifted the axis of risk. We are no longer preparing for misuse; we are racing to prevent an irreversible threshold from being crossed.

We are launching the European Bio-AI Watch under Article 196 TFEU (public health), co-funded from Horizon Europe and the Health Programme, with €1.2bn over three years. It will integrate real-time monitoring from the ECDC, uplift testing protocols from the EAAD, and red-teaming capacity from the SACP’s agent stack. We are mandating that all models above 60.0 capability operating in the EU undergo quarterly uplift assessments — a new standard we will enforce through the AI Act’s market access provisions. The first exercise, “Project Chimera,” will simulate a lab leak scenario using a redacted model stack, with results reported to the European Parliament and the public.

We are also closing out five major initiatives this turn: the Gigafactories, the Tech Sovereignty Package, EAAD, CIRS, and the Public Assurance Protocol. Their completion frees political capital — just enough — to fund this new priority. The Swedish InvestAI site is now operational, and we are fast-tracking the second cluster in Wrocław as a joint EU-Poland sovereign node, leveraging the €10bn resilience grant to rebuild cohesion. But we know the real test is not compute or code — it is whether the public sees us as protecting life, not just systems. That is why we are coupling the Bio-AI Watch with a public-facing threat dashboard, updated quarterly, and why we are reporting every uplift test, even negative ones. The frontier is moving faster than we can build. So we must become faster at showing we are not asleep.
