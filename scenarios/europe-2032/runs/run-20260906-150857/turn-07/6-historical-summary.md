AI capability reaches 70.0 following a US lab breakthrough in self-improving agents, further undermining existing safety frameworks and widening Europe’s strategic deficit. The EU retires completed initiatives, including the InvestAI Gigafactories and Public Assurance Protocol, and launches the European Bio-AI Watch under public health law to monitor high-capability models with biological access, in coordination with the ECDC. Legal and governance delays slow implementation, while public sentiment rises slightly due to transparency from “Project Chimera,” though protests emerge over concerns of centralized control. Poland remains partially aligned, with ongoing talks on the Wrocław cluster. The US maintains its tiered AI access policy, deepening European dependency. Political capital recovers modestly amid renewed strategic focus, but sovereignty declines as external advancements outpace domestic progress. Unofficial talks intensify among France, Germany, Japan, and Taiwan to circumvent chip export controls, while national bioethics boards raise legal objections to mandatory uplift testing, questioning its scope and jurisdiction.
