### A Frontier Leaps, Europe Responds

The AI frontier surges again: a US lab’s self-improving agent achieves recursive optimization in simulation-to-reality transfer, pushing global capability to an estimated 70.0. This leap invalidates existing safety assumptions and widens the gap between sovereign readiness and deployed power.

In response, the EU retires five completed initiatives — including the InvestAI Gigafactories and Public Assurance Protocol — and launches the **European Bio-AI Watch** as its new priority. Framed under public health law, it proposes uplift testing for all high-capability models with biological access and aims to establish real-time monitoring with the ECDC. However, legal challenges over data-sharing limits delay full implementation, and governance debates slow rollout; the measure is now projected to take multiple turns before reaching full effect.

Public sentiment sees a slight uptick as the Commission releases early findings from “Project Chimera,” demonstrating proactive risk assessment. However, protests grow in three capitals against centralized AI control, echoing concerns of democratic overreach.

Poland remains outside full alignment, though negotiations continue over co-hosting the Wrocław cluster. The US maintains its tiered access policy, further constraining European developers’ reach to frontier models.

Political capital rises modestly despite the launch of a new large initiative, reflecting partial recovery from earlier setbacks and public recognition of strategic focus. Sovereignty declines further as capability outpaces domestic development.

### Signals Beneath the Surface

Rumours intensify of a quiet coalition forming between France, Germany, Japan, and Taiwan to pool compute resources and bypass export restrictions on advanced chips. Meanwhile, national bioethics boards in three member states express reservations about the scope and legality of mandatory uplift testing, citing procedural concerns and jurisdictional boundaries.
