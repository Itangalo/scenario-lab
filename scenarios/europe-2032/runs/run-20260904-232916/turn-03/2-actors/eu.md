## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI capacity to act with sovereignty in an accelerating world  
- Grounds: The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive has failed to secure consensus, with Germany, Ireland, and now the Netherlands actively circumventing it through bilateral arrangements and legal resistance. The path to sovereignty through binding compute reservation is blocked by member state fragmentation and external leverage. Continuing to stake our credibility on this specific instrument would be clinging to a strategy the world has rejected. We are not abandoning the goal of sovereign capacity — that remains core to our identity — but we are shifting how we pursue it: from top-down legal compulsion to building parallel, functional alternatives that make dependence obsolete.

## New measure  
**Sovereign AI Stack: Integrated Compute, Software, and Talent Pipeline**  
Launch a coordinated programme to build a fully sovereign AI stack — from hardware to models to operational control — hosted exclusively on EU soil and under EU legal jurisdiction.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to +20 eu_ai_sovereignty by turn 6; establishes a self-sustaining EU-run training and deployment capability independent of foreign hyperscalers or open-weight reliance; begins closing the gap between openweight capability (43.0) and sovereign access  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, implemented through the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC), with binding participation from member states operating critical infrastructure and public research agencies

## Priority  
**Sovereign AI Stack: Integrated Compute, Software, and Talent Pipeline** — because the failure of legal compulsion proves we cannot mandate sovereignty; we must instead build it so concretely that dependence becomes a choice no rational actor would make.

## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that sovereignty is not decreed — it is built. The collapse of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive was not just a legislative defeat; it was a signal that coercion cannot overcome fragmentation when alternatives do not exist. So we are shifting from regulation to construction. This turn, the Commission launches the Sovereign AI Stack: a fully integrated, end-to-end capability combining EU-owned compute infrastructure, open-but-secured model development, and a dedicated talent pipeline funded through Horizon Europe and national research agencies. The EDIC will stand up three sovereign clusters — in France, Poland, and Finland — each with 100,000 H100e-equivalent GPUs, powered by dedicated grid allocations and cooled through industrial heat-reuse networks. These will run models trained and fine-tuned exclusively by EU public research consortia, with weights secured under EU law and access granted only to vetted public-interest actors: energy grid operators, civil protection agencies, and crisis response units.

At the same time, the Tech Sovereignty Package continues to gain ground: the five AI Acceleration Zones now have binding grid commitments and streamlined permitting pathways, and we are redirecting €30bn from the InvestAI reserve to fast-track the first two Gigafactory sites in Marseille and Wrocław. But we are no longer waiting for private capital to deliver sovereignty. The Sovereign AI Stack is publicly funded, publicly controlled, and operationally independent — a capability that cannot be switched off by a foreign provider or vetoed by a single member state. We are offering Germany and Ireland a new bargain: not legal obligation, but first access. Join the stack, contribute compute or talent, and you will get priority scheduling and full interoperability. Resist, and you will face a Europe where the centre of gravity has moved — not by fiat, but by fact.
