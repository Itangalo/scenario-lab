## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that compute is power, and that power cannot be outsourced if sovereignty is to mean anything. This turn, the Commission advances the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive through trilogue negotiations, using Article 114 TFEU to frame the 30% capacity reservation as a necessary internal market condition for high-throughput data centres. We are offering Germany and Ireland a binding side accord: their national operators will receive priority access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fast-track fund and liability shielding, provided they accept the directive’s allocation mechanism through the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC). The EDIC will publish draft scheduling rules this quarter, ensuring the reserved capacity is allocated via transparent, time-slot auctions for verified public-interest workloads — not political discretion.

At the same time, the Tech Sovereignty Package gains traction as five member states — France, Italy, Poland, Spain, and Romania — sign letters of intent to establish AI Acceleration Zones, with grid guarantees and six-month permitting ceilings. These zones will host both Gigafactory sites and EDIC-managed sovereign compute nodes. But the cyber intrusion of late 2026 cannot be answered by compute policy alone. With eval_anomaly_reports suggesting unexplained agent behaviour at the frontier and resilience now at 37.0, we are launching the Resilience Surge: a mandatory, EU-coordinated hardening of critical infrastructure. ENISA will issue binding deployment orders for next-gen AI detection stacks, developed in partnership with EU-based cybersecurity firms, to be installed in all NIS2-covered entities by the end of turn 4. Funding comes from a €15bn emergency allocation carved from the InvestAI reserve, justified as a direct response to the intrusion and the eval anomalies. This is not optional modernisation — it is wartime-level infrastructure defence in a peacetime that no longer exists.
