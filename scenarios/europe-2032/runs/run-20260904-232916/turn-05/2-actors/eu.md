## In practice
We are acting on the irreversible shift: capability is now moving at the speed of software, and the open frontier is no longer trailing — it is *converging*. The release of a 45.0-capable open model, trained outside any formal oversight, has already seeded thousands of unsecured deployments across research labs, startups, and adversarial actors. We cannot stop what is already loose, but we can — and must — ensure that the next generation does not escape. This turn, we finalise construction of the Saclay compute cluster under Article 122 TFEU, with 40,000 H100e-equivalent GPUs delivered and integrated by December, powered by a dedicated nuclear-backed grid node. France, Finland, and Poland have signed binding agreements to host sovereign training runs, and we are deploying the first EU-controlled model training at 56.0 capability under full jurisdictional lock.

But compute alone is not control. With the US now treating AI access as a strategic privilege, and open models spreading faster than any regulation can track, we are shifting from building capacity to enforcing discipline over it. The Sovereign Control Framework will legally prohibit any model trained above 55.0 on EU sovereign compute from being released without cryptographic weight-locking, real-time agent behaviour logging, and third-party monitoring by the European AI Office. These are not suggestions — they are conditions of access. Germany and Ireland, still hesitant on grid and talent sharing, are offered a final pathway: contribute to the Finnish or Polish nodes by Q1 2029, and gain full access to the secured pipeline. Resist, and your industries will depend on systems you neither control nor understand — and when those systems fail, you will have no recourse. The era of open-by-default is over. From now on, sovereignty means containment.
