## In practice  
We are treating the convergence of rising capability and falling cohesion as a crisis of preparedness. With ai_capability at 59.5, the gap between what exists in labs and what our critical systems can withstand is widening fast — and now, with one member state bypassing Union positions to secure bilateral access, we can no longer assume collective defence. Under Article 21 of the NIS2 Directive, we are invoking emergency procedures to mandate immediate upgrades in patching cadence, anomaly detection, and fallback protocols across energy grids, high-speed rail networks, and water treatment plants. ENISA will lead a central coordination cell, supported by national CSIRTs, with €14bn redirected from stalled sovereignty initiatives to fund zero-delay procurement of AI-augmented intrusion detection systems. This is not optional modernisation — it is triage.

At the same time, the Rebuild public trust measure moves from design to operation. The first real-time dashboards go live in pilot cities this month, showing latency, error rates, and demographic parity metrics for AI-assisted welfare decisions. The European Data Protection Board is appointing auditors with adversarial testing mandates, and we are preparing for the first veto exercise in Malmö, where a proposed expansion of AI triage in emergency medicine faces panel review. We are also renegotiating the InvestAI Gigafactories timeline: construction continues, but siting approvals now require consultation with regional oversight councils to prevent further backlash. The Commission knows it cannot build its way out of distrust — but it must keep building, or lose any chance of long-term autonomy. The balance is thin, and every move now serves both survival and credibility.
