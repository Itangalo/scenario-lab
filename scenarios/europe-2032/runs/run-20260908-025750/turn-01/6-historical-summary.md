A coordinated cyber intrusion across European energy, port, and water systems—detected by chance—revealed the growing threat of AI-enabled attacks and triggered a major EU response. The Commission launched the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators, a binding NIS2 directive mandating real-time detection, automated response, and red-teaming for essential infrastructure. Backed by increased Cohesion Fund allocations and preferential InvestAI access, the initiative faced resistance from smaller states over costs and from energy firms opposing operational mandates. The Energy Council deadlocked, but the measure passed via the Internal Market formation using qualified majority voting, though implementation delays are expected in at least four member states.

Globally, AI capability advanced rapidly, reaching 53.5 due to accelerated development cycles and AI-driven research, while AI safety stagnated at 32.0 amid a lack of enforceable safety standards. The cyber incident highlighted systemic vulnerabilities, and despite ongoing initiatives like InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package, EU AI sovereignty declined by 1 point due to lagging domestic capacity. The heavy regulatory push cost significant political capital—−10 overall—dropping it to 40.0, with no offsetting gains. Public sentiment remained stable at 42.0, as security concerns temporarily outweighed growing anxiety over job displacement.
