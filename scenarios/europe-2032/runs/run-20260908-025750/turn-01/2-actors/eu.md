## New measure
Cyber Shield for Critical Operators  
A binding directive requiring real-time intrusion detection, automated response protocols, and mandatory red-teaming for all operators of essential services in energy, water, transport, and digital infrastructure.

## Priority
Cyber Shield for Critical Operators — this measure responds directly to the cyber_test_shot event, which exposed critical gaps in detection and response across vital infrastructure; without urgent hardening, the Union’s ability to absorb AI-enabled attacks will erode faster than sovereignty can be built.

## In practice
We are acting on the intrusion discovered this autumn not as an isolated breach but as a signal: the frontier’s capabilities are now diffusing into tools that can bypass traditional defences. This turn, we launch the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators under the NIS2 Directive, using Article 21 to issue binding obligations. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will draft technical standards by February 2027, with member states required to transpose them into national law by mid-2028. We are targeting a 90-day implementation window for detection systems and quarterly red-teaming cycles, funded through the Digital Europe Programme and matched by national co-financing.

Resistance is expected from smaller member states citing cost and capacity, and from some energy firms arguing that existing protocols are sufficient. To overcome this, we are offering a 40% uplift in Cohesion Fund allocations for digital resilience to participating states and fast-tracking access to InvestAI compute subsidies for compliant operators. The Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formation will vote in December under a qualified majority, bypassing stalled consensus in the Energy Council. This is not about catching up — it is about ensuring that the EU’s critical systems do not become the soft underbelly through which external AI capabilities exert strategic control.
