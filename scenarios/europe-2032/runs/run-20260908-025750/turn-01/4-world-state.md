### A Signal in the Dark
The discovery of a coordinated intrusion across energy grids, a port authority, and a water utility sent shockwaves through European security circles. Though no systems were disrupted, the depth and stealth of the access—revealed only by chance—confirmed worst-case assumptions: AI-enabled offensive tools had evolved beyond signature-based detection. The European Union responded swiftly, declaring a strategic priority on cyber resilience.

### Cyber Shield Launched
The Commission introduced the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators, a binding directive under NIS2 requiring real-time intrusion detection, automated response, and mandatory red-teaming for essential infrastructure. ENISA began drafting technical standards, backed by a 40% uplift in Cohesion Fund allocations for compliant states and preferential access to InvestAI compute. The move aimed to harden the Union’s weakest links before the next incident.

However, resistance flared. Smaller member states questioned the fiscal burden, while major energy firms lobbied against operational mandates, arguing that existing protocols were sufficient. The Energy Council deadlocked, forcing the Commission to bypass it via the Internal Market formation, where qualified majority voting allowed the measure to pass in December. Implementation will be uneven, with delays expected in at least four member states.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Shrinks
Despite the assertive stance, the EU’s strategic position weakened. AI capability advanced globally, driven by compressed release cycles and agent-driven research, pushing `ai_capability` to 53.5. With no safety measures landing on shipped systems and the gap between capability and control widening, `ai_safety` dropped to 32.0. The `cyber_test_shot` incident, while not catastrophic, underscored systemic fragility.

The `InvestAI Gigafactories` and `Tech Sovereignty Package` remained in flight, yielding no immediate sovereignty gains. Meanwhile, rising frontier capability without equivalent domestic capacity eroded `eu_ai_sovereignty` by 1 point. Political capital fell sharply: the large new measure cost −3, the priority −1, and the two existing large measures −6 total, for a portfolio charge of −10. With no offsetting gains, capital dropped to 40.0.

Public sentiment held at 42.0, as job displacement concerns grew but were overshadowed by security fears.
