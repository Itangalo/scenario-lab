## New measure

## Priority  
**ASML Sovereignty Guarantee** — because securing control over the tools that make the chips is now the decisive bottleneck in our two-year commitment to autonomous AI capability, and because US pressure has revealed that access to even mature-node equipment cannot be taken for granted, making this a now-or-never moment for industrial sovereignty.

## In practice  
We are executing the ASML Sovereignty Guarantee through a dual-track approach: legally under the European Chips Act and the Anti-Coercion Instrument, and financially through the InvestEU framework and the European Innovation Council’s strategic investment arm. The Commission, led by the Vice-President for Values and Transparency and supported by the Internal Market Commissioner, is in direct talks with ASML’s board, offering fast-tracked permitting for its own expansion in Eindhoven and access to InvestAI capital in exchange for embedding EU governance rights—co-ownership, veto rights on relocations, and audit access—into the contracts governing lithography tools deployed in EU fabs. Parallel negotiations are underway with national governments in Germany, France, and Italy to co-invest in redundancy units for mature-node production, targeting 30% of capex in exchange for guaranteed access during supply shocks.

Domestically, we are managing backlash from the campaign coalition by reinforcing that this is not about unchecked AI expansion, but about securing the physical foundations of digital sovereignty. We are also relying on the European Interpretability Initiative’s progress to maintain credibility on safety: the first verification tools are being piloted in public AI deployments, and we are preparing a joint publication with ENISA to demonstrate that transparency and sovereignty are reinforcing, not competing. With the Cyber Shield now operational, we are redirecting some of the freed technical bandwidth toward monitoring supply-chain cyber risks in semiconductor logistics, though resilience gains remain incremental. The mood in the Council is tense; cohesion hinges on whether southern and eastern members continue to see this as a strategic necessity rather than a northern industrial subsidy. We are holding firm: if we cannot make the machines, we will never control the intelligence they produce.
