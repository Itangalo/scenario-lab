## In practice  
We are treating the collapse of the ASML deal and the failure of the Interpretability Initiative not as isolated setbacks but as symptoms of a deeper legitimacy deficit. The Tech sovereignty package remains in flight, but its credibility depends on political cohesion, which is fraying. The InvestAI Gigafactories continue construction, but siting delays and protests threaten timelines. With the ASML Sovereignty Guarantee and European Interpretability Initiative now finished—and having delivered no operational capability or control tools—we must stabilise the political ground beneath us.

We are launching the new measure through the AI Act’s Article 28a reporting framework, expanding it to mandate real-time transparency for all AI systems in health, education, and social services. The European Data Protection Board will host public dashboards; ENISA will conduct quarterly audits; and pilot cities—starting with Lisbon, Budapest, and Malmö—will host citizen oversight panels with veto rights over system expansion. Funding comes from repurposing €18bn in stalled sovereignty initiatives, redirected via comitology. This is not symbolic: we are binding the operation of public AI to public scrutiny, because trust must now be earned in the open, not assumed from authority. The Commission will report progress to the June European Council, where cohesion hangs on whether southern and eastern states see this as inclusion, not control.
