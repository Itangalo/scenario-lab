The EU enters 2029 politically weakened, with the collapse of the ASML Sovereignty Guarantee and the failed European Interpretability Initiative leaving four major initiatives stalled. Political capital, at 11.0, barely avoids paralysis, forcing a strategic pivot from infrastructure to legitimacy. Amid rising public backlash, including sabotage of data centers, the Commission launches a transparency and accountability framework under the AI Act, introducing third-party audits, real-time dashboards, and citizen oversight panels in pilot cities—Lisbon, Budapest, and Malmö—with veto rights in key public sectors. Funding is redirected from stalled sovereignty projects to avoid new budget approvals.

However, the tech sovereignty package yields no immediate gains, and reliance on foreign-controlled supply chains persists. With no category 4 measures completed and global AI capability rising to 59.0, EU AI sovereignty declines further. Public sentiment sees a fragile rebound due to improved public-sector AI performance and the transparency push, but physical backlash highlights ongoing instability. As the U.S. prepares to implement stricter AI export controls, the EU faces heightened external pressure and diminished strategic autonomy.
