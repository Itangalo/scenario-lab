### Cyber Shield Lands, ASML Gambit Begins

The Cyber Shield for Critical Operators directive completes implementation, mandating real-time intrusion detection and automated response across energy, water, transport, and digital infrastructure. Early audits show improved detection latency and coordinated red-teaming becoming routine, marking the EU’s first systemic upgrade to digital resilience in years.

Buoyed by this, the European Union launches the ASML Sovereignty Guarantee, a bold bid to shield mature-node lithography access from foreign interference. The plan offers co-ownership of critical equipment, 30% co-investment in fab expansions in Germany, France, and Italy, and legal protections under the Anti-Coercion Instrument. ASML engages cautiously, eyeing access to InvestAI capital and faster permitting. However, Washington signals strong disapproval, warning that the move undermines allied coordination.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Keeps Pace

Global AI capability advances to 56.5, driven by iterative improvements in agent coordination and software automation. The unified evaluation framework, now informally adopted by several mid-tier developers, contributes to a modest safety gain, as interpretability tools begin to inform compliance checks. No major incidents occur, and the framework’s growing credibility supports incremental trust in model behaviour.

### Political Strain Persists

The completion of Cyber Shield frees political capital, but the new large measure—paired with the priority tag—imposes a heavy burden. Member states are divided: southern and eastern members support the sovereignty play, while others fear trade retaliation. Public sentiment inches up slightly, as the narrative of securing foundational technology resonates with industrial communities, though job displacement concerns persist.
