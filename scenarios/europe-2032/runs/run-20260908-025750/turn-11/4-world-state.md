### The Open Frontier

A new open-weight model, nearly matching the closed frontier in capability, spread globally within days of release. Downloaded by researchers, hobbyists and hostile actors alike, it now runs on distributed consumer hardware across continents. With `ai_capability` rising to 64.0, the gap has collapsed: open systems can now execute sustained agent-like tasks with real-world impact, including designing biologically plausible pathogens and automating cyber-intrusion chains. Governance through developer control is effectively over.

However, adoption at scale remains limited by hardware access and deployment know-how. While the model’s existence marks a threshold, its operational footprint grows gradually. `openweight_capability` increases only to 53.0, reflecting early but uneven uptake — a +1.0 rise consistent with diffusion timelines for prior open models under similar infrastructure constraints.

### EU Responds: Compute as Sovereignty

In response, the European Union announces exploratory planning for a **Sovereign Compute Surge**, aiming to anchor AI sovereignty in physical infrastructure. The initiative proposes exascale clusters under EU legal jurisdiction, backed by energy guarantees and new obligations for foreign hyperscalers. A draft Sovereign Compute Directive would require any high-capability system used in public services to be runnable in a verifiably sovereign environment.

However, no formal launch occurs. With the Emergency Biocontainment Programme still active and consuming −3 portfolio charge, and naming a new priority prohibited under current rules, the surge cannot advance beyond concept stage this turn. The political cost of initiating a category 4 measure is not paid; legitimacy remains too weak to absorb additional burden, and no bonus applies.

Still, the signal is clear: the EU seeks to shift strategy — from regulating access to securing execution. Preliminary design work and interagency coordination begin quietly, laying groundwork that may support future action.

### Fragile Gains Amid Crisis

The newly ratified middle-power coalition activated joint biosurveillance protocols after early warnings in two member states, enabling rapid containment of suspicious synthesis orders. This **joint threat response** boosts `resilience` by +3, reaching 49.0, as coordinated detection proves more effective than unilateral action.

Yet trust lingers at rock bottom. Public sentiment holds at 24.0, reflecting widespread anxiety. Labour unrest grows in tech-adjacent sectors, and protests continue outside data centres. The interpretability pivot stalls, unable to keep pace with opaque, distributed models.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` rises slightly to 11.0: +1 for initial conceptual development of a category 4 measure without full commitment, offset by −1 due to rising `ai_capability`. No capacity event occurs. The gain reflects nascent activity, not implementation — symbolic preparation in the absence of resources or political headroom.
