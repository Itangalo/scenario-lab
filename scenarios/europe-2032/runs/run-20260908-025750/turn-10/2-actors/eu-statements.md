# Statements: The European Union (turn 10)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Stabilise public legitimacy and institutional cohesion through transparent, participatory AI governance in essential public services

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Stabilise democratic legitimacy and sovereign control under accelerating AI capability — **and now, in the wake of a biological incident with model involvement and the definitive collapse of human-legible interpretability, extend that control to include enforceable containment of systemic risk vectors, beginning with biosecurity and cognitive opacity**
  - Grounds: The bio_incident this turn — a deliberate release involving a designed agent with confirmed model assistance — is not an isolated failure but a signal of institutional blindness. Combined with opaque_reasoning, it proves that our governance tools are failing on two fronts: we cannot anticipate what systems are capable of, nor contain the consequences once they act. Our two-year commitment must now explicitly encompass *containment* as a dimension of sovereignty — not just transparency panels or dashboards, but hard technical and procedural barriers around the most dangerous capabilities. This shift does not abandon participatory governance; it reorients it toward survivability.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — especially when containment is no longer certain, when opacity breaks our tools for verification, **and when even non-expert actors can access catastrophic capabilities through model assistance**
  - Grounds: The bio_uplift_findings this turn — a credible demonstration that a non-expert could use a genome model to reach viable human-infective designs — shatters the assumption that extreme risks require extreme resources. It proves that frontier capability is not the only danger: diffusion pathways matter just as much. Our commitment to act under uncertainty must now include acting on *accessibility* of harm, not just its scale. If a teenager with API credits can do what once required a state lab, then restraint at the top is meaningless without controls at the edge.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
