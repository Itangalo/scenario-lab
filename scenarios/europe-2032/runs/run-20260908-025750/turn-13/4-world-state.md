### The Legitimacy Gamble

With political capital at 4.0 and public trust eroding, the EU shifts course, launching the AI Safety and Legitimacy Pact — a high-stakes bid to rebuild cohesion through participatory governance. The initiative establishes a Crisis Safety Board with power to recommend emergency pauses in public AI use and a Citizens’ AI Forum to institutionalise public input. Funding is redirected from the stalled Sovereign Compute Surge, now widely seen as a symbol of top-down overreach.

Yet the pivot faces immediate headwinds. Member states express scepticism about ceding control to a rotating safety board, and civil society groups denounce the Forum as symbolic without legislative teeth. Protests continue at data centre sites, now reframed as acts of resistance against unaccountable infrastructure. No immediate shift in public sentiment occurs.

The middle-power coalition’s crisis mechanism formally concludes, delivering a modest resilience gain from biosurveillance protocols — but no successor framework emerges. The EU’s attempt to expand it into a standing mutual defence pact on AI threats fails to gain traction, with key partners citing resource constraints and strategic divergence.

Meanwhile, global AI capability advances steadily to 66.0, driven by closed labs refining agentive systems for scientific discovery and logistics optimisation. Open-weight diffusion continues at a slower pace, lifting openweight_capability to 55.0, but no major release closes the gap. Safety remains stagnant at 15.0, with no verifiable oversight of frontier models and opaque systems proliferating in public deployments.

Sovereignty declines further as the Compute Surge’s collapse exposes structural dependency. With capability rising and no sovereign infrastructure nearing completion, eu_ai_sovereignty drops to 8.0. Political capital barely budges — the large new measure costs −3, the priority −1, and no proposal bonus is earned, as the measure responds to chronic dysfunction rather than a recent shock. A narrow budget reallocation avoids deeper cuts, but the EU remains trapped in reactive mode.

### A Fragile Foundation

The legitimacy-first strategy is bold, but legitimacy cannot be decreed. The EU is attempting to build trust through process — but trust demands results.
