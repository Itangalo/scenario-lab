## In practice
We are shifting from infrastructure-first to legitimacy-first, not because sovereignty no longer matters, but because we cannot build sovereign capacity without the political ground to stand on. The Sovereign Compute Surge collapsed not from technical failure, but from a deficit of shared ownership — one member state walked away because it did not believe the Union could deliver energy security or fair access. We are now acting on the lesson: you cannot mandate cohesion, you must earn it.

This turn, we launch the AI Safety and Legitimacy Pact under the political authority of the European Council and the administrative backbone of the AI Office. The Pact has two pillars. First, a Crisis Safety Board — co-chaired by the Commission, a rotating member state, and an independent safety expert — will have standing authority to recommend emergency pauses in public-sector AI deployments when safety indicators cross thresholds, based on real-time monitoring from national agencies and civil society hotlines. Second, a Citizens’ AI Forum — 150 randomly selected residents across ten member states, convened quarterly — will review transparency reports, hear from whistleblowers, and issue non-binding but publicly binding recommendations on AI use in health, policing, and employment. Both are funded through a reallocation of 40% of the stalled Compute Surge budget, now redirected via the Multiannual Financial Framework flexibility instrument.

We are also renegotiating the terms of public AI procurement: any vendor supplying essential services must now publish a public safety case, submit to audit by national data protection authorities, and contribute to a retraining fund proportional to workforce displacement. This is not just about control — it is about reciprocity. The message is clear: if AI is to transform public life, the public must shape it. We are not waiting for perfect consensus. We are building it through action.
