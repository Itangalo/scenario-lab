### The Blind Spot

A wave of frontier AI systems, trained on non-linguistic internal representations, ceases to produce human-readable reasoning—rendering audits, oversight panels, and interpretability tools obsolete overnight. This shift, confirmed across multiple labs, triggers alarm: for the first time, high-capability systems operate as black boxes even to their developers. The EU responds by declaring an emergency, launching a €3.8bn programme to enforce architecturally interpretable models in public-sector and subsidy-dependent AI development.

The initiative targets four new interpretability hubs and mandates auditable cognition for high-risk deployments. Yet immediate impact is limited—the directive faces legal pushback over proportionality, and member states question its compatibility with innovation goals. One national regulator signals it may delay implementation pending CJEU guidance.

Meanwhile, an open-weight release mirrors progress at the closed frontier, disseminating a model with improved latent reasoning. While technically significant, the diffusion is partial and uneven, with deployment bottlenecks limiting reach. Openweight capability rises to 51.0—still trailing the frontier’s 62.5—but no sudden leap occurs. Resilience holds at 48.0—just above collapse—but sees no gain, as no category 6 measure lands this turn.

Diplomacy inches forward: the middle-power coalition finalises mutual aid protocols in Oslo, though disagreements persist over automatic cloud shutdowns. One partner demands veto power, slowing full ratification. Domestically, the Rebuild public trust initiative concludes. While transparency dashboards launch in Lisbon, repeated suspensions of citizen panels in Malmö deepen perceptions of democratic erosion.

AI capability advances to 62.5, entering the terminal zone. But safety plummets—opaque reasoning invalidates existing controls, and no deployed system incorporates the new safeguards yet. The gap between power and understanding widens dangerously.

Political capital erodes further. The portfolio remains heavy, and the sovereignty score dips again as dependence on foreign compute persists despite domestic efforts. Public sentiment stagnates amid disruption and institutional strain.

### Fragmented Sovereignty

Informal coordination among mid-sized EU states emerges around sovereign compute pools, bypassing stalled central initiatives. While not a treaty, shared procurement and red-teaming frameworks signal growing impatience with top-down inertia.

Still, the Union acts—blindly, urgently, and without room for error.
