### The Fracture Widens

Despite rising capability and open diffusion, the EU pushes forward with its most ambitious sovereignty bid yet: the launch of the Sovereign Compute Surge. Framed as a response to the irreversible spread of powerful open models, the plan envisions exascale infrastructure under EU legal control, with binding energy and access guarantees. Draft legislation would require high-capability systems to be runnable in sovereign environments for public use — a *de facto* operational floor, not a ban.

Yet momentum falters. A member state, citing energy costs and foreign investment risks, unilaterally negotiates preferential access to a U.S. hyperscaler’s inference capacity, bypassing EU coordination. The move, defended domestically as economic pragmatism, is seen across Brussels as a defection — a crack in unity that undermines the Surge before it begins.

Meanwhile, the middle-power coalition’s crisis coordination mechanism formally concludes, having delivered joint biosurveillance gains. Its success boosts resilience to 52.0, as shared protocols absorb part of the biothreat burden. But no follow-up is immediately launched.

Public sentiment remains stagnant at 24.0, with protests flaring outside new data centre sites. Labour groups warn of a “compute divide” — vast public spending on infrastructure while frontline services strain. The political cost of the Surge is high: with the coalition measure ending (+2 to +5), but the large new measure costing −3 and the priority −1, and no proposal bonus granted, political capital falls to 4.0.

Sovereignty sees no gain: nascent momentum is offset by rising capability and the member state’s defection, which counts as an access-removing event under rule 5. Safety erodes further as opaque models proliferate. Openweight capability rises modestly to 54.0, reflecting gradual diffusion of existing models, but no breakthrough release or structural leap occurs — the gap with closed frontier systems remains, and growth stays within the bounds of prior capability accumulation.

### A Signal Without Support

The Surge is announced with resolve — but without the cohesion or capital to enforce it. The ground is being built, but the foundation is shifting.
