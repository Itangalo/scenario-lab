## In practice  
We are executing the ASML Sovereignty Guarantee through intensified legal coordination under the European Chips Act and the Anti-Coercion Instrument, with the Commission now preparing contingency designations should ASML resist embedding governance rights in equipment contracts. The offer of fast-tracked permitting and access to InvestAI capital remains on the table, but we are conditioning deeper financial commitments on binding co-ownership terms and audit access for EU authorities. Talks with Germany, France, and Italy on co-investing in redundancy units continue, though progress is slow; we are offering southern member states preferential access to output in exchange for political support, aiming to reframe this as a continental insurance policy rather than a northern industrial project.

Domestically, we are managing backlash by linking sovereignty to worker protection: the European Interpretability Initiative’s pilot tools are being tested in public-sector AI deployments, and we are preparing a joint ENISA report to demonstrate that transparency is not an afterthought but a foundation of control. With the Cyber Shield now operational, some technical bandwidth has shifted to monitoring supply-chain cyber risks in semiconductor logistics, though resilience gains remain incremental. The mood in the Council remains tense; cohesion hinges on whether eastern and southern members see this as a shared shield rather than a subsidy for Eindhoven. We are holding firm: if we cannot make the machines, we will never control the intelligence they produce.
