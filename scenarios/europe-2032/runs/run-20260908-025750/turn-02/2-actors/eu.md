## Priority  
**European Interpretability Initiative** — this measure captures a fleeting strategic window: a real safety breakthrough that the frontier labs themselves want to adopt, which we can turn into sovereign capability and enforceable standards before it diffuses unevenly or gets captured by external control points.

## In practice  
We are acting on the safety_breakthrough not as a technical footnote but as a pivot point: for the first time, a control method is both effective on deployed systems and in demand by developers. We will launch the European Interpretability Initiative under Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme, co-funded by the InvestAI instrument, to build out public capacity in model introspection and certification. The Joint Research Centre will lead a consortium with national labs, academic hubs like ELLIS, and vetted private partners to operationalise the technique, with deliverables tied to interpretable agent behaviour, runtime anomaly detection, and certifiable reasoning chains. By turn 5, we aim to have sovereign, auditable tools that can be required under the AI Act for high-risk systems.

This effort is urgent because the supply_chain_coercion event proves we cannot rely on external chokepoints — or trust that others will use them in our interest. The same logic applies upstream: if we do not anchor this safety advance in our own institutions, it will become another dependency. We are offering model developers fast-track compliance and reduced audit burdens if they integrate our open verification stack, creating pull alongside the push of regulation. Resistance from some industry players is expected — particularly those with closed pipelines — but the offer of regulatory efficiency and access to public compute subsidies will counterbalance the cost of adaptation. This is not just about safety; it is about making the EU a source of trusted, controllable AI, not just a rulemaker.
