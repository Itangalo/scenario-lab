## In practice
This turn, we are enforcing the reality that the EU cannot be sovereign over AI if it does not control the stack beneath it. The Trusted Infrastructure Integration Directive is now in flight, and we are acting through Article 114 TFEU to fast-track adoption in the Internal Market Council. ENISA has been instructed to issue technical standards by Q2 2031 defining “verifiable EU-controlled maintenance” — focusing first on firmware integrity, repair rights, and subsystem provenance for servers used in energy grid optimization, core financial messaging, and telecoms routing. National regulators are being convened to align inspection protocols, and we are preparing delegated acts that will make compliance a condition for operating in these sectors. The message is clear: if your AI runs critical functions in the EU, the hardware must be inspectable, maintainable, and ultimately controllable by EU authorities — no exceptions.

At the same time, we are closing the final phase of the Regional Oversight Integration Initiative. The €600 million in co-funding for data sovereignty hubs in Vilnius, Riga, and Bratislava is now tied to full API integration with the EU threat layer by the end of this turn. ENISA is offering co-ownership of the incident response logic — not just access — to address the trust deficit that has stalled cohesion. This is not a technical fix; it is a political investment in shared ownership of our defences. Without it, even the strongest directive will collapse under fragmentation. We are acting on the understanding that sovereignty is not only built in fabs and data centres, but in the daily trust between member states to act as one. We are using this turn to harden both — one through law, the other through shared stakes — because if we fail at either, we fail at both.
