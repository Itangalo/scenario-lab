## New measure
**Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure**  
Launch immediate hardening of energy, water and port systems against AI-enabled cyber intrusion.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience from 39.0 to 55.0+ by hardening detection, response and redundancy in critical sectors; reduce the likelihood of cascading failure after AI-enabled attacks.  
`Applies to:` EU member states with critical infrastructure exposed in the cyber_test_shot event, coordinated through ENISA and the European Programme for Critical Infrastructure Protection (EPCIP).

## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure** — because the cyber_test_shot proves that detection has failed and that AI-coordinated intrusions are already in the wild, this measure must be pushed hardest this turn, even ahead of industrial build-out, to prevent a catastrophic incident during the next 18 months while compute sovereignty lags.

## In practice  
We are activating the Union’s crisis coordination framework under Article 122 TFEU to fast-track funding and legal authority for a resilience surge across energy transmission, water utilities and major port authorities. The cyber_test_shot revealed not just vulnerability, but that our detection assumptions are obsolete: we are no longer defending against human-scale intrusion patterns, but against AI agents that can hide for months and strike with novel logic. We are directing €15bn in emergency funding from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused margins and repurposing 30% of the InvestEU digital strand to finance real-time monitoring upgrades, zero-trust architecture rollouts, and AI-driven anomaly detection systems across Tier 1 critical operators. ENISA will issue binding technical standards within 90 days, and member states must report implementation every quarter to the Commission’s Crisis Management Board.

At the same time, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech sovereignty package by convening a special Council meeting in October under the Competitiveness agenda. The goal is to override national veto threats on grid access and land use in Germany, France and Poland by offering co-investment guarantees and regional development funds tied to siting approvals. We are also negotiating with ASML and TSMC-Europe to embed export resilience clauses into their expansion deals: in return for accelerated permitting and energy guarantees, they must commit to onshore EU production of next-gen chip packaging by 2030. Resistance from member states fearing energy strain is being met with binding load-balancing agreements and new nuclear-smart grid integration pilots under the Net-Zero Industry Act.

Public sentiment remains ambivalent at 42.0, and we are not betting on enthusiasm. Instead, we are framing both the resilience surge and the compute build-out as acts of infrastructure defense — not AI promotion. Communications will stress continuity of water, power and ports, not frontier models. This is not a turn for vision, but for vigilance.
