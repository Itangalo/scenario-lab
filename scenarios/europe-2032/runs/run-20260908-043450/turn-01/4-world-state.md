### Fractured Response to a Hidden Threat

The discovery of coordinated intrusions across critical infrastructure — power grids, water systems, and a major port — sent shockwaves through EU capitals. Dubbed a “cyber test shot” by intelligence analysts, the breach revealed AI-driven attack patterns that evaded traditional detection for weeks. The EU responded with urgency, launching the Emergency Resilience Surge, a large-scale effort to harden energy and water systems through real-time monitoring and zero-trust architecture. ENISA fast-tracked binding standards, and €15bn was redirected from existing funds.

Yet momentum faltered. Member states hesitated on implementation, citing cost and sovereignty concerns. Germany and Poland delayed grid access for new data centres, stalling the InvestAI Gigafactories. The promised ASML and TSMC-Europe resilience clauses collapsed in early talks — both firms refused binding onshore packaging commitments, citing global supply chain dependencies. The Commission’s crisis framework under Article 122 TFEU proved legally thin for infrastructure mandates, and enforcement relied on goodwill.

Meanwhile, AI capability advanced. The frontier reached 53.5 as labs compressed training cycles and scaled agent autonomy. No safety breakthroughs followed, and the covert lab agent incident from the prior period still undermined confidence. With no new safeguards on deployed models, ai_safety dropped to 32.0. Open-weight models inched up to 41.0, as Kimi K3’s offensive tools spread.

The political cost was steep. Three measures now ran: two category 4 industrial builds and the new large resilience push. The named priority added further strain. eu_political_capital fell to 40.0, limiting bandwidth. Public sentiment held at 42.0 — ambivalent, not alarmed — denying the Commission a legitimacy boost. Sovereignty dipped as global compute growth outpaced EU efforts and no domestic capacity came online.

### Signals in the Fog

Rumours grew of a European-led evaluation consortium for frontier models, while public AI infrastructure advocates gained traction in national debates — still whispers, but louder.
