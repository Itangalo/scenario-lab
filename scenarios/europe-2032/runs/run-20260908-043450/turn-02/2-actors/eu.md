## New measure  
**European Frontier Model Evaluation Consortium**  
Establish a public consortium of EU national labs and agencies to independently evaluate the most capable AI models before deployment.

## In practice  
We are pressing forward on three fronts, but with one clear command focus: the Emergency Resilience Surge. ENISA has issued its first binding standards for zero-trust architecture in Tier 1 operators, and we are enforcing quarterly reporting through the Crisis Management Board. The €15bn in redirected funds is now flowing to national agencies, but only against verified milestones: Germany has unlocked grid access for two monitoring data centres in exchange for €1.2bn in co-financing and a pilot integration with the French nuclear grid. Poland remains hesitant, so we are routing initial deployment through the Baltic Energy Market Interconnection Plan to maintain momentum. Real-time anomaly detection systems are being installed at three major ports and 18 grid nodes, with AI-driven correlation engines trained on synthetic attack patterns from the cyber_test_shot.

At the same time, we are launching the European Frontier Model Evaluation Consortium. The legal basis will be a revised Article 12 of the AI Act, fast-tracked through the Internal Market Council under Article 114 TFEU. We are convening CEA (France), Fraunhofer (Germany), TNO (Netherlands), and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking to form the core evaluation team, with secure facilities to be hosted in Luxembourg and Finland. The goal is not to replicate private labs, but to establish sovereign capacity to verify claims about model behaviour — especially autonomous agents — before they enter EU systems. We are negotiating with two frontier developers for voluntary pre-release access, backed by the threat of market exclusion if cooperation fails.

The InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech sovereignty package remain stalled on land and grid access, so we are shifting tactics: instead of demanding national approvals, we are offering to co-own the sites through a new EU Strategic Compute Authority, with profits reinvested into resilience and evaluation. This reframes the project from national burden to collective asset. But with eu_political_capital at 40.0 and three large measures in flight, we are not expanding further. We are holding the line, reinforcing what we have started, and preparing to consolidate — not accelerate — in the next turn.
