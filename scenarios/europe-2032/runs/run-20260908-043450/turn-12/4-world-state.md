### Stalled Sovereignty, Deepening Gaps

AI capability advanced to 63.0, driven by continued gains in materials discovery and logistics optimisation. However, no corresponding progress in safety was made. On the contrary, growing evidence of unexplained reasoning shifts in frontier models—observed during stress tests at two independent labs—confirmed a systemic opacity. With no safety measures completed and control slipping, `ai_safety` dropped sharply to 22.0.

The EU pressed forward with the **Trusted Infrastructure Integration Directive**, now in its final enforcement phase. ENISA’s technical baseline for verifiable maintenance was deemed operationally sound, but political consensus faltered. The Internal Market Council deadlocked over Article 114 TFEU, with Germany warning of WTO incompatibility and France demanding bilateral assurances from U.S. cloud providers. No harmonised standard was adopted. U.S. hyperscalers offered no commitment to co-certification, citing technical infeasibility and liability risks. The directive, though symbolically potent, failed to achieve binding adoption.

Despite this, the **Regional Oversight Integration Initiative** concluded successfully. The three Eastern hubs formalised API access for threat monitoring, and shared liability frameworks were signed. While limited in scope, the agreement improved cross-border incident coordination. As a category 6 measure, its completion boosted `resilience` by +3 to 57.0.

Geopolitically, the situation remained tense. The Taiwan blockade persisted, and Washington’s export controls on lithography tools remained in place. The EU’s leverage in the semiconductor supply chain—through specialty gases and metrology software—was acknowledged but unexercised, lacking the sovereign execution capacity to act decisively. No new access event occurred, but the structural dependence remained acute.

With political capital already at 1.0, the ongoing cost of the directive (−3) and the priority (−1) pushed the EU to the edge of paralysis. No completion bonus applied—the directive did not land—and no new measure was proposed. The portfolio charge was unsustainable, but no corrective action was possible under current constraints.

Public sentiment held at 38.0, unchanged by the absence of visible progress or major incident.

Behind closed doors, contingency planning intensified. Some member states began exploring unilateral enforcement under national security clauses, risking fragmentation. The Union’s digital cohesion frayed further.
