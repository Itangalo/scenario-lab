## In practice  
This turn, we are forcing convergence. The Integrated Sovereign Defence Protocol moves from legal drafting to implementation: Article 114 TFEU gives us the basis, but adoption requires Council agreement under the Internal Market formation. We are negotiating with Germany and France to co-chair a crisis cell linking ENISA, the AI Office, and national CERTs, with authority to trigger defensive escalation when evaluation or incident data confirms novel AI-driven threats. Funding of €4.2bn—diverted from the Gigafactories co-ownership tranche—will activate secure API gateways between sovereign enclaves in Luxembourg and Finland and Tier 1 infrastructure operators. Resistance from smaller states fearing command centralization is real; our offer is veto-proof access logs and full audit rights, not just participation.  

The European Frontier Model Evaluation Consortium finishes this turn with no evaluations conducted—a second straight failure to reach the frontier. We are shifting its mandate midstream: instead of waiting for developer cooperation, it will now ingest classified incident data from the new Critical Incident Integration Directive and reverse-engineer threat signatures from observed attacks. This is not ideal, but it is action. Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package limps forward—permitting zones are drafted, but investor interest has cooled since election_retrenchment removed U.S. hyperscalers as reliable partners. We are offering tax stability and grid priority to any firm committing compute to EU legal control by Q2 2029. It may not be enough. Public sentiment remains fragile at 36.0, and another unmet promise could break cohesion entirely. So this turn, we deliver one thing that works: when the next attack comes, someone will act.
