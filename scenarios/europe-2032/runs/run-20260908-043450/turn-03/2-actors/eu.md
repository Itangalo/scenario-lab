## In practice  
We are still fighting on multiple fronts, but the ground is shifting beneath us. The Emergency Resilience Surge remains our command focus: ENISA has issued binding zero-trust standards for Tier 1 operators, and we are enforcing compliance through the Crisis Management Board. The €15bn in redirected funds is flowing, but only against verified milestones. Germany’s €1.2bn co-financing unlocked grid access for two monitoring nodes, and we are integrating them with the French nuclear grid as a pilot. Poland’s refusal to grant access forces us to route through the Baltic interconnectors — a workaround that preserves momentum but delays full coverage. Real-time anomaly detection is now live at three major ports and 18 grid nodes, correlating signals using synthetic attack profiles from the cyber_test_shot. This is not ideal, but it is operational.

The European Frontier Model Evaluation Consortium is now legally grounded in a fast-tracked revision of Article 12 of the AI Act, pushed through the Internal Market Council under Article 114 TFEU. CEA, Fraunhofer, TNO, and EuroHPC are forming the core team, with secure facilities planned for Luxembourg and Finland. But voluntary access from frontier developers remains uncertain. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Evaluation Authority Pilot: a lean, high-signal effort to stand up actual verification capacity by turn 4. We are repurposing €3.8bn from the stalled Gigafactories co-ownership fund to build secure compute enclaves and hire red-team evaluators. The pilot will not assess all models — only those exhibiting autonomous agent behaviour — and will report directly to the European AI Office. This is not a substitute for regulation; it is the beginning of sovereign technical judgment.

Meanwhile, we are reframing the InvestAI Gigafactories not as a national burden but as a collective asset, co-owned by the EU through a proposed Strategic Compute Authority. But with political capital at 30.0 and four large measures now in flight, we cannot expand further. We are holding the line, reinforcing what we have started, and preparing to consolidate — not accelerate — in the next turn. The backlash from both left and right demands that we prove control before we claim capacity. And so we do.
