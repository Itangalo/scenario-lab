## New measure
**Trusted Infrastructure Integration Directive**  
Establish a binding framework requiring all AI systems used in EU critical infrastructure to run on hardware stacks with verifiable EU-controlled maintenance, subsystem production, and firmware integrity, starting with energy, telecoms, and financial settlement systems.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty from 10.0 to 22.0 by anchoring a meaningful fraction of critical AI workloads to EU-sovereign infrastructure, while applying downward pressure on ai_safety risk by reducing reliance on externally controllable systems.  
`Applies to:` ENISA, national regulators, critical infrastructure operators, ASML, EU semiconductor manufacturers, and foreign hyperscalers operating in the EU.

## Priority
**Trusted Infrastructure Integration Directive** — because the Sovereign Semiconductor Assurance Programme has failed to deliver sovereignty gains, and we can no longer afford to treat compute infrastructure as a neutral utility; this directive forces the integration of industrial and digital sovereignty into a single enforceable standard, starting where failure would be most catastrophic.

## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from aspiration to enforcement. The failure of the Sovereign Semiconductor Assurance Programme to break through U.S. export controls has left us with no path to hardware sovereignty through procurement alone. We must now use regulation to create demand for what we cannot yet produce. The Trusted Infrastructure Integration Directive will be drafted under Article 114 TFEU and fast-tracked through the Internal Market Council, with ENISA developing the technical standards for verifiable maintenance and subsystem control by Q1 2031. We are instructing the Commission to prepare delegated acts that will require all new AI deployments in energy grid management, core telecoms routing, and high-value financial transactions to run on systems with auditable EU-controlled firmware and repair rights — no cloud instance, no matter how powerful, will be allowed to operate in these domains unless the stack beneath it is legally and physically inspectable by EU authorities.

At the same time, we are doubling down on cohesion. The Regional Oversight Integration Initiative continues, with ENISA now offering Central and Baltic states co-ownership of the evaluation stack’s incident response logic, not just access. We are releasing €600 million from the unused portion of the InvestAI fund to co-finance local data sovereignty hubs in Vilnius, Riga, and Bratislava, conditioned on full API integration with the EU threat layer by Turn 10. This is not a concession — it is the price of unity. Without it, we are not a Union, but a patchwork of vetoes. We are acting on the truth that sovereignty is not just about who makes the chips, but who trusts whom to defend the network. We are building that trust while forcing the market to build the infrastructure we need — because if we do not, no one else will.
