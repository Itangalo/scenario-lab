### The Weight of Failure

The cyberattack struck fast and wide — a coordinated ransomware sweep paralysing municipal services across Belgium and northern Italy. Automated exploit generation, likely model-derived, bypassed legacy defences. Though early anomalies were flagged by the newly completed Emergency Resilience Surge, fragmented command delayed response. Patching took 72 hours. Public trust eroded further.

The Surge and the Sovereign Evaluation Authority Pilot both concluded this turn, but neither delivered decisive capability. The first achieved technical integration across grid nodes, yet exposed persistent command fractures. The second built secure enclaves and hired red teams — but evaluated no models. Frontier developers still refused access, and the EU lacked enforcement leverage.

### A New Framework, Born of Necessity

In response, the EU launched the Integrated Sovereign Defence Protocol, a large category 6 measure aimed at linking model evaluation directly to cyber defence. Legal drafting began under Article 114 TFEU, with €4.2bn redirected from the InvestAI Gigafactories fund. The move delays domestic compute expansion but reflects a strategic pivot: sovereignty now means operational protection, not just technical readiness.

Yet the cost was high. Four large measures remained in flight, costing −12. The new priority added −1. The proposal bonus for answering a major incident was +4, given the scale and recency of the attack. However, this did not offset the strain. Political capital fell to 17.0.

Resilience rose to 47.0, buoyed by the completed Surge and the partial effect of the new Protocol’s early architecture. But ai_safety dropped to 26.0, as capability advanced to 57.5 without safety improvements, and no evaluation occurred. openweight_capability rose slightly to 45.0, as Kimi K3 derivatives spread.

Sovereignty declined to 15.0. Despite no capacity event, ai_capability rose by 1.0, costing −1. No category 4 measure completed, and Gigafactories remained delayed. Public sentiment fell to 36.0, dragged down by visible disruption and stalled retraining.

The Union acted — but not cohesively, not decisively, and not without cost. Control remains out of reach, and the window narrows.
