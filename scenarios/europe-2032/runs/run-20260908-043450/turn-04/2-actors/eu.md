## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but when they diverge, we must act in ways that preserve both the capacity to choose and the conditions under which choice remains meaningful.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not persist in a strategy that consumes cohesion without delivering control.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor European autonomy in sovereign AI capacity, beginning with evaluation and control — not compute at any cost, but capability we can verify and govern. This turn, that verification must now include operational resilience: sovereignty is not just understanding models, but defending the systems they can break.

`Trigger: the development this turn you are reacting to`: The **cyber_major_incident**, landing with real public damage and no attribution, proves that uncontrolled AI capabilities are already weaponised and in motion. The failure of the Sovereign Evaluation Authority Pilot to conduct any actual evaluations — despite €3.8bn repurposed and enclaves initiated — reveals that preparation without access is ritual, not power. Meanwhile, resilience at 42.0 is insufficient, political capital has collapsed to 20.0, and public sentiment is falling into hostility. The Union cannot claim sovereignty if it cannot detect, respond to, or contain AI-enabled attacks. The original commitment assumed verification would be the bottleneck. It is not. The bottleneck is integration: between evaluation, defence, and enforcement.

`Grounds`: The identity update reflects that preventing harm is not secondary to autonomy — it is foundational. If AI disables essential services or fractures social order, the EU cannot determine its future at all. The commitment updates to reflect that verification without operational integration — without linking evaluation findings to real-time defence and incident response — fails the test of sovereignty. The revised phrasing binds evaluation to resilience, making it clear that sovereign capacity means not just inspecting models, but securing the society they operate within.

## In practice  
The Emergency Resilience Surge finishes this turn — a milestone, but not a victory. Twenty-four grid nodes and five ports now run real-time anomaly detection, correlating signals using synthetic profiles from past test shots. Germany’s integration with the French nuclear grid is operational, but the Baltic interconnectors remain a weak flank. The system detected the early stages of the cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across municipal services in Belgium and northern Italy — but response was delayed by fragmented command. No single authority could authorise defensive escalation, and patch deployment took 72 hours. That cannot stand.

The Sovereign Evaluation Authority Pilot also finishes, but with no model assessed. Enclaves in Luxembourg and Finland are built, red teams are hired, but no developer granted access. The European AI Office lacks enforcement power, and the AI Act revision did not close the gap. We are technically ready but politically stalled. That ends now. The Integrated Sovereign Defence Protocol will bind evaluation findings directly to ENISA’s threat intelligence feeds and national CERTs. If a model is verified to generate zero-day exploits, that signature will auto-populate into defensive systems across Tier 1 operators. We are drafting the legal basis under Article 114 TFEU, with emergency override protocols under the Crisis Management Board. Funding of €4.2bn will come from reprioritising the Gigafactories co-ownership tranche — a further delay, but one we can no longer avoid.

Public sentiment is our silent constraint. Protests in Spain and Sweden have halted data centre expansions, and displaced workers in logistics face months without retraining slots. We cannot ask for more trust without delivering protection. This measure is not about building more — it is about making what we have actually defend us. That is the only sovereignty that matters now.
