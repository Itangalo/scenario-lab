Despite completion, neither the Surge nor the Sovereign Evaluation Authority Pilot yielded actionable model evaluations, as frontier developers withheld access and enforcement mechanisms remained absent. In response, the EU launched the Integrated Sovereign Defence Protocol, a category 6 initiative under Article 114 TFEU, redirecting €4.2bn from the stalled InvestAI Gigafactories fund to link model evaluation directly with cyber defence. This strategic pivot prioritized operational protection over compute expansion, but deepened political strain: four large measures remained unresolved, political capital fell to 17.0, and public sentiment declined to 36.0 amid disruption and unmet worker support. Resilience rose to 47.0 from partial system integration, but ai_safety dropped to 26.0 as capability reached 57.5, driven by autonomous agents and unchecked Kimi K3 derivatives. Open-weight models gained traction, pushing openweight_capability to 45.0, while AI sovereignty fell to 15.0 amid stalled domestic capacity growth. The Union’s actions reflected urgency but not cohesion, leaving control elusive and enforcement unrealized.
