### The wards go dark, then flicker back
Autumn brought a largely automated ransomware sweep across hospitals, town halls and regional grids. Appointments vanished from screens, prescriptions reverted to paper, and in several cities emergency departments diverted ambulances for days. The tooling was model-generated, freely available, and attribution dragged. Press called defenders visibly behind.

What kept it from cascading was new defensive tooling deployed at speed: automated patching that closed vulnerabilities almost as fast as they were found, and detection that caught swarm behaviour rather than signatures. ENISA, with health agencies, pushed it through existing shield budgets and emergency digital funds, and cross-border restoration teams moved between showcase triage sites and municipal services. Wards stayed open, thinly. Queues did not collapse, but recovery took weeks and smaller clinics waited longest.

### Rationed chips and a separate deal
As restoration dominated, export controls tightened again. Washington rationed allied buyers alongside others on volume licences, with no restoration date for pooled supply. Brussels sent demarches seeking observer status.

Worse for cohesion, a capital cut its own supply arrangement on terms that undercut the common line, defended at home as pragmatism. The Commission condemned it publicly but started no procedure it could not win. The capped-water, sequenced-grid drafts with the two freeze councils stayed warm; no vote was pushed, no digger restarted.

Washington and Beijing meanwhile announced a limited risk agreement on weights security and certain biological design tools, with thin verification. The Union was informed afterwards. Delegations were tasked to report the verification terms.

By December, services were running but trust was thinner: fraud and outage coverage dominated, and parties openly hostile to data centres gained.
