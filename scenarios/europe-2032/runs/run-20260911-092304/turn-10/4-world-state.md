### Holding the line
January to June 2031 became a semester of maintenance. With no new money and no new law on the table, Brussels nursed what it had: the continuity stack for hospitals, the showcase triage helpers, and long negotiations over water and power for the frozen factory sites.

DG Energy and the Legal Service shuttled between the two freeze councils, court-appointed experts and grid operators. The offer was capped water use, sequenced grid connections and earmarks for local clinics in exchange for lifting suspensions site by site. No injunctions were challenged. By June drafts existed, but no digger had restarted and activists claimed the delay as proof the envelopes were still too thirsty.

Where European triage tools already worked, queues for routine scans and first specialist visits held or edged down, and regional press repeated the Commission's line. Elsewhere staff improvised on slower helpers, locum money gone, wards thin but open.

### A harder outside world
Two shocks from outside changed the argument. A contested biosecurity paper claimed a genome model had helped produce a viable human-infecting design, with authors accused both of hype and of publishing too much. The fight stayed inside expert circles, but health agencies took notice.

At almost the same moment a freely downloadable release landed close to the closed frontier. Hundreds of thousands of copies spread in days. Whatever it could do was now on private machines beyond recall.

HERA, the disease control centre and the cyber agency shifted to low-cost watching: syndromic monitoring, guidance to hospitals and towns on freely available frontier models, cyber-hygiene checks. Washington answered the pooled-volume demarche with sympathy and no restoration date. The capital with its separate supply deal kept it; no second capital broke ranks, but the common pledge not to undercut each other remained exhortation.

By June the Union had survived without a new rupture, and finished its unity pact. It had not rebuilt, unfrozen, or restored.
