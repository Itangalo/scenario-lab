### A quiet semester
Brussels proposed nothing new in the second half of 2032, and for once inaction looked deliberate. With no new funding fights to lose, the Commission let the emergency health patch programme close out and tasked its operators with keeping what worked running.

That work benefited from an unexpected tailwind. A control result from the major laboratories — a method for predicting and certifying behaviour of deployed systems rather than arguing about it afterward — was taken up quickly because the labs wanted it themselves. Rolled into hospital, municipal and grid tooling by cross-border teams, it made automated fixes more predictable and reinfections measurably rarer. Small clinics, long the laggards, finally began clearing backlogs.

### Plateau, not transition
The other news came from ordinary offices. Studies across law, accountancy, administration and consulting showed solid productivity gains, largest among junior staff, with no matching fall in employment. The Commission's employment and communications arms seized on the finding: the upheaval had levelled off, retraining panic could ease.

The message landed unevenly. Fraud and outage stories still dominated evening news, and grid-connection hearings for data centres remained hostile in several regions, with construction halts stretching on. Anti-infrastructure parties lost some urgency but kept their seats.

On supply, Brussels held its line without building. Diplomats worked the middle-power chokepoint group for licence relief, while another capital's separate cheap supply deal was noted and left unpunished. The leaked foreign benchmark got only a technical note. Wards stayed lit, dependence stayed deep, and the Union ended the year slightly more trusted, slightly more resilient — and still unable to switch off anyone else's machines.
