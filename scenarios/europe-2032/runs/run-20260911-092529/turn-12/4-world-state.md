### Rebuild under fire
The first half of 2032 arrived as a collision. An automated ransomware sweep tore through municipal administrations, hospitals and local utilities in a matter of days — locked patient files, frozen benefits payments, dark council offices. The tooling was clearly machine-written, the blast radius widened by a poisoned update library, and attribution collapsed into months-long forensics.

Almost simultaneously, a new openly downloadable model family appeared that matched the closed frontier within months. Hundreds of thousands of copies spread in a week. Patchers used it; so did the intruders.

Brussels answered with what it could still move. The turn's single new measure was civil protection mobile teams, backed by emergency medical funds, fanning out with clean rebuild kits, pooled transformers and generators, and guarded convoys. The black-start fallback drills from last year kept the worst-hit regions degrading rather than stopping. Certification of the failure-predicting inspection method was pushed to clinic systems to cut false trips during restores.

It partly worked. In showcase hospitals, nurses reported fewer false alarms and faster restores. Elsewhere, rebuilds stalled for lack of spares.

Spares were the second front. Washington tightened chip and model licences again, this time rationing even allies on volume quotas. The Hague complied under protest; servicing bays stayed empty. Tokyo and Seoul, courted quietly for emergency parts, sent boxes, not a line. A further servicing-compensation pact was discussed but not tabled as a measure this turn — compensation talk stayed stuck in Council working groups with no proposal, staffing or funds attached.

The public dividend showcase formally closed — vouchers paid, dashboards published — but its launch was drowned by outage maps and quota headlines.

Robots, much hyped, changed little. A few logistics yards and defence pilots deployed boxy carriers on fenced routes; factories waiting for versatile machines kept waiting.

By June, anger was visible. A handful of local lists campaigning explicitly to tear out automation projects drew crowds and a few council seats in rumour and single leaflets, while transformer yards needed uniformed guard against a new round of arson.
