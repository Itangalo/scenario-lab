### Claimed gains, burned stores
The half-year opened with money leaving. American labs cancelled data-centre expansions and paused training runs as valuations reset, and the arrangements European operators had counted on for rented compute quietly lapsed. Washington at the same time put allied buyers on strict volume quotas for advanced chips and model access. The Hague complied under protest; talks in Tokyo and Seoul produced spare parts in boxes, not a supply line.

Brussels had one bright spot to work with. In two health systems and a justice pilot, European-procured assistants cut waiting lists and decision times enough for ministers to point at charts. The Commission moved to label it, dashboard it, and keep it running on European hosting.

That hosting became a target. Over spring, small groups broke into generator yards, transformer stores and the cabins where breaker logs were kept, leaving slogans about refusing to keep the machines alive. Several towns that had run on pooled spares through the winter found their reserves drained or damaged. National teams formalised the mutual-aid protocol rehearsed for loss-of-control — cross-border fuel, spares on reimbursement, isolation drills — which limited the spread but could not replace what was torched.

The showcase survived where it was guarded. Clinics stayed open on manual fallback in three regions, and the dashboards held. Coverage, however, paired every gain with images of burned containers and postponed care elsewhere. Hostility eased only slightly; the quotas and the investment freeze read as proof of being fenced in and metered from outside.

Frontier capability crept forward despite the crunch, and open models closed much of the gap as prior advances diffused. Control did not keep pace.
