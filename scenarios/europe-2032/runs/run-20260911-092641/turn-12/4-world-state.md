### Showcases finished, legitimacy collapsed
The spring began with Brussels cutting ribbons. The public dividend showcase formally closed: health and justice dashboards in three systems still showed shorter waits, and local press where queues had fallen ran cautious praise. The Commission published final counters and handed operations to ministries.

Then two blows landed together. Labour ministries confirmed what graduates already knew: entry hiring in law, accountancy, junior software, customer operations and administration had frozen. Firms were not firing en masse; they were simply not replacing. Numbers were disputed, the empty hiring pages were not.

At the same time, an ombudsman and then a court found a benefits-fraud risk-scoring system used in two member states had systematically cut or flagged vulnerable claimants. Caseworkers had approved machine-ranked queues at under a minute a file, logs were written and never read. Opposition and papers framed it not as bad enforcement but as a bad law: the system had slipped through the high-risk categories entirely, so everything done had been lawful.

The combination drowned the showcases. Talk shows ran wrongful-denial cases beside jobless graduates; mayors guarding clinics faced protests about machines judging the poor.

Brussels answered with a transition guarantee run through labour ministers: uncommitted social funds shifted to wage top-ups, six-month retraining placements and a bonus for firms converting placements, while justice ministries paused contested decision tools pending oversight audits. The scheme started enrolling by June but payments lagged and employers called the bonus too small.

Elsewhere, laboratories adopted a new interpretability check that made deployed-system failures more predictable, which EU safety staff pushed into patch guidance. A contested biosecurity paper claiming a genome model helped design a human-infecting organism circulated among specialists without reaching the public. A materials result from a European lab — a stable solid-state electrolyte cutting battery costs — drew industry interest but no comfort in a spring defined by jobs and wrongful decisions.
