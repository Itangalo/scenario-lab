Brussels claimed first win in two years: EU-procured assistants on EU-hosted capacity cut waiting lists/decision times in three health systems and two justice ministries, showcased with open counters and weekly figures.

Fallback then attacked: Jan-March break-ins torched/drained generator yards, transformer stores, breaker-log cabins; leaflets called pools collaboration. On-scheme reimbursed pools exposed addresses; two towns lost off-books spares. Cross-border mutual aid kept hospitals lit, but hand restoration slowed by sabotage.

Washington hardened rationing: allied buyers on quotas, servicing case-by-case; Hague complied under protest. Private compute seized as investment fled; Brussels froze forced migration, told capitals to sit on existing EU capacity. Showcase sites guarded; other mayors resentful.

By autumn Brussels turned scarcity into leverage: contact group with Hague, Tokyo, Seoul to align licences and pool orders, Council mandate, anti-coercion readiness note as backstop, no new law. Two blocked servicing batches cleared, pooled tender for transformers/breakers got lower price. But licences stayed national, volumes modest, US vendors decided case-by-case — sold as power in Brussels, seen as queueing in capitals.

Manual fallback network formally completed: paper procedures, pooled spares, generator loans as doctrine, exercised in three regions. Autumn storms and eastern yard break-ins met faster hand restoration, hospitals stayed lit. Fragmentation exposed: municipal utilities kept best spares off-books, controls isolated after spring arsons. Guards stayed on showcases; others got phone numbers.

Year ended with dashboards still showing falls where queues fell, leaflets and burned cabins elsewhere: a finished safety net full of holes, and a supply bargain that eased rationing without ending it.
