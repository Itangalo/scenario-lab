## In practice  
We are launching the EU AI Incident Response Corps under the authority of the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA), with binding powers to access critical infrastructure telemetry, conduct red-team exercises, and trigger containment protocols across energy, water, and port systems. The legal base will be Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market to override fragmented national regimes, and we will propose a Council Regulation under the Internal Market and Consumer Protection formation to fast-track adoption. The €4.2bn budget will come from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, reallocated from stalled digital infrastructure lines. Resistance from member states protective of national sovereignty — particularly in Germany and Poland — will be met with guarantees of operational subsidiarity: the Corps will act only when cross-border impact is confirmed, and only with a two-member-state threshold to activate.

At the same time, we are advancing both InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech sovereignty package. For the former, we are finalising site selections in Finland, Spain, and Romania, with grid-connection timelines accelerated through emergency energy security provisions. ASML is being offered a non-discriminatory access guarantee to future EU-built compute in exchange for technical support in localising chip packaging. For the latter, we are brokering a deal between EDF, TenneT, and private hyperscalers to co-invest in 150GW of new low-carbon power capacity by 2032, with permitting fast-tracked in designated zones. The package’s credibility hinges on proving that EU compute can scale without relying on foreign choke points — and the current American exclusion order on Mythos 5 makes that argument sharper by the day.
