In 2028, the EU's AI ambitions faltered as the Investment Winter shattered private-sector confidence, collapsing the Tech Sovereignty Package and stalling data centre expansion. Member state disunity worsened when an Eastern state bypassed EU oversight with a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, exposing Brussels’ limited leverage. The Commission’s pivot to public-funded InvestAI Gigafactories stalled due to Dutch opposition to invoking Article 346, leaving EU compute sovereignty in limbo.

Despite political gridlock, ENISA successfully implemented AI-specific cyber resilience standards for critical infrastructure, enforced through funding conditionality, marking a rare governance success. However, the Labour Transition Accelerator and AI Incident Response Corps ended without replacement, hampered by voluntary national participation and lack of enforcement.

Public trust eroded amid rising job displacement fears and failures in consumer AI applications. Globally, AI capability growth slowed under the new U.S. administration’s retrenchment policy, which imposed deployment restrictions and curbed data centre expansion, reinforcing a fragile, fragmented international landscape. EU AI sovereignty remained stagnant at 20.0, while the governance gap widened amid accelerating technological change and weakening cohesion.
