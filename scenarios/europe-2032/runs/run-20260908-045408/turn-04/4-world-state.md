### The Weight of Reaction

The EU’s focus on resilience intensifies in the wake of the ransomware cascade, but momentum falters under the strain of overlapping commitments. The expansion of the Cyber Resilience Certification advances technically, with ENISA drafting AI-hardening standards, yet political resistance mounts in Eastern and Southern member states, who decry the compliance burden. Tying certification to cohesion funds sparks legal warnings from the EFTA Court, slowing implementation.

The EU AI Incident Response Corps conducts its first cross-border drill, simulating an AI-coordinated grid failure. While operationally promising, the exercise reveals gaps in data-sharing agreements and real-time authority. Without full legal mandate—still pending in the Council—the Corps remains a shadow of its intended self.

Poland’s bilateral AI deal with Washington hardens into precedent. Romania and Hungary open quiet talks with U.S. officials, testing the limits of EU solidarity. The Commission warns of “strategic fragmentation,” but lacks leverage to stop the drift. ASML continues withholding support for InvestAI sites, citing U.S. export guidance; Brussels threatens Article 346 invocation, but legal advisors caution it may trigger retaliatory supply curbs.

The Labour Transition Accelerator sees modest uptake, but German and Irish courts uphold challenges to employer levies, weakening the funding model. Retraining programmes persist on a reduced scale, now dependent on national co-financing.

Public sentiment sours further as media spotlight job losses in legal and administrative sectors. A strike by municipal clerks in Lyon and Rotterdam draws national attention, reinforcing perceptions of AI as a disruptive force with few immediate benefits.

### Capability Creeps Forward

Despite global turbulence, AI capability advances steadily, reaching 58.0 as frontier labs automate portions of model alignment testing. Open-weight models benefit, with stronger community forks now capable of independent fine-tuning on consumer hardware. No safety breakthroughs emerge, and the gap between capability and control widens.
