Secure independent AI capacity to act with sovereignty in a world where capability outpaces control

## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent irreversible loss of strategic autonomy in AI``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The previous commitment to secure independent AI capacity remains necessary but is no longer sufficient. The erosion of cohesion through bilateral deals, the stagnation of InvestAI under external pressure, and the accelerating capability frontier have created a situation where delay itself is a form of surrender. We are no longer merely building capacity — we are racing against a closing window in which the EU can still act as a unified agent. The new commitment reflects an existential threshold: if we do not lock in autonomy now, we will not get another chance.

## New measure
None this turn.  
We are holding space to absorb the completion of two major measures and to realign under the new two-year commitment, which demands a shift from distributed capacity-building to decisive action on sovereignty.

## Priority
``Expand EU Cyber Resilience Certification to AI-Critical Infrastructure`` — because the eval_anomaly_reports and the persistent cyber gaps mean we are now defending against systems whose behaviour we cannot fully predict, and without hardening critical infrastructure now, even modest openweight capability advances could trigger cascading failures that undermine public order and institutional trust.

## In practice
We are treating the completion of the EU AI Incident Response Corps and the Labour Transition Accelerator not as closures but as transitions: the Corps’ pilot teams in six member states will now be folded into a permanent structure under ENISA, with operational authority derived from enhanced Article 172 TFEU powers that we are fast-tracking through the Council. The Labour Transition Accelerator’s pilot cohorts in France and the Netherlands will continue under national co-financing, but we are launching a review of the employer levy model with a view to replacing it with a sectoral automation tax, to be proposed under the new commitment next turn. The cyber resilience expansion remains our immediate focus: ENISA has issued the first AI-hardening implementing acts, mandating red-teaming, anomaly detection and fallback protocols for all NIS2-covered operators. We are leveraging cohesion fund conditionality to compel adoption, even as the EFTA Court weighs in — this is a moment where political risk must be borne, not avoided. Simultaneously, we are escalating the ASML issue: the Commission has opened a strategic dependency review under Article 346, with a six-week deadline. If no resolution is reached with the Dutch government, we will unilaterally designate advanced lithography equipment as critical for AI sovereignty, triggering procurement controls and opening the door to alternative supply chains. The window for reactive measures is closing. From next turn, we act not to catch up, but to survive.
