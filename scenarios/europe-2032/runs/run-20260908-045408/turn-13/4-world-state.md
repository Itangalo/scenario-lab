### Stalled Resolve in a Shifting World

The EU’s strategic pivot toward sovereign compute remains frozen in place. Despite the Commission’s emergency repurposing of the InvestAI Gigafactories fund into full EU-owned infrastructure, Council ratification of Article 122 emergency borrowing drags into Q4 with no consensus in sight. Constitutional objections persist, and the political cost of overriding them grows. Without funding, the French and Finnish compute sites remain blueprints.

The ultimatum to the Netherlands on ASML export controls expires unmet. The Dutch government rejects both binding controls and joint lithography development, citing WTO obligations and innovation sovereignty. Brussels threatens Article 346, but without Council unity or financial leverage, the move lacks credibility. The EU’s access to critical semiconductor tools remains dependent on foreign goodwill.

No new EU-wide measure launches, and with political capital stagnant at 11.0, even symbolic action falters.

### Fragility at the Core

The annulled election triggers a crisis of legitimacy, with half the population rejecting the court’s intervention. No coordinated response emerges. The EU’s inability to secure its technological foundation or stabilise its political fabric leaves it exposed—not to a single catastrophic event, but to the slow erosion of authority, capability, and trust.
