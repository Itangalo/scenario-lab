## Priority
InvestAI Gigafactories — because with private capital withdrawn and openweight capability now within striking distance of the frontier, only direct EU control over sovereign compute can prevent irreversible dependence before the next capability threshold is crossed.

## In practice  
We are treating the completion of InvestAI Gigafactories not as an endpoint but as a foundation: the €200bn fund is now being repurposed from co-investment to full EU ownership and operation of two sovereign compute sites, one in France and one in Finland, under direct Commission oversight and EU legal jurisdiction. Emergency borrowing under Article 122 TFEU has been activated, with the European Investment Bank preparing to issue bonds backed by cohesion and digital funds, conditional on Council ratification by Q2 2031. We are designating AI compute as critical infrastructure under the Critical Entities Resilience Directive, which unlocks fast-tracked permitting and state aid flexibility — a move we are advancing through the Internal Market Council, despite resistance from member states wary of precedent.

At the same time, we are escalating pressure on the Netherlands over ASML. The six-week ultimatum stands: agree to binding export controls on deep-UV and next-gen EUV components and commit to joint development of EU-accessible lithography capacity, or face unilateral invocation of Article 346. The Commission has drafted the regulation and is briefing core allies — Germany, France, Italy — on the necessity of breaking commercial dependency to preserve strategic autonomy. We are not seeking confrontation, but we will not accept a future where the EU’s access to foundational technology depends on the goodwill of third parties. The world has shifted; we are acting as if we see it.
