## In practice  
We are launching the Labour Transition Accelerator as a Council Regulation under the Employment and Social Affairs formation, co-financed at 75% by the European Social Fund Plus and 25% by national co-investment. The programme targets workers in legal, accounting, and administrative roles displaced by AI systems now performing at junior professional level. It offers two years of wage insurance at 70% of prior income, full tuition for accredited retraining in AI-augmented professions (cybersecurity, system auditing, human-AI coordination), and mobility bonuses for geographic relocation. To prevent free-riding, participation is conditional on employer contributions: firms automating more than 15% of roles in a given occupation over 12 months must fund 40% of displaced workers’ transition packages or face a levy.

Simultaneously, we are pressing forward on all three in-flight measures. For the EU AI Incident Response Corps, we are revising the legal basis to Article 172 TFEU (civil protection and humanitarian aid operations), reframing the Corps as a cross-border emergency response capability rather than a security intrusion, thereby sidestepping German and Polish objections to internal market overreach. ENISA will lead operationalisation, with pilot teams standing up in Q2 2027 in coordination with national CERTs. The €4.2bn budget remains drawn from reprioritised Digital Europe and REACT-EU lines.

For the InvestAI Gigafactories, we are invoking emergency energy security provisions to fast-track grid connections in Finland and Spain, treating compute infrastructure as critical energy off-takers. We have offered ASML a binding guarantee: in exchange for technical support on advanced packaging and test infrastructure, the first 10% of non-reserved EU-built compute capacity will be reserved for Dutch industrial users. This offer is now under review in The Hague.

The Tech Sovereignty Package remains on track for completion in turn 6. We are finalising the co-investment deal with EDF and TenneT, now including Iberdrola and Statkraft, to deliver 150GW of low-carbon power by 2032. The permitting fast-track zones will be legislated under a new Energy Security and Digital Infrastructure Directive, to be tabled in Q3 2027 under the Internal Market formation. The argument is clear: if the US can weaponise access to Mythos 5, then compute is not infrastructure — it is terrain. And terrain must be held.
