### Labour Displacement Sparks EU Response  
A wave of job losses in legal, accounting and administrative sectors sweeps across major EU economies, driven by AI systems now performing at junior professional level. Public debate intensifies, with unions and political leaders demanding action. In response, the EU launches the Labour Transition Accelerator — a large-scale retraining and wage-insurance programme — as its new priority. The scheme offers displaced workers two years of income support, full tuition for AI-augmented roles, and mobility incentives, funded jointly by the EU and member states. Employers automating over 15% of roles must contribute to transition costs or face levies.

### Legal Pivot for Cyber Resilience  
To break the deadlock on the EU AI Incident Response Corps, the Commission shifts its legal basis from Article 114 to Article 172 TFEU, reframing the unit as a civil protection mechanism. This bypasses resistance from Germany and Poland, and pilot teams begin forming in coordination with national CERTs. However, full operational capacity is delayed beyond turn 5 due to staffing and mandate clarity issues.

### Sovereignty Efforts Stall Amid External Pressures  
The InvestAI Gigafactories in Finland and Spain face continued grid integration delays, despite emergency energy provisions. ASML remains cautious, withholding technical support pending clarity on U.S. export policy. No new compute capacity comes online, and rising AI capability widens the gap with EU control. The Tech Sovereignty Package progresses slowly, with permitting reforms still in drafting.

### Political Capital Erodes  
With three large measures active and the new priority, political capital is strained. The Labour Transition Accelerator is launched, but early criticism over complexity and employer burden dampens enthusiasm. Public sentiment rises slightly due to visible action, but resilience declines as cyber preparedness lags and no category 6 measure is completed.
