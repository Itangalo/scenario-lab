## In practice
We are moving forward on all four measures in flight, under acute pressure. The Labour Transition Accelerator is now live as a Council Regulation under the Employment and Social Affairs formation, co-financed at 75% by the European Social Fund Plus. We are fast-tracking accreditation of retraining programmes in cybersecurity, AI auditing, and human-AI coordination, with pilot cohorts launching in France, Italy, and the Netherlands by Q4. To secure buy-in from member states, we are offering flexibility in how the 25% national co-investment is sourced—allowing regional funds or local levies—while maintaining the core condition: no access to EU digital innovation grants or public procurement preferences without full participation. The employer contribution mechanism is already under legal challenge from business federations in Germany and Ireland, but we are holding firm: if automation extracts value, it must also fund adjustment.

On the cyber front, the EU AI Incident Response Corps is being operationalised under Article 172 TFEU, with ENISA coordinating pilot teams in six member states. However, the recent **cyber_major_incident**—a model-generated ransomware sweep that encrypted municipal services in Belgium, Luxembourg, and parts of northern Italy—has exposed how far behind we are. The attack used AI-optimised phishing and lateral movement tools, and defenders were blind to the coordination until cascading failures began. Attribution remains unclear, but forensic analysis confirms the tooling was not human-scale in design or deployment speed. In response, we are accelerating the new measure: expanding the EU Cyber Resilience Certification to include mandatory AI-specific safeguards for all NIS2-critical operators. This will require continuous monitoring, red-teaming with AI-generated threats, and air-gapped fallback protocols. ENISA will draft the implementing act by Q1 2028, with compliance checks beginning in turn 5.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain delayed—grid integration in Finland is now projected for Q3 2028, and ASML has not yet accepted our offer. We are escalating to the Dutch government, warning that if technical support is withheld while U.S. labs advance unimpeded, the EU’s entire compute sovereignty strategy will collapse. On the Tech Sovereignty Package, we are finalising the Energy Security and Digital Infrastructure Directive, which will designate AI compute as strategic infrastructure, enabling fast-track permitting and priority grid access. The argument is gaining traction: compute is terrain, and we are losing ground. But with **member_state_defection**—Poland signing a bilateral compute access deal with Washington that bypasses EU coordination—we are now fighting not just capability gaps, but fragmentation. Political capital is thin, but we cannot afford to pause. We hold the course.
