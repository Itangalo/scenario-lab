### Cyber Shockwaves and Political Fractures

A wave of AI-optimised ransomware cripples municipal services across Belgium, Luxembourg, and northern Italy, overwhelming response teams. The attack’s speed and coordination point to automated planning and execution, marking a turning point in offensive cyber capability. Public trust in digital infrastructure wavers, and emergency meetings convene across capitals. The EU’s nascent AI Incident Response Corps, still in pilot phase, lacks mandate and tools to act decisively.

In response, the Commission fast-tracks a major expansion of the EU Cyber Resilience Certification, mandating AI-specific defences for all critical infrastructure under NIS2. The move is welcomed by security agencies but criticised as reactive. Enforcement will take years, and compliance remains uneven.

Meanwhile, Poland signs a bilateral agreement with the United States for preferential access to frontier AI models and compute, bypassing EU coordination. Framed as a national security necessity, the deal undermines the Union’s unified posture and triggers alarm in Brussels. Other member states signal interest in similar arrangements, threatening a patchwork of dependencies.

The Labour Transition Accelerator launches amid mixed signals. Retraining pilots begin in France, Italy, and the Netherlands, offering displaced legal and administrative workers pathways into AI oversight and cybersecurity. However, employer levies face legal challenges in Germany and Ireland, and uptake is slow. Media coverage shifts from hopeful to sceptical as automation continues outpacing adaptation.

InvestAI Gigafactories remain offline, delayed by grid bottlenecks and ASML’s withholding of technical support amid U.S. export uncertainty. The Tech Sovereignty Package advances slowly through legislative channels, with permitting reforms still in draft.

Public sentiment dips as job losses mount and the cyberattack exposes fragility. Political capital erodes under the weight of four large concurrent measures and growing intergovernmental tension. The priority on labour transition yields limited legitimacy gains, overshadowed by systemic vulnerabilities.
