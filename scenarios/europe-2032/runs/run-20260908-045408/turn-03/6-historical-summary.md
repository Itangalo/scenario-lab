In mid-2027, a surge in AI-powered ransomware attacks disrupts critical infrastructure across several EU regions, exposing systemic vulnerabilities and overwhelming under-resourced response mechanisms. The EU accelerates its Cyber Resilience Certification to include AI-specific safeguards for critical systems, though implementation delays and uneven compliance limit immediate impact. Poland’s unilateral deal with the U.S. for access to advanced AI technology fractures EU coordination, sparking fears of divergent national strategies and dependency fragmentation. The Labour Transition Accelerator launches retraining pilots in key sectors, but legal challenges and slow adoption weaken its effectiveness, while automation accelerates. InvestAI Gigafactories remain stalled due to infrastructure and export control bottlenecks, deepening the compute sovereignty gap. Amid mounting job losses, cyber insecurity, and political strain from overlapping initiatives, public trust declines and political capital deteriorates further. EU AI sovereignty drops to 20.0, reflecting widening gaps in resilience, control, and strategic unity.
