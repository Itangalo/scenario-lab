## New measure
**EU Essential Services Cut-Off Fallback**
It keeps hospitals, ministries and logistics operators running by switching cut-off workflows to European-controlled models on existing capacity with funded human-in-the-loop cover.
This is a Preparedness and resilience move, not a new compute build, because with access denied this half-year, permits frozen and insurers forcing rollback, the two-year commitment to keep essential services running demands operation on what we already have, not promises of Gigafactories we cannot permit.

## Priority
New fallback measure, because the cut-off landing on hospitals and firms this turn outranks the M7 bridge bid which bought no observer seat and no speed.

## In practice
We task DG CNECT, HERA and ENISA under the NIS2 and Health Emergency frameworks to inventory cut-off-affected workflows in health, interior ministries and logistics, and re-route them to EU-hosted models maintained under M3 evaluation protocols and M6 continuity stack playbooks, hosted on already-permitted capacity. No new siting vote is asked; we pay deployment and staffing from repurposed Digital Europe and EU4Health lines.

We meet the insurer pullback directly: the fallback funds temporary human-in-the-loop staffing for autonomous back-office roles repriced after February, negotiated with the insurers and employer federations in the Employment Council as a six-month bridge, to stop disorderly rollback. We tell protest regions no new substation works are ordered under this measure, to avoid triggering the blockades that stalled permits.

M7 continues at low burn: EEAS keeps the observer request open and feeds grid telemetry and incident forms to the US-China working groups, but we sell it at home no longer as breathing space but as evidence that dependence without leverage means exclusion, backing the fallback switch.
