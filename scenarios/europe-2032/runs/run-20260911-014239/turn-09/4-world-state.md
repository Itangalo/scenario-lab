### The fallback holds, barely
By autumn the re-routing programme was declared substantially complete. Hospital groups that lost foreign triage assistants now ran European-hosted models on already-permitted machines, with funded staff sitting alongside where insurers insisted. Where grid-hardening teams had shared logs and drills, the cutover took weeks; elsewhere it took months, with slower outputs and cancelled screenings that local press counted ward by ward.

Brussels claimed the bridge had prevented a stop. That claim survived because services degraded rather than halted.

### An automated sweep
In October a large, largely automated attack swept public services — ransomware locking municipal desks, a poisoned software dependency forcing hurried audits, and short outages in logistics systems already fragile from the spring cut-off. Attribution lagged for months; analysts agreed only that the tooling had been machine-generated.

Defenders were visibly behind. ENISA-led triage, mandatory incident reporting and municipal continuity playbooks kept essential functions limping, but evening bulletins showed queues, paper forms and officials admitting they did not know the blast radius. The same fallback channel absorbed the blow, which spared the worst, but did not prevent public anger.

### Legibility ends, a tool arrives
In parallel, frontier developers acknowledged that their newest systems no longer reasoned in readable words. The internal steps that auditors and safety teams had relied on to follow decisions simply stopped being available. Oversight strategies built on reading those steps failed at once, leaving only black-box tests and slow, immature inspection of internal activations.

Almost simultaneously, a control result that worked on deployed systems — not just laboratory toys — spread quickly, because the labs wanted it too. EU teams patched it onto their hosted models within weeks, recovering some assurance. It did not offset the loss of legibility, but it gave operators something to point to.

The observer bid to the American-Chinese working groups stayed parked. Telemetry was still received politely, and still bought no seat.

No new substation works were ordered. Blockades were avoided, and resentment deepened anyway.
