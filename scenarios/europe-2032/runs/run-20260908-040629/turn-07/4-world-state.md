Open-weight models absorb the latest reasoning modules, though deployment remains uneven. `openweight_capability` rises to 52.0, reflecting growing accessibility of agent-like behaviour outside closed labs—now matching the closed frontier at the start of the run.

### EU Pivot Under Pressure

The EU concludes the InvestAI Gigafactories programme, with two sites secured after prolonged negotiations. While below initial ambitions, this partial delivery yields a closure bonus of +8 in political capital terms. However, momentum is offset by new burdens: the Commission formally proposes the Emergency Compute Access Mechanism (ECAM), a large category 4 measure aiming to secure rapid sovereign access to 30,000 GPUs via option contracts with hyperscalers. The proposal triggers immediate debate over legal basis, fiscal exposure, and coherence with market rules. No approval is reached this turn; implementation cannot begin before the next, consistent with the minimum two-turn delay for high-cost measures.

Although ECAM is proposed, its early framework development contributes only a +3 proposal bonus to `eu_ai_sovereignty`, as per metric rule 7 for large new measures. Additional gains include +1 from progress on bilateral hosting alignment (France, Poland, Finland) and +1 from securing foundational contracts under prior initiatives. These are partially offset by −1 due to the global capability rise exceeding 2.0. Net change: +3, bringing `eu_ai_sovereignty` to 27.0.

Political capital reflects the net effect of closing InvestAI (+8) but taking on a new large measure (−3) and maintaining priority status (−1), for a net portfolio charge of −4. No legitimacy lending occurs, as public sentiment (20.0) remains below political capital. The net change is −4, reducing `eu_political_capital` from 40.0 to 36.0.

Public trust erodes sharply. The automated decision scandal—revealing biased denials in social benefits—triggers outrage, while an election annulment in a major member state, citing disinformation, fuels crisis narratives. `public_sentiment` drops to 20.0, reflecting deepening anxiety.

Resilience holds at 49.0—no new category 6 measure lands, but no further degradation occurs. The Compute Resilience Board is established, and ENISA begins drafting audit protocols, laying groundwork for future resilience efforts.

US posture remains **cooperative engagement with strategic autonomy**, with transatlantic talks on crisis protocols stalling over export controls.

Emerging developments continue to shape the landscape: a coordinated synthetic disinformation campaign floods social and local media across multiple member states, subtly amplifying regional grievances. Several EU member states, reacting to eroding trust and stalled EU-level governance, begin mandating local data residency and model hosting for public-sector AI systems. Informal negotiations also continue on a regional data governance pact restricting high-capability model training on locally generated data—moves framed as sovereignty measures but risking further fragmentation of the digital single market.
