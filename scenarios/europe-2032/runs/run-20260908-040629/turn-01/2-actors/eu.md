## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_test_shot as a wake-up call. This turn, the Commission activates the Crisis Coordination Mechanism under Article 14 of the Treaty on European Union, designating AI-enabled infrastructure intrusion as a cross-border threat requiring immediate action. The ENISA Standing Committee meets in emergency session to adopt a binding framework for intrusion detection, network segmentation, and mandatory red-teaming of grid operators, water utilities, and major ports — funded through a €12bn reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility. National cybersecurity agencies are required to report compliance by Q2 2027, with peer review built in to prevent free-riding.

Simultaneously, we are pushing the InvestAI Gigafactories forward by convening the first site selection panel under the European Investment Bank’s fast-track mandate. The legal basis is Article 336 TFEU, and we are offering host states priority access to sovereign AI compute shares in exchange for expedited grid connections and tax incentives. Resistance from regional planning authorities in Germany and France is being met with binding arbitration clauses and promises of regional innovation spillovers. The Tech sovereignty package advances through COREPER II this month, with the Commission offering member states a carve-out on local content requirements in exchange for unanimous support on accelerated permitting zones. Public sentiment remains ambivalent, but the cyber intrusion gives us cover to frame compute build-out as defence, not just development.
