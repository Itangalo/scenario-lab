## In practice  
We are treating the ASML pressure as a strategic inflection point. This turn, the Commission activates Article 346a TFEU to designate lithography equipment — particularly DUV systems with EUV-adjacent components — as dual-use critical infrastructure. We launch the Accelerated Lithography Access Initiative (ALAI), committing €9bn in Horizon Europe and Innovation Fund resources to pre-purchase and co-invest in DUV tooling for EU-based semiconductor manufacturing hubs in Dresden, Leuven, and Grenoble. The goal is not just stockpiling, but creating a legally anchored, operationally independent EU pool of tools that can be redeployed under crisis conditions. We are negotiating directly with ASML management under a new framework that ties continued EU market access and R&D collaboration to binding commitments on non-diversion of mid-tier tools destined for allied fabs.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the Tech Sovereignty Package through COREPER II by offering a compromise: member states gain preferential access to InvestAI compute shares in exchange for fast-tracking permitting for both data centres and supporting energy infrastructure. The Cyber Resilience Surge continues implementation, with ENISA conducting its first peer-reviewed compliance audit this month; Italy has committed to full alignment by Q2, while Poland faces infringement proceedings unless it submits its red-teaming plan by April. The InvestAI site selection panel meets next week — we are pushing forward with interim grid reservations based on provisional environmental assessments, invoking force majeure on standard review timelines due to the declared state of strategic urgency. Public messaging frames ALAI not as protectionism, but as insurance: sovereign access to the machines that make the chips that run the models on which everything now depends.
