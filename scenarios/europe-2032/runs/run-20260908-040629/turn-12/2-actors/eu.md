## New measure
**European AI Watchtower**  
Create a central, real-time monitoring and early-warning system for AI-enabled disinformation, cyber intrusions, and model misuse, integrating national intelligence, platform data, and civil society reporting to enable rapid coordinated response.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` +7 to `ai_safety`, +5 to `resilience` by enabling detection and containment of emergent threats before they cascade, and reducing blind spots in public situational awareness  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national security agencies, major digital platforms operating in the EU, and accredited civil society observers

## In practice
The Emergency Resilience Surge concludes this turn, but its legacy is mixed. ENISA has finalised binding cyber standards for AI-integrated critical infrastructure, and they will be formally adopted under the NIS2 Directive by the Council in June. However, only ten member states have fully implemented the required logging and audit access; Hungary, Poland, and Austria continue to resist, citing sovereignty, and are now operating parallel national frameworks. The eu-LISA Disinformation Response Cell remains under-resourced and siloed—its detection models are overwhelmed by the volume and sophistication of the current synthetic wave. We are treating the December failover test as conditionally suspended: it will proceed only if compliance reaches 75% by turn 13, a threshold that now seems unlikely.

We are launching the European AI Watchtower as a political necessity as much as a technical one. This is not another layer of bureaucracy—it is an operational nerve centre, modelled on the ECDC’s pandemic response architecture, with legal authority under Article 16 TFEU and co-governed by a triad: the Commission, eu-LISA, and a rotating presidency-appointed crisis coordinator. It will integrate classified threat feeds from national intelligence services, real-time content takedowns from platforms under the DSA, and ground-level reports from civil society via a secure, anonymised portal. Funding of €2.1bn comes from the Internal Security Fund and the Crisis and Investment Facility, with staffing drawn from eu-LISA, Frontex, and national cyber units on secondment. The system will be hosted in Luxembourg with edge nodes in Helsinki and Marseille for redundancy.

We are offering member states full opt-in transparency: they can see all data concerning their territory and must, in return, feed verified incidents into the system. For those still resisting, we are pairing this with a new cohesion incentive: full reimbursement of Watchtower integration costs plus a 15% bonus on digital infrastructure funds for early adopters. The goal is not just detection—it is to rebuild trust in a common reality. Without that, no testbed, no standard, no sovereignty measure will hold. The Watchtower becomes the anchor of a new phase: not just defending against harm, but proving we can see it together.
