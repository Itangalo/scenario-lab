## Priority  
`Openweight Containment Framework` — because the openweight_frontier_release event has abruptly collapsed the diffusion lag, putting broadly dangerous capability into unrecallable distribution, and our two-year commitment to sovereignty now demands we control what enters the commons as much as what we build ourselves.

## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that sovereignty is not only about having compute and chips on our soil, but about deciding what kinds of intelligence circulate freely within our jurisdiction. The sudden narrowing of the gap between closed and open capability — now only 14 points — is not a technical detail; it is a regime change. A model that can be downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in a week and runs on consumer hardware cannot be governed through laboratories or export controls. It can only be governed through law.

This turn, the Commission tables a draft regulation under Article 114 TFEU, establishing the Openweight Containment Framework. It sets a hard cap: no model above 45.0 in capability may be publicly released without a safety license from the European AI Office, contingent on verifiable weight security, third-party interpretability audits, and a misuse mitigation plan. Cloud platforms will be required to block unauthorized redistribution, and hosting providers must report releases above threshold 40.0 for monitoring. We are coordinating with Germany and France to fast-track this through COREPER, offering concessions on data sovereignty clauses to secure support from member states wary of overreach. The framework is designed to be technology-neutral — it does not ban open weights, but demands that freedom does not mean recklessness. The moment of release is now the moment of accountability.
