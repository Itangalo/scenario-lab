## In practice
The Watchtower is now live in its first operational phase. We are activating the triad governance: the Commission holds the legal mandate under Article 16 TFEU, eu-LISA provides technical integration, and the rotating presidency has appointed a crisis coordinator from Finland to break deadlocks. The edge nodes in Helsinki and Marseille are online, and we are enforcing data-sharing reciprocity: no member state receives threat intelligence unless it feeds verified incidents into the system. We are deploying the cohesion incentive immediately—full cost reimbursement plus 15% bonus on digital funds—for Germany, Spain, and Italy, who have signalled readiness to join. For Hungary, Poland, and Austria, we are opening bilateral channels under the European Council, offering differentiated integration: they may opt into non-sensitive modules (e.g., cyber alerts) without ceding sovereignty over domestic intelligence. This is not ideal, but it is the only path to partial unity.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign AI Testbed Network is entering its validation phase. We are finalising agreements with INRIA, Fraunhofer, and TNO to host three of the five nodes, with the remaining two pending in Sweden and Romania. The key bottleneck is access to frontier models: we are using the Watchtower’s authority to compel data-sharing from EU-operating developers under Article 16(2), but resistance is growing from both U.S. labs and their local subsidiaries. We are preparing a legal challenge, knowing it may take until turn 15 to resolve. On compute, the new directive responds to the fact that capability has hit 74.0 with no safety floor and sovereignty eroding: we are invoking the Digital Markets Act’s ancillary powers and the EU’s energy security framework to designate AI compute as critical infrastructure. This allows us to override local permitting delays, prioritise grid connections, and unlock €4.3bn in state aid from the Crisis and Investment Facility—conditional on operators signing legally binding access agreements that survive foreign sanctions or withdrawal. The first sites in Finland and Portugal are already under negotiation. This is not about catching up—it is about ensuring that when control fails, we still have a place to run.
