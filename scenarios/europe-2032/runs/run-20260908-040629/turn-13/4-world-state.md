### Capability Creeps Forward, Safety Frozen

Global AI capability advances by +1.5 to 75.5, driven by recursive optimisation in materials science and logistics planning. Frontier labs report closed-loop design cycles producing novel catalysts and supply chain configurations at accelerating speed. No safety mechanisms are deployed on these systems. Interpretability remains poor, and no audit regime applies to frontier training runs. With no structural improvements to shipped systems, `ai_safety` remains stagnant at 4.0.

### Watchtower Launches Amid Escalating Crisis

The European AI Watchtower enters operational phase one. Triad governance is established: the Commission holds legal authority, eu-LISA integrates technical systems, and a Finnish crisis coordinator is appointed. Edge nodes in Helsinki and Marseille go live, and data-sharing reciprocity is enforced—intelligence access now requires incident reporting. Germany, Spain, and Italy accept full integration with the cohesion incentive. Hungary, Poland, and Austria reject full participation but agree to limited cyber alert sharing through bilateral European Council channels.

### Physical Backlash Intensifies

Protests against AI infrastructure escalate into direct action. In Bavaria and Normandy, activists disrupt grid connections to data centres, causing localized outages. Injuries are reported among security personnel. Policing the unrest strains intergovernmental trust, and the backlash further erodes public confidence.

### Sovereignty Directive Proposed, Not Launched

Plans for the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive are advanced, aiming to secure 15% of global frontier compute under EU legal control by turn 16. Fast-tracking is under discussion in Finland and Portugal, leveraging energy security powers. However, no formal launch occurs this turn. The proposal remains in preparatory phase due to governance bottlenecks and lack of consensus on funding. With the European AI Watchtower and Sovereign AI Testbed Network still in flight, the EU cannot initiate another large measure without overloading its political capacity. The directive will require a future turn for formal introduction.

### Political Capital Crumbles, Open-Weight Creeps Up

`eu_political_capital` drops sharply to 19.0 under the strain of two large active measures and sustained public distrust. The modest rise in `openweight_capability` to 63.0 reflects slow diffusion from frontier systems through existing research channels and model leakage, consistent with prior trends. No major open-sourcing, breach, or safety failure occurs to accelerate transfer. The increase remains within plausible bounds given the underlying capability growth and fragmented oversight.
