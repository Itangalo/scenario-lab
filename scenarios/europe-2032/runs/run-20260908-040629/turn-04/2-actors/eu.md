## Priority  
`Taiwan Blockade Response: Secure Domestic Compute Resilience` — because the sudden severing of advanced semiconductor exports has turned compute sovereignty from a long-term ambition into an immediate security imperative, and our two-year commitment to act independently now demands we close the gap between legal jurisdiction and physical availability before the Union becomes a spectator in its own future.

## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that the Taiwan blockade has changed everything: the EU’s upstream position in lithography is now both its greatest strategic asset and its most dangerous exposure. The U.S. pressure on the Netherlands over ASML’s DUV exports has already triggered delays in maintenance and upgrades for critical customers — a warning that our leverage is only as strong as our resilience. We cannot afford to wait for the next escalation. This turn, the Commission invokes Article 356 TFEU on enhanced cooperation in times of crisis to fast-track the emergency procurement of 50,000 H100-equivalent GPUs, to be physically located and legally anchored within the EU by turn 6. We are negotiating binding agreements with Meta, AWS, and OVHcloud to ensure at least 30% of this capacity is available for public-sector use during supply shocks, in exchange for preferential access to EU energy and permitting. The European Defence Fund will co-finance the purchase, reframing compute as critical infrastructure.

At the same time, we are pushing the Openweight Containment Framework through COREPER II, offering Germany a seat on the audit board and France an exemption for national security models in exchange for support. The draft regulation will now include a sunset clause: if `openweight_capability` exceeds 50.0 before turn 5, the licensing threshold automatically drops to 42.0. The Cyber Resilience Surge concludes this turn with a final exercise across six member states’ energy grids — results show detection improved but response coordination remains weak, justifying continued investment in category 6. We are not slowing the Tech Sovereignty Package or the Gigafactories, but we are re-prioritizing their outputs: every new data centre must now include a sovereign partition, legally insulated from foreign control, to serve as a fallback node in a fragmented world.
