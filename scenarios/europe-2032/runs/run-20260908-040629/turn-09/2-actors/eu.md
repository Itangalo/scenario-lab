## In practice
This turn is about landing what we have built. The Emergency Compute Access Mechanism is now legally activated under Article 122 TFEU, with 30,000 H100-equivalent GPUs registered in the EU Compute Ledger across AWS, OVHcloud, and national providers in eight member states. The mechanism’s activation criteria — systems rated above 60.0 capability during a declared incident — are codified in the Council decision, and ENISA has begun its first audit cycle, focusing on access controls and failover protocols for public-sector AI in health, energy, and elections. The sunset clause of 2032 holds, but the integration of DUV tools from ALAI into domestic fab efforts in Dresden and Grenoble, co-financed by the European Defence Fund, signals our intent to make this crisis instrument the foundation of long-term supply-side resilience.

We are also confronting quiet fragmentation. The emergent Regional Data Pact, now in its fourth turn, risks fracturing the single market under the guise of national enforcement. While we respect member-state concerns, we cannot allow de facto data walls to erode the sovereignty ECAM was designed to secure. The Commission has opened informal consultations, but our leverage lies in proving that central coordination delivers: the first cross-sector failover test under ECAM protocols will run in December, involving live rerouting of election integrity systems in two member states. If successful, it will demonstrate that sovereignty is not abstract — it is operational. If not, the political cost will be high, and the Regional Data Pact may harden into a rupture.

Public sentiment remains at 20.0, anchored by the ongoing disinformation wave and the automated decision scandal in social insurance systems. We are not blind to the anger, but we cannot answer it with restriction alone. The Openweight Containment Framework’s 45.0 threshold remains dormant — crossing it would ban all open models now in use, and legitimacy would collapse. Instead, we are working through national ombudsmen to map harm patterns, aiming for targeted, evidence-based interventions next turn. For now, our mandate is clear: act under uncertainty, finish what we started, and prove that the EU can still move when it matters.
