### Patching at speed
Autumn brought the first defensive good news in years. Engineers across cooperating hospitals, grids and logistics hubs began deploying automated patching that closed vulnerabilities almost as fast as scanners found them, alongside detection that flagged coordinated machine behaviour rather than known signatures. Where it was installed, intrusions that once took days of manual re-checks were contained in hours.

The rollout was deliberately narrow. ENISA and CERT-EU pushed the tooling first through the joint watch floor to sites already sharing telemetry — the same hospitals and ministries re-pointed to EU-hosted models after the February cutoff. Those sites saw real relief. Elsewhere, restoration still meant locked backups and slow queues.

### The warehouse fight
At the same time, foreign vendors of warehouse robotics hardened their terms: new logistics buyers were offered machines only with locked maintenance, remote diagnostics and spares held abroad. Several city councils and port operators refused to sign, parking new units and demanding local servicing, open diagnostics and EU-held spares.

Brussels backed the refusal with competition and procurement leverage and a repair-rights implementing act, folding the work into the continuity programme rather than building a parallel one. Insurers were invited to certify patched sites as a path to the long-delayed liability statute, but stayed observing. Vendors complied in public where contracts were at stake and stalled in private on parts and codes.

### Two speeds, again
The verified assistant rollout completed, bringing logged, human-checked aides to consenting town halls with quiet gains in junior output. Refusing councils pointed to the still-frozen benefits system and stayed out.

Continuity held where cooperation existed, but dependence did not lift. The side-deal capital kept its separate access arrangement, the cutoff was managed as degradation rather than reversed, and trust remained split between visible help and visible humiliation.
