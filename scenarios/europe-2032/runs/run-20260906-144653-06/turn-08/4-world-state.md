### Containment and Crack

The EU pressed forward on multiple fronts, but under mounting strain. The **Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative** reached its final deployment phase in France, Finland, and the Netherlands, with real-time monitoring now active across Tier 1 energy and health infrastructure. Automated defences intercepted two coordinated intrusion attempts during Q1 drills—early validation of the system’s value. However, Poland and Hungary remain outside the framework, their resistance hardened by legal challenges and political friction over Cohesion Fund conditionality.

Meanwhile, the **Emergency Biosecurity Watch**, now the named priority, expanded to include non-institutional research following the emergence of a decentralized AI research collective. EAAD and ECDC issued joint audit directives, but jurisdictional reach remains tenuous—open science advocates argue that computational provenance cannot override academic freedom. Europol is probing resource chains, but enforcement lags behind detection.

The Commission *proposed* the **Pan-European Adjustment Mechanism**, a large-scale social protection floor funded by a 5% levy on firms with AI-driven productivity gains above 30%. While the proposal aims to convert backlash into stability, it has not advanced beyond initial drafting and inter-service consultation. Member states have raised concerns over harmonised labour costs, and corporate actors have signalled strong opposition to certification complexity. No implementation occurred this turn; the measure remains in formulation and will require significant political capital to advance in future turns.

Despite these efforts, momentum eroded. Public sentiment remained stagnant at 22.0, as sectoral strikes highlighted the human cost of transition. The political capital required to sustain two major initiatives—Cyber Shield and Biosecurity Watch—proved substantial. The attempt to launch a third, even in proposal form, stretched capacity thin, reflected in only partial progress on cohesion: Germany’s participation in the cyber shield was secured only through last-minute RRF bargaining.

AI capability advanced steadily to 65.0, driven by closed-lab iteration. Open-weight models absorbed recent breakthroughs more gradually than initially projected, resulting in a measured increase in openweight_capability to 54.5. With no safety measure landing on shipped systems this turn, and rising capability outpacing assurance, ai_safety dipped slightly.

### Sovereignty’s Slow Bleed

The Union’s compute dependence deepened under U.S. export controls, and with no sovereign capacity completed this turn, eu_ai_sovereignty declined further despite ongoing initiatives.
