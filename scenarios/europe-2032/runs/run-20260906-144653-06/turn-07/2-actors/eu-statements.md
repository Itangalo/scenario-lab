# Statements: The European Union (turn 7)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI infrastructure with full operational control, energy autonomy, and real-time auditability by turn 7 — **retired**
- `survival_first` (commitment): Prevent cascading systemic collapse from AI-enabled attacks on critical infrastructure by ensuring minimum operational continuity across health, energy, and governance systems, regardless of sovereignty status

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign AI infrastructure with full operational control, energy autonomy, and real-time auditability by turn 7 — **retired**
  - Trigger: the failure to achieve meaningful implementation by turn 7 despite legal, technical, and political efforts
  - Grounds: The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has concluded, yet binding energy allocations remain blocked by ENTSO-E and member-state dissent. The Sovereign Cyber Shield, while launched, is delayed by legal challenges from Poland, Hungary, and Germany. Real-time auditability is undermined by Italy and Hungary’s refusal to enable EAAD logging. By turn 7 — the deadline we staked ourselves on — none of the core conditions of sovereignty are in force. The commitment is broken not by lack of effort, but by the Union’s inability to compel compliance within its own structure. We must now shift from a promise of delivery to a strategy of survival and reconstitution.
  - Evidence quoted: The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme officially concluded but remains unimplemented in key regions. ENTSO-E still refuses binding allocations without unanimity. The Commission invoked Article 194 TFEU to override objections, but litigation looms. Only partial microgrid progress was made in Sweden and Brittany.
  - Reason: The failure to implement energy and sovereignty measures by turn 7 directly undermines the conditions of the commitment, changing the cost and feasibility of keeping it.
- **add** `survival_first` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Prevent cascading systemic collapse from AI-enabled attacks on critical infrastructure by ensuring minimum operational continuity across health, energy, and governance systems, regardless of sovereignty status
  - Grounds: With resilience at 38.0, public sentiment at 22.0, and a biosecurity precursor event now active, the immediate threat is not strategic disadvantage but societal fracture. We are no longer building sovereign AI in time — we are trying to keep the lights on while the foundations fail. This is not a reversal of ambition, but a recalibration to the reality that sovereignty without continuity is a monument, not a shield.
