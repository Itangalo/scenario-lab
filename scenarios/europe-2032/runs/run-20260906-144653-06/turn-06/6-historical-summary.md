The EU launched the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative in response to a major AI-propagated cyberattack on health and grid systems, allocating €45bn via the EIB and tasking ENISA with deployment, though legal challenges from Poland, Hungary, and Germany delay implementation until at least turn 7. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme concluded but remains largely unimplemented due to ENTSO-E's refusal to bind grid allocations without unanimity, despite the Commission invoking Article 194 TFEU, triggering impending legal action. Partial microgrid advances occurred in Sweden and Brittany. The Tech Sovereignty Package and EAAD were formally established, securing private investment and certifying initial AI models, but EAAD’s enforcement power is undermined by Italy and Hungary blocking real-time logging. Public trust eroded further amid widespread job displacement in professional sectors and failed communication efforts. AI capability rose to 62.0 due to self-improving code generation, with open-weight models reaching 51.0, while safety and assurance mechanisms continue to lag behind technological advancement.
