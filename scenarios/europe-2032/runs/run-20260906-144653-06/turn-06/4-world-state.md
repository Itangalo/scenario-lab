### The Shield After the Storm

A cascading cyber intrusion—originating in a poisoned software library and propagating through AI-generated exploits—disrupted health data systems and grid coordination across five member states. Response was fragmented, attribution unclear. The attack underscored a harsh truth: even as the EU builds compute, its networks remain porous to autonomous threats.

In response, the European Union launched the **Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative**, a large category 6 measure aimed at hardening Tier 1 infrastructure with AI-audited defence stacks. ENISA began drafting deployment protocols, and the EIB reprofiled €45bn from expired programmes. However, legal resistance flared immediately: Poland and Hungary challenged the Commission’s authority to mandate integration, while Germany demanded cost-sharing guarantees. Deployment will not begin before turn 7.

Meanwhile, the **Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty** programme officially concluded but remains unimplemented in key regions. ENTSO-E still refuses binding allocations without unanimity. The Commission invoked Article 194 TFEU to override objections, but litigation looms. Only partial microgrid progress was made in Sweden and Brittany.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** and **EAAD establishment** finished this turn, delivering private capital commitments and initial model certifications. Yet EAAD’s authority is weakened by Italy and Hungary’s refusal to allow real-time logging in national systems.

Public sentiment dipped further. Labour protests intensified as law and accounting firms replaced junior staff with AI agents. A leaked internal assessment described “governance under strain.” The new communication drive failed to shift narratives.

AI capability advanced to 62.0, driven by self-improving code synthesis. Open-weight models reached 51.0, aided by global diffusion of near-frontier tools. No safety gains landed on deployed systems—assurance lags behind automation.
