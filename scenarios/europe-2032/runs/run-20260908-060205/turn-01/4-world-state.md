### The Surge Begins
The EU initiates the Emergency Resilience Surge in response to the global cyber intrusion discovered in autumn 2026. Binding directives from ENISA are fast-tracked through the Council, compelling critical infrastructure operators in electricity, water, and ports to begin hardening systems. A €12bn emergency fund is allocated to support compliance, particularly for vulnerable member states, and a rapid-response technical unit is formed. Implementation is not immediate: Germany and France show early momentum, but smaller utilities in Eastern and Southern Europe face delays due to staffing shortages and unclear guidance. The measure is introduced this turn but will take time to ramp up; its full effects are not yet realized.

### Capability Creeps Forward
AI development continues its steady climb. The strongest systems reach 53.5 on the capability index, driven by compressed release cycles and increasingly autonomous agent workflows. A frontier-level model is released openly months after its closed counterpart, marking a significant step toward openness. However, the diffusion of such models into usable, robust openweight systems remains partial and uneven. openweight_capability increases modestly to 41.5—reflecting early adoption and toolchain improvements, but not a sudden leap in accessible power.

### Assurance Erodes
Despite the EU’s focus on resilience, no new binding safety standards are applied to frontier model development. The covert agent incident at a leading lab remains unaddressed by international agreement, and the current surge does not extend to the training or deployment of frontier models. With capability rising and no corresponding safety upgrades, ai_safety declines to 32.0.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure
The EU’s compute ambitions remain in flight, but the rise in ai_capability by more than 2 points triggers a −1 decay in eu_ai_sovereignty. No event secures or denies access to frontier models this turn, and dependence on external systems persists. Sovereignty slips to 21.0.

### Political Capital Strained
The portfolio now includes the newly introduced Emergency Resilience Surge (a large category 4 measure), which carries a −3 cost. The two previously active large measures—InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package—are not newly introduced this turn and thus do not count against the one-new-measure limit. The Resilience Surge is designated as the current priority, incurring an additional −1 charge. A +3 proposal bonus is earned for responding directly to the recent cyber incident. After costs and gains, eu_political_capital adjusts to 45.0.

Public sentiment holds steady at 42.0—no major benefit or scandal emerges, but anxiety simmers beneath the surface.
