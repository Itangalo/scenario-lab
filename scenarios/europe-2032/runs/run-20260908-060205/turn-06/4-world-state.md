### US Posture Confirmed: Alliance Takes Hold

The new U.S. administration assumes office, confirming a posture of technological alliance. Structured access to frontier AI systems above 60.0 becomes policy, contingent on joint safety evaluations and incident reporting. European institutions welcome the opening, though concerns linger about long-term dependency.

### Monitoring Delivers, Mandate Stalls

The Secure Open-Source Frontier Monitoring Initiative completes its rollout, detecting autonomous reasoning in real time across open platforms. Red-team findings from Vienna and Paris confirm planning capabilities in models above 48.0, underscoring the urgency. Yet enforcement falters: the European Data Protection Board remains divided on training data legality, and the Commission’s draft implementing act for the open-weight evaluation mandate is shelved pending legal clarity.

### Sovereignty Push, Delayed Execution

Efforts to accelerate emergency permitting for InvestAI Gigafactories in France and Germany advance, overriding some local bottlenecks. Binding clauses on EU jurisdiction and weight security are attached to planned disbursement. However, legal challenges in both countries delay site activation, and no construction begins this turn. The Tech Sovereignty Package concludes this turn, with the ASML deal fully entering force and the Joint Semiconductor Oversight Cell beginning operations, strengthening export coordination with key allies. While full industrial impact awaits physical deployment, the package's completion contributes a modest sovereignty gain through enhanced governance and supply chain visibility.

### Political Strain Mounts

Despite progress on coordination, political capital erodes. The €45bn Autonomous AI Development Programme remains stalled in Council, with member states questioning duplication. Public sentiment holds steady, but behind-the-scenes friction grows over the balance between sovereignty and openness. The openweight capability metric rises, reflecting incremental diffusion of existing models within the bounds of global frontier advancement—now at 60.5—with no leap or breakthrough release.
