**Leverage ASML for Coordinated Export Control Framework**

We are launching a formal EU-level coordination mechanism to align export controls on critical semiconductor manufacturing equipment, beginning with ASML’s EUV and deep-UV lithography systems, to prevent unilateral decisions under external pressure and to strengthen the Union’s strategic autonomy in AI-relevant supply chains.

## Priority  
**Secure Open-Source Frontier Monitoring Initiative** — because the openweight_frontier_release event has collapsed the time-to-diffusion window, making post-release evaluation obsolete: if we do not monitor open models at the edge, we lose oversight of capability permanently, and no amount of compute sovereignty can compensate for that gap.

## In practice  
We are activating the AI Office’s mandate under Article 46 of the AI Act to stand up the Secure Open-Source Frontier Monitoring Initiative, deploying automated scanning infrastructure to track model releases across GitHub, Hugging Face, and peer-to-peer networks. The Joint Research Centre is building secure testbeds in collaboration with ELLIS nodes in Vienna and Paris, where downloaded weights will be evaluated for autonomous planning, tool use, and reasoning depth using red-teaming protocols adapted from the U.S. AI Safety Institute. We are negotiating with open-source stewards like the Hugging Face governance team and the EleutherAI collective to co-design transparency norms — offering €200m in fellowships and safety tooling grants in exchange for early notification and access. Resistance is high among decentralised developers, so we are framing this not as oversight but as shared responsibility: when capability spreads this fast, safety must be open too.

Simultaneously, we are responding to the emergent_supply_chain_leverage event by convening DG Trade, the European External Action Service, and ASML in a closed forum under the auspices of the Competitiveness Council to draft a coordinated export control framework for lithography equipment. This is not about blocking U.S. allies — it is about ensuring the EU is not left out when decisions are made. We are proposing a tripartite consultation mechanism with the U.S. and Japan that respects alliance ties but formalises our voice, using the Trade and Technology Council as the venue. ASML has signalled willingness, but only if the EU offers legal shielding from retaliation and a clear industrial roadmap in return. We are drafting a Council Implementing Regulation under the Dual-Use Regulation to institutionalise this coordination by turn 5, backed by a €1.2bn package of state aid conditionality for semiconductor infrastructure — tying future access to EU gigafactories to compliance with collective export decisions. This is sovereignty in practice: not just building capacity, but controlling the levers that make it strategic.
