### The Push for Open-Weight Control

The EU advances its strategy to govern open-weight AI, launching a binding evaluation mandate for high-capability models. The Secure Open-Source Frontier Monitoring Initiative now flags autonomous reasoning in real time, and the AI Office drafts emergency regulations under the Digital Services Act to compel pre-release disclosure above a 45.0 threshold. Hugging Face agrees to test automated compliance checks, but decentralized platforms reject the framework as illegitimate. A parallel open audit commons is launched, yet skepticism grows that transparency can be enforced where code is borderless.

### Sovereignty Gambit with ASML

The Commission finalizes a bilateral agreement with ASML: the firm will notify the EU of all EUV export applications and defer shipments for 30 days. In return, it gains legal shielding from extraterritorial sanctions and access to €1.2bn in R&D co-funding. While not full control, it marks the EU’s first formal leverage point in the semiconductor supply chain. Germany and the Netherlands back the deal, but Ireland and Malta abstain, citing risks of U.S. retaliation. The Competitiveness Council approves the regulation by a narrow margin.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

Global AI capability rises to 58.0 as self-improving loops accelerate training. Openweight_capability follows to 49.0, with a new model enabling autonomous cyber operations on consumer hardware. Despite the new mandate, no enforcement has yet occurred, and the open frontier remains opaque. ai_safety inches up slightly due to institutional momentum, but no shipped systems carry new safeguards.

### Political Capital Erodes

The EU runs five large measures simultaneously, including the new category 3 mandate. With political capital strained, the priority yields no acceleration. Transatlantic friction and internal dissent limit gains, and public sentiment holds steady—neither scandal nor breakthrough shifts the mood.
