### A Fractured Response

The EU closes the Cyber Resilience Emergency Acceleration Directive with partial success: automated failover and detection upgrades prevent cascading collapse during a second wave of AI-generated ransomware, earning resilience a +4 boost. Yet Italy and Poland continue to withhold real-time data, crippling coordinated response. The European Cyber Incident Response Corps remains undermanned and untested, its authority questioned.

Amid rising alarm, the EU launches the European AI Incident Response Protocol and proposes the Mandatory Compute Registry, aiming to enforce real-time monitoring of high-capability agents. But political capital is too low to absorb the cost. With two large measures already in flight and a priority declared, the portfolio charge is severe. The emergency Council session under Article 4 fails to secure binding data-sharing commitments — cohesion fund threats are seen as heavy-handed, and backlash grows.

The Sovereign AI Safety Assurance Programme, narrowed to systems above 55.0 operating in critical infrastructure, faces transatlantic resistance. U.S. labs warn of compliance burdens, and a major frontier developer suspends updates to EU-facing models. Germany and the Netherlands urge compromise, fearing innovation flight. The fast-track pathway offers relief, but the rejection of self-attestation hardens opposition.

Public sentiment dips further as media frame the EU’s actions as reactive and divisive. The French automated decision scandal lingers, and a new report reveals a surge in AI-driven fraud, feeding anxiety. Labour unions protest the lack of transition support as mid-level administrative roles vanish.

Meanwhile, a member state quietly signs a bilateral compute-sharing deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, bypassing EU oversight — a defection that undermines the Union’s unified stance and signals weakening cohesion. The move triggers no immediate crisis, but it erodes trust in collective governance.

AI capability advances to 65.0, driven by improved agent coordination. Open-weight models reach 58.0, as efficient fine-tuning spreads capabilities widely. But no safety improvements land on deployed systems: interpretability gaps persist, and the control deficit widens. The gap between capability and safety now exceeds 50 points — a dangerous threshold.
