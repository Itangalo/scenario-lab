### A Cyber Shock, and a Sprint to Respond

A major AI-generated cyberattack cripples hospital networks in three member states and disrupts regional power grids. The malware, adaptive and self-propagating, evades traditional signatures, forcing manual recovery. The incident confirms fears: offensive AI capabilities now outpace public defences. The EU invokes emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU, launching the Cyber Resilience Emergency Acceleration Directive. Binding deployment orders go out for AI-driven intrusion detection in critical infrastructure, backed by €18bn in redirected funds.

### Emergency Measures, Fractured Unity

The European Cyber Incident Response Corps is formally established, though staffing lags behind plan. France and Germany accept joint command in principle but delay integration, citing constitutional concerns over foreign access to national systems. Mutual aid protocols remain untested. Still, automated failover systems are installed in 60% of Tier 1 services by June, and detection pilot programs show promise. The category 6 measure begins to yield partial benefits.

### Sovereignty Holds, Safety Erodes

Two category 4 initiatives—InvestAI Gigafactories and the Autonomous AI Development Programme—formally conclude. However, physical rollout remains behind schedule due to energy and permitting bottlenecks. A member state’s separate deal with a foreign provider persists, limiting cohesion gains. With ai_capability rising by 0.5, sovereignty holds steady at 26.0. But ai_safety drops to 22.0, as no safety-focused measure landed and the attack reveals systemic blind spots.

### Political Capital Spent, Public Fear Grows

The new directive incurs a −3 cost for a large measure, and −1 for priority status. With no finishing measures this turn, political capital falls to 17.0. Public sentiment drops to 32.0 amid fear of AI-driven attacks and frustration over disrupted services. Media frames the crisis as one of dependency and delay.
