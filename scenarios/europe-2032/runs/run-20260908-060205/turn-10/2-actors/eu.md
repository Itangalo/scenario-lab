## New measure
**Cyber Resilience Emergency Acceleration Directive**  
To immediately harden critical public services against AI-generated cyber threats in response to the major incident, focusing on detection, failover, and public continuity.

- `Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
- `Size: large`  
- `Finishes on turn: 12`  
- `Targeted effect:` Increase resilience from 52.0 to at least 60.0 within one year; reduce time-to-detection and time-to-response across healthcare, energy, and transport sectors; ensure essential services degrade rather than collapse under coordinated attack.  
- `Applies to:` EU member states, with binding obligations for national critical infrastructure operators and cross-border service providers.

## Priority  
Cyber Resilience Emergency Acceleration Directive — because the cyber_major_incident has crossed a threshold: when offensive capability is AI-generated and defensive readiness is lagging, the Union cannot afford to wait for its sovereignty build-out to finish; we must protect what we have while we build what we need.

## In practice  
We are declaring a state of cyber emergency in response to the attack. This is not a declaration of war, but of operational urgency. Under Article 122 TFEU and the NIS2 directive, we are invoking emergency powers to fast-track implementation of detection systems, automated failover protocols, and cross-border mutual aid in cyber response. The Commission is issuing a binding implementing act this quarter, requiring all member states to deploy AI-powered intrusion detection systems—vetted for robustness and interpretability—within six months in all Tier 1 services: power grids, hospitals, rail networks, and emergency communications. We are repurposing €18bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme to fund this, with disbursements tied to verified deployment, not just planning.

We are also standing up a European Cyber Incident Response Corps: a standing team of 1,200 engineers and red-team specialists, drawn from national CERTs, Eurojust, and pre-vetted private partners, trained and exercised quarterly to respond to cascading incidents. Their mandate includes pre-authorised access to critical systems during declared emergencies, with oversight by the European Data Protection Supervisor to prevent mission creep. The French and German interior ministries have raised concerns about sovereignty, but we are offering joint command and shared threat intelligence in return for access. This is not about control—it is about survival. The attack did not discriminate by border, and neither can our defence.
