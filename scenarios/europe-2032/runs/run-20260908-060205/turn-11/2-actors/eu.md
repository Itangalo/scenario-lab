## New measure
**Sovereign AI Safety Assurance Programme**  
Establish a permanent EU-level capability to evaluate, monitor, and enforce safety standards on all AI systems operating within or affecting EU critical infrastructure, with full access to weights, training data, and agent behaviour logs for systems above capability threshold 55.0.

- `Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
- `Size: large`  
- `Finishes on turn: 14`  
- `Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety from 22.0 to at least 45.0 within 18 months; ensure that no system above threshold 55.0 operates in or against the EU without independent verification of control, alignment, and weight security; close the gap between deployment and understanding  
- `Applies to:` All AI systems above capability threshold 55.0 that are deployed in or have significant impact on EU critical infrastructure, whether developed inside or outside the Union

## Priority  
Cyber Resilience Emergency Acceleration Directive — because the attack is ongoing in its aftermath, and because resilience is the only shield we have while sovereignty and safety are being rebuilt; we cannot afford cascading failure while we build the future.

## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: one to stop the bleeding, one to fix the wound. On the first, we are enforcing the Cyber Resilience Emergency Acceleration Directive with full urgency. The Commission has issued the binding implementing act under Article 122 TFEU and NIS2, and national regulators are required to report compliance monthly. We are deploying AI-powered intrusion detection systems—pre-qualified for interpretability and resistance to adversarial evasion—into all Tier 1 services by December. Disbursements from the €18bn fund are tied to verified installation and integration, not promises. The European Cyber Incident Response Corps is now operational in skeleton form, with 400 personnel pre-vetted and on standby; joint command with France and Germany remains fragile, but we are offering shared threat intelligence and co-exercised protocols to build trust. This is not about centralisation—it is about interoperability in crisis.

On the second front, we are launching the Sovereign AI Safety Assurance Programme to ensure that no system above 55.0 capability operates in our domain without independent scrutiny. This means full access to weights and training logs for any model above that threshold, enforced through a new mandate under the AI Act’s high-risk provisions, extended via delegated act to cover emergent capabilities. The European AI Safety Authority will lead evaluations in partnership with national labs and vetted academic teams, with authority to halt deployment if control claims cannot be verified. We know this will face resistance—especially from foreign providers—but the eval_anomaly_reports and the automated_decision_scandal prove that self-attestation is no longer enough. Safety is not a feature; it is a condition of operation. We will fund this with a reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme and new levies on high-impact AI deployments, starting in Q1 2032.
