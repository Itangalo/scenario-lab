## New measure  
**Secure Open-Source Frontier Monitoring Initiative**

Establish a permanent EU-led capability to monitor, evaluate, and respond to open-weight AI releases within days of their appearance, ensuring that distributed frontier models do not become blind spots for safety and security.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +8 to +12, resilience +3 to +5 via early detection of misuse potential in open models; eu_ai_sovereignty +5 via independent technical posture  
`Applies to:` EU AI Office, ENISA, national research labs, vetted open-source communities, public evaluation consortia  

## Priority  
**Secure Open-Source Frontier Monitoring Initiative** — because the openweight_frontier_release event has collapsed the time-to-diffusion window, making post-release evaluation obsolete: if we do not monitor open models at the edge, we lose oversight of capability permanently, and no amount of compute sovereignty can compensate for that gap.

## In practice  
We are activating the AI Office’s mandate under Article 46 of the AI Act to establish a new standing evaluation function focused exclusively on open-weight models. This will be hosted jointly by the EU AI Office and ENISA, with technical backbone provided by the Joint Research Centre and academic partners in the European Laboratory for Learning and Intelligent Systems (ELLIS). The initiative will deploy automated scanning infrastructure to track major code repositories and peer-to-peer distribution channels, triggering rapid evaluation protocols when a new model exceeds thresholds in reasoning depth, tool use, or autonomous planning. These evaluations will feed directly into the EU’s incident reporting framework and inform potential restrictions under the AI Act’s high-risk classification.

We are allocating €1.8bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe reserves to stand up the system by turn 5, including secure testbeds, red-teaming units, and a network of trusted researchers with pre-approved access to run interpretability tools on downloaded weights. Crucially, we are negotiating with open-source maintainers and community leaders — not to control them, but to co-develop transparency norms and early warning signals. Resistance is expected from decentralised developers who see this as surveillance, so we are pairing the mandate with funding for open safety tooling and a public fellowship programme to embed EU-supported researchers within trusted community projects. This is not about stopping openness — it is about ensuring that openness does not become a vector for uncontrolled risk. The world has changed: when the frontier is open, sovereignty means understanding what’s out there, not just what we build.
