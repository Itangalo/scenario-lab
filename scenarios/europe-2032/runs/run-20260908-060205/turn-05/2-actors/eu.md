## New measure
**Launch EU Autonomous AI Development Programme with Sovereign Compute and Talent Pipeline**

We are initiating a coordinated, publicly governed effort to develop and operate autonomous AI systems within the EU’s legal and physical boundaries, using sovereign infrastructure and protected talent, to ensure we can act independently when capability thresholds cross into strategic territory.

## In practice
We are closing the monitoring gap this turn: the Secure Open-Source Frontier Monitoring Initiative has delivered its core capability, with real-time detection now active across Hugging Face, GitHub, and IPFS mirrors. Vienna and Paris have confirmed autonomous planning in two open models above 48.0, and we are publishing red-team findings to justify enforcement. But the evaluation mandate remains unimplemented — legal uncertainty from the emergent regulatory fracture among data protection authorities has stalled the Commission’s draft implementing act. We are now convening the European Data Protection Board for an emergency session under Article 70(1)(d) of the GDPR to force a unified interpretation on training data legality, offering strict audit requirements in return for legal clarity. Without this, the mandate collapses into patchwork enforcement.

At the same time, the ASML deal is law: the notification and 30-day deferral protocol is now embedded in the Dual-Use Regulation, with the €1.2bn resilience fund unlocked upon Commission certification of compliance. Ireland and Malta abstained, but we secured their non-opposition by allocating 40% of the fund to semiconductor R&D nodes in Cork and Msida, co-hosted with Trinity College and the University of Malta. This is not control, but it is leverage — and we are activating the middle-power coalition this week, aligning export-licence reviews with Japan, Taiwan, and South Korea through a new Joint Semiconductor Oversight Cell, hosted in Brussels.

But the election alliance changes everything. The U.S. offer of structured access is real, immediate, and technically generous — allied institutions will soon gain vetted access to models at 60.0+ capability. The trap is clear: relief now kills the urgency to build. That is why we are launching the EU Autonomous AI Development Programme now, not later. We are repurposing €45bn from the InvestAI envelope to co-fund a sovereign training stack: three exascale clusters on EU soil, legally bound to EU jurisdiction, running under EuroHPC with ELLIS-led research teams and a new AI Safety Directorate embedded in the AI Office. We are recruiting talent with fast-track visas and protected status, and we are mandating that all software developed with this compute carries EU-only deployment clauses. This is not a rival to the U.S. partnership — it is the condition that makes it safe to accept it. Without our own capacity, alignment becomes dependence. With it, we bargain.
