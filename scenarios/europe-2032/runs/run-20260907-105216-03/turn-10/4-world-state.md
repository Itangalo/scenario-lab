### The Cut and the Response

In January 2031, access to the world’s most advanced AI systems vanished for EU institutions overnight. Hospitals lost diagnostic support, border agencies faced processing halts, and research stalled—no explanation came, only silence. The disruption confirmed a new reality: Europe’s critical functions depend on foreign intelligence it cannot control.

The Commission responded by launching the Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative, repurposing stalled data centres in Finland, Luxembourg, and Bulgaria. Legal authority was invoked under crisis protocols, and funding redirected from digital rollout budgets. Despite fast-tracked approvals, northern member states resisted, citing unfair advantages and energy strain. Concessions were made—co-location rights and shared staffing—but delays mounted. Construction begins in Q2, but no operational capacity comes online this turn. The measure is high-cost and will take multiple turns to complete; early progress earns partial credit only.

### A Continent Reacts, Not Leads

Europe builds redundancy not because the future demands it, but because exclusion left no choice. The response is real—but reactive, underfunded, and strained. No measure concludes fully this turn except the Sentinel Nodes. The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative is underway but far from complete, and its benefits remain prospective. The portfolio remains overloaded, with no prior measure retired, limiting overall effectiveness.
