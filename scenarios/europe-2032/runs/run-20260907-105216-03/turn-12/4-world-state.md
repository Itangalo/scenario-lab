### The Fracture Deepens

Global AI capability leaps to 97.5 as a single breakthrough—self-verifying reasoning in high-dimensional systems—enables autonomous scientific and engineering design. The jump is abrupt, rendering prior forecasts obsolete. Defensive frameworks struggle under the weight of uncheckable outputs. Open-weight models, now at 80.5, distribute much of this power. A widely shared, fine-tuned variant of a frontier model allows local actors to run near-frontier inference—no longer just reasoning, but planning.

A major cyber incident sweeps across municipal services in Germany and France. Emergency dispatch systems are rerouted, water treatment logs falsified, and benefit portals locked. The attack is adaptive, evading detection for days. Attribution remains unclear, but forensic analysis points to AI-generated tooling. Defenders are outpaced.

### EU’s Sovereignty Gambit

The Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory officially completes, delivering a unified telemetry stack. But Paris and Warsaw withhold real-time data, citing national sovereignty. In response, the Commission conditions access to emergency compute clusters on full data sharing—triggering a diplomatic standoff.

The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative, now prioritized, sees partial progress. Finland accelerates grid work in Kajaani. Bulgaria repurposes a military site under emergency EuroHPC authority, though legal challenges loom. Luxembourg’s hydrogen backup plan advances, but slowly.

Plans for Sovereign Execution Nodes—intended to create autonomous response sites in Kajaani, Sofia, and Bologna—are drafted and debated. However, with two major measures already in flight and political capital critically low, the Commission defers formal launch. Legal design begins, but full implementation is shelved pending resolution of the current portfolio.

Public sentiment remains at rock bottom, unchanged at 10.0. No visible benefit offsets the scandal and disruption.

### Political Collapse

With two large measures in flight and a priority, political capital is drained. The cyber incident and capability jump deepen the crisis. Member-state resistance hardens. Estonia and Austria signal support for the emergent municipal sovereignty network, bypassing Brussels entirely.
