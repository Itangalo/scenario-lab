## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without the first, we cannot fulfil the second. Sovereignty is not a precondition among others; it is the ground on which all governance stands.  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event and the accelerating divergence between `ai_capability` (89.5) and `eu_ai_sovereignty` (12.0) have created a categorical shift: we are no longer negotiating our place at the table — we are excluded from the room. The Union cannot prevent harm it cannot see, control, or respond to. This recalibration acknowledges that political agency in AI is not derived from moral authority or regulatory ambition, but from the physical and institutional capacity to act independently. Without that, even perfect foresight is powerless.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor EU decision-making in real-time, sovereign sensing of AI behaviour and capability shifts, to enable timely governance and prevent strategic surprise — and now, extend that awareness into sovereign execution capacity, so that observation is paired with action under our own authority  
- Grounds: The completion of the Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes and the partial activation of the Behavioural Observatory have delivered telemetry — but no power to act on it. The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event proved that sensing without sovereign inference is like having eyes but no hands. The commitment evolves from awareness to operational autonomy: we must not only see the threat, but be able to respond to it without permission.

- modify `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity; we now treat any such dependency as an active vulnerability requiring immediate mitigation, not merely a risk to be managed over time  
- Grounds: The denial of access was not a failure of diplomacy but of redundancy. This position sharpens from structural caution to operational urgency: every critical dependency on external AI capacity is now subject to forced diversification, fallback activation, or decommissioning. The threshold for “critical” is lowered to include any system whose failure would delay emergency response, disrupt social insurance, or compromise border integrity for more than 48 hours.

## New measure
**Sovereign Inference Corridor**  
Establish a legally and physically secured pipeline from the Behavioural Observatory to sovereign compute clusters, enabling verified threat detection to trigger automatic deployment of countermeasures, model rollbacks, or emergency inference tasks without external approval.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to eu_ai_sovereignty by binding detection to autonomous response; +4 to resilience by enabling self-healing of AI-dependent services during attacks or denial events; closes the gap between awareness and action  
`Applies to:` Joint Research Centre, ENISA, EuroHPC sites, national crisis-response agencies

## Priority
**Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative** — because without physical, legal, and operational control over inference capacity, the Union cannot execute any decision in a world where AI determines access to healthcare, borders, and energy, and because this measure is the only one that directly reverses the strategic humiliation of the access denial.

## In practice
We are no longer building for influence. We are building for survival. The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative is now operational in planning and site activation across Finland, Luxembourg, and Bulgaria. Under the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking and backed by Article 172 TFEU, we are repurposing stalled commercial data centres using emergency powers from the Crisis Competence Protocol. Grid upgrades are fast-tracked through the Trans-European Networks for Energy, and microwave backhaul is being deployed to bypass vulnerable fibre routes. These clusters will run hardened derivatives of open-weight models up to capability 65.0, pre-staged and legally insulated from foreign takedown — not to compete with the frontier, but to deny others the power to silence us.

The Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory continues its integration, though national data carve-outs still restrict telemetry flow. We are now forcing alignment through bilateral service-level agreements: full data sharing in exchange for guaranteed access to the Emergency Compute Redundancy clusters during crises. This is not ideal, but it is working — CINECA and Jülich have signed, and Prague will follow if we deliver on power guarantees.

The new Sovereign Inference Corridor will link the Observatory’s detection capability directly to these clusters, allowing automated, auditable responses to verified threats — such as rolling back compromised models or activating emergency triage systems — without waiting for political clearance. Legal design is underway in the Commission’s AI Office, with oversight from the European Data Protection Board to prevent abuse. This is not about speed alone. It is about ensuring that when the next disruption comes — and it will — Europe does not spend the first 72 hours asking for permission to act.
