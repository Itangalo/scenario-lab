# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience
- `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity; we now treat any such dependency as an active vulnerability requiring immediate mitigation, not merely a risk to be managed over time

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without the first, we cannot fulfil the second. Sovereignty is not a precondition among others; it is the ground on which all governance stands.
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event and the accelerating divergence between `ai_capability` (89.5) and `eu_ai_sovereignty` (12.0) have created a categorical shift: we are no longer negotiating our place at the table — we are excluded from the room. The Union cannot prevent harm it cannot see, control, or respond to. This recalibration acknowledges that political agency in AI is not derived from moral authority or regulatory ambition, but from the physical and institutional capacity to act independently. Without that, even perfect foresight is powerless.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Anchor EU decision-making in real-time, sovereign sensing of AI behaviour and capability shifts, to enable timely governance and prevent strategic surprise — and now, extend that awareness into sovereign execution capacity, so that observation is paired with action under our own authority
  - Grounds: The completion of the Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes and the partial activation of the Behavioural Observatory have delivered telemetry — but no power to act on it. The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event proved that sensing without sovereign inference is like having eyes but no hands. The commitment evolves from awareness to operational autonomy: we must not only see the threat, but be able to respond to it without permission.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `no_single_point_of_failure` — applied
  - Tier: position
  - Text: No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity; we now treat any such dependency as an active vulnerability requiring immediate mitigation, not merely a risk to be managed over time
  - Grounds: The denial of access was not a failure of diplomacy but of redundancy. This position sharpens from structural caution to operational urgency: every critical dependency on external AI capacity is now subject to forced diversification, fallback activation, or decommissioning. The threshold for “critical” is lowered to include any system whose failure would delay emergency response, disrupt social insurance, or compromise border integrity for more than 48 hours.
