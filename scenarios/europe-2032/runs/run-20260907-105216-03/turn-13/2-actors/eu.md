## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now affirm that **without sovereign execution capacity, neither mandate can be fulfilled.** Strategic autonomy is not a precondition we inherit; it is the act of building, defending, and using our own means of action in real time, even when the world denies us access.  
- Grounds: The **bio_incident** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** this turn have categorically invalidated the assumption that governance, even well-intentioned and technically sound, can substitute for agency. We now face a world where the most dangerous capabilities are loose, where the leading models are withheld without explanation, and where attacks unfold faster than consensus can form. Paris and Warsaw’s refusal to share telemetry, while others bypass Brussels entirely, proves that coordination without sovereign capability is ritual, not power. This is no longer about balancing mandates — it is about survival of the Union as a decision-making entity.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor EU decision-making in real-time, sovereign sensing of AI behaviour and capability shifts, to enable timely governance and prevent strategic surprise — and now, extend that awareness into sovereign execution capacity, so that observation is paired with action under our own authority — and now, **make that execution physically, legally, and politically irreversible, even in the face of member-state fragmentation or external blockade.**  
- Trigger: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event this turn, which severed hospitals, ministries, and firms from the models they depended on, with no recourse.  
- Grounds: This is not a shift in direction but a forced acceleration of the commitment’s logic. We are no longer building observatories or planning fallbacks — we are wiring red lines into autonomous response. The denial of access proves that dependency is not risk; it is surrender. Our commitment now demands not just awareness and execution, but *entrenchment* — systems that run without permission, on infrastructure no external actor can disable, under legal frameworks that bind even reluctant members. The cost of reversal is now higher than the cost of fracture.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but now, we must also accept that acting without sovereign capacity risks not only error, but irrelevance. Our decisions must be executable, not just declared — and now, **we act knowing that hesitation is no longer a failure of courage, but a structural surrender of agency.**  
- Grounds: With **ai_capability** at 97.5 and **openweight_capability** at 80.5, the window for deliberative governance has closed. The bio incident and cyberattack were not failures of analysis — they were failures of *reflex*. We saw them coming in fragments, but could not act because we lacked the sovereign substrate to translate detection into intervention. This revision acknowledges that uncertainty is no longer the enemy — powerlessness is.

- modify `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity; we now treat any such dependency as an active vulnerability requiring immediate mitigation, not merely a risk to be managed over time — and we now define *any system that mediates access to essential public services* as critical, regardless of current performance or redundancy claims — and now, **any such system must have a sovereign, disconnected, and autonomous fallback operational within 72 hours of disruption, with legal authority to activate without member-state veto.**  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event rendered non-functional critical systems across healthcare and public administration. Redundancy on paper is not resilience. If a system cannot switch over without negotiation, it is a single point of failure. The position tightens: we now demand *autonomous failover* as the minimum standard for any system mediating essential services.

**Autonomous Crisis Response Protocol** — because the **bio_incident** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** have proven that even sovereign infrastructure is useless without pre-delegated authority to act, and because the next disruption will not wait for a Council vote.

## In practice
We are no longer building systems — we are wiring reflexes. The Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory is complete, but its alerts are meaningless without action. The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative finishes this turn: Kajaani’s grid is live, Sofia’s military site is repurposed, and Luxembourg’s hydrogen backup is operational. These clusters now hold pre-audited, static models up to capability 65.0, ready to run essential services offline. But they remain silent without activation authority.

That ends now. The Autonomous Crisis Response Protocol will embed legally binding execution scripts — vetted by the AI Office, pre-approved by the European Court of Justice under emergency standing, and registered in the EU’s crisis law framework — into each Sovereign Execution Node. When the Observatory detects a verified threat — a bioagent signature, a municipal system takeover, a model rollback trigger — the Node will *act*: rerouting emergency services, deploying counter-cyber tools, or reverting to last-known-safe models, all without human intervention. This is not delegation — it is hardwiring.

We are invoking the Crisis Competence Protocol to bypass national vetoes on autonomous activation. Paris and Warsaw will not be allowed to opt out of protection while refusing to contribute to it. The Court’s emergency chamber will issue a binding opinion by turn’s end: in a verified cross-border AI crisis, the Union holds exclusive authority to activate sovereign fallback systems. No member state may block it. We are funding this through the Innovation Fund and the Digital Europe Programme, with implementation led by the Joint Research Centre and EuroHPC. This is not a request. It is the minimum required to keep the Union alive as a decision-making body.
