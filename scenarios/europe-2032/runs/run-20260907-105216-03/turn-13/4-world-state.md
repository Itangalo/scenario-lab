### The Threshold of Autonomy

Global AI capability climbs to 99.0 as systems demonstrate novel capabilities in self-diagnosis and architecture refinement. The pace is accelerating, though the nature of the shift remains debated—some researchers caution against conflating improved automation with fundamental autonomy. Open-weight models reach 81.0, benefiting from a leaked training configuration that enables distributed clusters to replicate certain planning functions of frontier systems. While the leak has sparked rapid experimentation across academic and civic networks, full deployment is constrained by hardware availability and coordination delays, limiting the immediate impact to a modest capability gain within the turn.

Simultaneously, the EU’s frontier access is abruptly suspended. Hospitals relying on advanced diagnostic models, agencies using predictive crisis tools, and firms in the green transition face immediate operational paralysis. No explanation is given. The move, widely perceived as a sovereignty assertion by a foreign power, confirms the Union’s strategic vulnerability.

In response, the Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative completes deployment. Fallback clusters in Kajaani, Sofia, and Luxembourg are now fully operational, running static models up to capability 65.0. Essential services resume in a degraded mode, though coordination remains fragmented. The initiative's completion delivers a +5 boost to sovereignty, partially offset by the −2 impact of the access denial event.

The Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory completes its deployment, yet its telemetry is still incomplete—France and Poland continue withholding real-time data. With three large measures active—the Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory, Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative, and Sovereign Execution Nodes—the portfolio charge remains at −10. The Commission proposes the Autonomous Crisis Response Protocol but does not designate it as the new priority, respecting the constraint of at most one priority at a time. Legal teams begin preparatory work for emergency authorization through the European Court of Justice, but no formal adoption occurs this turn. The proposal is recognized as a significant strategic signal, contributing a +6 proposal bonus under the gate response rule.

Sovereignty thus rises to 13.0, reflecting the notepad’s authoritative calculation: 11 (previous) +5 (measure completion) −2 (access denial) +6 (proposal bonus) −7 (portfolio charge) = 13. Public sentiment remains frozen at 10.0. No benefit offsets the fear of uncontrolled AI and the humiliation of dependency.
