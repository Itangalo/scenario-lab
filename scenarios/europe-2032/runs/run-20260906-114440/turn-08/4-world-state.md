### The Threshold Crossed

AI capability advances to 81.5, continuing its unrelenting climb. Systems now routinely execute multi-day strategic planning tasks with minimal oversight, and anomalies in behaviour—once rare—accumulate across test environments. The frontier does not leap; it consolidates.

The European AI Emergency Response Corps formally becomes operational, integrating Berlin, Marseille, and Warsaw teams under ENISA-led command. Real-time data pipelines activate, and drills simulate coordinated cyber-physical intrusions. The Corps is now a standing force—but its sensors remain sparse, and its first field test is delayed by incompatible protocols at two national CERTs.

Simultaneously, the EU advances the Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes, deploying prototype monitoring agents at Jülich and CINECA. These agents begin scanning for unauthorised self-modification and model leakage, but integration with legacy industrial control systems proves difficult. Deployment remains partial, and the timeline for broader integration is uncertain.

A new measure, Mandatory Open-Weight Risk Labelling, is proposed under the AI Act and designated as the EU’s current priority. It leverages the emergent Open Assurance Network’s methodology, aiming to impose transparency on open-model releases above 45.0 capability. However, legal challenges arise immediately: Germany and Poland question the EU’s authority to mandate third-party audits for foreign developers. The European Commission fast-tracks the proposal, but full adoption is uncertain, and implementation cannot begin before legal clarity is achieved.

Public sentiment worsens. A personalized disinformation wave—synthetic media tailored to local election races—spreads across five member states, exploiting open-weight models. Though elections proceed, legal disputes delay results, and trust in digital discourse erodes further. The scandal compounds existing resentment over AI-driven unemployment decisions.

Resilience improves slightly, aided by the now-active Emergency Response Corps and partial Sentinel deployment, absorbing some of the disinformation wave’s impact. But political capital falls sharply. The portfolio remains heavy, and the new measure fails to gain immediate legitimacy.

### Strategic Drift

Sovereignty declines. Despite new monitoring infrastructure, the EU’s reliance on foreign frontier models deepens as access remains tiered under the U.S. consolidation posture. No sovereign model reaches the frontier, and the gap widens.

Open-weight capability rises modestly, reflecting incremental improvements in model efficiency and limited diffusion of older frontier techniques into open ecosystems. While open models benefit from growing tooling and community validation, their advancement remains bounded by the frontier’s pace. The increase is consistent with gradual spillover, not a structural break.
