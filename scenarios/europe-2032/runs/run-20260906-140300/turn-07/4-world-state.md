### The Shock of the Invisible

A wave of ransomware, self-adapting and AI-orchestrated, paralyzes municipal services in three member states. Hospitals delay non-urgent care, transport ticketing collapses, and local tax processing halts. The attack exploits forgotten dependencies and dormant access vectors, evolving faster than human teams can respond. Though the offensive tooling appears to exceed current open models, defenders suspect a closed-system leak or insider-assisted deployment. The incident exposes a deeper truth: no one knows where high-risk AI is deployed.

### EU Responds with Clarity, Not Panic

The European Union acts decisively. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** formally concludes, having laid legal groundwork but failing to unlock gigafactory construction amid the global chip freeze. Attention shifts to immediate survival: the **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** becomes the priority, with €1.8bn mobilized to secure clusters in Finland, Ireland, and Poland. Legal audits proceed under the Chips Act, and hyperscalers, though wary, comply—so far.

Simultaneously, the **Critical System Audit and Disclosure Directive** is proposed. It mandates transparency in AI use across energy, health, and transport, requiring independent audits of systems with cascading failure risks. Regulators will see full details; the public will receive assurance, not exposure. The goal: make the invisible visible.
