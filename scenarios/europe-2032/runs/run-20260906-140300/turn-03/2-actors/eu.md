## In practice
We are acting on the fact that law without execution is theatre. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive has cleared its final vote this turn, but its passage does not mean capacity exists — only that the legal right to reserve it now does. We are immediately launching the Sovereign Compute Implementation Acceleration programme through the European Chips Act’s implementation office, co-funded by the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility. This is not a new law, but a sprint to materialise what the law demands: physical racks, power, cooling, and enforceable access controls. We are tasking the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) with identifying three anchor sites — in Finland, France, and Romania — where EU-controlled data centres can host the reserved compute, with priority access for ENISA, the European Medicines Agency, and national cyberdefence units.

Implementation will be fast-tracked through emergency permitting under the revised TEN-E regulation, with grid upgrades prioritised under the new Critical Infrastructure Power Resilience window. We are offering sovereign cloud providers — including OVHcloud and Deutsche Telekom — a 70% subsidy on qualifying infrastructure and liability shielding for compliance-related delays, in exchange for binding commitments to local data governance and audit access. Resistance from US hyperscalers remains fierce, but we are leveraging the recently passed Digital Markets Act enforcement rulings to condition continued market access on non-interference with sovereign compute enclaves. The Tech Sovereignty Package continues in parallel: we are now using the crisis momentum to secure binding local agreements in eight additional AI Acceleration Zones, linking permit speed to access to InvestAI loan guarantees. This is not just about building faster — it is about ensuring that when the next attack comes, the systems defending Europe answer to no one else.
