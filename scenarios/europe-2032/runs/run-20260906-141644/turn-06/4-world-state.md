### The Core That Wasn’t

The European Union declared the Sovereign AI Core Programme a decisive pivot—state-built, militarily hardened infrastructure to anchor sovereignty. But ambition collided with reality. The redirection of €80bn from expired and pre-deployment funds triggered immediate legal pushback. Germany and Austria filed an emergency injunction at the CJEU, arguing the use of Article 122 TFEU for AI infrastructure exceeds emergency powers. The EIB delayed bond issuance, citing unresolved legal risk.

Three proposed sites—Brittany, northern Sweden, and the Alps—faced local resistance. In Brittany, protests over nuclear waste transport halted construction permits. In Sweden, indigenous land rights claims froze land allocation. Only the Alpine site saw limited groundwork, delayed by months. Italy and Hungary rejected the "no audit, no access" ultimatum, blocking the Apennine node. ENISA’s runtime monitoring tools remained offline—no sovereign stack existed to host them.

The cyber_major_incident—a cascading ransomware attack on public health IT via a poisoned open-source library—exposed fatal dependencies. Automated patching failed when commercial cloud providers invoked force majeure, refusing access during crisis. Response coordination splintered across national CERTs, with no shared operational backbone. The EU’s fragmented resilience collapsed under pressure.

Public sentiment fell further. Displaced professionals staged walkouts in Berlin and Paris, blaming the EAAD for “regulating ghosts while jobs vanish.” A leaked internal audit revealed only 12% of high-risk public AI systems were EAAD-certified. Trust in EU action evaporated.

The US election outcome loomed. Markets braced for sweeping restrictions from the incoming administration, set to take office next turn.

### Sovereignty in Freefall

Despite the launch, no physical core was operational. The failure to deliver infrastructure, combined with legal defeats and member-state defiance, accelerated the erosion of EU AI sovereignty. Political capital, already thin, was drained by the costly new measure and the fallout from inaction.
