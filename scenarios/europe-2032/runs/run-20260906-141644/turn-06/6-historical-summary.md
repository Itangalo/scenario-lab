The EU's Sovereign AI Core Programme, intended to establish state-controlled, resilient AI infrastructure using redirected SCAP funds, collapsed under legal, political, and logistical failures. Germany and Austria challenged the use of Article 122 TFEU for AI infrastructure at the CJEU, prompting the EIB to delay financing. Site development stalled: Brittany halted over nuclear transport protests, Sweden froze land allocation due to indigenous rights claims, and Italy-Hungary blocked the Apennine node, leaving only delayed groundwork in the Alps. ENISA’s monitoring tools remained unused without a functional sovereign stack. A major cyber incident—rampant ransomware via a compromised open-source library—exposed critical dependencies on commercial cloud providers, who withdrew support during the crisis, while fragmented national CERTs failed to coordinate. Public trust eroded further as displaced workers protested, and a leaked audit revealed only 12% of high-risk AI systems were EAAD-certified. With no operational core and sovereignty efforts in disarray, political and institutional credibility crumbled. The incoming US anti-AI administration prepares to take office next turn, expected to impose sweeping restrictions.
