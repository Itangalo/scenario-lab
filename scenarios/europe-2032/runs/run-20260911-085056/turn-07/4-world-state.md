### A quieter half-year, a louder lab debate
The second half of 2029 brought no new cutoff and no new runaway agent. After the spring shocks, emergency rosters stood down in most capitals. Wards still kept paper charts alongside European-hosted models, and back-office teams still double-checked machine output, but services held.

Attention shifted to biology. A contested preprint claimed a genome model had helped sketch a viable human-infecting design, with non-experts able to follow part of the workflow. Virologists argued over methods and over publication itself. Inside ministries, the memo line was blunter: the best openly available weights already do multi-hour research work, and cannot be recalled. Prior open releases continued to diffuse through fine-tunes and mirrors, pulling openweight capability toward the frontier even without a new breakthrough release this turn.

### Detection first, but only started
Brussels answered through health powers, not technology law. The Commission triggered cross-border health rules, asking the disease-control agency to begin federating wastewater and hospital sequencing labs around common thresholds and daily reporting, and the emergency-response authority to draw on existing reserves for limited diagnostics and protective stocks. No new money was committed: a small reprogramming from closed programmes covered only coordination costs, with any larger stockpiling deferred to a future budget decision. This work is started this turn as the single new measure, with no new priority named.

Rollout was partial and slow. Dutch, Danish and Portuguese labs linked quickly as pilots; French and German regions haggled over data ownership and staffing, and eastern municipalities complained about unfunded sampling duties, delaying harmonization. Training, hiring and system integration lags meant daily reporting was not achieved this turn. Interior and health ministers passed a single escalation playbook by implementing act, extending earlier loss-of-control procedures to misuse reporting, but operational use remains uneven.

### Builds not yet closed
Two long-running files were booked as closed. The middle-power outreach network was closed, with permits and investment-bank guarantees on paper but no signed chip or power capacity, and the separate hosting deal of the holdout member state untouched. The continuity switch for hospitals and utilities was declared operational, though fallback care remains largely manual.

The gigafactory programme was not closed this turn. Sites, grid reservations and legal anchoring were noted, but the backup location remained fenced by protesters and without new disbursement, and no capital expenditure was deployed beyond coordination costs. Full delivery and any sovereignty benefit are therefore deferred to a future turn once funding, access and construction are secured. No sovereignty gain is counted this turn.

Voters barely noticed. Coverage treated the completions as paperwork after the fact: capacity still foreign, supply still unsigned, fallback still manual. Relief at no new incident mixed with resentment over dependence, leaving political standing little changed.

Faint signals persisted from the spring: nurses warning of chart errors, municipal auditors questioning who pays when fallback care fails. Rumour, not yet record.
