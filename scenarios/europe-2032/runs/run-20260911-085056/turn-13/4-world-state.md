### Kits out, jobs empty
The autumn push on machine-speed defence moved from demonstration to deployment. Through emergency channels, pre-checked patch kits and behaviour-based monitors reached large hospitals in weeks; energy operators followed. Funded teams fanned out to small clinics and town halls, with rehearsed manual fallback kept on the wards. Where it landed, intrusions were caught faster and recovery shortened.

It did not land everywhere. Smaller councils queued for staff through winter, and two regional hospital groups paused re-digitisation after faulty integrations. The assurance cell created to verify outside models produced checklists and joint test logs, but American and Chinese labs shared briefings, not access.

### Weights loose, graduates waiting
Mid-autumn a new open-weight release matching near-frontier performance spread to hundreds of thousands of downloads in days. Security services warned that every offensive capability it implied was now unrecallable; Brussels ordered no new restriction, leaning on monitoring and fallback instead.

For graduates, little changed. Entry posts in law, accountancy, support and admin did not return. The new Guarantee — paid placements, stipends and hiring bonuses run through employment services on repurposed funds — launched slowly, with first placements in public services and small firms before Christmas. Unions called it a start; student groups called it a map, not jobs.

The Dutch tool curbs stayed under consultation, shipping insurance stayed high, and papers paired joblessness with dependence. Patching prevented cascades, but not discontent.
