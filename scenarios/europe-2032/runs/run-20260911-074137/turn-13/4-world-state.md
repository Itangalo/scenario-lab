### Winter on the fence line
The cold arrived early and the blockades with it. In four countries, coordinated groups chained gates at fenced compute halls and sat on access roads to grid substations, livestreaming diesel heaters against floodlights. Two sites went to emergency shutdown for days; load-shedding rolled through neighbouring towns. Police moved in and out, wary of turning a power cut into a confrontation.

Brussels answered with what it could run without a new law. Interior ministries pooled gendarmerie units, grid operators published hospital-first shedding orders, and cyber teams fed coalition telemetry to site guards to separate protesters from intruders probing during the noise. Stipends and long-delayed welfare arrears were pushed forward to the hardest-hit municipalities, with town-hall meetings on siting and jobs. Lights stayed on in wards, but brownouts and closed server halls made the Union look besieged in its own winter.

### A separate deal
Midway through the unrest, one capital announced its own supply arrangement — volume compute and tooling outside the common export discipline, defended as keeping hospitals and industry alive. Partners called it queue-jumping that gutted months of joint bargaining with Korean and Japanese suppliers. The Secretariat left the chair empty but open, offering to fold the volumes back into the joint pool.

### A battery from the desert
From Arizona and Texas came the season's genuine breakthrough: a room-temperature-stable, high-density solid-state electrolyte with an AI-designed interface, running on pilot lines. Specialists called it the end of a decade-long dendrite argument, and storage and auto firms began retooling plans within weeks. In Europe it landed as dependency news — another American industrial lead to license, with chemicals and permits now chased through the middle-power channel rather than celebrated at home.

By December, continuity held but authority thinned: essential services degraded rather than stopped, while public anger fixed on dark substations, a broken common front, and jobs that had not returned.
