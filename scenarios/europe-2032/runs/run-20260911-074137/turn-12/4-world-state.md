### The sweep
In February the ransomware sweep arrived as hospitals and city halls had feared. A compromised update pushed through managed-service providers locked records in three countries, then appointment systems, then port logistics dashboards. The tooling was plainly machine-written, iterating faster than helpdesks could answer. Emergency wards went to paper, ferries queued, and evening news carried the same blue lock screen in different languages.

Defenders were behind. The joint cyber command pooled telemetry and closed the worst propagation within days, and continuity protocols kept power and water degrading rather than stopping. But recovery took weeks, backlogs took months, and attribution slipped away into competing claims.

### Rationing and the coalition
While systems were still being rebuilt, Washington tightened export licensing again. Under federal control, frontier access came on defence terms and European buyers found volume licences thinned and defensive tooling delayed. Commissioners stopped talking about structured access and started talking about continuity.

The answer was state-to-state. Through the Council Secretariat, envoys aligned export positions on lithography spares, chemicals and power equipment with Korea, Japan and others holding bottlenecks, pooling European telemetry and biosurveillance samples as a contribution. It did not restore American supply, but it kept patching inputs and some compute flowing to municipalities and hospitals, and gave Brussels a joint table to bargain from.

### Biology and work
A contested genome-model study claiming a non-expert path to a human-infecting design circulated in March. Health agencies treated it as a precursor, exercising sampling and stockpile protocols without public alarm. No outbreak followed, but the argument left biosecurity circles for ministerial briefings.

By spring, the labour data buried the last augmentation hope. Studies showing productivity without job loss described a market already gone: entry hiring did not return, graduate unemployment held at records, and the welfare redress queues finally paying arrears only underlined how slow human oversight was. Relief that automated decisions could be reversed sat beside anger at locked wards and vanished first jobs.
