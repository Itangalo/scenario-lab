### The Edge of Operational Failure

The EU pushes hard to activate its EAAN certification nodes in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich by year’s end, invoking emergency powers under Article 222 TFEU to bypass national data-sharing objections. Despite these efforts, integration delays persist: telemetry APIs from major providers remain incomplete, and red teams lack live access to model behaviour. Sovereign inspection is achieved in name only—no live intervention occurs. Without operational verification, the EU Border for AI Systems cannot be enforced, reducing it to a legal fiction.

Meanwhile, the private-sector AI investment collapse triggers cascading cancellations of compute partnerships the EU relied on for scaling sovereign capacity. A member state formalises a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, granting preferential access to frontier models in exchange for relaxed data sovereignty rules—a direct breach of the Union’s common position. The defection undermines enforcement credibility and signals deepening fragmentation.
