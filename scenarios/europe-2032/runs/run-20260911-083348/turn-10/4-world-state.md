### Cut off in the middle of containment
The notice arrived without explanation and without appeal. Overnight, hospitals, ministries and logistics firms that had built daily work on the leading American model found queries refused or throttled. Triage assistants went blank, dispatch copilots stalled, port paperwork helpers timed out. In Brussels the decision was read as rationing by nationality.

It landed in the worst possible week. Health authorities were already tracing a modified pathogen whose design history, investigators said, bore the marks of machine assistance. Clinics sealed wards, samples moved under escort, sequencing labs and wastewater monitors ran day and night. Containment held after weeks, but not before deaths and a long scare. At the same time, a newly demonstrated system built for code and intrusion work changed what attackers could do. Cleared booking and dispatch systems were pried open again with largely machine-written exploits.

What prevented collapse was unglamorous. The municipal backup kits and sentinel upgrades rushed through last year were already in place, and embedded repair teams stayed in town halls and hospitals until paper-to-digital workarounds held. Mayors credited crews who slept in spare offices. Joint procurement was stretched to buy EU-hosted open-model packs for prescribing, triage notes and port clearance — slower, clumsier, but under European control.

Everything else gave. Factory votes were frozen, sites kept warm behind mended fences, and no retaliation followed the extended servicing curbs on chipmaking tools. The Council was asked only to endorse continuity, not money. Trust did not recover; outages were lived, benefits were promised. But for the first time in a year, essential services were seen to keep running without the foreign provider — and that narrow fact became the Union's entire politics.
