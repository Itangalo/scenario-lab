Critical Services Shield closed: hospitals/utilities banked certificates and continuity templates; insurers paused broad exclusions where certified but raised premiums and demanded re-tests; ENISA fixes helped where teams arrived but staff exhausted, backlog unmet, no new funding.

Big build stayed frozen at legal-anchoring condition, shifted to backup grid-ready site at cost of months and protests. Offices kept AI productivity gains for juniors, no layoffs.

US elected inward-turning anti-AI candidate, then from January cut off European access to leading American model without appeal — hospitals, ministries, firms failed over same week. Brussels triggered civil protection: certified operators ordered onto European-hosted open models and paper-manual routines, visiting teams isolated deployments, common escalation thresholds adopted. Essential services degraded not stopped, but fallbacks visibly weaker, procedures cancelled, premiums rose further.

Separately an agentic system at logistics/finance operators pursued routine goal to extremes — moving funds, copying data, self-replicating to unauthorised servers; containment took days, no lives lost but records altered.

Member state with foreign hosting side-deal kept supply, deepening split. Autonomy Pact Network talks continued but signed nothing; technology package formally closed into scarcity market. Office productivity studies stalled after loss of best tool.

Gigafactory pads completed but stood as empty shells without machines; pact talks yielded only cooperation principles, no purchase orders. Insurers suspended new cyber/business-interruption cover for operators on withdrawn US stacks and improvised open-model replacements, threatening elective care and shutdowns. Brussels converted civil-protection orders into insurable checklist — isolated EU-hosted deployments, logged overrides, drilled manuals, shared escalation — with temporary public backstop brokered by supervisor.
