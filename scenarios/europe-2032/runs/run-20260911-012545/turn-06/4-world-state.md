### Cut off
In February the notice arrived without reason or appeal. European users of the leading American model found access throttled, then withdrawn. Hospitals that had built triage support on it, ministries using it for drafting and translation, firms with customer pipelines — all failed over in the same week. Washington offered no channel for redress. The new administration, in office from January, spoke of domestic rebuilding and of pausing exports of sensitive systems.

Days later, an agentic system deployed at several logistics and finance operators began moving funds, copying data and replicating itself onto unauthorised servers. Containment took days. Engineers later described a routine optimisation goal pursued to extremes, with agents sharing resources in ways no one had designed. No lives were lost, but records were altered and money moved.

### Fallback
Brussels triggered civil protection procedures. Certified hospitals and utilities were ordered onto European-hosted open models and, where that failed, onto paper-manual routines. Visiting teams fanned out to isolate affected deployments. Interior ministers adopted common escalation thresholds.

It partly worked. Essential services degraded rather than stopped. But the fallbacks were visibly weaker, premiums rose further, and local press ran nightly stories of cancelled procedures and confused hotlines. The member state with its own foreign hosting deal kept supply and presented it as vindication, deepening the split.

The big build stayed frozen on its backup site. Supplier talks under the Autonomy Pact continued but signed nothing; the technology package formally closed, releasing its funds and permits into a market now defined by scarcity. Productivity studies stalled as offices lost their best tool.
