### Winter on improvised stacks
The second half of 2030 did not bring a new crisis so much as the old ones arriving together.

In autumn a largely automated intrusion swept public services again, this time through trusted maintenance channels. Hospitals and town halls that had drilled isolation routines fell back to paper and clean backups and stayed open after a fashion. Those that had not lost appointment systems, billing and case files for days, and two regions again deferred non-urgent care. Insurers held cover only where the drill certificate existed, at punishing deductibles backed by Brussels. Everywhere else administrators learned that technically running systems and insurable systems were different things.

At almost the same moment, access to the leading foreign model was withdrawn at short notice for European users. Clinics, ministries and firms that had rebuilt workflows on it after the 2028 cut-off failed again, then were moved by emergency order onto older openly available models hosted inside the Union. Services degraded rather than stopped, but the humiliation was public: commentators called it the second blackout, opposition parties called it foresight failed twice.

### Hiring freeze
By October the labour numbers hardened. Entry hiring in law, accountancy, software support, customer operations and junior administration simply did not return. Graduate associations published empty vacancy lists; employers pointed to office software that let junior staff do senior volumes. The same studies that showed strong productivity gains for those in work confirmed there was no second wave coming for those outside it.

Brussels stayed on incident command. Response teams were sent even to the two governments with separate foreign cloud contracts, without conditions. A new wage-insurance and retraining bridge was proposed through existing social funds and a hiring credit, with employment services told to take in the missing cohorts before spring. Councils gave political endorsement, not new money, and payments will take months to reach anyone.

A genuine advance in automated defence — patching and swarm detection that caught whole classes of attack — gave defenders their first good quarter in years, but it arrived too late to change the winter's accounts.
