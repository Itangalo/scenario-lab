## Statement changes  
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now see that without sovereignty in capability, prevention itself becomes contingent on powers that do not share our interest.``
  - **Grounds:** The convergence of *eu_frontier_access_denied* and *bio_incident* this turn reveals that risk prevention is no longer separable from control over the systems that create the risk. We cannot regulate what we cannot access, and we cannot respond to harm if our critical functions depend on externally controlled infrastructure. Our identity must reflect that sovereignty is not a means to an end — it is now a precondition of both mandates.

* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor sovereignty and risk resilience, with a binding target to host and operate a model at or above 60.0 capability by turn 8.``
  - **Trigger:** *eu_frontier_access_denied* — hospitals, ministries, and firms were abruptly cut off from a leading model with no appeal, exposing systemic fragility.  
  - **Grounds:** What was a strategic objective is now a demonstrable vulnerability. The commitment must sharpen: we are not merely building capacity, we are racing to ensure that by turn 8, the EU operates a sovereign system at or above the threshold where AI begins to displace professional work and contribute to its own development. Without that, resilience and safety regimes are unenforceable.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the Sovereign AI Core Programme (SACP)  
Establish a publicly governed, EU-operated AI development and deployment stack — from training infrastructure to agent deployment — capable of reaching 60.0 by turn 8, with full weight control and auditability.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 25–30 points; ensure the EU can independently train, secure, and operate a model at or above 60.0 capability by turn 8  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, selected member-state research labs, and EU-anchored compute providers, with binding access and control clauses

## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
We are accelerating this measure because the *supply_chain_coercion* event proves that compute is not just infrastructure — it is the first line of strategic denial, and we cannot build sovereign models without sovereign hardware; this site must come online early, not just on time.

## In practice  
We are treating the *bio_incident* and *eu_frontier_access_denied* events as a single systemic failure: the Union lacks both the technical autonomy to respond and the political leverage to demand accountability. In response, we are transforming the InvestAI Gigafactories from a capital deployment programme into a mission-critical sovereignty project. The first Swedish site will now receive priority grid allocation under Article 122 TFEU, with the Commission directly managing interconnection timelines. We are activating the Solidarity Fund to compensate Brittany and Norrland for energy cost impacts, offering €1.2bn over 18 months in exchange for lifting local moratoria. The goal is to bring the first exaflop-scale cluster online by turn 7, not turn 8, and to reserve 40% of its capacity exclusively for the Sovereign AI Core Programme.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is now being weaponized in response to *supply_chain_coercion*. We are convening a special Council formation on Economic Security to invoke the Anti-Coercion Instrument against any further unilateral disruption of ASML deliveries, while simultaneously offering a binding EU-wide procurement commitment for next-gen lithography tools developed jointly with Zeiss and IMEC. We are also fast-tracking the permitting harmonization directive through the COREPER process, with the "dormant adoption" clause now tied to national access to the EU AI Compute Pool — if a state blocks a site, it loses priority access to sovereign capacity.

The EAAD, though still interim, is pivoting to crisis mode. In light of the *bio_incident*, we are demanding full training logs and agent-behaviour evaluations from the hyperscaler involved in the release, using the NIS2 framework to compel cooperation from EU-based subsidiaries. We are also expanding our incident monitoring network to include bio-labs and clinical AI systems, with CERN and the Paul Ehrlich Institute leading a new technical task force. Meanwhile, CIRS is being restructured: we are replacing voluntary red-teaming with mandatory cross-border exercises under ENISA, funded through conditional recovery fund access, and fast-tracking detection upgrades in water and energy systems via a new emergency procurement lane.
