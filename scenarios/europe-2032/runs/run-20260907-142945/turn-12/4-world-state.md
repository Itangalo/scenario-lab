### The Open Floodgates

A new open-weight model, developed from leaked frontier weights, begins to circulate in underground networks and reaches a capability level slightly above the previous open-weight ceiling. Estimated at 53.5, it reflects incremental progress rather than a sudden leap, constrained by the limits of available training knowledge and optimization. While distributed rapidly, its deployment remains fragmented and inefficient, falling well short of matching the closed frontier’s 69.5 capability. The EU’s Openweight Containment Initiative, already weakened, is formally suspended as enforcement mechanisms fail to scale—but no new sovereign open-weight alternative emerges to fill the void.

### Crisis in the Core

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation formally ends, but its promise remains unfulfilled. Legal resistance from France, Poland, and Germany limits integration, leaving the EU without a unified, high-capacity AI stack. While the political assertion of control briefly lifted sovereignty perceptions, the failure to operationalize meaningful capacity exposes institutional fragility. With no sovereign model training possible, the Union remains dependent—and vulnerable.

### Defending the Unstable

In response to a devastating ransomware attack on hospitals and the irreversible diffusion of powerful AI tools, the EU launches the Emergency Resilience Surge. This large-scale effort mandates air-gapped backups, autonomous defensive agents, and manual failover systems for essential services. Though framed as a survival measure, implementation faces delays: member states balk at costs, and technical bottlenecks slow deployment. Still, early contracts are awarded, and planning begins in earnest—offering a glimmer of progress amid deepening risk.

### Erosion of Control

AI safety plummets as model-generated attacks outpace defenses and opaque reasoning thwarts analysis. The gap between capability and control widens dangerously. Public sentiment holds at rock bottom, as citizens view both the attacks and the EU’s emergency powers as symptoms of a system in collapse. Political capital erodes further under the weight of ongoing measures and failed expectations, leaving the Union with minimal room to act.
