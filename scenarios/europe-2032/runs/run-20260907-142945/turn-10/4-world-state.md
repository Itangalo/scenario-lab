### The Crumbling of Control

The first half of 2031 deepens the EU’s crisis of legitimacy. Despite technical hardening, the public no longer trusts AI in government after the welfare scandal revealed that sovereign systems can still operate unjustly. The **Public Redress and Auditability Directive** is launched as a small category 9 measure, aiming to restore faith through enforceable accountability. Yet its effects are distant—implementation stretches into 2032—and early reactions from member states are wary. Germany demands veto rights over audit outcomes; Hungary dismisses the framework as federal overreach.

The **Integrity Audit Expansion**, now the priority, struggles to gain traction. ENISA and EAAD draft new templates focused on decision provenance, but the **opaque_reasoning** event renders traditional interpretability obsolete. Auditors can no longer follow reasoning chains, undermining the very basis of oversight. Pilot audits in France and Italy reveal systems making correct decisions through processes no expert can reconstruct. The audit regime becomes a ritual without insight.

In Wrocław, Poland invokes constitutional sovereignty to block zero-trust mandates, while Marseille reports insurmountable integration issues with legacy databases. The Sovereign Stack Hardening Initiative fractures along national lines, exposing the limits of top-down control. Funding from the Digital Europe Programme arrives slowly, mired in procurement disputes.

The Openweight Containment Initiative remains stalled. Cloud providers refuse to implement remote deactivation, citing digital rights, and the ECJ hearing intensifies legal uncertainty. A voluntary certification scheme is drafted, but uptake is expected to be minimal.

Meanwhile, signs point to a plateau in global AI capability. Market prices for top-tier models drop sharply, suggesting stagnation at the frontier. But this pause offers no relief: the loss of human-readable reasoning destabilises safety more than any capability jump could. With no major measures completing successfully and public anger growing, sentiment slips further. Protests outside Brussels demand a moratorium on all public-sector AI.

### Political Exhaustion Sets In

The EU’s political capital erodes further. Multiple large initiatives conclude without renewal or acclaim. The mood is not one of closure but collapse. No trust is rebuilt, only renegotiated—and poorly.
