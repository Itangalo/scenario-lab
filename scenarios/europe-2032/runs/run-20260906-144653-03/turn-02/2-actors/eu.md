## New measure  
**Secure European Access to Advanced Lithography**  
A coordinated legal and financial instrument to ensure the EU retains operational access to critical chipmaking equipment, particularly mature-node lithography tools from ASML, despite U.S. extraterritorial pressure.

## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because without enforceable, immediate control over compute deployed on EU soil, every other sovereignty play—lithography access, talent retention, or industrial scale—depends on permission from powers whose interests are now visibly misaligned with ours, and the Kimi K3 incident proves that uncontrolled compute is already enabling distributed cyber harm.

## In practice  
We are treating compute as strategic infrastructure, not commercial real estate. This turn, the Commission is finalising the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive under Article 114 TFEU, preparing it for trilogue with the Council and Parliament. The text mandates that any operator of a data centre exceeding 100 petaFLOPS of AI-specific throughput must reserve 30% of capacity for EU-defined critical uses—infrastructure resilience, public health, safety research, and authorised defence applications. This is not nationalisation, but a condition of market access at scale, akin to spectrum licensing or grid interconnection rules. The European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) will manage allocation via a transparent, auction-based utility model to prevent politicisation and ensure technical neutrality.

To overcome resistance from Germany, Ireland, and the Competitiveness Council, we are activating the tripartite offer: fast-track access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund for compliant operators; a liability shield for those acting in good faith; and a binding commitment to independent, auditable scheduling of reserved compute. At the same time, we are advancing AI Acceleration Zones with seven member states, locking in six-month permitting caps and guaranteed grid connections—these will become the physical nodes of sovereign capacity. But the supply_chain_coercion event has changed the game: if we cannot protect access to the tools that build AI, even sovereign data centres will run on borrowed time. That is why we are launching a new measure to secure European access to advanced lithography—by co-funding a parallel maintenance and servicing network for ASML tools under EU legal control, using the European Defence Fund and semiconductor champions like STMicroelectronics and Infineon as anchors. This is not retaliation—it is survival.
