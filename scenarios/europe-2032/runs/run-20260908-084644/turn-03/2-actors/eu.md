## Priority  
**EU Cyber Shield Fund** — because the Emergency Cyber Resilience Directive has now landed but lacks enforcement teeth without investment capacity, and with Germany and Poland still hesitant, we must convert obligation into incentive before the next breach exposes systemic fragility.

## In practice  
The Emergency Cyber Resilience Directive is now law: adopted under comitology with Article 122 TFEU invoked for urgency, it binds transmission system operators, major water utilities, and key transport hubs to deploy AI-driven intrusion detection systems and conduct quarterly red-team exercises. Compliance reporting begins next quarter through the Digital Governance Platform, with ENISA overseeing validation. The Openweight Containment Assessment also concludes this turn — its findings confirm that openweight models at 43.0 are already being repurposed for offensive cyber and early-stage bio-design, but legal fragmentation across member states makes a unified restriction threshold politically unviable for now. We will publish the report transparently, framing it not as a call for ban but for containment: better monitoring, watermarking, and compute-tracing as fallbacks.

With the Directive now in force but underfunded, and resilience stagnant at 38.0, we pivot to implementation leverage. The new EU Cyber Shield Fund will be structured through the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused margins and topped up with cohesion funds redirected via qualified majority voting in the Council (Economic and Financial Affairs formation). Disbursement is tied directly to NIS2 compliance milestones — not just procurement, but verified integration and staff training. We are offering Germany fast-tracked approval for its national AI defence cloud in exchange for full participation, and Poland will receive priority access for its eastern border infrastructure as part of broader eastern flank resilience. The fund also creates a procurement corridor for EU-based cybersecurity firms, reinforcing industrial capacity alongside preparedness. This is how we close the gap between rule and reality: not only by legislating defences, but by paying to build them.
