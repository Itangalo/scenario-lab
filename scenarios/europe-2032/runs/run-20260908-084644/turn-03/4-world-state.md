### The Frontier Creeps Forward

AI capability rose to 55.5 as leading labs improved agent coordination and simulation fidelity, continuing the slow climb despite murmurs of stagnation. While no breakthroughs shattered expectations, the compound effect of iterative gains kept the frontier moving. Developers dismissed concerns of a plateau, but investors grew cautious as returns on scale began to thin.

### Resilience Gains Ground

The Emergency Cyber Resilience Directive formally entered into force, mandating AI-driven detection systems and red-teaming across critical energy, water, and transport operators. Though Germany and Poland delayed full implementation, the legal framework is now binding. This, combined with the launch of the EU Cyber Shield Fund, triggered a resilience boost. The €15bn fund — financed through redirected cohesion and Recovery Facility funds — will co-finance NIS2 compliance, tying disbursement to verified deployment of defensive AI and staff training. Early commitments from France, the Netherlands, and Estonia signal momentum.

### Openweight Risks Confirmed, Action Deferred

The Openweight Containment Assessment concluded, revealing that models at 43.0 are already being repurposed for offensive cyber and rudimentary bio-design. However, legal and political fragmentation blocked a unified restriction threshold. Instead, the EU will pursue watermarking, monitoring, and compute-tracing as fallbacks, stopping short of bans.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Tightens

With no sovereignty-building measure completing and ai_capability rising by 1.0, eu_ai_sovereignty fell to 20.0. Political capital declined sharply: four measures in flight cost −11 (three large, one small), the new priority added −1, and the proposal bonus for the Cyber Shield Fund (+4, due to its direct response to prior cyber fragility and the bio-incident) only partially offset the drain. Public sentiment dipped further as campaign backlash against AI intensified, with moratoriums on data centres and worker protections gaining mainstream traction.
