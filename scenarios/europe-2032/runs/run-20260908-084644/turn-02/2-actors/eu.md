## In practice  
We are finalising the draft of the Emergency Cyber Resilience Directive in the Commission’s Directorate-General for Communications Networks, Content and Technology, with input from ENISA and the European Cyber Resilience Board. The text mandates deployment of AI-powered intrusion detection systems across transmission system operators, water utilities serving over 50,000 people, and major transport hubs. Member states will be required to conduct quarterly red-team exercises and report compliance through a new module in the EU’s Digital Governance Platform. Germany and Poland remain cautious, but we have secured their provisional support by linking compliance to priority access to the upcoming EU Cyber Shield Fund and fast-tracking interconnector projects under the Trans-European Networks for Energy. Legal basis remains NIS2, but we are invoking Article 122 TFEU to justify urgency and bypass full comitology.

Meanwhile, site selection for the InvestAI Gigafactories advances: we have narrowed to three final candidates — one in northern France, one in southern Spain, and one in Hungary — each offering pre-negotiated grid reinforcement plans. Final decisions will be tied to binding commitments from national governments on energy allocation and workforce development, coordinated through the European Chips Act secretariat. The Tech Sovereignty Package continues to attract conditional interest from hyperscalers, but we are now linking energy access to legally enforceable anchoring clauses: no priority permitting without irrevocable commitments to EU-controlled compute infrastructure. On the safety front, the new Openweight Containment Assessment responds directly to the bio_incident and the growing risk of distributed capability: we must know whether the EU can still draw a line at 45.0 before someone releases a model that crosses it. This is not about banning open research — it is about preventing unrecallable risk from becoming our new baseline.
