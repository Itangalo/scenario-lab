### The Weight of Progress

AI capability climbed steadily to 54.5 as leading labs refined agent coordination and scaled simulation throughput. The frontier advanced not through leaps but through compounding iteration — a trajectory unbroken by the growing murmurs of a plateau. Yet confidence wavered: a flagship model release underperformed expectations, and spot prices for top-tier compute fell sharply, feeding speculation that the pace of breakthroughs may be slowing. The signal was ambiguous, disputed by developers but impossible to ignore.

### A Crisis Changes the Game

A biological incident involving an AI-designed agent shook global confidence. Though contained after weeks of emergency response, the breach revealed how model capabilities could be weaponized in ways existing safeguards did not anticipate. The event triggered immediate regulatory soul-searching across Europe. The EU launched the Openweight Containment Assessment to study whether restricting open-weight models above 45.0 capability was feasible — a move aimed at preventing unrecallable risks from spreading. However, the initiative faced scepticism from open-source communities and legal hurdles over free research protections.

### Cyber Resilience Stalls

The Emergency Cyber Resilience Directive, named priority, advanced slowly. Germany and Poland withheld full commitment despite incentives, citing sovereignty concerns over mandatory red-teaming. Internal delays pushed back the draft’s finalization, and resilience efforts lost momentum. With no major measure completed, and capability rising, resilience eroded by 2 points.

### Sovereignty and Capital Fade

The EU’s compute initiatives remained in flight, but no breakthroughs landed. With ai_capability rising by 1.0 and no category 4 measure completing, eu_ai_sovereignty fell by 1. Political capital was hit harder: three ongoing large measures cost −9, and the priority added −1.
