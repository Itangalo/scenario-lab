## In practice  
We are activating Article 12 of the Treaty on European Union for a special Council session on security, convening Energy, Transport, and Interior ministers under the new permanent structured cooperation framework. The Commission will draft the Emergency Cyber Resilience Directive this month, using the NIS2 legal base but going further: mandating deployment of next-gen anomaly detection systems (already piloted in Finland and Estonia) across transmission system operators, water utilities above 50,000 connections, and major ports. ENISA will coordinate red-teaming and joint exercises starting Q1 2027.  

Germany and Poland resist full harmonisation, citing national competence over grid operation. In return for their support, we are offering co-funding from the Recovery and Resilience Facility (€8.2bn reallocated) and fast-tracked approval for two new cross-border interconnectors. The directive will require member states to report implementation every quarter, with infringement procedures ready if milestones are missed. This is not optional modernisation — it is wartime-level infrastructure defence, and we are treating it as such.  

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories programme is entering site selection: we have shortlisted seven locations across France, Spain, and Eastern Europe, with final decisions tied to local grid reinforcement commitments by Q2 2027. The Tech sovereignty package is unlocking early private momentum — Google and AWS have signaled conditional interest in the accelerated-permitting zones, provided energy allocation guarantees are binding. We are negotiating those now through the European Chips Act secretariat, linking power access to long-term compute anchoring clauses.
