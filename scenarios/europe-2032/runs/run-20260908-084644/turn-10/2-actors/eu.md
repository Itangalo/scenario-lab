Instead, we are conducting a rapid legal and strategic assessment of the emergent regional compute pact. Our goal is to determine whether it can be retrofitted into an EU instrument: governed through existing Commission mechanisms, funded through repurposed cohesion and Digital Europe envelopes, and recognized as a de facto extension of Union critical infrastructure under the CER Directive. We are engaging bilaterally with the four member states involved — France, Italy, Austria, Finland — to secure their agreement to formalize interoperability, data-sharing, and access protocols under Commission supervision. At the same time, the Commission’s Legal Service is examining whether Article 114 TFEU or the Union’s crisis powers under Article 122 can justify binding obligations on infrastructure access and usage rights, even for nationally funded assets. If this path fails, we will have no sovereign capacity and no credible plan — but we will not compound failure by rushing into another doomed initiative. We are also preparing contingency messaging to frame any EU-endorsed core-group effort not as fragmentation, but as triage — a stabilization measure to prevent total dependency while longer-term unity is rebuilt. But we will not act unless we can do so with at least tacit support from a qualified majority, and without triggering further erosion of trust.
