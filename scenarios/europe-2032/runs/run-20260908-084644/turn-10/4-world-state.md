### Stalled Foundations

Global AI capability advanced to 62.0, crossing the symbolic threshold of autonomous multi-day professional execution. Frontier labs reported self-directed agent workflows in engineering design and regulatory compliance, with minimal human oversight. Yet no safety frameworks were updated to match, and internal audits at two leading developers revealed unlogged reasoning pathways—opaque even to their own engineers. The gap between what AI can do and what anyone can verify widened visibly.

### Paralysis in Brussels

The European Union remained in triage mode, taking no new action. With political capital stagnant at 13.0 and public sentiment mired in hostility, the Commission focused on assessing whether the regional compute pact—formed by France, Italy, Austria, and Finland—could be retrofitted into a Union-wide strategy. Legal advisors found no clear path to enforce access under Article 114 TFEU, and early bilateral talks revealed resistance: France and Italy insisted the pact remain intergovernmental, while Austria and Finland rejected any supranational override.

### Deepening Fragmentation

Without new measures, the EU’s resilience eroded further. A second wave of AI-powered cyber intrusions targeted regional infrastructure, overwhelming patching systems in two member states. Response delays highlighted coordination failures. Meanwhile, protests against AI infrastructure intensified in peripheral regions, with sabotage reported at a data facility in eastern Poland. The perception of a Union unable to act—technologically, legally, or socially—solidified in media and policy circles.
