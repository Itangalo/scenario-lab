## New measure  
### Member-State Compute Compact  
Launch binding agreements with five strategically positioned member states to co-invest in sovereign AI compute clusters — jointly owned, EU-governed, and legally insulated from external access — with first deployments within 18 months.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 6  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +15 (by establishing first non-contingent EU-controlled frontier-scale capacity), resilience +3 (via hardened public-sector AI infrastructure), public_sentiment +2 (demonstrating tangible progress on autonomy)  
`Applies to:` selected member states (initially France, Italy, Spain, Belgium, and Lithuania), EuroHPC JU, EIB, and the Commission’s Digital Europe Programme

## Priority  
**Member-State Compute Compact** — because the two-year commitment demands we close the gap on strategic dependency, and with frontier access now denied to the Union at short notice, we must act as a bloc to lock down physical, legal, and financial control over foundational AI capacity — not just fund it, but own it.

## In practice  
The denial of frontier model access — `eu_frontier_access_denied` — lands like a power cut in ministries from Brussels to Athens. Hospitals relying on foreign-hosted diagnostic agents face delays. Research consortia lose weeks. The Commission’s AI Office issues a terse statement: “Dependency is vulnerability.” This is not a surprise, but it is a reckoning — one we must answer not with protest, but with infrastructure. We are convening an emergency meeting of the Competitiveness Council under Article 114 TFEU, invoking strategic autonomy as a legal basis for joint procurement and shared ownership of AI compute. The Member-State Compute Compact will be structured as a EuroHPC+ initiative, co-financed 50% by the EU (drawn from unspent Digital Europe and Connecting Europe Facility funds) and 50% by participating states, with the European Investment Bank providing guarantees to accelerate construction. Clusters will be sited in sovereign data zones — newly designated under national law — with physical access restricted to EU-vetted personnel and no remote administration rights granted to foreign entities. We are offering Italy fast-tracked access to decommissioned military bases for low-cost siting, and Lithuania will receive additional cohesion funds tied to grid modernisation. France, already ahead in deployment, will host the first control node. The model is clear: not subsidy, but co-ownership. Not access by grace, but capacity by right. This is how we turn the two-year commitment from aspiration into brick, wire, and law.
