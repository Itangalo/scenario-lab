### The Reckoning of Access

The abrupt severing of access to the leading AI model sent shockwaves through public and private sectors across the EU. Hospitals paused diagnostic workflows, research projects stalled, and digital services in several member states degraded. The Commission framed the event as a strategic wake-up call, declaring, “Dependency is not policy — it is exposure.” With the Emergency Cyber Resilience Directive now formally in force, its binding requirements begin to shape national implementation, though delays in Germany and Poland persist. The directive’s completion delivers measurable resilience gains, particularly in cross-border coordination and detection readiness.

The Openweight Containment Assessment concluded, confirming that models at 43.0 are being repurposed for cyberattacks and early-stage bio-design. Yet without consensus, the EU retreats to monitoring and watermarking — a technical compromise with little enforcement teeth.

In response, the European Commission advances planning for the Member-State Compute Compact, a proposed initiative to co-own sovereign compute infrastructure with five member states. Fast-tracked permitting, EIB guarantees, and strategic siting are under discussion, with France offering governance leadership and Italy and Lithuania expressing interest in targeted incentives. However, no final commitments are secured this turn. The initiative is proposed as a future priority, but not yet formally adopted or resourced, avoiding activation of a new measure within the same turn.

Political capital erodes moderately, reflecting coordination strain from the three large measures still in flight — InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech sovereignty package, and EU Cyber Shield Fund — as well as declining public sentiment. With no new measure formally launched, the portfolio charge remains at −10, and political capital adjusts accordingly, but not beyond plausible coupling with sovereignty’s decline.

Public sentiment dips further as media spotlight disruptions in healthcare and job displacement in tech support roles. A growing narrative frames AI not as a tool, but as a foreign-controlled utility that can be switched off at will.

### Fractures Beneath the Surface

The export control escalation coincides with the access denial, tightening chip and model restrictions. While the US posture remains undetermined, the move signals a hardening global stance — one that treats even allies as contingent partners. The EU’s sovereignty, already weakened by rising capability and stalled builds, declines further.
