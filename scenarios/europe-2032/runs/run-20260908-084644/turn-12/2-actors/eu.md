## In practice  
We are not launching any new measure this turn. The Union remains in a state of functional paralysis, with political capital too low to initiate or enforce any cross-cutting action. Our immediate work is confined to technical and legal assessment: the Commission’s Joint Research Centre and Legal Service are finalizing a joint analysis of the France-Italy-Austria-Finland compute pact, focusing on whether its infrastructure, access protocols, and governance can be aligned with Union law under Article 114 TFEU or the emergency provisions of Article 122. This assessment will determine whether integration is legally feasible without treaty change — and if not, whether a fallback model under Article 88 TFEU (economic and monetary coordination) could provide a temporary, non-disruptive framework for mutual access and oversight.

At the same time, we are mapping the fiscal envelope — primarily repurposed Digital Europe and Connecting Europe Facility funds — that could be used to incentivize integration without requiring new appropriations. Bilateral consultations continue with the four member states, emphasizing that the EU does not seek to seize their assets, but to establish interoperability standards and mutual access guarantees under Commission oversight, akin to the EuroHPC model. We are also preparing a contingency framing for public communication: if integration fails and a 'coalition of the willing' emerges, we will position it not as a breakaway, but as a temporary stabilization mechanism preserving the possibility of reintegration once political capital recovers. But we will not act unless we can do so with at least tacit support from a qualified majority, and without triggering further erosion of trust. The US-China agreement on limited AI risk governance passes us by — we are not consulted, not included, and not even informed in a timely way. Our absence is not an oversight. It is a fact.
