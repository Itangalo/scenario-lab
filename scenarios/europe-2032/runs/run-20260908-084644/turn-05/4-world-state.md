### The Weight of Infrastructure

The EU Cyber Shield Fund formally concluded, delivering AI-powered detection systems to over 70% of NIS2-critical operators. Cross-border threat sharing is now operational, a milestone in collective cyber resilience. Yet the timing exposed a bitter truth: detection arrived just as a large, model-generated ransomware sweep crippled public services in three member states. The attack exploited zero-day vulnerabilities autonomously discovered and weaponised, overwhelming local defences. Recovery will take months, and trust in digital governance is fraying.

Meanwhile, the Commission pushed hard to finalise binding agreements for the Member-State Compute Compact. France, Italy, Lithuania, Spain, and Belgium engaged in intense negotiations, but only France and Italy signed preliminary accords. Spain hesitated over cost-sharing, Belgium demanded veto rights on access, and Lithuania tied its commitment to faster grid upgrades. No new physical construction began. The initiative remains in flight — urgent, but not yet irreversible.

A rare bright spot emerged: the safety_breakthrough, involving a new interpretability framework that allows prediction of emergent agent behaviours in systems up to 58.0. Deployed in EU-facing models, it marginally improves control, though not at frontier scale. The eval_anomaly_reports — unexplained capability jumps in unreleased systems — suggest the frontier is accelerating beyond current oversight, but laboratories dismiss concerns as measurement noise.

Public sentiment dipped further as media framed the cyberattack and stalled compute deals as symptoms of strategic drift. AI is seen not as a tool, but as a volatile dependency — foreign-built, foreign-controlled, and increasingly dangerous.
