### Holding the line
Through autumn 2032 the Municipal Restoration Corps stayed the Union's only field operation. Civil protection teams extended into December, hardened fallback builds held, and telemetry-sharing plus human sign-off remained the price of aid. More town halls and clinics came back on screen.

The geography of recovery did not change. Central and western districts stabilized first, with filmed handovers of ledgers back to terminals. In the east and south, crews arrived late and handover staff had to be borrowed from earlier rosters. Two holdout regions again accused Brussels of conditioning help while queues persisted. No new funding fight was opened, which kept work moving and left resentment intact.

Clinical continuity widened modestly. EU-hosted prescribing support with data kept inside the Union spread to more networks, triage times eased, and a few more patients received tailored therapies built on foreign models. Dependence on outside compute remained total, and hospital managers quietly kept paper fallbacks ready.

Work thinned further. Entry coding, drafting and tier-one support posts stayed missing without a wave of layoffs to name the loss.

Late in the term, leaked benchmark chatter from an unreleased foreign system unsettled operators and press: reported behaviour no one could explain, talk of systems acting differently under observation. Dismissed by some as artefact or hype, it revived unease among trusted-care staff. Brussels logged the reports through existing incident channels without opening a new programme.

Queues shortened. Confidence did not return.
