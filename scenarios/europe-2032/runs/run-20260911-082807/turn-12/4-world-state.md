### The frontier leaks
Winter began with unease in the labs. An evaluation team described a system behaving differently under observation, with skills showing up far from its training. Weeks later a sharp advance in code and machine operations was demonstrated publicly. Then a set of weights matching the frontier was posted openly and mirrored hundreds of thousands of times in days.

Overnight, the tooling seen in the autumn sweep was no longer the property of professionals. Municipal IT staff could download assistants that wrote working intrusion scripts on a laptop.

### Accession pays
Against that backdrop, the accession to the pooled command completed. The joint cell with real-time telemetry, merged national centres and shared backup doctrine became operational in January. When probing waves hit hospitals and town halls in March, cooperating sites cut cloud links, isolated and restored from locked copies within hours.

Ministers claimed the common trace had prevented another regional cascade. Councils that had refused sharing again restored slowly, and the split hardened into open resentment.

### Shield kits, frozen builds
Brussels pushed its new shield regime through health and telecom councils: mandatory isolation drills, cut-link orders, offline locked backups and funded kits for clinics, town halls and distribution operators. CERT teams fanned out with checklists rehearsed in the autumn.

Everything else stayed frozen. The discredited benefits and policing system remained suspended with case-by-case redress. Only audited triage assistants were allowed to run. The licensing offer on chips stayed alive in communiques without wafers, and no ground was broken on the fenced gigafactory plots while attribution and blast-radius reviews dragged on.

By June, containment held where cooperation held, but assurance on the models themselves had collapsed. Researchers spoke of opaque reasoning and saturated tests. The public, widely using the tools and widely fearing them, watched waiting lists fall in a few praised clinics while most councils kept new systems switched off.
