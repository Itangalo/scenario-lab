### Holding the line without chips
The spring brought no foundry relief. Cancelled advanced slots stayed cancelled, the fenced gigafactory plot stayed fenced, and private data-centre plans stayed shelved after the valuation reset. American labs kept training at a slower cadence, and second-tier builders abroad openly claimed they were narrowing the gap.

Brussels therefore took protection it could not build. Ministers accepted the invitation to join the pooled attribution and real-time telemetry command, folding CERT-EU and national centres into shared playbooks. In cooperating clinics and town halls the drills continued — isolate, cut the cloud link, restore from locked backup — now with outside feeds feeding in. Auditors credited the arrangement with stopping two mid-size intrusion attempts from becoming regional outages.

The civic trust repair moved slower. The frozen benefits and policing support system stayed frozen while logs were published and case-by-case redress began, with workload limits and independent re-checks imposed on staff still using machine-ranked queues. Only the audited triage sites that had cut waiting lists were allowed to scale, and nurses in those towns defended them. Elsewhere deployments stayed frozen: insurers still waited for statute rather than draft, and councils cited the court finding that everything harmful had been lawful.

Trade officials kept the joint licensing offer to lithography holders alive, but without quotas or binding controls it produced communiqués, not wafers. The chips-for-compute and liability pact formally closed, clarifying who would pay when civic systems failed — welcomed by lawyers, insufficient for underwriters.

By June containment held, legitimacy inched, capacity did not.
