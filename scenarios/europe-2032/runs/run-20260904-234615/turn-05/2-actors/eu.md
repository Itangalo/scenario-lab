## New measure
**Measure:** Launch Sovereign Model Development Programme  
Launch a dedicated EU-led effort to train and deploy a frontier-capable AI model on sovereign infrastructure, using secured compute from InvestAI Gigafactories and public research talent, with strict safety and transparency protocols.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase `eu_ai_sovereignty` by 15–20 points and `ai_safety` by 5–8 by anchoring a high-capability system under EU control, reducing dependence on foreign models and enabling trusted evaluation and deployment

## Priority
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
This remains the linchpin of our sovereignty: without operational compute, the new Sovereign Model Development Programme cannot start, and every delay deepens our exposure to external access cuts like the one we just suffered — which proved that reliance on foreign goodwill is not a strategy, but a vulnerability.

## In practice
We are treating the denial of frontier model access — the sudden cutoff affecting hospitals, ministries, and firms — not as an anomaly, but as the new baseline condition of operating in a contested technological order. This is not a failure of diplomacy; it is the consequence of strategic dependence. That is why we are doubling down on InvestAI Gigafactories. The Commission has now formally triggered Article 9 of the Crisis Regulation, overriding the Brittany court’s suspension on national constitutional grounds, and we are moving to condition Cohesion Fund disbursements on immediate grid access. Simultaneously, we are finalising the Finland site agreement with Norrland Energy and LKAB, securing geothermal-powered locations that offer both energy resilience and political tractability. The pivot will cost €18bn in rework and delay, but we are repurposing €12bn from the Tech Sovereignty Package’s private-investment contingency fund to absorb it — a reallocation we are justifying under Article 178 TFEU on industrial competitiveness in times of strategic shock.

The Sovereign Model Development Programme will launch as soon as the first gigafactory comes online, which we now expect in early turn 6. We are tasking a newly convened EU AI Taskforce — drawn from DG CONNECT, the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, and red-team leads from the EAAD — to define the model’s architecture, safety envelope, and governance framework by turn 6. It will be trained on EU-secured compute, using curated public data and synthetic datasets vetted for integrity, with full telemetry and interpretability requirements baked in from the start. This is not about matching the frontier at 58.0; it is about owning the stack at 55.0, so that when the next cutoff comes, the EU still has a brain. We are already in talks with Germany, France, and the Netherlands to co-fund the first training run, and we are offering access to the model to all member states under strict usage controls — not as a product, but as a public good. The EAAD will evaluate it, HERA and ENISA will stress-test it, and the European Parliament will oversee its mandate. This is how we close the gap between capability and control. We are not waiting. We are building.
