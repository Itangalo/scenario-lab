### US Election Shifts the Global Landscape  
The 2028 US election is decided by a powerful anti-AI backlash, with the incoming administration pledging sweeping domestic restrictions, data centre moratoriums, and sector-funded job guarantees. While the full posture takes effect next turn, the signal is clear: American frontier progress slows not from lack of resources, but from political retreat. The global AI race recalibrates overnight, with non-US developers gaining narrative momentum.

### EU Cutoff Triggers Sovereignty Crisis  
Without warning, the EU is cut off from the leading frontier model. Hospitals lose diagnostic support, ministries face operational delays, and firms built on foreign AI infrastructure scramble. The incident is not framed as retaliation, but as arbitrary access control — a stark reminder of dependence. Public and political anger mounts, directed both at foreign providers and at the EU’s own failure to deliver sovereign capacity.

### Gigafactories Break Deadlock — At a Cost  
Under Crisis Regulation Article 9, the Commission overrides the Brittany court ruling, forcing grid access and restarting construction. A parallel site in Finland, powered by geothermal energy and backed by Nordic industrial partners, is proposed and fast-tracked for approval. However, the pivot incurs €18bn in rework costs and delays. The Tech Sovereignty Package absorbs part of the blow through fund reallocation, but investor confidence wavers.

### New Model Programme Announced Amid Skepticism  
The EU announces the Sovereign Model Development Programme, aiming to train a high-capability model on domestic infrastructure. The initiative is formally proposed and enters planning, but full funding and intergovernmental agreement are pending. While framed as a public good under strict oversight, member states express caution over commitments. The EAAD and Joint Threat Response Integration complete their mandates, delivering modest gains in safety and resilience.

### Political Capital Holds — Just  
Despite the access denial and setbacks, the completion of two major measures offsets some strain. Public sentiment dips slightly as dependency humiliation fuels resentment, but the decisive response prevents a deeper collapse.
