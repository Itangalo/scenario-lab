## New measure  
**Measure:** Expand EAAD Authority to Cover In-Development Systems and Open-Weight Threats  
Grant the European AI Assurance Directorate legal authority to conduct evaluations not only before release but during training and development — including remote audits of systems approaching 50.0 capability — and extend its mandate to assess open-weight models circulating within the EU, even if not officially released.

Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)

## In practice  
We are finalising the legal basis for the EAAD’s expanded mandate under Article 114 TFEU, invoking internal market harmonisation to override member-state objections to extraterritorial enforcement. The Commission is drafting a regulation that compels any entity training a model above 48.0 within the EU’s jurisdiction — or distributing one with equivalent capability — to submit to remote audits, telemetry checks, and staged evaluations during training. For open-weight models, the EAAD will designate “capability thresholds of concern” and require hosting providers, academic repositories, and cloud marketplaces to report or restrict distribution — backed by fines and disconnection orders. This is not a ban; it is a verification regime modelled on nuclear safeguards. We are negotiating with Germany, France, and the Netherlands to host the first three audit nodes, and with CERN and EuroHPC JU to repurpose high-performance computing monitoring tools for agent-behaviour tracking.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories are still blocked in Brittany, where local courts have suspended construction pending a ruling on the co-ownership model’s compatibility with national property law. We are responding by invoking the newly ratified Crisis Regulation’s Article 9, which allows override of national legal impediments for infrastructure deemed critical to strategic autonomy. The Commission has issued a formal notice to the French government, backed by the threat of withholding Cohesion Fund tranches unless grid access is granted unconditionally by mid-March. We are also accelerating negotiations with Norway and Finland to shift one gigafactory site to Norrland if Brittany remains intractable — a costly pivot, but one that preserves the timeline. Power procurement is now tied to green hydrogen commitments, turning energy security into a regional development offer that may yet win local support.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is being leveraged in parallel: we are conditioning access to the EU’s accelerated permitting zones on foreign hyperscalers’ agreement to host sovereign AI workloads on EU-controlled infrastructure, with data and control legally ring-fenced. The Blue Card+ fast-track has placed 780 engineers so far, with a focus on red-teaming and verification — a critical input into the EAAD’s new mandate. And the Joint Threat Response Integration is entering its operational phase: ENISA and HERA now run biweekly cross-border drills with national CERTs and health agencies, simulating coordinated cyber-physical incidents involving autonomous agents. These are not theoretical: the leaked Chinese model has already been adapted for phishing and medical coding fraud, and we are treating 47.0 in the wild as our new baseline threat. We are not waiting for perfection. We are acting.
