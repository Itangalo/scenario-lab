## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
This remains the cornerstone of our two-year commitment: sovereign compute is the only foundation on which containment, evaluation, and resilience can stand — and with openweight diffusion accelerating via uncontrolled leaks, we cannot afford to wait for external coordination or rely on goodwill from hyperscalers who bypass our unity.

## In practice  
We are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by finalising power allocation agreements with ENTSO-E and national grid operators, now backed by the reclassification of AI zones as critical infrastructure under Article 9 of the Crisis Regulation. This unlocks priority grid access and overrides local commercial claims. In Brittany and Norrland, we are deploying the Cohesion Fund to co-finance green hydrogen infrastructure tied directly to the gigafactories, turning energy dependency into regional economic leverage. The Commission is enforcing compliance with co-ownership models through the Recovery and Resilience Facility disbursement mechanism — withholding funds from member states that undermine the collective framework. These steps are politically costly, but they are necessary to ensure that by turn 7, the EU has not just promised sovereignty — it has concrete, powered, legally anchored compute that no foreign actor can disable.

On the Open-Weight Containment Initiative, we are activating emergency protocols in response to the emergent clandestine model leak. The EAAD, in coordination with Europol and national cybersecurity agencies, is conducting rapid capability assessments of the leaked model, treating it as a live threat test. Hosting providers and academic networks within the EU are being served with provisional takedown notices under Article 20 of the Digital Services Act, and we are pushing for a Council decision under the Cyber Resilience Act to grant the EAAD temporary enforcement powers ahead of the turn 4 deadline. Legal services are preparing a binding opinion on extraterritorial reach, but we are acting now under the principle that uncontrolled diffusion cannot be met with perfect legality — only with credible containment.

Meanwhile, the Tech sovereignty package is being weaponised in trade talks: we are conditioning access to accelerated permitting on semiconductor firms’ commitment to EU-based maintenance hubs and restricted export clauses for advanced tooling. The Blue Card+ scheme is now live, fast-tracking residency for 1,200 engineers in red-teaming and agent alignment, with placements in the EAAD, national labs, and sovereign cloud operators. On the EAAD front, we are finalising the first evaluation protocol for models above 50.0, modelled on nuclear safety stress tests, and conducting a red-team exercise simulating an autonomous cyber-intrusion using a controlled proxy model — results will be published before year-end to build public trust. The levy on non-EU hyperscalers continues to be collected provisionally under the DSA, pending CJEU ruling. We are not waiting.
