## New measure  
**Frontier Access and Safety Pre-Registration Framework**  
Establish a mandatory pre-deployment registration and evaluation regime for any AI system above 58.0 capability entering the EU market, requiring full model documentation, weight escrow, and third-party safety testing before operational use.

## In practice  
We are acting on the understanding that sovereignty is not just about compute or models—it is about epistemic control. The eval_anomaly_reports are not yet a crisis, but they are a warning: we are losing the ability to know what the frontier can do before it acts. This turn, we launch the Frontier Access and Safety Pre-Registration Framework under Article 114 TFEU, treating unverified high-capability systems as a market-distorting risk. The AI Office will draft implementing acts by April, with ENISA and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre standing up a joint evaluation cell by June. We are requiring all developers to submit model cards, training data summaries, and weight hashes to a secured escrow—access limited to vetted evaluators. Third-party labs in the EU will run standardised stress tests for goal drift, evasion, and recursive improvement potential. Non-compliant systems will be barred from public procurement and essential infrastructure contracts. We are offering transitional recognition for systems already evaluated under the U.S. AI Safety Institute framework, but only if they agree to real-time telemetry sharing during early deployment.

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive is now law—its passage marks a political win, but its implementation begins now. We are activating the EDIC auction protocol: national operators must register available frontier-class capacity by Q2, with the first 30% reservation to be scheduled by Q3. To incentivise compliance, we are linking access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund—operators who pre-register will receive fast-tracked permitting and a 15% subsidy top-up. Two northern member states have already committed; we are negotiating with southern operators by offering grid-stability bonuses tied to renewable co-location. The sunset clause remains a vulnerability, but we are treating it as a forcing function: if we do not demonstrate value within five years, the directive will not be renewed.

The Resilience Acceleration Pact continues under strain. ENISA has issued telemetry mandates to 87 essential operators, but only 52 are compliant. We are deploying the AI Incident Response Pool to conduct live audits in Poland and Italy, pairing them with grid engineers from the InvestEU resilience window. The €1.8bn substation upgrade is now tied to data centre licensing: no new compute without upgraded local infrastructure. Meanwhile, the Sovereign Model Development Initiative moves from concept to structure. The EAIRC task force is convened under DG CNECT, with France and Germany appointing lead scientists. We are repurposing 15% of InvestAI compute for sovereign training runs, starting with a 54.0 baseline model to test control protocols. This is not about matching the frontier today—it is about ensuring that when the next leap comes, we have a system we can trust because we built it, tested it, and control its every layer.
