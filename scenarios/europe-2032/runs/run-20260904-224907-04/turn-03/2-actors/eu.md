## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because it finishes this turn and its legal adoption is the last chance to lock in binding access to compute before the ASML coercion precedent normalises, and because without enforceable control over the means of production, every sovereign capability we try to build—including this turn’s new model initiative—depends on infrastructure someone else can withdraw.

## In practice  
We are acting on the understanding that compute without control is an illusion, and control without capability is empty. This turn, the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive reaches its final trilogue. We are pressing for adoption under Article 114 TFEU, treating compute access as a market integrity issue, not a national reserve. The Council’s legal service has raised concerns about proportionality, so we are offering a sunset clause—five years, reviewable—and a transparency board with cross-party, cross-agency membership to audit scheduling decisions. In parallel, the EDIC has finalised the auction protocol: reserved capacity will be allocated via competitive bidding weighted for public-interest use, with penalties for non-compliance. We are also fast-tracking access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund for operators who pre-commit to the directive’s registry, turning compliance into a competitive advantage.

The Resilience Acceleration Pact is now operational: €12bn has been mobilised, with 40% allocated to AI-driven intrusion detection in energy and healthcare networks. ENISA is issuing the first binding directives under the new mandate, requiring real-time telemetry from all operators of essential services. The AI Incident Response Pool is being staffed from national cyber units and vetted academic teams, with training beginning in October. But we are seeing pushback in regions where grid strain is already acute—Poland and southern Italy—so we are redirecting €1.8bn from the InvestEU window to accelerate substation upgrades in AI Acceleration Zones, tying grid resilience directly to compute expansion.

The new Sovereign Model Development Initiative begins in stealth: we are convening a task force under DG CNECT to stand up the European AI Research Consortium by turn 4, drawing on EuroHPC’s JUPITER-class machines and repurposing 15% of the InvestAI compute for sovereign training runs. We are not waiting for unanimity. France and Germany have signalled support; we will use the cohesion argument—this is not subsidy, it is survival—to bring others in. The model will not be open-weight at launch, but its safety evaluations will be, using a new public audit framework under development at the AI Office. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that when the next leap comes, the EU has a system it can interrogate, control, and trust—because it built it itself.
