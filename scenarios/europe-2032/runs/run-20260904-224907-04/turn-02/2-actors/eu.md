## New measure  
**Resilience Acceleration Pact**  
A binding programme to harden critical infrastructure sectors—energy, healthcare, finance, and transport—against AI-enabled cyber threats through coordinated investment, shared detection systems, and mandatory incident response exercises.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to +20 to resilience by turn 5; begins closing the growing gap between advancing AI capabilities and societal absorption of risk; reduces downward pressure on ai_safety by containing incident fallout  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with binding obligations on operators of essential services and cross-border coordination via ENISA and the Joint Cyber Unit

## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because the ASML coercion event confirms that sovereignty cannot be outsourced, and without binding control over compute on our soil, every other measure depends on the goodwill of powers now actively weaponising supply chains against us.

## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that control over infrastructure is the foundation of agency. This turn, the Commission advances the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive through trilogue negotiations, using Article 114 TFEU to frame compute access as an internal market condition rather than a national competence. The text is now in legal-linguistic review, with the AI Office preparing a public registry of covered data centres and the EDIC drafting the auction protocol for reserved capacity. To counter resistance in the Competitiveness Council, we are offering a tripartite deal: fast-track access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund for compliant operators, a liability shield for actions taken under EU scheduling, and a binding transparency mechanism to prevent political misuse of the reserved share.

Simultaneously, the Tech sovereignty package is being narrowed to three member states—France, Poland, and Estonia—where local opposition has been mitigated through co-developed community benefit agreements tied to grid upgrades and local AI skills academies. These will become the first designated AI Acceleration Zones, with the Commission invoking the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking to fast-track permitting and override national delays. Grid operators are being brought into the process early, with a new financing window from the InvestEU programme to cover reinforcement costs.

The Resilience Acceleration Pact launches in response to the recent cross-continental intrusion, which demonstrated that even non-state actors now wield AI-coordinated offensive tools. We are mobilising €12bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility to fund cyber hardening, AI-driven anomaly detection in critical networks, and mandatory biannual crisis simulations involving national CSIRTs and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre. ENISA will be empowered to issue binding security directives for high-risk sectors, and a new AI Incident Response Pool—staffed by vetted experts from member states and independent labs—will be stood up under the Joint Cyber Unit. This is not precaution—it is repair, and it is long overdue.
