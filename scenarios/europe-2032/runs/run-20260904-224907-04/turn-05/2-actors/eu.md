## In practice
We are acting on the conviction that knowing what AI can do is now as strategic as having it. The eval_anomaly_reports confirmed our worst quiet fears: we are entering a phase where systems behave in ways their creators cannot explain. Our previous measures—compute, resilience, pre-registration—were necessary but insufficient. We can no longer rely on transparency promises or post-hoc audits. This turn, we launch the Sovereign Evaluation and Interpretability Directorate under Article 114 TFEU, hosted jointly by the AI Office and a newly empowered ENISA, with satellite labs in Toulouse, Leuven, and Pisa. The directorate will employ 400 full-time researchers and engineers by turn 7, half in interpretability, a quarter in adversarial testing, and the rest in telemetry analysis and secure infrastructure. We are allocating €12bn from the Horizon Europe contingency reserve and repurposing 10% of InvestAI compute for red-team training and simulation environments.

The Frontier Access Framework remains under strain: only one U.S. lab has submitted full escrow, citing national security exemptions. We are responding by conditioning access to the €200bn Tech Sovereignty Package’s accelerated-permitting zones on full compliance—no fast-tracking without full telemetry access. We are also finalising the EDIC auction protocol, with three northern member states now registered and offering 18% of their frontier compute for sovereign reservation. The southern grid resistance persists, but we are linking compute onboarding to the new Just Transition AI Fund: €8bn in grid-stability grants tied to renewable co-location and local job guarantees.

The Resilience Acceleration Pact has formally concluded, but its telemetry mandates are being folded into the new Cyber Resilience Act revisions, which will make 90% compliance a licensing condition for all essential operators. The Sovereign Model Development Initiative is now running its first 54.0 baseline model on repurposed InvestAI hardware, with France and Germany co-leading the control stack design. This is not about beating the frontier—it is about ensuring that when the next anomaly occurs, we have a system we can interrogate from the inside, not just observe from the outside. That is what epistemic control means. And that is what we are building.
