## In practice  
We are launching a new resilience backbone: a permanent red-teaming and incident coordination network under joint ENISA and HERA leadership. This is not another study group. It will be staffed with rotating experts from national agencies, trusted researchers vetted under the EU’s new security clearance framework, and seconded engineers from non-US-aligned AI labs participating in the emergent cooperation initiative. The legal base is Article 188 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU, invoked for cross-border threats to critical infrastructure. Funding of €4.8 billion will come from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused allocations and redirected from stalled cohesion projects in two member states where digital readiness lags.

We are already negotiating with Germany, France, and Poland to host the coordination cell, with Belgium offering secure facilities near EU institutions. Resistance is coming from smaller member states worried about centralisation and cost-sharing, so we are offering guaranteed access and regional nodes in exchange for political buy-in. The unit will conduct quarterly live-fire exercises starting in Q2 2027, and its first mandate is to map all known attack vectors from the Kimi K3 release and simulate high-consequence scenarios — including rogue agent persistence and synthetic biology misuse — by the end of turn 4. This does not replace industrial sovereignty, but without it, any compute we build may simply become a larger target.
