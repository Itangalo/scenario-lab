### Frontier Leaps, Open Weights Follow

The second half of 2026 sees a discontinuous leap in narrow AI capabilities: automated red-teaming tools crack zero-day exploits at scale, and code-generation agents autonomously patch entire legacy systems under adversarial pressure. This **capability_jump** pushes `ai_capability` up by +2.0. In response, a coalition of open-weight developers releases a new model within weeks, achieving roughly 78% of the new frontier’s performance — a strong effort, but not a full catch-up. The release is widely shared, downloaded over 300,000 times, and marks a **partial_openweight_response**. However, the jump from 40.0 to 42.0 reflects only modest progress, consistent with rapid adaptation but not miraculous convergence. `openweight_capability` rises to 42.0, maintaining its lag behind the frontier and respecting the accumulation constraint.

Despite months of preparation, no major incident was prevented. A quiet but sophisticated intrusion across European grid operators — part of a broader **cyber_test_shot** affecting three continents — went undetected by existing systems, only uncovered by accident. The breach exposed weaknesses in both monitoring and coordination.

### EU Responds with Speed, Meets Resistance

In response, the EU launches the *Strengthened Red-Teaming and Incident Response Network*, a small category 6 measure aimed at building rapid-response capacity across cyber and bio domains. It draws on the nascent **emergent_lab_cooperation** with non-US labs for early threat data. Legal basing under Article 188 TFEU proceeds, and funding is reallocated from stalled cohesion projects.

However, pushback emerges immediately. Smaller member states resist centralisation, delaying host agreements. Only preliminary staffing begins, yielding no immediate resilience boost beyond planning. The measure is now in flight, but delivers no full effect yet.

### Sentiment Dips, Sovereignty Erodes

Public concern grows as media highlight both job losses in legal and administrative sectors — the ongoing **emergent_labour_displacement_wave** — and the recent cyber intrusion. Trust in AI governance erodes, nudging `public_sentiment` down slightly.

Assurance fails to keep pace with capability. The **capability_jump** triggers a −5 to −12 penalty on `ai_safety`, and with no safety-focused measures landing this turn, it drops to 32.0. Meanwhile, rising AI capability costs `eu_ai_sovereignty` −1, and no sovereign compute comes online, leaving it at 21.0.

Political capital falls under strain: two large measures and one small are now active, costing −8, plus −1 for the named priority, totaling −9. No completion bonus offsets this, and the proposal earns no bonus — though timely, it responds to unfolding rather than past shocks. Capital drops to 40.0.
