## In practice  
We are treating the completion of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Access Directive as a foundational shift: the legal and technical scaffolding for sovereign compute is now in place, with Finland, Estonia, and the Czech Republic formally integrated into the EU’s crisis reserve. ENISA and the European Defence Agency have signed joint protocols under NIS2 and Article 122 TFEU, and the first live simulation — testing continuity of health diagnostics and energy grid optimisation during a multi-day outage — will run next month. This is not full sovereignty, but it is resilience with teeth: we can now act under duress, and that changes the balance of pressure.

At the same time, we are in final negotiations with Poland over the EuroHPC veto. The offer remains on the table: Wrocław becomes a Tier-2 sovereign node with guaranteed compute allocation, talent pipelines from AGH and the University of Wrocław, and fast-tracked access to 6,000 GPUs from the Chips Act strategic reserve. President von der Leyen has linked progress to cohesion fund disbursements and the eastern rail electrification package, leveraging cross-sectoral dependencies to break the deadlock. If Warsaw agrees by mid-June, we can initiate stacking training runs in Q3. If not, we trigger formal contingency planning for dual nodes in Szeged and Cluj, though this delays sovereign model training by at least six months and risks fragmenting the initiative.

The red-teaming network’s expansion into biosecurity has delivered a +3 resilience gain, and we are now integrating early-warning feeds into the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control. Meanwhile, the Labour Transition Fund’s post-mortem shows strong uptake in Belgium and Spain but lagging employer engagement in Germany and Italy — a reminder that retraining without labour-market anchoring risks becoming charity, not transition. We are preparing a successor measure focused on co-investment, but will not launch it until political capital stabilises. Every euro, every negotiation, every political bet must now serve the pivot from contingency to capability — because the frontier does not wait, and neither will the next incident.
