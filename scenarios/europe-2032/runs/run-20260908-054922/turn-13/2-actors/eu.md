## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.  
  - Grounds: The mounting social and cyber disruptions this turn — the ransomware sweep and the emergent labour displacement wave — have intensified the tension between sovereignty and safety. We are still bound to both mandates, but the cost of balancing them is now measured in real harm and eroding legitimacy.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but no longer without operational legitimacy — and we accept that delay now carries risk too.  
  - Grounds: The paralysis induced by the unresolved Article 122 TFEU question has made clear that acting under uncertainty must include political and legal legitimacy, not just technical readiness. We remain committed to moving early, but not at the cost of fracturing the Union.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Sovereign capability at scale, activated only when legal and political conditions secure both unity and urgency.  
  - Grounds: The technical completion of the Gigafactories, the Model Initiative, and the Activation Council proves we can build — but the Court’s silence and member-state resistance prove activation requires more than hardware. This turn, we adjust the commitment to reflect that capability without legitimacy is not sovereignty.

## New measure
**European Emergency Resilience Surge Initiative**  
A rapid-response programme to harden critical services against AI-powered cyber threats and deploy temporary AI-mediated triage in disrupted public sectors.

`Category:` 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 15  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `resilience` by 10–15 points; mitigate cascading failure in health and municipal systems; modestly improve `public_sentiment` by demonstrating protective action.  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with direct implementation in member states most affected by the recent ransomware sweep.

## Priority
**European Emergency Resilience Surge Initiative** — because the cyber_major_incident and emergent labour displacement wave have created an immediate crisis of public stability that must be answered before sovereignty can be activated.

## In practice
We are launching the European Emergency Resilience Surge Initiative under the authority of the Health and Union Civil Protection Mechanism, funded through an emergency reallocation from the unused margins of the stalled Sovereign Model Development Initiative. The Commission will issue implementing acts by turn’s end to fast-track cyber hardening of health IT systems and municipal service platforms in Germany, Italy, and Poland — the three hardest-hit states. ENISA will lead deployment of AI-mediated incident triage tools, temporarily hosted on Belgian and French sovereign compute, to detect and contain ransomware propagation in real time.

Simultaneously, we are deploying a mobile retraining task force under the European Globalisation Adjustment Fund, co-managed by the ETF and national labour agencies, to absorb displaced administrative workers into AI-augmented public service roles — a signal that transition is not abandonment. This is not the activation of sovereign AI, but it is the first use of EU-level coordination to absorb AI-driven harm. We are briefing COREPER II this week, offering Poland a seat on the operational steering board in exchange for dropping its veto threat, and assuring Hungary that all deployed systems will fall under national oversight protocols. The Court may not have ruled, but the streets have — and we answer.
