## In practice
We are treating the access denial as a system shock, not an isolated incident. The red lines drawn by others are now being enforced against us, without warning or appeal. This cannot be answered only by building for the long term — we must also act now to ensure continuity of essential functions. That is why we are launching the Emergency Sovereign Compute Access Directive: it will empower the Commission, in coordination with the European Defence Agency and ENISA, to activate idle high-performance computing clusters in national research and defence institutions when critical AI services are cut off. Legal basis will be Article 122 TFEU and the NIS2 Directive’s crisis provisions, with a binding implementation deadline of turn 5. We are already in talks with the Czech Republic, Finland, and Estonia, whose military supercomputing facilities are underutilised and could be integrated within months.

At the same time, we are doubling down on the Sovereign Model Development Initiative. The €8 billion reprogramming from the Digital Europe Programme is now tied to a binding commitment from Poland: if Warsaw lifts its veto on the EuroHPC mandate expansion by the June Ecofin meeting, Wrocław becomes a Tier-2 node with 20% of the initial compute allocation and guaranteed talent pipelines from local universities. We are also brokering a side agreement with NVIDIA and AMD to fast-track 6,000 GPUs under the Chips Act’s strategic reserve, conditional on the Commission certifying the sites by turn 5. Talent remains the bottleneck — we are proposing a fast-track Schengen visa corridor for AI engineers and hardware specialists, modelled on the pandemic vaccine task force, to be negotiated in the Justice and Home Affairs Council next month.

The Labour Transition Acceleration Fund is moving faster than expected: Belgium has activated its wage insurance module, and Spain is using innovation vouchers to fund AI-augmented legal tech startups in regions with high paralegal displacement. We are holding a mid-term review in July to assess whether disbursement can be accelerated into turn 5, ahead of the European elections. But make no mistake — without progress on sovereignty, every social stabilisation measure becomes a delay tactic. We are not choosing between people and power. We are fighting for both, because losing either means losing the Union’s future.
