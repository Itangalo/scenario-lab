## In practice
We are pushing forward on the Sovereign Model Development Initiative despite the delays. The EuroHPC JU’s mandate expansion is now before the European Parliament’s ITRE and IMCO committees, backed by a joint letter from the French and German research ministers. We are offering Poland a secondary compute node in Wrocław in exchange for dropping its veto, and we have secured a bridging agreement with ASML to prioritise lithography maintenance for the three core sites. Funding remains the tightest knot: we are reprogramming €8 billion from the Digital Europe Programme’s cloud budget and calling on member states to match with national cohesion funds, tied to hosting commitments. The full 10,000-GPU target is now phased — 6,000 by turn 5, the rest by turn 8 — to align with delivery timelines from NVIDIA and AMD under the Chips Act pipeline.

At the same time, we cannot ignore the rising social pressure. The emergent labour displacement wave is no longer a forecast — it is in the streets, in union halls, in regional elections. We are launching the Labour Transition Acceleration Fund under the European Social Fund+, with a fast-disbursing mechanism modelled on the pandemic recovery grants. It targets regions where AI-driven job losses exceed 5% in administrative and legal support roles over the past 12 months. Wage insurance will cover 60% of previous income for up to 18 months, paired with modular retraining in AI-augmented professions — not just coding, but prompt engineering, audit, and oversight roles that will be in demand as we scale sovereign systems. The fund also includes €5bn in innovation vouchers for SMEs to adopt AI in ways that create jobs, not just cut them.

This dual track — building sovereign capacity while stabilising the social floor — is the only way to hold both mandates. We are not abandoning competitiveness for safety, nor sacrificing people for speed. But we are stretched: with four active measures, our political capital is at risk of collapse if one fails. We are betting that delivering tangible support to displaced workers will buy the legitimacy to keep pushing on the model initiative — and that without both, neither will survive the next election cycle.
