## In practice
We are treating the completion of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Access Directive as a turning point: legal activation is done, and the Czech, Finnish, and Estonian clusters are now under binding agreement to integrate into the EU’s crisis compute reserve by Q1 2029. ENISA and the European Defence Agency have signed joint protocols for access, authentication, and audit trails, using NIS2 emergency powers and Article 122 TFEU as the basis. The Commission’s Crisis Coordination Cell will hold its first live test next month, simulating a multi-day outage in health diagnostics and energy grid optimisation. This is not sovereignty yet—but it is continuity under duress, and that is something we can build on.

At the same time, we are pressing Warsaw hard on the EuroHPC expansion. The offer stands: lift the veto, and Wrocław becomes a Tier-2 node with guaranteed compute share and talent pipelines from AGH and the University of Wrocław. We’ve secured side letters from NVIDIA and AMD to fast-track 6,000 GPUs through the Chips Act strategic reserve, but only if the Commission certifies the site by turn 5—which it now has. The bottleneck is no longer hardware or funding; it is political will in one capital. We are leveraging cohesion funds and eastern rail electrification talks to close the deal, with President von der Leyen personally engaged. If Poland says yes, we can begin stacking training runs by early 2029. If not, we trigger fallback planning for alternative nodes in Hungary and Romania, though that delays us by at least six months.

The Labour Transition Acceleration Fund has completed disbursement, with Belgium and Spain reporting strong uptake. A post-mortem review confirms it prevented cascading unemployment in targeted sectors, but also shows retraining lags where local employers did not co-invest. We are preparing a successor proposal focused on employer-linked upskilling, but will not launch it until political capital stabilises. For now, every euro and every negotiation must serve the pivot from emergency patch to enduring capability. We are no longer just responding—we are building, and what we build must scale.
