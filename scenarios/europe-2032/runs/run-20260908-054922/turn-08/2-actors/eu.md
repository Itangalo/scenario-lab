## In practice  
We are treating the cyberattack not as a disruption but as a signal: the gap between capability and safety is no longer theoretical, and the public will not wait for layered governance to catch up. The InvestAI Gigafactories are complete — physical sovereignty exists — but it is meaningless if the systems we build are immediately compromised. That is why we are launching the Emergency Resilience Surge Directive under Article 122 TFEU, bypassing Council deadlock to impose a six-month compliance window for all NIS2 entities. ENISA will issue binding technical standards by the end of Q1, drawing on the red-teaming protocols developed under the previous initiative, and member states must report weekly on implementation progress to the Commission. Non-compliant operators will face temporary suspension of public contracts and access to sovereign AI services.

The Sovereign Model Development Initiative remains in flight, but its success hinges on the Activation Council passing in turn 9. We are renegotiating with Poland not through threats, but through guarantees: a dedicated interpretability task force based in Warsaw, full audit access to all training runs, and co-ownership of the first EU sovereign model’s safety stack. Hungary’s resistance is harder to bridge — their opposition is ideological, not strategic — so we are preparing fallback nodes in Romania and Slovakia, funded from the Chips Act reserve, ready to activate if consensus fails. But legitimacy must be earned, not seized: the resilience directive is our offer to the public. We are not just building power — we are proving we can wield it responsibly. Without that, no council, no compute, no model will save us from irrelevance.
