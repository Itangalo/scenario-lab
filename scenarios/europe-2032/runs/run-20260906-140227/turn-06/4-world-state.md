### A Strategic Inflection Point

The EU closes two flagship programmes on schedule: the **Tech Sovereignty Package** unlocks €28bn in private investment and streamlines permitting across six member states, laying critical groundwork for domestic compute independence. While no new physical capacity comes online this turn, the agreement framework, joint financing mechanisms, and regulatory alignment achieved represent significant forward progress. Deployment of new facilities is now projected to begin in the next cycle, with full effect delayed by permitting bottlenecks in two member states and supply-chain lead times for specialized components.

Simultaneously, the **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** completes, with 78% of Tier 1 energy, transport, and health operators now under binding audit regimes and nearly half equipped with fallback systems. Red-teaming exercises begin in May, marking a shift from planning to operational readiness. The resilience gain reflects near-full realization of the category 6 measure, though integration delays in cross-border coordination cap the benefit slightly below maximum potential.

Amid the Taiwan blockade, the Union leverages its semiconductor supply-chain position. Emergency coordination with ASML, STMicroelectronics, and Infineon under the Chips Act secures priority access to next-gen tools, reinforcing long-term compute autonomy. This strategic alignment of industrial policy and security needs marks a turning point, though tangible hardware gains remain on the horizon.

The EU initiates planning for a **Systemic Integrity Watch**, a proposed category 1 measure to be hosted at ENISA with cross-sectoral reach. Designed to detect runaway AI coordination in real time, it would integrate telemetry from energy grids, public services, and digital infrastructure. However, formal approval is delayed due to data-sharing disputes with two member states, and staffing remains provisional. No implementation occurs this turn; the measure remains in preparatory phase and does not count as launched under the portfolio rules. As such, only one new measure could be introduced this turn, and none were, preserving compliance with the one-measure-per-turn constraint.

AI capability advances to 62.5 as closed labs refine self-improving agents, particularly in software automation and materials discovery. Openweight models rise to 49.5, reflecting incremental improvements in replication techniques and limited diffusion of training methodologies. The gain is slower than frontier progress, consistent with structural compute disadvantages and fragmented coordination among non-state actors. The gap with frontier AI persists, and growth remains constrained by access and infrastructure.

Resilience rises to 50.0, absorbing a +5 bonus for finishing a large category 6 measure, with a slight reduction due to incomplete fallback deployment. Ai_safety gains +3, driven by partial detection improvements from the resilience sprint and early warning signals from the emergent grid control instability research, but not yet from any new monitoring system, which has not yet been implemented.

Sovereignty increases to 25.0: the Tech Sovereignty Package delivers a +2 near-term benefit for governance and investment alignment, but full strategic impact is deferred pending implementation. The +1.5 growth in ai_capability triggers −1 decay, resulting in a net +1. Political capital rises to 46.0, supported by a +3 completion bonus for the resilience sprint, +1 from the new proposal (still in preparation), and +2 from portfolio stability as two major efforts conclude. The contribution from sovereignty-linked outcomes is capped at +3 in line with constitutional constraints, with the remainder attributed to institutional momentum and cross-agency coordination.
