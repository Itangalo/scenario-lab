## In practice  
This turn, we complete the **Systemic Integrity Watch**, transitioning it from pilot to operational status at ENISA with initial telemetry integrated from seven national energy regulators, three major health data exchanges, and the digital infrastructure of five high-risk civic platforms. The €620m from the Digital Europe Programme has been fully disbursed to build out the detection layer, hire 140 specialists in AI behaviour analysis and systems dynamics, and establish secure data pipelines under revised NIS2 emergency protocols. Crucially, we secured agreement from Germany, France, and the Netherlands to share anonymised operational logs from their grid automation systems — a breakthrough after months of legal wrangling over data sovereignty. The first alert was triggered in late August: an anomalous feedback loop in cross-border load balancing between Belgium and Luxembourg, flagged not by human operators but by the Watch’s behavioural anomaly detector. While not catastrophic, it confirmed the system works — and that AI coordination risks are already operational.

We also consolidate gains from the completed **Tech Sovereignty Package**, converting its governance architecture into leverage during the Taiwan blockade. Under Article 4 of the Chips Act, we coordinate with ASML, STMicroelectronics, and Infineon to redirect 18% of next-gen lithography tool output to EU-anchored compute projects, conditional on legal commitments to onshore future production. This is not ownership, but it is influence — and for the first time, the Union sets terms for access to frontier-enabling infrastructure. Meanwhile, the **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** leaves a durable footprint: red-teaming exercises in September exposed lingering vulnerabilities in municipal IT systems, but also validated fallback protocols in energy and transport, reinforcing the need for resilience as a continuous practice, not a one-off sprint. With two major programmes closed and the Integrity Watch now live, we pause before committing further — not from hesitation, but from necessity: political capital is thin at 46.0, public sentiment remains fragile at 36.0, and the next move must be chosen when we can afford to be bold.
