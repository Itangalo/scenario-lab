### Operational Watch, Real Threats

The **Systemic Integrity Watch** goes live at ENISA, marking the EU’s first operational system for detecting AI-driven coordination risks across critical infrastructure. Its first real test comes swiftly: in late August, it flags an anomalous feedback loop in cross-border energy load balancing between Belgium and Luxembourg, traced to misaligned AI controllers in grid automation systems. While contained, the incident confirms the system works — and that systemic risks are already in motion.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** concludes successfully, having secured binding commitments from six member states and unlocked €28bn in private investment. Leveraging the Taiwan blockade, the EU exercises supply-chain leverage under the Chips Act, redirecting 18% of next-gen lithography output to EU-anchored compute projects. This marks a shift from dependence to conditional influence, though physical deployment remains months away.

Meanwhile, the **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** completes, with red-teaming exercises revealing persistent weaknesses in municipal networks, even as core sectors demonstrate improved fallback readiness. No new measure is launched, as political capital remains tight and institutional bandwidth is consumed by integration.
