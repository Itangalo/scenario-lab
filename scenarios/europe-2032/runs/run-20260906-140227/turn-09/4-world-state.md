### Accelerating Frontiers, Narrowing Windows

AI capability advances to 67.0 as closed labs integrate new agent frameworks capable of sustained, goal-directed behaviour across software and scientific domains. The discovery of a viable synthetic organism design by a genome model — hotly debated but not retracted — signals that AI-driven bioengineering is no longer theoretical. Simultaneously, a verification breakthrough in legal contract analysis allows fully automated validation, accelerating capability in regulated domains where human oversight was once assumed essential.

Openweight models reach 55.0, benefiting from leaked training recipes and public replication of agent scaffolding. While still behind the frontier, they now enable automated attack chaining and synthetic biology prototyping by well-resourced non-state actors.

Despite the earlier safety breakthrough, ai_safety drops to 32.0. The verifiable reasoning framework has not yet scaled to fast-evolving agent systems, and the gap between deployment speed and assurance widens. No new safeguards land on frontier models this turn.

### Sovereignty Through Crisis Response

The EU advances its Cyber Shield programme, standing up six regional recovery hubs and conducting red-team drills. These improve coordination and yield early resilience gains (+1), but full operational capacity remains distant. Meanwhile, planning begins for the Sovereign Incident Response Corps — a new large category 4 measure aimed at closing the execution gap in systemic crises. It is not yet operational, but its legal and technical foundations are laid.

Sovereignty declines to 25.0 as ai_capability rises 1.5, costing −1, with no completed category 4 measure to offset it. Political capital falls to 47.0, weighed down by the new large measure (−3) and the ongoing priority (−1), with no completion bonus. A modest +1 from public sentiment above capital is outweighed by portfolio costs. Public sentiment rises slightly to 41.0, reflecting continued trust in crisis response.
