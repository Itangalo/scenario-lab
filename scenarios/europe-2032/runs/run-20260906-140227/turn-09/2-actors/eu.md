## In practice
This turn, we advance **Cyber Shield for Public Services** by standing up the first six regional hubs under ENISA’s coordination, allocating €600 million from the Digital Europe Programme and leveraging NIS2’s Article 13(4) emergency powers to bypass protracted procurement. We are negotiating with COREPER II to activate comitology for rapid adoption, offering smaller member states co-directorship and encrypted telemetry sovereignty to ease concerns over centralisation. The hubs are already conducting red-team drills using synthetic attack patterns derived from openweight models at 53.5, simulating AI-driven lateral movement and zero-day exploitation chains. These exercises feed into the design of AI-audited recovery scripts, now in prototype, which will validate patch integrity and rollback safety before deployment.

At the same time, we launch planning for the **Sovereign Incident Response Corps**, recognising that even the best-prepared defences fail if no EU entity can act decisively during escalation. This is not another advisory body — it will have secure, on-premise compute clusters in Luxembourg and Helsinki, direct API access to national CSIRTs and critical infrastructure telemetry (under strict data governance), and legal authority to trigger overrides during declared incidents. We are initiating trilogues with the European Parliament’s LIBE and ITRE committees to establish its mandate, drawing on the precedent of the EU Health Emergency Preparedness and Response Authority (HERA). Initial staffing will draw from national cyber units and EU cyber diplomacy pools, with recruitment fast-tracked through the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre. The Corps responds to the **bio_uplift_findings** and **verification_widens** developments: if AI can automate organism design or validate legal contracts without human review, the window for response shrinks to hours. We cannot outsource that speed to foreign labs or commercial providers whose interests may not align with ours. This is sovereignty not as ambition, but as necessity — the ability to act when everything else has failed.
