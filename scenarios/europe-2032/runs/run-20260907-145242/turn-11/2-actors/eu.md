## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing. But when one mandate consumes the other, we have already lost. Sovereignty without continuity is hollow. Safety without capacity is surrender. Now we see that continuity without cohesion is illusion.``  
- Grounds: The Emergency Continuity Response Initiative has exposed that even when tools exist, their failure to activate in time reveals a deeper fracture — not just in systems, but in the Union’s ability to act as one body. Our identity must now reflect that political fragmentation is not a side risk, but a core failure mode of both mandates.

* ``- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement will now be conditional on the operational readiness of the Sovereign AI Core Programme, and we will not cut off a member state from critical capabilities unless we can offer a viable, secure alternative within 90 days. We act to defend the regime, not to punish into helplessness — and we now add: when a member state fails, we assume shared responsibility.``  
- Grounds: The cyber incident revealed that non-compliance was not defiance but collapse — Thessaloniki’s node failed not from refusal, but from exhaustion and under-resourcing. We cannot enforce unity by threat alone. The reversal costs us leverage, but gains legitimacy: markets note the shift from punitive to shared burden, and resisting capitals signal conditional openness to re-engagement.

* ``- retire `no_time_for_perfection```  
- Grounds: The Emergency Continuity Response Initiative has absorbed this principle into action. The commitment was a bridge from doctrine to practice; now, practice must stand on its own. We do not abandon the idea, but we no longer need to name it.

* ``- add `repair_before_replace` (commitment): We will not build new systems while old ones lie broken. Every new capability we enable must first prove it can sustain what already exists.``  
- Grounds: The collapse of air-gapped nodes was not due to poor design, but to neglected maintenance and untested procedures. We are now committing to operational discipline: no new AI-enabled functions in public services will be approved until continuity systems are validated in live drills. This binds future ambition to present reliability.

A recurring, mandatory exercise regime requiring all member states to validate their Continuity Assurance Framework nodes under live attack conditions, with EU-led red-teaming and mutual assistance triggers.

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Cross-Border Maintenance Drills Programme  
We are prioritizing this because the Emergency Continuity Response Initiative fixed nodes — but fixing them once is not continuity. We must prove that the EU can maintain them together, or the next failure will be the last.

## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from emergency repair to enduring discipline. The Emergency Continuity Response Initiative succeeded in getting 17 critical nodes back online — but in Thessaloniki, Athens, and Bratislava, we found that operators had never been trained on failover procedures, and in two cases, air-gapped systems had been powered down to save costs. The framework was law, but it was not lived. We cannot accept a Union where continuity depends on the vigilance of individual administrators.

So we are launching the Cross-Border Maintenance Drills Programme under the EAAD, funded from the same €1.8bn Crisis Solidarity Mechanism, with €420m allocated over 18 months. This is not another one-off intervention — it is a regime of recurring, unannounced drills, simulating model-generated ransomware, data poisoning, and silent divergence. Each drill will be led by a rotating EU red team, with participation mandated by Article 122 TFEU and enforced through conditional cohesion disbursement. When a node fails, the response is not blame — it is activation of mutual aid, with technical teams from compliant states deployed within 24 hours.

We are offering resisting capitals a new deal: not compliance through coercion, but co-ownership through contribution. Those who host red teams or provide trainers will receive priority access to the Sovereign AI Core Programme’s early capacity. The message is clear: sovereignty is not a wall — it is a web. And we will now test the web, not just the nodes.
