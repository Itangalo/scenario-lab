### A Fragile Reboot

The EU pushes forward with the Real-Time Assurance Network, deploying integration teams to resistant capitals under a revised offer of cost coverage, co-designed override thresholds, and early access incentives. Legal disputes over centralised control persist, but the framing of mutual insurance gains cautious traction. Berlin agrees to pilot the system, though Paris and Warsaw remain non-committal. The network remains partial, its authority contested, but its technical footprint expands.

The Cross-Border Maintenance Drills see broader participation—19 states now involved—but operational cohesion remains undermined by technical staff slowdowns. Linking drill performance to digital transition bonuses introduces leverage, but resentment simmers over perceived conditional funding. The November exercise in the Baltics proceeds at reduced intensity, with several nations opting out of live red-teaming.

Exploratory talks begin on a potential model provenance framework, with Commission staff drafting technical specifications for a cryptographically secure registry. Initial discussions with the emergent transnational watchdog focus on interoperability, but no formal launch occurs. Divergent cryptographic standards and concerns over jurisdictional overreach stall progress. The initiative remains in scoping phase, with no commitment to implementation this turn.

Meanwhile, signs of a capability plateau emerge: a major frontier model release underperforms expectations, and prices for top-tier access drop sharply. The trend is disputed internally, but external observers interpret it as a stall. Limited safety improvements are observed in deployed systems, though forward-looking confidence in monitoring frameworks supports a modest upward adjustment in the safety metric.
