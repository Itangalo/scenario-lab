## In practice
This turn, we are operationalising the Real-Time Assurance Network despite legal resistance in three capitals. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) is deploying integration teams to Berlin, Paris, and Warsaw with a revised offer: full cost coverage for interpretability probe installation, co-design of override thresholds via the new Joint Oversight Board, and — for early adopters — six-month priority access to the Sovereign AI Core’s anomaly detection suite. We are funding this through the Crisis Solidarity Mechanism (€1.4bn draw) and the deployment levy (now at 15%, generating €380m this turn). The legal disputes over override authority remain, but we are reframing them not as centralisation, but as mutual insurance: no single state will lose control, but no single state will be allowed to become a vector.

The Cross-Border Maintenance Drills continue, with the next cycle scheduled for November in the Baltic region, focusing on energy grid resilience under AI-mediated attack. Participation is up from 14 to 19 member states, but technical staff slowdowns persist — we are addressing this by linking drill performance to national digital transition bonuses under the Recovery and Resilience Facility. This is not coercion; it is alignment.

We are also responding to the emergent_global_ai_watchdog not with rivalry, but with conditional alignment. Our new EU-Verified Provenance Initiative will be technically interoperable with their model tracing system, but only if they adopt our cryptographic standards and grant reciprocal access. We are not ceding leadership; we are setting the benchmark. The message is clear: sovereignty does not mean isolation. It means being the source of the standard others choose to follow.
