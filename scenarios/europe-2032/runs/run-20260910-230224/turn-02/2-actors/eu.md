## New measure
**EU Semiconductor Allocation and Leverage Regime**
Creates EU-coordinated allocation of scarce advanced chips for critical services and gigafactory build-out, paired with joint control of lithography maintenance, spares and upstream inputs as bargained leverage.
This is International coordination and leverage because the Taiwan blockade turns every compute question into a security negotiation overnight, and the Union's only card that cannot be switched off from outside is its upstream bottleneck held jointly rather than picked off member-state by member-state.

## Priority
EU Semiconductor Allocation and Leverage Regime (new) – because without allocated chips and a joint ASML position the gigafactories stall and the Shield has nothing to run on, outranking even our own build-out this turn.

## In practice
We are moving the new regime through the Foreign Affairs Council and the Trade configuration under the Anti-Coercion Instrument and the Chips Act emergency provisions, with DG TRADE and DG CNECT jointly running a clearinghouse for advanced-chip stocks, forward orders and gigafactory requirements. Member states with fabs, packaging and materials are offered priority allocation for essential operators in exchange for accepting EU-level export authorisation for lithography services and spares.

At the same time we keep M1 and M2 alive but sequenced behind allocation: site selection continues, but grid-connection and guarantee decisions are now conditional on secured chip supply, to stop a subsidy race for capacity that cannot be equipped. M3 drills are re-tasked to islanding scenarios — operating grids, ports and hospitals through prolonged supply cutoff — with HERA adding pooled procurement of sequencing-based biodetection to answer the genome-model finding before it leaves the biosecurity community.
