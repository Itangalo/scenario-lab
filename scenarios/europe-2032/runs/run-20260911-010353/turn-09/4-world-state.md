### Patching at machine speed
The winter ransomware wave did not repeat. Through the autumn, hospital IT teams and municipal duty desks installed automated patching and swarm-detection stacks delivered through the Union's cyber agency and the allied liaison cells. Where the tooling landed, intrusions that had previously spread for weeks were caught in hours. Mayors who had drilled continuity last year found insurers willing to talk again; those who had not were now told deployment plus drills was the price of cover.

The relief was real but partial. Rollout favoured sites already on telemetry and with staff to run weekly exercises. Smaller town halls and eastern hospitals lagged, short of administrators and wary of swapping one dependency for another. Auditors noted the new stacks were largely American models running on hosted capacity, re-licensed at short notice.

### Weights loose, robots working
Any sense of regained control broke on two other fronts. A new openly released model, close to the closed frontier, was downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in days. Police cyber units privately conceded recall was now impossible; the same tooling they used to defend was available to probe with.

At the same time, logistics robots arrived in numbers in Rotterdam, Antwerp and Lodz — picking, palletising, resupplying. Warehouse operators cut agency shifts; unions marched under banners about judgement and care. The machines came from Chinese factories with American control software, underlining how little of the stack the Union owned.

That dependence sharpened when Washington forced a Dutch lithography champion to cut servicing and older-machine exports further. The Hague protested, Brussels offered joint buying and legal cover, but deliveries slipped and the second gigafactory stayed in court. Papers called it humiliation: Europe's one chokepoint used by someone else.

Brussels held the grid works and allied purchasing warm without new money, betting the defensive rollout would close the window before next cascade season. It did not say the finishing dates were slipping.
