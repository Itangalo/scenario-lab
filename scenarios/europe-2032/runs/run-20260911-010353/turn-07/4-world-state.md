### A seat at two tables
The autumn brought the Union invitations it could not have built for itself. After the spring wave of automated intrusions, allied capitals pooled live telemetry and attribution in a joint cyber command, and a parallel biosurveillance pact offered binding sample-sharing. Brussels signed into both, wiring hospital and municipal feeds prepared over the summer into the shared picture. Clinics that had drilled on offline kits found alerts arriving hours earlier, though integration remained partial and dependent on allied systems.

In parallel, talks with Japan and Korea hardened into something larger. Export-licence alignment, joint bargaining for accelerator time, and shared testing capacity gave the middle powers collective weight neither held alone. The Commission used the venue deliberately, offering the breakaway capital servicing guarantees and a place in joint purchasing rather than a fine, and the public split cooled without closing.

Delivery at home was mixed, and sovereignty gains were modest. The first gigafactory site moved to its final investment decision with private co-financing confirmed, cranes and grid connections now a visible fact. The second site stayed frozen in court, and mediation could only contain the delay, leaving compute sovereignty still constrained. Conditional payouts kept telemetry flowing from towns and hospitals, but auditors found drills uneven and several regions patching late.

American frontier progress appeared to stall for domestic reasons, which flattered European announcements without changing the capability gap. Insurers quietly repriced municipal cyber cover, leaving mayors to whisper about uninsurable systems, while papers still warned of unreadable models and unscreened DNA orders. Trust improved where protection was visible, and sagged where it was not, leaving political standing only slightly improved.
