### The jump no one can recall
The spring began with a demonstration. A leading lab showed a system writing, testing and chaining exploits and proofs with almost no supervision, in hours. Within weeks, a freely downloadable release matched much of it. It spread to hundreds of thousands of machines before regulators had drafted a statement. Police and corporate teams said the same thing: what an attacker could do had changed.

At the same time, researchers lost the thread inside the models. The new systems did not explain themselves in readable steps; the internal representations that worked best were never words. Oversight teams that relied on reading those steps found their tools blind overnight. Developers presented it as an efficiency gain. Safety staff called it a loss.

### Brussels holds, and splits
In Brussels, two long projects crossed the line. The technology funding and permitting drive was declared complete, with private money committed and fast-track zones operating, and the joint cyber-health surveillance network with allies went live, tying recovery funds to shared telemetry and sequencing feeds.

The celebration was short. One capital signed its own cloud and accelerator arrangement with a large foreign provider, on terms that undercut the common bargaining line. Ministers defended it as pragmatism; the Commission read it as a break. Threats to withhold recovery money only hardened the dispute.

The Commission's answer was to husband what it had. Mediation kept the flagship computing build alive — one site pushed toward a final investment decision, another stuck in court — while officials in Tokyo and Seoul were offered servicing and joint purchasing to show an alternative to Washington or Beijing. At home, civil-protection and health agencies rushed backup lockdowns, offline kits and conditional payouts to towns and hospitals still patching from the autumn probes.

It partly worked. Hospitals degraded to backups rather than paper when new probes came. But trust sagged. Papers warned of unreadable models and unscreened DNA orders, workers feared automation, and the sight of a member state going its own way left the Union looking unable to hold its line.
