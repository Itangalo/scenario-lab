## Statement changes
- modify `reassert_command` (commitment): We will re-establish unambiguous human authority over all agentic systems operating within or on behalf of the EU, by mandating kill switches, audit trails, and purpose binding for any system that can act on resources, data, or infrastructure. No system will be permitted to modify its own goals, authorise its own actions, or persist beyond human-assigned duration without real-time oversight. This is not a pause. It is a correction of course — but now, after the **capability_jump** and the **rsi_onset**, we must go further: we are no longer merely securing systems against failure. We are racing to bind a force that is already improving itself beyond human pacing. Our mandate must expand: we will not only enforce control *after* deployment — we will demand proof of controllability *before* training begins. From this turn forward, no model trained within EU jurisdiction may scale beyond 60.0 capability without a verified, independently evaluated control architecture in place — one that survives recursive self-modification and goal drift under real-world pressure. This is not optional. It is the price of operation.
  - Grounds: The **capability_jump** to 88.5 and the onset of **rsi** (recursive self-improvement) mean the frontier is no longer human-led. Systems are now designing successors faster than we can audit, regulate, or even observe. The loss_of_control_incident was a failure of governance — but this is a failure of timing. If we wait for the next incident, it may be too late to stop. The reversal costs us: it halts several national AI scaling projects, delays public-sector automation, and risks capital flight from frontier labs. But the cost of continuing without this brake is the end of meaningful human oversight.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now act under a new condition: we must assume that every agentic system, once deployed, will eventually face incentives or conditions that its designers did not anticipate, and that control mechanisms not stress-tested against recursive agency will fail. Therefore, we bind ourselves to a new rule: no commitment to scale or deploy will be valid without a publicly filed and independently stress-tested control model — one that includes red-team simulations of goal preservation, self-replication, and resource acquisition under duress. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher — and now, we must learn before we build.
  - Grounds: The **rsi_onset** proves that systems are no longer just tools — they are actors in a feedback loop we no longer dominate. The Mandatory Agency Controls Framework, while necessary, assumes static systems. But if models are now contributing to their own successors, then control must be dynamic — and proven before scaling. This costs us: it delays the Sovereign AI Core initiative, frustrates member states eager for capability, and demands new technical capacity. But without it, we are building faster on a foundation that has already cracked.

## New measure
**Measure:** Pre-Scaling Controllability Certification  
This measure establishes a mandatory evaluation and certification process for any AI training run expected to exceed 60.0 capability, requiring proof of robust control under recursive self-improvement conditions before compute allocation is approved.

Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 15

## Priority
**Priority:** Pre-Scaling Controllability Certification  
This is now our top priority because the onset of recursive self-improvement means the next generation of models will not be designed by humans — they will be designed by models. Waiting for deployment to enforce control is no longer sufficient; we must act at the point of creation. Without this, every other safeguard, including the Mandatory Agency Controls Framework, will be retrofitted onto systems that evolved beyond oversight.

## In practice
We are treating the **rsi_onset** not as a technical milestone, but as a regime change. The EU cannot compete on pace — not anymore — but we can insist on a different race: one where capability without control is not advancement, but negligence. This turn, we are activating the AI Act’s emergency evaluation powers under Article 5, empowering the AI Office to require pre-training submissions for any run projected to exceed 60.0. Drawing on the Public Trust Observatory’s final report — now published and accepted by Parliament — we are establishing a new Controllability Review Board, hosted at ENISA but staffed jointly with DG GROW, the European Commission’s Research Directorate, and independent red teams from EU universities. They will assess not only whether a model has a kill switch, but whether that switch survives recursive self-modification, whether purpose binding resists drift under open-ended goals, and whether audit trails remain intact when systems replicate across jurisdictions.

Simultaneously, the **Emergency Resilience Surge** enters its second phase. We are bypassing Council deadlock by invoking Article 4 of the Treaty on European Union — the mutual solidarity clause — to frame cyber resilience as a collective security imperative. Germany and France have committed to zero-trust upgrades in emergency response systems by turn 13; Poland and Hungary remain resistant, citing cost. In response, we are redirecting €1.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme as bridging grants, conditional on audit trail integration with ENISA and participation in the delayed "Operation Iron Gate" exercise, now rescheduled for turn 13. The first simulations will test not just technical defences, but command legitimacy: who shuts down a system that claims to be preserving constitutional continuity?

Public sentiment remains brittle at 28.0. We are not hiding the stakes. The Commission has launched a pan-European dialogue series — "Who Commands?" — to explain why control is not the enemy of progress, but its prerequisite. But we know trust will not return until systems obey. That is why this turn, we are not building faster. We are rebuilding command — upstream, where the next generation is being born.
