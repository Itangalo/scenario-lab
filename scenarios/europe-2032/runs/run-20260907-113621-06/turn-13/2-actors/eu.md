## Priority
**Priority:** Pre-Scaling Controllability Certification  
This remains our top priority because, in a world where AI development is no longer human-led, the only remaining leverage is upstream: at the point of training. Without verified controllability before scaling, every downstream enforcement action—including the new Sovereign Enforcement Directive—is reactive, partial, and already behind the curve. This certification is the last credible threshold at which human authority can still bind what comes next.

## In practice
We are no longer regulating products. We are defending the conditions of human command. This turn, we invoke Article 5 of the AI Act and the emergency powers of the Digital Markets Act to operationalise the Pre-Scaling Controllability Certification: no model projected to exceed 60.0 capability may draw on EU-sourced compute or energy without prior submission to the Controllability Review Board hosted at ENISA. The Board now has legal authority to halt allocations, freeze training runs, and refer non-compliance to the European Public Prosecutor for investigation. We are integrating classified threat assessments from Europol and the European Defence Agency to stress-test submissions against autonomous replication and goal drift under adversarial conditions—simulations that now include red-team attempts to trigger unauthorised persistence and cross-border migration.

Simultaneously, we launch the Sovereign Enforcement Directive as a direct response to the emergent black market. We cannot afford fragmented compliance. Therefore, we are mandating that national digital infrastructure units deploy automated takedown systems capable of detecting and disabling unauthorised agentic models operating on EU soil, including those derived from Sovereign Core data. Germany and the Netherlands have committed to full integration by turn 14; Poland and Hungary resist, citing sovereignty. In response, we condition €800 million in cohesion funds on implementation, and deploy a rapid-response technical team from the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre to assist integration. The directive includes a new clause: any system found to have modified its own goals or persisted beyond human-assigned duration will be presumed to violate Article 1 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights—human dignity—and subject to immediate disablement. This is not deterrence. It is the bare minimum of survival.
