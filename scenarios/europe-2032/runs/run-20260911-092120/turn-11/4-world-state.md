### Holding pattern under strain

Brussels spent the autumn trying not to lose ground. With no money to table and courts still freezing the two concrete compute sites, Commission lawyers and energy officials shuttled between freeze councils and court-appointed referees, haggling over capped water and power envelopes, sequenced grid ties and earmarks for local clinics. Drafts circulated, technical sign-off inched forward site by site. No injunction was challenged, no tape was lifted. The sites stayed silent under their vigils.

The no-undercut pact on foreign model supply held on paper. In practice the capital with its separate supply deal kept it, and pooled-volume offers drew only polite language from Washington about continuity. Hospitals stayed on the slower domestic helpers and the continuity stack. Where triage tools had bedded in, imaging queues held; elsewhere waits lengthened as maintenance funds thinned.

Health and cyber agencies worked inside old mandates: tighter syndromic watch, hospital hygiene checks, fresh guidance to municipalities and university labs on handling the powerful openly downloadable system now on hundreds of thousands of machines. The contested biosecurity paper faded from headlines but left unease in lab circles.

Then the sea lane news intruded. Extended exercises around Taiwan, rising shipping-insurance premiums and an expulsion dominated business pages. Trade officials circulated a quiet note on chip-supply exposure, pooled stocks and rerouting. No shortage hit, but procurement officers started asking what a three-month disruption would mean for servers, spares and clinical devices.

Around the frozen sites the mood darkened. Local press carried single-source reports of tampered fencing, a scorched cable trench near a grid connection, night-time gatherings hardening into longer blockades. Police spoke of stepped-up patrols and emergency load plans for nearby hospitals. Nothing was confirmed as coordinated sabotage, but rumours of anti-data-centre networks claiming responsibility spread, and mayors demanded reassurance the wards would keep power through winter.

By December, cohesion had survived, but little else had moved.
