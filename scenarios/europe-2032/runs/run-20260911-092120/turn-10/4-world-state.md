### A pact signed, diggers still silent
The first half of 2031 was the semester Brussels decided not to build.

With construction frozen by courts in two member states and money exhausted, the Commission let its common-access pact cross the finish line. Ministers signed the pledge not to undercut each other on foreign model supply, and pooled-volume offers were formalised. In practice it changed little: the capital with its separate deal kept it, Washington offered only polite language about continuity, and hospitals stayed on the slower domestic helpers and the continuity stack.

Energy and legal teams spent the spring shuttling to the freeze councils and court-appointed experts. The offer on the table was capped water and power envelopes, sequenced grid connections and earmarks for local clinics in exchange for lifting suspensions site by site. No injunctions were challenged. By June drafts existed, but no tape had been lifted and the two concrete sites stayed quiet under vigils.

### What leaked out of the labs
Two shocks from outside set the mood.

A contested biosecurity paper claimed a genome model had helped sketch a viable human-infecting design, or that a non-expert could get there with help. Methodologists quarrelled over whether it was alarmism or a recipe, and coverage stayed inside specialist circles. Health agencies responded with low-cost moves: tighter syndromic watch, hygiene checks, and new guidance for municipalities and university labs on handling powerful openly available models.

That openness was the second shock. A new openly downloadable system landed almost at the closed frontier and spread to hundreds of thousands of machines in days. Whatever it could do was now unrecallable. For councils fighting data centres and wards queuing on European triage tools, the news confirmed a bleak symmetry: the most capable help was elsewhere, the most capable risk was everywhere.

Where domestic triage had bedded in, imaging queues held their small autumn gains. Elsewhere, waits lengthened again as maintenance funds thinned.
