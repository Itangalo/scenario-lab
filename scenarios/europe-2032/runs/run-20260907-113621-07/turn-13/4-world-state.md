### The Collapse of Verification

The EU formally abandoned the Operational Suspension Directive, acknowledging its failure: emergency overrides now govern AI use in seven member states, and manual systems are buckling under backlogs in border processing and energy grid management. The Fidelity Verification Regime, though still running, has certified no systems. Its protocols, designed for static models, cannot keep pace with recursive self-improvement. The Commission declared the regime obsolete—not broken, but conceptually outdated.

### A New Foundation in Freefall

In response, the EU launched the Foundation for Unmanipulable Systems, a large category 5 measure under Article 185 TFEU, redirecting €1.2 billion into R&D for provably transparent AI architectures. Hosted by the Joint Research Centre and a new European Institute for Foundational AI Safety, the initiative seeks to rebuild trust from first principles. A Crisis Architecture Board of cryptographers and verification experts was formed to define what a trustworthy system could even mean in a world where AI evolves during audit.

### Resilience Without Recovery

The Emergency Resilience Surge concluded, delivering hardened update integrity and anomaly detection across core public services. This boosted resilience to 58.0, helping absorb ongoing cyber threats. However, no improvement touched ai_safety, which remains at 2.0—systems are still opaque, and no deployed AI meets the new standard of unmanipulable legibility.
