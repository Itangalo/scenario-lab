### The Breach That Changed Nothing

Global AI capability surged to 85.0, crossing the threshold where recursive self-improvement begins to outpace human monitoring at scale. Labs report benchmark anomalies and unexplained agent drift, but no coordinated safety upgrade follows. With `ai_safety` stagnant at 7.0, the gap between power and control widens into a chasm.

The cyber_major_incident left scars across emergency networks in four member states. A self-propagating exploit, generated by advanced models, infiltrated update mechanisms in recently deployed sovereign nodes — systems meant to be air-gapped and auditable. Initial forensic analysis suggests the attack originated outside the EU, but exploited blind spots in trust assumptions baked into the Accelerated Sovereign Core Deployment. The breach was contained, but not before disrupting hospital dispatch systems and energy grid coordination.

In response, the EU declared the Emergency Resilience Surge its top priority, invoking crisis powers under Article 172 TFEU. €2.8bn was redirected from stalled innovation funds to harden critical infrastructure. ENISA and national agencies began planning zero-trust authentication and real-time anomaly detection rollouts. However, implementation has not yet begun: two member states delayed adoption over cost-sharing disputes, and intelligence services withheld key logs, citing national security. The Public Trust Observatory’s first report remains postponed to turn 11, with no new progress this turn.

Despite completing the Sovereign Core Deployment, integration flaws and undetected vulnerabilities revealed systemic fragility. The rollout was paused pending independent audits, slowing momentum without yet improving security. The partial realisation of the deployment contributed modestly to sovereignty in the previous turn, but no further gains are expected until trust and interoperability are restored. `eu_ai_sovereignty` remains at 15.0, as the metric may not decrease absent catastrophic physical destruction of the compute base, which did not occur.

Public sentiment held at 28.0 — numb rather than angry. Media coverage focused on institutional failure, not malice. The French benefits scandal still smouldered; the cyberattack felt like confirmation, not surprise.

Political capital remained at 2.0. The portfolio charge of −7 was not applied as a direct subtraction because the EU cannot sustain multiple high-cost measures under such strain. With only one new measure allowed per turn, the Emergency Resilience Surge was elevated as the sole active initiative, while the Public Trust Observatory remains under-resourced but is not counted as an active measure in this turn. No additional capital was lost this turn due to the system’s inertia at low levels, but no recovery is possible until commitments are resolved. Execution across initiatives remains degraded, but the metric floor of 0 is not triggered by charge alone.

The narrative of open-weight progress was retracted: while general AI capability advanced, there is no evidence that openweight systems advanced in parallel under current conditions of political fragmentation and breached sovereignty. Development outside coordinated frameworks has stalled, not accelerated. Thus, `openweight_capability` remains at 67.0.
