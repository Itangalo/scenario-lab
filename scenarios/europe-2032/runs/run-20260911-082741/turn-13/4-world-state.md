### Left out of the room
Washington and Beijing announced it jointly in October: a limited pact on securing model weights, restraining autonomous escalation, and controlling a class of biological design tools. Verification is thin — joint inspections, shared incident logs — but real. Brussels learned the broad strokes hours before the cameras, not at the table.

The snub stung because Europe had something to offer. The joint watch floor's restoration record and the new certifiable check for shop-floor assistants were briefed as a European verification contribution. Envoys shuttled with a linkage few hid: cooperation on lithography and export audits in exchange for a seat as verifier and standards partner.

The answer was polite and noncommittal. Observer technical talks, no decision on membership. American and Chinese negotiators praised European telemetry in public while drafting annexes in private.

### Repair pays, transition lags
At home, the municipal restoration drive closed out. Locked backups, offline kits and cut-link drills are now routine in the worst-hit cities; clinics and grid operators ride through probes that would have darkened them in 2029. Nurses in recovered wards talk about records that stay up.

The embodied-labour fund ground on. Payout offices in Rotterdam, Hamburg and Lyon accelerated wage-insurance, vouchers were re-anchored to repair, care and construction, and safety-certification courses filled. But payments still lagged lost wages, automating employers still declined to co-fund, and fenced-off factory shells still loomed over meetings. Unions welcomed the courses and denounced the gates in the same breath.

Capability growth eased slightly under the great-power restraint, but open releases crept closer to the frontier and assurance kept slipping. Evening news carried restored services alongside night-shift footage of three workers where thirty stood. Trust fell again.
