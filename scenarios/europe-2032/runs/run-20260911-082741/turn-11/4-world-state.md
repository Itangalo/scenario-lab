### The sweep
In late summer the automated ransomware sweep arrived as feared: a compromised update pushed through municipal IT providers, encrypting town halls, clinics and two regional grid operators within hours. Screens demanded payment while appointment systems and billing went dark. Shared telemetry lit up at once, and attribution quickly stalled.

Where the joint watch floor had drilled, isolation worked. Cross-border teams cut cloud links, rolled clinics to locked backups and offline kits, and restored core services in days. Where councils had refused telemetry sharing, recovery took weeks, with paper registers and diverted ambulances.

### Repair that lands, trust that doesn't
The civic trust programme closed out its formal work this autumn. Audited triage assistants kept cutting waiting lists in cooperating cities, the redress portal processed claims from the frozen benefits system, and the liability rules finally gave lawyers a text to point to.

It did not move the national argument. Evening news split between nurses praising restored records and opposition MPs filming the fenced, empty gigafactory site. Insurers still refused to underwrite new civic deployments, data-centre cancellations left no spare capacity to borrow, and talks on lithography equipment stayed stuck on demands for binding controls first.

Brussels answered with a restoration surge, funnelling crews, backups and isolation drills to the worst-hit municipalities. The Commission was careful to keep the frozen welfare automation frozen, insisting restoration was not re-imposition.

Europe ended the year with cascade prevented but exhaustion visible: better at surviving the blow than at explaining why the blow keeps landing, still protected by borrowed telemetry, still without machines of its own.
