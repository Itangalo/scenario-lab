### The machines arrive
The first half of 2032 is when warehouse robots stop being pilots. Chinese-built units running American control software take over picking, sorting and palletising across logistics hubs in Rotterdam, Hamburg and Lyon almost completely. Welding lines and port handling follow. Unions film night shifts with three workers where thirty stood.

The line hardens quickly: repair bays, care wards and construction sites where plans are wrong stay manual. Military use stays in the same place — resupply convoys, mine clearance and perimeter patrol — lethal targeting still argued over, still nominally human.

For Europe it is a double exposure. The hardware is imported, the models are licensed, and neither can be substituted.

### A result that helps
In parallel, a control result from the labs lands on deployed systems. For the first time operators can certify a bounded safety property on shop-floor assistants rather than argue for it. Adoption is fast because developers want it. Factory inspectors in cooperating regions start using it for certification.

It does not close the assurance gap, but it stops the bleeding.

### Repair holds, transition stalls
The joint cyber command work formally closes. Pooled telemetry and the joint watch floor now restore clinics and grid operators in days without improvisation. The restoration surge in the worst-hit municipalities continues — locked backups, offline kits, cut-link drills — and core services hold.

Brussels launches a wage-insurance and retraining fund for displaced logistics workers, tied to repair, care and construction roles, with a safety-certification track. The offer is real but thin: payments are slow, vouchers do not match lost wages, and co-funding from automating employers largely fails to materialise. Regions with fenced gigafactory sites see it as abandonment by another name.

With funds exhausted and cohesion frayed, naming the restoration surge as the priority changes nothing on the ground. Timelines do not move, and everyone in the Council knows it.

Evening news splits again: nurses praising restored records, warehouse workers protesting robot gates. Trust falls further.
