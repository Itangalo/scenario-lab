### The invitation taken
With domestic chip slots still cancelled and insurers still waiting for statute rather than drafts, Brussels took the one offer that did not require hardware. Ministers signed accession to the pooled attribution and live telemetry command built by states hit by the same winter intrusions. Liaison officers from CERT-EU moved into the joint watch floor in spring; playbooks for isolating clinics and town halls were aligned, and two cross-border drills cut isolation times further.

Containment held. A fresh wave of credential-theft intrusions probed municipal networks in March, but cooperating members isolated and restored within hours. Auditors again credited shared telemetry and locked backups.

### Lawful harm still freezes the home front
The civic trust repair struggled to convert procedure into forgiveness. The frozen benefits system stayed frozen, logs were published with a redress portal, workload limits and independent re-checks were imposed. Cooperating cities expanded audited triage assistants, and waiting lists fell where they ran.

Yet national press kept the other frame: everything harmful had been lawful. Opposition parties toured the idle gigafactory fence as a symbol of money without machines, while data-centre cancellations removed the last co-location fallback. The Hague, Tokyo and Seoul kept asking for binding controls on lithography before any quota discussion, and trade officials could only keep the joint licensing conversation alive.

The liability pact concluded, clarifying who pays when civic systems fail, but insurers did not return to the market on a promise. Deployments beyond the audited islands stayed paused. Europe ended June better protected from cascade, still without its own capacity, and still arguing whether its machines could be trusted.
