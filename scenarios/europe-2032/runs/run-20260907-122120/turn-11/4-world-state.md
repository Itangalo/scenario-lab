### Crisis and Response

A wave of AI-powered ransomware cripples municipal systems across Belgium, the Netherlands, and northern Italy, exploiting legacy ERP software with machine-generated payloads that evade traditional detection. The attack, attributed to a transnational cybercrime group using model-derived tooling, forces emergency shutdowns in public service portals and delays critical infrastructure maintenance. In response, the EU launches the **Sovereign AI Incident Response Network**, integrating municipal teams from Bologna, Ljubljana, and Malmö into ENISA’s framework, deploying shared playbooks and leveraging EuroHPC compute for forensic simulation. Mandatory 24-hour reporting is introduced for critical operators, marking the first coordinated pan-European technical response to an AI-driven cyber incident.

Meanwhile, the **Sovereign Evaluation Regime for High-Capability Systems** moves forward under emergency powers, with evaluations beginning on a voluntary basis for six frontier models. A tripartite leadership team (French, German, Polish) is appointed, and incentives like early red-team insights are offered to encourage compliance. However, legal challenges from U.S. and UK labs loom, and Hungary continues its parallel integration with a U.S. hyperscaler’s moderation stack, undermining unity.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

AI capability advances to 71.5, driven by self-improving agents in code generation and supply chain logistics. Open-weight models reach 59.5 as efficient architectures diffuse further through developer channels—still short of the frontier but narrowing the gap.

Despite the new evaluation regime, no safety improvements land on deployed systems this turn. The gap between advancing capability and weak assurance deepens, exacerbated by the ransomware attack’s use of generative tools. ai_safety drops to 20.0.

Resilience rises to 61.0, reflecting the new incident response network’s structural gains. Political capital holds steady at 34.0 despite high costs: the portfolio charge is −4 (large measure + priority), offset by a +4 proposal bonus for responding directly to a severe cyber incident. Public sentiment falls to 28.0 as municipalities struggle with service outages and media highlight dependency on foreign AI infrastructure.

Sovereignty dips to 22.0: the rise in ai_capability costs −1, and Hungary’s continued defection counts as a second occurrence of member_state_defection, costing another −1. No sovereign capacity was secured this turn.
