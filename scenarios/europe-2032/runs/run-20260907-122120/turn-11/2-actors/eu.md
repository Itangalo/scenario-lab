## In practice
This turn, we finalise the launch of the Sovereign Evaluation Regime for High-Capability Systems, activating the European AI Office’s new evaluation division with €680 million from the Digital Europe Programme and Innovation Fund. We appoint a tripartite leadership—French, German, and Polish technical leads—to signal unity and secure early buy-in, while offering compliant developers early access to red-team findings as an incentive to cooperate. Legal teams from the Commission’s DG CONNECT and DG JUST are preparing for challenges from U.S. and UK labs, grounding our authority in Article 12a of the AI Act and the Union’s right to protect its internal market. The Council’s Internal Market configuration will vote on full adoption in early 2032; until then, we operate under emergency powers to begin evaluations on a voluntary basis, with six high-capability systems already in the pipeline.

Simultaneously, we respond to the cyber_major_incident—a ransomware sweep across municipal services in Belgium, the Netherlands, and northern Italy, enabled by AI-generated exploits targeting legacy ERP systems—by launching the Sovereign AI Incident Response Network. This builds on the emergent_sovereign_tech_alliance, integrating municipal AI teams from Bologna, Ljubljana, and Malmö into a formal ENISA-coordinated structure with shared detection tools, playbooks, and access to EuroHPC’s sovereign compute for forensic simulation. We mandate incident reporting within 24 hours for all critical operators and fund regional CERT upgrades through the Digital Europe Programme. The measure is large because it requires staffing, interoperability agreements, and live exercises—but it is necessary: defenders are reactive, and the offensive is now automated. We tie it to the existing Emergency Democratic Integrity Protocol’s fusion cells to avoid duplication, ensuring disinformation and cyber arms of attacks are treated as one threat.

Hungary’s continued defection to a U.S. hyperscaler’s moderation stack remains a political wound. We do not escalate, but quietly strengthen bilateral data-sharing agreements with Austria and Slovakia to isolate the breach in coordination. The emergent_sovereign_tech_alliance gives us hope: it shows that local, lightweight, EU-rooted AI can gain public trust and resist external capture. We will nurture it—not through grants, but by opening procurement pathways and certifying its tools under the new evaluation regime. This is how sovereignty grows: not just in compute, but in legitimacy.
