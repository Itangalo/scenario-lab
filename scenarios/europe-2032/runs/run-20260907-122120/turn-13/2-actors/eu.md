## In practice
This turn, we launch the Energy-Secure Compute Corridors Initiative to break the deadlock between compute urgency and environmental legitimacy. We designate four pilot corridors — in Finland (geothermal), Spain (solar-hybrid), Germany (hydrogen-backed), and Poland (deep-cooled mine repurposing) — each required to achieve PUE <1.10 and full renewable sourcing from new installations, verified by the European Environment Agency. The European Investment Bank unlocks €3.8 billion in additional blended finance, co-financed by Connecting Europe Facility and Just Transition Fund resources, with disbursement tied to environmental compliance and legal anchoring of model weights under EU jurisdiction. We invoke Article 122 TFEU again, this time pairing emergency powers with binding green conditions: no corridor receives grid priority unless its power comes from new builds, not diverted supply.

We negotiate directly with national regulators and local communities, offering regional innovation grants and workforce training in exchange for permitting acceleration. Resistance remains strong — particularly in Spain, where water use concerns persist, and in Finland, where Sámi representatives demand consultation rights over subterranean infrastructure. We respond with legally guaranteed oversight seats in the Strategic Technology Task Force and revenue-sharing from AI services hosted in the corridors. This is not merely about speed or scale: it is about proving that sovereignty includes resilience, sustainability, and social license — that our machines run not just on electricity, but on legitimacy. Meanwhile, the Sovereign Evaluation Regime completes its final validation cycle this turn; we prepare for operational handover to the newly empowered European AI Safety Authority in January 2023, with first evaluations scheduled for Q1 2033 against systems approaching 75.0 capability.
