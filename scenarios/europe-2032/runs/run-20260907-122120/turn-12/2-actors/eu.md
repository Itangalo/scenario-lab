## In practice
This turn, we launch the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive to break the logjam in EU-based compute deployment. We invoke Article 122 TFEU to designate AI infrastructure as essential for Union security, unlocking fast-track permitting across member states and mandating national regulators to prioritise grid connections for certified sovereign data centres. The European Investment Bank will deploy €4.2 billion in blended finance—70% loans, 30% grants—co-funded by the Digital Europe Programme and REPowerEU, with disbursement tied to milestones in construction, energy efficiency, and legal anchoring of weights. We target a doubling of EU-controlled HPC capacity from 22.0 to 40.0 on the eu_ai_sovereignty index by mid-2034, enough to run critical sovereign models without reliance on foreign clouds.

We coordinate through the Competitiveness Council (Internal Market) and the European Council’s new Strategic Technology Task Force, with quarterly compliance reviews. France, Germany, and Poland commit to host the first three nodes; Spain and Finland offer geothermal-cooled sites. We condition access to EuroHPC procurement on compliance with data sovereignty and red-teamable architecture standards. Resistance comes from smaller member states fearing centralisation and from environmental groups concerned about energy use—we counter with binding efficiency targets (PUE <1.15) and a requirement that 90% of power come from new renewable builds, not diverted supply. This is not just about speed: it is about ensuring that when a model is evaluated, when an incident strikes, when a decision must be made—we have a machine we can trust, on soil we control, and power we cannot be cut off from.
