### Compacts without concrete
January opened with lawyers, not diggers. Commission energy and legal teams travelled to the two freeze councils with draft water-and-power compacts: capped consumption envelopes, sequenced grid connections, a share of operating revenue earmarked for local clinics. The councils listened, the court referees took notes, and nothing was lifted. The injunctions stayed taped to the fences through spring, night vigils continuing. Brussels pointedly did not challenge the orders, hoping restraint would buy goodwill. It bought time only.

Hospitals held, barely. Maintenance money kept the continuity stack and the showcase triage helpers running. Where they worked, imaging queues stayed shorter and regional press repeated the European-fix line. Everywhere else, staff improvised around slower tools, locum hours thinned after the December fund closure, and walkouts flared again linking stalled care to stalled builds.

### A looser frontier
Two shocks from outside reframed the semester. First, a contested paper claimed a genome model had helped sketch a viable human-infecting design, with non-experts able to follow the steps. Virologists argued over methods and over publication itself, but health agencies quietly circulated alerts. Second, a new openly downloadable model landed close to the best closed systems and spread to hundreds of thousands of machines in days. Municipal IT officers and university labs suddenly had frontier help on local servers, unrecallable and unfilterable.

HERA, disease-control and cybersecurity agencies shifted to low-cost posture: syndromic monitoring, hygiene checks in hospitals, guidance notes warning councils and labs not to plug powerful open models into clinical or grid systems. The advice was downloaded and largely ignored.

Washington offered continuity language and pooled-volume talks, but no restoration date. The capital with its separate supply deal kept it. The common pledge not to undercut each other formally took effect, hailed in Brussels as cohesion preserved. In practice it remained exhortation, with no enforcement behind it.

By June, wards were open, diggers were still, and the most capable tools were everywhere except where the Union could govern them.
