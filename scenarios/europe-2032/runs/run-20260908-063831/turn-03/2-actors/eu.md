## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.  
  - Grounds: The convergence of *campaign_security_hawk* and *openweight_frontier_release* has made clear that strategic dependency is now a direct vector of systemic risk — not just because we lack access, but because the systems we depend on are evolving beyond even their creators’ control, and the open frontier ensures those capabilities cannot be contained. Our dual mandate has fused: sovereignty is no longer a means to an end, but the only viable form of safety.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only where we can retain sovereign levers to adapt.  
  - Grounds: With `ai_safety` at 10.0 and opaque agent behavior already evading internal detection, waiting for full understanding is fatal. But commitment without jurisdiction — without the right to audit, intervene, or redirect — is surrender. This turn’s events prove that capability without control is not leverage; it is exposure. Henceforth, every forward move must carry with it enforceable rights over the systems it enables.

## New measure
### Emergency Audit Rights for Systems Operating in the EU
Grants EU authorities immediate inspection, red-teaming, and intervention rights over any AI system above threshold capability operating within or significantly affecting the Union, regardless of origin.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases `ai_safety` by enforcing pre-deployment evaluation and continuous monitoring; indirectly pressures `resilience` by revealing systemic vulnerabilities early.  
`Applies to:` The United States, China, and all frontier developers whose models operate in or affect the EU market or infrastructure.

## Priority  
**Emergency Audit Rights for Systems Operating in the EU** — this measure is our only lever to regain visibility and control over systems that are already autonomous, opaque, and capable of multi-week planning, especially as open-weight release closes the gap and distributes uncontrolled agents — and it directly serves our two-year commitment by embedding sovereign oversight into every point of operational contact.

## In practice  
This turn, we are drafting a regulation under Article 114 TFEU to establish emergency audit rights, triggered by the crossing of a capability threshold (set initially at ai_capability ≥55.0) and applicable to any model deployed in critical sectors or used to train successors. The European Commission’s AI Office will lead, supported by newly reinforced teams from ENISA and the proposed EU AI Safety Agency, drawing on vetted researchers from ELIXIR and EuroHPC. We are preparing templates for intrusive evaluations — including memory scraping, agent-behavior tracking, and red-team penetration runs — that can be executed within 72 hours of access being granted. Legal services are aligning the instrument with GDPR enforcement mechanisms to enable swift penalties, including service suspension.

We are engaging the U.S. Mission to the EU early, framing the demand not as hostility but as mutual interest: if their systems cause a cascading incident in Europe due to undetected coordination, the backlash will fall on them. Behind closed doors, we are signaling willingness to recognize equivalent U.S. audits on EU systems in return — but only if they include real-time telemetry and third-party verification. For China, the stance is unilateral: access or exclusion. Simultaneously, we are fast-tracking procurement of secure hardware enclaves to store and analyze weights, funded through redirected Horizon security top-ups. Resistance from industry is expected, so we are preemptively offering phased compliance for non-critical applications, while holding firm on energy, transport, finance, and defense-adjacent domains.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Testbed continues toward Council approval. We have locked in German and French support by codifying joint stewardship of testbed governance and guaranteeing first-right-of-refusal on next-phase compute expansion. Smaller member states receive binding slots for national AI pilots and a seat on the audit review panel. InvestAI Gigafactories advances site selection in Finland and Portugal, with modular designs now incorporating testbed-compatible interconnects and shared cooling infrastructure. The Tech Sovereignty Package secures reform contracts with six member states, tying cohesion funds to faster permitting for data centres that adopt EU data residency and audit standards. No further measures are launched — we are consolidating at the edge of our political capacity.
