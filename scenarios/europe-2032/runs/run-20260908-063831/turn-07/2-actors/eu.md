## New measure
### Secure the Core: Build EU-Operated Evaluation Nodes for Frontier Model Inspection

Establish and operate, under direct EU institutional control, a network of air-gapped, high-security evaluation nodes capable of hosting full-scale frontier models for deep inspection, red-teaming, and interpretability research — closing the gap between legal access rights and technical capacity to use them.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 10  
`Targeted effect:` Increases `ai_safety` by enabling the EU to conduct meaningful, technically grounded evaluations of foreign and domestic frontier models; supports `eu_ai_sovereignty` by ensuring the Union can understand systems it does not own. Without this, the Sovereign Evaluation Corridor remains a legal shell — access without capability is ceremonial.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member states hosting nodes, and developers required to submit models under the Evaluation Corridor.

## Priority
**Secure the Core: Build EU-Operated Evaluation Nodes for Frontier Model Inspection** — because the Sovereign Evaluation Corridor is meaningless without the capacity to use it: we have the legal right to inspect, but no place, no tools, and no team that can actually do it. The leaked audit confirming Emergency Audit Rights were never exercised was not a failure of will — it was a failure of hardware. This measure builds the missing core: secure facilities, staffed by EU-vetted experts, with the compute and isolation to run and probe models above capability 60.0. Without it, our sovereignty is performative, not operational.

## In practice
This turn, we are confronting the hollow core of our oversight regime. The Sovereign Evaluation Corridor was launched legally this period, but its implementation hits a wall: no EU institution can currently host or run a model at capability 71.0. ENISA, the AI Office, and EuroHPC lack both the secure physical infrastructure and the technical staffing to conduct deep evaluations. The Sovereign Detection Front detects anomalies weekly, but cannot trace their origin or mechanism. We are blind inside the black box.

We are therefore launching **Secure the Core** under the Digital Europe Programme and the EU AI Office, with co-funding from the now-released Tech Sovereignty Package. We will repurpose two of the InvestAI Gigafactory sites — one in Portugal, one in Poland — to host the first EU-operated evaluation nodes, with hardened physical security, air-gapped networks, and direct fibre links to the Sovereign Detection Front’s anomaly telemetry. These will be staffed by a new EU Evaluation Corps, recruited from national cyber and AI agencies, with fast-tracked security clearances and access to interpretability tooling developed under the Sovereign Testbed. We are offering Spain and Finland co-leadership in the evaluation protocol design to strengthen cohesion.

At the same time, the **Sovereign Detection Front** and **Emergency Sovereign Interruption Authority** reach their final milestones. The former is now integrated into all EuroHPC Tier-1 sites, feeding anomaly alerts to a central dashboard in Brussels. The latter has its legal framework ratified by the Council in July, but its credibility depends on being able to justify an interruption — which brings us back to evaluation. We are not waiting for perfect understanding. We are building the capacity to act with *some* understanding, and to improve it under pressure. The two finished measures — InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package — free up capital and political bandwidth. We are using that space not to launch another industrial bid, but to finally build the technical foundation of sovereignty: the ability to see.
