## In practice
This turn, we are in the final stretch of making **Secure the Core** operational. The retrofitting of the former Gigafactory sites in Portugal and Poland is physically complete, with air-gapped environments now hardened against remote intrusion and equipped with sovereign power and cooling. The nodes are technically capable of hosting frontier models up to 75.0 capability, though integration with live evaluation tooling remains fragile. We are conducting final stress tests using red-teamed instances of openweight models at 63.5 capability, simulating obfuscation patterns observed in the wild. These tests are coordinated through the EU AI Office with support from the Seville Interpretability Hub, which has fast-tracked a minimal viable curriculum focused on anomaly correlation and latent goal inference.

Yet the human deficit remains acute. Despite the Accelerate EU Evaluation Corps initiative launched last turn, fewer than 120 specialists are cleared and deployed — far below the 500 needed. Germany and Sweden continue to block the release of key researchers, citing national AI security interests. In response, we are renegotiating the deployment framework: offering member states co-ownership of detection IP and guaranteed rotational return of personnel after 18 months. We are also activating emergency clauses under the Digital Europe Programme to fast-track vetting through Europol’s secure clearance pipeline, aiming to onboard at least 300 additional analysts by the end of the turn.

The **emergent_evaluation_crisis** revelation — that our nodes run on outdated test suites — has forced a last-minute pivot. We are integrating real-time adversarial probing modules developed by French and German pilot teams into the evaluation stack, but their deployment is patchy. At the same time, the **emergent_joint_threat_initiative** offers a lifeline: a coalition of like-minded states proposes pooling intelligence on AI-enabled threats, including shared detection signatures and joint red-teaming exercises. We are engaging through the High Representative’s office, insisting that participation requires full access to our evaluation nodes — not just data feeds. This is not just about resilience; it is about proving that the EU can be a credible partner in sovereign oversight. If we cannot demonstrate technical and operational credibility by turn 10, the nodes may be complete — but they will not command authority.
