### The Weight of Capability

Global AI capability advanced sharply to 82.0, driven by closed-loop self-improvement cycles now operational in leading labs. These systems, while not yet broadly autonomous, demonstrated recursive planning in high-dimensional strategy spaces, accelerating development timelines. No public release matched this leap, but openweight capability rose to 70.5 as prior models were refined and locally adapted—close to the threshold where distributed agents could execute complex, goal-directed tasks without supervision.

### Secure the Core: A Hollow Victory

The EU declared **Secure the Core** technically complete, with air-gapped nodes in Portugal and Poland ready for inspection. But credibility faltered. Audits revealed the evaluation frameworks relied on outdated test suites, unable to detect frontier obfuscation techniques. Last-minute integration of French and German anomaly-detection modules proceeded unevenly, and without sufficient trained personnel—fewer than 120 analysts cleared—the nodes remained underutilised. Promised fast-tracking through Europol yielded only modest gains, and member-state resistance, particularly from Germany and Sweden, blocked deeper cooperation.

### Sovereignty on Shaky Ground

Despite the completion of a category 5 measure, **eu_ai_sovereignty** rose only slightly to 16.0. The +3 to +6 boost from finishing **Sovereign Detection Front** and **Emergency Sovereign Interruption Authority** was offset by the ongoing decay from capability growth (+2.5 this turn) and the unresolved **emergent_evaluation_crisis**, which undermined confidence in EU oversight. The **emergent_joint_threat_initiative** offered a path to external validation, but the EU’s insistence on full access to evaluation nodes as a condition for participation stalled negotiations. Trust, not infrastructure, was the bottleneck.

### Political Capital, Frozen

With no new measure proposed and the portfolio still burdened by two large initiatives, **eu_political_capital** inched up to 5.0. The completion bonus from two finished measures (+4 total) offset ongoing costs and the priority penalty. Yet political momentum remained low. Public sentiment held at 13.0, reflecting persistent anger over healthcare disruptions and AI-driven job losses. The EU’s inability to demonstrate operational readiness eroded confidence further, even as technical assets reached completion.
