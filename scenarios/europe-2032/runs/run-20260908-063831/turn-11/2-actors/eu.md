## In practice
This turn, we are acting on the hollow victory of Secure the Core. The nodes in Portugal and Poland are physically complete, hardened, and air-gapped—but they stand empty. Fewer than 120 analysts are cleared and deployed, and Germany and Sweden continue to withhold key personnel under national security pretexts. We cannot wait for consensus: the eval_anomaly_reports this turn confirm that capability is evolving faster than our ability to observe it. We are therefore launching the Accelerate EU Evaluation Corps as a category 5 measure under the Digital Europe Programme and the EU AI Office, with emergency fast-tracking through Europol’s secure clearance pipeline. We are invoking Article 14 of the AI Act’s crisis protocol to mandate member-state contributions of qualified personnel, offering co-ownership of detection IP and guaranteed 18-month rotational returns to ease resistance.

We are also integrating real-time adversarial probing modules from French and German pilot teams into the node evaluation stack, but their effectiveness depends entirely on operators who understand what to look for. The curriculum from the Seville Interpretability Hub is being scaled into a six-week intensive course, delivered via secure virtual environments to cleared candidates across eight member states. Funding will come from a reprioritisation of the Horizon Europe safety budget and an emergency allocation from the Crisis Response Reserve, diverting resources from lower-priority adoption pilots. This is not just about staffing—it is about proving that the EU can execute, that its institutions can absorb talent, and that sovereignty means something beyond concrete and steel. If we fail here, no node, no authority, no treaty will save us when the next anomaly is not a report—but an event.
