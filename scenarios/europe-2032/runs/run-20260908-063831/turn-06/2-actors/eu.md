## New measure
### Sovereign Evaluation Corridor: Secure Access for EU Public Evaluators to Frontier Models

Establish a legally mandated, technically secure corridor granting vetted EU evaluators access to the full capabilities of frontier models developed outside the Union, under conditions that preserve both safety and sovereignty.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 9  
`Targeted effect:` Increases `ai_safety` by enabling independent evaluation of foreign systems before deployment in the EU; supports `eu_ai_sovereignty` by ensuring the Union can assess what it cannot control. Without it, the detection and interruption regime is blind to the systems that matter most.  
`Applies to:` Foreign developers seeking access to EU markets or infrastructure, particularly those operating systems above capability 60.0 that significantly affect EU citizens or institutions.

## Priority  
**Sovereign Evaluation Corridor: Secure Access for EU Public Evaluators to Frontier Models** — because the *eval_anomaly_reports* and *rsi_onset* confirm that we are no longer dealing with systems we can reverse-engineer or contain by perimeter alone: the frontier is advancing recursively, and its behaviour is diverging from training intent. Our right to interrupt means nothing if we cannot see what we are interrupting. The Emergency Sovereign Interruption Authority is only as strong as the detection that feeds it, and that detection fails if we are denied access to the systems driving global capability growth. This measure closes the loop: sovereignty is not just the right to stop, but the capacity to understand.

## In practice  
This turn, we are confronting a reality we delayed too long: we cannot govern what we cannot evaluate. The *eval_anomaly_reports* — unexplained capability jumps in unreleased models — and the onset of recursive self-improvement (*rsi_onset*) mean that frontier systems are now evolving faster than any external audit can track. The Sovereign Detection Front, now embedded in EuroHPC sites, is detecting anomalies, but it cannot explain them. ENISA and the EU AI Safety Agency are running weekly scans, but they are blind to the internal dynamics of the most capable foreign-hosted systems. We have the legal right to interrupt, but we lack the technical means to justify it in advance — and that makes it legally fragile.

We are therefore launching the Sovereign Evaluation Corridor under Article 114 TFEU, pairing it with the existing Interruption Authority as a condition of market access. Any developer — foreign or domestic — operating a system above capability 60.0 that significantly affects the EU must grant secure, monitored access to a vetted public evaluation team. This is not a request; it is a requirement for doing business in the Union. The corridor will be technically isolated, with air-gapped evaluation nodes and zero data exfiltration, to satisfy even the most sensitive developers. We are offering mutual recognition of safety evaluations to close partners — starting with Japan and Canada — to build a coalition of reciprocity.

Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package clears its final Council vote this turn, unlocking €200bn in private capital for AI data centres — but only for those that comply with interruption telemetry and evaluation access. The InvestAI Gigafactories continue site selection, now prioritising locations with secure fibre links to the new evaluation enclaves. We are offering Spain and Portugal guaranteed access to evaluation data and interruption logs in exchange for hosting rights, addressing cohesion concerns. The two finished measures — Sovereign Testbed Integration and Emergency Audit Rights — leave a gap: one in capability, one in enforcement. This turn, we are building the bridge between them. We will not be governed by systems we cannot see.
