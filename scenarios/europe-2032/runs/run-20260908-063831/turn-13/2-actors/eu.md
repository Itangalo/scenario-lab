The commitment must now include the operational capacity to act under exclusion, or it fails its own purpose.

## Priority
**Emergency Sovereign Access Protocol: Immediate Activation of Fallback Systems and Redeployment of Public Compute for Crisis Response** — because the denial of frontier access this turn has turned contingency into necessity, and we must demonstrate that the Union can function when the systems it depends on are cut off, or lose all credibility in building sovereign capacity.

## In practice
This turn, we are acting on the fact that the frontier has shut us out—not through negotiation, not through warning, but by silent revocation. Hospitals in three member states lost diagnostic support overnight. Ministries recalibrating energy contracts faced sudden model unavailability. The market assumed access; we assumed sovereignty. We were wrong. In response, we are activating the Emergency Sovereign Access Protocol under Article 12 of the Digital Crisis Response Framework, repurposing 4 exaflops of existing public research compute—originally allocated to climate and materials modelling—into immediate fallback capacity for essential AI-dependent services. This is not new hardware; it is re-leveraging what we already fund, under emergency powers, to run open-weight models at capability 74.75 in place of the denied closed systems. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) is directing the rerouting, with ENISA certifying minimal integrity standards for model substitution.

We are pairing this with a binding directive to member states: any public service relying on foreign AI must, within six months, register a sovereign fallback path using either EuroHPC resources or approved national clusters. No new procurement may proceed without it. Finland, already aligned through its participation in Secure Sovereign Compute, is offering immediate hosting; France and Poland are being asked to release idle academic capacity under temporary Union control. This is not the full solution—but it is the first time the Union has treated compute as critical infrastructure in real crisis, not just in planning. It forces the question: if we cannot run our own systems when the world cuts us off, what does sovereignty mean? We are answering now, before the next cutoff comes.
