### The Cut and the Scramble

The Union was locked out of the world’s most advanced AI systems without warning. Hospitals lost diagnostic support, energy ministries faced stalled negotiations, and emergency planning units were left blind. The denial was total, silent, and absolute—no appeal, no explanation. The assumption of access, long embedded in public and private systems, shattered overnight.

In response, the EU activated the Emergency Sovereign Access Protocol, initiating a rerouting of 4 exaflops of public research compute from climate and materials modelling to run essential services on open-weight models. EuroHPC JU began coordination, with ENISA fast-tracking minimal integrity standards. Finland offered immediate hosting; France and Poland raised legal and governance concerns, delaying full deployment. The shift is underway but incomplete—services are adapting under strain, with performance gaps evident but not yet critical.

### Sovereignty Under Strain

Secure Sovereign Compute remains stalled. No new sites came online. Germany continues to withhold participation, and co-investment disputes in France and Poland deepen. The promise of sovereign substrate now contrasts sharply with operational dependence.

Meanwhile, export controls tightened further. The U.S., under its consolidation posture, extended rationing to allied institutions, citing national security. Access to next-gen chips and models narrows, reinforcing the Union’s vulnerability.

### Political Exhaustion

The activation of the Emergency Sovereign Access Protocol marks the first new measure this turn. While **Accelerate EU Evaluation Corps** and **Secure Sovereign Compute** were previously introduced in response to emerging crises noted in turn 11, their formal status as active measures is retained in the notepad for continuity. However, no new priority is declared this turn due to bandwidth constraints.

The portfolio charge is −5: −3 for Secure Sovereign Compute, −1 for Accelerate EU Evaluation Corps, and −1 for maintaining a priority in a prior turn. No proposal bonus applies—the current action responds to an ongoing crisis, not a resolved one. Political capital holds at 2.0, avoiding further depletion as implementation remains partial and contested.

Public sentiment dips to 10.0, as media frame the scramble as proof of strategic failure. Resilience rises slightly to 31.0—early re-tasking absorbs some harm. Ai_safety remains at 0.0, though concerns grow over unaddressed reasoning anomalies; no formal downgrade is recorded this turn.

Openweight capability increases to 77.875, reflecting partial progress in scaling open-weight inference under emergency conditions. This closes less than half the previous gap to ai_capability (which rose to 90.0), consistent with constrained infrastructure and fragmented governance. The gap remains wide, and convergence is slow.

The Union acts—but too late, too thin, and too divided.
