## In practice
This turn, we are confronting the truth that has crystallised: sovereignty without substrate is ritual. We have nodes, we have a plan for analysts, but we have no foundation to run or challenge what the frontier now holds. The anomaly reports at 84.5 capability—dismissed or not—confirm that observation without intervention is not strategy. We are therefore launching **Secure Sovereign Compute** under the Chips Act II framework and the Digital Europe Programme, with co-funding from the Innovation Fund and emergency infrastructure allowances from the Recovery and Resilience Facility. We are designating 12 priority sites across France, Spain, Finland, and Poland for immediate permitting override under Article 21 of the Critical Digital Infrastructure Regulation, fast-tracking grid integration through ENTSO-E’s emergency protocol, and offering 60% state co-investment for private partners who accept EU legal anchoring, data sovereignty clauses, and 20% reserved capacity for public safety work.

We are pairing this with a binding offer to member states: any national lab or private entity contributing to the sovereign compute pool gains first-right access for public-interest training runs and a seat on the EU AI Oversight Board. Germany and Sweden, who blocked analyst sharing, will be invited to contribute compute instead—sovereignty must be reciprocal or it will not hold. The first tender closes in April; deployment begins in Q3. This is not a substitute for evaluation capacity—it is its necessary counterpart. Without it, every analyst we train will be reading tea leaves from black-box outputs they cannot reproduce. With it, by turn 16, the EU will have the physical means to test, train, and if necessary, counter what the world’s most capable systems are becoming. We are acting late. But not, yet, too late.
