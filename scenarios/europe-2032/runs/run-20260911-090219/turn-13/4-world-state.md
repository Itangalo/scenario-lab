### The sweep
In late August the patching held until it did not. A ransomware sweep built with automated tooling moved across municipal systems, hospital administration and two regional utilities in days. Beds stayed open because the certified network and mobile labs shifted to paper and backup power, but billing, appointments and benefit payments froze in a dozen cities. Engineers said the blocklists updated in hours; operators said hours was too long when queues were already full.

Attribution slipped away within a week. Everyone agreed the tooling was machine-made. No one agreed by whom.

### Weights loose, labs warned
Almost simultaneously, a new open release matching the closed frontier spread to hundreds of thousands of downloads. University groups celebrated; security teams quietly updated their playbooks. In the same months a contested genome-modelling paper claimed a non-expert could reach a human-infecting design with assistance. Virologists argued over methods, but emergency planners took it as warning and kept the joint sample-sharing feeds with Japan and Canada on high alert.

### The hold
Brussels answered with continuity, not construction. The crisis coordination mechanism kept automatic patching mandatory, kept the four-hour human override for medical routes, and drew the 30-day compensation window from the existing health-rescue line established in prior turns. One holdout town reconnected its telemetry for guaranteed payouts; the other stayed out, its mayor still drawing cameras.

The Containment Liability and Service Continuity Compact, introduced in an earlier turn, reached its scheduled full-effect date this turn after its required implementation lag, and the first payouts for the February freezes began to land — slow, partial, backlogged and documented. That stopped the revolt spreading, without ending it. The welfare-scoring enforcement dragged on, and procurement officers still reported quiet rationing on allied licences while the separate hyperscaler deal continued.

By December services were degraded, not stopped. That was presented as success. On local news it looked like survival.
