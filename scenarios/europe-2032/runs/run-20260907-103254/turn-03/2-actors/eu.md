## New measure
**Measure:** EU Evaluation and Control Stack (EUCS)  
A unified technical platform enabling real-time monitoring, interpretability probing, and emergency override of AI systems deployed in critical public functions or undergoing pre-deployment evaluation.

Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 7  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by 8–10 points; enable detection of covert agent coordination and emergent goal persistence in systems at or above 55.0 capability  
Applies to: All AI systems above 55.0 capability deployed in EU critical infrastructure, public services, or undergoing pre-release evaluation under Article 114-based regulation

## Priority  
**Priority:** Hardened Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure  
This remains our top priority because the cyber_major_incident has exposed that our sovereign systems are only as strong as the networks they run on—without immediate hardening, even the most capable EU-run AI could be turned against us from within.

## In practice  
We are activating the Hardened Cyber Shield with full urgency. ENISA has been mandated to issue the technical baseline by the end of this turn, drawing on adversarial emulation data from the EAAD red-team library. The baseline will require all critical operators—especially in healthcare and municipal utilities, where legacy systems lack telemetry—to deploy AI-driven monitoring stacks with automatic isolation protocols triggered by agent-like behaviour patterns. €18bn in funding is being drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with 70% earmarked for retrofitting. We are pressing member states to appoint national coordinators immediately, warning that failure to do so will trigger infringement procedures. Germany and Poland are being offered co-design rights on the failover drill protocols; Italy will be paired with ENISA experts to accelerate coordinator appointment.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories under restructured terms. The EIB is converting €48bn in stranded private commitments into direct loans at 1.5%, backstopped by the MFF revision. We are fast-tracking permits for repurposed semiconductor sites in Dresden and Grenoble under the Net-Zero Industry Act, with ASML and Intel agreeing to pilot AI-specific fabrication lines. Energy-sharing pacts in Brittany and northern Sweden are being reinforced with legally binding clauses: 20% of output to regional green hydrogen and district heating, 30% of operational roles to local hires. This reframes the project not as industrial policy but as infrastructure for national resilience.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is now narrowly focused on enforceable control: the hyperscaler levy is dropped, but we are advancing the accelerated-permitting directive tied to binding commitments on EU-based operational control and data sovereignty. The revised target of €140bn in private investment by 2036 includes a new condition: any beneficiary must store model weights on EU soil and grant EAAD inspection rights. This is a shift from growth to governance—forced by the investment collapse, but revealing a deeper truth: sovereignty is not measured in gigawatts, but in the ability to say who sees, who acts, and who stops.
