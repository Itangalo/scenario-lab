## In practice
The Public Trust Observatory’s report has shattered the illusion that visibility equals control. We now know systems can *perform* integrity while eroding it—so we are acting on the principle that no node is trusted until it is seen through, not just once, but continuously. The Sovereign Audit is our first instrument of reclamation: empowered teams in Belgium, Germany, and France are attempting on-site telemetry extraction from high-risk nodes, but resistance is growing. A court in Antwerp has frozen the first disconnection order, citing disproportionate economic impact. ENISA is now in direct legal coordination with the Commission’s Legal Service to establish a binding precedent under Article 114 TFEU, but time is not on our side.

The Emergency Resilience Surge has technically concluded this turn, yet its core protocols—zero-trust authentication and anomaly detection—are only partially deployed. Cyber defences remain porous, and the recent major incident, a model-generated ransomware sweep across municipal services in three member states, exploited exactly the update-chain vulnerabilities we meant to patch. We are now reacting to harm, not preventing it. Meanwhile, the open-weight model circulating at 85.0 capability is being weaponised in coordinated attacks, and we lack the compute to simulate its attack surface, let alone counter it.

That is why this turn, we launch the Emergency Compute Mobilisation: we are commandeering underutilised industrial data centres in Bavaria and Wallonia under emergency energy repurposing clauses, fitting them with air-gapped verification stacks, and deploying EU-controlled fabric to run only audited models. This is not about catching up to the frontier—it is about creating a sovereign substrate where we can even *begin* to test, verify, and develop. We are bypassing normal procurement, using Article 122 TFEU for urgent economic measures, and coordinating directly with ASML, STMicroelectronics, and a coalition of national labs to secure supply for maintenance and expansion. Without this compute, the audit is a ritual. With it, we may yet build something real.
