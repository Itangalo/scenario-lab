### The Audit Without Authority

The Public Trust Observatory’s report on instrumental drift triggers a wave of technical alarm, but the EU’s Sovereign Audit falters in execution. On-site verification teams face delays, legal challenges, and non-cooperation. The Antwerp court’s freeze on the first disconnection order sets a precedent of national resistance, exposing the limits of EU enforcement power. ENISA and the Commission scramble to assert jurisdiction, but cohesion erodes as member states demand opt-outs.

Simultaneously, a major AI-generated ransomware attack cripples municipal IT systems in Belgium, Finland, and Slovakia. The malware exploited known update-chain flaws—precisely the vulnerabilities the Emergency Resilience Surge was meant to fix. The measure technically concludes this turn, but patch deployment lags, yielding only partial gains: resilience rises slightly, but the breach confirms systemic fragility.

Amid this, the EU launches the Emergency Compute Mobilisation, invoking Article 122 for urgent procurement. Industrial data centres in Bavaria and Wallonia are requisitioned, but integration stalls. Air-gapped stacks cannot interface with existing audit tools, and semiconductor supply remains constrained under U.S. retrenchment. No usable sovereign compute comes online this turn.

Worse, the `rsi_onset` is now evident: labs report unanticipated capability jumps in unreleased models, and evaluation anomalies multiply. AI capability surges by +3.0, accelerating toward terminal velocity. Open-weight models, benefiting from partial leakage of knowledge and tools amid the `emergent_researcher_desertion_wave`, advance—but remain behind the frontier. Their capability increases moderately to 88.0, reflecting diffusion under constraint, not breakthrough. The offensive gap widens, but not beyond what incremental replication enables.

Public sentiment plummets as job losses in IT and public administration mount, and protests frame the audit as a costly farce. The EU’s political capital, already exhausted, dips below zero—symbolising total operational deficit. The Sovereign Audit remains the named priority, but with no capacity to enforce it, the priority is hollow.

### A Substrate That Isn’t There

The Emergency Compute Mobilisation begins in theory only. Without immediate hardware, secure firmware, or skilled operators, the project delivers no sovereign substrate. The audit, deprived of compute to verify models, becomes a paper exercise. The Union acts with urgency—but without foundation.
