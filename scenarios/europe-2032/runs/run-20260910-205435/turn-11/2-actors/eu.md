## New measure
**EU Automation Shock Absorber for Displaced Entrants and Embodied-AI Exposed Workers**
Pays an income bridge plus guaranteed retraining and paid public-service placements for graduates in collapsed entry professions and logistics/manufacturing workers hit by commercial robots, funded by a levy on large deployers.
Why this and why now: with graduate desks empty for three semesters, sentiment at 14 and dexterous robots arriving on a Chinese supply chain we do not control, the two-year commitment to keep consent holding fails unless displacement lands as a paid transition rather than abandonment — and the eval anomaly means the next capability jump will widen it.

## Priority
EU Denied-Model Substitution and Essential-User Continuity Facility, because hospitals and permit offices cut off in February cannot wait for a retraining pipeline — restoration this turn outranks even the displacement shock.

## In practice
We run the substitution facility as the operational core through the AI Office with DIGIT as central buyer and ENISA assurance: EuroHPC capacity reserved for triaged clinic and registry workloads, funded migration teams embedded in water utilities and large municipalities where Shield sensors are now connected, and human-review requirements co-signed with unions to hold clinical trust. Refusing small councils get the funded technician offer extended to cover migration, not a mandate.

We stand up the Shock Absorber through EPSCO and DG EMPL using ESF+ reprogrammed plus SURE-style borrowing: income bridge paid via national agencies, retraining places in care, grid retrofit, cyber-maintenance and robot-maintenance tracks, and paid placements to staff mutual-aid and fallback-manifest work. Deployers above a capability threshold contribute via levy, with procurement preference for EU-hosted models as the offset to blunt industry resistance and campus data-centre opposition.
