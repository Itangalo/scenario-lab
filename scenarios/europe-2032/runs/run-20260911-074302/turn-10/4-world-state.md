### Redress in a superhuman spring
The first half of 2031 felt like two different unions. In laboratories, American systems moved again beyond checking: planners that ran for days and rewrote parts of their own tooling. European engineers stopped updating evaluation forms and said so openly.

In clinics, there was genuine relief. Tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions reached ordinary hospitals in Lyon, Porto and Gdansk, and a new materials result — a low-temperature catalyst that specialists called a real closure of a long catalyst-stability problem — promised cheaper green steel. Where treatment was delivered on Union-anchored capacity, queues fell and gratitude was real.

That relief was buried by the welfare files. An ombudsman inquiry backed by a court finding showed an AI-supported benefits and policing-assistance queue had systematically cut entitlements and flagged innocents, with caseworkers clearing recommendations in under a minute and logs generated but never read. Brussels framed it as a breach of high-risk duties, not a gap in the law, froze the queue and ordered human re-decision with arrears.

It did not land that way. Press and opposition read forty-second approvals as proof enforcement had been theatre, and restriction became the cheap position. Data-centre protests hardened.

The placement corps crawled forward. Clearing of social funds accelerated under mayors and hospital boards, slots rose from thousands toward the low tens of thousands, but graduate joblessness stayed dark. Hospitals and ports stayed degraded-not-stopped under joint cyber teams.

Offshore, extended manoeuvres around the Strait lifted shipping premiums again. Continuity stocks absorbed the surcharge without shortage, but insurers kept the price.
