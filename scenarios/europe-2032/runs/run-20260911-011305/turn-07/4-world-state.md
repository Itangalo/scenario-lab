### Holding the line
July to December 2029 was a half-year of maintenance, not momentum. Brussels proposed nothing new. With trust and fiscal room exhausted, the Commission kept the joint repair teams on their existing footing, paid from reprogrammed funds, and published daily lists of towns restored and towns still waiting.

Where teams arrived, clinics rebooked appointments and benefit payments resumed. Where they did not — a string of smaller municipalities in the east and south — mayors accused the centre of triage by visibility. The cell held together on paper, but rosters thinned as national experts were recalled for domestic incidents.

The five reserved building sites stayed fenced and permitted but unbuilt. Custody passed quietly to permit offices and lenders; no concrete was poured. Power-price anger did not fade. A few councils debated refusing grid connections outright, and street protests flared briefly around clinic closures, though they never coalesced into a national movement.

The solo capacity deal signed the previous winter hardened into fact. The defecting capital received its foreign compute tranche and stopped attending joint tier-access meetings. Paris and The Hague kept the common licensing table alive with the Netherlands, Japan and Korea, but Washington acknowledged European requests and deferred them again under its tiered access regime.

Autumn brought hard employment data. Entry-level hiring in routine coding, standard analysis, first-draft documentation and tier-one support did not recover. Unions called it proof of replacement; employers called it automation of checkable work and nothing more. The wider forecast wave did not arrive. National job centres absorbed the displaced into existing retraining schemes, with no new European fund behind them.

By December two files formally closed: the gigafactory programme and the middle-power supply-chain compact. Both were recorded as delivered. On the ground, delivery meant permits, pledges and a preserved negotiating table — leverage for a future build, not capacity today.
