## Priority
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
This remains the cornerstone of our two-year commitment: sovereign compute is the foundation without which no resilience, evaluation, or industrial policy can be sustained — and delays in site approvals now threaten to lock in a decade of dependency.

## In practice
We are pressing forward on InvestAI Gigafactories through the European Investment Bank and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with binding co-financing agreements now secured from Germany, France, and Sweden. Legal authority under Article 122 TFEU and the Net-Zero Industry Act is being used to fast-track permitting, and we have secured 12GW of reserved grid capacity from ENTSO-E across five designated zones — though final approvals in Brittany and northern Sweden remain stalled. To break the deadlock, we are offering binding reinvestment packages: 30% of operational profits from each site will be channelled into regional green hydrogen hubs, and 40% of technical jobs will be reserved for local residents, with training pipelines established through the European Social Fund+. The Commission is also preparing a delegated act to override permitting bottlenecks under Article 22 of the NZIA if member states do not act by turn 3.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is advancing through the Digital Markets and Digital Services Council, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable commitments on energy efficiency, EU-based operational control, and talent retention. The legal service is finalising a directive under Article 114 TFEU to harmonise permitting timelines, aiming to cut approval from 48 to 12 months. We are also structuring the private capital tranches: 40% will be tied to ASML and EUV supply chain resilience via the Chips Act framework, 30% to Blue Card+ talent incentives, and 30% to long-term PPAs with offshore wind and nuclear providers. Investor hesitation remains, but clarity on permitting and grid access is beginning to shift sentiment.

The new Critical Infrastructure AI Resilience Shield (CIARS) is being launched in direct response to the recent intrusion. Under the NIS2 Directive, we are mandating that all essential operators deploy AI-assisted detection systems by turn 5, with funding split 70/30 between the EU and member states. The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre will coordinate procurement of vetted AI tools, and ENISA will run biannual red-team exercises simulating agent-driven intrusions. We are fast-tracking development of open-source detection models through a new CNECT call, and seconding experts from national CERTs and intelligence agencies to ensure operational readiness. This is not optional: the breach was a warning shot, and we will treat systemic fragility as a strategic threat.
