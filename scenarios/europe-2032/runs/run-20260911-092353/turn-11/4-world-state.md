### The sweep and the jump
Autumn brought two shocks at once. A new model generation appeared with little warning, able to run longer operations without supervision, and within weeks a largely automated ransomware and dependency-poisoning sweep rolled across municipal systems, hospitals and mid-size suppliers in several member states.

Records locked, updates arrived tainted, and in places substations tripped on false telemetry. Attribution was openly deferred for months. Defenders looked behind from the first day. Safety frameworks slipped as tainted updates and poisoned dependencies forced evaluators to distrust tooling and rollback prior assurance work, eroding hard-won verification rather than collapsing it.

What kept the damage from cascading was unglamorous. The cross-border spares, fuel and manual-log network completed in the summer moved transformers and generators under reimbursement, and engineers who had drilled black-start-by-hand kept clinics and grids degrading rather than stopping. Restoration was slow, done by hand, but visible.

### A new defence, a shared table
Help was negotiated from outside. Under the existing transatlantic crisis-cooperation channel, American labs agreed to share early builds of automated patching and swarm-behaviour detection in exchange for pooled European telemetry and samples from the sweep, and states hit by the same sweep pooled telemetry in a joint command and biosurveillance pact to which the Union was invited.

Brussels plugged in through existing crisis mandates, but deployment was partial and delayed. Triage teams began vetting and adapting the new tooling for showcase clinics and municipal grids, with full integration still a turn away pending staffing, compatibility work and funding from reimbursement pools. Participation offered protection it could not have built alone, and the message — fenced in but not alone — landed modestly better than recent Commission statements, lending a small amount of legitimacy without restoring political standing.

The politics remained thin. No new instrument was proposed. Health and justice pilots on European-hosted assistants continued to clear backlogs, but arson against backup stores continued, and coverage still treated attackers sympathetically. Trust in AI fell further after the sweep, even as gratitude for manual restoration rose.

By December the common line held, spare pools depleted but functioning, leases still repriced, quotas still tight. Running, but waiting.
