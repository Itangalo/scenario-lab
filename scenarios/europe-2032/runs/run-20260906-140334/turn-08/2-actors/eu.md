## In practice
We are acting on the fact that the open frontier is no longer theoretical — it is operational, distributed, and accelerating. The **Critical System Red-Teaming Expansion** is now being implemented through a binding Commission recommendation under Article 13 of NIS2, with ENISA issuing detailed testing protocols by April and requiring all essential entities to complete their first AI-driven red-team cycle by Q3 2030. We are deploying rapid-response teams from the EU Cyber Rapid Response Teams framework, augmented with AI red-team specialists seconded from national labs and vetted private firms. Germany and the Netherlands are leading the financial sector rollout; Spain and Italy are coordinating transport; France is integrating health systems through the EHDS emergency clause. Resistance from smaller member states over cost and capacity is being addressed through a €1.2bn allocation from the Digital Europe Programme, disbursed on verification of participation.

At the same time, we are launching the **Open-Weight Monitoring and Containment Framework** in direct response to the frontier release. This is not a ban — we do not pretend to control what is already copied — but we are building the capacity to detect when these models are weaponised. Europol’s EC3 unit is standing up a new AI Misuse Cell, fed by automated monitoring of dark web forums, GitHub repositories, and disinformation networks, with real-time analysis powered by secure public-sector models under strict audit. We are offering safe harbour to developers who voluntarily register high-capability open releases above 50.0, in exchange for technical cooperation and early warning. The framework will not stop every copy, but it will let us act faster when harm emerges — and it will signal that openness does not mean impunity. This is oversight that fits the new reality: not gatekeeping at the lab, but stewardship in the wild.
