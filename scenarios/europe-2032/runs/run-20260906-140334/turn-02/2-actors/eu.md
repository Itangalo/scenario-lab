## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that capability without control is surrender. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep exploiting a poisoned software dependency — has already disrupted hospital scheduling in three member states and triggered emergency protocols in two national grid operators. Attribution remains unclear, but the tooling bears the signature of an AI-driven development pipeline: polymorphic payloads, adaptive evasion, and coordinated timing across jurisdictions. This is not a drill. Our resilience at 37.0 is insufficient, and we are not waiting for the next wave.

This turn, the Commission activates the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint under Article 12a of the NIS2 Directive, leveraging emergency powers to mandate rapid deployment of AI-resistant controls: mandatory binary transparency for all software updates touching critical systems, air-gapped fallback configurations, and real-time anomaly detection using non-AI rule-based monitors trained on pre-2025 patterns. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will deploy rapid-response teams to each member state within 72 hours of request, funded from the Digital Europe Programme’s crisis reserve. We are also fast-tracking procurement for sovereign, non-transformer-based intrusion detection systems through the European Defence Fund’s dual-use innovation window.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive, now in its second turn of negotiation. The Council’s Competitiveness formation remains divided, but we have secured provisional support from five member states by linking compliance to preferential access to the revived InvestAI capital pool — now restructured as loan guarantees rather than grants, reducing fiscal pressure.
