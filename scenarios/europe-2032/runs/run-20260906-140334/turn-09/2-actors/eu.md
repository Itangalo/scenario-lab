## In practice
We are concluding the **Critical System Red-Teaming Expansion** with binding implementation across all NIS2 sectors. ENISA has issued the final red-teaming protocols, and the first wave of AI-driven adversarial tests is now live in financial, energy, and health systems in France, Germany, and the Netherlands. The EU Cyber Rapid Response Teams have embedded AI red-team specialists into national CERTs, and a central reporting hub under ENISA is aggregating findings. The €1.2bn Digital Europe allocation has unlocked participation from smaller member states, with conditionality tied to verified staffing and exercise completion. By the end of this turn, every essential entity in the EU will have either completed its first AI red-team cycle or be under formal non-compliance procedure.

At the same time, we are operationalising the **Open-Weight Monitoring and Containment Framework** in response to the 53.0 open model release. Europol’s new AI Misuse Cell is now active, deploying secure, audited models to scan dark web forums, GitHub repositories, and disinformation networks for signs of weaponisation. We are not banning open models — that would be futile — but we are building the capacity to detect when they are used to attack. Safe-harbour agreements are being finalised with key open-source developers, offering legal protection and technical collaboration in exchange for early warning and cooperation during incidents. The framework is already under strain from staffing gaps and inter-agency friction, but we are fast-tracking recruitment through the Digital Europe Talent Pool and establishing a joint coordination cell between ENISA, Europol, and the newly empowered AI Resilience Board.

Simultaneously, we are launching the **EU Compute Sovereignty Acceleration Programme** to break our strategic dependence on foreign compute. This is not just about building more data centres — it is about ensuring they are legally anchored, energy-secure, and under enforceable EU jurisdiction. We are invoking the revised Critical Infrastructure Directive to fast-track permitting for EuroHPC sites, pairing new clusters with dedicated renewable microgrids under the Net-Zero Industry Act. We are renegotiating terms with U.S. hyperscalers: access to EU markets and grid priority will now be contingent on compute sovereignty clauses — verifiable legal control, audit rights, and non-extraterritoriality guarantees. The programme will cost 3 political capital per turn, but it is the only path to closing the gap between our defensive ambitions and our material independence. This is not a choice between safety and sovereignty — it is the recognition that without sovereignty, safety is a request, not a policy.
