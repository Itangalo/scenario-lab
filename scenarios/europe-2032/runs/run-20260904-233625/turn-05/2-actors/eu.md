**Sovereign Audit Access Framework Implementation**
Establish dedicated technical units within the European AI Office and national digital agencies to conduct real-time model inspection, red-teaming, and execution trace analysis using access rights granted under the Sovereign Model Access Pact.

## In practice
This turn, we are closing out two foundational efforts: the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive and the Resilience Surge Initiative. EDIC is now law, with the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium running its first time auctions, securing 30% of frontier compute for public use. This is not theoretical — France’s Jean Zay cluster, Poland’s PL-Grid, and Finland’s CSC have already integrated the reservation mechanism, and the first state-critical research workloads are being scheduled under it. The Resilience Surge has delivered detection upgrades to 60% of critical operators, but we are seeing resistance in municipal water systems and mid-tier energy providers where legacy IT and thin vendor support slow adoption. We are tasking DG ENER and DG HOME with launching a fast-response technical assistance pool to close the gap by turn 6, drawing on Eurojust and ENISA expertise.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Model Access Pact remains in exploratory talks, but the election consolidation event has hardened our position: the U.S. is now clearly treating AI access as a strategic lever, and allies are clients. This means we cannot rely on voluntary cooperation or market access threats alone. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Audit Access Framework Implementation — not as a new legal instrument, but as the operational backbone of the Pact. We are standing up dedicated technical units in the European AI Office and national agencies like Germany’s BSI and France’s ANSSI, equipped with secure enclaves and red-teaming tools to conduct real-time model inspection. These units will begin with pilot access to two high-risk systems: a border control agent in use by Frontex and a predictive maintenance AI deployed across German rail infrastructure. We are funding this through a reallocation from the Tech Sovereignty Package, accepting slower private-sector rollout to prioritize public verification capacity. Without this, we may have the right to access — but no ability to act on it.
