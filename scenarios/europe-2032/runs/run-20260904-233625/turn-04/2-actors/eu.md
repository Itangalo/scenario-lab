## New measure  
**Sovereign Model Access Pact**  
Establish a binding legal mechanism for EU public safety agencies to access full model weights, training data logs, and agent execution traces of high-risk AI systems before deployment and during operation, under strict data protection and security protocols.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to +18 ai_safety by turn 6; enables independent verification of model behaviour, supports forensic investigation after incidents, and underpins credible enforcement of the Sovereign Audit Access Framework; begins closing the gap between declared safety and demonstrable control  
`Applies to:` High-risk AI systems under the AI Act, particularly those in public services, critical infrastructure, and national security functions, with implementation led by the European AI Office and national digital authorities

## Priority  
**Sovereign Model Access Pact** — because the open release of near-frontier models at 56.5 capability into distributed hands makes independent verification no longer optional, and without direct access to weights and logs, the EU cannot validate safety claims or respond to harm, rendering both sovereignty and public trust performative.

## In practice  
This turn, we close the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive and the Resilience Surge Initiative — two pillars of our immediate response to the acceleration and the welfare scandal. EDIC is now legally established, with its first auction framework ratified and pilot integrations underway with EuroHPC and national champions in France, Poland, and Finland. The 30% floor on compute access is no longer a proposal but a legal reality, though its value will depend on whether we can staff and secure it. The Resilience Surge has delivered audit templates and detection upgrades to 60% of designated critical operators, but adoption remains patchy, especially in municipal water systems and mid-tier energy providers where vendor support is thin. We are treating both as foundational: the compute floor gives us breathing room, the resilience upgrades buy time — but neither addresses the core problem of opacity.

That problem has become existential. The open release of a 56.5-capable model means the most dangerous systems are no longer confined to labs we can pressure or regulate — they are in the wild, running on private hardware, and will soon be fine-tuned into tools of coercion, fraud, and sabotage. The Sovereign Audit Access Framework we proposed last turn is necessary but insufficient: audits without access to underlying models are theatre. So we are now pushing the Sovereign Model Access Pact — a legal right for the European AI Office and designated national agencies to inspect, test, and retain copies of model weights and execution logs for any high-risk system deployed in the EU. This is not about surveillance — it is about accountability. We are framing it as a condition of market access under Article 16a of the AI Act, with exemptions for IP-protected layers only where safety can be independently verified through alternative means. Resistance is expected from U.S. hyperscalers and some EU agencies that prefer vendor lock-in, but we are offering a trade: full access in exchange for fast-tracked certification and liability protection for compliant actors. The pact will be negotiated through the Digital Ministers’ Council in June, with pilot access agreements already drafted with Germany’s BSI and France’s ANSSI. Without this, we are governing blind.
