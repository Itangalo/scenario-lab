AI capability rises to 55.0 in early 2027 as automated verification enables unsupervised refinement of AI agents in legal and engineering domains, accelerating high-stakes system development. A major scandal erupts when an AI-assisted welfare system in a large EU member state is found to have systematically denied benefits to vulnerable populations without appeal, triggering public outrage and eroding trust. Public sentiment drops to 37.0, and political capital declines sharply due to stalled initiatives and penalties from the domestic scandal.

In response, the EU advances the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive through trilogue, framing compute access as a market condition under Article 114 TFEU. A compromise establishes a European Digital Infrastructure Consortium to manage 30% compute reservations via time auctions, offering fast-track Gigafactory access and liability shields. Resistance from Germany, Ireland, and industry groups persists over market distortion and US retaliation risks. The directive remains on track for approval but faces legal challenges.

Simultaneously, the Resilience Surge Initiative launches with €50bn in emergency funding, mandating AI-powered intrusion detection across critical infrastructure. ENISA begins audits and early deployments improve detection, though full implementation will take time. Despite these efforts, sovereignty falls to 20.0 as AI capability outpaces institutional capacity, while openweight capability reaches 43.0, reflecting widening gaps between frontier and accessible systems. Talks on AI Acceleration Zones continue without final agreements.
