## New measure  
**Resilience Surge Initiative**  
A targeted €50bn emergency programme to harden critical infrastructure against AI-enabled cyber intrusions, with mandatory audits and automated detection upgrades across energy, water, and transport systems.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to +15 resilience by turn 4; begins closing the detection and response gap exposed by the recent intrusion; reduces systemic fragility that undermines both public trust and sovereign capacity  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with binding obligations on operators of essential services and access to fast-tracked EU funding for compliance

## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because without binding control over compute deployed on our soil, all other measures depend on permission from others, and the Kimi K3 release proves that openweight capability is already weaponising at scale beyond governance reach.

## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that compute is power, and that power cannot be outsourced if sovereignty is to mean anything. This turn, the Commission advances the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive through trilogue negotiations, leveraging Article 114 TFEU to frame compute access as a condition of operating at scale in the internal market. The text now includes a binding allocation mechanism: the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) will schedule the reserved 30% capacity via a transparent, time-auction model, ensuring that no member state or operator can hoard or politicise access. To break Council resistance, we are offering a tripartite package: fast-track access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund for compliant operators, a liability shield for good-faith participation, and a sunset clause tying the directive’s duration to the emergence of a verifiable global compute governance framework — a signal that this is not permanent autarky, but emergency preparedness.

At the same time, we are responding to the automated decision scandal and the verification widening by launching the Resilience Surge Initiative. The cyber intrusion was not an outlier — it was a stress test we failed.
