## New measure  
**Sovereign Audit Access Framework**  
Establish a legal right for EU public agencies to inspect model weights, training data summaries, and agent behaviour logs of any AI system deployed in critical infrastructure or public services.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to +15 ai_safety by turn 5; closes the gap between voluntary developer testing and enforceable accountability, especially after the welfare scandal; begins building public trust through demonstrable control  
`Applies to:` AI systems deployed in EU critical infrastructure and public services, with enforcement through national digital authorities and the European AI Office

## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because it finishes this turn and locks in binding access to compute on EU soil, which is the foundational precondition for all other sovereignty measures, especially as openweight capability surges and private investment collapses.

## In practice  
We are closing the final trilogue negotiations on the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive, with the Commission insisting on the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) as the neutral scheduler of the reserved 30% compute capacity. The liability shield and fast-track Gigafactory access have secured enough industry buy-in to counter German and Irish resistance, though legal challenges under state aid and internal market rules are already being drafted. We are treating this as a fait accompli in parallel planning: national digital champions are being mapped, and EDIC’s initial auction framework is being stress-tested with ENISA and EuroHPC. This is not symbolic — it is the first time the EU will command a floor of usable compute that no foreign power or corporation can deny.

At the same time, the collapse in AI investment is both a crisis and an opening. The Gigafactories fund remains untouched by market swings — it was never about propping up valuations, but about anchoring physical capacity. We are using the downturn to renegotiate land and energy terms in the five candidate zones, locking in lower costs and faster timelines. The Resilience Surge Initiative moves into its second phase: ENISA has issued the first audit templates, and early adopters in the energy sector are deploying AI-powered intrusion detection with EU co-funding. But the openweight frontier release changes everything — a system near 55.0 capability is now permanently distributed, and we cannot govern what we cannot see. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Audit Access Framework: no more blind trust in black-box systems governing public lives. This will require overcoming fierce resistance from both foreign developers and domestic agencies used to opaque procurement. We are framing it as a condition of market access under the AI Act’s high-risk provisions, with enforcement tied to the upcoming AI Office inspections. This is not about safety alone — it is about restoring the state’s ability to answer for its own decisions.
