### Compute Locked In, Trust Still Fraying  
The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive clears its final legislative hurdles, establishing the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium to manage 30% of frontier compute capacity via time auctions. Though legal challenges loom, the EU now has a binding mechanism to secure compute for critical public functions—marking a rare win for sovereignty. The directive’s passage, combined with lower land and energy costs amid the investment downturn, strengthens long-term compute access. However, the broader market collapse chills private participation in the Tech Sovereignty Package, delaying permitting discussions in several member states.

### Open Frontier, Open Risk  
An unprecedented open-weight release delivers a model near the current frontier—capability indistinguishable from the most advanced closed systems—into the hands of anyone with a GPU. This release significantly advances openweight capability, but diffusion remains limited by hardware availability and integration barriers. As a result, the immediate jump in capability is substantial but not instantaneous at scale. ENISA warns that the Resilience Surge Initiative, while progressing, cannot yet counter such distributed threats. In response, the European Commission announces plans for the Sovereign Audit Access Framework, proposing mandatory inspection rights over model weights and logs for AI in critical infrastructure. The framework is under consultation and faces pushback from U.S. developers and skepticism from national agencies wary of operational burdens. It has not yet been adopted as a formal measure.

### Stalled Momentum  
Despite finishing a major measure, political capital erodes further. The portfolio remains heavy, and the proposed audit framework—while symbolically potent—fails to shift public sentiment, which remains stagnant amid lingering distrust from the welfare scandal. The investment collapse indirectly dampens resilience efforts, slowing vendor readiness and integration. Sovereignty dips again as capability growth outpaces institutional absorption, and no access security event offsets the decay. The openweight capability rise reflects early adoption of the new release but remains below frontier AI capability, consistent with real-world deployment lags.
