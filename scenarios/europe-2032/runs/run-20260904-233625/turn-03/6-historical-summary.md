A major welfare scandal in an EU member state—where an AI system denied benefits without appeal—triggers public outrage, dropping public sentiment to 37.0 and weakening political capital. In response, the EU passes the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive, establishing the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium to reserve 30% of frontier compute via time auctions, securing critical access despite legal and political challenges from Germany, Ireland, and industry groups.

The Resilience Surge Initiative launches with €50bn, deploying AI-powered intrusion detection across critical infrastructure, though implementation lags. Sovereignty declines to 20.0 as institutional capacity fails to keep pace with AI advancement. Openweight capability jumps to 43.0 following a frontier-level open release, but diffusion is constrained by hardware and integration barriers. ENISA warns of growing distributed risks, prompting the European Commission to propose the Sovereign Audit Access Framework, mandating inspection rights for AI in critical systems. The proposal faces resistance from U.S. developers and EU agencies and remains under consultation. Amid ongoing investment decline and stalled reforms, political capital and public trust remain low, with no significant recovery in sovereignty or sentiment.
