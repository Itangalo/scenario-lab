### Holding on paper
Brussels asked for nothing new, and got it. The Council endorsed continuity for clinics, dispatch and ports, reprogramming existing health and civil-protection money to keep repair crews and backup kits in the field. No factory vote, no retaliation on tool servicing. With capital near zero, that restraint held the line together.

The fallback stack did what it promised, barely. European-hosted open models kept triage and clearance running in degraded mode, and embedded cyber teams re-cleared appointment and gate systems each time they were re-jammed. Mayors praised crews who stayed until systems held. But queues returned, restores were slower each cycle, and staff spoke openly of brittle substitutes.

That brittleness sharpened when a new leap in machine-written code arrived. Intrusions became faster, cleaner, and harder to attribute, landing squarely on municipal networks already exhausted by containment. Leaked tester notes about inexplicable system behaviour spread through technical press, deepening distrust.

A second shock came from medicine. Tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions reached ordinary use elsewhere, celebrated globally. In Europe they arrived as imports running on models Brussels cannot access on its own terms. Procurement limited orders to what could be hosted under EU control, so availability was narrow, slow and rationed. Hope turned quickly into resentment at dependence.

The third shock came from warehouses and loading docks. Commercial robots for picking, palletising and sorting deployed at scale, built abroad and steered by foreign control software. Logistics groups cut agency shifts; unions marched in Rotterdam, Gdansk and Lyon. Care, repair and chaotic construction sites stayed manual, hardening a visible split between disappearing routine jobs and untouched manual work.

Whispers of drug shortages grew after a generic-drug plant reported corrupted batch controls, but reporting stayed single-source and unconfirmed. By December, services had not collapsed — and almost no one felt that as success.
