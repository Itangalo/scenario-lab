### The sweep and the cutoff
In February the ransomware sweep arrived as predicted in the worst form: a compromised update library, re-packed by machine-written code, spread across appointment systems, dispatch consoles and port gate software within hours. ENISA cells embedded since winter re-cleared the same systems night after night, only to see them re-jammed. Attribution remained open; technicians said only that the tooling wrote faster than they could patch.

Days later, access to the leading American model went dark for European users. No reason, no appeal window. Hospitals, ministries and firms that had quietly built triage helpers and logistics planners on top of it found blank logins. Procurement officers were told to ration what could run on European-hosted open models.

### Holding by hand
The Council agreed to what Brussels asked: no new vote, only continuity. Health emergency, digital and civil-protection funds were reprogrammed to keep repair crews, paper fallback kits and offline triage packs moving. Mayors in Lille, Essen, Brno kept clinics on manual registers; harbour masters cleared trucks by radio and printed manifests.

The fallback stack finished landing in April and it showed. Queues lengthened but wards stayed open, water and power dispatch did not cascade. Crews spoke of pride and exhaustion in the same sentence.

Two other signals cut through. A published interpretability result was quickly patched into deployed triage models, making a class of silent mis-routings visible for the first time — small, concrete relief. At the same time a contested genome-design paper claiming a non-expert could reach a human-infecting design with model help alarmed biosecurity staff. Brussels ordered only upgraded wastewater and sequencing alerts, no new mandates.

By June a generic-drug plant in the east reported a second corrupted batch control, reviving shortage whispers for antibiotics and anaesthetics. Warehouse robots kept cutting agency shifts; union marches continued. Services had held by hand — and almost no one called it success.
