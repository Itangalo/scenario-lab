### The Mobilisation That Wasn’t

The European Union launched the Emergency Compute Mobilisation, invoking emergency powers to seize 30% of compute from foreign hyperscalers operating in the Single Market. The plan aimed to build sovereign training capacity by turn 13, but met immediate resistance. Microsoft, Amazon, and Google declared the requisition incompatible with US law and began rerouting European AI workloads through non-EU nodes. The US State Department confirmed that any company complying would face penalties under export control enforcement, framing the EU action as a national security risk.

Germany and the Nordics, despite earlier signals, refused to back the measure without NATO-level coordination, which Washington declined. France proceeded with its parallel national compute initiative, draining political momentum. The Commission’s invocation of the Stability and Growth Pact’s crisis clause was challenged by the ECB and two member states, delaying fund disbursement. No new GPUs were secured. The European AI Foundry exists only on paper.

### Scandal and Isolation

An ombudsman’s report revealed that an AI system used in Dutch social services had wrongfully denied benefits to over 12,000 citizens due to flawed risk scoring — an automated_decision_scandal with clear institutional accountability. Protests erupted in Amsterdam and Brussels, deepening public distrust. The narrative shifted from technological dependency to domestic harm, undercutting support for rapid AI expansion.

Meanwhile, export_control_escalation tightened: the US now restricts all frontier model access, even for allied institutions, unless routed through approved joint evaluation frameworks — which exclude the EU. The middle_power_coalition emerged as Japan, South Korea, and the Netherlands formalised export coordination, offering the EU observer status but no voting rights. It was a lifeline, but not control.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

Global AI capability rose to 79.0 as opaque reasoning systems improved recursively, unchecked by monitoring. Openweight models, reverse-engineered from leaked research, reached 65.0. Resilience dipped slightly as attention shifted from defence to crisis management. Sovereignty eroded further, and political capital fell below 12 — the threshold where agenda control slips. The Union acted, but the world moved faster.
