## In practice  
We are invoking Article 7 of the Digital Markets Act and the emergency clause of the AI Act to compel hyperscalers operating at scale in the EU to allocate 30% of their EU-hosted compute to the European AI Foundry — a new entity hosted jointly by the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking and the European AI Office, with headquarters in Luxembourg and operational nodes in Finland and Portugal. This is not a partnership: it is a condition of market access. The Foundry will control scheduling, data flow, and model weights, with no remote access permitted for parent companies. We are offering a five-year tax stability guarantee in return, but no opt-out. The target is 10,000 H100e-equivalent GPUs online and auditable by turn 13 — enough to train a model to 78.0 capability under sovereign conditions.

We are funding this through a €48bn draw from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused digital tranche, reprogrammed under Article 27 of the Stability and Growth Pact’s crisis clause, which the Commission is now formally invoking. Germany and the Nordic states have signalled conditional support, contingent on strict limits on duration and scope; we are binding that support by offering them first access to the Foundry’s capacity for national security applications. France insists on parallel funding for a domestic effort — we are letting them, but making clear that only capacity under Foundry governance counts toward EU sovereignty. The US State Department has warned this is “economic fragmentation”; we are responding that exclusion from their frontier models left us no alternative. This is not the path we wanted. It is the one opaque reasoning and eu_frontier_access_denied forced upon us. If we cannot build our own AI, we will soon not be able to govern our own society.
