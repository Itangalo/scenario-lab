## In practice
We are acting to enforce the Emergency Compute Mobilisation through every legal instrument still standing. The Commission has filed infringement proceedings against Microsoft, Amazon, and Google under Article 7 of the Digital Markets Act and the emergency clause of the AI Act, arguing that refusal to reroute EU AI workloads to EU-controlled infrastructure constitutes a breach of enforceable obligations tied to market access. Interim measures to suspend certain data transfer privileges under the GDPR are being prepared, pending a green light from the European Data Protection Board. Simultaneously, we are convening an extraordinary Justice and Home Affairs Council to secure a qualified majority affirming that market access in the Single Market carries binding operational obligations—not voluntary cooperation—especially during declared systemic risk emergencies.

Behind the scenes, we are renegotiating with Germany and the Nordics, offering expanded access to public research and healthcare AI development on Foundry infrastructure in exchange for political backing and joint legal defence against U.S. pressure. We are also engaging France not to merge its national compute, but to align its security accreditation with the European AI Office under a shared sovereignty framework, so parallel efforts still converge on common standards. The €48 billion in Recovery and Resilience funds remains frozen under ECB objection; we are preparing bilateral loan guarantees with fiscally resilient member states, ring-fenced and legally insulated, to ensure that if the fiscal gate closes, the mission does not. This is no longer just about capacity—it is about whether the EU can still act as a sovereign entity when its institutions fracture under pressure.
