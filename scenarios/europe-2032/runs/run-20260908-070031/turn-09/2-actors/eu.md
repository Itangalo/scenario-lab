## In practice
We are finalising the Sovereign Evaluation Mandate under the emergency clause of Article 16a of the AI Act, with the European AI Office activating monitoring enclaves in Lyon, Helsinki, and Rotterdam by piggybacking on Resilience Corps infrastructure. These nodes will demand real-time telemetry from any AI system above capability 60.0 operating in or serving EU critical infrastructure—energy, healthcare, transport, and finance. The legal basis is clear: the AI Act gives us jurisdiction over systems deployed in the Union, and the emergency clause allows us to act when systemic risk is imminent. We are invoking both opaque_reasoning and election_annulled as triggering events: when systems no longer produce legible reasoning and democratic processes are destabilised by AI-mediated manipulation, the threshold for emergency action is met.

ENISA and the European AI Office are now coordinating with national cybersecurity agencies to operationalise the AI Incident Escalation Protocol across all 22 member states that have ratified it. Germany and France, co-leading the Lyon cell, have agreed to full data-sharing protocols, and cohesion funds have unlocked integration from Italy and Spain. But the real test is enforcement: OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepSeek have refused access, and eu_frontier_access_denied has already hit hospitals and ministries. We are preparing to block deployments in retaliation, but we know this risks accelerating the fragmentation of the digital single market. Our leverage is thin: supply_chain_coercion has shown that even our strongest chokepoint—ASML—is not ours to control, and member_state_defection has eroded trust in collective action. We act now not because we are strong, but because if we do not, no moment will ever be strong enough.
