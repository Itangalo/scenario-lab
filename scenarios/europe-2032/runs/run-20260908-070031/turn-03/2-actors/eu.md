## New measure  
**Open-Weight Containment Protocol**  
Introduce mandatory pre-release review and risk thresholding for open-weight models above capability index 45.0, with enforceable liability for downstream misuse and a public audit trail of releases.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` openweight_capability −6 (by delaying or blocking releases at 45–55 range), ai_safety +3 (via traceability and deterrence), public_sentiment +2 (if framed as closing a dangerous loophole)  
`Applies to:` All developers releasing model weights publicly, whether based in the EU or seeking access to EU users, markets, or compute infrastructure

## Priority  
**Sovereign Evaluation Mandate** — because the collapse in private investment has created a narrow window to establish independent evaluation authority before the next capability wave, and because member state defection makes it urgent to demonstrate that the EU can enforce its own standards without relying on foreign goodwill or market discipline.

## In practice  
We are treating the ai_investment_collapse not as a brake on risk, but as a moment of fragility: when frontier labs shrink training runs, open-weight releases become more likely as a cost recovery strategy, and safety corners are cut fastest where no one is watching. The rise of open models at 49.0 capability — already capable of sustained research tasks under supervision — means that any such release today distributes a tool that was, two years ago, a state-level strategic asset. The Open-Weight Containment Protocol closes this loophole by establishing that public release above 45.0 is not a right, but a privilege subject to review, audit, and enforceable accountability. We are drafting a new Article 52a to the AI Act, to be adopted under the Digital Services Act’s rapid response mechanism, requiring developers to notify the European AI Office 90 days before any open release above threshold, submit to a risk assessment including fine-tuning proliferation vectors, and accept liability for demonstrable misuse chains originating from their weights. Non-compliant entities face exclusion from EU cloud infrastructure and app stores.

Simultaneously, we are accelerating the Sovereign Evaluation Mandate by fast-tracking the secure compute enclaves in France and Finland, pulling forward delivery by six months through emergency procurement under TEU Article 122. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking is repurposing 40% of its upcoming exascale allocation for red-teaming and interpretability workloads, and we are offering senior evaluation roles to researchers from the emergent lab cooperation network, integrating their red-teaming protocols into our baseline. To overcome member state defection, we are invoking the EU’s market power: any hyperscaler wishing to operate in the Union must grant reciprocal access to evaluation teams for systems above 50.0, with symmetric data sovereignty guarantees. For the Tech Sovereignty Package, we are linking disbursement of the first InvestAI tranche to binding commitments on grid priority for two sites — one in Sweden, one in Spain — and using the Foreign Direct Investment screening mechanism to block any cloud expansion that does not include EU-controlled fallback capacity. Political capital is thin, but we judge that allowing evaluation dependence to persist would erode sovereignty faster than any fiscal strain.
