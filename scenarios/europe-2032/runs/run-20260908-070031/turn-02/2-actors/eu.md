## New measure  
**Sovereign Evaluation Mandate**  
Establish an EU-owned, technically autonomous capability to evaluate frontier AI systems before deployment, with binding authority over any system accessing EU markets or infrastructure.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +8, eu_ai_sovereignty +5 (via independent verification leverage), public_sentiment +2 (if communicated as containment of foreign risk)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member states, and all developers seeking to deploy systems above capability index 50.0 in the EU

## Priority  
**Sovereign Evaluation Mandate** — because the widening gap between capability and safety, now accelerated by automatic verification in new domains, means we can no longer rely on foreign developers’ self-assessment when those systems are already shaping our critical infrastructure, legal processes, and clinical workflows.

## In practice  
We are treating the verification_widens event as a strategic inflection point: domains once thought to require human judgment now fall under algorithmic validation, removing a key brake on capability growth. This erodes the assumption that frontier AI progress is confined to narrow, checkable domains — and proves that safety margins based on human-in-the-loop assumptions are evaporating. In response, we are launching the Sovereign Evaluation Mandate to build an EU-owned evaluation capacity that can test, interpret, and if necessary block systems above 50.0 capability index before deployment. The European AI Office will lead this under a reinforced Article 40a of the AI Act, to be proposed in January 2027, granting it access to model weights, training data summaries, and agent-behaviour logs under strict data sovereignty protocols. We are allocating €4.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe to stand up three evaluation centres — in France, Germany, and Finland — with secure compute enclaves and red-team labs by mid-2028.

At the same time, we are pressing the Tech Sovereignty Package through national permitting bottlenecks, linking disbursement of the first tranche of InvestAI funds to binding commitments from member states on grid access and environmental waivers for two priority zones — one in Sweden, one in Spain. The Council’s Competitiveness Working Party will meet in February to resolve the ASML-US cloud access deadlock, using the Foreign Direct Investment screening mechanism to tie expanded US cloud capacity in the EU to reciprocal compute access for EU-based developers. We are aware that political capital is tight, but we judge that allowing unchecked evaluation dependence — where the EU must accept safety claims from powers with divergent interests — is a greater long-term drain on sovereignty and trust than the cost of building our own eyes on the frontier.
