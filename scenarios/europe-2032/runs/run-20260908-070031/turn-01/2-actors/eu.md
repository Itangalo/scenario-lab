## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_test_shot proves we are already in a threat environment where detection fails and attribution is diffuse, and no amount of sovereignty or evaluation will matter if essential services collapse before we can act.

## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_test_shot as a live-fire warning. This turn, the Commission invokes Article 4 of the NIS2 Directive to trigger an emergency coordination mode, mobilising ENISA and the newly activated Joint Cyber Resilience Board to deploy rapid assessment teams to the affected grid operators and water utility within 72 hours. We are directing €12bn in emergency cohesion funds — drawn from unused pandemic recovery allocations — to finance immediate hardening: air-gapped control systems, AI-driven anomaly detection in operational technology networks, and mandatory red-teaming of all Tier 1 operators by January 2027. The Council will convene an extraordinary session in November under the Integrated Political Crisis Response framework to agree binding upgrade timelines, with the European Defence Agency seconding cyber units to support civilian infrastructure protection.

At the same time, we are pressing member states to fast-track the Tech sovereignty package’s accelerated-permitting zones, linking disbursement of Gigafactories funding to national progress on grid access for new data centres. The Finance and Competitiveness Councils will meet jointly in December to resolve the tension between ASML’s export constraints and our compute ambitions, using the leverage of the Foreign Direct Investment screening framework to condition US cloud partnerships on reciprocal compute access. This is not just about building faster — it is about anchoring capability where we can defend it, and acting while the political window from the election_annulled and cyber_test_shot remains open.
