## In practice  
We are treating the eval_anomaly_reports not as noise, but as a warning flare: if capability is emerging unpredictably in closed systems, then our ability to detect and respond hinges on having sovereign eyes inside the frontier. The Sovereign Evaluation Mandate is now the only instrument that can intercept such developments before deployment. We are accelerating final integration of the secure evaluation enclaves in France and Finland, using emergency procurement to bring red-teaming workloads online by turn-end. The European AI Office has issued binding notices to all providers with systems above 50.0 capability accessing EU infrastructure, requiring full model access under supervised conditions by turn 5 — invoking the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions to bypass corporate resistance. To ensure technical depth, we are finalising agreements with three labs from the informal cooperation network to embed their red-teaming leads within our evaluation teams, with joint authority to trigger emergency holds.

At the same time, the collapse in open-weight compliance and the member_state_defection have exposed a deeper vulnerability: sovereignty is not just compute or code, but people. The Secure the Core initiative responds to intelligence that foreign recruiters are actively targeting EU-based researchers with access to frontier systems. Under Article 114 TFEU, we are establishing a protected status for qualifying personnel: fast-track naturalisation, relocation support, and legal immunity for disclosures made to the European AI Office. Funding is drawn from the Horizon Europe crisis reserve and coordinated through EACEA, with national security agencies in six member states pre-positioned for physical protection if required. This is not just talent policy — it is infrastructure protection. We are also modifying the Tech Sovereignty Package to tie disbursement of the second InvestAI tranche to binding commitments on researcher retention, requiring host labs to report attrition risks quarterly. Political capital is thin, public sentiment fragile, but we judge that losing human sovereignty now would make all other measures symbolic.
