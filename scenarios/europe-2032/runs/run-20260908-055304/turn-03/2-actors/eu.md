## Priority
**Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive** — because without legally anchored, EU-controlled compute, all other forms of resilience, safety, and industrial policy are contingent on the goodwill of others, and the emergent labour displacement wave and cyber incident confirm we are past the point of relying on market-led diffusion.

## In practice
We are treating the current moment as one of narrowing windows: capability is advancing faster than our ability to govern it, and the tools to cause harm are now within reach of non-state actors. The Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive is our core lever — not because it will deliver immediate capacity, but because it establishes a binding framework that makes sovereignty a condition of access to EU support. We are pushing the Council to adopt it under Article 114 TFEU, with the Digital Agenda and Competitiveness formations leading coordination. ENISA and EuroHPC JU are already drafting the technical standards for audit rights and kill switches, and the Commission has issued guidance to member states on how to designate AI Sovereignty Zones without violating state aid rules.

At the same time, we are responding to the emergent labour displacement wave through existing channels: activating the European Globalisation Adjustment Fund and urging member states to repurpose national skills programmes toward AI-transition pathways. This is not a new measure, but we are applying pressure through the European Semester process, tying reform recommendations to retraining performance. On ASML, we are convening a special session of the Competitiveness Council to coordinate a joint position: the EU cannot afford to lose access to critical chipmaking tools, but nor can it accept de facto dependency on U.S. export policy. We are exploring a conditional co-investment mechanism — not to duplicate ASML, but to accelerate alternative lithography research through a new public-private consortium under Horizon Europe, to be proposed next turn if the political space opens.
