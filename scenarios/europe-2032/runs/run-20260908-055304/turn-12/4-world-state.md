### The Weight of Action

Global AI capability advances to 65.0 as non-linguistic reasoning systems deepen integration into scientific discovery pipelines. The closed frontier pulls further ahead, but diffusion continues at a measured pace. Open-weight models gain modestly in capability, reaching 55.0, as limited compute availability and persistent chip shortages constrain large-scale retraining. The narrative of open access under pressure grows louder, but no breakthrough release or sudden leap occurs.

The **Runtime Verification Mandate** formally lands. Final standards are adopted under Article 114 TFEU, establishing certified monitoring frameworks for AI in energy, transport, health, and finance. Kill-switch protocols are integrated, and phased compliance begins: new systems must comply within six months, legacy ones within twelve. To counter U.S. trade threats, the Commission invokes essential security exceptions, asserting non-discrimination. Technical assistance is offered via the Digital Europe Programme, but uptake is slow among mid-tier operators. Eastern member states abstain in the Competitiveness Council, citing unresolved telemetry access disputes, underscoring lingering fragmentation.

Meanwhile, the **Sovereign Compute Corridor** transitions from planning to early construction in Marseille, Prague, and Vilnius. Fast-track permits are issued under the Net-Zero Industry Act, and term sheets with two hyperscalers include model escrow and runtime monitoring. Yet chip shortages and cooling delays push initial capacity to late 2033 — the build lags behind capability growth.

The proposed **EU-Accredited Biosecurity Red-Teaming Labs Network** does not launch this turn. While €420 million is earmarked and legal groundwork begins via delegated act under the AI Act, the measure cannot be introduced alongside the Runtime Verification Mandate. The Commission defers formal adoption to the next turn, prioritising the verification framework. Internal debate continues over enforcement scope and lab accreditation criteria. The bio-risk signals remain alarming, and pressure mounts — but action is delayed by procedural discipline.

### Fragmentation and Focus

The northern technical alliance continues its parallel runtime monitoring work, now developing interoperable kill-switch protocols outside EU structures. Trust in central enforcement erodes further, even as the Commission pushes unity.

Political capital sees a modest rise, up +2.0 to 27.0, reflecting recognition of decisive movement on runtime verification amid growing risk. This is not a surge of unity, but a narrow gain from acting on a clear priority despite constraints. No broader restoration of cohesion is observed.
