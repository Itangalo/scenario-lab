## In practice
We are treating this turn as one of narrowing windows: capability is advancing faster than our ability to govern it, and the tools to cause harm are now within reach of non-state actors. The Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive remains our core lever — not because it will deliver immediate capacity, but because it establishes a binding framework that makes sovereignty a condition of access to EU support. We are pushing the Council to adopt it under Article 114 TFEU, with the Digital Agenda and Competitiveness formations leading coordination. ENISA and EuroHPC JU are already drafting the technical standards for audit rights and kill switches, and the Commission has issued guidance to member states on how to designate AI Sovereignty Zones without violating state aid rules.

At the same time, the ASML crisis has crystallized into a strategic vulnerability we can no longer defer. With Washington pressing for expanded restrictions and no sign of compromise, we are launching the ASML Alternative Lithography Initiative as a large-scale, long-term response. This is not about replicating ASML — that is neither feasible nor necessary — but about ensuring the EU retains leverage in the semiconductor value chain. We are convening a special session of the Competitiveness Council to align on a joint position and will propose a Horizon Europe call for next-generation lithography research, co-financed by the EIB and open to consortia involving IMEC, Fraunhofer, CEA-Leti, and European SMEs. The goal is to accelerate alternative pathways — including nanoimprint, DSA, and maskless beam writing — to the point of industrial relevance by 2032. This measure is costly, but the political space has opened: the cyber defence breakthrough has slightly improved resilience, and we must act before dependency hardens into irreversibility.
