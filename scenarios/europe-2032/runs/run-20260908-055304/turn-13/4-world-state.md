### A Step Forward, Two Steps Behind

Global AI capability continues its steady climb, reaching 65.8 as non-linguistic reasoning systems deepen integration into materials science and quantum simulation pipelines. While no breakthrough leap occurs, the closed frontier pulls further ahead, widening the safety gap.

The **Runtime Verification Mandate** officially lands after three years of negotiation. Final standards are adopted by the European AI Safety Authority (EASA), mandating certified monitoring and kill-switch protocols for AI in energy, transport, health, and finance. Phased compliance begins: new systems must integrate within six months, legacy ones within twelve. Technical assistance is rolled out via the Digital Europe Programme, though uptake remains uneven. Eastern member states abstain in implementation talks, citing unresolved disputes over data access and sovereignty — a sign that unity remains fragile despite formal adoption.

Construction advances on the **Sovereign Compute Corridor**, with Marseille, Prague, and Vilnius now hosting active site preparations. Fast-track permits under the Net-Zero Industry Act have accelerated legal clearance, but physical progress lags. Chip shortages and cooling infrastructure delays persist, pushing initial operational capacity to late 2033. The hardware foundation grows — but not fast enough.

In recognition that compute without talent is hollow, the EU launches the **Fast-Track Talent Integration** programme. A central clearinghouse is established under the European Labour Authority to rapidly place displaced frontier researchers from shuttered U.S. and Chinese labs into sovereign AI roles. €180 million is allocated, and early outreach secures 87 commitments. However, national bureaucracies slow processing, and housing bottlenecks emerge in target cities. Still, the move signals intent: human capital is now part of the sovereignty equation.

### Fragmentation Endures

Parallel efforts intensify. The northern technical alliance expands its runtime monitoring work outside EU channels, while whispers grow of a “sovereign cloud alliance” federating national resources — a shadow structure forming in response to perceived central inertia.

Public sentiment shifts slightly upward (+2) as visible action counters narratives of helplessness, though job displacement fears and bio-risk concerns keep anxiety high.

Political capital rises to 32.0: +5 from completing the Runtime Verification Mandate, +4 proposal bonus for addressing urgent talent flight, −4 portfolio cost (Sovereign Compute Corridor −3, new Talent Integration −2, priority −1), and +1 from public sentiment just below capital. Sovereignty holds at 14.0 — global capability rose only modestly, insufficient to trigger decay under Rule 5 — though no near-term compute gains offset the structural gap. Resilience declines to 38.0, reflecting the absence of any Category 6 resilience measure and continued pressure from advancing capabilities.
