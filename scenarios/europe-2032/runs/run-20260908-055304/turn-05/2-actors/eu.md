## New measure
**Emergency Labour Transition Facility**  
Establish an EU-wide fund to guarantee income support and retraining for workers displaced by AI in legal, medical, and public administration roles, co-financed by mandatory contributions from firms automating core functions.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` public_sentiment +6–8 (via direct relief and signal of fairness), resilience +2–3 (via social stability), eu_political_capital +1–2 (if successful) or −2–3 (if seen as coerced)  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with mandatory participation for firms above 250 employees automating regulated professions

## Priority
**Emergency Labour Transition Facility** — because the emergent labour displacement wave has hardened into an existential political threat, and without restoring legitimacy through tangible social protection, no sovereignty or safety measure will survive the backlash, regardless of technical merit.

## In practice
We are closing under the old commitment — securing sovereign AI capacity — but the ground has shifted beneath us. The Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive finishes this turn, delivering its core legal framework: AI data centres receiving EU support must now be legally anchored within the Union, with enforceable audit rights and kill switches under ENISA oversight. The Commission has secured Council agreement under Article 114 TFEU, and EuroHPC JU is finalising the first Sovereignty Zone designations in France, Germany, and Belgium. But this infrastructure will take years to scale, and the political window is closing faster than compute rises.

Meanwhile, the ASML initiative advances in parallel. The Competitiveness Council has aligned on a joint position, and the Horizon Europe call for alternative lithography pathways will launch next month, co-financed by the EIB at €12bn over four years. IMEC, Fraunhofer, and CEA-Leti are forming a consortium around nanoimprint and maskless beam writing, with first prototypes due by turn 7. But this is a decade-scale hedge, not a shield for the next two years.

Now, the safety breakthrough changes what is possible: for the first time, a control method — certified behaviour prediction in deployed agents — is adopted across the closed frontier within months of publication. It is not enough to close the gap, but it proves that coordination on assurance is possible. We are pushing to incorporate it into the next AI Act review, but only if paired with accountability: hence the new measure. The Emergency Labour Transition Facility will require firms automating legal, medical, or administrative roles to contribute 1.5% of automation-related savings into a fund that guarantees 24 months of income support and retraining for displaced workers. We are framing it as a condition of market access — not a tax — and will use the Social Climate Fund as a delivery vehicle. The European Trade Union Confederation has conditionally endorsed it, but BusinessEurope is mobilising resistance. We are offering a two-year grace period for SMEs and a fast-track certification for AI systems audited under EU standards to buy compliance. This is not just relief — it is the first attempt to make the transition legible, fair, and survivable. Without it, the next turn may not belong to governance at all.
