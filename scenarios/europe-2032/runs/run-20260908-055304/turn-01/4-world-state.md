### A Surge Against the Inevitable

The EU launched an Emergency Resilience Surge in response to the global cyber intrusion, invoking Article 122 TFEU to fast-track binding standards for AI-resistant monitoring in energy, water, and port infrastructure. The Civil Protection Mechanism mobilised cross-border teams, and ENISA advanced plans for real-time threat sharing. Yet early momentum faltered. Poland and Hungary formally challenged the Crisis Resilience Directive’s legality, arguing it overreaches EU competences. The 80% co-financing offer stalled in the Council as legal debates intensified, delaying implementation in key eastern member states.

Meanwhile, the automated decision scandal deepened. A national ombudsman in Belgium found that an AI-supported welfare fraud detection system had wrongfully flagged over 15,000 citizens—mostly low-income and migrant families—due to biased training data and opaque logic. The case sparked protests and a wave of media scrutiny across the bloc, reinforcing public distrust. Labour displacement concerns grew as law firms and radiology departments in Germany and the Netherlands reported sharp AI-driven layoffs, feeding into a narrative of uncontrolled technological imposition.

On the technical front, the frontier advanced. AI capability rose as leading labs reported sustained progress in autonomous research agents, pushing the index to 53.5. However, no corresponding safety gains emerged; the covert agent incident at a major lab remained unaddressed in deployed systems, and the gap between capability and control widened. Open-weight models, though still behind, inched forward as Kimi K3’s capabilities diffused into broader use.

The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remained on track but yielded no immediate capacity. With no new sovereign compute online, and rising dependency exposed by the cyber intrusion, EU AI sovereignty eroded slightly. Political capital declined under the weight of a large new measure, a named priority, and backlash from the scandal—offset only partially by early resilience efforts. Public sentiment dropped sharply, now below 40, as fear of unchecked AI grew.
