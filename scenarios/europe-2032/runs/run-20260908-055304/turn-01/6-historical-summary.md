The EU launched an Emergency Resilience Surge following a major cyber intrusion, invoking Article 122 TFEU to accelerate AI-resistant infrastructure standards and mobilizing cross-border response teams. However, legal challenges from Poland and Hungary stalled the Crisis Resilience Directive’s implementation, weakening cohesion. A scandal erupted over an AI-driven welfare fraud system in Belgium that wrongfully targeted 15,000 vulnerable citizens, fueling public distrust and protests. Job losses in legal and medical sectors due to AI automation intensified societal concerns. Technologically, AI capabilities advanced to 53.5 with progress in autonomous research agents, but safety measures lagged, widening the control gap. Despite ongoing efforts, no new sovereign compute capacity came online, eroding EU tech autonomy. Political capital declined amid backlash, and public sentiment fell below 40, reflecting growing fear of uncontrolled AI.
