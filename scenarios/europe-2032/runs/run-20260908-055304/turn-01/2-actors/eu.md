## Priority
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_test_shot proved that detection failed and exposure is real, and we cannot wait for better assurance when the frontier is already inside our grids.

## In practice
We are treating the cyber_test_shot as a wake-up call: the intrusions were not stopped by existing defences, and their discovery was accidental. This means our critical systems are vulnerable to AI-driven attacks that evolve faster than human-led monitoring can track. This turn, we are activating the Civil Protection Mechanism to coordinate a cross-border resilience surge, starting with electricity transmission, water utilities and port authorities. The Commission will table a temporary Crisis Resilience Directive under Article 122 TFEU, mandating immediate implementation of AI-resistant monitoring stacks — including air-gapped logging, behavioural anomaly detection, and multi-party verification of control commands — across all critical operators. Funding will come from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused allocations and the Digital Europe Programme, reprogrammed with urgency derogations.

At the same time, we are pushing the ENISA mandate expansion through COREPER II, insisting on real-time threat sharing and mandatory red-teaming for all Tier 1 operators. Germany and France support the move, but Poland and Hungary are resisting on grounds of sovereignty and cost. We are offering 80% co-financing from a repurposed Just Transition Fund window to overcome that resistance. The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre will be tasked with delivering reference toolkits by Q1 2027, and we are fast-tracking procurement rules for AI-hardened security stacks under the new Crisis Procurement Protocol. This is not about catching the frontier — it’s about making sure it cannot paralyze us when it arrives uninvited.
