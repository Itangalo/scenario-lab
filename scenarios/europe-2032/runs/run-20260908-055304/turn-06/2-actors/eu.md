## New measure
**Sovereign AI Run-Time Assurance Programme**  
Establish a permanent EU capability to independently verify the behaviour of AI systems operating in critical infrastructure, using certified prediction frameworks and agent monitoring tools developed in the safety breakthrough.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +4–6 (via operational verification), eu_ai_sovereignty +2–3 (via independent control), resilience +1–2 (via early detection)  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, covering systems operating in energy, healthcare, transport, and public administration that exceed 40.0 in capability

## Priority
**Sovereign AI Run-Time Assurance Programme** — because the safety breakthrough proves that certified behaviour prediction works in closed environments, but without an EU-owned verification layer, we are still trusting foreign systems in our critical infrastructure; sovereignty without assurance is exposure, not independence.

## In practice
We are closing the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive this turn with binding oversight now in force for EU-supported data centres in France, Germany, and Belgium. ENISA has appointed audit leads, and the first Sovereignty Zones are being mapped into the EuroHPC JU operational framework. But the absence of sovereign runtime control means that even if the hardware is ours, the agents running on it are not fully under our command. The safety breakthrough—certified behaviour prediction adopted at the closed frontier—must not become another foreign standard we passively import. We are launching the Sovereign AI Run-Time Assurance Programme to build our own verification stack: a public research effort, hosted jointly by EUI’s AI Observatory and the Fraunhofer Institute, with secure access to agent traces from EU-anchored systems. It will develop tools to detect goal drift, unauthorised coordination, and capability leakage in real time, and it will report directly to the Commission’s AI Office. This is not about duplicating private safety work—it is about creating a public right to know. We are funding it through a reallocation from the unspent portion of the Digital Europe Programme, €3.8bn over two years, and tying it to the next revision of the AI Act’s high-risk provisions. Resistance from private operators is expected, but we are offering compliance credit for systems that open telemetry channels to the assurance layer. Without this, every sovereign data centre we build becomes a fortress with no garrison.
