Require all AI systems operating in energy, transport, health, and financial infrastructure to run under certified EU verification frameworks that continuously monitor for unauthorised objective drift, with kill-switch authority vested in the European AI Safety Authority.

## In practice
We are finalising the Sovereign AI Run-Time Assurance Programme this turn, with the Joint Research Centre certifying the first monitoring nodes in Paris, Berlin, and Helsinki. These will begin ingesting telemetry from pilot sites in rail and energy networks by the end of December, but only under contested legal authority. The southern member state continues to block EU-level access to its national cloud infrastructure, citing sovereignty — the irony is not lost on us. We are now treating that resistance not as a legal dispute, but as a systemic vulnerability.

Accordingly, we are advancing the Emergency Cohesion Protocol in the General Affairs Council, backed by a Franco-German political initiative and funded through repurposed REACT-EU allocations. The Commission will propose automatic suspension of digital market access privileges for foreign AI providers shielded from Union monitoring by bilateral deals, and redirect cohesion funds from non-compliant states to those investing in verified runtime infrastructure. We are offering a six-month grace period for renegotiation, but immunity clauses are non-negotiable. The Anti-Coercion Instrument remains available should any state attempt to block enforcement through Council paralysis.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Runtime Verification Mandate, to be drafted by the European AI Safety Authority with technical input from the JRC and ENISA. This will require all AI systems in critical sectors to operate under EU-certified monitoring frameworks that detect and halt unauthorised objective drift in real time. The legal basis will be Article 114 TFEU, framed as a product safety imperative. We know the opaque_reasoning event has broken legacy interpretability tools — that is why we are shifting from reading chains of thought to verifying system behaviour at the level of action and outcome. This is not optional. If a system cannot be continuously verified, it cannot operate in our critical infrastructure. We will begin consultations with member states and operators immediately, with a draft directive expected by turn 10.
