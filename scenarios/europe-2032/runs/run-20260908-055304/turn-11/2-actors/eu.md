## In practice  
We are treating the openweight frontier release as a strategic inflection point: the gap between closed and open capability has narrowed to under five points, and with non-linguistic reasoning now standard, even basic interpretability tools fail. This means that whatever dangerous capability exists at the frontier is now, effectively, already distributed. The emergent distributed bio red-teaming effort confirms this — open models are already being used to probe for attack vectors, and while the effort is uncoordinated, it proves the intent and feasibility. We cannot rely on developer goodwill or national silos. Our response must be binding, technical, and enforceable at runtime.

Accordingly, we are advancing the Runtime Verification Mandate with full legal force under Article 114 TFEU, treating unchecked AI in critical infrastructure as a product safety hazard. The European AI Safety Authority, supported by ENISA and the JRC, is finalising the technical standards for continuous monitoring of agent behaviour, including anomaly detection, objective drift alerts, and automated kill-switch triggers. We are offering phased compliance—six months for new systems, twelve for legacy—but no exemptions for foreign providers. The U.S. has threatened WTO action, but we are holding firm: safety is not a trade barrier, it is a condition of operation. Member states are being engaged through the Competitiveness Council to secure qualified majority support, and we are offering technical assistance funds to help smaller operators adapt.

Simultaneously, the Sovereign Compute Corridor Initiative is moving from design to site activation. Permits in Marseille, Prague, and Vilnius are being fast-tracked under the Net-Zero Industry Act, with grid access guaranteed by newly declared strategic infrastructure status. We are negotiating with three major hyperscalers to anchor their EU-critical workloads in these zones, offering tax incentives in exchange for full runtime monitoring and model escrow. No remote administration by foreign entities will be permitted. Talent recruitment has begun, targeting researchers displaced by export controls, with residency fast-tracked under the EU Blue Card+ scheme. This is not about parity—it is about ensuring that when the next crisis hits, the EU controls the infrastructure that matters.

We are also acting on bio-risk. The emergent red-teaming effort, while unsanctioned, has revealed real vulnerabilities. We cannot wait for a synthesis event. Therefore, we are introducing a new restriction: any open-weight model above a capability threshold—set at 50.0 on the index—must undergo mandatory biosecurity red-teaming by an EU-accredited lab before release. The test must simulate plausible dual-use biological design pathways, and results must be submitted to the European AI Safety Authority. No ban on release, no restriction on capability—but no untested diffusion either. This is not perfect, but it is enforceable, and it buys time. Legal services are preparing a delegated act under the AI Act’s high-risk provisions, and we will propose it to the Parliament’s Internal Market and Environment committees in early Q1 2032.
