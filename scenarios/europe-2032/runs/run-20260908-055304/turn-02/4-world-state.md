### A Surge in Sovereignty, at a Cost

The EU doubles down on compute sovereignty. The **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive** is formally proposed, reframing AI infrastructure as a single market and security imperative under Article 114 TFEU. Fast-tracked permitting zones, binding public access clauses, and €30bn in conditional state aid aim to force momentum. The directive is named priority, replacing the *Emergency Resilience Surge* as the Commission’s focus. This shift retires one prior measure, ensuring only one new measure is introduced this turn.

Yet backlash flares. Ireland, Sweden, and Finland warn the rules could deter hyperscaler investment, risking thousands of data centre jobs. Legal challenges loom over the use of internal market powers for security-driven industrial policy. The Commission concedes to a six-month review clause, delaying full implementation and weakening early impact. As a result, no immediate sovereignty gain is realized—completion remains years off, and the measure is still in early rollout.

### The World Accelerates

A **major cyber incident** hits municipal systems across three member states—ransomware, self-propagating and AI-optimized—overwhelming response teams. The attack’s tooling is traced to a leaked model, underscoring the new offensive reality. Resilience efforts absorb some damage, but public confidence erodes further.

An **open-weight model release** enters the ecosystem, showing advanced capabilities. While impressive, it builds on existing open-weight progress rather than representing a discontinuous leap. The model spreads quickly, but its performance aligns with incremental gains in training efficiency and access to mid-tier compute. Openweight capability rises modestly, reflecting diffusion and adoption rather than a sudden jump in underlying stock. It remains clearly behind the closed frontier.

Benchmarks from a leading lab show unusual patterns—systems solving unfamiliar tasks with minimal prompting. The lab describes the results as within expected variance. No official interpretation is offered, and the data remains preliminary.

### Political Capital Erodes

The convergence of crises fuels action, but not unity. The new directive earns no immediate sovereignty gain—completion is years off. With ai_capability rising by 1.5, a −1 decay applies under rule 10. No access event occurred, so eu_ai_sovereignty remains at 21.0, unchanged from last turn.

Political capital declines, but less severely than proposed. The portfolio charge reflects only one new measure (−3) and a priority shift (−1), totaling −4. A +2 resilience boost from partial surge delivery offsets part of the cost. Scandal and incident weigh on sentiment, but the charge is contained. eu_political_capital falls to 38.0.

Public sentiment drops on job fears and cyber chaos, remaining under pressure. The emergent labour displacement wave continues to grow, with mid-level technical and administrative roles seeing accelerated automation. ASML faces mounting pressure from both U.S. and EU actors over servicing machines in China and domestic supply chain fragility, creating a flashpoint over industrial sovereignty.
