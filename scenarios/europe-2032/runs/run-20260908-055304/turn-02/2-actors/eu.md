## In practice
We are treating the convergence of the cyber_major_incident and openweight_frontier_release as a structural turning point: the offensive tools are now model-generated, and the capability to run them is spreading beyond any single state’s reach. At the same time, our own industrial base is not moving fast enough. The InvestAI Gigafactories are still on paper, and the Tech Sovereignty Package’s reliance on market incentives has not closed the gap. We cannot harden what we do not control, and we cannot control what we cannot run. This turn, we are shifting from encouragement to compulsion.

The Commission will table the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive under Article 114 TFEU, framing AI compute as a single market issue with systemic security implications. It mandates that all member states designate at least one “AI Sovereignty Zone” with pre-approved environmental and grid connection plans, fast-tracked within 90 days. These zones will only qualify for enhanced cohesion and Just Transition Fund disbursements if they commit to hosting infrastructure where at least 70% of capacity is contractually bound to EU public and industrial users, with kill switches and audit rights under EU law. We are offering an additional €30bn in state aid allowances under the new Crisis Innovation Framework, but only for projects that deliver sovereign capacity by Q4 2028.

Resistance is expected from member states reliant on foreign hyperscaler investment, particularly Ireland and the Nordics, who fear losing data centre jobs. To counter this, we are pairing the directive with a guarantee: any sovereign facility receiving EU support must hire locally and share training pipelines with national digital academies. ENISA will co-develop security certification standards with the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, ensuring that sovereign does not mean weak. This is not about building the most compute — it’s about building the compute we can still switch on when everything else goes dark.
