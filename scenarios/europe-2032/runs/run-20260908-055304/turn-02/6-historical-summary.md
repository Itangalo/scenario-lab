The EU shifted focus from the Emergency Resilience Surge to the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive, invoking Article 114 TFEU to advance AI infrastructure and tech sovereignty, backed by €30bn in state aid and fast-tracked permitting. However, legal concerns and opposition from Ireland, Sweden, and Finland over investment risks led to a six-month review, delaying implementation and preventing immediate gains in sovereignty. A major AI-optimized ransomware attack disrupted municipal systems across three member states, exposing vulnerabilities and further eroding public trust. An open-weight model release circulated widely, reflecting incremental progress rather than a breakthrough, while closed models continued to outpace it. Preliminary lab results showed anomalous AI capabilities, but remained unverified. Amid rising AI capability and stagnant sovereignty, political capital declined to 38.0 due to the new directive’s costs, partially offset by resilience gains. Public sentiment weakened further under job displacement fears and cyber instability. ASML emerged as a flashpoint in industrial sovereignty debates, caught between U.S. and EU pressures over China operations and supply chain resilience. EU AI sovereignty remains at 21.0, with no immediate increase.
