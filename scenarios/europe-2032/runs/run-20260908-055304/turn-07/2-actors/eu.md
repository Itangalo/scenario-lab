## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity to act independently in a world where capability outpaces control — and now, with runtime verification as a condition of that independence, not a complement  
- Grounds: The original commitment evolved last turn to reflect that sovereignty requires not just hardware but control. This turn, the failure to operationalise verification despite the safety breakthrough proves that anchoring compute is not enough: if the EU cannot observe and enforce limits on agent behaviour in its own critical infrastructure, it does not own the systems running on its soil. The commitment must now explicitly bind runtime assurance to sovereignty, or the gap between possession and agency will widen unchecked.

## New measure  
**Runtime Transparency Mandate**  
Require all AI systems above 45.0 capability operating in EU critical infrastructure to provide real-time telemetry to the Sovereign AI Run-Time Assurance Programme, with penalties for obfuscation or non-compliance.

`Category: 2 (Transparency and reporting)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +3–5 (via mandatory telemetry enabling detection of drift), eu_ai_sovereignty +2 (via enforceable right to observe), resilience +1 (via early warning)  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, covering energy, healthcare, transport, and public administration

## Priority  
**Sovereign AI Run-Time Assurance Programme** — because telemetry access without enforcement is a suggestion, not a right, and with member states already undercutting the Union’s line, we must anchor our verification authority in binding transparency before any further erosion of control.

## In practice  
We are closing four major measures this turn: the InvestAI Gigafactories, the Tech Sovereignty Package, the ASML Alternative Lithography Initiative, and the Emergency Labour Transition Facility. The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package have delivered binding legal frameworks and fast-track permitting zones, but private capital has not yet fully materialised due to export-linked uncertainty. The ASML Initiative has seeded three alternative lithography pathways in Leuven, Dresden, and Grenoble, though none are near production scale. The Labour Facility has processed 18,000 claims and is being evaluated for extension. These closures free €12bn in annual political capital burden, but we are reinvesting it immediately into enforcement and telemetry access for the Run-Time Assurance Programme.

The new Runtime Transparency Mandate will be proposed under Article 16 of the AI Act, with enforcement tied to market access and critical infrastructure licensing. We will negotiate its adoption in the Internal Market Council, backed by a coalition of France, Germany, and the Nordic states, and funded through a reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme’s unused adoption budget. Resistance is expected from hyperscalers and from member states with bilateral deals that exempt foreign operators from local oversight. We are offering compliance grace periods and audit credit for early adopters, but we will invoke the Anti-Coercion Instrument if any state blocks implementation by granting foreign providers immunity. This is not about slowing innovation — it is about ensuring that when an agent acts in our grid, our hospitals, or our rail networks, we are not blind to its intent. Without this, sovereignty is performance, not power.
