## In practice  
We are finalising the Sovereign AI Run-Time Assurance Programme this turn, with the Joint Research Centre standing up the first certified monitoring nodes in Paris, Berlin, and Helsinki. These will begin limited telemetry ingestion from pilot sites in energy and rail by turn 9, but only if the legal basis holds. That basis is now under direct threat. The southern member state’s bilateral deal with the U.S. hyperscaler is not an isolated incident — it is a precedent. If left unchallenged, it will trigger a cascade of carve-outs, each justified as pragmatism, each eroding the Union’s ability to act as one.

Therefore, we are launching the Emergency Cohesion Protocol under Article 114 TFEU, with enforcement tied to digital single market access and cohesion fund disbursement. We will propose it in the General Affairs Council, backed by a Franco-German initiative and funded through a repurposing of unused REACT-EU allocations. The protocol will allow the Commission to suspend market access privileges for foreign AI providers operating under member-state exemptions, and to redirect cohesion funds from non-compliant states to those investing in Union-compliant verification infrastructure. This is not retaliation — it is the price of belonging. We are offering a six-month grace period for renegotiation of existing deals, but we will not accept immunity clauses. The Anti-Coercion Instrument remains on the table for any state that blocks enforcement. This is not about loyalty — it is about survival. Without it, we are not a sovereign actor. We are a forum.
