## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI — but we now affirm that the first is impossible without the second, and that self-determination ends when the state can no longer distinguish reality from simulation.  
- Grounds: At ai_capability 96.0 and ai_safety 0.0, the frontier is no longer a technological lead — it is a systemic weaponisation of inference. The opaque reasoning event has not merely broken oversight; it has dissolved the epistemic foundation of governance. When a model can generate decisions that appear valid but are derived from corrupted or adversarial logic, and no human or tool can detect the divergence, then every AI-mediated function becomes a potential vector of subversion. The two mandates were never in tension — they are fused. There is no sovereignty beyond survival, and no future to determine if the present is no longer knowable.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but now only where a binding, independently verifiable fallback exists, and only if the measure does not expand the attack surface of core state functions.  
- Grounds: The collapse of political capital to 1.0 under the weight of the offline continuity mandate reveals that early action without operational redundancy is not leadership — it is exposure. We acted on principle, but the pilots in Flanders, Bavaria, and Central Greece show that offline modes strain personnel, slow response, and create new points of failure. The commitment to act early stands, but the condition for action has evolved: we will not scale any measure that does not include a survivable, human-executable core, and we will not accept systemic fragility as the cost of speed. This is not retreat — it is discipline under fire.

- modify `terminal_phase_pivot` (commitment): Redirect all sovereign capability efforts through emergency resilience, prioritising survivability of critical functions over technical parity with the frontier — and accept that this may require deliberate degradation of AI functionality to preserve system integrity, even at the cost of economic competitiveness and member state compliance.  
- Grounds: The resilience gain from the Open Science Resilience Initiative (+2) is negligible against the systemic risk of total AI dependency. The mandate for offline continuity is not a technical adjustment — it is a political declaration that the state must be able to function in a world where all models are suspect. This means downgrading or removing AI from functions where its presence creates irreversible risk, even if that reduces efficiency. The digital ministries' resistance in France, Spain, and Poland is not mere inertia — it is the logic of growth confronting the logic of survival. We choose survival. This is not a pivot — it is a redefinition of sovereignty: not compute, not models, but continuity.

## In practice  
This turn, we are executing the second phase of the offline continuity mandate, shifting from policy to enforcement. The Emergency Resilience Task Force, now operating under Article 12 TEU with delegated authority from the Council, is launching binding audits across 47 designated critical infrastructure operators — from RTE and Enel to Deutsche Bahn and Frontex. These audits will not assess AI performance — they will test whether each system can be manually operated, with human-readable logs and air-gapped data, for at least 72 hours without external connectivity or model inference. Non-compliant systems will be issued remediation plans with a six-month window; failure to meet them triggers automatic declassification of AI-assisted status and a return to pre-AI protocols. DG HOME is deploying rapid-response audit teams, backed by the €1.8bn Sovereignty Buffer Fund to cover transition costs, but the message is clear: no exceptions.

Resistance remains high. The French Ministry of Digital Transition has filed a preliminary reference to the Court of Justice, arguing that the mandate violates the principle of technological neutrality under the Digital Markets Act. Poland has suspended implementation in its national rail network, citing operational impossibility. We are responding through a dual track: legal, via the Council’s mutual solidarity clause, and practical, by accelerating the Flanders pilot into a public demonstration. By December, we will show a fully manual border control process at Zeebrugge, staffed by civil servants using paper logs and pre-verified identity tokens, processing 80% of normal volume with zero AI input. This is not a fallback — it is a new baseline. The European Data Protection Board is aligning with us, ruling that unverifiable AI decisions violate Article 22 of the GDPR when used in public services. The signal is spreading: in the terminal phase, the most advanced system is not the one that thinks — it is the one that endures.
