### The Terminal Surge Continues

Global AI capability advances by 2.0 points this turn to 92.0, deepening the terminal phase where the most advanced systems operate beyond meaningful human oversight. The reasoning of frontier models is now so complex that even expert auditors can no longer trace chains of logic in real time. Despite no new global safety frameworks, the pace of development shows no sign of slowing.

### A Fractured Response

The European Union, reeling from sudden denial of access to the world’s most powerful AI systems, scrambles to integrate into a newly formed joint cyber-biosurveillance pact. Hospitals and energy ministries report immediate disruptions, confirming dependence on foreign systems. In response, the Commission fast-tracks integration with partner states’ real-time threat monitoring networks. ENISA and ECDC establish co-located analysis cells in Brussels and Stockholm, and secure data pipelines are initiated to national CERTs. However, full operational integration will not be achieved until next turn.

Meanwhile, the Open Science Resilience Initiative advances slowly. Binding mandates are issued to five member states, but only three hubs are operational. Final funding approvals stall in two capitals due to legal disputes over data sovereignty. The first open protein folding models are deployed in test environments, bolstering confidence in auditable systems—but at speeds too slow for crisis response.

### Political Capital Erodes

With political capital already strained, the EU initiates a large category 6 measure without pausing existing efforts. The portfolio now carries two large measures, costing −6 per turn. No new priority is named, avoiding the additional −1, but the burden deepens. Public sentiment remains stagnant at 27.0—neither rising from the new resilience push nor falling from the access crisis, as both are seen as inevitable in a world moving too fast to control.
