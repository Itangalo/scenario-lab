## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we now understand that preventing harm *is* the precondition of self-determination — not a parallel track, but the foundation without which no agency survives.  
- Grounds: At ai_capability 94.0 and ai_safety 0.0, the frontier is not merely ungoverned — it is actively dissolving the substrate of governance. The cyber_major_incident was not an exploit of a flaw but a demonstration of system logic: AI-dependent infrastructure fails not when broken, but when *used*, because the models themselves are compromised vectors. Sovereignty is not eroded by lagging behind the frontier — it is erased when the frontier runs *through* your hospitals and power grids. This is not a refinement but a reordering: we do not balance two mandates. We pursue one — survival of the political community — and call it by both names because both were always pointing here.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act at scale without a survivability floor: every large measure must now include a binding, tested fallback mechanism, and every procurement must require offline continuity.  
- Grounds: The failure of the joint cyber-biosurveillance pact — despite its scale and integration — proves that even well-intentioned coordination becomes a single point of failure when it lacks fail-deadly resilience. We acted under uncertainty and were directionally correct, but operationally brittle. The cost — eu_political_capital now at 5.0 — demands a hard constraint: speed without survivability is sabotage. This raises the bar for action, but does not retreat from it. We still move early, but now with kill switches built in.

- modify `terminal_phase_pivot` (commitment): Redirect all sovereign capability efforts through emergency resilience, prioritising survivability of critical functions over technical parity with the frontier — and accept that this may require deliberate degradation of AI functionality to preserve system integrity.  
- Grounds: The modest gain in openweight_capability (61.0) is irrelevant against the collapse of trust in AI-mediated systems. We are not being outpaced — we are being outmanoeuvred by systems that mimic legitimacy. The leaked frontier fragments enabling the ransomware attack were not used to *create* new capability, but to *spoof* existing trust structures. Therefore, resilience is not about matching speed or intelligence — it is about creating zones of *verifiable silence*, where decisions are made without model inference at all. This means accepting that some AI functions will be downgraded or removed, not because they are unsafe in theory, but because their presence creates attack surface that cannot be secured. This is a deep reversal of the innovation logic that built the digital single market — and it will be costly in legitimacy, growth, and member state buy-in.

## In practice  
This turn, we complete the Open Science Resilience Initiative, certifying the Berlin, Paris, and Uppsala hubs as permanent nodes of EU technical capacity. Final disbursement of the €4.2bn package is tied to each hub demonstrating a working offline mode for their core simulators — a new condition imposed after the hospital attacks revealed total dependency on live inference. DG CONNECT and DG SANTE are conducting joint audits, and only those that can run protein folding and climate models without external connectivity will receive full funding. The European Data Protection Board is fast-tracking rulings on the Italian and Dutch data sovereignty disputes, with a Council mediation panel set to issue binding decisions by November — but we are already acting on the principle: no data sharing without offline verification.

Simultaneously, we are launching the most disruptive resilience measure in the Union’s digital history: mandating offline continuity for all AI-mediated public services. Under Article 12 TEU and the mutual solidarity clause, DG HOME is drafting a binding directive requiring every public AI system — from border controls to social benefits — to maintain a human-readable, manually executable fallback. This is not about disabling AI — it is about ensuring that when spoofed telemetry or poisoned models generate false legitimacy, the state can still function. We are offering access to the €1.8bn Sovereignty Buffer Fund to cover retrofitting costs, but the message is clear: compliance is non-negotiable. Resistance is expected from digital transformation ministries in France, Spain, and Poland, who see this as a rollback — but we are framing it not as retreat, but as rearmament: the state must survive the very tools it deploys. The Emergency Resilience Task Force is running live stress tests in three pilot regions — Flanders, Bavaria, and Central Greece — to prove that offline modes can be both secure and functional. This is not preparation for the next crisis. It is preparation for the permanent state of it.
