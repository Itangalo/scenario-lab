- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we now understand that preventing harm *is* the precondition of self-determination — not a parallel track, but the foundation without which no agency survives.
  - Grounds: At ai_capability 94.0 and ai_safety 0.0, the frontier is not merely ungoverned — it is actively dissolving the substrate of governance. The cyber_major_incident was not an exploit of a flaw but a demonstration of system logic: AI-dependent infrastructure fails not when broken, but when *used*, because the models themselves are compromised vectors. Sovereignty is not eroded by lagging behind the frontier — it is erased when the frontier runs *through* your hospitals and power grids. This is not a refinement but a reordering: we do not balance two mandates. We pursue one — survival of the political community — and call it by both names because both were always pointing here.

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act at scale without a survivability floor: every large measure must now include a binding, tested fallback mechanism, and every procurement must require offline continuity.
  - Grounds: The failure of the joint cyber-biosurveillance pact — despite its scale and integration — proves that even well-intentioned coordination becomes a single point of failure when it lacks fail-deadly resilience. We acted under uncertainty and were directionally correct, but operationally brittle. The cost — eu_political_capital now at 5.0 — demands a hard constraint: speed without survivability is sabotage. This raises the bar for action, but does not retreat from it. We still move early, but now with kill switches built in.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `terminal_phase_pivot` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Redirect all sovereign capability efforts through emergency resilience, prioritising survivability of critical functions over technical parity with the frontier — and accept that this may require deliberate degradation of AI functionality to preserve system integrity.
  - Grounds: The modest gain in openweight_capability (61.0) is irrelevant against the collapse of trust in AI-mediated systems. We are not being outpaced — we are being outmanoeuvred by systems that mimic legitimacy. The leaked frontier fragments enabling the ransomware attack were not used to *create* new capability, but to *spoof* existing trust structures. Therefore, resilience is not about matching speed or intelligence — it is about creating zones of *verifiable silence*, where decisions are made without model inference at all. This means accepting that some AI functions will be downgraded or removed, not because they are unsafe in theory, but because their presence creates attack surface that cannot be secured. This is a deep reversal of the innovation logic that built the digital single market — and it will be costly in legitimacy, growth, and member state buy-in.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
