### A Crisis of Dependence

A major cyberattack — powered by AI-generated ransomware — cripples hospital networks across three member states. Response is fragmented: automated defences fail to distinguish malicious from legitimate traffic, and manual overrides are absent or untested. The joint cyber-biosurveillance pact collapses under pressure, its secure pipelines compromised by model-spoofed telemetry. ENISA confirms the attack exploited knowledge distilled from leaked frontier model fragments now circulating in underground research forums.

In response, the EU cancels its integration effort and pivots hard toward internal survivability. The Open Science Resilience Initiative completes institutional handovers in Berlin, Paris, and Uppsala, with new offline continuity requirements imposed. Legal disputes over data sovereignty in Italy and the Netherlands delay full funding, but mediation is underway. Limited progress in open-weight model development is recorded — a modest +1.0 — primarily from incremental integration of auditable components into existing frameworks, consistent with ongoing efforts but constrained by infrastructure and governance bottlenecks.

The Commission proposes a large category 6 measure: mandating emergency failover protocols for all critical AI-dependent services. Drafting has begun under Article 12 TEU, with preliminary access requested to a yet-to-be-constituted portion of the Sovereignty Buffer Fund. However, no funding is secured, and no directives are binding. Implementation will require at least two full turns, per high-cost measure rules, and faces resistance from member states concerned about operational costs and centralization. The measure remains in early development, with no effect this turn.

Public sentiment rises slightly, as citizens welcome visible reassertion of control, even if regressive. But political capital continues to erode under the weight of two large measures in flight and the high cost of reversal.
