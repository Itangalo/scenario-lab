## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we no longer believe these can be pursued separately: without preventing harm, there is no future to determine.
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that crippled hospital networks in three member states — and the emergent_frontier_leak confirm that uncontrolled capability growth is not an external risk but an active dissolution of sovereignty. At ai_capability 92.0 and ai_safety 0.0, the frontier is not just opaque — it is adversarial by default. Our capacity to act depends on whether essential systems survive at all, not whether we lag behind the curve. Resilience is no longer a complement to sovereignty; it *is* sovereignty now. This is not a shift in priorities but a collapse of the boundary between them: if AI undermines the state’s basic functions, no amount of industrial policy or access negotiation will restore it.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer pretend that speed alone can compensate for fragility.
  - Grounds: The failure of two large category 6 measures to meaningfully raise resilience or public sentiment, despite full execution, shows that scale and urgency are not enough when the underlying architecture is brittle. The joint threat integration delivered telemetry pipelines but not decision latency reduction; auditable models ran too slowly to stop cascading failures. We acted under uncertainty and were wrong in timing and form — not in direction. The cost has been extracted in political capital, now at 6.0, and trust remains broken. We hold the commitment to act early, but now bind it to a new constraint: no measure this size will proceed without a live failover test or resilience stress benchmark.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **retire** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **add** `terminal_phase_pivot` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Redirect all sovereign capability efforts through emergency resilience, prioritising survivability of critical functions over technical parity with the frontier
  - Grounds: The Open Science Resilience Initiative produced valuable tools but failed its core test: they were too slow to deploy during the cyber_major_incident. Meanwhile, the emergent_frontier_leak proves that even fragments of frontier models are diffusing into unregulated environments, accelerating offensive asymmetry. At this stage, "sovereign technical agency" cannot mean matching capability — that race is lost — but ensuring that EU systems *persist* under attack. Our credibility now rests not on what our models can do, but on whether anything we run still functions when the next wave hits. This is a reversal of the previous commitment’s logic, not a refinement, and it carries cost: markets will read this as retreat, and some member states will resist abandoning the prestige of frontier pursuit. But the hospitals that went dark this turn did not fall to invasion — they fell to automation without assurance.
