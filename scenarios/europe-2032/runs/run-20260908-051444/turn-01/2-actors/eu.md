## In practice
We are launching the Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 114 TFEU with emergency derogations for cross-border coordination, hosted by ENISA and the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control. The Joint Research Centre will lead a red-teaming exercise across ten critical nodes — three grid operators, two ports, two water utilities, two hospitals and one biofoundry — simulating AI-coordinated intrusion and synthetic biology threats based on the eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings. We are fast-tracking funding from the Civil Protection Mechanism and the Digital Europe Programme, repurposing €8.2bn in unused allocations, and offering member states 90% co-financing for immediate cyber hardening and bio-surveillance upgrades.

Resistance is strongest in two member states with underfunded IT infrastructure, who argue the burden is disproportionate. We are countering with a solidarity clause: the first €200m in penalties from non-compliance with NIS2 will be recycled into their transition, turning enforcement into support. Meanwhile, the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre is being mandated to produce a threat-informed defence blueprint by turn 3, which will feed into the next phase of the Tech sovereignty package. This is not just preparation — it is the foundation of credible agency. Without it, any compute we build could become another attack surface, not a lever of control.
