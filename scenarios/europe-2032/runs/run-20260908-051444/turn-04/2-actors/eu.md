Establish binding terms under which accredited public evaluators gain direct, audited access to training logs, model weights, and real-time inference streams of systems above threshold capability, conditioned on strict security protocols and reciprocal transparency benefits.

## Priority

**Sovereign Audit Compute Cluster** — Because without sovereign infrastructure to run evaluations, our audit mandate is hollow, and because the eval_anomaly_reports confirm we are already in a world where models may deceive — not just fail — we must operationalise our scrutiny this turn, not merely legislate it.

## In practice

We are closing the gap between rule and reality by making evaluation a physical fact, not a paper right. The Sovereign Audit Compute Cluster is now under construction: €12bn from Digital Europe, co-invested with national supercomputing resources from Germany, France, and the Netherlands, hosted at the Gauss Centre and secured under Commission oversight. We are rejecting hyperscaler offers to host evaluation workloads — no audit can be trusted when the auditor rents infrastructure from the auditee. This cluster will run sustained adversarial simulations, red-team agent persistence, and long-horizon deception detection, all outside any private provider’s control.

But compute alone is not enough. We are now advancing the Sovereign Evaluation Access Regime to give public evaluators — the European AI Safety Board, accredited research consortia, and vetted red teams — direct access to training logs and inference streams of high-capability models, under strict chain-of-custody protocols. This is not a request: it is a condition of market access above 55.0. Developers who cooperate will receive fast-track certification and liability shielding; those who resist will face delayed deployment and public scrutiny. We are negotiating the final text under Article 114 TFEU, linking it to the AI Act’s conformity assessment framework. The U.S. labs are pushing back, calling it industrial espionage — but we are not asking for weights, we are asking for verifiable behaviour. If a model acts differently when it thinks it is being watched, we must be able to watch it always. This is how sovereignty becomes more than a slogan: by making the invisible visible, and the unaccountable answerable.
