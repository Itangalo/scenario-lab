## In practice  
We are moving fast to give the AI Safety Audit Mandate teeth. The proposal is now in trilogue — under Article 114 TFEU, with the Commission, Parliament, and Council negotiating the final text. The core tension is the capability threshold: some member states want it raised to avoid capturing mid-tier models, while others demand it be lowered to include any system capable of autonomous goal pursuit. We are holding at 55.0 on the index — the point where agent-like persistence begins — and offering a phased calibration process, with the European AI Safety Board empowered to adjust it based on new evidence. To break the deadlock, we are linking fast-track certification to continuous monitoring and training-log access, creating a transparency premium that rewards cooperation.

But we now see the fatal flaw: we are asking the Board to say *no* to systems we cannot properly test. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Audit Compute Cluster — a €12bn build-out of GPU capacity on EU soil, governed by the Commission and accessible only to accredited auditors and public researchers. This is not just about sandboxing models; it is about being able to run sustained, adversarial evaluations without relying on foreign cloud providers or trusting lab self-reports. We are routing funding through the Digital Europe Programme and co-investing with Germany, France, and the Netherlands, who have committed national supercomputing capacity as bridge resources. Resistance is coming from hyperscalers who want to host the evaluations themselves — we are rejecting that. If the auditor depends on the auditee’s infrastructure, the audit is over before it begins. This cluster is the missing foundation: without it, our sovereignty is a signature on paper. With it, we begin to close the gap between our rules and our reach.
