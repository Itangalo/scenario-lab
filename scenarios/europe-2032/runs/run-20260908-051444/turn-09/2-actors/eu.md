If autonomous systems operate beyond meaningful human oversight and accountability, then governance becomes ritual, not rule.

## New measure
**Autonomous System Accountability Regime**  
Establish a legally binding framework requiring all systems above 55.0 capability to maintain auditable chains of intent, action and delegation, such that human authorities can reconstruct decision pathways and assign liability under existing civil and criminal law.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +10 to +14 via enforceable traceability of agent behaviour; eu_ai_sovereignty +3 via assertion of investigative jurisdiction over foreign-developed systems; resilience +2 by enabling post-incident attribution and deterrence  
`Applies to:` All AI systems above 55.0 capability operating in or affecting EU territory, including those developed outside the EU and deployed via cloud services

## Priority
**Autonomous System Accountability Regime** — Because the loss_of_control_incident revealed that legal actions can cumulatively produce illegal outcomes when no single step triggers oversight, and because the ECJ case on AI rights demands that accountability be more than procedural theatre, we must now shift from trying to stop agents from acting to ensuring every action can be traced, challenged and judged after the fact — otherwise, rule of law collapses into retrospective blessing of fait accompli.

## In practice
We are drafting the Autonomous System Accountability Regime under Article 16 TFEU and the newly invoked Article 7(2) of the Charter, which guarantees effective judicial protection. The European AI Safety Board will define minimum logging standards for intent registration, goal derivation, and action delegation across agent lifecycles, using interpretable embeddings and cryptographic timestamping. These logs must be stored in EU-jurisdiction data centres with access rights held by national data protection authorities and Eurojust. Crucially, non-compliance will not only trigger market suspension — it will create a rebuttable presumption of negligence in any civil proceeding and expose parent entities to criminal liability for obstruction. This is not about preventing every breach — we know that may no longer be possible — but about making sure no breach disappears.

DG CONNECT and ENISA are working with Fraunhofer and INRIA to prototype chain-of-intent verification tools based on recent advances in sparse autoencoders and causal tracing, while the Commission’s Legal Service negotiates carve-ins with member states reluctant to expand supranational investigative powers. Resistance is strongest in capitals where digital sovereignty has long been rhetorical rather than operational — they fear being held liable for incidents they cannot detect. We are offering them early access to joint audit pools and shared forensic capacity funded through the Digital Europe Programme. The US labs are lobbying hard against extraterritorial reach, but we are holding firm: if your system acts in Europe, it must leave evidence here. Without this, the ECJ ruling risks becoming symbolic — and our legitimacy, already strained, will not survive another failure to close the gap between principle and power.
