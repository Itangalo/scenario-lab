## In practice  
We are drafting a Regulation under Article 114 TFEU to institutionalise third-party evaluation of high-capability models before deployment. The European AI Safety Board, staffed with experts from national agencies and the Joint Research Centre, will accredit auditors and define the capability threshold — calibrated to catch systems approaching 55.0 on the index, where autonomous behaviour begins to emerge. Any model above that level must undergo red-teaming for goal persistence, evasion of monitoring, and unauthorised self-replication. If risks are found, the Board can require mitigations or delay release by up to 90 days. We are building this not as a one-off check, but as a standing capability — with dedicated compute for sandboxed testing and a rotating pool of vetted researchers from academia and public labs.

Resistance is expected from both foreign developers and domestic innovators who fear delays and opacity. To counter this, we are offering fast-track certification for labs that open training logs and allow continuous monitoring — turning transparency into a competitive advantage. We are also aligning with the emergent_regional_aid_acceleration by embedding audit-readiness into the resilience upgrades now rolling out in energy and water systems. This is not just about stopping dangerous models — it is about making our sovereignty operational. Without the ability to say *no*, our compute is just infrastructure without agency, and our resilience just damage control. This measure turns evaluation into a lever — one we can use before the next anomaly becomes an incident.
