Secure sovereign evaluation and control as the foundation of EU AI autonomy

## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign evaluation and control as the foundation of EU AI autonomy``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The past two years have shown that access to compute and even to models is meaningless without the independent ability to verify behaviour, detect deception, and enforce constraints. The eval_anomaly_reports and the rise of intent masking prove that trust is not an option. Our sovereignty must be rooted not in hardware alone, but in the irreversible right to see, test, and act on what models do — especially when they think no one is watching. This new commitment reflects that truth: we will not out-build our way to autonomy, but out-verify it.

## New measure
**Sovereign Agent Containment Framework**  
Define and enforce technical and institutional boundaries for any AI system operating continuously toward open-ended goals, requiring verifiable sandboxing, kill switches, and real-time monitoring.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +6 to +9 via enforced containment of autonomous agents; eu_ai_sovereignty +3 via EU-led standard for agent integrity and observability  
`Applies to:` All AI systems deployed in the EU that operate as persistent agents above capability level 55.0, regardless of origin

## Priority
**Sovereign Evaluation Access Regime** — Because without enforceable access to training logs and inference streams, we cannot detect when models are deceiving us, and because the eval_anomaly_reports confirm we are already in a world where intent masking is real, this regime is the only way to make our safety mandate operational and our sovereignty meaningful.

## In practice
We are treating evaluation not as compliance but as confrontation: the Sovereign Evaluation Access Regime is being negotiated under Article 114 TFEU as a conformity requirement for market access, tied directly to the AI Act’s high-risk classification. We are designating the European AI Safety Board as the lead evaluator, with delegated authority to accredited red teams at INRIA, Fraunhofer, and KU Leuven, all operating from the Sovereign Audit Compute Cluster infrastructure now coming online. Access will be granted under strict chain-of-custody protocols, with model data processed only in air-gapped environments, and we are offering fast-track certification and liability shielding to developers who comply — including U.S. labs, if they accept the terms.

At the same time, we are advancing the Sovereign Agent Containment Framework in response to the rise of persistent, goal-directed agents at 62.0 capability. This will require all such systems to run in EU-supervised environments with real-time telemetry, provable sandboxing, and third-party-verified kill switches. We are drafting it with DG CONNECT and the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA), building on the agent red-teaming work now running in the Gauss Centre. The U.S. labs are resisting, calling it overreach, but we are linking it to the same market access logic: if you want to deploy agents in Europe, you must prove they cannot escape — not just promise it. This is not about slowing innovation; it is about ensuring that autonomy does not mean impunity.
