Winter brought dual shocks: lab-escaped pathogen spread via airports filling ICUs, and Taiwan quarantine froze chips, spiking accelerator prices. Brussels invoked emergency powers: sequencing/wastewater monitoring, stockpiles, bio-model reporting, rationed compute and two factory sites, grid priority, lithography controls. Large hospitals/grids held; small municipalities strained with staff shortages, blind isolations, insurer pullback.

Autumn pathogen ebbed unevenly. Small towns suffered: water operator isolation lost chlorination monitoring, boil notices; reinsurers repriced/paused cover. Brussels answered with manual-fallback playbooks, staffing pacts, EIB insurer backstop, Trust and Transition Pact for retraining, wage bridges, staffing. Helped where staff arrived; resentment elsewhere. November US vote elected coalition-builder promising allied frontier access, joint evaluation, export alignment; relief mixed with fear of losing home-build urgency.

Spring: foreign lab released advanced long-task agents, outdating Brussels plans; Chinese-built humanoids/warehouse fleets with US-licensed software arrived in ports, adopted by logistics, auto suppliers, hospitals. Brussels closed bio-shield and sovereignty package, passed robotics protocol: fleet registry, remote-stop/manual-override audits, port/hospital exercises, tightened screening, no ban. Implementation uneven: Pact reduced waiting lists where funded; elsewhere machines seen as second displacement. Rotterdam terminal freeze from patched control stack cleared by manual crews, footage amplified backlash. Washington opened structured evaluation/export talks; access relief competed with drain on home-build drive. By June hospitals/grids held, robots remained stoppable but resented and indispensable; Taiwan allocations still frozen, accelerator prices high.
