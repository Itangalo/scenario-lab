Winter brought lab-escaped pathogen via airports and Taiwan quarantine freezing chips, spiking accelerator prices. Brussels imposed emergency bio-surveillance, compute rationing, factory sites, grid priority, lithography controls. Large systems held; small towns strained.

Autumn pathogen ebbed; small towns lost chlorination monitoring, faced boil notices and insurer pullback. Brussels responded with fallback playbooks, staffing pacts, EIB backstop, Trust and Transition Pact for retraining and wage bridges — uneven relief. November US vote brought coalition-builder promising allied access and joint evaluation.

Spring: foreign long-task agents and Chinese-built humanoids/warehouse fleets with US software arrived, widely adopted. Brussels closed bio-shield/sovereignty package, passed robotics protocol with registry, remote-stop audits, exercises, screening, no ban. Implementation uneven; Rotterdam freeze cleared manually amid backlash. Washington opened evaluation/export talks, dulling home-build drive. By June hospitals/grids held, robots stoppable but resented, chips still frozen.

Second half: Lyon and Magdeburg factory shells completed with grid live but empty — Taiwan allocations still frozen, prices high, no European model ready. Municipal pact closed, effective only where wage-bridge funds arrived. August preprint claimed genome model produced viable human-infecting design guidable by non-expert — contested but categorically new. Commission ordered mandatory reporting of high-uplift bio models, identity-checked genome-tool access, synthesis checks, reactivated wastewater/sequencing stockpiles; labs cited vague thresholds, firms warned of offshoring. Washington reviewed paper via joint channel without sharing internals. Robotics exercises in Rotterdam, Antwerp, Hamburg proved fleets stoppable via remote-stop/manual crews, but footage also fed resentment; unconfirmed dockworker leaflets and vandalism clips circulated. By December hospitals and grids held, robots remained.
