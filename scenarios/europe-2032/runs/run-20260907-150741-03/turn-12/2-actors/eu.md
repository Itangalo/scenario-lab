**Priority:** Launch the Component Integrity Sprint

## In practice

This turn, we are stepping back from the edge. The Sovereign Reliability Sprint has collapsed in practice: Athens never went live, Ljubljana was bypassed, Marseille underperformed. The lights didn’t just go out — they flickered and died mid-demonstration. The Frontier Access Guarantee finishes this turn, but with Washington’s export controls tightening and Canada still silent, the access we secured is evaporating. We are not being locked out — we are being left behind.

So we are not scaling up. We are scaling down — to the level where we can still control the variables. The Component Integrity Sprint will run in closed environments at ENISA and three national labs, testing only three things: can we prove a model hasn’t been tampered with? Can we run inference without leakage or corruption? Can we trigger a failover without human intervention? These are not flashy, but they are necessary. We are using red teams from Estonia and Finland to simulate adversarial conditions, and we will publish the results — failures included. No dashboards, no press conferences, no political incentives. Just proof.

We are doing this through the Digital Ministers’ Council, under Article 12 of the Digital Markets Act as legal basis, with €180m redirected from the defunct Emergency Trust Restoration Initiative. The money is small, the ask is narrow, but it is all we can afford. If we cannot prove these components work in isolation, we have no business deploying them in public. We are not rebuilding trust yet. We are rebuilding the floor.
