## Statement changes

* `- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.`  
  - Trigger: The cyber_major_incident and the uncontrolled leap in ai_capability to 74.0, occurring without warning or containment, have demonstrated that acting under uncertainty is no longer sufficient — speed without control is self-defeating.  
  - Grounds: The Union remains committed to early action, but the collapse of ai_safety to 5.0 and the total depletion of eu_political_capital mean that errors now cascade into systemic failure. We can no longer afford to act broadly on intuition. From this turn forward, we will act under uncertainty only when the intervention is *contained, reversible, and bounded* — a narrower, more disciplined form of commitment. The principle stands, but its scope must contract to match our capacity to absorb failure.

* `- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI continuity to ensure critical public functions remain operational during systemic shocks`  
  - Trigger: The Emergency Trust Restoration Initiative has failed to gain traction in Athens and Marseille due to lack of political capital and local resistance; the Sovereign AI Core Programme remains non-operational, and no member state believes a 90-day alternative can be delivered.  
  - Grounds: The commitment to operational continuity under duress remains valid, but it cannot be fulfilled through centralized mandates or unilaterally imposed infrastructure. With eu_political_capital at 0.0 and public sentiment at rock bottom, the path forward is not enforcement, but *demonstrated reliability*. The commitment is now reinterpreted to mean: we will ensure continuity not by decree, but by proving sovereign AI works — first in narrow, high-impact domains, then by scaling what holds. The goal is unchanged; the theory of change has shifted from compulsion to credibility.

* `- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement will now be conditional on the operational readiness of the Sovereign AI Core Programme, and we will not cut off a member state from critical capabilities unless we can offer a viable, secure alternative within 90 days. We act to defend the regime, not to punish into helplessness.`  
  - Trigger: Greece has announced it will bypass EU channels to procure direct access to a U.S.-based frontier model for border security, citing the failure of the Ljubljana pilot and lack of EU delivery.  
  - Grounds: The Union cannot enforce compliance when it cannot provide. The principle of no free rides remains, but with the Sovereign AI Core Programme still non-operational and no 90-day alternative available, enforcement is impossible. The commitment is now suspended in practice: we will not penalize member states for seeking alternatives while we fail to deliver our own. This is not a reversal of principle, but an admission of incapacity — and a warning that the regime is fracturing.

## Priority

**Priority:** Launch the Sovereign Reliability Sprint

## In practice

This turn, we are shifting from mandate to proof. The Continuity Assurance Framework has formally concluded, but its authority did not land: two member states refused certification, and the Thessaloniki drill revealed that even those who complied do not believe the systems work. The cyber_major_incident confirmed that threats are now autonomous and fast — but our defences are still manual and slow. The Frontier Access Guarantee continues, but Washington’s export_control_escalation looms, and Canada may not renew its SLA. We are holding the line, but barely.

So we are launching the Sovereign Reliability Sprint — not as a new initiative, but as a repurposing of the failed Emergency Trust Restoration Initiative. The same €1.8bn is redirected, the same three sites — Marseille, Ljubljana, Athens — but now with tighter metrics, public scorecards, and weekly reporting through the EAAD. We are not asking for trust. We are offering evidence. The stack is locked at 62.0 capability — auditable, not frontier — and all decisions are logged. We are working through the Digital Ministers’ Council to secure buy-in, offering early access to SACP-70 as incentive. The lights went out. Now we are showing the fire can be contained.
