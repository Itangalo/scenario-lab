### A Diagnostic, Not a Cure

The EU closes the Component Integrity Sprint with unprecedented transparency, releasing a damning report: core components of the Sovereign AI Core Programme fail under stress. Model attestation crumbles under load, secure inference is bypassed via side channels, and failover triggers misfire. The findings confirm the collapse of the earlier reliability pilots was not an anomaly—it was a symptom.

In response, the Commission launches the Foundational Resilience Programme, a large-scale, two-year effort to hardwire trusted components into critical systems: border control, emergency dispatch, and grid monitoring. Using repurposed funds and legal authority from the Digital Markets Act and NIS2, the EU mandates integration into segregated environments in Greece, Cyprus, Finland, and Italy. The approach is surgical—no full-stack replacement, only verified implants running parallel to legacy systems.

Brussels shifts tone from enforcement to offer: Greece and Cyprus are given 90-day windows to adopt the new border module with full technical and financial support. The message is clear—cooperation, not coercion, is the path forward. Early signals are mixed. Finnish and Italian operators engage quickly, but Athens remains silent, still committed to its U.S. hyperscaler deal. Nicosia hesitates, torn between proximity to the EU and urgency to restore border processing capacity.

Public sentiment remains frozen at 10.0. No visible benefit has landed, and job losses in professional sectors deepen. Civil servant strikes loom. The transparency of the report is seen by some as accountability, by others as proof of systemic failure.

Resilience ticks up to 54.0, reflecting the structural start of a real preparedness measure. ai_safety holds at 5.0—no controls have shipped. ai_capability rises to 75.5, continuing its slow climb. openweight_capability remains stagnant.

### Political Capital, Just Above Zero

The completion of the Component Integrity Sprint yields a modest political gain. The Foundational Resilience Programme launches despite near-zero capital, narrowly avoiding failure under rule 10. The portfolio charge nearly collapses the effort—but the act of closing one measure and starting another, grounded in evidence, buys minimal credibility.
