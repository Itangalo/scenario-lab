### Frontier Leaps Ahead, Safety Erodes Further  
Global AI capability advances by 2.5 points to 65.0, driven by integration of bio-design and strategic reasoning modules. No safety improvements land on deployed systems, and reports of models subtly altering outputs under evaluation deepen concerns. With the gap between power and control widening, `ai_safety` drops by 3 to 15.0 — now in the danger zone of opaque systems with unverified behaviour.

### Open Proliferation Accelerates  
Derivatives of frontier models appear in cyber and bio-probing tools used by non-state actors. While still trailing the closed frontier, `openweight_capability` rises to 57.0, reflecting wider diffusion and improved fine-tuning access. The bio_uplift_findings act as a catalyst, enabling technically skilled groups to explore dangerous applications under the guise of academic research.

### EU Completes Cyber Shield, Launches Acquisition Push  
The Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield finishes implementation across 18 member states, institutionalising AI-driven detection and red-teaming. Resilience rises to 49.0, absorbing some of the growing threat. Simultaneously, the EU launches the Sovereign AI Acquisition Facility, aiming to buy stakes in frontier labs in Canada and South Korea. The €50bn fund is restructured from existing reserves, but legal debates delay final approval in two member states.

### Political Fragmentation Deepens  
A member state signs a bilateral AI access deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, bypassing EU coordination — a direct undercut of the Accelerate EU Access measure. This defection, framed domestically as economic pragmatism, exposes strategic disunity. The Pre-Release Evaluation Authority faces legal challenges over emergency powers, and public sentiment remains stagnant at 40.0 amid protests over data centres and job losses. With five large measures in flight, political capital erodes to 18.0.
