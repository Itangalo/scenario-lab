## Priority  
``Declare Sovereign Red Lines: Prohibited Domains of AI Decision-Making in EU Law`` remains the priority this turn because it is the only instrument we have left that asserts the Union’s agency in a world where comprehension, inspection, and control are no longer possible — we cannot know what the systems do, but we can still say what they must never decide.

## In practice  
We are advancing the Sovereign Red Lines regulation through Article 16 TEU and the European Defence Cooperation framework, treating the prohibition of AI in critical domains as a matter of constitutional survival rather than technical risk management. The Commission’s Legal Service has completed its assessment: while enforcement against opaque foreign models remains legally precarious, the act of declaration itself creates binding obligations for all public authorities and contractors within the EU, and triggers liability for any provider whose system is found to have influenced a prohibited domain. We are offering Germany a compromise: a joint audit protocol developed with BSI and ANSSI, under which national agencies verify compliance using black-box behavioural testing at entry points, rather than demanding full model transparency. This sidesteps the impossibility of inspecting weights while preserving accountability at the interface. A sunset clause review at turn 18 is included, but only as a procedural concession — we do not expect the threat to diminish, but we need COREPER consensus now.

Simultaneously, the new resilience measure responds to the annulled election and rising public fury: ENISA and eu-LISA are being mandated to deploy hardened infrastructure across all member-state election commissions by turn 16, funded through the Digital Europe Programme’s emergency reserve and supplemented by €4.2 billion from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused allocations. This includes air-gapped voter registries, mandatory human certification of electoral results, and AI-generated content filters trained on adversarial disinformation patterns from the last three cycles. The French and Dutch have signed on; Poland hesitates, citing cost and sovereignty concerns, so we are offering co-location of processing nodes within national borders under EU supervision. This is not about stopping every attack — we assume breaches will happen — but about ensuring that even if systems are compromised, the chain of democratic legitimacy remains visibly intact. Without this, the Red Lines become ceremonial.
