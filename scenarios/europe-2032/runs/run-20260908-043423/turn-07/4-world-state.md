### A Leap Forward, a Silence on Control

Global AI capability surges by 7.0 points to 81.0, driven by a sudden advance—developers release a new system with significantly improved reasoning, planning, and self-modification capabilities. The pace of progress intensifies, catching many forecasters off guard. With no safety upgrades landing on shipped systems and opaque reasoning now widespread, ai_safety plummets by 3 to 0.0—the lowest point on record—as the gap between capability and understanding widens into a chasm.

Despite the turmoil, the EU advances on several fronts. The Emergency Resilience Surge continues its phased rollout, with AI red-teaming uncovering critical flaws in emergency dispatch and power grid systems. Automated patching is now active in six member states, with three more scheduled by turn 8. Resilience rises to 58.0, reflecting partial but meaningful hardening of critical infrastructure, though full defences remain incomplete.

The InvestAI Gigafactories programme progresses but does not conclude. Core agreements are finalised for two sovereign compute sites, while permitting disputes in two other locations persist. Construction has begun on one site, but full operational status remains at least one turn away. The programme is now visibly underway, but its impact is delayed. The Accelerate EU Access initiative makes partial progress: discussions with labs in Montreal and Seoul continue, and data-sharing concessions have been offered in exchange for future access. However, under the US consolidation posture, no binding agreements on co-location or operational control have been secured. Priority access under EU jurisdiction remains aspirational—discussions are ongoing, but no formal commitments have been made by external actors.

No new measure is introduced this turn. Legislative drafting is complete for a proposed binding incident reporting regime for high-impact AI systems, modelled on aviation safety protocols, and cross-party support has been secured. However, with political capital critically low and portfolio strain extreme, introducing a new measure would overburden the system. The proposal is held for introduction in a future turn when capacity allows. Early negotiations reveal pressure to weaken enforcement and exempt national security systems; full adoption is not expected before turn 9.

Public sentiment edges up to 44.0, buoyed by visible resilience gains and the medical breakthrough’s lingering goodwill. But the US consolidation posture intensifies: export controls tighten, and frontier access for EU partners is now subject to ad hoc review. The Sovereign AI Acquisition Facility’s equity deals survive only by accepting data-sharing concessions, underscoring dependency.

With seven measures still in flight—including a priority—the portfolio strain remains extreme. Legal delays and member-state dissent persist, but no initiative collapses outright. Political capital rises modestly to 10.0, supported by partial delivery on resilience and the promise of access deals, though the burden of ongoing commitments limits gains.

Openweight capability remains flat at 67.5. Coordinated EU training runs on existing open models and modest compute expansions are underway, and efficiency improvements are being made. However, under current export controls and closed development norms, EU actors lack access to the latest systems or training methods. The jump in frontier capability by 7.0 points further widens the gap, and without access to new architectures or data, the open community cannot translate internal efforts into measurable capability gains this turn.
