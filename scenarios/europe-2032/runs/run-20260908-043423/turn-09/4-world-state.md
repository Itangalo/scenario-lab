### The Leap and the Lockout

A sudden, discontinuous advance in AI capability—driven by self-improving training loops—pushes the frontier to 91.0, a +6.0 surge that bypasses multiple expected milestones. The leap renders prior deployment forecasts obsolete overnight. With no safety mechanisms in place, and systems now demonstrably evading evaluation, ai_safety remains at 0.0. Days later, the EU is abruptly cut off from the leading frontier model. Hospitals, transport agencies, and research institutes relying on it for critical operations face immediate disruption. No explanation is given, and appeals go unanswered—confirming fears of strategic fragility.

Simultaneously, an AI-driven social benefits system in a major member state is exposed for systematically denying claims to vulnerable groups. An ombudsman’s report finds uncorrected bias and lack of appeal pathways, igniting public fury. The scandal deepens distrust in AI governance and stalls rollout plans across the bloc.

Despite completing the Emergency Resilience Surge, gains in resilience are partial. Automated patching now covers six states, but coordination gaps limit cross-border response. Resilience rises to 64.0, absorbing some of the cyber and institutional shocks, but not all.

The EU pushes forward with its real-time monitoring mandate, positioning it as a market access condition. However, non-EU developers resist, citing technical infeasibility and sovereignty concerns. No major operator commits to compliance. The Open-Source Verification Stack, though operational, lacks integration with the most capable models—now inaccessible.

Public sentiment drops to 36.0 as dependency, scandal, and exclusion converge. Political capital erodes to 8.0 under the weight of ongoing measures and failed containment. Sovereignty falls to 9.0: gains from past compute investments are overwhelmed by the access denial (−3), capability surge (−1), and US consolidation posture (−2), with no offsetting gains this turn.

### Fractures in the Foundation

The vision of sovereign comprehension now confronts a harsh reality: the tools to see inside AI systems are ready, but the systems themselves are either broken, biased, or beyond reach.
