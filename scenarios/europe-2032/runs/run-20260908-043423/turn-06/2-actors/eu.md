## In practice
We are in the second phase of the Emergency Resilience Surge. ENISA has activated binding mutual aid obligations under Article 222 TFEU, deploying rapid response teams to the three affected member states, where AI-driven red-teaming is now probing municipal software stacks in real time. Automated patching agents are being rolled out across Tier 1 systems, with full deployment targeted within 90 days. The Civil Protection Mechanism has released €18bn of the €30bn fund, and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre is coordinating a live dashboard of vulnerabilities closed — not for public release, but to show member states that action is synchronized and effective. This is no longer about preparation: it is about proving the Union can act as one when systems fail.

At the same time, we are shifting from legal negotiation to operational delivery on sovereignty. The Sovereign AI Acquisition Facility has finalised its first two equity deals: a 49% stake in the Montreal-based Introspect Lab and a 35% share in Seoul’s Strategic Reasoning Institute, both under EU legal jurisdiction with data sovereignty clauses and board-level veto rights on export or remote access. The Anti-Coercion Instrument has been pre-notified to host states as a deterrent, and we are preparing to publish the transparency agreements by mid-year. This is not access — it is ownership. But we know that ownership without understanding is hollow. That is why we are launching the Open-Source Verification and Safety Stack: a suite of tools hosted on EU sovereign cloud infrastructure that enables vetted researchers to run behavioural audits, extract reasoning traces, and validate safety claims without requiring full model release. It will be interoperable with the Pre-Release Evaluation Authority’s framework — now legally established, though stripped of permanent emergency powers — and will feed into a new EU-wide incident reporting system we are building under category 2 next turn. We are acting under fire, but we are not acting blind.
