## In practice  
We are executing the *Distributed Control Nodes* initiative under continued political strain, but with renewed leverage from the **middle_power_coalition**. In France and Germany, we are integrating the nodes directly into national cyber defence commands, using the Digital Europe Programme to fund dual-use civilian-military red teams. In Poland and Spain, we are reframing the nodes not as enforcement but as mutual defence infrastructure, offering reciprocal access to coalition audit data in exchange for hosting. ENISA has finalised the interruption protocol, and Frontex is deploying mobile inspection units to border-linked data centres — but the real test is whether we can enforce beyond our borders.

The *Emergency Continuity Hubs* have now been activated in all member states except Poland, where legal challenges persist. We have used bilateral emergency funding under Article 122 TFEU to secure participation in Greece and Italy, and the first live cross-sector war game — simulating a cascading failure in rail signalling and hospital triage — was conducted in October. The exercise revealed critical gaps in model interpretability, but also proved that cross-agency coordination is possible under duress. The hubs will now transition into permanent crisis architecture, their authority codified under the revised Critical Entities Resilience Directive.

This turn, we are launching the audit trail mandate through a combination of market access rules and coalition alignment. The Commission is drafting a delegated act under the AI Act to condition market access on audit trail registration, while we are negotiating mutual recognition of audit standards with the middle_power_coalition. The Netherlands and South Korea have already agreed in principle; Canada is holding out for reciprocity on chip access. We are using the **supply_chain_coercion** moment to argue that if the US can weaponise chokepoints, the EU must build its own — not over hardware, but over visibility. The legal basis is fragile, the backlash from open-source communities will be fierce, and public sentiment may dip — but with *ai_safety* at 0.0 and recursive systems at 85.0, we cannot afford to govern only what we own. We must govern what moves.
