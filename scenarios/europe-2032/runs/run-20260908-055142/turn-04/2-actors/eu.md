## New measure
`Reclaim the Investment Vacuum`  
We launch an emergency EU acquisition and build-out programme to purchase stranded AI infrastructure projects and repurpose them under EU legal and energy sovereignty, beginning with three shuttered hyperscale sites in Ireland, Finland, and Poland.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +10–15, resilience +5–10, public_sentiment ±0 (risk of backlash from national asset seizures vs. gain from visible action)  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with binding conditions on foreign hyperscalers seeking access to EU markets

## Priority
**Reclaim the Investment Vacuum** — because the ai_investment_collapse has shattered the private capital model underpinning our sovereignty strategy, and this is the only moment in six years when physical AI infrastructure is available at fire-sale prices; if we do not act now to anchor it under EU control, the next frontier leap will leave us further behind, not closer.

## In practice
We are acting under Article 122 TFEU and the Temporary Crisis Instrument to justify emergency intervention in energy-intensive infrastructure markets. The Commission, backed by the EIB and the European Defence Fund, is preparing to invoke conditional market access: any foreign hyperscaler seeking to operate AI infrastructure in the EU must now accept binding commitments on data sovereignty, energy sourcing, and workforce training in exchange for grid access and tax incentives. Three sites — two in Ireland, one in Finland — have already halted construction due to funding withdrawal; we are offering to purchase them at 40% of projected value in exchange for 20-year service agreements under EU jurisdiction. Poland’s stalled facility will be reactivated under the Cyber Shield’s security framework, with dual-use capacity for civilian resilience and crisis response.

Resistance is shifting: the Dutch government remains firm, but ASML engineers privately confirm that without EU service hubs, mid-node tools degrade within 18 months. We are now offering to host those hubs — not through ownership, but through binding service contracts under EU inspection, funded by redirected Tech Sovereignty Package capital. The French scandal has weakened our hand domestically, so we are pairing the acquisition push with a targeted flexicurity expansion under the Social Fund for Migration and Integration, offering retraining to displaced public-sector workers in AI audit and red-teaming. This is not a return to dirigisme — it is a controlled landing in a collapsing market, using the Union’s remaining fiscal weight to secure what the private sector has abandoned. The window is open for six months. After that, the assets will be scrapped, or bought by states with fewer scruples.
