## In practice  
We are acting under Article 356 TFEU to initiate an EU-level strategic intervention in the semiconductor supply chain, targeting ASML’s production of mid-node lithography tools and associated servicing contracts. The European Commission, in coordination with the Dutch government and the European Investment Bank, will offer a €15bn equity stake purchase in ASML’s non-EUV production lines, conditional on binding commitments that servicing, maintenance, and tool deployment decisions for EU customers are subject to joint EU-Dutch oversight. This is not nationalisation — it is co-sovereignty: we are offering Dutch industry stability, access to broader EU markets, and protection from retaliatory trade actions in exchange for shared control.

Resistance will come from both Washington and The Hague. The U.S. views mid-node tools as dual-use enablers; the Dutch government fears diplomatic rupture and market retaliation. We are offering a carve-out: the EU will not block U.S.-requested restrictions on Chinese access, but any such restriction must be jointly reviewed and formally adopted by an EU-US Critical Tech Coordination Forum, which we will propose in parallel under category 8. We are also fast-tracking a complementary measure — not a new one — under the Tech sovereignty package to accelerate permitting for domestic lithography maintenance hubs in Germany, France, and Belgium, reducing long-term dependency. This is not just about tools — it is about decision rights. If we cannot act here, then eu_ai_sovereignty is a fiction, and our two-year commitment unravels at the physical layer.
