## New measure  
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators**  
Establish mandatory intrusion detection and cross-border incident response protocols for energy, water, and port operators, with AI-driven threat modelling and red-teaming.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases resilience by +15–20 points; reduces likelihood of cascading failure after cyber intrusion; begins closing detection gap revealed by cyber_test_shot  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, ENISA, national CSIRTs, and relevant agencies in third countries for coordination

## Priority  
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators** — because the cyber_test_shot proved existing defences are blind to advanced persistent threats, and resilience is now the only layer between the Union and systemic disruption, regardless of sovereignty gains down the line.

## In practice  
We are activating Article 13a of the NIS2 Directive to mandate AI-enhanced intrusion detection systems across electricity transmission system operators, major water utilities, and container port authorities in all member states. This is not a guideline — it is a binding requirement, backed by the authority of the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA), which will be expanded by 300 staff and given a €4.2bn envelope from the Digital Europe Programme and Recovery and Resilience Facility top-ups. We are deploying red-teaming units modelled on the US Cyber Solarium Commission, using AI agents trained on known adversarial tactics, to simulate attacks every quarter. The first exercises will run in Q1 2027.

The resistance is real: some member states, especially those with recent history of national cyber sovereignty assertions, are framing this as an overreach. We are offering co-location of data processing in national hubs, shared command over red-team findings, and a Council-level Cyber Resilience Board to preserve national input. Germany and the Nordic states are on board; Poland and Hungary are conditionally supportive if co-financing is adjusted. The clock is against us — the next intrusion may not be discovered by accident. This measure will cost political capital, but less than a blackout would.
