## New measure
`Operational Continuity Hubs`  
We establish EU-staffed, EU-operated technical hubs to maintain, monitor, and intervene in critical AI infrastructure, beginning with the reclaimed hyperscale sites and extending to post-lithography maintenance and red-teaming.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +12–18, resilience +8–12, ai_safety +5–10 (indirect, through monitoring and intervention capacity)  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with mandatory staffing and access rights for EU-certified operators at all facilities receiving EU market access or energy support

## Priority
**Reclaim the Investment Vacuum** — because this turn is the last in which these sites remain available for acquisition at fire-sale prices, and failure to close the deal now will leave the EU without physical leverage in the next capability leap — but we are now acting not just to own, but to operate, which is why the new measure under Category 5 begins immediately to staff and secure what we reclaim.

## In practice
We are finalising acquisition agreements with Ireland and Finland under Article 122 TFEU and the Temporary Crisis Instrument, offering 40% of projected value in exchange for 20-year service agreements under EU jurisdiction and mandatory staffing by EU-certified technical operators. The Commission, backed by the EIB and the European Defence Fund, is preparing legal notices that tie grid access and tax incentives to binding commitments on data sovereignty, energy sourcing, and workforce training — now including direct recruitment into the new Operational Continuity Hubs. Poland’s site remains under review, but we are offering to integrate it into the Cyber Shield’s expanded framework, with dual-use capacity for civilian resilience and crisis response, conditional on governance reforms.

With *Secure the Lithography Lifeline* now canceled, we are redirecting its capital and political weight toward hosting EU-maintained service hubs for mid-node tools, funded through the Tech Sovereignty Package and staffed by engineers trained under the new Operational Continuity Hubs programme. This is not ownership, but it is control: ASML engineers confirm that without regular maintenance by trusted teams, tool degradation will cut effective supply by 40% within 18 months. We are offering to be that team — not as owners, but as operators with legal standing and intervention rights.

Domestically, the French AI scandal and job losses continue to erode public trust, so we are pairing infrastructure acquisition with a targeted expansion of the Social Fund for Migration and Integration, offering retraining in AI audit, red-teaming, and operational monitoring to displaced public-sector workers. This is not a return to dirigisme — it is a controlled landing in a collapsing market, using the Union’s remaining fiscal weight to secure not just hardware, but the human and institutional capacity to operate it when the next crisis hits. The window is open for six months. After that, the assets will be scrapped, or bought by states with fewer scruples — and we will be left with promises, not power.
