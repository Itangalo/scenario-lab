## Priority

## In practice
We are acting under Article 356 TFEU to initiate an emergency intervention in the semiconductor supply chain, targeting ASML’s mid-node lithography tools — the backbone of current AI hardware production. The European Commission, backed by the European Investment Bank, has offered a €15bn equity stake in ASML’s non-EUV lines, conditional on joint EU-Dutch oversight of servicing, deployment, and export decisions for EU customers. This is not nationalisation, but co-sovereignty: we offer Dutch industry long-term stability, access to EU markets, and insulation from unilateral U.S. export edicts in exchange for shared decision rights.

Resistance remains fierce. The Hague fears diplomatic rupture; Washington sees overreach. We have proposed a parallel forum — the EU-US Critical Tech Coordination Forum — to jointly review any U.S.-requested restrictions on China, offering consultation in exchange for predictability. Domestically, we are fast-tracking maintenance hubs under the Tech sovereignty package in Germany, France, and Belgium to reduce long-term dependency. But the window is narrow: if we cannot convert resistance into partnership this turn, the physical foundation of EU AI sovereignty will remain foreign-controlled, and the two-year commitment will stand on sand.
