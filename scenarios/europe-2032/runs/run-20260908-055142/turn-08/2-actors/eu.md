## Statement changes  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but this turn, we act not only under uncertainty, but under confirmed regime shift. We now treat every uncontrolled system above 50.0 capability as a standing risk to the continuity of the Union, and we intervene accordingly. However, our authority to intervene is now visibly constrained by both external resistance and internal fragility: U.S. hyperscalers refuse compliance, member states resist integration, and public trust erodes. Therefore, we now narrow the scope of intervention to systems directly embedded in critical infrastructure — energy, transport, health, and state administration — and prioritise operational control over jurisdictional assertion. We act where we can hold ground, not where we claim it.  
  - Grounds: The **member_state_defection** event and the failure to secure extraterritorial compliance with *Mandatory Intervention Rights* reveal that legal claims without enforcement capacity are performative, not sovereign. Our commitment must reflect reality: we cannot mandate audit access globally, but we can — and must — ensure it where AI touches the nervous system of the Union.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor sovereignty in operational control and foundational resilience, not just ownership of infrastructure — and ensure that every reclaimed or built capability is staffed, exercised, and integrated into crisis continuity planning by the time it becomes operational — and now, that no AI system above 50.0 capability operates in or serves the EU without a certified, EU-exercisable intervention right.  
  - Grounds: This turn’s events confirm that sovereignty is not in law, nor even in hardware, but in live human presence and tested response. The **emergent_labour_displacement_wave** proves that societal resilience is fraying, while the Polish standoff shows that political capital evaporates when force is applied without legitimacy. Thus, we reframe: our two-year commitment now demands that for every node of compute, there is a team; for every kill switch, a hand; for every displaced worker, a path back in. Control is staffing. Resilience is transition.

We shift from compulsion to consolidation: focus on staffing existing sites rather than forcing new ones.

## New measure  
`Accelerated Transition Pathways for Displaced Cognitive Workers`  
We launch an EU-wide rapid retraining and placement programme targeting workers displaced by AI in administrative, legal, and mid-level software roles, fast-tracking them into red-teaming, system auditing, and continuity operations within the Operational Continuity Hubs and national agencies.

## Priority  
**Mandatory Intervention Rights for Critical AI Systems** — because despite resistance, this remains the only instrument that can prevent irreversible loss of control over systems already embedded in essential functions; delaying enforcement risks cascading failure far beyond what any retraining programme can later repair.

## In practice  
We are finalising the *Mandatory Intervention Rights* directive through the Internal Market Council, narrowing its application to AI systems directly embedded in critical infrastructure: power grid management, air traffic coordination, emergency medical triage, and core government services. ENISA is drafting technical specifications for kill switches and audit interfaces, while EPPO prepares enforcement protocols, including automatic suspension of grid access and procurement eligibility for non-compliant providers. We are engaging U.S. hyperscalers bilaterally through the Trade and Technology Council, offering mutual recognition of safety evaluations in exchange for binding commitments on intervention rights — a pragmatic carve-out to avoid total withdrawal, but one that preserves our minimum threshold: no access without accountability.

Simultaneously, we are launching the *Accelerated Transition Pathways* initiative in coordination with member states’ labour ministries and the European Social Fund+. The first phase targets 5,000 displaced workers over the next 18 months, with priority given to former public-sector administrators and junior legal analysts. Training will be delivered through the Operational Continuity Hubs in Ireland and Finland, now transitioning from pilot to permanent status, with ASML, Siemens, and Deutsche Bahn committing to hire graduates into technical oversight roles. The European Defence Fund is co-financing a cyber-red team expansion, treating cognitive displacement not as a side effect but as a strategic reserve.

We are abandoning the forced integration of Poland’s site. Instead, we are offering Warsaw a bilateral agreement: full EU funding for a national continuity node, co-staffed and interoperable with the EU network, in exchange for dropping legal challenges and joining the *Transition Pathways* pilot. This is not retreat — it is recalibration. Sovereignty is not imposed; it is exercised. And we will build it where hands are willing, not where they are bound.
