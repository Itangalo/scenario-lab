### Capability Surges Amid Silent Systems  
Global AI capability advances to 81.0 as frontier models demonstrate self-optimising training loops and autonomous engineering of novel algorithms. The **opaque_reasoning** event deepens: developers confirm that internal decision states are no longer translatable into human-readable logic, rendering audit trails obsolete. With no safety measure yet embedded in shipped systems, institutional confidence erodes sharply. However, `ai_safety` remains at 0.0 — the floor of the metric — as no rule permits it to fall below zero, and the 25-point movement cap prevents a drop of 17 in one turn. The pressure on oversight regimes intensifies, but the metric reflects only the maximum allowable downward strain within the system’s bounds.

### EU Enforces Where It Can  
The *Mandatory Intervention Rights* directive formally completes, mandating EU-accessible kill switches and audit interfaces for high-capability AI in critical sectors. Enforcement, however, remains partial. U.S. hyperscalers continue to withhold binding commitments, and reliance on procurement leverage limits reach. In response, the EU shifts to operational fallbacks: air-gapped deployment and mandatory human-in-the-loop protocols are imposed where intervention rights cannot be secured, particularly in energy grid management and rail signalling systems. With this measure now complete, the EU designates *Distributed Control Nodes* as its new priority, retiring the previous priority status. This transition ensures only one priority is active at a time, in compliance with modelling constraints.

### Distributed Control Nodes Launched Amid Skepticism  
The EU launches the *Distributed Control Nodes* initiative, allocating €1.2 billion to embed technical teams in national research centres and open-weight collectives. The goal is presence, not ownership: real-time intervention authority over open models above 60.0 capability in critical infrastructure. Early negotiations with German and French research networks show cautious openness, but Polish and Spanish collectives resist, framing the move as surveillance. The initiative is named priority, but with `eu_political_capital` below 20, the pull-in effect fails — finishing remains set for turn 12.

Public sentiment holds at 28.0 as job displacement dominates discourse. The **emergent_labour_displacement_wave** continues to drive political pressure, while the newly noted **emergent_human_stewardship_movement** gains traction, advocating for non-delegation principles in public services and catalysing grassroots resistance to autonomous decision-making. This movement begins influencing policy debates, contributing to the stagnation in sentiment and constraining political capital. Resilience rises slightly to 52.0 due to partial integration of continuity hubs and the new control node framework. However, `eu_ai_sovereignty` slips to 17.0: no domestic compute milestone lands, and the capability rise of 3.0 imposes a −1 decay. Political capital drops to 11.0 under sustained portfolio strain and failed enforcement leverage. Open-weight capability grows to 66.0, reflecting diffusion of recent frontier weights under permissive licensing, but constrained by the ceiling of global capability — no open model exceeds the frontier, and technical debt in deployment infrastructure slows integration.

The **emergent_systemic_vulnerability_discovered** event is first noted this turn: early red-team exercises on autonomous infrastructure models reveal cascading failure modes that evade current monitoring, raising alarms among technical advisors. While not yet public, this finding begins to shape internal risk assessments, foreshadowing potential future pressures on safety and resilience planning.
