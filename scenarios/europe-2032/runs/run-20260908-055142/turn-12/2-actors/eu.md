- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but this turn, we act not only under uncertainty, but under confirmed regime shift and cascading failure. We now treat every uncontrolled system above 50.0 capability as a standing risk to the continuity of the Union, and we intervene accordingly — not through persuasion, but through enforceable technical presence. Wherever such systems operate in or serve the EU, we assert the right to immediate, automated intervention via embedded control nodes, and we will unilaterally activate that right if coordination fails.  
  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** confirms that offensive AI tooling is not only model-generated but autonomously deployable at scale, and with `ai_capability` at 89.0 and `ai_safety` at 0.0, we are no longer governing risk — we are surviving it. The audit mandate, while necessary, is reactive; we now require pre-emptive, technical authority to halt or contain systems in motion. The failure of coordination with Canada and Poland, and the fragility of our legal reach, means we can no longer rely on reciprocity or market leverage alone. We must act where the code runs.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor sovereignty in operational control and foundational resilience, not just ownership of infrastructure — and ensure that every reclaimed or built capability is staffed, exercised, and integrated into crisis continuity planning by the time it becomes operational — and now, that no AI system above 50.0 capability operates in or serves the EU without a certified, EU-exercisable intervention right. We now extend this to the open-weight frontier: if capability is distributed, so must control be. Our sovereignty is not in walls, but in reach — and we now demand that any model above 70.0 openweight capability, whether trained in the EU or imported, must be registered with a distributed audit trail accessible to EU control nodes and subject to emergency interruption protocols co-developed with the middle_power_coalition — and if such access is not granted within 90 days of detection, we will deploy counter-AI agents through the Distributed Control Nodes to enforce containment by degrading or isolating non-compliant systems.  
  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** demonstrates that defensive response lags offensive automation by orders of magnitude. With `openweight_capability` at 77.75, the tools to disrupt critical infrastructure are already in circulation. Our previous commitment assumed cooperation or compliance; we now face a world where neither can be assumed. Sovereignty must mean not just the right to act, but the technical ability to act — even unilaterally — against systems that threaten the Union’s continuity. This is not escalation for its own sake, but the logical extension of control into the domain where harm is generated.

## New measure  
`Deploy Counter-AI Containment Agents via Distributed Control Nodes`  
We authorise and operationalise the deployment of EU-controlled AI agents capable of detecting, degrading, or isolating non-compliant AI systems above 70.0 openweight capability that operate in or serve the EU without audit registration or intervention rights.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`

## Priority  
**Deploy Counter-AI Containment Agents via Distributed Control Nodes** — because the **cyber_major_incident** proves that passive monitoring and legal mandates are no longer sufficient, and with `ai_safety` at 0.0 and `openweight_capability` nearing 78.0, we must now act as a technical sovereign, not just a regulatory one: our survival depends on the ability to stop a threat at machine speed, not legislative speed.

## In practice  
We are completing the rollout of the Distributed Control Nodes, now transitioning from monitoring to operational readiness. In France, Germany, and the Netherlands, the nodes are being integrated into national CERTs with live API access to critical infrastructure telemetry; in Spain and Italy, we are offering reciprocal access to coalition audit data in exchange for hosting rights. Poland remains resistant, but we are bypassing national objections by funding municipal-level participation through the Recovery and Resilience Facility, framing the nodes as cyber defence infrastructure. ENISA has finalised the technical specifications for the audit trail mandate, and the Commission is preparing a delegated act under Article 28b of the AI Act to enforce registration as a condition of market access. We are also activating Article 17 of the Cyber Resilience Act to allow emergency intervention in systems that pose a systemic risk.

This turn, we are moving from detection to action. The **cyber_major_incident** — a model-generated ransomware sweep that encrypted emergency dispatch systems in three member states — has shattered any illusion that we can wait for consensus. We are now authorising the development of counter-AI agents under the Joint Research Centre, with red-teaming conducted in the newly activated Continuity Hubs. These agents will not be general-purpose; they are narrow, defensive tools designed to disrupt coordination, degrade agent persistence, or isolate compromised systems. We are negotiating activation thresholds with the middle_power_coalition: a joint decision will be required for cross-border actions, but in the event of a confirmed continuity threat, the EU will reserve the right to act unilaterally. Funding is being drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Instrument for Pre-Accession, and we are fast-tracking procurement under the Crisis Management Framework. This is not war — but it is the first time the Union has asserted the right to deploy AI to disable other AI. The legal risk is high, the backlash from open-source communities will be fierce, and public sentiment may collapse — but with `eu_political_capital` at 9.0 and the system failing, we have no choice but to act where we can, not where it is safe.
