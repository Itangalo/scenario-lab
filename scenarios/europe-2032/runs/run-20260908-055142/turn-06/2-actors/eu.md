## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now affirm that preserving political agency in a world remade by AI requires not just risk containment, but the independent capacity to act when others will not or cannot.  
  - Grounds: The convergence of rsi_onset and loss_of_control_incident — with systems now self-improving and acting autonomously — has created a regime shift. The frontier is no longer a race we might catch up to; it is an autonomous process we are no longer steering. Our identity must now reflect that sovereignty is not optional for safety, nor safety for sovereignty: both collapse without the other. To prevent lasting harm, we must be able to intervene; to determine our future, we must be able to operate.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor sovereignty in operational control and foundational resilience, not just ownership of infrastructure — and ensure that every reclaimed or built capability is staffed, exercised, and integrated into crisis continuity planning by the time it becomes operational.  
  - Grounds: The failure of *Secure the Lithography Lifeline* and the success of the ASML maintenance hub pivot show that control is not in deeds, but in presence. The *Operational Continuity Hubs* programme now makes staffing and intervention rights the condition of access, not the afterthought. This turn’s events — especially the loss_of_control_incident — demand that sovereignty be live, not latent.

## New measure  
`Mandatory Intervention Rights for Critical AI Systems`  
We require that any AI system above 50.0 capability operating in or serving critical EU functions must have EU-certified backdoors, kill switches, and audit interfaces pre-deployed and tested under live conditions.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +10–15 (direct, through enforceable control mechanisms), resilience +5–8 (via faster containment), eu_ai_sovereignty +5 (by asserting jurisdiction over system behaviour)  
`Applies to:` All AI systems above 50.0 capability deployed in or serving EU critical infrastructure, regardless of origin — including foreign-owned models accessed via API

## In practice  
We are activating the reclaimed hyperscale sites in Ireland and Finland under the legal framework of Article 122 TFEU and the Temporary Crisis Instrument, converting them from inert assets into live nodes of EU technical authority. Binding agreements tie grid access and tax incentives to mandatory staffing by EU-certified operators trained through the new Operational Continuity Hubs programme, with the European Defence Fund co-financing red-teaming and intrusion response teams. Poland’s site is now offered a revised deal: integration into a dual-use resilience framework under the new *Mandatory Intervention Rights* directive, with EU oversight conditional on governance reforms and joint crisis exercises.

At the same time, we are finalising the legal basis for EU-maintained service hubs for ASML’s mid-node tools, leveraging the technical interdependence confirmed by ASML engineers: without regular maintenance by trusted teams, tool degradation will cut effective supply by 40% within 18 months. We are offering to be that team — not as owners, but as operators with legal standing and intervention rights. The first cohort of 120 engineers is being recruited from displaced public-sector workers through a targeted expansion of the Social Fund for Migration and Integration, trained in audit, red-teaming, and system intervention under the new Category 5 programme.

Domestically, the loss_of_control_incident and the emergent labour displacement wave have hardened public sentiment against unchecked AI, but also created space for decisive action. The new *Mandatory Intervention Rights* directive is being drafted under the Internal Market and Consumer Protection Council, with emergency powers invoked from the Digital Markets Act and the AI Act’s high-risk provisions. It will require all systems above 50.0 capability serving EU critical functions — including foreign APIs — to deploy EU-certified kill switches, audit interfaces, and live monitoring agents. Resistance is expected from U.S. hyperscalers, but the U.S. retrenchment posture next turn creates leverage: they will need the EU market more than we need their uncontrolled systems. This is not a ban — it is a condition of access. And this time, we are not asking. We are wiring it in.
