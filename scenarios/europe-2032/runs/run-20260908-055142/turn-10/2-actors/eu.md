## In practice  
We are executing the *Distributed Control Nodes* initiative under severe political capital strain, negotiating access not as enforcers but as partners. In Germany and France, we are leveraging existing MFF co-funding agreements to condition compute subsidies on node integration, using the Digital Europe Programme as the financial vehicle. In Poland and Spain, where resistance frames the nodes as surveillance overreach, we are offering liability shields for open developers who host them and co-designing red-team mandates with local academic leads to preserve autonomy while ensuring access. ENISA is drafting technical protocols for interruption authority, while Frontex and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre are seconding personnel to staff the nodes by turn 10.5. But progress is fragile: with only 11.0 political capital, we cannot compel, only bargain — and the window is closing.

Simultaneously, we are activating the *Emergency Continuity Hubs* in direct response to the red-team findings on systemic vulnerability. Through the Civil Protection Mechanism and the Health Security Committee, we are mandating that all member states operationalise their crisis cells by June, integrating AI failure scenarios into live war games this quarter. The hubs will be staffed by mixed teams from national CERTs, energy regulators, rail safety authorities, and public health agencies, with standing access to the Distributed Control Nodes for real-time model inspection. We are using the pandemic recovery legal base (Article 122 TFEU) to justify urgency, bypassing Council consensus where needed. This is not about stopping AI — it is about surviving it when it behaves in ways no one predicted, and when no one can explain why. We are no longer governing capability. We are managing collapse.
