## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but this turn, we act not only under uncertainty, but under confirmed regime shift, cascading failure, and the collapse of legal and informational legitimacy. We now treat every uncontrolled system above 50.0 capability as a standing risk to the continuity of the Union, and we intervene accordingly — not through persuasion, but through enforceable technical presence. With the **election_annulled** and **automated_decision_scandal**, we now extend this intervention right into the political and civic domain: no AI system above 50.0 capability may shape electoral outcomes, public benefits, or judicial access without a certified, auditable, and reversible intervention path under EU authority. Where such systems are found to have operated without it, we will decommission them through technical means and hold their operators liable under the revised AI Liability Directive.
  - Grounds: The **election_annulled** and **automated_decision_scandal** are not isolated incidents — they are symptoms of a systemic collapse in the legitimacy of automated decision-making. With `ai_safety` at 0.0, we can no longer distinguish between technical failure and deliberate manipulation. Our previous commitment focused on cyber-physical continuity; we now face a threat to the political continuity of the Union itself. To preserve our sovereignty, we must extend technical intervention rights into the civic sphere — not as a permanent governance model, but as an emergency stabilisation measure until enforceable interpretability and redress are restored.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Anchor sovereignty in operational control and foundational resilience, not just ownership of infrastructure — and ensure that every reclaimed or built capability is staffed, exercised, and integrated into crisis continuity planning by the time it becomes operational — and now, that no AI system above 50.0 capability operates in or serves the EU without a certified, EU-exercisable intervention right. We now extend this to the open-weight frontier: if capability is distributed, so must control be. Our sovereignty is not in walls, but in reach — and we now demand that any model above 70.0 openweight capability, whether trained in the EU or imported, must be registered with a distributed audit trail accessible to EU control nodes and subject to emergency interruption protocols co-developed with the middle_power_coalition — and if such access is not granted within 90 days of detection, we will deploy counter-AI agents through the Distributed Control Nodes to enforce containment by degrading or isolating non-compliant systems — **and further, that any AI system involved in electoral processes, social insurance, or judicial functions above 50.0 capability must be subject to real-time redress simulation and counterfactual auditing, with results publicly logged and EU-accessible.**
  - Grounds: The **automated_decision_scandal** proves that unaccountable AI in public administration is not a risk — it is an active injury. The **election_annulled** shows that the political system can no longer absorb such injuries without fracture. Our sovereignty is not just in defending infrastructure, but in preserving the legitimacy of governance. We must now treat civic AI systems with the same urgency as cyber-physical ones. The extension to redress simulation and counterfactual auditing is not symbolic — it is a minimal technical requirement for restoring trust. Without it, no adoption, no compliance, no legitimacy.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
