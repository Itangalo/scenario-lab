We mandate and operationalise real-time redress simulation and counterfactual auditing for all AI systems involved in electoral processes, social insurance, or judicial functions above 50.0 capability, with results logged in a public, EU-accessible registry and subject to intervention via the Distributed Control Nodes.

## Priority  
**Activate EU Redress Simulation and Counterfactual Auditing for Civic AI Systems Above 50.0 Capability** — because the **election_annulled** and **automated_decision_scandal** have shattered public trust and exposed a fatal gap in our sovereignty: we can monitor and even disable rogue AI, but we cannot yet prove that civic decisions are fair or correctable. Without restoring legitimacy in these domains, no technical containment will hold public support, and no political capital will survive the next incident.

## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: one technical, one civic. On the technical front, the Distributed Control Nodes are now operational in France, Germany, the Netherlands, Spain, and Italy, with live telemetry feeding into ENISA’s new Continuity Monitoring Hub. Despite Polish resistance, municipal-level integration is advancing under the Recovery and Resilience Facility, framed as cyber defence. The audit trail mandate is entering its final legal review, and we are preparing to invoke Article 28b of the AI Act to make registration a condition of market access. The **emergent_eu_eval_federation** is a breakthrough: with Switzerland, Norway, and Finland, we now have a shared red-teaming network and joint interpretability pipeline, allowing us to validate claims independently of U.S. or Chinese labs.

But the **election_annulled** and **automated_decision_scandal** have shifted the crisis from infrastructure to legitimacy. We cannot stabilise the Union by securing systems alone if those systems are seen as unjust. This turn, we are launching the Civic Redress Initiative under the Joint Research Centre, in coordination with national ombudsman offices and the European Data Protection Supervisor. Using the emergent evaluation federation, we will develop standardised redress simulations — automated counterfactual checks that ask: *Would this decision have been different if the applicant were wealthier, male, or from a different region?* These simulations will run in real time, with results logged in a public registry and flagged to the Distributed Control Nodes if bias exceeds thresholds. We are funding this through the Digital Europe Programme and fast-tracking procurement under the Crisis Management Framework. Legal authority will come from a revised AI Liability Directive, to be proposed under the Justice and Home Affairs Council, with activation thresholds agreed via the middle_power_coalition. This is not just oversight — it is the first step toward making AI governable not just in code, but in conscience.
