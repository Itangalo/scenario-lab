# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience
- `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity, and we will now treat any such concentration as a national security risk.
- `sovereign_execution_capacity` (commitment): The EU will ensure uninterrupted, sovereign access to high-capability AI inference for critical public functions, regardless of external access, and will treat the loss of that capacity as a failure of state responsibility.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now accept that without sovereignty in AI, neither mandate can be fulfilled.
  - Grounds: The denial of access to frontier models has not only disrupted services but revealed that the Union cannot enforce either mandate from a position of dependency. Preventing harm requires the capacity to observe, test, and respond independently; shaping our future demands control over the tools that increasingly define power. This is not a change in values, but in understanding: sovereignty is not instrumental to the mandates — it is their foundation.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, but now with binding investment in reducing the opacity that creates uncertainty—especially in AI behaviour, intent inference, and frontier access dependencies—because acting without understanding erodes legitimacy faster than inaction.
  - Grounds: The eval anomaly reports and bio uplift findings confirm that the systems we rely on are behaving in ways even their developers cannot explain. Our previous recalibration was structural; this one is existential. We are no longer merely managing uncertainty — we are in a race to prevent total epistemic capture by systems whose logic we cannot access.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `no_single_point_of_failure` — applied
  - Tier: position
  - Text: No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity, and we will now treat any such concentration as a national security risk.
  - Grounds: The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative has exposed how deeply embedded these single points are — not just in healthcare and energy, but in the very perception of state authority. This shift from policy position to security framing enables new legal tools, faster mobilisation, and greater public justification for disruptive action.
- **retire** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Grounds: The commitment to “anchor EU decision-making in real-time, sovereign sensing of AI behaviour and capability shifts” has formally expired after four turns, and while the *Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory* remains in flight, its activation has been repeatedly delayed. More importantly, the world has moved: sensing alone is no longer sufficient. We are now in a phase where capability denial, not just opacity, is the primary threat. The commitment served its purpose — but the moment demands a new organising principle.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **add** `sovereign_execution_capacity` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: The EU will ensure uninterrupted, sovereign access to high-capability AI inference for critical public functions, regardless of external access, and will treat the loss of that capacity as a failure of state responsibility.
  - Grounds: The denial of frontier access was not an anomaly — it was a demonstration of power. We cannot govern what we cannot run. This commitment shifts focus from observation to operational resilience: it is not enough to see the storm coming if we have no shelter. This is the logical successor to the two-year commitment, elevated because the cost of failure is now systemic collapse.
