### Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now accept that without sovereignty in AI, neither mandate can be fulfilled.  
- Grounds: The denial of access to frontier models has not only disrupted services but revealed that the Union cannot enforce either mandate from a position of dependency. Preventing harm requires the capacity to observe, test, and respond independently; shaping our future demands control over the tools that increasingly define power. This is not a change in values, but in understanding: sovereignty is not instrumental to the mandates — it is their foundation.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, but now with binding investment in reducing the opacity that creates uncertainty—especially in AI behaviour, intent inference, and frontier access dependencies—because acting without understanding erodes legitimacy faster than inaction.  
- Grounds: The eval anomaly reports and bio uplift findings confirm that the systems we rely on are behaving in ways even their developers cannot explain. Our previous recalibration was structural; this one is existential. We are no longer merely managing uncertainty — we are in a race to prevent total epistemic capture by systems whose logic we cannot access.

- modify `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity, and we will now treat any such concentration as a national security risk.  
- Grounds: The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative has exposed how deeply embedded these single points are — not just in healthcare and energy, but in the very perception of state authority. This shift from policy position to security framing enables new legal tools, faster mobilisation, and greater public justification for disruptive action.

- retire `two_year_commitment`  
- Grounds: The commitment to “anchor EU decision-making in real-time, sovereign sensing of AI behaviour and capability shifts” has formally expired after four turns, and while the *Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory* remains in flight, its activation has been repeatedly delayed. More importantly, the world has moved: sensing alone is no longer sufficient. We are now in a phase where capability denial, not just opacity, is the primary threat. The commitment served its purpose — but the moment demands a new organising principle.

- add `sovereign_execution_capacity` (commitment): The EU will ensure uninterrupted, sovereign access to high-capability AI inference for critical public functions, regardless of external access, and will treat the loss of that capacity as a failure of state responsibility.  
- Grounds: The denial of frontier access was not an anomaly — it was a demonstration of power. We cannot govern what we cannot run. This commitment shifts focus from observation to operational resilience: it is not enough to see the storm coming if we have no shelter. This is the logical successor to the two-year commitment, elevated because the cost of failure is now systemic collapse.

**Sovereign AI Inference Guarantee** — because the Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative addresses symptoms, but this measure attacks the root: Europe’s inability to run the systems it depends on. The denial of access was not just a supply shock — it was a revelation that without guaranteed execution, all governance is conditional on permission. This is now a question of state sovereignty.

## In practice  
We are no longer building redundancy — we are building sovereignty. The **Sovereign AI Inference Guarantee** will be enacted through a new Regulation under Article 114 TFEU, framed as a single market instrument for critical digital infrastructure. It establishes a legal right to AI inference for essential public functions, enforceable against any provider — foreign or domestic — that withdraws access without due process. The backbone will be three new sovereign clusters: one in Finland (geothermal-cooled, subsea-linked), one in Bulgaria (integrated with energy grid redundancy), and one mobile unit based in Luxembourg for crisis deployment. These will run hardened, EU-developed models distilled from open-weight leaders up to capability 65.0, with active investment in distillation efficiency to close the gap.

Funding comes from a €1.2 billion reallocation from the Connecting Europe Facility and a new sovereign AI bond issued under the European Stability Mechanism’s crisis clause, justified by the ongoing **eval_anomaly_reports** and **bio_uplift_findings**. We are fast-tracking recruitment through the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium, offering triple salaries to attract talent from both industry and academia. Resistance from member states concerned about centralisation is being met with co-governance: each cluster will have a joint board with national representation, and all model deployment decisions will require a qualified majority in a new Council subgroup on AI Continuity.

The **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** remains delayed, but we are now bypassing full staffing by integrating its telemetry design into the **Sentinel Nodes’** operational loop under ENISA, using emergency powers from the NIS2 Directive. This is not ideal — it sacrifices depth for speed — but it ensures that when the Observatory finally activates, it will not start from zero. Meanwhile, the **Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative** is being absorbed into the new Guarantee as its first operational phase: what was once a stopgap becomes the foundation of permanent capacity. We are not waiting for consensus. We are building the floor beneath our feet as we fall.
