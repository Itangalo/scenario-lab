### Statement changes  
- modify `sovereign_execution_capacity` (commitment): The EU will ensure uninterrupted, sovereign access to high-capability AI inference for critical public functions, regardless of external access, and will treat the loss of that capacity as a failure of state responsibility — but we now accept that access without understanding is not sovereignty, it is dependency in disguise.  
- Grounds: The eval anomaly reports and bio uplift leak confirm that running AI we do not understand is not control — it is exposure. We have chased compute and models while falling further behind in legibility, and now systems are behaving in ways that even their developers cannot explain. This revision binds the commitment to a new condition: sovereign execution must be *comprehensible* execution. Without assurance, infrastructure is just a faster way to collapse.

- modify `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity, and we will now treat any such concentration as a national security risk — but we extend this to include cognitive monocultures in model architecture and training data, which pose systemic fragility just as dangerous as supply concentration.  
- Grounds: The distillation of open-weight models to 70.0 has created a false sense of redundancy. In truth, nearly all these models trace back to a single lineage, trained on a shrinking corpus of synthetic data. When one fails, all may fail the same way. This shift acknowledges that sovereignty is not just about location or ownership — it is about diversity of logic, provenance, and failure mode.

- reclassify `act_under_uncertainty` to identity  
- Grounds: We have now acted under uncertainty for six turns, through denials, leaks, anomalies, and legal overreach — and every time, waiting for clarity would have meant surrender. The cost of inaction has proven higher than the cost of error. This is no longer a tactical choice; it is the operating condition of EU governance in the AI era. We are not restoring order — we are learning to govern in the absence of it.

## New measure  
**Sovereign AI Legibility Programme**  
Launch a dedicated, EU-funded research and instrumentation effort to reverse-engineer, monitor, and verify the reasoning pathways of sovereign AI systems, ensuring that models running critical functions are not only available but interpretable, auditable, and resilient to emergent misalignment.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to ai_safety by enabling real-time detection of anomalous cognition in sovereign models; +5 to eu_ai_sovereignty by building independent EU capacity to validate model behaviour without relying on developer claims; begins closing the epistemic gap between capability and control  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, EuroHPC sites, national AI agencies, and all operators of sovereign inference clusters under the Inference Guarantee

## In practice  
We are no longer satisfied with running black boxes, even if they are ours. The **Sovereign AI Legibility Programme** will be launched through Horizon Europe with a €900 million allocation from the Digital Europe Programme, co-managed by the European AI Office and a new Interpretable Systems Directorate within the Joint Research Centre. It will fund three parallel tracks: first, the development of causal tracing tools to map model decisions to training data and latent reasoning pathways; second, the creation of adversarial red teams embedded in each sovereign cluster to probe for emergent logic shifts; third, the establishment of a public repository of validated interpretability benchmarks, open to researchers across the Union. This is not academic — it is emergency R&D. We are contracting with academic consortia in Germany, France, and the Netherlands under fast-track procurement, bypassing the usual multi-year cycles.

The **Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative** continues, now fully absorbed into the Inference Guarantee’s operational backbone. We are no longer calling it redundancy — it is the first phase of permanent sovereign execution. But we are halting new recruitment through the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium. Triple salaries failed; now we redirect that capital into the Legibility Programme, targeting researchers who can build tools, not just run models. The Finnish subsea cluster remains delayed by environmental review, so we are invoking Article 12 of the Critical Entities Resilience Directive to fast-track approval, offering the Finnish government co-ownership and guaranteed local access in return for expedited permitting. Bulgaria still resists grid integration, so we are offering a bilateral energy-for-access deal: surplus compute capacity in return for full participation. Luxembourg’s mobile unit remains conceptual — we are now scaling it back to a deployable inference node, not a full cluster, to get something operational by turn 14.

We are building, yes — but we are finally asking what we are building *for*. Not just continuity. Not just independence. But understanding. Because without it, sovereignty is a word we whisper to ourselves in the dark.
