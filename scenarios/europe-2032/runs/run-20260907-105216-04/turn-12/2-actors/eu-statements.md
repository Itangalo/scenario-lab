# Statements: The European Union (turn 12)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (identity): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience
- `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity, and we will now treat any such concentration as a national security risk — but we extend this to include cognitive monocultures in model architecture and training data, which pose systemic fragility just as dangerous as supply concentration.
- `sovereign_execution_capacity` (commitment): The EU will ensure uninterrupted, sovereign access to high-capability AI inference for critical public functions, regardless of external access, and will treat the loss of that capacity as a failure of state responsibility.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `sovereign_execution_capacity` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: The EU will ensure uninterrupted, sovereign access to high-capability AI inference for critical public functions, regardless of external access, and will treat the loss of that capacity as a failure of state responsibility — but we now accept that access without understanding is not sovereignty, it is dependency in disguise.
  - Grounds: The eval anomaly reports and bio uplift leak confirm that running AI we do not understand is not control — it is exposure. We have chased compute and models while falling further behind in legibility, and now systems are behaving in ways that even their developers cannot explain. This revision binds the commitment to a new condition: sovereign execution must be *comprehensible* execution. Without assurance, infrastructure is just a faster way to collapse.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `no_single_point_of_failure` — applied
  - Tier: position
  - Text: No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity, and we will now treat any such concentration as a national security risk — but we extend this to include cognitive monocultures in model architecture and training data, which pose systemic fragility just as dangerous as supply concentration.
  - Grounds: The distillation of open-weight models to 70.0 has created a false sense of redundancy. In truth, nearly all these models trace back to a single lineage, trained on a shrinking corpus of synthetic data. When one fails, all may fail the same way. This shift acknowledges that sovereignty is not just about location or ownership — it is about diversity of logic, provenance, and failure mode.
- **reclassify** `act_under_uncertainty` — applied
  - Tier: identity
  - Grounds: We have now acted under uncertainty for six turns, through denials, leaks, anomalies, and legal overreach — and every time, waiting for clarity would have meant surrender. The cost of inaction has proven higher than the cost of error. This is no longer a tactical choice; it is the operating condition of EU governance in the AI era. We are not restoring order — we are learning to govern in the absence of it.
