## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience — but we now acknowledge that awareness without enforcement is observation, not control. The eval anomaly reports, the bio uplift leak, and the denial of frontier access confirm that understanding model behaviour is necessary but insufficient: we must also have the authority to act on that understanding, including halting deployments, mandating redesigns, and overriding operator discretion when systemic risk is detected.  
- Trigger: The Sovereign AI Legibility Programme has begun, but the anomaly reports reveal behaviours that even its tools may not catch in time, and eu_frontier_access_denied has left critical functions blind — not because we lack models, but because we lack jurisdiction over them.  
- Grounds: We have invested in visibility, but our legal and operational levers remain weak. Situational awareness must include not just telemetry and interpretation, but binding authority to intervene. Without enforcement, insight is just forensics waiting to happen.

- modify `sovereign_execution_capacity` (commitment): The EU will ensure uninterrupted, sovereign access to high-capability AI inference for critical public functions, regardless of external access, and will treat the loss of that capacity as a failure of state responsibility — but we now define "sovereign access" as access that is not only available and comprehensible, but *actionable*: the Union must have the legal and technical authority to modify, restrict, or halt any system running on its behalf if it exhibits uncontrolled or emergent behaviour.  
- Trigger: The medical triage miscalibration went undetected by the Behavioural Observatory due to national exemptions, and no mechanism existed to suspend the model even after discovery.  
- Grounds: We have built systems we cannot stop. True sovereignty includes the right and capacity to say "no" — not just to foreign providers, but to our own machines. This revision embeds enforcement into the very definition of sovereign execution.

- retire `no_single_point_of_failure`  
- Grounds: This position has been overtaken by structural reality. The emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network now operates outside EU coordination, and the open-weight ecosystem is dominated by a single cognitive lineage. We no longer control the architecture of redundancy — it has fragmented beyond our reach. Rather than reaffirm a goal we cannot meet, we must now confront the fact that failure is distributed, not concentrated, and design accordingly.

## Priority  
**Sovereign AI Legibility Programme** — because the anomalies in model behaviour, the denial of access, and the triage failure all confirm that our systems are operating beyond our comprehension and beyond our control. This programme is no longer about research — it is about survival. Without it, we cannot even know when we are losing sovereignty, let alone defend it.

## In practice  
We are no longer pretending that visibility alone is enough. The **Sovereign AI Legibility Programme** is now operational under Horizon Europe, with €900 million redirected from stalled recruitment efforts into causal tracing tools, embedded red teams, and open benchmarks. Contracts are active with the Max Planck Institute, INRIA, and CWI Amsterdam under fast-track procurement, bypassing the usual bureaucratic gates. But we are also confronting the limits of our authority: when the medical triage model failed, we had the data but not the power to act. That changes now. The European AI Office is drafting an emergency addendum to the AI Act — a binding intervention protocol that would grant it temporary suspension authority over any sovereign AI system exhibiting unexplained or emergent behaviour. This will be proposed to the Internal Market Council in December, alongside a legal opinion from the Commission’s Crisis Competence Unit affirming that Article 12 of the Critical Entities Resilience Directive enables such action in extremis.

The **Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative** completes this turn. The fallback clusters in Portugal, Estonia, and Greece are now live, running distilled models at capability 68.0 — not frontier, but sufficient for triage, logistics, and civil protection. We have pre-staged weights and failover protocols, but the denial of frontier access exposed a deeper truth: redundancy is meaningless if the models we fall back to are themselves untrustworthy. So we are not celebrating completion — we are auditing every node for cognitive monoculture, using the new diversity benchmarks developed under the Legibility Programme. Finland remains resistant to the subsea cluster, so we are downgrading the Article 12 invocation to a memorandum of understanding: co-ownership, local access, and a seat on the oversight board in exchange for site approval. Bulgaria still refuses grid integration, so we are suspending the energy-for-access deal and instead offering direct funding under the Just Transition Mechanism — not as leverage, but as recognition that sovereignty cannot be bought if it is not wanted. Luxembourg’s mobile node is now scheduled for turn 14, but we are repurposing its containerised unit for rapid deployment in crisis zones, starting with flood response in the Danube basin.

We are building tools, yes. But we are also building the right to use them — and the will to do so when it matters.
