### The Shield Breached, the Stack Proposed  
A major cyber incident sweeps across public services in three member states, exploiting AI-generated zero-days in legacy software dependencies. Critical hospital systems are locked down, and municipal utilities face cascading failures. The attack is not state-sponsored in any clear chain, but forensic teams confirm the tooling was model-optimized—proof that offensive capability has outpaced defence. The EU’s Hardened Cyber Shield is still in drafting, too late to stop the damage. Public trust erodes further.

In response, the European Commission begins drafting the **EU Evaluation and Control Stack (EUCS)**, a proposed category 5 measure for real-time monitoring and override of high-capability AI in public functions. The concept gains rapid traction as a necessary response to the breach, but formal proposal is delayed by inter-institutional debate over oversight authority and compatibility with existing digital rights frameworks. No implementation begins this turn; the measure remains in preparation, with full launch expected next turn. The portfolio of active measures therefore remains unchanged: **InvestAI Gigafactories**, **Tech Sovereignty Package**, and **Hardened Cyber Shield** continue in flight, with the latter still the declared priority.

The **Hardened Cyber Shield** progresses slowly. ENISA issues preliminary baselines, and Germany and Poland accept co-design rights on failover drills, easing resistance. However, Italy still lacks a national coordinator, and retrofitting in pilot regions reveals widespread telemetry gaps—many critical systems cannot even report their status. Progress is partial and uneven.

**InvestAI Gigafactories** proceed under restructured EIB loans, and ASML-Intel pilot lines are announced. Energy-sharing pacts are legally reinforced, calming some local opposition. Yet the broader investment climate remains frozen, and sovereignty gains are offset by the cyber failure.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** narrows to enforceable data control and inspection rights, dropping the hyperscaler levy. Private investment targets are cut, but the new condition—model weights stored on EU soil—signals a shift toward governance.

Public sentiment plummets as disinformation surges around the attacks and job losses in IT sectors. The **campaign_backlash** spreads, with parties across the spectrum calling for AI moratoriums. A **member_state_defection** occurs when Hungary signs a bilateral AI infrastructure deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, undermining EU unity.

A **safety_breakthrough** in model interpretability improves assurance on deployed systems, offering a rare positive. It is rapidly adopted, marginally lifting ai_safety. Openweight models benefit from this diffusion, allowing `openweight_capability` to rise in line with the frontier—growing by half the increase in `ai_capability` (from 45.0 to 47.5), consistent with gradual spillover, not a sudden leap.
