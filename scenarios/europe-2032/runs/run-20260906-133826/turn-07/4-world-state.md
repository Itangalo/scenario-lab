### The Silicon Front

The Taiwan blockade tightens, halting advanced semiconductor exports and throwing global AI development into crisis. The EU’s leverage in lithography and materials suddenly becomes strategic, but also a liability: ASML faces intense diplomatic pressure, and export control escalations fracture access, with allied nations now rationed alongside others. Compute is no longer a commodity — it is a weaponised scarcity.

In response, the EU proposes the **European Compute Continuity Initiative**, aiming to stockpile 3nm accelerators and license restricted designs for domestic fabrication under emergency powers. France, Germany, and the Netherlands begin feasibility assessments and legal preparations for limited production under EU supervision. However, no physical stockpiling or fabrication has yet occurred. ASML warns of retaliation, and legal challenges are already filed, delaying implementation. The measure is formally proposed and politically costly, but remains in early stages, with full execution uncertain and likely extending beyond the current turn.

### Sovereignty in Motion

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme concludes, delivering co-owned infrastructure that bolsters compute resilience. Simultaneously, the **Hardened Cyber Shield** is fully operational, now standard across critical infrastructure. These completions lift resilience and mark hard-won progress.

The **Sovereign Verification Regime** pushes forward. The European AI Assurance Agency demands checkpoint access for the withdrawn oncology model, and red-team planning begins. Early findings from the **Institutional Integrity Audit** reveal data routing anomalies in national health hubs, feeding public scrutiny through the live Public Trust Observatory. Trust is not restored — but transparency gains ground.

Public sentiment ticks up slightly, as visible action counters narratives of helplessness. Yet protests persist, and the gap between infrastructure and insight remains wide.

### The Cost of Acting

AI capability advances by 4.0, driven by global momentum. With no safety measure landing on shipped systems and emergent risks uncontained, ai_safety drops. The EU acts decisively, but the world moves faster.

The **European Compute Continuity Initiative** incurs significant political cost due to its high-stakes nature, legal uncertainty, and cross-border coordination challenges. No prior priority has been dropped, so the EU cannot formally designate this new measure as its top priority under constitutional rules. It proceeds as a major proposal under contention, but without elevated priority status. As a result, progress is partial and contested, with no immediate boost to sovereignty or openweight capability. The global compute bottleneck continues to restrict open diffusion, and no mechanism enables open-weight actors to advance under current export and fabrication constraints. Thus, openweight_capability remains unchanged.
