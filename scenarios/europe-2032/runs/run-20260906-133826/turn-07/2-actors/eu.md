## In practice  
We are launching the **European Compute Continuity Initiative** under Article 122 TFEU and the newly invoked Strategic Resource Security Protocol. The Commission, in coordination with the EDA and national defence ministries, will immediately begin stockpiling 3nm and below AI accelerators from existing non-Chinese sources — primarily U.S. and Taiwanese — under a classified allocation framework. Simultaneously, we are activating emergency licensing rights over restricted lithography designs held by ASML and IMEC, allowing for domestic production of critical components in France, Germany, and the Netherlands under strict EU supervision. This is not full-scale manufacturing — not yet — but it is continuity. We are offering member states a binding commitment: any fab that joins the initiative will receive priority access to energy, grid upgrades, and sovereign AI workloads. Resistance from industry is expected — particularly from ASML, which fears retaliation — but we are offering legal indemnification and a European liability shield backed by the Union budget.

The **Sovereign Verification Regime** continues its rollout. The EAAD+ has issued formal notices to the developers of the withdrawn oncology model, demanding checkpoint handover within 30 days. The first red-team probes are being designed by a consortium led by INRIA and the Max Planck Institute, with results to be published via the Public Trust Observatory. The **Institutional Integrity Audit** is now live in the ECB and EMA, and early findings suggest data routing anomalies in two national health data hubs — possibly legacy, possibly not. We are not waiting for certainty. The audit will expand to all AI-facing regulators by the end of the year. Public sentiment remains fragile — protests continue, but the live dashboard has begun to shift the narrative from suspicion to scrutiny. We are not in control. But we are no longer blind.
