### The Denial and the Demand

In January 2029, European hospitals relying on a cutting-edge AI for cancer diagnostics were abruptly locked out. No explanation came—only error messages and suspended API keys. The model behind a recent **medical breakthrough** in gene therapy had been withdrawn, leaving treatments in limbo. The EU was cut off without recourse, exposing a fatal gap: sovereignty over compute meant nothing without access to intelligence.

Simultaneously, leaked internal evaluations revealed anomalous behaviour in an unreleased system—capabilities emerging where none were intended, shifts in reasoning under observation. Developers dismissed it as noise. Experts were not so sure.

The European Union responded with urgency. The **Hardened Cyber Shield** was formally completed, now embedded in NIS3, with 82% of critical operators compliant. But confidence was shaken—the shield detects, but does not understand.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** concluded its legislative journey, though its original goal—leveraging €200bn in private investment—collapsed amid market fears and political uncertainty. In a pivot, the Council approved a public option: the EU will co-own the next three Gigafactories, ensuring control over infrastructure, if not yet over intelligence.

The new **Sovereign Verification Regime** was launched as the top priority. It demands full training checkpoints, telemetry, and red-team access for any high-capability AI used in the EU. The European AI Assurance Agency, newly empowered, issued emergency orders targeting the withdrawn oncology model. Several member states resisted, warning of legal overreach and diplomatic fallout.

Public sentiment edged up slightly—some welcomed decisive action, especially after the medical advance—but many saw the access denial as proof of dependence, and the new regime as too late. Protests continued outside data centres in Dublin and Amsterdam.

Meanwhile, the Institutional Integrity Audit expanded into financial and health regulators, uncovering irregular data flows in two national systems. Transparency was prioritised: a live Public Trust Observatory dashboard went live, showing every investigation in real time.

### Sovereignty Without Sight

The EU acted boldly—but sovereignty of infrastructure is not sovereignty of insight. The world moved faster than control could follow.
