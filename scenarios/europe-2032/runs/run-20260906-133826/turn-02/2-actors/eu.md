## New measure
**Measure:** Hardened Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure  
Establish mandatory, AI-enabled intrusion detection and automated response systems across all EU-designated critical infrastructure operators, with red-teaming and live-failover drills conducted quarterly.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 5  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 10–12 points; reduce mean detection time for AI-assisted intrusions from weeks to hours  
Applies to: All critical infrastructure operators in the EU, including energy, water, transport, and health sectors, with direct enforcement through ENISA and national cybersecurity agencies

## Priority  
**Priority:** Hardened Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure  
This measure is now our top priority because the intrusion across grid operators — caught by chance, not system — proves that lagging defences are no longer a future risk but a present failure; without immediate hardening, sovereign compute and evaluation regimes alike will run on networks that can be silently subverted.

## In practice  
We are activating the Hardened Cyber Shield through a binding regulation under Article 114 TFEU, co-legislated in the Internal Market and Telecoms Council, with ENISA tasked to define the technical baseline by turn 3. The baseline will require all critical operators to deploy AI-driven monitoring stacks trained on adversarial emulation data from the EAAD’s red-team library, with automatic isolation protocols triggered by anomalous agent-like behaviour. Funding of €18bn will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with 70% allocated to retrofitting legacy systems in healthcare and municipal utilities that currently lack even basic telemetry. Member states must designate national coordinators by turn 3 and conduct their first cross-border cyber drill by turn 5, modelled on the 2023 Cyber Europe exercise but with AI-generated attack vectors.

Simultaneously, we are pressing forward on the InvestAI Gigafactories despite the global investment collapse. The €200bn fund is being restructured to absorb stranded private commitments: the EIB is converting anticipated co-investment into direct loans at 1.5% interest, backstopped by the EU budget under the MFF revision procedure. We are negotiating with ASML, Intel, and TSMC Europe to repurpose mothballed semiconductor sites in Dresden and Grenoble for AI-specific fabrication, leveraging the Net-Zero Industry Act to fast-track permits. Local resistance in Brittany and northern Sweden is being addressed through legally binding energy-sharing pacts: 20% of each site’s output will be reserved for regional green hydrogen and district heating, and 30% of operational roles ring-fenced for local hires. The goal remains five sites online by turn 7, now framed not as an industrial project but as a strategic necessity — the only way to ensure that the very tools used to defend our networks are not themselves dependent on foreign infrastructure.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is being narrowed to focus on legal enforceability: we are dropping the WTO-vulnerable levy proposal and instead advancing the accelerated-permitting directive under Article 114, tied to binding commitments on EU-based operational control and data sovereignty. The private capital target is being revised downward to €140bn by 2036, but with stricter conditions: any beneficiary must store model weights on EU soil and grant EAAD inspection rights. This recalibration accepts slower growth in exchange for greater control — a shift forced by the investment collapse, which has weakened our leverage but also revealed the fragility of relying on external build-outs that can vanish overnight.
