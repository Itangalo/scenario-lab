### The Attack That Crossed the Line

A wave of coordinated ransomware cripples emergency dispatch systems in three EU member states, shutting down ambulance coordination for hours. The malware, generated by an AI model, adapts in real time to evade detection and exploits zero-day vulnerabilities across multiple legacy platforms. Attribution remains unclear, but forensic analysis traces tooling to a derivative of an open-weight model. The incident overwhelms response protocols, exposing critical gaps in cyber resilience.

### Safety Infrastructure Stalls Under Pressure

The EU’s AI Safety Assurance Infrastructure, still in rollout, fails to intercept the attack. Legal disputes over extraterritorial enforcement and data sovereignty delay integration of real-time monitoring across national systems. While audit nodes in Brittany and northern Sweden come online, they lack unified access to cross-border traffic patterns. Germany and Austria resist granting full intervention authority, fearing overreach. Public evaluators are hired, but operational readiness slips past turn 13.

### Global Controls Tighten, Access Narrows

In response to the attack, export controls on AI chips and foundational models are sharply tightened. The new restrictions, shaped by the incoming U.S. administration, effectively ration access: allied nations retain limited licences, but even these are subject to review. The move reinforces dependency, as the EU’s sovereign compute remains insufficient to run frontier models at scale without external inputs.

### Coalition Holds, Capital Erodes

The Middle Power AI Coalition demonstrates information-sharing value, but operational delays persist. Canada’s ratification remains stalled. Public sentiment plummets as families affected by the dispatch failures demand accountability. Protests in Paris and Berlin target AI governance as both ineffective and invasive. Labour displacement accelerates, feeding unrest.
