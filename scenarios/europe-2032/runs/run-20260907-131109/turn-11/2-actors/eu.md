## In practice
We are activating the first nodes of the AI Safety Assurance Infrastructure on sovereign compute clusters in Brittany and northern Sweden. Under the mandate of the European Agency for AI Defence (EAAD), public evaluators are being recruited from national cyber agencies, academic red teams, and vetted industry experts. Their mission is clear: continuous, real-time monitoring of any model above 60.0 capability operating in or targeting the EU, with legal authority to interrupt, fork, or freeze deployments showing signs of uncontrolled agency or evasion. The legal basis is Article 114 TFEU, with emergency override under Article 122, and we are applying it extraterritorially—access to EU markets and infrastructure now requires compliance with real-time auditing.

Germany and Austria remain cautious about centralised intervention powers, but we have secured their continued participation by offering co-leadership of the oversight board and full visibility into audit triggers and escalation protocols. Finland and Norway are contributing red-team capacity and stress-testing detection rules against synthetic agent behaviours. Japan and South Korea are watching closely; they have indicated they will mirror enforcement if we publish clean, reproducible evaluation standards by turn 13. We are not waiting for perfect consensus. The cyber_major_incident this turn proves that the frontier is already weaponised, and our sovereignty—though still thin—is now just sufficient to act. This infrastructure turns passive observation into active defence. We are no longer just logging attacks. We are building the capacity to stop them before they land.
