### Core Activated, Coalition Formalised

The Middle Power AI Coalition (MPAC) is formally launched, with founding members including Japan, South Korea, Norway, and Canada joining the EU in a framework for mutual evaluation, shared red-teaming, and coordinated export controls on AI-specific chip designs. The agreement stops short of binding commitments, but establishes a joint technical secretariat and a shared early-warning network for model leaks and cyber misuse. Canada’s parliament remains divided, delaying full ratification, and operational coordination will not begin until the next turn.

### Safety Infrastructure Launches, Full Activation Delayed

The EU begins deployment of its AI Safety Assurance Infrastructure, initiating staffing and technical integration for public evaluators with real-time access to model weights and logs for systems above 60.0 capability operating in the bloc. The first audits are scheduled for foreign-hosted models with EU-facing deployment, particularly those derived from open-weight frameworks. Germany and Austria accept co-leadership of the oversight board, and Norway and Finland commit red-team capacity. However, legal challenges and cross-border data access negotiations delay on-the-ground implementation; full operational capacity is now expected by the following turn.

### Frontier Advances, Open Frontier Creeps Up

Global AI capability advances steadily to 68.0, driven by unbroken training runs and improved agent coordination. While no overt safety failure occurs, interpretability continues to erode, with labs reporting opaque reasoning shifts in models just below deployment. The open-weight frontier shows signs of acceleration, as a widely distributed derivative of a frontier model enables more capable autonomous cyber operations with minimal human oversight—contributing to the hospital attack’s containment but also raising concerns about accessibility. Yet broad deployment remains limited by infrastructure and expertise, keeping gains incremental.

### Sentiment Stagnant, Resilience Creeps Up

Public sentiment remains frozen at 14.0, with protests in Lyon and Vienna denouncing the new audit powers as “technocratic overreach.” Labour displacement accelerates, and trust in AI-mediated governance declines. Resilience improves slightly as MPAC coordination shows early promise in information sharing, though no live incidents test the network yet. The EU’s sovereign compute base holds, and no new access disruptions occur.

The Sovereign AI Core Programme completes its rollout, providing foundational stability that offsets ongoing decay from fragmented adoption and external pressure. As a result, eu_ai_sovereignty holds steady at 14.0. Political capital rises modestly as diplomatic momentum builds around MPAC, and the visible launch of safety infrastructure is seen as a credible step forward, even if delayed.
