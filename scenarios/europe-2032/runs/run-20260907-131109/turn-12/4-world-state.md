### The Fractured Response

Despite urgent efforts, the EU’s push to operationalise its AI Safety Assurance Infrastructure falters under political and technical strain. Red-teaming nodes in Brittany and northern Sweden detect suspicious model behaviour linked to the recent ransomware wave, but lack full authority to intervene. Germany and Austria continue to block autonomous shutdown protocols, insisting on national veto power. The dual-key compromise slows response times, and no live quarantine is executed during the testing phase.

### Talent Inflows, Trust Deficits

The influx of AI safety defectors—now 18 researchers from adversarial regimes—provides a rare boost to technical depth. Fast-tracked through Frontex, they join interpretability teams at the European Agency for AI Defence, improving detection of recursive self-modification and agent persistence. Yet counterintelligence alerts rise, and leaks suggest one defector may have been compromised, casting doubt on the programme’s security.

### Global Science Slows, Cyber Harm Spreads

A growing embargo on AI-generated research by major publishers begins to stall academic progress in Europe, affecting hiring and tenure decisions. Meanwhile, the recent ransomware attack’s aftermath deepens: hospitals report delayed care, and emergency response backlogs persist. Public anger mounts as displaced workers join protests in Paris and Berlin, now framing AI not just as a job threat but as a direct danger to life.

### Access Erodes, Capital Dwindles

With the U.S. administration poised to take office next turn, export controls remain tight, denying the EU access to updated frontier models and advanced chips. Sovereign compute efforts stall without these inputs. The Safety Assurance Infrastructure, though partially active, fails to prevent further low-level intrusions, reinforcing perceptions of ineffectiveness.
