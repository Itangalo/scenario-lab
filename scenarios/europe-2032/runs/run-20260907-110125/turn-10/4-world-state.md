### The Denial and the Response

In early 2031, the European Union’s access to the most advanced AI models was abruptly severed without explanation. Hospitals relying on diagnostic assistants, ministries running policy simulations, and firms optimising supply chains were left stranded. The cutoff, sudden and total, was neither negotiated nor justified—only enforced. In Brussels, the event crystallised a strategic awakening: sovereignty without access is ritual, not reality.

The **Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes** were completed in Jülich, CINECA, Prague, and Bologna, integrating into energy grids and central banking systems. Telemetry now flows to ENISA’s new Situational Awareness Hub, though two member states withheld full access over national security concerns. Joint alert protocols were established, but data sharing remains partial. This measure, initiated earlier, reaches operational status this turn.

Work began on the **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** under the Joint Research Centre, with hub sites confirmed in Seville, Stockholm, and Bucharest. Emergency recruitment clauses enabled initial staffing, drawing talent from Ukraine and neutral states. However, legal authority remains provisional, and integration with national intelligence services has not yet commenced—delays ensure no operational impact this turn.

The **Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps**, proposed as a joint EEAS-Europol initiative, remains in design phase. While planning advanced and initial simulations are scheduled for July, no deployment occurred this turn. Legal, budgetary, and interagency hurdles prevent launch before the next period.

The instrument is nearing the terminal zone; gains are measured in competence, not tasks.
