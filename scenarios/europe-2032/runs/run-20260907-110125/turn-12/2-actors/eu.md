## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that without the capacity to act independently, neither mandate can be fulfilled. Sovereignty is not a means to an end; it is the condition of agency itself. However, this turn has revealed a new truth: sovereignty without legitimacy is brittle. When the public rejects the tools meant to protect them, even the most capable system becomes a liability. Our agency must be not only technical but also political — embedded in trust, exercised with transparency, and answerable to those it serves.  
- Grounds: The **public_sentiment** metric has collapsed to 15.0, matching the **election_annulled** event and now compounded by the **cyber_major_incident**, which landed visibly and painfully across public services. The **joint_threat_response** offer from allied states reveals a paradox: the EU is invited into coalitions for resilience, yet lacks domestic legitimacy to accept or act on them. Our identity must now reflect that self-determination is not just about control over compute or intelligence, but over the social contract that makes control legitimate.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but only through instruments we control, using sensing, analysis, and response chains that cannot be severed — and only when those instruments are seen as legitimate by the citizens they are meant to protect.  
- Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** exploited AI-generated tooling to disrupt public services, and the response was delayed not by technical failure but by political hesitation — member states refused to activate shared protocols without parliamentary debate, and the public dismissed early warnings as alarmism. Our previous commitment assumed legitimacy could follow action; this turn proves it must precede it. We cannot act sovereignly if we are not seen as sovereign.

- modify `new_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Build sovereign capacity to detect, interpret, and counter AI-driven strategic deception — not only as a resilience function, but as the core of political sovereignty in an age where reality itself is contested — and anchor it in public legitimacy through transparent attribution, judicial oversight, and civic participation in verification.  
- Grounds: The **election_annulled** event and the current **cyber_major_incident** confirm that deception is no longer a technical threat but a constitutional one. The Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps cannot operate in legal or social isolation. This turn, we saw that even when intelligence is correct, disbelief can paralyze response. The commitment must now include not just capability, but the mechanisms that make it credible.

## New measure  
**Civic Verification Corps**  
Establish a pan-European network of independent civic auditors, legal experts, and technical validators empowered to review and publicly certify AI-related threat assessments and response actions taken by the Behavioural Observatory and Counter-Deception Corps.  

`Category: 2 (Transparency and reporting)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to public_sentiment by restoring trust in AI governance through visible, participatory oversight; reduces friction in crisis response by pre-establishing credible third-party validators; indirectly supports resilience by enabling faster public compliance during incidents  
`Applies to:` EU member states, national ombuds institutions, European University Association, European Digital Rights (EDRi), and accredited civil society organisations  

## Priority  
**Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** — because it is the only measure that transforms raw data into sovereign understanding, and without it, both our detection and response capabilities operate in the dark. This turn, with the **cyber_major_incident** confirming the offensive use of AI-generated tooling and the **joint_threat_response** offering real-time intelligence sharing, the Observatory must be fully operational and legally empowered by the end of June. Its integration with national agencies is no longer optional — it is the line between coordinated defence and fragmented collapse.

## In practice  
We are completing the activation of the **Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes** in Jülich, CINECA, Prague, and Bologna, with the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking confirming full telemetry integration into energy grid controls and central banking systems. Despite two member states still withholding full data sharing, we are invoking Article 4(2) TEU to initiate infringement procedures, while offering reciprocal access to hardened cyber defence modules developed under the Secure Foundational Compute Initiative. The Crisis Diplomacy Unit is brokering bilateral agreements to convert political resistance into binding technical cooperation, with a deadline of June 30 for full protocol alignment.

The **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** is entering its final phase, with hubs in Seville, Stockholm, and Bucharest now at full staffing. We are finalising a legal act under Article 114 TFEU to grant the Observatory binding authority over national intelligence data flows related to AI deception, and launching a joint task force with Europol and ENISA to operationalise red-teaming of election systems by October. The Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps is being structured under a new legal basis combining NIS2, the Cyber Resilience Act, and the recently activated EU Emergency Powers Framework, with simulation exercises scheduled for November.

Simultaneously, we are launching the **Civic Verification Corps** through a call for proposals under Horizon Europe’s Democratic Resilience strand, allocating €120 million to fund regional verification hubs in 15 member states. These will include university-led consortia, national audit offices, and civil society validators, with a mandate to review and publicly certify all major threat assessments issued by the Behavioural Observatory. This is not oversight as constraint — it is oversight as enablement. By building legitimacy into the system now, we ensure that when the next crisis hits, the public does not see the response as a power grab, but as a collective defence.
