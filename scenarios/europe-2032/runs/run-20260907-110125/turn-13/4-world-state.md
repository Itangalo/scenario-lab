### The Fracture Deepens

AI capability continues its relentless climb, reaching 98.5 as self-improving systems refine strategic reasoning beyond human oversight. With no safety countermeasures in place, `ai_safety` remains at 1.0 — a void where control once stood. The open-weight frontier inches to 87.0, as leaked model shards are reassembled into cyber tools now probing industrial control systems across Eastern Europe.

The EU formally completes the Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes, but their integration remains partial. Three member states still resist data-sharing mandates, undermining the system’s coherence. Emergency provisions under NIS2 spark legal challenges, with courts in two nations suspending enforcement pending constitutional review.

The Civic Verification Corps launches in Berlin, Lyon, and Warsaw, but public attention is elsewhere. Media partnerships produce little beyond technical bulletins, drowned out by fresh reports of AI-synthesised disinformation influencing local elections in the Baltics. Trust remains frozen at 10.0.

Brussels pushes forward with the Emergency Compute Access Compact, dispatching envoys to Tokyo, Seoul, and Taipei. Yet the emergent coalition, though born from EU-aligned concerns, resists subordination. Japan and South Korea welcome cooperation but reject EU oversight, citing sovereignty. Taiwan offers technical collaboration but insists any agreement must avoid provoking Beijing. The compact stalls in drafting, its ambition outpacing political reality.

Meanwhile, the Secure Foundational Compute Initiative crawls forward. Fast-tracking under energy regulations triggers protests in southern France and northern Italy, where communities denounce the move as authoritarian. No new compute comes online.

With no measure delivering tangible gains, and all four initiatives still in flight, political capital plummets. The Union acts, but its actions fracture against external resistance and internal decay.
