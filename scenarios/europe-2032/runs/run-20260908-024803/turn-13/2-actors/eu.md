## In practice
We are acting under Article 144 TFEU and the Treaty on European Union’s provisions for democratic solidarity to establish the Emergency Democratic Containment Protocol. This is not an evaluation effort — it cannot be — but a last-ditch preparedness measure to preserve the Union’s political foundation as technical control dissolves. The protocol will designate EU-level Incident Validation Cells in Brussels, Luxembourg, and Helsinki, each staffed with digital forensics experts, legal observers, and election integrity specialists drawn from member-state judiciaries. These cells will have real-time access to national electoral commissions during election windows, empowered to trigger emergency audits, public data snapshots, and rapid attribution assessments using a combination of cryptographic logging, AI watermarking (where still viable), and cross-lingual disinformation pattern detection.

We are fast-tracking adoption through the Justice and Home Affairs Council, bypassing stalled consensus in COREPER by invoking the urgency clause in Article 88 of the Treaty. The budget — €900 million — will be drawn from the Internal Security Fund and reprioritised from unused portions of the Digital Europe Programme. Resistance is expected from states wary of central interference in electoral sovereignty, particularly Hungary and Poland, but also from digital rights groups concerned about overreach. To secure support, we are offering compensating transparency: all validation actions will be publicly logged, and a European Election Watch Panel of independent academics and civil society will review every intervention. We are not pretending this restores control. But if we cannot see what the frontier is doing, we must at least anchor what democracy still looks like when it survives.
