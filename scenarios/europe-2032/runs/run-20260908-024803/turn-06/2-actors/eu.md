## New measure
**Mandatory Cyber Resilience Expansion**  
Extend binding cyber resilience requirements to healthcare, municipal services, and mid-sized industrial operators, with mandatory incident reporting, tested response plans, and EU-level monitoring via ENISA.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases `resilience` (+10–14), reduces systemic risk from AI-generated attack vectors, and strengthens enforcement credibility for future AI-related directives by demonstrating capacity to impose and monitor compliance in sensitive sectors.  
`Applies to:` Member states and critical service operators in healthcare, local government, and mid-tier industry across the EU.

## Priority
**Mandatory Cyber Resilience Expansion** — because the **cyber_major_incident** this turn exposed the fragility of essential services now that offensive AI tooling is in circulation, and with `resilience` at 41.0 and falling, we cannot secure evaluation without also hardening the ground it stands on: a sovereign evaluation enclave is meaningless if the infrastructure it protects collapses under the first coordinated strike.

## In practice
We are treating the **cyber_major_incident**—a model-generated ransomware sweep now paralyzing regional health systems in three member states—as a wake-up call that resilience is no longer a background task but a frontline defence. The attack exploited AI-optimized phishing and zero-day discovery at scale, bypassing legacy detection systems. With `resilience` at 41.0 and uneven across sectors, we are now activating emergency powers under Article 114 TFEU to expand the scope of the *Hardened Critical Networks* directive. This new measure will bind healthcare providers, municipal IT systems, and mid-sized industrial operators—long the weakest links—to ENISA-mandated cyber standards, including real-time monitoring, quarterly red-teaming, and mandatory incident reporting within one hour of detection.

The pushback is expected, particularly from smaller member states citing cost and sovereignty. To secure Council approval in the Competitiveness formation, we are pairing the directive with a €15bn emergency resilience fund drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, prioritizing co-financing for regions most exposed. We are also fast-tracking integration with the *Secure Open-Weight Access* repository: red-team models now in our possession will be used to simulate attacks and validate defences, closing the loop between offensive insight and defensive readiness. This is not just patching holes—it is building a reflexive system where threat intelligence from open and frontier models directly informs public protection. The *EU Frontier Evaluation Enclave* continues its drafting phase, but we will not wait for it to become operational to ensure that when the next attack comes, someone is already watching.
