## Priority
**Emergency Sovereign Evaluation Mobilisation.** This is the last viable window to re-establish sovereign oversight before capability diverges beyond any institutional response — and it directly serves our two-year commitment to secure independent frontier evaluation and control, even as the world has moved past the assumptions that made that commitment seem achievable.

## In practice
We are invoking Article 176 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union to establish an Emergency Task Force for AI Evaluation under the Joint Research Centre, with delegated authority from the Commission and binding access mandates. The task force will stand up three shielded high-security evaluation nodes — in Saclay, Karlsruhe, and Padua — each equipped with air-gapped compute clusters and empowered to run adversarial diagnostic protocols on full-scale models. We are fast-tracking legal access through a new Regulation on Critical AI System Transparency, to be proposed to COREPER next week, which asserts the EU’s right to evaluate any system above 75.0 capability index operating in its markets or infrastructure.

This requires breaking long-standing resistance from member states wary of sovereignty clashes and from industry partners reliant on US-based frontier models. We are offering compensating access: EU-developed safety tooling, priority simulation time for compliant developers, and a seat on the Oversight Science Board. But we are also prepared to act unilaterally — the Council Presidency has secured qualified majority support in principle, contingent on demonstrating first results by turn 14. The budget will draw €1.8 billion from the Crisis Response Instrument and reprioritise 70% of Horizon Europe’s AI safety envelope. Without this, we have no path back to agency — only reaction.
