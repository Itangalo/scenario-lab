## In practice
We are executing the Frontier Cyber Shield Initiative through a binding update to the NIS2 directive, co-decided in the Competitiveness and Justice Council formations, with enforcement tied to cohesion fund disbursements. ENISA is leading the technical rollout, deploying AI-driven intrusion detection systems trained on synthetic attack patterns generated by the Joint Research Centre’s newly activated AI Red Cell. These systems will be mandatory for all Tier 1 operators in energy, health, transport, and government services, with real-time threat feeds shared through a newly established Cross-Border Cyber Alert Network. Funding of €18bn is drawn from reprioritised Digital Europe Programme allocations and emergency cohesion reserves, with immediate deployment to the three member states affected by the ransomware cascade.

We are also negotiating a temporary public interest licensing framework with EU-based cybersecurity firms—including Darktrace, WithSecure, and Heimdal Security—to integrate their AI threat models into the shield, in exchange for liability protection and preferential procurement status. Resistance remains from member states citing national sovereignty over critical infrastructure, particularly in Germany and Sweden, but we are framing this as a minimum survivability standard: no one is asking for perfect security, only that systems do not collapse under automated assault. The InvestAI Gigafactories, now operational, are being partially repurposed to host sovereign AI defence stacks, ensuring that the shield runs on infrastructure the EU controls. This is not a long-term solution—but it is the only one that lands in time.
