## New measure  
**Frontier Cyber Shield Initiative**  
Launch a binding, EU-coordinated cyber defence surge to harden all critical public infrastructure against AI-generated attacks, with mandatory AI-specific threat modelling, red-teaming, and real-time detection systems deployed within 12 months.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases `resilience` by +8–12 through rapid deployment of AI-adaptive cyber defences across energy, health, transport, and government networks; reduces the impact of the **cyber_major_incident** and prepares for future automated attacks now inevitable at current `openweight_capability` (69.0) and `ai_capability` (80.0).  
`Applies to:` All EU member states, with enforcement through updated NIS2 directives and conditional cohesion fund disbursements.

## Priority  
**Frontier Cyber Shield Initiative** — because the **cyber_major_incident** this turn revealed that our critical systems are not just vulnerable but structurally unprepared for AI-synthesised attacks, and with `resilience` at 43.0 and falling, we cannot rely on detection or attribution—only on absorption and continuity, which we do not yet have.

## In practice  
We are treating the **cyber_major_incident**—a model-generated ransomware cascade that locked municipal health records and disrupted emergency dispatch in three member states—as a wake-up call: our defences are still built for human-scale threats. AI is now the offensive medium, and it operates at speeds and scales that render traditional incident response irrelevant. The Frontier Cyber Shield Initiative will mobilise the European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA), national CERTs, and a newly formed AI Red Cell under the Joint Research Centre to deploy AI-driven intrusion detection systems, automatically updated threat signatures, and mandatory AI-specific penetration testing across all Tier 1 critical operators. Funding will come from a reallocation of the Digital Europe Programme and emergency cohesion reserves, with €18bn committed over 18 months.  

We are pushing this through the Council’s Competitiveness and Justice formations, leveraging the political momentum from the recent **loss_of_control_incident** to override national resistance to centralised cyber mandates. Countries with weaker digital infrastructure will receive fast-track technical support from the newly operational InvestAI sites, now repurposed in part to host sovereign AI defence stacks. We are also negotiating with private cybersecurity firms—particularly those with EU-based AI research teams—to contribute tools under a temporary public interest licensing framework, in exchange for liability protection and preferential procurement. Resistance is expected from member states wary of sovereignty loss and from vendors protecting proprietary systems, but we are framing this as a survival-level upgrade: no one is asking for perfect security. We are asking for survivability. The EU Frontier Evaluation Enclave, finishing this turn, will give us post-incident analysis; this measure ensures we don’t collapse before the analysis can even begin.
