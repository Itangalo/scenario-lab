## In practice  
We are holding firm on the two-year commitment: sovereignty through independence. But independence is not just compute, and not just hardware — it is access to capability under our own terms. The Gigafactories and Tech sovereignty package remain our long-term compute anchors, but they are years from completion, and the world is already past us in capability. We cannot wait. That is why we are advancing a new front: public technical capacity to understand and control what is already loose.

The new measure, *Secure Open-Weight Access*, responds to the quiet crisis in *ai_safety*, which has now dropped to 29.0 — a level where even basic assurance is eroding. Open weights at 41.0 are now capable enough to enable offensive cyber operations, as Kimi K3 demonstrated, yet no EU body can evaluate them before they spread. We are establishing a controlled repository under the Joint Research Centre, with verification protocols developed in coordination with ENISA and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking. Models above 40.0 will be submitted voluntarily by developers or retrieved from public sources, then vetted for dangerous capabilities by a new public evaluator unit funded through Horizon Europe. Access will be granted only to pre-cleared researchers and agencies, with strict auditing.

This is not a substitute for frontier control — but it is the first step toward closing the loop on what is already in circulation. We are framing it as a research and security measure, not a restriction, to avoid triggering resistance from open-source communities and member states wary of overreach. The legal basis will be Article 185 of the TFEU, enabling R&D programmes for security purposes, paired with NIS2 provisions on threat intelligence. The cost is modest, but the signal is clear: the EU will not be blind to the systems that shape its infrastructure. If we can evaluate what is loose, we can begin to govern it — and from that, sovereignty grows.
