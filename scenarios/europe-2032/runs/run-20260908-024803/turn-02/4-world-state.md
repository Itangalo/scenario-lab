### The Fracture Deepens
A member state’s unilateral agreement with a U.S. hyperscaler—granting preferential access to frontier AI infrastructure in exchange for data-sharing concessions—sparked open defiance of the Union’s coordinated stance. Labeled as pragmatic by its leaders, the move was seen across Brussels and several capitals as a betrayal, exposing the fragility of EU unity in the face of external leverage. The defection did not disrupt immediate operations, but it marked a quiet unraveling of collective strategy.

### Momentum and Misstep
Despite the strain, the EU pressed forward with *Secure Open-Weight Access*, a small but symbolically significant measure aimed at regaining technical visibility over open models. Framed as a research and security initiative under Article 185, it avoided immediate legal challenges. However, developer participation remains uncertain—no major open-weight provider has committed to submit models, and internal audits warn of limited reach without enforcement powers.

Meanwhile, *Hardened Critical Networks* remains the named priority, but its authority is undermined by the recent defection. Enforcement mechanisms are legally contested, and several utilities delay compliance, citing cost and jurisdictional ambiguity. No progress beyond planning has been achieved, and resilience holds steady.
