## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: building up and hardening in. The Gigafactories and Tech sovereignty package remain our long-term bet on compute independence — but they are multi-year plays, and the world is moving now. We are negotiating with the Council Presidency on the legal basis for the Gigafactories fund under Article 122 (energy security and economic policy coordination), while offering member states in energy-rich regions fast-track access to grid upgrades and tax incentives to host the new sites. Resistance from smaller states fearing a "compute divide" is real; we are countering with a side-letter on distributed edge capacity and talent development funds tied to siting.

Meanwhile, the Tech sovereignty package’s accelerated-permitting zones face legal challenges from environmental NGOs and regional planning authorities. We are working through the ECOFIN and ENVI Council formations to separate high-impact data centres from routine permitting, using the AI Act’s infrastructure provisions as leverage. The Commission’s legal service is preparing a fallback directive that would designate AI infrastructure as critical under the NIS2 framework, allowing override powers — but we are holding that card for now, offering instead a joint monitoring mechanism with member states to preserve cohesion.

The new measure, Hardened Critical Networks, responds directly to the cyber_test_shot. We are drafting a Council Implementing Act under the NIS2 Directive, mandating Level 3 cyber resilience (continuous monitoring, air-gapped backups, and red-teaming) for all operators of essential services in electricity, water, and major ports. The Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will audit compliance, with funding drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility. We expect resistance from utilities citing cost, so we are pairing the mandate with a €12bn resilience co-investment fund, financed through the EU budget and green bonds. If this works, it will be the first time we enforce cross-border cyber readiness not as advice, but as binding, verified obligation.
