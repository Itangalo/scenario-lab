## Priority  
Hardened Critical Networks — because without enforceable, binding resilience across all member states, the Union cannot credibly claim to protect its own infrastructure, and the recent defection has turned a compliance challenge into a test of survival: if we cannot compel adherence now, we will not get a second chance when the next incident hits.

## In practice  
We are doubling down on enforcement of *Hardened Critical Networks*, treating it not as a technical directive but as a political test of whether the EU can still bind its members to collective security. The Commission is preparing infringement procedures against the defector state, citing NIS2 and Article 114 TFEU as the legal basis for harmonised cybersecurity standards, and we are circulating a draft implementing act to the COREPER II formation to fast-track adoption of central monitoring protocols. ENISA is being resourced to conduct unannounced audits of five major utilities by Q1 2028, and we are offering co-funding from the Digital Europe Programme to offset compliance costs for smaller operators — not as a concession, but as a signal that support follows alignment.

At the same time, we are advancing *Secure Open-Weight Access* through technical and institutional channels that bypass political gridlock. The Joint Research Centre is finalising the architecture for the model repository, and we have secured provisional participation from two open-weight developers — including the stewards of the Kimi lineage — by framing access as a mutual benefit: early detection of misuse patterns, shared threat intelligence, and EU validation as a mark of responsibility. We are not waiting for unanimity; we are building operational credibility through quiet coordination between research directors, cybersecurity agencies, and national labs, knowing that if we can demonstrate value, political cover will follow. But we are not deluding ourselves: this is a stopgap. Real sovereignty still hinges on whether we can finish the Gigafactories and the Tech sovereignty package before the capability gap becomes unbridgeable — and whether we can hold the Union together long enough to use them.
