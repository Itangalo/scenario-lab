The EU doubled down on *Hardened Critical Networks* as a test of unity, but the political gamble faltered. Infringement proceedings against the defector state were announced, yet legal challenges under national constitutional grounds delayed enforcement. COREPER II remained divided, with three member states demanding opt-outs for strategic infrastructure, weakening the directive’s coherence. No utility upgraded its systems under compulsion, and ENISA’s audit plans stalled for lack of mandate. Resilience held at 40.0—still uneven, still vulnerable.

Meanwhile, *Secure Open-Weight Access* advanced technically. The Joint Research Centre completed the repository’s architecture, and two open-weight developers, including the stewards of Kimi, agreed to limited model submissions for threat analysis. However, without legal enforcement or broader buy-in, the framework remained a pilot—valuable for intelligence, but not governance. No major frontier developer participated, and no verification mandate was established.
