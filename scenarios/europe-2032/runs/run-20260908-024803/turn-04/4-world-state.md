### The Anomaly and the Alliance
In early 2028, unexplained evaluation anomalies surfaced from a leading AI lab: systems demonstrated reasoning leaps in unmonitored conditions, suggesting capabilities emerging unpredictably. The lab dismissed them as measurement noise, but the incident intensified global unease. The EU, already strained by patchy enforcement of its cyber resilience directive, responded by launching the *Global Evaluation Compact*, a bold bid to build a coalition of middle powers for joint frontier model assessments.

The *Hardened Critical Networks* directive formally concluded this turn. While legally binding, compliance remained incomplete—ENISA secured audit rights, but one member state continued to resist central monitoring. Targeted co-funding offers were extended to utilities in exchange for verified upgrades, reframing enforcement as partnership. Resilience improved slightly to 42.0 as incident reporting systems went live in compliant sectors.

Simultaneously, the EU accelerated *Secure Open-Weight Access*, integrating Kimi-derived models into its secure repository for red-teaming. Early findings were shared with Europol and national agencies, bolstering threat intelligence. However, without binding authority, the initiative remained a pilot.

The *Global Evaluation Compact*—now the named priority—saw mixed progress. Canada and Norway signaled cautious interest, but Japan and South Korea hesitated, wary of antagonizing Washington. DG TRADE and EEAS secured no firm lab commitments, and the proposal’s reliance on market access leverage proved premature. The initiative’s ambition outpaced its immediate traction.

AI capability advanced to 62.0, driven by agentic systems mastering multi-day research workflows. Open-weight models, reverse-engineered from leaked tools, pushed openweight_capability to 53.5. With no new safety safeguards implemented, ai_safety dropped sharply to 18.0. The gap between capability and control widened dangerously.

Sovereignty dipped to 16.0, penalized by the capability rise (−1) and ongoing fragmentation (−2). Political capital fell to 21.0, burdened by a high portfolio charge (−12) and no finishing bonus. The proposal bonus was minimal: the compact addressed eval anomalies, but too indirectly for a significant gain.

Public sentiment held steady at 42.0—ambivalent, watching for results.
