## New measure
**EU Frontier Evaluation Enclave**  
Establish a legally sovereign, technically isolated evaluation environment within the EU to conduct mandatory pre-deployment assessments of frontier models, including full access to agent logs, training data summaries, and internal reasoning traces, enforced through binding hosting agreements.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`

`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases `ai_safety` (+10–14), strengthens `eu_ai_sovereignty` (+5–7) by anchoring evaluation authority on EU soil, and pressures `openweight_capability` governance by setting a new benchmark for transparency.  
`Applies to:` Frontier developers operating in or accessing the EU market, with enforcement via hosting agreements and market access conditions.

## In practice
We are treating the failure of the *Global Evaluation Compact* not as defeat but as revelation. The refusal of labs to share internal logs exposed the fragility of voluntary cooperation, but the lab’s subsequent offer to relocate core research to a EU-hosted cloud enclave—framed as a bid for neutral oversight—has handed us a lever we did not expect. We are now moving to convert that offer into a binding precedent. The European Commission, through DG CNECT and the Joint Research Centre, is drafting a hosting agreement under Article 114 TFEU that conditions market access on full technical transparency within a legally sovereign EU enclave. This is not merely a repository—it is a monitored, auditable environment where agent behaviour, training data provenance, and internal state evolution are subject to continuous evaluation by EU-certified experts.

At the same time, we are accelerating the operationalization of *Secure Open-Weight Access*. The Joint Research Centre has now onboarded Kimi-derived weights and two other models into the secure repository, enabling red-teaming under strict containment. Findings are being shared with Europol and national agencies, but we are now reframing this not as a standalone pilot but as the first tier of a two-layer strategy: open models assessed at scale, frontier models assessed in depth. The completion of *Hardened Critical Networks*—while patchy—provides a foothold: ENISA’s audit rights, though resisted by one member state, establish a precedent for central oversight that we are now extending into the frontier space. We are offering that state targeted Digital Europe Programme co-funding in exchange for binding commitments on future readiness, not as punishment but as proof that cohesion can be built through credible offers. The era of relying on goodwill is over; the era of sovereign capability has begun.
