### A Step Forward, One Step Back

The EU formally closes the **Tech Sovereignty Package**, securing €48bn in private investment and operationalising three accelerated-permitting zones. Though short of its €200bn goal, the zones now host 1.2 exaFLOP/s of EU-anchored compute, offering a foothold against external disruption. The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** advances, with physical audits underway across 17 clusters. Legal negotiations with U.S. hyperscalers remain tense; one major provider resists audit access, triggering a formal warning under the Cyber Resilience Act. The investment and deployment proceed through binding agreements with European subsidiaries and local infrastructure partners, leveraging access to EU energy grids and data markets—concessions made despite the U.S. RETRENCHMENT posture, but not indicative of broader alignment.

No new measure is introduced this turn. The Commission had considered advancing a biosecurity framework in response to emerging concerns, but after internal consultation, decided to delay any formal proposal. Instead, HERA convenes a technical working group on biothreat evaluation, and preliminary discussions are held with French and German research institutes on potential monitoring approaches. Voluntary reporting guidelines are drafted but not yet disseminated, reflecting early exploratory work rather than the introduction of a new measure. The EU remains focused on a single priority: strengthening compute assurance and audit capacity in critical infrastructure.

### Capability Leaps, Safety Erodes

A sudden **capability jump** occurs in narrow engineering and cyber domains, pushing `ai_capability` to 64.0. Attackers now automate multi-stage intrusion chains with adaptive evasion. Concurrently, **eval_anomaly_reports** surface from a leading lab, where an unreleased model demonstrated untrained problem-solving in biological design — a red flag internally debated, with some researchers suggesting it may reflect a measurement artefact, while others urge deeper investigation. With no new safety measures implemented on deployed systems and growing evidence of uncontrolled model drift, `ai_safety` declines to 19.0, reflecting moderate erosion due to oversight gaps and rising uncertainty. The drop is notable but not systemic, consistent with stagnation and delayed oversight rather than a breach or runaway event.

Resilience rises to 50.0 as audit protocols from the Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative are integrated into national frameworks, building detection capacity. Sovereignty holds at 23.0: gains from the completed Tech Sovereignty Package are offset by the −1 decay from capability growth and the absence of new sovereign measures. Political capital remains unchanged at 44.0; while the completion of the Tech Sovereignty Package delivers a proposal bonus, it is fully offset by ongoing portfolio charges. Public sentiment remains stagnant at 34.0, as fears of biological misuse and infrastructure fragility dominate headlines.

`openweight_capability` remains at 50.0, as no new open-source model releases, training breakthroughs, or capability transfers are reported this turn. The observed capability advance is concentrated in proprietary, narrow systems and does not extend to open-weight models.
