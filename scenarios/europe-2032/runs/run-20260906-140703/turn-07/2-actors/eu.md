## Priority
**Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** — because without assured access to functioning compute, all other defences — including the new biosecurity framework — collapse before they can activate, and the Taiwan blockade has made hardware sovereignty the precondition of all action.

## In practice
The **Tech sovereignty package** formally concludes this turn. While it unlocked €48bn in committed private capital — well short of the 2036 target — its real legacy is the creation of three accelerated-permitting zones in Finland, Ireland, and Poland, now hosting EU-anchored clusters with combined capacity approaching 1.2 exaFLOP/s. We are converting its momentum into bilateral agreements with ASML and Zeiss to fast-track lithography tool delivery for EU-facilitated fabs, using the European Chips Act’s safeguard clause to prioritise dual-use resilience. The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** enters full implementation: the European Commission, through DG CONNECT and the Joint Research Centre, is conducting physical verification of firmware and supply chain provenance across 17 high-priority clusters. Legal anchoring is being negotiated via modified service-level agreements with U.S. hyperscalers, offering recognition of equivalent U.S. Department of Energy safeguards in return for audit access and failover control. Where resistance persists — notably with one major cloud provider operating a 2.1 exaFLOP/s facility in Germany — we are invoking Article 7 of the Cyber Resilience Act to condition market access on compliance by turn 8.

At the same time, the **Biosecurity Rapid Response Framework** is being scoped in emergency coordination between HERA, the ECDC, and national biosafety institutes in France, Germany, and Sweden. Triggered by the bio_uplift_findings precursor, it will mandate that any AI-assisted design of organisms with human infectivity potential — whether in public research or private labs — be reported within 24 hours to a newly activated EU Biothreat Evaluation Cell. This cell, hosted at HERA and staffed with rotating experts from member states, will have authority to request model weights, suspend experiments, and coordinate containment drills. The framework leverages existing NIS2 reporting channels but extends them into life sciences, where regulatory overlap has left gaps. We are funding it through a €320m reprioritisation from the Health Union Programme, avoiding new budget requests that would strain political capital further. This is not a permanent institution — yet — but a sprint to establish detection and response muscle before the bio gate fully opens.
