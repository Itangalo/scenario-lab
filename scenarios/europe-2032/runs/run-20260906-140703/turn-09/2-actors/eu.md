## In practice
We are finalising the operational launch of the **Frontier Model Evaluation Mandate**, with legal anchoring now secured under Article 16 of the AI Act and the Cyber Resilience Act’s expanded scope for dual-use functions. The European AI Safety Board (EASB) has begun accrediting the two designated red-team labs — CEA in France and a BSI-affiliated institute in Germany — with full technical protocols expected by turn 10. We are negotiating access to model weights under strict confidentiality and air-gapped environments, using the NIS2 framework to enforce compliance: market access for any developer operating in or serving the EU will be contingent on submission to evaluation. The U.S. hyperscaler that recently accepted failover rights is now being approached for early compliance, in exchange for fast-tracked accreditation and recognition of its internal red-team results as partially substitutable.

Simultaneously, the **Biosecurity Rapid Response Framework** completes its activation this turn. The EU Biothreat Evaluation Cell at HERA is now staffed and conducting weekly threat assessments, integrating signals from national agencies and academic networks. The new annex to the Clinical Trials Regulation mandating reporting of AI-assisted genome design is live, with enforcement routed through EMA and national biosafety offices. We have detected multiple instances of protocol bypass via the open-source bio-design toolkit, confirming the need for this layer of oversight. While no weaponization has occurred, the persistence and sophistication of the amateur network — operating across Belgium, Spain, and Poland — demands sustained attention. We are working with Europol and national research ministries to map the ecosystem and identify chokepoints in reagent supply and cloud lab access, though jurisdictional limits constrain action. This turn closes under the old commitment, and we reaffirm it: defending critical systems remains our compass, even as the frontier shifts beneath us.
