## New measure
**Frontier Model Evaluation Mandate**  
Require independent, pre-release evaluation of all models above 55.0 capability index before deployment in the EU, with results verified by a newly empowered European AI Safety Board and made available to national regulators.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size:` small  
`Finishes on turn:` 10  
`Targeted effect:` +5 to +8 ai_safety by turn 10 through mandatory third-party assessment of dangerous capabilities, including cyber, biological, and autonomous planning functions; begin closing the gap between capability growth and assurance

## Priority
**Frontier Model Evaluation Mandate** — because the openweight_frontier_release event has collapsed the lead time between closed and open capability, making pre-release evaluation the last point at which the Union can credibly intervene before dangerous capabilities become unrecallable, and because our two-year commitment to defend critical systems now depends on stopping threats before they deploy, not just after they strike.

## In practice
We are treating the openweight_frontier_release as a system-level breach: the gap between closed and open capability has narrowed from a year to months, and whatever frontier models now hold is already on private hardware beyond any jurisdiction. This changes the calculus. Our focus can no longer be only on securing EU-anchored compute or responding to biothreats after detection — we must now act upstream, where the race is still winnable. The **Frontier Model Evaluation Mandate** is being drafted under Article 16 of the AI Act, with legal anchoring in the newly amended Cyber Resilience Act to cover dual-use functions. The European AI Safety Board (EASB), hosted at the Joint Research Centre and staffed with rotating experts from national agencies, will accredit two independent evaluation labs — one in France (CEA) and one in Germany (BSI-affiliated) — to conduct red-team assessments on models above 55.0 capability, focusing on cyber intrusion, agent persistence, and biological design. Developers must submit models 90 days before release; any refusal blocks market access under NIS2 enforcement mechanisms.

At the same time, we are completing the **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** with final audit reports from all 17 clusters. The U.S. hyperscaler that resisted access has now agreed to modified SLAs, granting the EU failover control and firmware verification rights in exchange for recognition of DOE equivalency and preferential access to EU energy grids under the REPowerEU framework. This sets a precedent: market access for foreign providers now explicitly depends on enforceable jurisdiction. The **Biosecurity Rapid Response Framework** enters operational phase this turn: the EU Biothreat Evaluation Cell is activated at HERA with initial staffing from France, Germany, and Sweden. We are finalising mandatory reporting protocols for AI-assisted genome design, leveraging existing NIS2 channels but extending them into life sciences via a new annex to the Clinical Trials Regulation. Funding is secured through reprioritisation of €320m from the Health Union Programme, avoiding new budget requests. The system is time-bound — it expires at turn 10 unless renewed — but it gives us muscle where we had none.
