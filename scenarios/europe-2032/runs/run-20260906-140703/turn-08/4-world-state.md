### A New Front Opens

The EU finalises the **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative**, marking a quiet milestone in its control over critical compute infrastructure. The 17 audit clusters are now verified under EU jurisdiction, and a major U.S. hyperscaler has accepted binding safeguards — firmware access and failover rights — in exchange for preferential energy terms. This secures access for now, though dependence remains.

In parallel, signs emerge of a significant open-weight model release: underground forums show rapid experimentation with AI-driven cyber tools exhibiting novel coordination and persistence capabilities. While the full impact is not yet clear, early assessments suggest a gradual uplift in accessible capabilities. However, no confirmed frontier open release has occurred. Without a clear breakthrough or diffusion cascade, `openweight_capability` remains at 50.0 — the observed activity is concerning but not yet sufficient to justify a metric increase.

In response, the EU launches the **Frontier Model Evaluation Mandate** as its new priority. Drafted under the AI Act and Cyber Resilience Act, it requires third-party red-teaming of high-capability models before EU market access. The European AI Safety Board will accredit labs in France and Germany to conduct tests on cyber, biological, and planning risks. Market access is tied to compliance — a strong lever, but one yet to be tested in practice.

The **Biosecurity Rapid Response Framework** becomes operational, activating a cross-agency cell at HERA and establishing reporting rules for AI-driven genome design. These steps, paired with the completed Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative, boost resilience to 53.0, reflecting improved detection and coordination.

`ai_capability` rises to 65.5, continuing its steady climb. With the new evaluation mandate only just proposed, no safety gains land on deployed systems yet — but the mere act of acting against a major open-release precursor delivers a political boost. `ai_safety` ticks up to 21.0, as the proposal signals intent to close the oversight gap.

Sovereignty reaches 28.0: the **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative**, a category 4 small measure, delivers its full +5 completion bonus. The initiative directly expanded EU-controlled frontier compute access through binding agreements and audit rights, qualifying it for category 4 benefits. Starting from 23.0, with `ai_capability` rising by 1.5 (below the 2.0 threshold), no decay applies. The +5 from completion yields a new total of 28.0.

Political capital rises to 47.0, lifted by the completion bonus of the Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative (+3 from proposal bonus due to bio_uplift_findings linkage), the legitimacy of launching a high-priority mandate in response to emerging threats, and the operationalisation of the biosecurity framework. Public sentiment, however, remains stagnant at 34.0 — the biotech breakthroughs and cyber tools in the underground feed anxiety, and no visible public benefit has yet emerged.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** is retired from the active portfolio, its earlier contributions having been fully accounted for in prior turns. Its benefits were realised in earlier phases of implementation, and no residual effects carry into this turn.

Only one new measure — the Frontier Model Evaluation Mandate — is introduced, preserving compliance with the one-new-measure-per-turn rule. Its full effects will unfold over time, with implementation requiring accreditation, lab readiness, and enforcement mechanisms still in development.
