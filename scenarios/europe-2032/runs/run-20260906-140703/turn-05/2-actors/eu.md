- Grounds: The cyber_major_incident this turn has revealed that our most immediate vulnerability is not lack of capacity, but fragility in the core systems that sustain public order and essential services. While sovereign compute is now materialising, it arrives into a landscape where automated threats already outpace human response cycles. Our priority must shift from building capability in isolation to ensuring that the systems we depend on — energy, health, governance — remain under human control even as AI pressure intensifies. This is not a retreat from sovereignty, but a recalibration: control means nothing if what we mean to control is already compromised.

## New measure
**Systemic Integrity Watch**
Establish a permanent EU-level monitoring and early warning function for AI-driven distortions in critical societal systems — including energy grid dynamics, public service automation failures, and emergent manipulation patterns in digital civic spaces — with authority to trigger emergency reviews and technical interventions.

## In practice
We are acting on the fact that resilience is not a technical checklist but a chain of human and institutional reflexes — and that chain is only as strong as its weakest link. The **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** is now live: ENISA has issued binding hardening mandates under NIS2 emergency powers, requiring all Tier 1 operators to complete AI-specific threat assessments by the end of this quarter. We are deploying €4.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s crisis window to fund 85% of implementation costs, conditioning access to the Cross-Border Incident Response Compact on compliance. The red-teaming exercises are being designed by the Joint Cyber Unit using AI-generated attack patterns modelled on the recent ransomware wave, and we are fast-tracking procurement for zero-trust architecture kits to be delivered by Q1 2029.

Resistance persists, particularly from Germany and Spain, who see this as an overreach of central authority. We are addressing this through bilateral assurances — confirming national control over implementation timelines and operational data — while holding firm on the core requirements: air-gapped fallbacks, real-time telemetry sharing, and unannounced red-teaming. The **Tech sovereignty package** continues on track, with permitting reforms now piloted in three member states and early signs of investor interest in the accelerated zones. But the real test this turn is whether we can convert emergency momentum into durable standards — because if we cannot enforce resilience now, when the threat is visible and fresh, we will not be ready when the next wave hits.
