### A Surge, a Breakthrough, and a Fracture

The frontier surges forward with alarming speed as a US laboratory demonstrates a self-improving agent architecture, pushing global AI capability to an estimated 67.0. This leap bypasses prior timelines, enabling autonomous breakthroughs in cryptanalysis and materials science. Simultaneously, a major safety advance emerges: a real-time circuit-tracing tool now allows developers to detect deceptive reasoning before output, rapidly adopted across leading labs, including one in France.

Yet progress deepens fragmentation. Poland signs a bilateral agreement with a US hyperscaler, embedding joint AI cells within its defence ministry and securing priority access to frontier models. Warsaw frames it as pragmatic deterrence, but the move undermines EU-wide containment and signals eroding cohesion.

In response, the EU accelerates integration of the new interpretability tool into the Sovereign Model Assurance Framework (SMAF), leveraging emergency powers under Article 114 TFEU. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) begins its first audit of the Sovereign AI Core Programme’s prototype, while the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge concludes and transitions to ENISA as a standing monitoring function. Ground breaks on the Swedish InvestAI Gigafactory, supported by Article 122 overrides.

The EU launches the Public Assurance Protocol, mandating annual public reports on safety and containment for high-capability sovereign systems. Coupled with an offer to co-host a second air-gapped cluster in Wrocław and a €10bn resilience grant, it aims to draw Poland back into the common framework. However, public sentiment remains stagnant at 25.0, reflecting deep skepticism. Resilience dips slightly as the capability surge outpaces defences, and political capital erodes under the weight of six ongoing initiatives. Sovereignty declines further as dependency risks crystallize.

### The Cost of Control

Ambition now outpaces execution. The EU acts decisively on containment and transparency, but without visible public benefit or restored unity, legitimacy does not follow. The tools to secure systems exist — but not yet the trust to sustain them.
