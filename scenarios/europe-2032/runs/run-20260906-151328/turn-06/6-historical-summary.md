Global AI capability surges to 67.0 due to a breakthrough in self-improving agents in the US, accelerating advances in cryptanalysis and materials science. A real-time circuit-tracing tool for detecting deceptive reasoning is rapidly adopted, improving safety oversight. The EU strengthens the Sovereign Model Assurance Framework (SMAF) by integrating the new interpretability technology under emergency powers, and the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) conducts its first audit of the Sovereign AI Core prototype. The Swedish InvestAI Gigafactory breaks ground, backed by Article 122 TFEU measures, while the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge concludes and transitions to ENISA for ongoing monitoring.

Fragmentation intensifies as Poland signs a bilateral defense AI pact with a US hyperscaler, gaining priority model access and establishing joint AI cells, challenging EU cohesion. In response, the EU launches the Public Assurance Protocol, requiring annual public safety reports, offers co-hosting of a second air-gapped cluster in Wrocław, and provides a €10bn resilience grant to re-engage Poland. Despite these measures, public sentiment remains stagnant at 25.0, trust erodes, and political strain grows under six concurrent initiatives. Resilience dips as capability outpaces safeguards, and sovereignty declines amid deepening dependencies. The gap between strategic ambition and effective execution widens, revealing the limits of containment without unity or legitimacy.
