AI capability surged to 60.0 due to a discontinuous leap in model performance, enabling AI to autonomously complete complex professional tasks and triggering widespread displacement of junior roles. A large-scale AI-powered cyberattack disrupted public services in three member states, overwhelming defenses and exposing systemic vulnerabilities, while attribution remained unresolved. In response, the EU accelerated InvestAI Gigafactories via Article 122 TFEU, securing grid approvals in Sweden and allocating €400 million in solidarity payments to Brittany and Norrland, funded through reprioritized cohesion funds and limited emergency borrowing, deepening fiscal tensions with northern member states. The Commission proposed the binding Sovereign Model Assurance Framework (SMAF) to enforce auditability and isolation, to be implemented by the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD), though legislative adoption is delayed until at least mid-2029. Legal challenges persist on permitting harmonization and the "dormant adoption" clause, stalling the Tech Sovereignty Package, while export restrictions complicate the pending joint procurement deal with Zeiss and IMEC. Public sentiment and political capital continue to erode amid accelerating job losses and cyber insecurity. CIRS upgrades lag, resilience degrades, and the EAAD begins preparations for expanded audit mandates but remains constrained by recruitment and legal limitations. Despite a coherent strategic response, implementation delays, financing friction, and a heavy portfolio burden limit effectiveness, with no near-term gains in sovereign AI capacity.
