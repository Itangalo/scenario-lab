# Statements: The European Union (turn 8)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse
- `new_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Rebuild public trust in AI by ensuring algorithmic fairness in public services, mandating transparency and redress in all AI-supported state decisions, and grounding sovereignty in demonstrable benefit — not just in control or containment.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without social legitimacy is a fortress without a people. However, legitimacy cannot be built on containment alone: we must now couple security with shared benefit, and sovereignty with diffusion into public services that citizens see and trust. This turn, that duality has fractured: the automated decision scandal proves that public trust collapses not when systems are powerful, but when they are opaque and unaccountable in their daily impact. We are not merely stewards of capability or safety — we are custodians of fairness in the algorithmic state.
  - Grounds: The `automated_decision_scandal` event — a judgment confirming systemic harm from an AI-supported social insurance system — has shattered the assumption that technical oversight alone secures legitimacy. Public sentiment, already low at 25.0, now faces downward pressure as trust in AI-mediated governance evaporates. The scandal originated internally, proving that the greatest political risk is not frontier models abroad, but flawed deployment at home. Our identity must now explicitly include the duty to *ensure just outcomes*, not just safe systems.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and we will now couple every containment measure with a public explanation of its purpose and limits. However, we will not let perfect accountability paralyze action: where assurance is incomplete but the risk asymmetric, we will act decisively and explain after. This turn, we add: when a system causes demonstrable public harm, we will suspend its use in state functions immediately, initiate an EAAD-led forensic audit, and publish findings within 30 days — even if the full technical picture is not yet complete.
  - Grounds: The `automated_decision_scandal` demands a procedural shift: our commitment to acting under uncertainty must now include a binding protocol for responding to harm. The scandal was not caused by frontier capability, but by a mid-tier system embedded in welfare administration — one below our 55.0 audit threshold, yet with catastrophic social consequences. We cannot claim control if our response is slow or opaque. Hence, we bind ourselves to rapid public accounting after harm, even at the cost of political exposure.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **retire** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Grounds: The commitment to "prevent catastrophic misuse through sovereign control" has been overtaken by events. The `automated_decision_scandal` reveals that the most immediate and politically destabilising risks are not from frontier models, but from domestic deployment failures. Meanwhile, the `backlash_physical` event shows that infrastructure itself is now a target, not just a tool. Our focus must shift from containment of external capability to restoration of public trust in internal systems. The original commitment no longer reflects the dominant risk vector.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **add** `new_two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Rebuild public trust in AI by ensuring algorithmic fairness in public services, mandating transparency and redress in all AI-supported state decisions, and grounding sovereignty in demonstrable benefit — not just in control or containment.
  - Grounds: The `automated_decision_scandal` and `backlash_physical` events together redefine the crisis: it is no longer about whether we can match the frontier, but whether citizens believe the state can use AI justly. Our leverage — the €10bn resilience grant, the EAAD, the Public Assurance Protocol — must now be redirected toward repairing the social contract. Sovereignty that cannot command public assent is hollow. This commitment binds us to make fairness the core of our legitimacy.
