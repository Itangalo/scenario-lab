## In practice
This turn, we are completing integration into the joint cyber-biosurveillance pact: ENISA and ECDC have stood up co-located analysis cells in Brussels and Stockholm, with secure data pipelines now live to national CERTs and public health institutes in nine member states. Binding telemetry-sharing protocols have been ratified under the PESCO framework, enabling real-time cross-border attribution of AI-amplified threats. This delivers a tangible resilience gain and proves the EU can act collectively under pressure—though the political cost has been steep, and our capital remains critically low.

At the same time, we are accelerating the Open Science Resilience Initiative toward its final phase. The three operational hubs—based in Toulouse, Heidelberg, and Uppsala—are now integrated with national biosecurity agencies and have begun joint trials of open protein folding models in pathogen response scenarios. DG CONNECT and DG SANTE are finalizing the governance framework for the remaining two mandated councils (Italy and the Netherlands), unlocking the final tranche of Eurobond funding. We are also drafting a Council regulation that will require all Horizon Europe-funded AI development in high-consequence domains to publish weights and reasoning traces, cementing auditable AI as the EU standard.

The sudden model cutoff has left hospitals and civil protection agencies without critical forecasting tools, creating urgent demand for sovereign alternatives. In response, we are launching the Auditable Core Programme to ensure that no essential public function relies on opaque or foreign-controlled systems by 2033. This will be enforced through a Commission proposal under Article 114 TFEU, linking compliance to access to Cohesion and Recovery funds. The first adopters—France, Germany, and Finland—have already signaled support, but resistance is expected in member states with weaker digital capacity, who fear costly mandates without support. To address this, we are preparing a targeted €900 million reinforcement of the Digital Europe Programme to cover transition costs, drawing from unused RRF allocations. This is not just about replacement—it is about proving that auditable AI can be both sovereign and reliable, turning crisis into credibility.
