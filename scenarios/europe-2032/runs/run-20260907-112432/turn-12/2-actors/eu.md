## Priority
**Auditable Core Programme** — because it is now the central lever of sovereign agency, and without urgent reinforcement through the Digital Europe Reinforcement, it risks fracturing cohesion and failing in practice, turning a commitment to self-determination into a source of division.

## In practice
This turn, we are declaring the Open Science Resilience Initiative and the joint cyber-biosurveillance integration complete. The three hubs in Toulouse, Heidelberg, and Uppsala are now operationally linked to national biosecurity agencies, and their open protein folding models have passed initial validation in simulated pathogen response scenarios. ENISA and ECDC have activated real-time telemetry sharing across nine member states, with binding protocols now embedded in PESCO frameworks—this marks the first fully operational EU-wide AI-amplified threat detection network. These are real gains, but they have come at the cost of political capital now reduced to zero.

With the Auditable Core Programme now law in draft, we are moving to implementation. The Commission has issued a formal proposal under Article 114 TFEU, mandating the use of sovereign, open-weight AI in critical public functions by 2033. Compliance will be tied to access to Cohesion and Recovery funds, creating a hard incentive for adoption. However, resistance in southern and eastern states—led by Poland, Hungary, and Greece—threatens to stall the entire programme. They argue the mandate is unfunded, unjust, and technologically premature.

To break this impasse, we are launching the Digital Europe Reinforcement: a €900 million reallocation from unused RRF allocations to fund the transition in the most vulnerable member states. This includes technical assistance teams from DG DIGIT, pre-vetted procurement templates, and fast-tracked access to the Open Science Resilience Initiative’s models.
