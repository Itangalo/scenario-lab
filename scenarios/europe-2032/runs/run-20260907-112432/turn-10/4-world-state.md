### The Terminal Surge Continues

Global AI capability advances by 2.0 points this turn to reach 92.0, continuing its steady climb into the terminal zone. At this level, even the developers of frontier systems admit their models operate with internal logic that resists full human interpretation. No new safety protocols have scaled to match this pace, leaving institutions reliant on outcome monitoring rather than process control.

### A Crisis of Access and Trust

The European Union is abruptly cut off from the leading foreign AI model, disrupting hospital diagnostics, infrastructure planning, and emergency forecasting systems across multiple member states. The withdrawal comes without warning or appeal, framed as a routine access policy update but widely interpreted as a geopolitical signal. This exclusion underscores the fragility of external dependencies.

In response, the EU fast-tracks integration into a newly formed international cyber-biosurveillance pact. Real-time telemetry sharing agreements are negotiated, and co-located analysis cells between ENISA, ECDC, and partner states begin initial operations in Brussels and Stockholm. These cells will enable faster joint attribution and coordinated response to AI-amplified threats.

Simultaneously, the Open Science Resilience Initiative progresses unevenly. Binding mandates are issued to five national research councils, but only three hubs demonstrate operational readiness by mid-year. Funding flows through redirected Horizon Europe allocations and a new Eurobond mechanism, though disbursement delays slow hardware procurement. Early open protein folding models show promise in controlled biosecurity trials, reinforcing the strategic value of auditable systems.

Public sentiment remains stagnant at 27.0, caught between alarm over the access cutoff and cautious hope in the resilience push. Political capital erodes further under the weight of two large ongoing measures, with no relief from expiring initiatives.
