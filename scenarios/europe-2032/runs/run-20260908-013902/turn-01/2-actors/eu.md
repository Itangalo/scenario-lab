## In practice
We are treating the cyber_test_shot as a wake-up call: the intrusions were not stopped by existing systems, and were found by accident. This means our current resilience is performative, not operational. In the Justice and Home Affairs Council next month, we will propose activating Article 4 of the NIS2 Directive to mandate baseline detection, response automation, and cross-border data sharing for all operators of essential services. ENISA will be empowered to conduct unannounced red-team audits starting in Q1 2027, funded through a reallocation from the Internal Security Fund. We are offering member states co-financing of up to 70% for SOC modernisation through the Digital Europe Programme to overcome resistance from those citing cost.

At the same time, we are defending the Tech sovereignty package in the Council of the EU, where some member states argue that fast-tracking data centres undermines environmental assessments. We are countering with a compromise: full strategic environmental assessments upfront for entire zones, not per project, to preserve scrutiny without sacrificing speed. The Commission is also negotiating with ASML and three major utilities to co-locate next-gen chip testing with Gigafactory sites, using the Chips Act’s framework to justify urgency. The safety_breakthrough in interpretability gives us leverage—labs are eager to adopt it, and we are offering fast-track validation through a new public research node under the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, which will also serve as a pilot for sovereign model evaluation. But we know the window is narrow: member_state_defection shows that bilateral deals are already eroding our position, and only tangible progress this turn can restore cohesion.
