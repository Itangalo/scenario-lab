### The Age of Consequences  
The EU enters 2030 under fire. A coordinated ransomware assault, powered by AI-generated exploits, cripples hospital networks in three member states. Recovery is slow, trust evaporates, and the incident becomes a symbol: the Union is no longer preparing for AI disruption—it is enduring it. Despite the formal completion of the Public Evaluation Node Expansion, no frontier developer has granted access. The Frontier Access Enforcement Directive, intended to compel compliance, instead triggered withdrawals by two major US labs, who suspended EU operations rather than submit to audits. Evaluation remains blind.

### Sovereignty, Built and Borrowed  
The InvestAI Gigafactories programme concludes its funding phase, with site agreements in Finland, Poland, and southern France. Construction has begun, but no operational EU-controlled frontier compute exists yet. Still, the tangible progress yields a modest sovereignty boost. With ai_capability rising by 1.5 this turn, the −1 decay applies. No category 4 measure completes this turn—final delivery milestones for the Tech Sovereignty Package remain unmet, deferring its sovereignty impact. Thus, eu_ai_sovereignty falls to 14.0.

### Resilience in Crisis Mode  
In response to the cyberattack and collapsing public confidence, the EU proposes the Sovereign Resilience Emergency Activation—a large category 6 measure. However, due to the existing portfolio burden (three ongoing large measures), full launch is delayed. Only preliminary coordination begins: Germany, the Nordics, and Benelux agree in principle to joint monitoring, but Hungary and Slovakia withhold commitment pending cost guarantees. No implementation milestones are achieved this turn. Resilience sees only partial stabilization, edging up slightly from emergency consultations but not from operational changes.

### Political Capital: Cost and Calculus  
The portfolio charge remains high: InvestAI Gigafactories (−3), Public Evaluation Node Expansion (−3), Sovereign Resilience Hardening Programme (−3), Frontier Access Enforcement Directive (−3), and priority (−1), totaling −13. The proposed resilience measure does not yet incur its −3 charge, avoiding overload. The failure to act decisively costs legitimacy, but the mere proposal earns a +1 bonus for acknowledging a severe incident. Completion of the Labour Transition Accelerator and Public Evaluation Node Expansion yields +2 (each +1). Public sentiment drops further due to backlash_physical and election fears, now at 25.0. These gains are offset by the inertia of crisis and lack of visible delivery. Political capital remains unchanged at 19.0. The Frontier Access Enforcement Directive remains the named priority this turn; no new priority is declared.

### Open-Source Capability: A Pause in Momentum  
The openweight capability shows no growth this turn. With ai_capability rising by 1.5, openweight_capability would typically gain from diffusion, but the absence of major open model releases, coordinated open-source sprints, or new leaks limits spillover. The narrative of stagnation holds: no significant open-weight progress occurs. Thus, openweight_capability remains at 52.0, preserving the gap with the frontier while complying with the principle that gains require cause.
