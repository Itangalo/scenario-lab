## In practice  
The Coalition for Enforceable AI Governance is now formally in flight, with the High Representative convening the first ministerial dialogue in Brussels next month under the Digital Partnerships Initiative. We are offering mutual recognition of safety audits and fast-tracked data-sharing through the emergent open-source intelligence alliance to lower the cost of compliance for partners. The real leverage lies in coordinated export controls: we are working with Japan and the Netherlands to map chokepoints in lithography tools and specialty gases, preparing joint thresholds that would restrict access for labs failing to meet coalition standards. But progress is slow — Seoul and Tokyo remain wary, demanding carve-outs for national champions, and without movement by turn 15, the measure risks stalling into irrelevance. We are holding firm: no exemptions, but flexibility in implementation timelines for those who commit to a verifiable roadmap.

Meanwhile, the Frontier Monitoring and Incident Response Protocol has formally concluded its implementation phase. We have secured full telemetry access from one EU-founded lab under Dutch jurisdiction, but the three largest providers — all with U.S. parentage — continue to withhold training logs, citing national security directives. The protocol is operational but asymmetric, and without coalition leverage or sovereign compute, its authority remains contested. We are now drafting a compliance escalation path, to be activated if access is not granted by turn 14, potentially invoking Article 114 TFEU with coalition backing — but only if we can show domestic capacity to act.

That is why we are launching the Autonomous Public Services Sovereignty Initiative. We are redirecting €1.2 billion from the Digital Europe Programme to create a fast-track fund for municipalities and regions that adopt AI systems with full source transparency, local audit rights, and kill-switch compliance. The Autonomous Public Services Pact already includes 47 cities across eight member states — we will meet with their leaders in Lyon next month to align technical standards and procurement templates. This is not just about diffusion; it is about proving that the EU can deliver safe, sovereign AI where it touches citizens directly. If we can make this work in emergency response and public health, we begin to rebuild legitimacy from the ground up — and create a domestic base of support that no foreign developer or diplomatic warning can override.
