## Priority  
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)** — This remains the measure I am pushing hardest this turn because it is the only one with a hard deadline: the binding ENISA standards must be adopted by member states before the next major release cycle hits, and without them, the Union remains exposed to cascading cyber failure despite our technical gains.

## In practice  
We are in the final stretch of securing qualified majority support for CS-CO, with the Permanent Representatives Committee meeting weekly to resolve objections from Hungary and Slovakia. The €1.2bn Implementation Fund is being leveraged as both incentive and enforcement tool: disbursement is tied to adoption of automated patching systems and real-time threat-sharing, with ENISA conducting on-site audits starting in October. We are framing this not as central overreach but as the institutionalisation of a defensive breakthrough the Union itself helped fund — and we are prepared to invoke Article 4 of NIS2 if voluntary compliance fails.

On the labour front, the LTA is now operational in pilot form, but political friction is mounting. The 15% levy is under legal challenge in two member states, and national co-financing delays threaten to starve the Polish and French programmes by turn 4. We are responding by fast-tracking bilateral agreements on credential recognition and offering temporary top-up funding from the European Social Fund+, but the narrative of “punitive taxation” is gaining traction. Public sentiment remains fragile, and we are seeing early signs of campaign_backlash crystallising around regional protests against data centre expansions.

Behind the scenes, the new Sovereign Evaluation Access Pact is being negotiated through DG CONNECT and the European Commission’s AI Office, using the credibility of our emerging evaluation infrastructure — housed in the public research node — as leverage. We are offering labs fast-track validation for AI Act compliance in exchange for model access, with a tiered framework based on capability thresholds. This is not charity: it is how we close the intelligence gap on systems that already operate inside our borders, and how we begin to turn safety into a vector of sovereignty. The campaign_atlanticist voices are being quietly mobilised to argue that shared technical understanding strengthens alliance resilience — not undermines it.
