### Acceleration and Anxiety
AI capability advances to 58.0 as frontier labs compress release cycles, deploying agents that refine their own reasoning in real time. While no single system crosses a dramatic threshold, the cumulative effect widens the offensive cyber frontier and raises concerns about autonomous coordination. ### Defending the Core
The Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO) inches forward. ENISA completes audits in seven member states, and automated threat-sharing activates in Germany and the Netherlands. However, Hungary and Slovakia continue to resist full adoption, citing national sovereignty, despite the lure of the €1.2bn fund. The Commission signals readiness to invoke NIS2 enforcement, but legal preparations delay any formal action until next turn.

### Labour Tensions Mount
The Labour Transition Accelerator (LTA) struggles with implementation. Legal challenges to the 15% automation levy stall in national courts, and co-financing shortfalls threaten the Polish pilot. Public frustration grows as media spotlight displaced legal professionals and mid-level engineers. Regional protests against new data centre projects intensify, fed by a rising narrative that AI benefits only distant corporations and urban elites.

### A Bid for Technical Leverage
The EU launches negotiations for the Sovereign Evaluation Access Pact, offering fast-track AI Act compliance in exchange for access to frontier models. Initial responses from US labs are cautious; while some see value in streamlined regulation, others resist ceding control over model evaluation. The campaign_atlanticist voices gain traction in policy circles, arguing that shared technical insight strengthens collective security—but no binding commitments emerge this turn.
