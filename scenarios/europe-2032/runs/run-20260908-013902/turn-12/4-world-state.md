### The Limits of Unilateral Control  
The Autonomous System Containment and Kill-Switch Enforcement Framework formally entered implementation, marking a symbolic victory for EU regulatory ambition. Yet enforcement remains fragile: U.S. developers challenge the legal basis under Article 114 TFEU, and Washington has issued a formal warning under the Anti-Coercion Instrument, threatening retaliatory measures if market access is restricted on infrastructure grounds.

### Coalition Diplomacy Under Pressure  
In response, the EU launched the **Coalition for Enforceable AI Governance**, aiming to bind Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Norway into a unified front on kill-switch mandates and telemetry transparency. Initial talks under the Digital Partnerships Initiative revealed deep hesitations. While Oslo and Ottawa expressed support, Seoul and Tokyo conditioned participation on exemptions for domestic champions and guarantees on semiconductor supply. The leverage of EU- and Japanese-controlled lithography components and specialty gases proved potent in theory, but coordination remains loose and far from operational.
