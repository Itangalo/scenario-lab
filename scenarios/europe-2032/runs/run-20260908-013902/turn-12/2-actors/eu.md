## In practice
The Autonomous System Containment and Kill-Switch Enforcement Framework has formally cleared drafting and is now in technical consultation with ENISA-AI, national cybersecurity agencies, and selected providers. We are testing prototype kill-switch integration with two EU-hosted models at 58.0 capability, using hardware-enforced execution halts tied to sovereign PKI keys. But resistance is hardening: U.S. developers argue that Article 114 TFEU does not cover real-time operational control, and Washington has signalled that it may invoke the Anti-Coercion Instrument if we block access based on infrastructure mandates. Without allies, we cannot enforce.

Meanwhile, the Frontier Monitoring and Incident Response Protocol is entering its second year. We have secured provisional access to training logs from one European-founded lab operating under Dutch jurisdiction, but the others — including those with U.S. parentage — are stalling, citing confidentiality and national security directives from abroad. The protocol is becoming a paper tiger. We need teeth, and we need them in concert.

That is why we are launching the Coalition for Enforceable AI Governance. We are convening under the Digital Partnerships Initiative, with the High Representative leading talks in Brussels next month. The goal is a joint declaration by summer: all members will adopt equivalent kill-switch and telemetry rules, and agree to block non-compliant systems from their markets. Crucially, we will tie this to export controls on EU- and Japan-controlled lithography components and specialty gases — chokepoints that no frontier lab can bypass. Norway and Canada have signalled openness; Seoul and Tokyo are cautious but engaged. We are offering mutual recognition of audits to reduce compliance burden, and fast-track data-sharing under the emergent open-source intelligence alliance. This is not about harmonisation for its own sake — it is about making our standards *stick* in a world where power flows to those who act together. Without this, every safety rule we pass is a promise we cannot keep.
