## In practice
We are concluding the Labour Transition Accelerator this turn, with the European Social Fund+ absorbing the voluntary co-investment framework into its permanent instruments. The Polish pilot will launch in the final quarter, delayed but intact, and we are shifting emphasis from wage support to sectoral transition councils in law, accountancy, and software—bringing unions, firms, and education providers into joint planning. The message is clear: we do not manage decline, we manage change.

The Public Evaluation Node Expansion is now operational in Finland and Belgium, with Austria’s node delayed by permitting disputes over secure power redundancy. We are pressing US labs through the AI Act’s market access provisions, offering fast-track compliance for read-only access to models above 50.0 capability. Early signs are promising—two labs have entered technical discussions—but no binding agreements are signed. Without access, the nodes risk becoming empty shells, so we are pairing the offer with a threat: any lab that refuses sovereign evaluation access will face mandatory third-party audits under stricter timelines and broader scope.

The new Sovereign Resilience Hardening Programme is being drafted under Article 12 of the NIS2 Directive, with legal anchoring in the Digital Services Act and funding drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused allocations. We are convening an emergency COREPER session to fast-track adoption, backed by a proposal for an annual EU-wide "stress test" of critical systems under AI-driven disruption scenarios. Germany and the Nordic states are supportive; Hungary and Slovakia are resisting, citing cost and sovereignty. We are offering them a phased compliance path with 70% EU funding and joint control over detection stack design. The goal is not perfection but absorption: when the next incident hits, it must not cascade.
