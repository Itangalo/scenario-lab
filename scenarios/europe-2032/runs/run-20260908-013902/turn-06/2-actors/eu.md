## In practice
We are concluding both the Tech Sovereignty Package and the Labour Transition Accelerator this turn. The former achieved its target of catalysing €200bn in private commitments, though permitting delays in France and Italy mean physical build-out lags. We are folding its accelerated-permitting zones into the ongoing InvestAI Gigafactories programme, ensuring continuity. The LTA, while limited by its voluntary model, established sectoral transition councils in ten member states and redirected €12bn in co-investment. We are transferring its framework to the European Social Fund+, with binding participation for firms receiving public AI procurement contracts — turning incentive into expectation.

The Public Evaluation Node Expansion is now technically operational in Finland and Belgium, but remains underutilised due to lack of access. Austria’s node will come online in turn 7 after resolution of the power redundancy dispute. We are now acting on the threat we signalled last turn: under Article 48 of the AI Act, we are initiating proceedings against two US-based developers who have refused read-only access to models above 50.0 capability. The Frontier Access Enforcement Directive, drafted under the same legal base, will make this a standing requirement — not a negotiation. The European AI Office is being staffed with red-team leads and verification specialists, and we are offering compliant labs fast-track certification and mutual recognition with like-minded partners.

The Sovereign Resilience Hardening Programme is advancing under Article 12 of NIS2, with Germany and the Nordics committed to full adoption. Hungary and Slovakia remain opposed to binding mandates, but we have secured a compromise: 70% EU funding, joint design of detection stacks, and a two-year extension for full compliance. The first stress test design — simulating a coordinated AI-driven disinformation and cyber cascade during election season — is being developed by ENISA with input from national CIRTs. The goal is not to prevent all incidents, but to ensure no single event collapses public trust or disables essential functions. This is resilience as sovereignty: when control is gone, endurance is the only power left.
