## New measure  
**Public Evaluation Node Expansion**  
Establish a distributed, high-assurance evaluation infrastructure with direct access to frontier models—foreign and domestic—capable of continuous monitoring, agent behaviour analysis, and red-teaming of deployed systems.  

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 8–12 points through persistent, independent assessment; stabilise eu_ai_sovereignty by creating a sovereign technical foothold; reduce dependence on voluntary lab cooperation by building institutional capacity to audit and stress-test models under real-world conditions.  
`Applies to:` EU-based public research institutions, with formal access agreements sought from US and UK frontier labs, conditional on market access and AI Act compliance.

## Priority  
**Public Evaluation Node Expansion** — This is now the measure I am pushing hardest this turn because it directly addresses the collapse in our technical leverage: without sovereign evaluation, we cannot verify safety, anticipate threats, or negotiate from strength — and at ai_capability 58.0, waiting for full industrial parity is a surrender of agency.

## In practice  
We are concluding the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators this turn, with ENISA certifying Germany, the Netherlands, and five others as fully compliant. For Hungary and Slovakia, we are activating the NIS2 dispute mechanism, referring non-compliance to the CJEU while releasing half the €1.2bn incentive as a bridge — enough to fund threat-sharing nodes but not full automation, preserving pressure for full adoption by turn 5. The shield is now operational in core sectors, and we are shifting focus from negotiation to exercise: a cross-border cyber incident drill will be conducted in May, simulating a coordinated attack on energy grid operators using AI-generated zero-days.

On labour, we are restructuring the LTA to survive legal challenges: the 15% automation levy is being reframed as a voluntary co-investment scheme, with matching funds now drawn from the European Social Fund+ and conditional on regional upskilling plans rather than direct taxation. This avoids immediate court blocks but slows rollout — Poland’s pilot will now launch in turn 5, not 4. We are pairing this with a public communication campaign highlighting AI-assisted diagnostics in public hospitals and permit processing in municipalities, aiming to shift public_sentiment from backlash to visible benefit.

The new Public Evaluation Node Expansion is being led by DG CONNECT in coordination with the European AI Office and a consortium of national research labs. We are repurposing €4.2bn from the stalled parts of the Tech Sovereignty Package to fund secure compute clusters in Finland, Belgium, and Austria, designed to host vetted models under air-gapped conditions. Our leverage is the AI Act’s market access clause: we are offering labs a fast-track compliance pathway in exchange for read-only, monitored access to models above 50.0 capability. The campaign_atlanticist network is being mobilised to frame this not as extraction, but as alliance infrastructure — a shared immune system for democratic AI. Without this, we are blind to the systems shaping our future.
