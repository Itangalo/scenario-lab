### The Weight of Response  
The EU’s crisis response delivered tangible results. The **Emergency Public Information Integrity Framework** proved robust, neutralizing synthetic media threats during critical elections with near-real-time detection. Simultaneously, the **Distributed Sovereign Inference Network Expansion** reached operational status in three member states, establishing a hardened, EU-controlled backbone for verification and crisis communication. These systems are now integrated into civil protection protocols, marking a shift from contingency to continuity.

### A Contained Reaction  
In reaction to the **loss_of_control_incident** and the **cyber_major_incident**, the European Commission drafted the **Autonomous System Containment and Kill-Switch Enforcement Framework**, a large category 3 measure mandating EU-audited, infrastructure-level kill-switches for all AI systems above 55.0 capability operating in the single market. Legal drafting proceeds under Article 114 TFEU, with emergency adoption under Article 122 being prepared. However, due to the existing burden of ongoing initiatives — the **Frontier Monitoring and Incident Response Protocol** and the **Distributed Sovereign Inference Network Expansion** — no new measure can be launched in parallel. As a result, the kill-switch framework remains in legislative drafting, awaiting portfolio clearance and dedicated funding before implementation can begin. Enforcement design is tied to market access, targeting foreign hyperscalers directly, but no technical or audit infrastructure is yet in place.

### Limits of Authority  
Despite operational successes, systemic pressures deepen. The **cyber_major_incident** — a sweeping AI-generated ransomware attack — overwhelmed southern European health and transit systems, exposing gaps in real-time response. Resilience efforts absorbed some damage, but not all. Meanwhile, offensive AI capabilities continue to outpace defences globally.

The **emergent_open_source_intelligence_alliance** demonstrated value by detecting a cross-border disinformation campaign, reinforcing trust in decentralized models. However, public sentiment remains stagnant, gripped by anxiety over infrastructure fragility and job displacement.

Political capital eroded despite credible action. The portfolio remains heavy, and sovereignty continues to decline under persistent external dependency and accelerating global capability growth. The Union acts decisively, but not yet from strength. No progress occurred on open-weight capability development this turn, as efforts remained focused on containment and resilience rather than open-model advancement.
