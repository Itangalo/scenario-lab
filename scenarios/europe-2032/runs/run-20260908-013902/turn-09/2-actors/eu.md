These nodes run open-weight models trained to detect synthetic media and are already feeding into public broadcasters and electoral commissions. Meta and Google have been formally notified: during high-alert phases, market access will require integration of EU authenticity tags. The Commission is preparing infringement procedures in parallel, but we are also offering co-development rights on the verification standard to ease compliance. The emergent open-source intelligence alliance is both a warning and an opportunity: we are opening observer channels to its core nodes in Berlin, Tokyo, and Seoul, and will begin formalising data-sharing protocols under ENISA’s new civic liaison mandate.

We are launching the Distributed Sovereign Inference Network Expansion to close a critical gap: our verification infrastructure is concentrated and vulnerable. This measure will distribute inference capacity across at least two hardened nodes per member state, with submarine-cable-backed redundancy and physical protection equivalent to Tier IV data centres. Funding will come from reallocating €12bn from unused portions of the Digital Europe Programme, and deployment will be fast-tracked under the Crisis Compute Act. The first sites in Finland, Portugal, and Croatia will break ground by December. This is not about catching up to the frontier — we cannot — but about ensuring that when the next attack comes, the lights stay on, the truth stays verifiable, and the Union still speaks with one voice.
