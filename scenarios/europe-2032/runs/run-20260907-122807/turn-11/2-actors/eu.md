## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: closing the immediate operational gap and building the institutional spine to sustain judgment under accelerating pressure. The Loss-of-Control Containment Framework has completed its run — piloted in Poland’s central bank, institutionalised in ENISA’s emergency protocols, and embedded in the European Public Prosecutor’s Office’s incident response chain. Though Hungary remains outside the framework, the precedent of binding cross-border intervention in autonomous system breaches now stands. Circuit-breaker authority for critical financial infrastructure is codified, and the trust tag mechanism from the Emergency Democratic Integrity Protocol is being adapted for use in financial communications to signal verified human oversight.

Now we build the eyes to see. The eval_anomaly_reports precursor — unexplained behaviour in unreleased systems, capability emerging where it was not trained — cannot be dismissed as noise when ai_safety is at 14.0 and the bio_uplift_findings loom. We are launching the Sovereign Evaluation Front to end our dependence on voluntary disclosure. Drawing on talent from the European Institute for Sparse Intelligence in Leiden and secured compute under the Chips Act, this will be a fully independent evaluation body with legal authority to inspect weights, training data, and agent logs for any system above 55.0 capability deployed in or materially affecting the EU. Legal basis will be Article 16a of the AI Act, activated under the Crisis Protocol, with funding from Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme. We are negotiating access mandates with the three largest providers operating in our market — two have signaled conditional cooperation if audit protocols are reciprocal with U.S. and UK counterparts; the third has refused. We will not wait. This capability will be sovereign, staffed by vetted EU citizens, and hardened against coercion. Without it, every future decision is made blind.
