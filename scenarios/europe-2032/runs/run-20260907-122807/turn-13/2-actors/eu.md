## New measure  
**Union-Wide Labour Transition Compact**  
Establish a centrally funded but nationally administered transition framework for workers displaced by AI in critical public-facing sectors, with wage insurance, fast-tracked reskilling into green and care economies, and incentives for employers to redeploy rather than replace.

## Priority

## In practice  
We are enforcing the Critical Infrastructure Hardening Protocol through binding directives under Article 114 TFEU, with implementation monitored by ENISA and the European Programme for Critical Infrastructure Protection (EPCIP). The three-way requirement — adversarial detection, 90-second human fallback, and cross-border failover — is now being audited in real time across 27 national regulators. Germany’s demand for delay is met not with concession but with sovereign compute vouchers tied to early compliance; Austria’s constitutional concerns are being addressed through a joint legal opinion from the Commission and the Court of Justice, fast-tracked for Q1 2033. Private operators are resisting cost exposure, so we are redirecting €1.8 billion from REACT-EU and the Digital Europe Programme to co-fund retrofitting AI safety stacks in grid control and hospital IT systems. This is not optional: non-compliance triggers suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from future EU AI procurement.

At the same time, the Sovereign Evaluation Front nears operational readiness. By turn 15, it will have conducted its first independent assessment of a model above 65.0 capability used in financial infrastructure, with full access to weights secured under the AI Act’s Article 28a. We are already seeing pushback from one U.S.-based developer refusing data access, citing extraterritoriality — a test case we will escalate through the European Court, using enforcement mechanisms built during the GDPR precedent. This capability is not just about safety scores; it is about sovereignty. If we cannot see what the models are doing, we cannot protect what they are running.

And yet, we cannot ignore the streets. The labour_displacement event — over 120,000 entry-level professional jobs lost in six months, concentrated in legal, accounting and administrative functions — is eroding legitimacy faster than any technical failure. Public sentiment at 27.0 means even necessary measures read as elite projects blind to lived reality. So we are launching the Union-Wide Labour Transition Compact: a large-scale, multi-year instrument that pairs wage insurance (up to 75% of previous income for 18 months) with reskilling pathways into renewable deployment, elderly care, and digital stewardship roles. Funding flows through EURES and the European Social Fund+, but delivery is national — with conditionality: member states must report quarterly on placement rates or risk rebalancing. This is not charity; it is strategic absorption. Without it, resilience remains brittle at the human level, no matter how hardened the grids become.
