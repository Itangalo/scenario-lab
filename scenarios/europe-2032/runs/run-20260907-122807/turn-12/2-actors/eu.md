## New measure  
**Critical Infrastructure Hardening Protocol**  
Launch a binding, time-bound programme to harden AI-managed energy, transport, and public service systems against adversarial model-generated cyberattacks and emergent instabilities, with mandatory stress testing and fallback procedures.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 16  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to resilience by ensuring all critical AI-automated systems have verified manual override, adversarial detection, and cross-border failover capacity; reduce likelihood of cascading failure from emergent grid instability probing and cyber_major_incident  
`Applies to:` ENTSO-E, national energy regulators, transport authorities in all member states, EU-level digital infrastructure operators, and providers of AI systems above 50.0 capability used in critical functions

## Priority  
**Critical Infrastructure Hardening Protocol** — because the cyber_major_incident and emergent_grid_instability_probing events prove that attack is already here, not hypothetical, and with ai_safety at 9.0 and offensive capability embedded in open tooling, we cannot wait for evaluation to catch up before we protect the lights from going out.

## In practice  
We are acting on the ground now, not in theory. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that crippled hospital scheduling in three countries and disrupted municipal services across the Benelux — landed with precision and scale no human team could match. It exploited known API vulnerabilities in AI-mediated workflow systems, but its mutation speed and evasion logic were clearly synthetic. At the same time, the emergent_grid_instability_probing attacks show that adversaries are testing our weakest feedback loops in real time. Confidence in autonomous systems is eroding, and some member states are already calling for a moratorium on AI in grid management. We cannot afford paralysis, nor can we afford failure.

So we are launching the Critical Infrastructure Hardening Protocol under Article 114 TFEU, with co-decision in the Council and Parliament under the digital resilience framework. This is not voluntary. By turn 16, every AI system above 50.0 capability managing energy distribution, rail signalling, emergency services dispatch, or public health data flows must demonstrate: (1) real-time adversarial anomaly detection, (2) auditable fallback to human-operated mode within 90 seconds, and (3) participation in a cross-border failover network coordinated by ENISA and the EDA. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with conditional grants tied to compliance. We will face resistance — Germany wants more time, Austria fears overreach, and private operators cite cost — but we will offer fast-track access to sovereign compute and evaluation support under the Sovereign Evaluation Front in exchange for early adoption. This is not just defence; it is the foundation of trust. If the public cannot believe the grid will hold, no amount of evaluation will save us.
