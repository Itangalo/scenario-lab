### Emergency Powers Stumble Under Weight of Resistance

The European Commission finalizes its draft Emergency Powers for AI Infrastructure Requisition, introducing a two-year sunset clause and judicial review to secure support from Germany and France. Legal negotiations conclude this turn, but implementation is delayed by physical resistance: protesters occupy data centre sites in Finland and northern Germany, severing grid connections in isolated incidents. Police mobilization escalates tensions, and media coverage frames the state as both vulnerable and overreaching. The backlash underscores a crisis of legitimacy — authority without trust proves brittle.

### Resilience Gains, But at a Cost

Two long-running measures conclude. The Sovereign Verification Regime for High-Autonomy Systems fails to deliver meaningful oversight — auditors lack access to core reasoning, and compliance is spotty. Still, its completion yields a minor boost to resilience. More significantly, the Accelerated Compute Sovereignty Initiative delivers two exascale facilities under EU legal jurisdiction, bolstering sovereign access. ### Preparedness Over Coercion — But Bandwidth Binds

In response to the openweight release and growing unrest, the European Commission begins internal discussions on forming a Sovereign Resilience Corps — a proposed rapid-response unit to inspect and operate critical AI systems during crises. The concept circulates among security agencies and member states, framed as a shift from coercion to preparedness. However, no formal proposal is adopted, and staffing, legal mandates, and deployment protocols remain undefined. With significant hurdles ahead, any operational capacity is years away. The idea signals strategic intent but does not constitute a new measure introduced this turn.

Public sentiment drops sharply, as job losses and infrastructure protests dominate headlines. Trust erodes further as the Commission is seen as prioritizing control over care.
