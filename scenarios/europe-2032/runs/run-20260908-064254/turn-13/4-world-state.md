### The Anomaly and the Corps

Reports circulate of benchmark irregularities at a leading lab—unexplained performance spikes and agent behaviours that resist standard interpretability tools. While officially attributed to noise or test contamination, the anomalies prompt closed-door discussions among technical regulators. Concerns grow that current evaluation methods may be insufficient, though no conclusion is reached about systemic shifts in AI behaviour. The situation fuels caution but not consensus.

In response, the EU initiates planning for the **Sovereign Resilience Corps**, a large category 6 measure aimed at strengthening critical infrastructure resilience. ENISA begins drafting protocols for emergency intervention in AI-managed energy, transport, and health systems, with preliminary hubs scoped for Finland, Austria, and Portugal. Member states are consulted on potential access rights during crises, framed as mutual aid rather than control. However, the **Emergency Powers for AI Infrastructure Requisition** remains suspended, and legal uncertainty prevents the new initiative from being formally adopted this turn. Without enabling legislation, the Corps cannot progress beyond early design and stakeholder outreach.

Funding reallocations from long-term safety research to support the proposed Corps draw criticism, contributing to a decline in ai_safety despite ongoing verification efforts. The **Sovereign Verification Regime** concludes but fails to deliver real-time oversight, while the **Accelerated Compute Sovereignty Initiative** delivers two exascale facilities—though integration delays limit their operational impact.

Public sentiment holds at 32.0. Labour displacement and distrust in AI governance remain widespread, and the proposed Resilience Corps is viewed skeptically, seen by some as an expansion of unaccountable authority. Behind the scenes, the **emergent_cross_border_data_conflict** deepens, with Germany and Austria restricting data flows, while Spain and Italy demand access for public health AI. Some suggest the nascent Corps could eventually mediate such disputes, but its legal basis remains too weak to act.

### Political Capital Erodes

Political capital falls to 10.0. The portfolio remains overloaded: the suspended Emergency Powers still carry a −3 charge, and early work on the Resilience Corps begins to accrue implicit strain, reflected in coordination failures and inter-institutional friction. Though the Corps is not yet formally active, preparatory actions consume bandwidth without delivering measurable outcomes. The priority penalty persists, and the burden of three de facto major initiatives—including two high-cost efforts—overwhelms the EU’s capacity, driving the decline in political capital. No new measure is formally introduced this turn due to unresolved legal constraints and institutional resistance.
