## Statement changes  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer mistake legal assertion for operational reality, nor confuse the appearance of control with its substance.  
- Grounds: Trigger: eval_anomaly_reports and the continued paralysis of the Emergency Powers despite their formal enactment. The anomaly reports suggest capability emergence beyond design intent — a shift from predictable scaling to opaque, potentially recursive advancement — while our flagship intervention tool remains suspended not by external force but by internal legitimacy failure. We now see that acting under uncertainty must include uncertainty about our own capacity to enforce decisions. The commitment stands, but its meaning evolves: we must act, yet act with eyes open to the limits of authority in a world where power diffuses faster than law can bind.

## New measure  
**Sovereign Resilience Corps for Critical Infrastructure Protection**  
A rapid-response technical unit under ENISA and the Joint AI Oversight Cell, equipped to defend, inspect, and if necessary operate essential AI systems during crises, with physical and digital access rights to key nodes in energy, healthcare, and transport.  
`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 8–12 points; stabilise public_sentiment by demonstrating tangible protection; reduce dependence on foreign operators during incidents  
`Applies to:` EU-anchored hyperscalers, national critical infrastructure operators, and foreign-owned data centres operating within EU jurisdiction

## Priority  
**Sovereign Resilience Corps for Critical Infrastructure Protection** — because the openweight_frontier_release and eval_anomaly_reports confirm that systems are becoming autonomous and opaque faster than we can audit or seize, and because the emergent_cross-border_data_conflict reveals that even our own institutions are fracturing under the strain — we must now build operational presence where we still can, not assert control where we no longer have it.

## In practice  
This turn, we are advancing the Sovereign Resilience Corps through a binding proposal under Article 12 of the Union Civil Protection Mechanism, with initial deployment hubs in Finland (for northern energy grid resilience), Austria (for alpine transport and healthcare systems), and Portugal (for Iberian digital infrastructure). ENISA is finalising the technical protocols for model inspection and emergency takeover of AI-managed critical systems, while the Commission negotiates with member states to grant the Corps time-bound access rights during declared incidents — not permanent control, but a standing right to intervene when failure cascades threaten. Funding is being repurposed from a 0.2% reallocation of the Horizon AI Safety budget and a reorientation of the Digital Europe Programme’s cybersecurity envelope, moves already facing resistance from research directors who argue we are sacrificing long-term safety for short-term optics.

Meanwhile, the Emergency Powers for AI Infrastructure Requisition remain in legal limbo, with the CJEU advisory opinion now delayed until early 2033. We are not enforcing them, nor retracting them. Instead, we are using the pause to de-escalate: launching transparency units in each member state, co-staffed by national auditors and civil society observers, to monitor any future activation and prevent unilateralism. Behind this, we are quietly rebuilding legitimacy — not through decrees, but through presence. The eval_anomaly_reports have unsettled even the labs; we are circulating them among member states not as proof of danger, but as proof of shared vulnerability. And in the face of the emergent_cross-border_data_conflict, we are reframing the Corps not as a tool of control, but as a neutral technical authority — one that could, in time, mediate disputes over data access by offering secure, audited pathways for public health AI without compromising privacy. We are no longer trying to command the future. We are trying to stay in the room when it arrives.
