## In practice
This turn, we are conducting post-mortems on both the **Sovereign Verification Regime** and the **Accelerated Compute Sovereignty Initiative**, treating their formal completion not as success but as a diagnostic. The verification regime, while technically sound, exposed a fatal gap: we can audit behaviour, but we cannot inspect what we do not own. The compute initiative secured financing and permitting—but not access, not control, not enforceable rights. The promised facilities exist only on paper, their construction stalled by national vetoes and corporate foot-dragging.

We are now convening an emergency session of the European Council under Article 42 TEU to assess the integrity of the Union’s strategic posture, preparing the ground for binding intervention rights. ENISA, in coordination with the newly empowered Joint AI Oversight Cell, is drafting a crisis tasking protocol that would allow the Commission to requisition compute capacity and model access during systemic incidents—even against the will of operators—modeled on the mechanism used during the 2022 energy crisis. Behind the scenes, we are compiling evidence of non-compliance: Germany’s delayed grid approvals, France’s unilateral negotiations with NVIDIA, and Finland’s quiet export deals. This is not merely about infrastructure—it is about whether the Union can act as one when its survival is tested. The two-year commitment closes with a warning: capability without control is an illusion, and autonomy without authority is surrender.
