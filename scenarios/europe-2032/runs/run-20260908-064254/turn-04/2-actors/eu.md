## In practice

This turn, we act on the lesson of the **cyber_major_incident**: that defensive lag is now lethal. While the European Agent Observatory builds long-term visibility, it is research-led and too slow for immediate protection. We therefore launch a binding directive under the revised NIS2 framework, using Article 8(2) to mandate adoption of anomaly detection systems that feed directly into both national CSIRTs and the Observatory’s early-warning layer. The protocol requires automated alert escalation, machine-readable threat sharing, and participation in a new EU-wide “Red Sky” drill series simulating cascading AI-driven failures. We assign ENISA to coordinate implementation, with funding drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and conditional cohesion transfers — ensuring even reluctant states adopt the standard.

At the same time, we defend the Sovereign Control Stack in inter-institutional talks, where northern member states continue to resist central kill-switch authority. To break the deadlock, we propose a hybrid governance model: operational decisions require consensus among a newly formed High Council for AI Resilience, composed of national cybersecurity leads, but the Commission retains emergency override — exercisable only after review by the European AI Safety Institute. This preserves collective legitimacy while ensuring decisive action remains possible. The €9.6bn package holds, though staffing remains tight as the Agent Observatory draws from the same talent pool.

We also respond to the **emergent_lab_cooperation** by formalising its role: the Commission issues a call under Horizon Europe to expand the consortium into a permanent EU Defence AI Collaborative, focused on open-source detection tools and adversarial evaluation. This strengthens category 5 capacity without duplicating the Observatory, and roots assurance in European science rather than imported frameworks. Still, public sentiment softens further — moratoriums gain traction, and protests grow near proposed Gigafactory sites. We prepare a counter-narrative linking safety, sovereignty, and social stability, but know it will take more than messaging to restore trust.
