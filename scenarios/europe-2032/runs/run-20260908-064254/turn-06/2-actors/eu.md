## New measure

**Sovereign Verification Regime for High-Autonomy Systems**

A binding requirement for all AI systems operating above a defined autonomy threshold in critical domains to submit to real-time behavioural verification by EU-accredited auditors, with access to internal logs, agent trajectories, and decision rationales.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +8 (via enforced transparency into agent behaviour), eu_ai_sovereignty +5 (by asserting jurisdiction over system operation, not just deployment), resilience +3 (through early detection of emergent misalignment)  
`Applies to:` frontier AI developers with systems operating in the EU, national regulators, ENISA, EU-accredited third-party auditors, European Agent Observatory

## Priority

**Mandatory Anomaly Detection & Response Protocols for Critical Infrastructure** — because without universal, interoperable detection now, the new **European AI Incident Response Corps** will have nowhere to plug in when the next attack hits, and because this directive is the only binding instrument this turn that closes a known, lethal gap in time.

## In practice

This turn, we finalise the implementation of the **Mandatory Anomaly Detection & Response Protocols**, leveraging the NIS2 legal base and conditioning cohesion fund disbursements on compliance. ENISA leads a technical surge, deploying interoperability envoys to national CSIRTs—especially in Visegrád states—to integrate their detection stacks with the European Agent Observatory’s early-warning layer. The first “Red Sky” cross-border drill, simulating an AI-driven cascade across health and energy, will validate the system in Q1, exposing gaps before they are exploited. We accept that observation without enforcement is ritual, which is why we are simultaneously operationalising the **European AI Incident Response Corps (EAIRC)**: recruitment begins under the Digital Europe Programme, and we push COREPER II to amend NIS2 with emergency override powers, offering judicial review safeguards to secure northern support.

Now that monitoring is in place, we go further: we cannot rely on developers’ self-reporting when agents act beyond human oversight. That is why we launch the **Sovereign Verification Regime for High-Autonomy Systems**, a new measure under Category 1 that institutionalises independent, real-time evaluation of agent behaviour. Drawing on the Observatory’s data, EU-accredited auditors—rotated across member states and backed by interpretability tooling from public research labs—will verify that high-autonomy systems remain within their operational bounds. This is not a post-hoc audit; it is continuous scrutiny of live agent reasoning, with authority to trigger EAIRC intervention if anomalies escalate. The regime applies to all systems above the autonomy threshold in critical sectors, closing the gap between detection and accountability.

Public sentiment remains fragile at 39.0, and we do not mistake legitimacy for control. But nor do we accept paralysis in the face of risk. The verification regime includes transparency obligations: logs used in audits will feed anonymised datasets into public research, and ex-post reviews by national parliaments and the European Ombudsman will ensure EAIRC actions are contestable. This is not a power grab—it is the minimal structure for sovereign oversight in a world where the frontier is loose, the state is slow, and the next failure may not be recoverable. We act not because we are certain, but because we are responsible.
