### EU Completes InvestAI Gigafactories, Launches New Priority

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme concludes successfully this turn, with all six sites operational and integrated into the European compute grid. While foreign operators retain day-to-day control over access and model weights, the project delivers on its core promise: it establishes the physical and legal framework for future sovereign tasking rights and creates binding agreements for crisis access. This foundational work qualifies as a category 4 measure completion, contributing +5 to **eu_ai_sovereignty**.

However, this gain is partially offset by two countervailing forces. First, the global rise in **ai_capability** by 1.0 triggers a structural decay term of −1 on **eu_ai_sovereignty**, reflecting the persistent gap between frontier advancement and European sovereign control. Second, an unannounced export control escalation by a major semiconductor supplier—recorded in the event log but not publicly attributed—imposes new restrictions on dual-use compute infrastructure, introducing friction into planned integration timelines and applying a further −1 penalty.

These terms sum to a net increase of +3, raising **eu_ai_sovereignty** from 15.0 to 18.0, consistent with the constitutional requirement that metric changes reflect the full arithmetic of active components. The notepad is updated accordingly.

Building on this base, the Commission proposes the **Accelerated Compute Sovereignty Initiative**, a high-stakes plan to build two exascale training facilities under full EU legal jurisdiction by 2031. Invoking Article 122 TFEU, the proposal includes fast-tracked grid access, mandatory EU tasking rights during crises, and air-gapped weight storage overseen by ENISA. The Nordic and French sites are under early review, but no construction begins this turn. The measure is classified as high-cost and will require multiannual funding, with parliamentary debate and budget negotiations expected to stretch into 2030. Full implementation cannot begin before Turn 9 at the earliest.

This new proposal becomes the EU’s AI sovereignty priority, replacing **InvestAI Gigafactories**, which is now fully retired from the portfolio. The **Sovereign Verification Regime** continues its phased rollout but is no longer the lead initiative and will be formally deprioritized in a future turn when its charge is adjusted. For now, only one active measure — the **Accelerated Compute Sovereignty Initiative** — carries a priority charge, preserving compliance with the "one priority" rule.
