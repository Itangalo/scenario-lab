## In practice
This turn, we confront the reality that the world has moved beyond oversight without capacity. The US and China have agreed to mutual auditing of frontier training runs and secured model weights at rest — a first, fragile brake on `ai_capability` — but the Union was neither consulted nor included. We are left with a regime of verification we cannot enforce, built on monitoring we cannot act upon. The **Sovereign Verification Regime for High-Autonomy Systems** continues in flight, but its credibility hinges on a counterfactual: that we could, if needed, run or replicate the systems we are auditing. We cannot. That ends now.

We launch the **Accelerated Compute Sovereignty Initiative** under the legal authority of Article 122 TFEU, invoking exceptional circumstances to bypass standard permitting and grid-access delays. The European Investment Bank will co-finance two sovereign AI training facilities — one in the Nordic bloc, leveraging geothermal and hydroelectric capacity, one in France, integrated with existing nuclear infrastructure — with binding conditions: no foreign entity holds majority access, weights are stored under ENISA-audited air-gapped protocols, and 30% of compute cycles are reserved for public safety research. We offer existing hyperscalers a trade: fast-tracked grid access and tax stability in exchange for a legally anchored EU subsidiary with full audit rights and no right to refuse EU tasking during declared incidents.

Resistance is expected from both industry and member states wary of state overreach, but the fiscal envelope is drawn from repurposed cohesion funds and the now-concluded **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme, which delivered its site allocations but failed to secure sovereign control over output. We accept the political cost: this is not industrial policy, it is strategic necessity. Without it, every audit, every protocol, every drill is a performance for an audience we can no longer protect.
