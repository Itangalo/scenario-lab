### Immediate Response to Cyber Intrusion

The discovery of AI-coordinated intrusions into critical infrastructure triggers a rapid EU-wide emergency response. Invoking Article 222 TFEU, the Commission directs ENISA to issue binding cybersecurity protocols within 30 days, mandating air-gapped monitoring, multi-factor authentication, and real-time anomaly reporting. €12bn in recovery funds are fast-tracked to cover implementation, but pushback emerges from several member states, particularly in Central Europe, who argue the timeline is unworkable and the conditions infringe on national authority. Compliance is patchy, and full hardening will likely slip beyond Q2 2027, though initial progress lifts resilience.

### Sovereignty Push Meets Headwinds

The InvestAI Gigafactories programme begins site selection, with France, Poland, and Spain in contention. However, early negotiations with ASML stall over US pressure on maintenance access, and no binding co-investment deal is reached. The Tech Sovereignty Package advances draft legislation for “AI Zones,” but industry lobbying intensifies, warning of legal challenges and delays. While the political will is clear, tangible compute sovereignty remains distant.

### Political Costs Mount

The Emergency Resilience Surge is named priority, but its urgency clashes with fiscal reality and member-state resistance. The denial of US frontier access — hitting hospitals and ministries — is widely framed as a strategic humiliation, undermining confidence. Though the response is swift, it is seen as reactive, not preventive. Public sentiment rises slightly due to visible action, but labour groups warn of coming disruptions.
