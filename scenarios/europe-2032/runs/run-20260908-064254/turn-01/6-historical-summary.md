A major cyber intrusion into EU critical infrastructure prompted an emergency response under Article 222 TFEU, with ENISA issuing binding cybersecurity mandates and €12bn in recovery funds allocated for rapid implementation. Resistance from Central European member states over sovereignty concerns and unrealistic timelines led to patchy compliance, delaying full system hardening beyond Q2 2027. The InvestAI Gigafactories initiative advanced site selection in France, Poland, and Spain, but negotiations with ASML stalled due to US restrictions on maintenance access, halting co-investment progress. Draft legislation for “AI Zones” faced intense industry opposition, slowing the Tech Sovereignty Package. Public confidence wavered amid perceptions of reactive rather than preventive action, exacerbated by the strategic blow of denied US frontier AI access affecting key institutions. While emergency measures provided a short-term boost in sentiment, growing labour concerns highlighted rising political and social tensions.
