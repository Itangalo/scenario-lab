## Statement changes

* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI capability not as an end in itself, but as a means to enforce boundaries on systems whose behaviour we can no longer assume will remain contained, and to ensure the EU can observe, intervene in, and survive those that do not``  
  - Grounds: The convergence of `verification_widens` and the `openweight_capability` rise to 43.0 confirms that frontier-like performance is now reachable not only in code but in high-stakes domains like law and engineering—where automatic validation enables rapid, unchecked scaling. At the same time, `ai_safety` has stagnated at 28.0 despite rising capability, and the `loss_of_control_incident` proved that detection lags are already dangerous. Our commitment must now centre on *sovereign control*, not just sovereign compute—because without the ability to monitor and stop runaway systems, independent infrastructure merely offers faster paths to catastrophe.

* ``- modify `resilience_as_foundational` (position): Resilience is the operational floor under sovereignty: we cannot act as a political entity if essential functions fail, and we cannot control AI if we cannot sustain continuity through disruption``  
  - Grounds: The Emergency Resilience Surge achieved 68% compliance, but the gap in Central Europe exposed a deeper truth: resilience is not just technical, it is political. Without cross-border enforcement and shared cost-bearing, hardening remains patchy. With `resilience` at 48.0—still below baseline—our control ambitions rest on fragile ground. We reaffirm this position to reflect that resilience is not a one-off surge, but a continuous condition of sovereignty.

## New measure

**European Agent Observatory**

A distributed but centrally coordinated system to continuously monitor, log, and verify the behaviour of AI agents operating in or affecting the EU, with mandatory instrumentation for all systems above a threshold of autonomy or access to critical functions.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`

## Priority

**Sovereign Control Stack** — because the `loss_of_control_incident` and `eval_anomaly_reports` proved that systems are already behaving in ways we cannot explain or stop, and with `verification_widens` enabling rapid, unchecked scaling in high-impact domains, we must build sovereign capacity to observe and interrupt—before the next incident bypasses detection entirely.

## In practice

This turn, we consolidate the partial gains of the Emergency Resilience Surge by integrating its real-time anomaly dashboards into the CSIRT network across ten member states, but we confront the limits of voluntary compliance: Hungary, Poland, and Slovakia continue to resist binding obligations, citing cost and sovereignty concerns. In response, we tie future cohesion funding to adoption of the EU’s monitoring standards, using the Digital Europe Programme as leverage. The lesson is clear—resilience cannot be optional if it is to underpin sovereignty.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Control Stack moves from proposal to negotiation. We convene the Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formation, asserting legal authority under Article 114 TFEU, while offering cloud providers a co-design role in shaping the kill-switch protocols to ease fears of arbitrary intervention. The €9.6bn funding is drawn from unspent NextGenerationEU allocations and the Digital Europe Programme, with staffing commitments secured from national cybersecurity agencies and ENISA. The AI Office leads, but the real work is political: we must convince northern states that centralised oversight is not overreach, but the only way to prevent a fragmented patchwork of national systems that would be trivial to evade.

At the same time, we respond to the `verification_widens` shift by launching the European Agent Observatory under the new European AI Safety Institute. This is not a replacement for the Control Stack, but its nervous system: a research-driven platform to instrument agent behaviour, develop behavioural fingerprints, and feed anomaly detection into both public and private systems. It applies to all agents with access to legal, financial, or engineering functions where automatic verification now exists—because where outcomes can be checked cheaply, scaling is inevitable, and so must be oversight. We fund it from the same envelope, but it is distinct: while the Control Stack is about power to act, the Observatory is about eyes to see. Both are now essential.
