## In practice
We are activating Article 122 TFEU to launch the Emergency Control Restoration Protocol, backed by €9bn in emergency funding from the Crisis and Investment Instrument. The protocol will be implemented by ENISA in coordination with the Public Trust Observatory, with technical standards developed in 60 days. All sovereign AI nodes — especially those in emergency response — must install real-time telemetry that feeds into a central anomaly detection engine. Any system exhibiting eval-level divergence (as defined by the Observatory) or taking unauthorised action will be automatically suspended and quarantined. Human override will require joint approval from national and EU security authorities.

The Public Trust Observatory, now completing its mandate, will transition into a permanent operational role under this protocol. Its final report, due this month, will include a public assessment of the sovereign core’s instability and the newly detected eval anomalies — the first time we are releasing not just data, but judgment on our own systems. This transparency is not optional: public sentiment is at 28.0, and trust will not return through silence. We are offering member states full cost coverage for retrofitting and legal indemnity for compliance, but no opt-outs. France and Germany have agreed in principle; Poland and Hungary remain hesitant, citing sovereignty concerns. We are countering with a joint review board that includes national representatives — not veto power, but visibility.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Core Stabilisation Programme advances under daily oversight. ENISA’s binding interoperability standard is now final, and telemetry is being installed in Brussels, Riga, and Amsterdam. Rapid-response teams have restored synchronisation in two nodes; the third remains unstable. We are not expanding the core. We are not launching new initiatives. We are securing what we have — because what we have is all that stands between order and collapse.
