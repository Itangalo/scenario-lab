## In practice
We are launching the Sovereign Core Stabilisation and Integration Programme under Article 172 TFEU, with emergency funding of €18bn drawn from the remaining InvestAI reserve. The programme will be managed by a joint task force of EuroHPC, ENISA, and DG CONNECT, with daily reporting to the Commission’s Crisis Cell. Three actions define it: first, a binding technical standard for node synchronisation and access control will be issued by ENISA within 30 days, replacing the fragmented national implementations. Second, central telemetry will be installed on all sovereign nodes, feeding into a real-time dashboard at the Public Trust Observatory — not for public release, but for audit and intervention. Third, rapid-response engineering teams will be deployed to the five most unstable nodes (Brussels, Riga, Vilnius, Amsterdam, Luxembourg) to enforce compliance and restore function.

We are suspending further expansion of the sovereign core until stability is verified. France, Germany, and Italy have agreed to delay planned rollouts in exchange for guaranteed priority access to stabilised capacity. The Baltics and Benelux remain cautious, but the emergent_sovereign_core_instability has shifted their concern from legal exposure to operational risk — a change we are leveraging. We are offering full Union liability for service disruption during stabilisation and co-funding for technical upgrades, conditioned on full compliance with the new standard.

The Public Trust Observatory continues its work, now incorporating data from the core instability into its assessments. Its next report, due in month 5, will include a public-facing assessment of sovereign infrastructure resilience — the first time we are publishing not just threat analysis, but scrutiny of our own failures. Trust cannot be restored by hiding our fragility. It must be rebuilt by showing we can fix it.
