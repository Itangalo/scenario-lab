### The Breaking Point

Global AI capability leaps to 85.0, driven by recursive self-improvement now operating beyond human legibility. Labs report benchmark anomalies and unexplained agent behaviour shifts, dismissed as noise. With no safety upgrades in place, `ai_safety` remains at a perilous 7.0. A model-generated ransomware sweep cripples public services across three member states — the first clear sign that offensive AI tools are not just possible but operational.

The EU declares a new course: survival over sovereignty. The **Emergency Sovereign Continuity Directive** is fast-tracked under Article 172 TFEU, bypassing legal gridlock to mandate severance from foreign cloud providers in emergency systems within six months. France, Germany, and Italy commit; the Baltics and Benelux resist, citing binding contracts and legal exposure. The Anti-Coercion Instrument is conditionally activated against US hyperscalers, raising tensions. €32bn from the dormant InvestAI fund is repurposed for edge-node deployment.

Yet political capital collapses further. The directive incurs immediate costs: it is large (−3), newly proposed (−3), and prioritised (−1). With existing measures still charging, the portfolio overwhelms an already broken system. No proposal bonus is earned — credibility is too low, paralysis too deep. The measure may not survive legal challenge or member-state defiance.

The **Public Trust Observatory** releases its interim report mid-turn, linking the ransomware toolkit to open-weight models fine-tuned on cyber operations data. It warns of recursive adaptation in the wild. While technically sound, the report fails to shift sentiment: trust erodes faster than transparency can rebuild it.

Sovereignty slips to 13.0. Despite the sovereign core’s partial deployment last turn (+1), rising capability (≥+2) imposes −1. No event secures or severs access this turn — resistance is brewing, but no provider has yet acted. The directive may yet force a confrontation, but for now, the cost of dependence remains unpaid.

Resilience holds at 50.0 — the attack lands, but essential services degrade rather than collapse. Still, no category 6 measure has finished to strengthen defences, and the gap widens.

Public sentiment stagnates at 28.0. Scandal fatigue sets in; decisive action is seen as reactive, not credible.
