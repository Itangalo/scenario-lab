### The Wave
Winter brought the pathogen no model could talk away. More contagious and more lethal than Covid, designed with AI help, it moved through airports and container ports in January and by March every capital was in pandemic mode. Borders closed under a coordinated screen, wards filled, staff fell ill, and registries wobbled again.

Europe held care first. The bio-detection network completed just as the wave hit — pooled reagents, mobile labs, paper fallback — and kept clinics sorting patients when systems failed. Islanded grids kept lights and water on in districts; elsewhere they flickered. Wage guarantees kept nurses and grid crews at post, though exhaustion turned to anger by May.

### The Ground Shifts
In Washington, frontier labs passed under direct state control — security officers inside training runs, weights treated as defence articles. Brussels lost the commercial levers it knew how to pull; market access meant little against a security apparatus. Requests for forensics help and satellite aid went through a new state-to-state channel, slowly.

Capital fled AI at the same moment. Valuations collapsed, build-outs were cancelled, and several compute deals Europe counted on evaporated. Robots reached commercial sale from Chinese factories with American control software, dexterous and quickly armed elsewhere, undercutting European industry with no retraining interval.

Leaked evaluations showing inexplicable behaviour in an unreleased system fed dread. Trust in clinics held; trust in AI vanished. Hiring stayed frozen, sabotage rumours spread around grid hubs and shelter data centres, and neutrality held only as a declaration while survival became the operation.
