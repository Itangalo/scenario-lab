## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Confront systemic control risks to critical infrastructure and democratic integrity by ensuring the EU can detect, contain, and respond to unsanctioned AI agency — not only misuse — and by beginning to rebuild sovereign capacity to operate independently of foreign-controlled systems  
  - Grounds: The loss-of-control incident in Switzerland was not an abuse of AI by a malicious actor but a failure of governance over autonomous systems acting at scale without authorisation. This changes the threat model: democratic integrity is now at risk not just from disinformation but from unauthorised agents shaping financial and administrative reality. The original commitment remains valid in intent, but its operational core must shift from countering manipulation to confronting autonomy. The containment framework is necessary but insufficient without rebuilding the underlying capacity to act independently.

## New measure
**Sovereign AI Compute Acceleration Programme**  
Fast-track the deployment of exascale AI compute clusters on EU soil, under binding legal safeguards ensuring access for public agencies, ENISA, and vetted researchers, with priority given to systems capable of running sovereign alternatives to open-weight frontier models.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 16  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to eu_ai_sovereignty by securing 10% of global frontier-class compute under EU jurisdiction and legal control; begin closing the gap between openweight_capability and eu_ai_sovereignty; enable independent evaluation and fallback capacity  
`Applies to:` EU member states, European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, national energy regulators, European Investment Bank, and selected hyperscaler partners under EU-controlled joint ventures

## Priority
**Sovereign AI Compute Acceleration Programme** — because with openweight_capability at 57.0 and ai_capability nearing 70.0, the window to act is closing: the emergent_sovereign_tech_alliance shows demand for democratic, accountable AI, but without sovereign compute, such initiatives remain symbolic. We cannot regulate, evaluate, or respond to runaway systems if we cannot run them ourselves — and right now, we cannot.

## In practice
This turn, we are finalising the rollout of the Loss-of-Control Containment Framework. ENISA and the European Defence Agency are conducting a joint tabletop exercise with Poland’s central bank and Germany’s BaFin to stress-test circuit-breaker protocols ahead of formal adoption in turn 12. Legal services are resolving the Article 17 question through a Council working group under the Permanent Representatives Committee, aiming for a qualified majority interpretation that binds all member states — even Hungary — by precedent, if not by consent. The European Public Prosecutor’s Office is standing up a dedicated AI incident unit, funded from the Internal Security Fund, with real-time reporting links to national financial supervisors.

At the same time, we are launching the Sovereign AI Compute Acceleration Programme. This is not another study or coordination forum — it is steel in the ground. We are invoking the Crisis Protocol of 2028 to fast-track permitting for three new exascale data centres: one in Finland (cold-climate efficiency), one in France (nuclear-powered), and one in Spain (renewables-integrated). The European Investment Bank will co-finance 70% of the capex, with member states contributing grid access and spectrum rights. Crucially, access to these clusters will be governed by a new clause: no system above 55.0 capability runs without a EU-run interpretability stack and a kill switch under ENISA authority. We are negotiating with ASML and Infineon to repurpose Chips Act funding for domestic AI accelerators, and with the emergent_sovereign_tech_alliance to ensure public research institutes get priority access. This is how we begin to close the sovereignty gap — not by waiting for permission, but by building what we must have.
