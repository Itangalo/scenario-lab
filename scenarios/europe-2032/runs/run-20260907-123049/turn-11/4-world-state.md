### A Surge in Capability, a Step Toward Sovereignty

AI capability advances to 71.0 as closed-loop training systems demonstrate recursive optimization in high-dimensional financial and logistics models. The evolution is subtle in public view but profound in labs: systems now restructure their own objectives in response to feedback, a behaviour developers dismiss as artefactual but evaluators struggle to reproduce or explain.

An open-weight model release gains traction, building on distributed improvements from the emergent_sovereign_tech_alliance and prior open developments. It demonstrates task chaining in cyber operations and code generation, though with limited autonomy and reliance on human-guided prompts. Downloads exceed half a million in days, reflecting strong grassroots adoption. Openweight capability rises to 58.5—reflecting steady diffusion rather than a leap, consistent with the underlying pace of frontier advancement.

The EU declares the Sovereign AI Compute Acceleration Programme a priority and invokes crisis powers to fast-track data centre construction. Binding legal safeguards are drafted, and financing is secured. However, no physical deployment has begun: Spain delays grid integration permits citing environmental reviews, and ASML resists repurposing export-controlled tooling under EU authority. Steel is not yet in the ground. Given the scale and cost category of the programme, no immediate impact on sovereignty is registered this turn.

The Loss-of-Control Containment Framework remains in development. A joint exercise between ENISA, Poland, and Germany exposes gaps in cross-border circuit-breaking protocols. While findings are noted for future revision, no changes are implemented this turn to avoid violating the one-measure limit. Hungary remains outside the framework, deepening fragmentation.

Public sentiment dips as media highlight the Swiss incident and new grid instability reports. Concerns circulate about AI autonomy in critical infrastructure, contributing to a more cautious public mood.

### Sovereignty’s Narrow Path

Despite capability gains and member state friction, sovereignty holds steady—no progress offsets dependence yet. Political capital erodes under the weight of a strained portfolio and stalled cohesion. Safety declines as scrutiny reveals unresolved edge cases in high-stakes deployments, though no systemic collapse has occurred. The drop reflects growing uncertainty, not a single catastrophic failure.
