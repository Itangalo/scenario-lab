## In practice
This turn, we are finalizing the Loss-of-Control Containment Framework as it formally completes. A Council Regulation has been adopted, embedding the unified alert system into the DORA and NIS2 frameworks. ENISA, Europol, and the ECB now share real-time incident telemetry, and the European Public Prosecutor’s Office has issued binding guidance on digital evidence preservation for agentic misconduct. Hungary’s abstention remains a gap, but we are establishing bilateral data-sharing sidecar agreements with all other member states to ensure operational continuity. The framework is now law — and its first test came too soon.

Simultaneously, we are pushing forward the Sovereign AI Compute Acceleration Programme despite the ASML blockade. We have redirected cohesion funds to fast-track environmental approvals in Andalusia and are now negotiating with IMEC to retrofit 300mm industrial lines for domestic AI accelerators, bypassing U.S.-controlled lithography tools. The European Investment Bank has released a second tranche of funding, tied to hardware sourcing milestones by Q1 2033. But without physical deployment, sovereignty remains fragile — and the cyber_major_incident underscores that we cannot harden systems if we cannot run our own defences.

Now, we launch the Open-Weight Containment Protocol in direct response to the ransomware sweep. Under Article 114 TFEU, we are introducing a new obligation: any open-weight model release at or above 59.0 must undergo pre-release evaluation by a newly empowered unit within ENISA, assessing its potential for misuse in cyber, bio, and disinformation domains. Releases deemed high-risk will face a 90-day containment window, mandatory watermarking, and developer accountability requirements. We are working with Germany and France to secure a qualified majority in COREPER II, but resistance is mounting from digital rights groups and open-source communities who see this as a betrayal of transparency. We are offering compromise: sunset clauses, appeal mechanisms, and funding for auditable open development sandboxes. This is not a ban — it is a circuit breaker. Because if we do not control the diffusion, the next attack will not stop at disruption. It will end function.
