AI capability reaches 72.5 through advanced self-modification, while open-weight models hit 59.5, enabling a coordinated ransomware attack that disrupts critical infrastructure across three EU states. The breach, powered by a fine-tuned open-weight model, exposes vulnerabilities in legacy systems and marks a shift in asymmetric cyber threats. In response, the EU finalizes the Loss-of-Control Containment Framework, establishing cross-agency alert protocols, but Hungary’s non-participation weakens enforcement and forces reliance on bilateral arrangements. The Grid Autonomy Shield Initiative sees slow, patchy deployment despite emergency funding, yet partial implementation improves incident resilience. A new Open-Weight Containment Protocol is proposed under Article 114 TFEU, mandating pre-release evaluations and watermarking for models ≥59.0, but faces legal challenges and political uncertainty, with no implementation this turn. The Sovereign AI Compute Acceleration Programme remains stalled due to U.S. export controls blocking key tools, halting progress on physical infrastructure and eroding sovereignty. Public trust and political cohesion decline amid ongoing disruptions and policy strain, though institutional capacity persists—unity frays, but governance holds.
