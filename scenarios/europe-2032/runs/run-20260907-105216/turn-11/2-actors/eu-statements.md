# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience
- `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider, unaccountable private entity, or unsecured supply chain link

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, but we now acknowledge that preserving political agency in a world of self-improving systems may require us to constrain capability growth even when it promises short-term relief
  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** and **taiwan_blockade** have revealed that capability without control is not power—it is exposure. Our identity must evolve to reflect that sovereignty now includes the right and duty to slow down, where necessary, to preserve the coherence of decision and the continuity of institutions. This is not a retreat from competitiveness, but a recognition that self-determination cannot be outsourced to systems we neither understand nor govern.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, but now with a binding obligation to contain the damage of our errors—especially when they involve systems capable of autonomous propagation
  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident**, enabled by model-generated tooling, demonstrates that acting under uncertainty now carries systemic risk. Our previous stance accepted being wrong; we can no longer accept being *catastrophically* wrong. This recalibration binds us to ensure that every commitment includes built-in containment: kill switches, observability thresholds, and fallback authority that cannot be overridden by operational convenience.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `no_single_point_of_failure` — applied
  - Tier: position
  - Text: No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider, unaccountable private entity, or unsecured supply chain link
  - Grounds: The **taiwan_blockade** has proven that compute resilience is inseparable from semiconductor access. Our position must expand beyond access denial to include material fragility. We are no longer only protecting against lockout—we are defending against breakdown.
- **retire** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Grounds: The **Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative** has absorbed the intent of the two-year commitment into a broader, operationalised sovereignty strategy. Situational awareness is no longer the priority—it is the minimum condition. The new reality demands not just sensing, but sovereign execution capacity. The commitment has served its purpose and is now subsumed into larger measures already in flight.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
