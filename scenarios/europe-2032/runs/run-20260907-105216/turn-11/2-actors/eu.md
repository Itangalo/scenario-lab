## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, but we now acknowledge that preserving political agency in a world of self-improving systems may require us to constrain capability growth even when it promises short-term relief  
- Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** and **taiwan_blockade** have revealed that capability without control is not power—it is exposure. Our identity must evolve to reflect that sovereignty now includes the right and duty to slow down, where necessary, to preserve the coherence of decision and the continuity of institutions. This is not a retreat from competitiveness, but a recognition that self-determination cannot be outsourced to systems we neither understand nor govern.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, but now with a binding obligation to contain the damage of our errors—especially when they involve systems capable of autonomous propagation  
- Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident**, enabled by model-generated tooling, demonstrates that acting under uncertainty now carries systemic risk. Our previous stance accepted being wrong; we can no longer accept being *catastrophically* wrong. This recalibration binds us to ensure that every commitment includes built-in containment: kill switches, observability thresholds, and fallback authority that cannot be overridden by operational convenience.

- modify `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider, unaccountable private entity, or unsecured supply chain link  
- Grounds: The **taiwan_blockade** has proven that compute resilience is inseparable from semiconductor access. Our position must expand beyond access denial to include material fragility. We are no longer only protecting against lockout—we are defending against breakdown.

- retire `two_year_commitment`  
- Grounds: The **Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative** has absorbed the intent of the two-year commitment into a broader, operationalised sovereignty strategy. Situational awareness is no longer the priority—it is the minimum condition. The new reality demands not just sensing, but sovereign execution capacity. The commitment has served its purpose and is now subsumed into larger measures already in flight.

## New measure
**Emergency Cyber Shield for Critical Services**  
Deploy automated, human-supervised containment systems across energy, health, and public administration networks to detect, isolate, and roll back AI-generated attacks within minutes.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to resilience by reducing attack dwell time and preventing cascading failures; -2 to public_sentiment risk from visible service collapse; enables faster attribution in post-incident analysis

## Priority
**Emergency Cyber Shield for Critical Services** — because the **cyber_major_incident** has proven that defensive lag is now lethal, and because without rapid containment, even sovereign compute becomes a liability when it runs compromised systems.

## In practice
We are treating the **cyber_major_incident** not as a breach but as a system test—one we failed. The attack exploited AI-generated zero-days in a widely used dependency chain, spreading across municipal services and regional energy operators before detection. We acted, but too slowly. That ends now.

This turn, we launch the Emergency Cyber Shield under the NIS3 framework, activated through the Crisis Competence Protocol and funded by a €1.2 billion emergency allocation from the Internal Security Fund, reallocated from border surveillance. The system will be built on a hybrid model: AI-driven intrusion detection trained on telemetry from the Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory, paired with human-supervised rollback protocols hosted within ENISA’s new Operational Resilience Cell. We are fast-tracking deployment in six member states most exposed to infrastructure drift—Italy, Greece, Poland, Croatia, Slovakia, and Belgium—using pre-approved vendor stacks from the EU Cyber Resilience Act list. National CISOs are being offered full cost coverage and liability shielding in exchange for full telemetry sharing and compliance with EU-level escalation orders.

Resistance is expected from states wary of centralised command, but the **taiwan_blockade** has shifted the debate: without rapid response, compute sovereignty means nothing. We are offering co-location of decision logic in national capitals and a sunset clause after three years—provided resilience reaches 70.0. The Shield does not replace human judgment. It ensures there is still a system left for humans to judge.
