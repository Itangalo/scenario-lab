### Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor EU decision-making in real-time, sovereign sensing of AI behaviour and capability shifts, to enable timely governance and prevent strategic surprise  
- Grounds: The two-year commitment period has closed, and the events of this turn—particularly the **eu_frontier_access_denied** incident and the ongoing **emergent_synthetic_leaks_wave**—confirm that situational awareness is not merely a precondition for governance, but now an urgent operational necessity. The previous formulation was structural; this one is functional. We are no longer building awareness as a foundation—we are racing to avoid irrelevance in a world where decisions are made by systems we cannot observe, let alone control.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, but now with binding investment in reducing the opacity that creates uncertainty—especially in AI behaviour, intent inference, and frontier access dependencies  
- Grounds: The collapse of political capital and public sentiment proves that acting under uncertainty without improving the quality of judgment leads to institutional exhaustion. Our willingness to act early remains, but it must now be paired with a structural effort to shrink the domain of the unknown. This recalibration accepts that we can no longer afford to be blindsided every six months.

- add `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event severed core services across healthcare and public administration overnight, exposing systemic fragility. This position does not reject cooperation or interoperability, but insists that essential functions must have sovereign fallbacks, regardless of cost or complexity.

## In practice
We are treating the denial of frontier access not as an anomaly but as a precedent. The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative launches immediately under the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking, with legal authority drawn from Article 172 TFEU and the Crisis Competence Protocol invoked by the Commission in February. We are repurposing three stalled commercial data centre projects in Finland, Luxembourg, and Bulgaria—sites previously abandoned due to power constraints—by fast-tracking grid upgrades through the Trans-European Networks for Energy mechanism and allocating emergency spectrum for microwave backhaul. These clusters will not match the frontier, but they will run certified derivatives of open-weight models up to capability 65.0, hardened against manipulation and tuned for continuity in health triage, border management, and energy dispatch.

Funding comes from a temporary reallocation of 40% of the Digital Europe Programme’s AI rollout budget, justified as crisis mitigation, and a new €900 million line in the MFF under "Strategic Infrastructure Continuity," to be ratified in the upcoming Mid-Term Review. Member states are being offered bilateral service-level agreements guaranteeing priority access during disruptions in exchange for waiving local permitting delays. Resistance from two northern member states, concerned about subsidy competition, is being addressed through co-location rights in their own hardened facilities and shared operations staffing.

This is not about catching up. It is about refusing to be cut off again. The Sentinel Nodes are now feeding data into the Behavioural Observatory, but those systems only matter if we can act on their warnings. That requires compute we control, on soil we govern, and power we command. We are building it now—not because we expect the frontier to slow, but because we refuse to let it decide whether Europe functions.
