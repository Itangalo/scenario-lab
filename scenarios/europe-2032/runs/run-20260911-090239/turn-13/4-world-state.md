### Restoration under fire
Autumn brought a rolling, largely automated assault on public services already running degraded. A poisoned software component used in municipal administration spread laterally into hospital scheduling, permit offices and two regional grid operators. Screens locked with ransom notes in several cities; elsewhere systems simply gave wrong answers. Attribution remained open, but responders said the exploit chains bore the marks of machine-generated tooling.

Defence looked thin. Shared testing of frontier systems still returned only black-box scores, and reasoning had grown harder to audit. National security centres triaged the blast radius while ENISA and CERT-EU teams pushed clean images and hardened open-weight replacements drawn from emergency quotas in unfinished gigafactory halls and supercomputing centres.

### Uneven recovery
Large certified hospitals and central ministries were re-imaged within days and kept running on fallback capacity. Smaller towns fared worse. Controllers from the 1990s could not take the replacements, forcing hands-on crews and temporary paper procedures. In a few utility towns monitors installed after the February cutoff were again switched off by maintenance staff who saw them as surveillance, requiring repeat visits.

Two lines completed amid the crisis. Wage-insurance and retraining payments continued through employment services, though take-up stayed slow as hiring froze. Fallback quotas and continuity plans formally closed as scheduled from prior work, folding into the restoration effort, restoring some sovereign capacity.

Meanwhile offices delivered quiet news: studies across law, accountancy and administration showed solid productivity gains, strongest for juniors, with no matching fall in employment. The finding steadied some workplaces but undercut hopes of a further automation dividend to pay for rebuilding.

The capital that kept its separate supply arrangement accepted restoration crews but refused to align export controls, keeping its side channel open. Press coverage mixed praise for visible crews with anger that a foreign cutoff followed by a major intrusion had left Europe patching by hand, leaving political standing flat despite the operational close-out.
