### Patch Tuesday for the grid
The spring belonged to the defenders for once. A new generation of automated patching tools — able to close vulnerabilities almost as fast as scanners found them — and detection that flagged coordinated swarm behaviour rather than known signatures was pushed out through national response centres to hospitals, municipal utilities and telecom operators.

In certified hospitals the rollout was smooth. In the small-town utilities where last autumn's drills had failed, it was messier: outdated control systems, missing inventories, contractors who did not answer the phone. ENISA teams spent weeks on site, and two large exercises had to be rerun after suspect servers were again misclassified. Progress was real but uneven, and far from finished.

### A genome paper no one wanted to publish
Parallel to the patching drive, a contested genome-model study circulated among biosecurity specialists. Reviewers said a non-expert with model help could have reached a viable human-infecting design. Methodologists called it alarmist; others said it published too much. Health authorities did not wait for consensus: hospital labs and wastewater sites began expanded sampling, and incident feeds were swapped with Japan, Korea and Canada for shared test results on opaque models.

The public noticed both stories. Local press covered the utility upgrades as competent, overdue work. Coverage of the biology paper was anxious and technical, contained largely to specialist pages.

### Concrete, certificates and empty desks
No new home build was funded. Backup compute shells and shield certificates continued on old budgets. Labour ministers finalised a retraining and income-support blueprint for graduate-entry professions hollowed out by assistants, but left it unfunded pending a budget recovery.

The holdout capital was offered priority access to the new defensive packages and pooled hosting if it aligned export rules and unwound its separate supply arrangement. It took the tooling briefings, kept the arrangement, and did not sign.
