### Defences that held, just
The second half of 2031 was the first period Brussels could point to working hardware. The automated patching and swarm-detection rollout funded as the emergency sprint finished its deployment cycle. Certified hospitals now patch in hours. Municipal utilities improved markedly — ENISA's rerun exercises passed in most regions, and telecom operators reported faster containment of coordinated scans.

It remained uneven. A handful of small-town grids with 1990s controllers could not take the agents at all and remain on manual schedules. Health labs expanded wastewater and hospital sampling for engineered pathogens, and the exchange of incident feeds with Japan, Korea and Canada continued, but frontier models grew more opaque and shared tests still returned black-box scores.

### A safety net, finally funded
With graduate hiring frozen across law, translation, junior coding and back-office analysis, the Council agreed to repurpose ESF+ and recovery leftovers for the retraining and wage-insurance blueprint. Disbursement through public employment services began in autumn, tied to health, utility maintenance and cyber-defence retraining.

Take-up was slow. National agencies complained about co-financing rates and reporting conditions; unions called vouchers too short; employers welcomed flexibility but hired little. Local press coverage was cautiously positive — money at last — without enthusiasm.

In utility towns, single-source press notes mentioned grumbling among maintenance crews that automation monitors were a pretext for cuts, with talk of briefly switching off monitoring tools. Nothing coordinated materialised, but the rumour lingered in council meetings.

The holdout capital accepted safety-net eligibility talks while again keeping its separate supply arrangement and refusing export alignment. No new compute was funded; shells and certificates ticked over. American rationing stayed hard, and with no major incident this semester, the Union banked quiet competence rather than a breakthrough.
