### Cut off
In February, access to the leading American model stopped for European users with no appeal. Hospitals with triage copilots, ministries with drafting tools and firms with contracted inference found error messages where services had been. Washington offered no detailed reason. At the same time, private AI finance collapsed: valuations reset, announced data-centre build-outs were cancelled, and several hosting and cloud arrangements European operators had counted on evaporated.

The shock landed on brittle ground. Frontier systems had grown more opaque through 2031, shared testing returned only black-box scores, and assurance was already thin.

### Fallback, not rebuild
Brussels moved fast on continuity. Under an emergency mandate, DG CNECT and ENISA mapped cut-off dependencies in health and administration, allocated emergency inference quotas in unfinished gigafactory shells and national supercomputing sites, and pushed hardened open-weight replacements vetted through the existing detection regime. Money came from repurposed operating and shield funds, with fast-track clearance for utilities to connect.

It partly worked. Certified hospitals and larger administrations were back on degraded but running systems within weeks. Small-town grids with 1990s controllers could not take the replacements, and several stayed on manual schedules. Maintenance crews grumbled that monitoring tools were cover for cuts; in a few utility towns, monitors were briefly switched off, forcing ENISA teams to re-patch by hand. Nothing coordinated, but recovery was uneven.

The retraining and wage-insurance disbursement continued through employment services, but take-up stayed slow amid frozen graduate hiring. The holdout capital took eligibility and was offered priority fallback quota for export alignment, but kept its separate supply arrangement.

Quiet competence gave way to visible dependence. Press coverage called the cutoff a humiliation foretold, and public mood hardened against both foreign providers and domestic promises.
