### Lights back on, slowly — and thinly stretched
The second half of 2031 opened with municipal offices still dark. A large automated intrusion campaign that had swept booking, payment and records systems left a patchwork: towns where cut-off switches and human sign-off for software agents had been fitted in earlier turns were back in days, towns without them queued paper forms for a sixth week.

Brussels authorized a limited surge — available French and Dutch containment teams reinforced with a small pool of contracted operators — paid for by emergency reallocation from cohesion and recovery funds and short-term borrowing that finance ministers openly warned could not be repeated. Cohesion payouts stayed conditional on installing the gates and feeding telemetry to the cross-border coordination cell, but staffing only allowed brigades to reach the worst-hit municipalities. Mayors who had resisted took the deal when the alternative was another month of closed counters. By December some of the dark municipalities were taking appointments again, though many remained backlogged and rebuilds from clean images were still queued into next year, with approval checks only partially retrofitted.

At the same time a quieter alarm spread among health and security officials. A published genome-model result suggesting a workable route to a human-infecting organism, contested but far more concrete than earlier warnings, led the Commission to begin drafting emergency biology screening under existing emergency powers. All domestic DNA synthesis firms were notified of forthcoming screening orders, hospitals received draft triage protocols, and European diplomats used the recent middle-power supply forum to open discussions on aligned screening abroad. Legal review, stakeholder consultation, and compliance planning were still underway, and small distributors and labs warned of liability and delays, with compliance lagging.

The fallback computing reserve for hospitals and ministries cut off from the American frontier model, prepared in prior turns through reserved supercomputer capacity, entered limited pilot operation with European-hosted open models handling a narrow set of live workloads. Engineers called it thin and understaffed, with networking, security validation and wider migration still pending and private co-financing hesitating.

The public saw outages, queues, and fresh headlines about engineered pathogens. Opposition to data centres hardened further, and trust in automated public services sank to a new low. In Council, health and interior ministers held together, but budgets tightened and several capitals warned even the limited surge could not be repeated.
