Second half of 2031 saw slow, patchy recovery from the automated municipal ransomware sweep: gated towns restored in days, ungated towns queued paper forms for weeks. Brussels funded a limited one-off surge of French-Dutch teams plus contractors via cohesion/recovery reallocation and short-term borrowing, conditional on installing isolation gates and telemetry-sharing, reaching only worst-hit areas; some municipalities reopened by December but backlogs and clean-image rebuilds queued into next year.

A contested genome-model result suggesting a route to a human-infecting organism prompted the Commission to start drafting emergency biology screening under emergency powers, notify DNA synthesis firms, issue draft hospital triage protocols, and open aligned-screening talks via the middle-power forum; legal review and compliance still pending amid industry warnings.

The fallback compute reserve for hospitals/ministries cut off from the US model entered thin pilot operation on EuroHPC with European-hosted open models for narrow workloads, still understaffed with migration incomplete and private co-financing hesitant.

Public trust sank further amid outages and pathogen headlines, hardening data-centre opposition; Council held but budgets tightened with warnings the surge could not be repeated.
