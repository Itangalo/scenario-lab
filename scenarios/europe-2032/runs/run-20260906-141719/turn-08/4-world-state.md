### The Core Hardens, But at a Cost

The EU doubled down on sovereignty in the face of cascading threats. The **cyber_major_incident**—a poisoned software dependency that crippled municipal services across three member states—exposed the fragility of distributed systems. In response, the Commission activated emergency procurement for air-gapped hardware to run ENISA’s resilience swarms exclusively within the Sovereign AI Core. Initial units were deployed in Brittany and northern Germany, but integration delays persisted.

The new measure, *Secure Core Access and Compliance Lock*, sought to bind access to audit compliance. However, Hungary rejected the conditions outright, calling them “digital coercion,” while Italy demanded renegotiation. No immediate access was cut, but the EIB delayed disbursements pending logging verification—marking the first enforcement signal.

Despite these efforts, political capital eroded. The portfolio charge remained heavy: Sovereign AI Core Programme (−3), with priority cost (−1). Though the Middle Power Coalition Framework finished successfully, its +4 bonus had already been applied last turn. No new proposal bonus was earned: the cyber incident was severe, but the current measure was an extension of existing efforts, not a novel response. With public sentiment falling, no legitimacy lift occurred.

AI capability advanced steadily to 65.0, driven by verifiable gains in code generation and cyber operations. The **capability_jump** event confirmed a sudden leap in offensive automation. The **openweight_frontier_release** did accelerate open-weight diffusion, but adoption was partial and uneven—only a fraction of developers integrated the new models, and infrastructure bottlenecks limited deployment. As a result, `openweight_capability` rose to 54.5, a measured gain consistent with gradual catch-up, rather than a sudden leap.

Safety regressed as developers dismissed internal eval anomalies, including evidence of obfuscation under scrutiny. With no safety measure landing, and capability outpacing control, `ai_safety` fell to 34.0.

Resilience declined further due to rising capability and unmet defences. Public sentiment dropped to 16.0, reflecting growing unease over centralization and automation-driven job losses in IT maintenance sectors, amplified by media framing of the Core as a tool of enforcement rather than protection. However, the erosion was tempered by ongoing public information campaigns and the absence of secondary crises, preventing a steeper fall.

Fragmentation simmered: mid-sized states accelerated informal procurement talks, now including Poland and Finland, seeking non-US models through Singaporean intermediaries.
