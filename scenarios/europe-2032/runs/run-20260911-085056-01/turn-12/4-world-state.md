### The second sweep
Winter did not bring quiet. A large automated attack landed early in the new year — ransomware again, but faster, moving through municipal registries and hospital administration and a tainted contractor update. City halls from the west to the east went dark a second time. Clerks who had just relearned paper fell back on it again.

Defenders were visibly behind. Seconded Union teams wiped and rebuilt city by city, but queues reformed. Communes restored in the autumn were hit again; others waited weeks for handwritten certificates. Attribution stayed open for months, with officials admitting privately the tooling was machine-built.

### Cordons close, reserve opens
The Union's bio-cordon programme finished landing this turn. Isolation stocks, triage procedures and cross-border transfer rules were now in place, and the winter pathogen memory eased into routine. It helped hospitals keep running through the cyber disruption, but locked doors were still the image many kept.

Brussels answered the sweep with a cheap continuity reserve: pre-staged clean images, offline registries, paper-fallback kits and pooled rebuild teams held under civil protection channels, first in the communes hit longest last autumn. Exercises for offline switchover began in hospitals already ordered off automated triage. It yielded nothing yet — kits staged, teams retained on reprogrammed funds — while the solo-contract member state stayed outside common logging.

The trusted restoration and therapy programme remained the public face. Manually checked tailored therapies dispensed only through Union-hosted logged systems produced visible remissions where they arrived, and restored wards were branded as a European decision. Where supply needed foreign compute and controlled chips, deliveries still stuttered on paperwork and rationing quarrels.

Relief from cures and anger at dark screens cancelled each other. Trust did not recover.
