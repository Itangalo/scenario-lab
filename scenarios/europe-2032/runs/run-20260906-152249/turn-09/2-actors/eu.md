## In practice
This turn, we are closing out two major programmes under immense strain: the Sovereign AI Core Programme and the Resilience Reinforcement Initiative. SACP technically finishes, but its outcome is compromised — no sovereign compute stack at 60.0 has come online. We have redirected RePowerEU contingency funds and invoked Article 122 TFEU to secure 500MW of off-take in Kiruna, but construction delays mean the first racks will not be operational until mid-2031. The protocol stack exists, the audit framework is in place, and the legal architecture for weight control is ready, but it runs on borrowed cloud capacity — and that undermines the entire premise of sovereignty. We are now negotiating with a US hyperscaler under the Digital Markets Act’s ancillary obligations to lock in long-term access, but it is a stopgap with no exit date.

Meanwhile, the Resilience Reinforcement Initiative has delivered AI-assisted monitoring to ENTSO-E and national CSIRTs, and emergency fraud-detection agents are live in three major subsidy systems. The red-to-red handover protocols under NIS2 are operational, but patching velocity remains below target in healthcare and local government — resilience improves only marginally. The joint_threat_response event gave us access to a nascent EU-US-UK cyber command with real-time telemetry sharing, which we are integrating into CSIRT coordination through ENISA. This lifts our detection capacity without requiring new build, but dependency on external intelligence deepens.

With political capital at 7.0 and public sentiment at 22.0, we are in survival mode. The Emergency Reskilling and Transition Acceleration is our only visible win: 42,000 workers have enrolled, and early feedback shows the AI-guided navigation is helping. But it is not enough to shift the narrative. The Sovereign Talent Corridor is now our last lever — we have fast-tracked 87 visas, but we are far below target. We are offering housing in Toulouse and Helsinki, legal protection from foreign subpoena under the new Dual-Use Regulation, and access to secure labs in Seville and Vilnius. Still, offers are being rejected for positions in California and Zurich. We are not just outspent — we are seen as incapable of delivering. That perception must change, or no talent will come, no matter the offer.
