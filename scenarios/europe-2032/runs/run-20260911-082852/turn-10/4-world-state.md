### The invitation taken, the trust not yet returned
Brussels said yes to the pooled command. After months of cancelled chip slots and frozen data-centre plans, the offer of shared attribution feeds and real-time telemetry was the one reinforcement the Union could get without hardware. Ministers signed the accession through foreign affairs and telecom formations, ENISA and CERT-EU wired cooperating clinics and town halls into the common picture, and the old playbooks — isolate, cut the cloud link, restore from locked backup — stayed live.

Technically it worked. A spring wave of intrusions probed municipal systems and two hospital groups; members inside the sharing scheme isolated in hours, as before. Those outside still went dark for days. The divide hardened into geography: protected corridors alongside unpatched neighbours, with auditors again crediting the joint feeds for stopping a cascade.

The liability pact that closed this turn helped at the margins. Insurers praised the clarified rules for civic systems but did not resume broad cover, waiting for case law. The joint licensing push to The Hague, Tokyo and Seoul stayed alive in working groups, with partners still demanding binding controls first. The fenced gigafactory site stayed fenced.

At home the trust repair dominated. The benefits and policing support system stayed frozen, logs were published with a redress window, workload limits and independent re-checks were imposed on caseworkers, and funds flowed only to audited triage sites where waiting lists visibly fell. Nurses in those towns defended the tools; elsewhere press kept the phrase lawful harm on front pages, and deployments beyond insurance stayed frozen.

Finance did not return. With American labs slowing training and valuations still reset, second-tier builders abroad openly talked of narrowing the gap while Europe conserved.

Containment now depends on allies, trust on proof that has yet to scale.
