### The wave that proved the wiring
The attack came in autumn as a rolling, machine-built sweep across municipal networks, clinic systems and two large hospital groups. Locked files, frozen appointment queues, ambulance diversions. Attribution feeds lit up within hours for those plugged into the pooled command; for those outside, phones went unanswered for days.

Inside the sharing scheme, the playbook held — isolate the segment, sever the cloud link, rebuild from locked backup with allied triage teams on site. ENISA and CERT-EU crews moved town to town, and auditors again stopped a wider cascade. Outside, mayors described dark town halls and paper lists. The map of recovery was the map of membership.

Brussels surged restoration teams and continuity funds only to sites sharing telemetry, a condition that saved services and deepened resentment in equal measure. Opposition councils called it aid with strings; cooperating nurses and clerks called it the reason wards reopened.

At the same time, laboratories demonstrated a sharp leap in verifiable tasks — code, maths, intrusion tooling. Defenders were visibly behind, and the tooling in this wave bore its signature. Safety reviewers warned that reasoning traces were less legible than a year ago, and no shipped safeguard had yet caught up, leaving existing reviews stretched but still in place.

The trust repair closed its formal phase: the frozen benefits and policing system stayed frozen, logs stayed public, redress stayed open, and only audited triage sites kept operating. Insurers still refused broad cover pending court rulings, so civic roll-outs beyond health stayed stalled. Press coverage split between wards that reopened and regions that stayed dark, and the old phrase about lawful harm returned.

Chip talks in The Hague, Tokyo and Seoul stayed in working groups. The fenced gigafactory stayed fenced. Finance stayed away.

Teams were still moving town to town as the year closed, with requests for help outpacing crews on the ground.
