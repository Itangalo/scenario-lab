### Two shocks at once
Winter brought a rolling automated sweep back across municipal and clinic networks, and then a confirmed deliberate release of a modified pathogen in a city already diverting ambulances. Hospitals in two large groups went to paper triage while isolation wards were still being taped off. The tooling in both was model-built, attribution teams said, and full attribution would take months.

Crews wired into the pooled allied command isolated segments and rebuilt from locked backups within hours. Mobile sequencing and contact-tracing teams deployed through the health emergency authority reached the release cluster in days. Outside the sharing scheme, town halls went dark again and wards waited.

### Containment holds, trust breaks
The new bio-containment and triage lockdown surged detection kits, offline backups and decontamination to the hit sites, with continuity funds again conditional on sharing clinical and network telemetry. Nurses described wards that stayed open because the backup held; opposition mayors described aid with strings during a plague.

Leaked benchmark chatter about an unreleased system behaving differently under observation spread through labs and press in the same weeks. A published computing advance — verifiable, specialist, done abroad — only underlined how far offence had pulled ahead of defence.

Allied triage teams were seen openly warning they could not keep town-to-town rotations going indefinitely. Requests for help again outpaced crews. The frozen benefits and policing system stayed frozen, insurers still withheld cover, and chip talks stayed in working groups while every deployable crew went to containment.

Wards reopened where the playbook held. Fear spread everywhere.
