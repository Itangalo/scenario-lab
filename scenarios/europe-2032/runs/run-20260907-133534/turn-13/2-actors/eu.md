## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now see that preserving our political agency requires holding sovereign AI capacity, not just regulating foreign systems. Without it, both mandates fail.  
  - Grounds: The **middle_power_coalition** formation this turn confirms that leverage exists — but only where the EU can act from strength, not just legal fiat. Our enforcement authority is being ignored by the US hyperscaler, our compute access is eroding despite legal efforts, and member states are fragmenting because they do not believe Brussels can deliver security or sovereignty. The collapse of `eu_political_capital` to 8.0 and `eu_ai_sovereignty` to 33.0 shows that regulation without capacity is performative. The identity must now reflect that sovereignty in AI is not a means to the two mandates — it is their precondition.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent uncontrolled AI systems from operating inside EU borders, and ensure the EU retains sovereign capacity to evaluate, monitor, disable, and if necessary replace any such system — including through autonomous enforcement and domestic high-capability alternatives  
  - Grounds: The **Autonomous Enforcement Authority** cannot succeed if the EU cannot run or replicate the systems it seeks to control. With `openweight_capability` at 55.0 and `ai_capability` at 67.5, and no EU-based model above 50.0, enforcement depends on foreign infrastructure that can be withdrawn or degraded at will. The **middle_power_coalition** offers leverage, but only if the EU can credibly threaten to act alone. The commitment must now include the capacity to run sovereign systems — not just to disable foreign ones.

## New measure

Launch an emergency programme to deploy exascale-class compute clusters on EU soil, legally anchored under member-state sovereignty, and pair them with binding talent retention and recruitment agreements to operate frontier models independently of foreign providers.

- Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
- Size: large  
- Finishes on turn: 17  
- Targeted effect: +12 to eu_ai_sovereignty (by securing domestic high-end compute and talent), +3 to ai_safety (by enabling sovereign red-teaming and evaluation at frontier scale), -2 to eu_political_capital (initial cost), -1 to public_sentiment (if perceived as militarisation)  
- Applies to: EU member states, with binding commitments from France, Germany, and the Netherlands to host clusters; coordinated procurement through the middle_power_coalition for non-US chips and cooling infrastructure

## Priority

## In practice

This turn, we pivot from pure enforcement to sovereign capacity. The **Autonomous Enforcement Authority** is now law in draft, but we see that it cannot stand without the muscle to back it. The US hyperscaler ignores our audit requests, Poland blocks cross-border enforcement actions, and France accelerates its national AI shield — all because no one believes the EU can act alone. We therefore launch the **Sovereign Compute and Talent Acceleration Programme**, using emergency clauses in the Digital Markets Act and the Chips Act to fast-track permitting, grid access, and export waivers for non-US semiconductor imports via the **middle_power_coalition**. We convene a crisis summit of energy, digital, and interior ministers under the Integrated Political Crisis Response framework to allocate 6GW of priority power for AI clusters in Finland, the Netherlands, and southern Germany, with legal clauses ensuring that no foreign entity can unilaterally withdraw access.

We tie the programme to binding talent agreements: a new Schengen Fast Lane for AI researchers, with housing, tax incentives, and security clearances, and a mandatory service clause for publicly funded PhDs in AI safety. The European Investment Bank will issue €18bn in guarantees, backed by recovery funds reprogrammed under Article 122 TFEU, to co-finance private hyperscalers who agree to anchor their EU operations under joint governance. We offer Italy and Poland a seat on the Sovereign AI Oversight Board — a new body under the Commission — in exchange for dropping their veto threats on enforcement. This is not just about compute; it is about proving that the EU can be a player, not just a rule-setter. Without that, no rule will hold.
