## New measure  
**Measure:** Establish the Open-Weight Risk Monitoring Network  
A distributed early-warning system to continuously monitor and evaluate the capabilities and misuse potential of openly released model weights, using red-teaming, automated capability probing, and threat modelling.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Stabilise or slow the rise of openweight_capability by enabling faster detection of dangerous capabilities; improve understanding of distributed risks; feed into future restriction decisions under the AI Act  
`Applies to:` Research institutions, public evaluators, and trusted third parties across the EU, with access to mirrored open-weight repositories and shared tooling

## In practice  
We are pressing forward on InvestAI with full force, converting political urgency into physical infrastructure. The European Investment Bank is now under binding instruction to disburse the second €40bn tranche, tied to firm grid interconnection agreements with ENTSO-E and verified site readiness in Brittany, northern Sweden, and a newly prioritised third site in Hungary. Emergency permitting under the Net-Zero Industry Act is being invoked to override local delays, but we are pairing it with enforceable regional reinvestment covenants — €5bn in total — to maintain social licence and prevent backlash. The Commission is also advancing a legal framework under Article 114 TFEU to ensure that any hyperscaler benefiting from accelerated permitting under the Tech Sovereignty Package must locate at least 60% of its EU-facing compute within these sovereign sites, with operational control legally vested in EU-domiciled entities.

Meanwhile, the European AI Assurance Directorate is transitioning from ENISA’s temporary oversight into its independent footing, with the first cohort of 75 staff now hired, including red-team leads and cyber-physical systems experts. We are finalising a trust framework for weight access with EU-based developers like Mistral and Aleph Alpha, while preparing legal action under the AI Act to compel disclosure from foreign providers ahead of high-risk deployment authorisations. The Resilience Shield is operationalising through CISA-EU, which has drafted the first set of mandatory stress tests for critical operators in energy and healthcare — these will be trialled in Q4 across six member states, with detection and failover benchmarks set at under four hours. Funding from the Recovery and Resilience Facility is being redirected to upgrade intrusion detection systems with AI-driven anomaly monitoring, though jurisdictional disputes in the Council still slow enforcement.  

The new Open-Weight Risk Monitoring Network launches this turn as a small but essential step to regain visibility: we cannot govern what we cannot measure. Through DG CONNECT, we are funding ten research hubs — from INRIA to Fraunhofer — to stand up automated evaluation pipelines that probe open models for emergent capabilities, particularly in offensive cyber and autonomous agent behaviour. This is not about stopping releases, not yet — it is about ending blindness. The network will feed intelligence directly into EAAD and CISA-EU, creating a feedback loop between open capabilities and defensive preparation. We are not waiting for 60.0 in openweight_capability to act — we are building the eyes to see it coming.
