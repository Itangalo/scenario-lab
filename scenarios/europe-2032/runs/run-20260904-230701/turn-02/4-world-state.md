### Frontier Access Cut, Sovereignty Tested  
The EU’s access to the leading global AI model was abruptly severed, affecting hospitals, ministries, and industrial AI pipelines across multiple member states. The move, unexplained and non-appealable, was widely interpreted as a strategic exclusion. In response, the Commission fast-tracked InvestAI Gigafactories under emergency clauses, releasing €40bn and overriding local objections in Brittany and northern Sweden. However, grid integration delays and supply-chain bottlenecks limited immediate progress, and the broader Tech Sovereignty Package saw only partial legal harmonisation.

### Resilience Gains Ground, Safety Erodes  
The new European Resilience Shield launched with €18bn in funding, setting binding cyber-physical hardening targets for critical infrastructure. CISA-EU began drafting stress tests, though full rollout was delayed by jurisdictional disputes over enforcement. While the measure began yielding early coordination benefits, systemic detection gaps remained. Meanwhile, the jump in global AI capability—from 55.0 to 58.0—outpaced safety efforts. No new safeguards landed on frontier systems, and the access denial incident revealed opaque model reasoning in operational settings, contributing to a decline in ai_safety.

### Political Capital Under Pressure  
With three large measures in flight—including the new Resilience Shield—and InvestAI as the named priority, the EU’s political capital declined by 10. The proposal bonus for the Shield was +3, justified by the recent intrusion and access denial, partially offsetting the burden. Public sentiment dipped slightly as media coverage framed the access cut as a humiliation, amplifying concerns over dependency and job displacement. The revised EAAD levy proposal eased WTO tensions but did not prevent internal legal disputes from stalling full agency independence.
