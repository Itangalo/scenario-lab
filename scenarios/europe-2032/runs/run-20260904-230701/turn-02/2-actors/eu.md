A coordinated, EU-wide programme to harden critical infrastructure against AI-enabled cyber-physical attacks, starting with energy, water, and healthcare systems.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`

## In practice  
We are accelerating the InvestAI Gigafactories through binding commitments under Article 122 TFEU and the Net-Zero Industry Act, now reinforced by the recent denial of access to the leading global model. That event — sudden, unilateral, and unappealable — has shifted the political gravity: what was a strategic hedge is now a demonstrable necessity. We are instructing the European Investment Bank to disburse the first €40bn tranche immediately, conditional on site finalisation by the end of this turn. Grid reservations with ENTSO-E are being converted into firm contracts, and we are invoking emergency permitting clauses in the NZIA to override local objections in Brittany and northern Sweden, paired with a binding €5bn regional reinvestment fund to maintain social licence.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is now tied directly to InvestAI’s delivery timeline: we are conditioning the accelerated permitting zones on enforceable commitments from hyperscalers to locate at least 60% of their EU-facing compute within the Gigafactory sites, with operational control vested in EU-domiciled entities. Legal work under Article 114 TFEU is advancing to harmonise permitting, while the Commission is launching a Blue Card+ fast-track for AI engineers, targeting 10,000 new frontier talent arrivals by 2030. The WTO threat over the EAAD levy has been met with a revised proposal: the 0.5% charge now applies only to models above 50.0 capability deployed in high-risk sectors, narrowing its scope and strengthening its proportionality argument.

Meanwhile, the European AI Assurance Directorate is being stood up under ENISA’s temporary governance, with its first 50 hires focused on agent behaviour red-teaming and cyber-physical risk profiling. We are negotiating access to model weights from EU-based developers under a new trust framework, while preparing legal action to compel access from foreign providers ahead of the AI Act’s high-risk provisions taking effect. The Resilience Shield, our new measure, launches under the NIS2 Directive, with CISA-EU (the expanded European Union Agency for Cybersecurity) tasked to run mandatory stress tests on all critical operators by turn 4. We are allocating €18bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility to fund intrusion detection upgrades, AI-driven anomaly monitoring, and failover system redundancy — with joint exercises to be held in Q3 across six member states. This is not precaution; it is preparation for the next intrusion, which will not be discovered by chance.
