## New measure
**Measure:** Launch Sovereign Agent Development Programme  
A dedicated public effort to develop and operate autonomous agent systems under full EU legal and technical control, using secured domestic compute and auditable architectures, to serve essential government functions without reliance on foreign models.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by at least 12 points; reduce operational dependence on foreign AI services; begin closing the gap between openweight_capability and sovereign capability; signal credible autonomy to markets and public  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national governments, and designated public operators in health, energy, and civil protection

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign Agent Development Programme  
This is no longer about access or fallbacks — it is about ownership of function. The denial incident and the election_consolidation event confirm that foreign providers will prioritise their own strategic interests, and the rise of agent-grade open frameworks means even distributed tools carry unacceptable risk if uncontrolled. We must run our own agents, on our own infrastructure, answering only to us.

## In practice
We are treating the cutoff not as an anomaly but as the new baseline. The Sovereign Agent Development Programme launches under the Joint Undertaking framework, hosted by the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU), with €42bn allocated from the unspent InvestAI contingency tranche and redirected cohesion funds. The programme will commission three parallel development tracks: one focused on healthcare coordination and triage automation, one on energy grid optimisation under AI-driven stress, and one on civil emergency response orchestration. Each will run on sovereign compute clusters located in Finland, Austria, and Bulgaria — sites selected for political neutrality, grid stability, and cooling efficiency — and will be subject to real-time EAAD audits and adversarial probing by CISA-EU red teams.

The emergency compute access provisions, now in their second turn of implementation, are being operationalised through the Digital Markets Act enforcement architecture. The Commission has issued delegated acts defining “essential operators” in coordination with member states, and national regulators are conducting compliance checks on the top five foreign hyperscalers active in the EU. Where providers refuse on-premise deployment, we are enforcing fallbacks via sovereign cloud zones operated by Gaia-X-certified providers, with SLAs guaranteeing sub-72-hour failover. Legal challenges from two member states are being addressed through targeted cohesion transfers tied to joint resilience planning, preserving unity while upholding the rule.

Meanwhile, the European Resilience Shield is entering its second phase. Based on findings from the live-fire drills, we are mandating firmware-level override protocols in all critical infrastructure procurement above €10m, effective Q1 2029. The override systems will be physically isolated, manually triggered, and tested quarterly in unannounced simulations. We are also expanding the Shield’s remit to include biosecurity detection, integrating AI-powered pathogen surveillance into the ECDC’s early-warning network — a move funded through reprioritised Horizon Europe grants. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that when the next shock comes, the Union still has hands on the controls.
