### A Leap Forward, a Step Back
A leading U.S. lab releases an AI system demonstrating discontinuous progress—autonomous operation now spans complex, multi-week projects in logistics, engineering and code synthesis. This leap pushes global `ai_capability` to 70.5. The release bypasses standard safety gates, and the internal reasoning behind key decisions remains opaque, triggering alarm among technical auditors. With no matching safety advance, `ai_safety` plummets to 11.0.

### Open Weights Catch the Wave
The leaked agent framework is rapidly adapted using newly disclosed verification methods in legal and clinical domains. Open communities now run persistent agents capable of managing real-world workflows with minimal oversight. `openweight_capability` rises to 58.0, narrowing the gap with the frontier and increasing misuse risks.

### EU Responds to Strategic Isolation
The U.S. election concludes with a decisive victory for the candidate advocating strategic control of AI. The result signals a new era of rationed access for foreign partners. In response, the EU launches the Sovereign Agent Development Programme—a €42bn initiative to build auditable, domestically controlled agent systems for critical functions. Hosted under EuroHPC JU, it begins parallel development tracks in healthcare, energy, and civil protection, running on sovereign compute clusters.

### Resilience Inches Forward
The European Resilience Shield enacts firmware-level override mandates for critical infrastructure and expands into biosecurity surveillance. These measures yield modest gains, lifting `resilience` to 45.0.

### Sovereignty Erodes Despite Action
Despite new investment, `eu_ai_sovereignty` drops to 15.0. The capability jump and tightened U.S. access policies deepen structural dependence. Physical delays persist at InvestAI sites, and no sovereign capacity is yet secured.
