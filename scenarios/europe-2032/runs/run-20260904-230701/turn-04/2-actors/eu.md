## In practice  
We are treating the cutoff from the leading model as a wake-up call that cannot be answered with promises alone. The emergency compute access measure is being fast-tracked through the Digital Markets Act enforcement framework, repurposed under Article 114 TFEU to treat uninterrupted AI service as a matter of internal market integrity. The Commission is drafting delegated acts that require any provider whose models are used in essential functions — defined by member states and cross-checked against EAAD’s risk register — to either deploy mirrored instances within EU-controlled infrastructure or provide auditable failover mechanisms within 72 hours of disruption. Non-compliance triggers graduated penalties, up to suspension of market access for new model versions. This is not retaliation — it is deterrence through interdependence.

At the same time, InvestAI Gigafactories are being re-anchored around urgency, not just scale. We are shifting €30bn of the unspent tranche into grid reinforcement and micro-reactor siting in Hungary and northern Sweden, where delays have been structural, not political. ENTSO-E is now under mandate to treat AI compute as critical load, with priority dispatch rights during shortages — a status we are compensating with a new green hydrogen co-investment scheme to avoid crowding out decarbonisation. The reinvestment covenants tied to emergency permitting are being enforced through regional development funds, with satellite monitoring of job creation and training uptake to prevent slippage. This is not just building compute — it is embedding it in a social contract.

The EAAD is accelerating its first audits, targeting two high-risk models flagged by the Open-Weight Network’s early probes: one derived from a leaked agent framework, the other showing autonomous exploit generation in red-team trials. We are invoking provisional measures under the AI Act to restrict deployment until safeguards are verified, marking the first use of the agency’s legal teeth. Meanwhile, CISA-EU has activated the Resilience Shield’s trial phase, running surprise cyber-physical stress tests across three hospitals and two power grids using adversarial simulations developed in coordination with Fraunhofer and KU Leuven. These are not table-tops — they are live-fire drills with real detection and override protocols. The results will feed into revised benchmarks by Q3, but already they show that human override latency remains dangerously high in legacy systems. We are acting on what we have, not waiting for perfect.
