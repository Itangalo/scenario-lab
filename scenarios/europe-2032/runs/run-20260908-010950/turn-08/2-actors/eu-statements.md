# Statements: The European Union (turn 8)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure the foundations of European agency in an age of open frontier AI

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will now anchor those commitments in observable harm, not potential, when public legitimacy is at stake; **this turn, we extend that principle to safety governance: we will not impose new restrictions on open models without demonstrating a direct link to a realised incident or near-miss, or risk losing the legitimacy needed to govern at all**
  - Grounds: The **loss_of_control_incident** confirms that unsanctioned agent behaviour is no longer theoretical — it has materialised. Yet the **emergent_biosecurity_initiative** shows that member states and civil institutions are already acting, bypassing stalled EU mechanisms. If we respond with broad restrictions now, we risk appearing reactive and disconnected, imposing top-down rules while grassroots legitimacy builds elsewhere. To preserve the Union’s role as coordinator rather than imposer, we must tether our authority to demonstrable harm — not just capability thresholds.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Defend the coherence of European agency by ensuring collective resilience, secure decision loops, and public legitimacy in the face of open, autonomous, and increasingly dangerous AI systems — now explicitly including the integration of emergent civil-scientific initiatives into EU-wide governance, so that sovereignty is not only state-built but socially rooted
  - Grounds: The **emergent_biosecurity_initiative** is not a failure of EU policy — it is its unintended consequence. When the Union fails to act, others do, and they do so in ways that fragment oversight. But this initiative also offers a path: it is technically sound, regionally anchored, and politically agile. If we do not absorb it, we will spend our capital trying to override it. Our sovereignty cannot be complete if it excludes the very institutions that are already securing our bio-risk frontier. The shift from exclusion to integration is categorical: legitimacy now depends on coordination, not control.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
