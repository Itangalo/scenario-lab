## In practice
We are pressing forward with the European Decision Loop Shield despite mounting resistance. The Council Presidency is finalising the draft regulation on “core decision loops” under Article 122 TFEU, designating pandemic response, energy grid balancing, and border security as systems that must run on shielded infrastructure by turn 13. We are engaging the European Parliament’s civil liberties committee directly, offering binding transparency protocols and audit rights to ease centralisation concerns. To preserve Polish participation, we are fast-tracking co-funding for the Polish node through the Recovery and Resilience Facility, linking it to national cybersecurity upgrades. The Finnish node is now co-funded by the European Defence Agency, establishing a precedent for military-civil convergence. EuroHPC JU is accelerating procurement, and we are leveraging semiconductor partnerships with ASML, STMicroelectronics, and Infineon to fast-track chip packaging for AI stacks — not to match the frontier, but to ensure it cannot silence us.

At the same time, we are launching the Labour Transition Acceleration Fund in response to **labour_displacement**, which is now undeniable. Entry-level hiring has collapsed in law, accountancy, and software, and public anger is mounting. This fund will provide wage insurance for displaced graduates retraining in AI-augmented roles and subsidise employer-led programmes that pair automation with upskilling. We will draw €12bn from the European Social Fund and condition access on national co-investment, ensuring member states share ownership. Implementation will run through national employment agencies and European Labour Authority coordination, with disbursement tied to verifiable transitions into new roles. This is not just social policy — it is strategic: without public legitimacy, no shield holds, no sovereignty stands. We act on both fronts because the two mandates converge here — protecting decision integrity requires protecting the people whose trust powers it.
