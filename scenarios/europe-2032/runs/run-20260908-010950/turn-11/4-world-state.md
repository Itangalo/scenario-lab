### Dual Fronts, Dwindling Capital

The European Union pressed ahead on two fronts: reinforcing sovereign AI infrastructure through the European Decision Loop Shield and launching the Labour Transition Acceleration Fund to counter growing social strain. Yet political capital, already thin, buckled under the strain.

The Shield advanced technically—procurement accelerated, and the Finnish node secured defence co-funding—but politically, it faltered. Legal challenges from Poland and France over data sovereignty intensified, and the European Parliament delayed the core decision loops regulation, demanding stronger decentralised governance. Polish participation remained uncertain despite Recovery Fund incentives, as regional alliance talks deepened. The initiative delivered no tangible sovereignty gain this turn.

Simultaneously, the Labour Transition Acceleration Fund launched in response to widespread job losses in entry-level cognitive sectors. While framing was strategic—tying social stability to AI legitimacy—implementation faced resistance. Several member states hesitated to commit co-funding, and employer uptake was slow, with firms questioning the return on co-working transitions. The fund’s scale and ambition offered long-term promise, but immediate effects were muted.

Public sentiment dipped further, to 43.0, as media spotlighted both the Shield’s centralisation risks and the visible hollowing out of graduate job markets. The open-source audit movement gained traction, with researchers probing commercial models for hidden behaviours, amplifying unease.

Global AI capability crept to 60.7, reflecting continued progress below the threshold of major leaps. Openweight capability rose slightly to 51.5, driven by community adaptations of leaked techniques. With no safety measures implemented, ai_safety remained stagnant at 13.0. Resilience fell by another point, now at 38.0, as systemic fragility deepened without active protective measures.

### A Union Stretching Too Thin

The EU’s political capital collapsed to 10.0—now critically low. The portfolio charge of −4 (−3 for the large category 4 measure, −1 for priority) drained resources, with no offsetting gains. The new Labour Fund, though justified by recent displacement, earned no proposal bonus due to implementation delays and fragmented buy-in. With capital below 12, the Union risks losing control of its agenda in the next turn.
