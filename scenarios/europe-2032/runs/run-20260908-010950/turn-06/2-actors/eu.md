## New measure
**Mandatory Pre-Release Evaluation for High-Capability Open Models**  
Require independent evaluation and risk assessment before public release of any open-weight model exceeding 45.0 capability, with enforcement through hosting liability and funding conditions for research entities.  

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to ai_safety over two turns by creating a procedural barrier to unvetted high-capability releases; slows openweight_capability growth if compliance is high, but may drive fragmentation if resisted  
`Applies to:` Open research communities, academic labs, and cloud-hosted model repositories operating within or serving the EU

## Priority
**EU-Wide AI Incident Response Drills and Cross-Border Mutual Aid Activation** — this remains the priority because the cyber_major_incident proved that even modestly capable models can disrupt essential services when coordination fails, and with mutual aid still untested, our only path to resilience is through exercised response, not just technical fixes.

## In practice
We are treating the post-incident period as a hinge: either we harden collective response now, or we accept recurring breakdowns as the new normal. The Resilience Surge has concluded its technical rollout phase, but its real test was always whether member states would submit to shared standards under pressure. ENISA’s emergency directives were met with resistance in regions citing procurement delays and understaffed IT teams—so we are now activating the Union Civil Protection Mechanism to reframe hardening as crisis preparedness, unlocking emergency funding and cross-border deployment rights. The three embedded Cyber Rapid Response teams in Belgium, Bavaria, and northern Italy are now conducting live red-teaming exercises, using low-capability models to simulate attack paths, while feeding lessons into the new EU-wide drills. We are pairing this with bonus access to InvestEU’s 2031 tranche for regions that complete zero-trust certification by turn 7, turning compliance into a forward-looking incentive.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories site selection enters final review, with political pressure mounting to prioritize locations near existing semiconductor clusters in Dresden and Leuven. The Internal Market Council’s stockpile review under the Chips Act is now complete, and we are quietly mandating 18 months of buffer stock for critical lithography components, treating supply resilience as a silent pillar of sovereignty. The Tech Sovereignty Package has fulfilled its initial mandate, and private-sector data centre investment is on track—but its narrative is shifting from growth to contingency, as insurers demand proof of cyber resilience before underwriting new projects. Public sentiment remains strained, and we are launching a targeted communication campaign through the Digital Europe Programme to frame resilience not as fear, but as preparedness: a civic counterpart to civil defence. But we know the next shock will test more than systems—it will test whether the Union still commands the trust to act.
