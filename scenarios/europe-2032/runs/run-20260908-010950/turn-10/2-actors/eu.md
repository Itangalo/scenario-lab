## New measure
**European Decision Loop Shield (category 4, Sovereignty and industrial capacity):** Establish a legally anchored, EU-operated compute grid with sovereign access to frontier-class AI systems, ensuring that critical decision-making loops in crisis response, defence, and essential public services cannot be disabled or influenced by external actors.

## Priority
European Decision Loop Shield — because the **member_state_defection** and **emergent_regional_aid_alliance** prove that when the Union cannot guarantee sovereign command over crisis response, member states will act alone; and because the **emergent_ai_audit_leak** confirms that even frontier labs are losing control of their systems — we cannot rely on external providers to safeguard EU decision integrity.

## In practice
We are launching the European Decision Loop Shield under Article 122 TFEU for critical infrastructure and the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions to compel access terms from foreign hyperscalers. The European High Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) will lead deployment, with €18bn in emergency cohesion funding redirected from stalled modernisation lines. Our target is 20 exaflops of sovereignly governed AI compute online by turn 13, distributed across three core sites — one in France, one in Poland, and one in Finland — with physical and legal guarantees that no external actor can throttle, monitor, or repurpose workloads. We are negotiating with ASML, STMicroelectronics, and Infineon to fast-track lithography and chip packaging capacity for AI-specific stacks, leveraging our upstream semiconductor position as collateral.

The Council Presidency is circulating a draft regulation to designate “core decision loops” — including pandemic response coordination, energy grid balancing, and border security operations — as requiring AI systems that run on this shielded infrastructure. Resistance is expected from member states with existing bilateral cloud deals, and from the European Parliament’s civil liberties committee, which fears centralisation. We are offering opt-in phasing for smaller states and binding transparency protocols to assuage oversight concerns. The European Defence Agency will co-fund the Finnish node to anchor military-civil convergence, while the European Social Fund will link retraining for AI operations roles to participation in shield maintenance — turning infrastructure into legitimacy. This is not about matching the frontier; it is about ensuring that when the frontier fails, Europe still has a voice that can be heard and a hand that can act.
