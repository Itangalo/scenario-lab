This compromise, framed as an emergency coordination regime under Article 122 TFEU, eased French and Polish resistance. ENISA was empowered to enforce protocols, while audit rights were granted to the European Data Protection Supervisor. The Polish node advanced via Recovery Fund incentives tied to cybersecurity upgrades and joint drills.

The Labour Transition Acceleration Fund began operations through national agencies, with early pilots in Germany and the Netherlands testing human-AI co-working labs. Disbursement was linked to verified job transitions, not just training completion, reinforcing accountability. However, broader uptake remained sluggish.

A quiet diplomatic breakthrough emerged: a coalition of middle powers, including Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Canada, and Norway, aligned on export controls and established shared evaluation capacity for frontier models. This framework strengthened the EU’s leverage without requiring unilateral sovereignty expansion.
