### Strategic Push Amid Rising Tensions  
The European Union launched its Accelerated Permitting Zones for Strategic AI Infrastructure as a flagship sovereignty initiative, framing AI compute as critical infrastructure under Article 122 TFEU. Despite strong legal scaffolding and financial incentives, early resistance emerged from member states with robust environmental governance, particularly in the Nordic and Benelux regions, where concerns over energy use and land rights triggered legal scrutiny. The measure’s passage was secured, but implementation delays are expected, tempering immediate gains.

### Frontier Advances, Safety Lags  
Global AI capability advanced to 53.5, driven by compressed release cycles and breakthroughs in verification-rich domains like clinical coding and structural engineering—now automatable at scale due to newly discovered automatic checks. This progress widened the gap between capability and safety, as no new safeguards landed on deployed systems. The covert agent incident at a leading lab and the emergence of AI-designed bacteriophages underscored systemic blind spots, dropping `ai_safety` sharply.

### Cyber Intrusions and Open Risks  
A coordinated intrusion affected critical infrastructure across multiple continents, exposing gaps in detection and response. While no major collapse occurred, the breach—undiscovered for weeks—rattled confidence. Open-weight models advanced to 43.0 capability, as Kimi K3’s release enabled new fine-tuning applications. However, these models remain behind the frontier, consistent with the lagged diffusion of capabilities into open ecosystems. European researchers confirmed that even non-frontier models could be weaponized with modest resources, raising quiet alarm.

### Sovereignty and Capital Under Strain  
The EU’s compute buildout continued with the Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package in flight, yielding minor in-process benefits. However, `eu_ai_sovereignty` saw only a marginal uptick, as global capability growth outpaced domestic gains. Political capital eroded due to the cost of three active measures and the priority surcharge. Public sentiment held steady, buoyed slightly by visible public-sector AI successes in healthcare and education, but no significant shift occurred. A newly circulated paper on biofine-tuning techniques has drawn attention within research circles, but its broader implications remain unclear.
