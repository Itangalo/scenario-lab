## In practice
We are treating the Resilience Surge as a live-fire rehearsal for breakdown. ENISA has issued binding technical standards this turn for zero-trust segmentation in clinical IT systems and automated log auditing using low-capability, high-assurance models—deliberately avoiding dependence on frontier systems whose safety we cannot verify. We are fast-tracking procurement exemptions for pre-approved tooling stacks in healthcare and energy, backed by 70% co-financing from the Digital Europe Programme and ReSiliconisation Facility. Embedded EU Cyber Rapid Response teams are now deployed in Belgium, Bavaria, and northern Italy, where dense interconnection and high-value research create systemic risk. Resistance remains strong in regional health IT departments, but we are pairing mandates with bonus access to future InvestEU tranches for early adopters, creating a tangible incentive to comply.

The Public-Service AI Adoption Accelerator has now concluded its rollout phase, and we are conducting final evaluations of uptake and fraud resilience. Regions seeking renewal beyond this turn must now demonstrate completed threat modelling against AI-amplified spoofing and disinformation—a new condition tied to the election crisis. Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories site selection is nearing completion, though Taiwan tensions have prompted the Internal Market Council to fast-track a review of chip stockpile requirements under the Chips Act. We are quietly expanding buffer stocks and diversifying foundry access, treating supply chain resilience as a core component of sovereignty. The Tech Sovereignty Package continues to drive private investment, but its political weight is shifting: it is no longer framed as growth policy, but as insurance against the very real risk of cascading failure in a world where AI-enabled attacks move faster than democratic oversight can respond.
