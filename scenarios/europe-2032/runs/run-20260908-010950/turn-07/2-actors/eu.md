## New measure
**European AI Transition Fund with Binding Public-Sector Automation Limits**  
Establish a €45bn fund to support displaced public-sector workers through wage insurance, retraining, and regional transition programmes, conditioned on a moratorium on new AI-driven layoffs in public services until safety and oversight mechanisms are verified.

## In practice
We are treating the strikes not as resistance to progress, but as a signal of broken trust. The Digital Europe Programme is now repurposing €8bn in existing funds to launch immediate retraining pilots in the most affected regions — Rhône-Alpes, Silesia, and Central Greece — while the European Social Fund Plus is fast-tracking disbursement rules to allow wage insurance within six weeks of displacement. The Council of Ministers will vote on the full Transition Fund in December under the legal base of Article 175 TFEU (cohesion), with a conditionality clause: no member state can receive AI modernisation grants if it automates public jobs without independent impact assessment. We are negotiating with the European Parliament’s S&D and Greens groups to secure support, offering them co-drafting rights on the verification protocol. At the same time, we are instructing the European Public Administration School to develop a certification standard for “human-in-command” AI use in public services, to distinguish between augmentation and replacement.

The completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories and the EU-wide drills marks a threshold: the technical foundations are laid, but they are now secondary to political survival. ENISA will publish a post-exercise report this quarter, showing that while red-teaming revealed gaps, the core mutual aid framework held — a message we are pushing through state broadcasters and public service unions to show that preparedness works. But we know the next test is not cyber, but civic. The openweight_frontier_release means we can no longer delay oversight — but we cannot impose it without legitimacy. So we are linking the final adoption of the pre-release evaluation rule to the launch of the Transition Fund, framing both as parts of a single compact: society will bear the risks of AI only if it sees the benefits and protections in return. This is not a delay — it is a recalibration. The Union must not only act, but be seen to act justly.
