## Priority
**Accelerated Permitting Zones for Strategic AI Infrastructure** — this remains the fastest lever to scale sovereign compute under EU control, foundational to both risk prevention and strategic autonomy, especially as openweight capability rises and external access proves fragile, and its legal and political momentum must be sustained through implementation.

## In practice
We are advancing the Accelerated Permitting Zones through the Competitiveness Council, finalising the delegated act that designates the first three zones in collaboration with ENTSO-E and the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium. The legal basis under Article 122 TFEU is being reinforced with environmental safeguards to pre-empt challenges: each zone must submit a full offset plan and a regional workforce integration scheme before activation. We are offering early access to RePowerEU grid modernisation funds and extended InvestEU co-financing to member states that fast-track site approvals, creating a tangible incentive for cooperation.

Meanwhile, the Gigafactories programme is in procurement phase, with site selection underway and chip supply chain assurances being negotiated through the European Chips Act framework. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being leveraged to attract private capital, though investor sentiment remains cautious due to energy cost volatility. On the ground, resistance persists in environmentally strong regions, but the recent cyber intrusion and biofine-tuning concerns are shifting the narrative toward systemic risk, giving our argument for sovereign control new weight. We are preparing a communication push to frame compute resilience not as industrial policy but as civil protection in an AI-driven era.
