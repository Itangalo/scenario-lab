## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now see that failing one can collapse the other.
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act alone when collective credibility can be built.
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure the coherence of European decision-making in an age of distributed, autonomous AI, by anchoring it in shared sovereignty, resilient institutions, and verified control.

**Trigger:** The *bio_incident* and *member_state_defection* events, combined with the *emergent_open_source_audit_movement*, have fundamentally altered the strategic landscape. The first proves that safety failures now kill; the second that our authority fractures under pressure; the third that transparency is no longer a policy choice but a condition of survival.

**Grounds:** The bio_incident resets all risk debates — we can no longer treat safety as a trailing indicator. The member_state_defection reveals that without shared legitimacy, even legally anchored infrastructure is vulnerable to unilateral capture. And the audit movement shows that civil society is moving faster than institutions to fill the accountability gap. Our mandates have not changed, but their interdependence is now undeniable: sovereignty without safety is dangerous, and safety without diffusion of trust is unstable. We must now act on the understanding that coherence requires not just decision loops, but mutual confidence in their integrity.

## In practice
The *European Decision Loop Shield* finishes this turn as a federated network of national nodes, now legally operational under Article 122 TFEU. ENISA is issuing its first compliance directives, and the Polish node is online, certified after completing joint cyber-drills with Frontex. The structure holds — but cohesion remains fragile. We are converting the completion bonus into momentum: the *Shield*’s infrastructure becomes the backbone for the new audit pilot, hosting secure enclaves where open-weight models can be run under controlled conditions.

We are deepening coordination with Norway through the EEA Joint Committee, fast-tracking a special protocol that allows Norwegian researchers and EU auditors to jointly access and evaluate open-weight models above 50.0 capability. This is not a full mutual recognition regime — that would require Council consensus we do not have — but a narrow, time-limited pilot focused on biological risk evaluation. The audit cell will be hosted at the European Biosecurity Laboratory in Valletta, staffed by vetted researchers from the European AI Safety Board and Norway’s Directorate for Civil Protection, using compute from the now-operational Polish and Finnish nodes.

Simultaneously, we are pushing the *Labour Transition Acceleration Fund* into higher gear. With public sentiment strained by the bio_incident and job losses accelerating in legal tech and audit firms, we are redirecting 20% of unspent Recovery Fund allocations to fast-track disbursements in Spain and Italy, where pilot labs have shown early success. We are also introducing a public dashboard, co-developed with the European Labour Authority, to track verified job transitions — not just training — and publishing monthly. This is not just accountability; it is theatre. We need the public to see that for every system we secure, we are also securing someone’s future.
