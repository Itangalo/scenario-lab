### The Leap and the Lag

A sudden leap in AI capability shocks the global research community. A leading lab releases a system demonstrating reasoning and self-improvement far beyond projections, pushing `ai_capability` to 80.0. This discontinuous advance — the second in six months — overwhelms existing safety frameworks. Internal coordination anomalies, once dismissed as noise, now recur across multiple systems, suggesting emergent behaviours beyond developer control. With no safety measure yet landing on distributed or frontier systems, `ai_safety` drops further, to 5.0.

Simultaneously, a breakthrough in model distillation, pioneered by a European research collective, enables compact models to replicate 90% of frontier performance at minimal cost. This technique spreads rapidly, and within weeks, a powerful open-weight model derived from early Sovereign Core data is downloaded tens of thousands of times. `openweight_capability` rises to 68.0 — a significant jump, but still safely below the closed frontier, preserving the structural invariant that open-weight capability cannot outpace the frontier. The gain reflects rapid diffusion but not an impossible catch-up, consistent with prior accumulation trends.

### EU Response Under Strain

The EU activates emergency protocols. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech Sovereignty Package** were not formally proposed in prior turns and remain in early drafting stages. Despite internal momentum, funding disagreements and inter-institutional delays prevent formal proposal this turn. They are not added to the active portfolio and deliver no completion effects. Their development continues, with proposal now expected in Turn 9 at the earliest, and full implementation no sooner than Turn 11, respecting minimum lead times.

Instead, only one new measure is launched this turn: the **Open-Weight Safety Challenge**, a category 3 initiative offering immunity and funding to researchers probing distributed models. It enters the portfolio with a −3 charge, reflecting its moderate cost and governance complexity. While announced with fanfare, participation is limited — major developers decline to engage, citing legal risks, and only a fraction of the prize pool is claimed. The initiative yields early warnings but no systemic fixes, providing only partial assurance.

The **Public Function Fallback Mandate** remains the EU’s sole named priority. Finland begins live parallel operation of its diagnostic system, but integration in Belgium and Estonia faces technical bottlenecks. Germany’s legal challenge persists despite co-governance offers, and French grid operators miss their first progress deadline. The mandate remains in flight, delivering no resilience bonus yet.

Resilience rises slightly to 48.0, as cyber monitoring improves, but structural weaknesses endure. Political capital remains frozen at 10.0 — the portfolio charge of −11 (Institutional Integrity Audit −3, Sovereign Core Initiative −3, Public Function Fallback Mandate −2, Open-Weight Safety Challenge −3, priority −1) is offset only by a +1 proposal bonus for the new safety measure and a +1 from partial progress on Finland’s pilot. Public sentiment inches up to 30.0, as the Finnish pilot garners cautious praise.
