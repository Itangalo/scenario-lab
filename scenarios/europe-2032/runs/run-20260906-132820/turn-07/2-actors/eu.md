## In practice
We are now enforcing the integration of the Sovereign Core into live public functions. Under Article 122 TFEU and the crisis clause of the Recovery and Resilience Facility, we are activating the €50bn Emergency Sovereignty Facility to fund the retrofitting of health diagnostics, grid load forecasting, and transport coordination systems to interface with the Sovereign Core stack. The first pilot — in the Finnish national health service — will run a parallel diagnostic track using the sovereign model for rare disease detection, with results audited by the European Medicines Agency. This is not a simulation: it is a live test of whether Europe can save lives with its own tools.

Resistance remains. Germany’s federal states object to centralised model mandates, citing subsidiarity; French energy firms warn of integration delays; and the European Parliament’s Legal Affairs Committee has opened a review of the emergency powers used to redirect EuroHPC capacity. We are negotiating with the Council’s Competitiveness formation to convert the Sovereign Core from an emergency project into a permanent pillar of digital sovereignty, with a new legal base under the Digital Governance Act. The Public Function Fallback Mandate ensures that by turn 10, every critical operator has not just a plan, but a tested, audited, publicly reportable fallback — no longer a theoretical core, but a working one. The **emergent_municipal_oversight_network** will be invited to observe the first round of drills, not as adversaries, but as partners in proving that sovereignty is not secrecy — it is service.
