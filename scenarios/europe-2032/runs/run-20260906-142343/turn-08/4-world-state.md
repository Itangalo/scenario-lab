### The Core Stands, But at What Cost?

The Sovereign AI Core Programme presses ahead in Brittany and northern Sweden, with microgrid construction beginning under emergency powers. In the Alps, modular units sit idle as German and Austrian authorities block access, citing unresolved CJEU proceedings. The Commission’s offer of co-leadership on environmental oversight fails to quell resistance—Berlin insists on halting all work until the Court rules on whether Article 122 TFEU permits AI infrastructure as an emergency.

Runtime monitoring expands under EAAD, but opaque reasoning defeats audit tools. ENISA’s heuristic tracers miss a cascading failure in Hungarian hospital AI triage systems, exposing flaws in agent drift detection. Brussels enforces “no audit, no access,” downgrading Budapest’s AI services—sparking a diplomatic rift framed as digital colonialism.

Public backlash deepens. The Human Futures Movement rejects the proposed Citizens’ Assembly as symbolic, demanding a full moratorium. Vandalism spreads to a second Austrian site. Polling shows opposition to the Core at 72%, with growing anger over job losses in law, accountancy, and software—sectors hollowed out by AI automation.

Globally, verification widens in clinical coding and contract review, accelerating capability growth. Entry-level hiring collapses across Europe. The EIB’s performance-linked bonds release no new tranches—milestones remain unmet, and legal uncertainty stalls disbursement.

### A Union Under Strain

With four large measures still active—including the Core—political capital evaporates. Italy’s constitutional challenge gains momentum, backed by legal scholars questioning EU overreach. Cohesion Fund threats backfire, eroding trust further. No new measure is proposed, but the priority remains, draining capital with no offsetting gains.
