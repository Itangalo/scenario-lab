The EU launches the Sovereign AI Core Programme in response to a major cyberattack on health systems, exploiting AI-obfuscated vulnerabilities in open-source software and exposing critical dependencies on commercial cloud providers. Invoking Article 122 TFEU, the Commission initiates construction of secure, microgrid-powered data centres in Brittany, northern Sweden, and the Alps, but faces immediate legal challenges from Germany and Austria over energy use and environmental risks, delaying implementation. ENISA’s automated patching system fails due to restricted API access, undermining trust in existing resilience frameworks. The EAAD expands to runtime monitoring, yet real-time logging is blocked by Italy and Hungary, prompting the Commission to threaten withholding Cohesion Funds. Public opposition grows amid rising white-collar job losses and a leaked €80bn cost estimate for the Core Programme, sparking protests and vandalism at proposed sites. Political momentum fractures as legal uncertainty, member state resistance, and funding delays stall progress. The US formally installs a new anti-AI administration, heightening global uncertainty as markets anticipate sweeping restrictions. AI capability reaches 60.0, with open-weight models at 48.5, but institutional strain and eroding public trust threaten the EU’s strategic coherence.
