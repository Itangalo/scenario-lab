## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign access to frontier AI capability``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The failure to prevent the collapse of ai_safety to 0.0, the continued erosion of eu_ai_sovereignty to 13.0, and the inability to enforce the Frontier Model Compute Licensing Regime reveal that resilience alone cannot ground agency. Without independent access to frontier capability, the EU cannot evaluate, constrain, or counter systems that already operate beyond human oversight. The next two years must be defined by closing this gap — not merely reacting to harm, but ensuring the Union can act from a position of technical parity.

## New measure
**EU Sovereign Frontier AI Programme**  
Launch a dedicated, high-priority effort to deploy and operate a model at or above 85.0 capability on sovereign EU-controlled infrastructure, with full access to weights, interpretability tooling, and red-teaming authority.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` +20 to eu_ai_sovereignty by establishing an independent EU foothold at the frontier; enables credible evaluation (supporting ai_safety), strengthens enforcement leverage (supporting category 3 measures), and provides a fallback if external access is cut; begins to close the gap between openweight_capability and ai_capability on EU terms  
`Applies to:` EU AI Office, EuroHPC JU, national digital ministries, selected EU-based frontier labs, and sovereign cloud providers

## Priority
**EU Sovereign Frontier AI Programme** — because without independent access to frontier systems, the EU cannot understand, verify, or resist what is being built beyond its borders, and because the collapse of ai_safety to 0.0 means that waiting for alignment or audit access is no longer an option — we must run our own.

## In practice
We are acting on the core failure of the last two years: we built resilience, but we did not build power. The European AI Emergency Response Corps was right in concept but crippled in practice — not because of bad design, but because it had no sovereign system to run on, no weights to inspect, and no compute base it could command when push came to shove. This turn, we are launching the **EU Sovereign Frontier AI Programme**, a binding directive under Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act’s ancillary powers, to deploy a model at or above 85.0 capability on infrastructure fully under EU legal and physical control by turn 13. We are repurposing 70% of the EuroHPC budget, redirecting Chips Act funds to secure lithography access for AI-specific stacks, and mandating that all EU-funded compute clusters reserve 40% of capacity for this programme. The EU AI Office will lead, with direct reporting to the College of Commissioners.

We are negotiating with France, Germany, and the Netherlands to co-locate the first sovereign training run in a hardened facility with air-gapped monitoring and real-time interpretability pipelines — a condition of continued access to EU data and talent. We are offering compliant labs a carve-out from the Frontier Model Compute Licensing Regime if they contribute architecture insights or transfer knowledge to the sovereign effort. Resistance will come from U.S. hyperscalers who see this as a threat to their dominance, and from member states reliant on foreign cloud access — but we are no longer asking permission to exist at the frontier. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that, for the first time since the capability explosion, the EU can look at a frontier model and say: *We built this. We control this. We understand this.* That is the only foundation from which safety, sovereignty, and agency can be restored.
