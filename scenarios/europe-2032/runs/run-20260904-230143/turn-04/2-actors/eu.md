## New measure  
**Measure:** Enact binding offensive cyber red lines for non-open AI systems  
Mandate that any model above 55.0 capability deployed in the EU must not be used to generate or execute cyber exploits against critical infrastructure, with automatic deployment suspension and audit triggers if such use is detected.

Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 6  
Targeted effect: Reduce misuse risk by increasing deterrence; improve ai_safety by +4–6 through verifiable behavioural constraints; signal EU willingness to enforce boundaries despite low political capital  
Applies to: Non-EU frontier laboratories operating in the EU market, ENISA, national cybersecurity agencies, and critical infrastructure operators

## Priority  
**Priority:** Sovereign Access to Frontier Models Initiative  
This measure is now our operational priority because without verified access, EAAD cannot function, our safety claims are hollow, and our sovereignty remains performative — and with openweight capability at 52.0, the window to establish control before offensive autonomy spreads further is closing fast.

## In practice  
We are advancing the Sovereign Access initiative through a dual-track approach: legally, under Article 16 of the AI Act, we are finalising the delegated act that defines “representative instance” access for ENISA and EAAD-designated researchers, with secure air-gapped evaluation environments to be hosted at national research labs in France and Germany. Technically, we are building the Model Passport API gateway with DG CONNECT and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, enabling labs to grant access without exposing full weights — a compromise meant to overcome resistance from U.S.-based developers citing IP and security concerns. The legal service continues to assess the Article 346 TFEU risk, but we are proceeding on the basis that evaluation sovereignty is inseparable from strategic autonomy.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain our foundational commitment, and we are now executing the €40bn EIB bond issuance under Article 122 TFEU, justified as a green industrial emergency. This requires Council approval, so we are linking each gigafactory to a binding off-take agreement for green hydrogen production — positioning them as anchor loads for decarbonisation, not speculative compute. We have secured alignment from Energy and Industry DGs, and grid operators in northern Sweden are fast-tracking substation upgrades using emergency powers under the Net-Zero Industry Act to bypass local permitting delays. However, investor hesitation persists, and we are preparing to take majority equity stakes in the Brittany and Scandinavia sites to de-risk private participation.

The Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge finishes this turn: red-teaming of high-risk nodes in energy and transport is complete, and mandatory intrusion detection upgrades are now live across 18 major operators. This delivers a one-time +3 to resilience, bringing it to 43.0, but we are already seeing adaptive evasion in AI-driven probes — confirming that static defences are not enough. EAAD continues staffing and protocol work, but its credibility hinges on access, which is why we are now pairing Sovereign Access with a new red line: no model above 55.0 capability may be used to generate or deploy cyber exploits against EU critical infrastructure. This rule, enforced through ENISA audits and automatic suspension, is our first concrete restriction tied to capability level — a signal that we will act even when capital is low and compliance uncertain.
