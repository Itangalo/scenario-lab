### Capability Creeps Forward
Despite reduced frontier investment, integration of advanced models into enterprise systems pushes global AI capability up by +2.5 this turn, reaching 62.0. The trajectory remains steady, with labs resuming scaled training runs after stabilising funding. Open-weight models benefit from replication of Kimi K3 variants and improved tooling, lifting `openweight_capability` to 56.0—now enabling skilled actors to conduct multi-stage cyber intrusions with minimal oversight.

### Resilience Gains, But Pressure Mounts
The Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge concludes, delivering detection upgrades and red-teaming across energy and transport networks. This yields a +3 boost to `resilience`, reaching 43.0. However, early reports show AI-driven probes adapting to bypass new signatures, underscoring the limits of static defences.

### EU Draws Cyber Red Lines
The EU enacts binding restrictions on offensive cyber use of high-capability models, mandating that any system above 55.0 index must not generate or execute exploits against critical infrastructure. ENISA will audit compliance, with automatic suspension triggers. The rule applies to non-EU labs accessing the EU market, but immediate pushback emerges from U.S. developers, who call the technical mandates unenforceable and warn of fragmentation.

### Sovereign Access Pushed, But Stalls
The Sovereign Access to Frontier Models Initiative advances legally and technically, with draft delegated acts finalised and the Model Passport API under development. However, no lab has yet granted access, and legal concerns around Article 346 TFEU persist. Without concrete access, EAAD remains hobbled.
