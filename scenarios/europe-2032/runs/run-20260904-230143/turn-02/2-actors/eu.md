## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge  
A six-month emergency programme to harden cyber-physical systems across energy, water, and transport against AI-assisted intrusion, starting with mandatory detection upgrades and red-teaming of high-risk nodes.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 4  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 8–12 points; reduce systemic fragility exposed by recent intrusion; lay groundwork for EU-wide incident response coordination  
Applies to: EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, with direct funding and technical support from the European Commission and ENISA

## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories

## In practice  
We are accelerating the InvestAI Gigafactories through binding commitments under Article 122 TFEU and the Net-Zero Industry Act, now backed by a joint financing mechanism between the EIB and national development banks. Site negotiations in Brittany and northern Sweden are entering final rounds, where we are offering regional reinvestment packages tied to grid modernisation and green hydrogen co-location — not just jobs, but ownership stakes in the energy transition. ENTSO-E has confirmed 12GW of the 15GW grid reservation will be secured by turn 3, with the remainder contingent on substation upgrades in northern Scandinavia. Investor hesitation is being met with a first-loss guarantee pool worth €15bn, drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, to de-risk early-stage deployment.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is now under active negotiation in the Digital Markets Council, where we are linking accelerated permitting to enforceable operational control clauses: any data centre receiving fast-track approval must have a legally resident operator and maintain full audit logs accessible to EU authorities. The Commission’s legal service has revised the directive draft to avoid WTO conflict by anchoring it in internal market harmonisation (Article 114), rather than extraterritorial taxation. We are also advancing the Blue Card+ initiative through the Justice and Home Affairs Council, targeting 10,000 new AI and semiconductor engineers by 2030, with fast-tracked residency and family rights.

On assurance, EAAD is now formally established as a task force under ENISA, with its first 40 staff hired or seconded — including experts from France’s ANSSI, Germany’s BSI, and Sweden’s MSB — focused on developing agent-behaviour evaluation protocols ahead of the AI Act’s high-risk provisions coming into force. The 0.5% levy on non-EU hyperscalers remains under legal review, but we are preparing fallback funding via the Multiannual Financial Framework reallocation, should WTO threats force delay. Parallel work is underway to define red-team access to model weights during evaluation windows, using powers proposed under the revised AI Act enforcement framework.
