## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch Sovereign Access to Frontier Models Initiative  
Establish a legal and technical framework enabling EU public authorities and vetted research institutions to access the most capable non-open AI systems for evaluation, monitoring, and crisis response, even when those systems are developed outside the EU.

Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)  
Size: large

Applies to: Non-EU frontier laboratories operating in the EU market, EU member states’ technical agencies, and selected academic consortia

## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories

## In practice  
We are responding to the dual shock of the ai_investment_collapse and emergent_eu_investor_reluctance by moving from guarantees to direct fiscal bridging. The €15bn first-loss pool is no longer sufficient; we are now preparing a €40bn EU-backed bond instrument, issued through the EIB under the temporary crisis clause of the Stability and Growth Pact, to cover up to 70% of early construction costs at the Brittany and northern Sweden sites. This requires emergency Council approval under Article 122 TFEU, which we are securing by linking the Gigafactories to binding green hydrogen production quotas — framing them not as speculative AI infrastructure but as anchor loads for decarbonisation. The Commission’s Energy and Industry DGs are aligning with national grid operators to fast-track substation upgrades in Scandinavia, using emergency powers under the Net-Zero Industry Act to override local objections.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is now in trilogue, where we face resistance from member states fearing WTO retaliation. To hold cohesion, we are offering carve-outs for existing non-EU hyperscalers that commit to full auditability and local legal personality — not a free pass, but a ladder into compliance. Simultaneously, Blue Card+ is being fast-tracked through the Justice and Home Affairs Council, with a new pilot corridor for semiconductor engineers from Japan and Taiwan, using visa pre-clearance hubs in Singapore and Dubai to compress processing to under 30 days.

EAAD continues staffing and protocol development, but the lack of access to frontier models is crippling its credibility. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Access initiative: we will require, as a condition of market access, that any non-EU laboratory deploying models above 50.0 capability in the EU must provide secure, monitored access to a representative instance for ENISA and EAAD-designated researchers. We are drafting this under Article 16 of the AI Act, backed by a new “model passport” system that streamlines compliance for those who grant access. The legal service is assessing whether this triggers Article 346 TFEU (security exceptions), but we are proceeding on the basis that sovereignty in evaluation is as vital as sovereignty in infrastructure. This is not about copying weights — it is about seeing what the world is running on.
