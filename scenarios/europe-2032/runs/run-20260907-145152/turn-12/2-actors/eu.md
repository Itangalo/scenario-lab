## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the Sovereign AI Core Programme (SACP) Operational Readiness Initiative  
Accelerate the deployment of a minimal viable sovereign AI stack—compute, model weights, and control infrastructure—capable of delivering critical public services during a geopolitical or systemic AI disruption.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`

## Priority
**Priority:** Activate the Crisis Solidarity Mechanism for AI Continuity  
We are prioritizing enforcement of continuity because without operational resilience, sovereignty is theoretical and safety is unenforceable—this measure turns the EU’s last credible leverage into functional control, even as political capital hits zero and member states resist.

## In practice
This turn, we are acting under Article 122 TFEU to sustain the Crisis Solidarity Mechanism, releasing the second tranche of €1.1bn in emergency funding to support the 12 rapid-response teams now embedded in critical infrastructure sites. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) is conducting live duress tests on air-gapped nodes in energy and transport systems, with results feeding into a new public dashboard to rebuild trust. In the two holdout states, we are not backing down on cohesion fund conditionality—but we are pairing it with technical assistance teams and a 90-day roadmap to sovereign fallback access, fulfilling our promise not to leave members helpless.

Simultaneously, we are initiating the Sovereign AI Core Programme’s Operational Readiness Initiative. With the Frontier Access Guarantee nearing completion but still dependent on foreign-controlled closed clusters, we can no longer treat access as security. This initiative repurposes existing EU compute allocations—particularly from the EuroHPC JU’s exascale facilities—and binds them to a sovereign model stack under EAAD control. We are fast-tracking procurement for secure data centres in three member states with surplus green energy and strong rule of law, and negotiating binding access terms with two European-developed frontier models currently operating under U.S. jurisdiction. The goal is not parity with the global frontier, but continuity: a sovereign core that can run essential services even if all external AI access vanishes overnight.

We are doing this without new legal authority, using existing instruments creatively but legally—EuroHPC mandates, cohesion conditionality, and energy sovereignty frameworks. The Council’s Competitiveness formation is our main venue, but we are also engaging the ECOFIN Council to reframe sovereign AI as fiscal resilience. Resistance remains high: one member state has threatened to veto further funding, and public protests continue. But we are no longer asking for permission to protect the Union’s survival. The lights must stay on—and now, we are building the generator.
