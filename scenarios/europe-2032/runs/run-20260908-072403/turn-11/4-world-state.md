### The Emergency Bridge and the Unbroken Ground

The EU pressed forward with the Emergency AI Security Response Directive, aiming to close the operational gap exposed by the recent cyberattack. Negotiations under Article 114 TFEU succeeded in bypassing unanimity hurdles, and the directive was formally adopted. However, U.S. providers—operating under Washington’s CONSOLIDATION posture—refused to grant access to their frontier models, citing national security and intellectual property. Only limited cooperation emerged from EU-based subsidiaries using older, less capable systems. The directive’s real-time access mechanism remains untested against true frontier models, reducing its immediate operational value.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme advanced on paper but not on the ground. Site preparations in Finland, France, and Romania faced delays due to unresolved security-clearance bottlenecks and a shortage of engineers with AI infrastructure experience. Intel and ASML maintained their commitments, but without physical deployment, no sovereign compute capacity came online. The emergency powers under the Strategic Infrastructure Act were invoked, yet their impact was symbolic—no construction began.

AI capability crept upward to 69.5, driven by self-improving research loops, while open-weight models stagnated at 53.0. With no safety measures landing on deployed systems and opaque reasoning patterns worsening, ai_safety dropped to 21.0. The cyber incident’s aftermath continued to erode trust, and public sentiment held flat at 41.0 despite the new directive, as visible benefits remained absent.

Resilience inched up to 55.5, reflecting partial gains from the new directive’s coordination framework and air-gapped threat analysis protocols. However, eu_ai_sovereignty fell to 18.0, penalised by rising capability and the absence of any tangible sovereign infrastructure. Political capital declined to 15.0: the portfolio charge of −4 (large measure −3, priority −1) outweighed a modest proposal bonus for the new small category 6 measure, which answered a recent incident but was only partially implemented.
