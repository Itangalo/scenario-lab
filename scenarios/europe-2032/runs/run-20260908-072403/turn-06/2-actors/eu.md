## New measure
**European AI Access Board Operationalisation**  
Stand up the European AI Access Board as a permanent technical authority under ENISA, with binding allocation powers over reserved compute capacity and direct procurement authority for frontier models on behalf of EU institutions and member states.

## Priority
**EU Frontier Access Guarantee** — This remains the priority because the cutoff from frontier AI has crystallised sovereign access not as a long-term ambition but as an immediate operational necessity, and because the U.S. election outcome confirms that foreign providers will act on strategic, not commercial, logic—making institutional capacity to enforce access non-negotiable.

## In practice
We are operationalising the EU Frontier Access Guarantee through a dual-track approach: legal and technical. On the legal front, the Commission is finalising implementing acts under Article 122 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act, conditioning continued market access for U.S. hyperscalers on compliance with 15% compute reservation for public-purpose use. These acts will be presented to the Internal Market Council in March, with trilogue negotiations expected to conclude by June. To secure broader member-state buy-in, we are offering co-financing of up to 70% for national contributions to the federated grid and linking participation to preferential access to the now-finalised Tech Sovereignty Package’s accelerated permitting zones—particularly valuable for Spain and Sweden, whose hesitation we must overcome.

On the technical front, we are launching the European AI Access Board this quarter, hosted within ENISA but with direct reporting lines to the Commission and Eurostat. The Board will begin with interim powers to allocate reserved capacity and will assume full procurement authority by turn 7, once its governance framework is ratified. We are fast-tracking staffing from national agencies and research institutes, with France and Germany already nominating senior technical leads. Simultaneously, we are accelerating procurement under the InvestAI Gigafactories programme, with site selection for the second phase now underway and early power and cooling infrastructure being pre-positioned in Finland and Romania. The Gigafactory in Romania is now fully integrated into the EU compute map, with foreign access vetoed and security protocols aligned with EU standards.

Despite the CJEU challenges to the Mandatory Pre-Release Evaluation Regime, ANSSI, BSI, and NL-CERT continue live evaluations on two incoming models above 60.0 capability, using emergency derogations under Article 16 of the Charter. Contingency protocols are being drafted to allow temporary enforcement via delegated acts if rulings delay implementation. ENISA’s after-action reports from the Loss-of-Control Response Protocol drills are being used to update cross-border mutual aid frameworks, though Poland and Hungary remain outside formal commitments. The Public AI Jobs Guarantee continues under ESF+ and Just Transition Fund disbursements, with 92,000 placements now logged—still below target, but training pipelines are stabilising and regional uptake is improving.
