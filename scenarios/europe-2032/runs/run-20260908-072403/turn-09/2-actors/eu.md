## In practice
We are treating Article 16 of the Charter as the legal foundation for action in the absence of unanimity. With the Emergency Compute Resilience Directive and the EU Frontier Access Guarantee now delivered, we have the framework — but not the teeth. The Taiwan blockade has made it clear: if we cannot secure access to frontier AI outside U.S. discretion, then our sovereignty is conditional and our resilience fragile. We are therefore activating the European AI Access Board as an executive instrument, not just a supervisory one. France and Germany have agreed to align their national procurement channels with the Board’s interim directives starting in Turn 9, and ENISA is fast-tracking a joint audit protocol to assess compliance within six weeks.

The mechanism will target three U.S.-based labs whose models exceed 64.0 capability and rely on European data and users for training feedback loops. They will be required to reserve 15% of their frontier inference capacity for vetted EU public missions — health, energy, defence planning — or face graduated restrictions on data transfers under GDPR, cloud infrastructure access under Cloud Act alignment rules, and participation in Horizon Europe funding streams. Legal challenges are expected, but we are coordinating with sympathetic members of the Court of Justice who recognise that strategic paralysis in the face of systemic risk violates the Union’s duty of care. This is not about protectionism — it is about ensuring that when the next crisis comes, the EU can think for itself.
