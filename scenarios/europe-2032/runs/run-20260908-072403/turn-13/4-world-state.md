### The Talent Gambit

The EU pushes forward with its narrow strategy: converting a surge of researchers fleeing restrictive regimes into sovereign capacity through the new Talent Integration Sprint. Labs in Germany, the Netherlands, and Sweden report a 40% spike in applications from engineers with experience in frontier systems, many from closed U.S. and Chinese labs. Fast-track residencies and streamlined security vetting are rolled out under the Blue Card+ framework, coordinated by EURES and the European Defence Agency. Early placements begin in Q4, though integration bottlenecks—especially in credential recognition and lab capacity—limit immediate impact.

The Emergency AI Security Response Directive formally expires, having delivered only partial utility. Despite air-gapped analysis environments, U.S. providers withheld models above 60.0 capability, rendering the system ineffective against advanced threats. This failure hardens support for the Frontier Access Enforcement Protocol, now being framed as a market-access quid pro quo. Legal teams finalise arguments under Article 114 TFEU, but resistance from several member states persists, fearing retaliation.

A major setback emerges: a key member state unilaterally negotiates direct access to a U.S. hyperscaler’s frontier models, bypassing EU mechanisms and citing national emergency powers. The move fractures the Union’s bargaining position and is condemned as strategic defection. Meanwhile, tighter chip and model export controls—aligned with the U.S. consolidation posture—now ration even allied access, further straining supply.

Yet a counter-current forms. Canada, Japan, and South Korea signal willingness to join a middle-power coordination framework on evaluation standards and export licensing. This nascent coalition bolsters the EU’s diplomatic standing, offering leverage beyond unilateral coercion.

### Sovereignty’s Thin Gain

Despite stalled physical compute, the influx of talent and coalition-building yields a narrow sovereignty gain. Political capital stabilises as the portfolio charge is absorbed, aided by modest sentiment improvement from visible action. Resilience dips slightly as response systems remain overtaxed, while ai_safety sees a small lift from expanded independent evaluation capacity.
