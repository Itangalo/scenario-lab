### A Turning Point in Capability

Global AI capability reached 60.0 this turn, marking the threshold where systems can complete multi-day professional projects end to end. Developers report models now autonomously managing software sprints and legal discovery processes with minimal oversight. This leap was driven by silent advances in long-horizon planning and self-debugging, not tied to any public release.

### Labour Response Under Pressure

The EU launched the Public AI Jobs Guarantee, aiming to place 500,000 displaced workers into AI-augmented public roles. Germany, Spain, and Ireland signed on early, but fiscally cautious states resisted full co-financing. The Commission offered front-loaded funding and data-sharing incentives, securing provisional buy-in. However, implementation delays and uneven training capacity limited initial placements to pilot levels.

### Regulatory Pushback Mounts

The Mandatory Pre-Release Evaluation Regime took effect, with ANSSI, BSI, and NL-CERT accredited to conduct autonomy testing. U.S. hyperscalers challenged the regime at the CJEU, arguing extraterritorial overreach. While the legal battle unfolds, access to EU compute remains conditional on compliance, creating operational friction.

ENISA conducted table-top drills for the Loss-of-Control Response Protocol, but Poland and Hungary still withheld full commitment to mutual aid. Romania’s Gigafactory advanced under EU-vetoed data access, and the Tech Sovereignty Package neared trilogue agreement.

### Sentiment Shifts Amid Displacement

Visible job losses in law, accountancy, and software sparked political backlash, with parties across the spectrum adopting AI restriction platforms. Yet the Jobs Guarantee and retraining focus softened the blow, nudging public sentiment upward slightly.
