## New measure  
**Public AI Jobs Guarantee**  
Launch a pan-European programme to place displaced workers, especially graduates, into AI-augmented public service roles in healthcare, education, and climate resilience, with retraining embedded in the job.  

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by maintaining social absorption capacity, stabilise public sentiment by demonstrating tangible support, and reduce political cost of AI adoption by converting displacement into visible public benefit.  
`Applies to:` Member states, conditional on co-financing and alignment with EU-defined job standards and training curricula.

## Priority  
**Public AI Jobs Guarantee** — This measure is now the priority because the labour displacement this turn is no longer diffuse or anticipatory but concentrated in entry-level cognitive professions, and because the campaign backlash shows public tolerance is turning punitive; we must demonstrate that AI serves people, not just systems, or lose the political capital to govern it at all.

## In practice  
We are launching the Public AI Jobs Guarantee through the European Social Fund Plus and the Just Transition Fund, allocating €35bn over four years to co-finance 500,000 jobs across member states, with a focus on regions most exposed to AI-driven job loss. The programme will place displaced graduates and mid-career workers into AI-supported roles: diagnostic assistants in understaffed clinics, personalised learning aides in schools, and climate adaptation planners in municipalities. Each placement includes a two-year retraining track co-designed with universities and sectoral bodies, funded at €15,000 per participant. We are negotiating with member states through the Employment, Social Policy, Health and Consumer Affairs Council (EPSCO) to adopt common job definitions and funding triggers, with early commitments from Germany, Spain, and Ireland. To counter resistance in fiscally cautious states, we are offering full EU financing for the first 18 months and tying access to the programme to participation in the Labour Observatory’s AI displacement early-warning system.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the Mandatory Pre-Release Evaluation Regime: the European AI Safety Board has accredited the first three evaluation bodies—ANSSI (FR), BSI (DE), and NL-CERT—and issued binding protocols for autonomy testing, effective immediately for all new model releases. U.S. hyperscalers have filed legal challenges at the CJEU, but we are holding firm on access to EU compute as leverage. The Tech Sovereignty Package is entering final trilogue, where we are insisting on data-localisation clauses and EU-controlled inference stacks in exchange for accelerated permitting. Romania’s Gigafactory bid is moving forward under the hybrid trust model, with the Commission securing veto rights over foreign data access. The Loss-of-Control Response Protocol remains on track, with ENISA conducting table-top drills in June ahead of the November cross-border exercise, though Poland and Hungary continue to delay ratification of mutual aid clauses. The Labour Transition Accelerator has now delivered its final outputs and is formally concluded.
