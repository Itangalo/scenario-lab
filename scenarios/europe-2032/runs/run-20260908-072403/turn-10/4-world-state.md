### The Bluff Ends, the Build Begins

The Interim Procurement Enforcement Mechanism expired without securing a single petaflop of frontier compute. U.S. labs ignored its directives, member states fractured, and the Court of Justice declined to intervene. The measure’s failure was sealed not by law but by power: Washington, under its CONSOLIDATION posture, continued rationing access along alliance tiers, and Poland’s separate deal with a U.S. hyperscaler exposed the EU’s inability to enforce collective discipline. The message was clear — coercion without capacity is ritual, not policy.

Simultaneously, a major cyber incident struck, exploiting AI-generated zero-days in a critical software library used across European public services. Emergency response units were blind to the attack’s scope, lacking real-time access to models above 60.0 capability. Recovery took weeks, and the breach compromised health and transport systems in five countries. Resilience absorbed some harm, but the incident underscored systemic fragility.

In response, the EU launched the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme, a €18 billion initiative to build three sovereign AI campuses by 2034. Fast-tracked under emergency powers, it overrides national objections and mobilises decommissioned HPC assets. However, construction has not begun, and physical progress will not start before late 2031 at the earliest due to planning delays, permitting disputes, and workforce shortages. The programme is formally committed, but no infrastructure is yet online, and no measurable sovereign capacity has been added this turn.
