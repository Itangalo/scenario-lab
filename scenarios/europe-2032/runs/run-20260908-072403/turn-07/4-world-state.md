### Steady Advance, Narrowing Window

Global AI capability rose to 64.5 as frontier systems demonstrated autonomous debugging and refinement in multi-week research loops, particularly in materials science and synthetic biology. These silent gains, invisible to public scrutiny, widened the gap between closed and open capabilities. With no major open-weight release and U.S. export controls tightening, openweight_capability advanced only modestly to 49.5—reflecting incremental distributed progress and minor spillovers from academic collaborations, but still trailing significantly behind frontier pace.

The EU’s **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme concluded successfully, with Romania’s facility operational and Finland’s nearing full capacity. This added tangible compute to the Union’s sovereign base, marking a rare delivery. However, private capital mobilisation fell short of the €200bn target, limiting scale. The completion of this high-cost measure contributed +5 to eu_ai_sovereignty and provided a portfolio charge, but broader strategic access remained blocked.

In response to rising vulnerabilities, the **Emergency Compute Resilience Directive** was proposed under fast-track procedures, mandating ENISA-led audits and hardening standards for public AI systems. While legislative adoption began in this turn, implementation has not yet commenced. Initial assessments are underway, but compliance checks and system upgrades will take time. As a result, resilience saw only a partial increase to 48.5, reflecting early momentum rather than full effect.

Meanwhile, the **EU Frontier Access Guarantee** remained stalled in trilogue, despite intensified diplomacy. The U.S. decision to ration allies under case-by-case approvals—rather than grant volume licences—confirmed strategic exclusion. In response, the EU threatened reciprocal compute reservation rules, but Spain and Sweden remained hesitant, demanding concrete guarantees. France and Germany began staffing the European AI Access Board, but without legal authority, its role remained advisory.

Public sentiment held at 42.0, reflecting ambivalence: appreciation for infrastructure gains tempered by anxiety over dependency. The **export_control_escalation** event underscored vulnerability, costing political capital through inaction on access, though the completed InvestAI measure provided a modest offset via the finish bonus.

Despite progress, **ai_safety** dropped to 32.0 as capability advanced without corresponding safeguards. No new safety measures landed on shipped systems, and the gap between frontier AI and human oversight widened. The **eu_ai_sovereignty** rose to 19.0, driven by the completion of InvestAI Gigafactories (+5), ongoing momentum from in-flight contributions (+1), partially offset by capability growth (−1), consistent with the authoritative notepad calculation.
