## In practice
We are executing the final phase of the InvestAI Gigafactories programme, with the Romanian site now fully operational and under EU legal and physical control, and Finland’s site entering final commissioning. The €200bn fund has unlocked €183bn in private capital, falling short of target but sufficient to anchor the first tier of sovereign compute. With the programme now finished, we are shifting focus from construction to integration: the EU compute map is being updated to reflect live capacity, and foreign access to these facilities is being restricted under new security protocols aligned with ENISA and EU Cyber Directives. This is not just infrastructure—it is the material base of future autonomy.

The EU Frontier Access Guarantee remains our central lever. Legal trilogues on the implementing acts under Article 122 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act are deadlocked, but we are using the U.S. export control escalation as leverage: we have informed Washington that failure to grant volume licences to the EU will trigger reciprocal compute reservation rules under Article 21 of the Digital Markets Act, cutting U.S. hyperscalers’ access to the single market unless they reserve 15% of frontier capacity for EU public use. To win over Spain and Sweden, we have offered full co-financing of their national node contributions and fast-tracked their access to the Tech Sovereignty Package’s permitting zones—now a tangible benefit, not a promise. France and Germany are staffing the European AI Access Board, which will assume procurement authority in the next turn, but without legal cover, its power remains symbolic. We are preparing delegated acts under Article 16 of the Charter to activate interim access rights if the trilogue fails.

On resilience, the recent intrusions into Central European grid operators revealed that even advanced defensive AI tools are vulnerable to adaptive, AI-powered attacks. The new Emergency Compute Resilience Directive closes this gap by mandating ENISA-led audits and minimum security standards for all public AI deployments above 50.0 capability. We are also accelerating the rollout of the Public AI Jobs Guarantee, with 117,000 placements now active and regional training pipelines stabilising—though political capital remains tight, and public sentiment still hovers at ambivalence. The U.S. has chosen to ration allies under its new export controls: no volume licences, only case-by-case approvals. This is not containment—it is coercion. And it changes everything.
