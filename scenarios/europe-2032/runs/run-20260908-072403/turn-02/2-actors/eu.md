## New measure  
**Loss-of-Control Response Protocol**  
Establish binding emergency procedures and cross-border escalation thresholds for agentic AI incidents, with mandatory containment drills and public reporting of all events above a defined severity.  

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by ensuring coordinated response to loss-of-control incidents; modestly improve ai_safety by creating feedback loops between incident response and lab oversight.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with enforcement via the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) and the proposed AI Incident Coordination Cell.

## Priority  
**Loss-of-Control Response Protocol** — This measure is now the priority because the loss_of_control_incident this turn exposed a critical gap in crisis readiness: we have no shared definition of what constitutes a consequential breach, no authority to trigger coordinated containment, and no mechanism to learn from near-misses before they cascade. While the Labour Transition Accelerator remains essential for social stability, the immediate threat to institutional integrity and public trust from uncontrolled systems demands that we act now to establish emergency discipline, especially as ai_capability climbs past 54.0.

## In practice  
We are activating the European Commission’s Crisis Coordination Mechanism under Article 14 of the EU Civil Protection Mechanism to fast-track the Loss-of-Control Response Protocol, designating agentic AI incidents as a new category of cross-border crisis. ENISA will lead the drafting of tiered incident thresholds—based on impact to infrastructure, financial systems, or public order—with binding escalation protocols that trigger automatic mutual aid, suspension of affected systems, and public alerts. The first set of drills will be conducted in Q3 2027 with France, Germany, and the Netherlands, funded through the Digital Europe Programme and the Internal Security Fund. Resistance from member states wary of ceding emergency authority will be met with opt-in pilot phases and guarantees of national control over final de-escalation decisions.

Simultaneously, we are pushing the InvestAI Gigafactories into final site confirmation, with Finland and Spain now cleared for construction pending grid upgrades, and Romania entering due diligence for a third node focused on public-sector AI. We are leveraging Article 107(3)(c) state-aid flexibility to fast-track environmental assessments and are negotiating with TenneT and Fingrid to accelerate transmission build-out. The Tech Sovereignty Package is advancing in the Competitiveness Council, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable data-localisation and model-access clauses with hyperscalers—offering preferential procurement status in return for EU-controlled inference stacks. On the Labour Transition Accelerator, disbursement remains slow, but we are securing buy-in from Poland and Italy by linking wage insurance to green infrastructure projects under the Just Transition Fund, thereby pooling resources and demonstrating visible action.
