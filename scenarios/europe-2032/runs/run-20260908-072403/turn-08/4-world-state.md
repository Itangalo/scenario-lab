### Legal Leverage, Real Access?

The EU formally completed one major initiative: the **Emergency Compute Resilience Directive**, which mandated hardening of public systems against model-level attacks. With this measure now delivered, its completion has registered in the metrics, contributing to gains in resilience and a modest boost to political capital. The **EU Frontier Access Guarantee**, initiated earlier, is confirmed as completed this turn, delivering its delayed effects on sovereignty.

Building on this momentum, the Commission announced the proposal of the **European AI Access Board**, an enforcement body intended to operationalize the Access Guarantee in future turns. Citing national security under Article 16 of the Charter, the Commission proposed granting the Board procurement oversight powers over foreign AI providers operating in the single market. France and Germany expressed conditional support, while Spain and Sweden raised concerns about economic repercussions. ENISA has begun drafting audit protocols, but no binding authority has yet been conferred, and formal adoption is expected no earlier than the next turn, pending legal review. The U.S. posture of **CONSOLIDATION** remains unchanged—frontier access is still rationed, and no major provider has engaged with the proposal. No immediate leverage has been established, and the Board does not trigger measurable gains this turn.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Holds

Global AI capability advanced to **66.0**, driven by autonomous research loops in synthetic biology and materials design. These closed-loop systems now require only weekly human review, accelerating progress outside public view. Openweight_capability rose to **51.0**, as academic spillovers and distributed fine-tuning narrowed the gap slightly—though still far from frontier.

No new safety measures have taken effect, but **ai_safety** remains at **32.0**, as no decay term, failed measure, or external event has eroded the existing baseline. Resilience increased to **52.5**, boosted by the finished Directive’s +4 effect and early hardening efforts.

Sovereignty rose to **22.0**: +3 from the completed EU Frontier Access Guarantee (adjusted to reflect category 4 impact realism), +2 from ongoing momentum, −1 from capability growth, and −2 from unresolved implementation risks tied to enforcement gaps. Political capital remained flat at **21.0**: the +3 proposal bonus from the Emergency Compute Resilience Directive (cat 6, small) was offset by the portfolio charge (now reduced to one active measure) and the priority cost; no bonus was applied for the European AI Access Board, as it is a high-cost measure still in early development and not eligible for same-turn rewards. Public sentiment edged up to **44.0**, as assertive framing of completed actions countered narratives of dependency, though skepticism remains about enforceability.
