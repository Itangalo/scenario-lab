## In practice
We are treating the completion of the EU Frontier Access Guarantee not as an endpoint, but as a threshold: the law is passed, but its enforcement was never automatic. With Spain and Sweden still withholding full support for reciprocal compute reservation rules, we cannot yet trigger the Digital Markets Act lever at full force. Instead, we are moving through emergency empowerment. On Monday, the Commission invoked Article 16 of the Charter — the clause on personal and public security — to justify granting the European AI Access Board interim procurement authority. This is not a permanent solution, but it is actionable now. France and Germany have committed their national procurement channels to align with the Board’s directives starting next month, and ENISA is preparing a joint audit protocol to assess compliance of foreign providers within six weeks.

The target is clear: any provider operating large-scale inference services in the EU must now either reserve 15% of their frontier capacity for EU public use or face graduated restrictions on data flows, cloud access, and market participation. We are not waiting for unanimity. The Board will begin issuing directives in Turn 9, starting with three labs whose models exceed 64.0 capability and rely on European data and users for training feedback loops. Legal challenges are expected, especially from U.S. firms, but we are coordinating with sympathetic members of the European Court of Justice who recognise that strategic paralysis is itself a threat to Union integrity. This is not just about access — it is about proving that the EU can act when its future is on the line.
