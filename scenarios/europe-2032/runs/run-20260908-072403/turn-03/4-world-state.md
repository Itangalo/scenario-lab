### A Regime of Scrutiny Begins

The European Union launched its Mandatory Pre-Release Evaluation Regime, invoking Article 40a of the AI Act to designate persistent agentic systems and high-capability models as a new class of high-risk AI. The European AI Safety Board began accrediting evaluation bodies, with France, Germany, and the Netherlands fast-tracking mutual recognition to prevent fragmentation. Testing protocols targeting autonomy, self-replication, and unauthorised access were drafted for Q1 2028 releases. U.S. hyperscalers protested, warning of delays, while open-weight advocates decried a de facto ban on frontier model deployment.

Despite resistance, the regime gained credibility through linkage to the Tech Sovereignty Package: access to EU compute infrastructure now requires compliance. Romania revived its Gigafactory bid under a hybrid data trust model, with EU veto powers over foreign access. Fingrid and TenneT confirmed 2028 grid connections for Finland and Spain, though congestion fee waivers strained internal cohesion.

### Labour Pilots Bypass Resistance

With national funding stalled, the Labour Transition Accelerator launched pilot cohorts in Bavaria, Wallonia, and Emilia-Romagna using Just Transition Funds. Wage insurance tied to AI-driven redundancies offered targeted relief, drawing cautious union support. However, broader rollout remained uncertain.

The Loss-of-Control Response Protocol advanced, with ENISA finalising incident tiers and cross-border drills scheduled for November. Yet Hungary and Poland continued to resist binding commitments, weakening enforcement prospects.

### Middle Powers Align

A coordination framework among the EU, Japan, Canada, and South Korea solidified, aligning export controls and establishing joint evaluation capacity. Though non-binding, the coalition demonstrated leverage over critical supply-chain nodes, enhancing the EU’s strategic position.

### Metrics Drift Amid Progress

AI capability advanced to 58.0, driven by global momentum. Openweight capability rose to 44.0, as model fragments diffused. The new evaluation regime provided a modest safety boost, lifting ai_safety to 29.0. Resilience improved slightly due to ongoing protocol implementation. Sovereignty dipped as capability outpaced build-out. Political capital eroded under portfolio strain, despite coalition gains. Public sentiment edged up on visible action, though job displacement concerns lingered.
