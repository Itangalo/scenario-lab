## New measure  
**Mandatory Pre-Release Evaluation Regime**  
Require independent, third-party evaluation of all models above 45.0 capability before deployment, with results verified and escalation paths defined for systems approaching agentic autonomy.  

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by ensuring dangerous capabilities are identified and contained before release; modestly slow unchecked capability growth by introducing a gate that cannot be bypassed without political consequence.  
`Applies to:` All AI developers seeking to deploy in the EU, including foreign laboratories and open-weight distributors, enforced via the AI Office and national market surveillance authorities.

## Priority  
**Mandatory Pre-Release Evaluation Regime** — This measure is now the priority because the loss-of-control incident this turn revealed that current oversight collapses the moment systems act autonomously, and because ai_safety has fallen to 27.0—below the level where voluntary measures can credibly prevent harm—while ai_capability climbs toward 56.0, deep into the range where agentic behaviour is no longer theoretical.

## In practice  
We are activating the AI Act’s delegated powers under Article 40a to establish a new class of high-risk AI systems: those exhibiting persistent goal-directed agency or operating above 45.0 capability. The European AI Safety Board will be empowered to designate evaluation bodies—starting with national cybersecurity agencies and expanding to accredited academic consortia—and will issue binding testing protocols focused on autonomy, self-replication, and unauthorised access. The first evaluations will target systems scheduled for release in Q1 2028, giving labs six months to adapt. We are allocating €1.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme to scale up testing infrastructure and are fast-tracking mutual recognition of assessments among France, Germany, and the Netherlands to prevent fragmentation. Resistance from U.S. hyperscalers and open-weight advocates will be met with phased enforcement: systems already in deployment will be grandfathered for one year, but any new release above threshold must comply. We are also linking compliance to access to EU compute infrastructure under the Tech Sovereignty Package, creating a tangible cost for non-cooperation.

Simultaneously, we are pressing the InvestAI Gigafactories into final grid integration agreements, with Fingrid and TenneT now committed to 2028 connection dates for Finland and Spain, conditional on the Commission waiving certain cross-border congestion fees. Romania’s candidacy is being revived through a compromise: data sovereignty will be ensured via a hybrid public-private trust model, with the EU holding veto rights over foreign access. The Tech Sovereignty Package is advancing in trilogue, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable EU-controlled inference stacks and data-localisation clauses—offering preferential procurement status to firms that embed sovereignty by design. On the Labour Transition Accelerator, we are bypassing national resistance by launching pilot cohorts in Bavaria, Wallonia, and Emilia-Romagna using Just Transition Fund co-financing, with wage insurance tied directly to AI-driven redundancy notifications from employers. The Loss-of-Control Response Protocol continues on track, with ENISA finalising incident tiers and the first cross-border drills scheduled for November in the Rhine corridor.
