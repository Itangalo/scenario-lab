## New measure
**Labour Transition Accelerator**  
Establish a rapid-response retraining and wage-insurance programme for sectors showing early signs of AI-driven displacement.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by softening labour shocks, modestly improve public sentiment by showing responsiveness to job losses.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with conditional funding tied to national implementation.

## Priority
**Labour Transition Accelerator** — This measure is the priority because the emergent labour displacement wave threatens to harden public sentiment against AI adoption within a year, undermining both political capital and the legitimacy of broader sovereignty efforts, even as cyber and capability risks mount.

## In practice
We are launching the Labour Transition Accelerator through the European Social Fund+ and the newly established AI Adjustment Facility, co-financing national programmes in Germany, France, and Poland that pair wage insurance with modular retraining in public services, green infrastructure, and AI-augmented healthcare roles. The legal base is Article 162 TFEU, and we are fast-tracking implementation via delegated acts to bypass full Council consensus, though we are offering member states flexibility in design to secure buy-in. Resistance from fiscally conservative members is being met with capped co-investment ratios and performance-based disbursement, ensuring accountability while maintaining speed.

At the same time, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by finalising site selection in Finland, Spain, and Romania, leveraging state-aid flexibility under Article 107(3)(c) to accelerate permitting and grid integration. The Commission is negotiating with ASML and Intel Europa to co-locate chip testing and maintenance hubs at two of the sites, deepening the industrial linkage. The Tech sovereignty package is being pushed through the Council’s Competitiveness formation, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable data-localisation clauses with hyperscalers — a delicate balance, as the US protests any de facto extraterritorial control.

We are not acting directly on eval anomalies or openweight risks this turn because our instruments are slow and our capital thin; we are instead preparing a proposal for third-party evaluation access under category 5, to be launched next turn once we have secured baseline capacity and social stability. We cannot afford to lose legitimacy now.
