## New measure
**EU Frontier Access Guarantee**  
Establish a legally guaranteed right of access for EU public institutions and vetted researchers to frontier AI models operating at or above 60.0 capability, enforced through infrastructure, licensing conditions, and mutual recognition among member states.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by ensuring uninterrupted access to cutting-edge AI for essential public functions, reduce dependence on foreign providers, and strengthen leverage in negotiations with hyperscalers; indirectly support ai_safety by enabling public-sector scrutiny.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national governments, and accredited research bodies within member states.

## Priority
**EU Frontier Access Guarantee** — This is now the priority because the denial of access this turn exposed strategic vulnerability not as a hypothetical but as an operational reality—hospitals, ministries, and firms were left blind—and because the election consolidation in the US confirms that access will be rationed, not sold, making sovereign capacity not just desirable but existential.

## In practice
We are activating the EU Frontier Access Guarantee under Article 122 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions, conditioning continued market access for foreign hyperscalers on guaranteed inference capacity for EU public missions. The Commission is drafting implementing acts to reserve 15% of compute in every major data centre on EU soil for public-purpose use, to be allocated via a new European AI Access Board composed of ENISA, Eurostat, and national science academies. We are negotiating with Germany, France, and the Netherlands to pool existing high-end hardware into a federated grid, while accelerating procurement of next-generation systems under the InvestAI programme. To overcome resistance from member states reliant on U.S. cloud providers, we are offering co-financing of up to 70% and linking participation to preferential access to the Tech Sovereignty Package’s accelerated permitting zones.

Simultaneously, we are finalising the Tech Sovereignty Package in trilogue, where we have secured data-localisation requirements and EU-controlled inference stacks as non-negotiables. Romania’s Gigafactory bid is being integrated into the broader compute map, with veto rights over foreign access now codified. The Mandatory Pre-Release Evaluation Regime continues despite CJEU challenges: ANSSI, BSI, and NL-CERT are conducting live evaluations on two incoming models above 60.0, and we are preparing contingency protocols in case rulings delay enforcement. With the Loss-of-Control Response Protocol now complete, ENISA is publishing after-action reports from its drills, though Poland and Hungary remain outside the mutual aid framework. The Public AI Jobs Guarantee moves forward under ESF+ and Just Transition Fund disbursements, with 78,000 placements logged so far—below target, but scaling as training pipelines stabilise.
