### A New Threshold, a Sudden Cut

Global AI capability advanced to 61.5, driven by silent progress in long-horizon reasoning and self-correction. Though no single release marked the shift, developers confirmed systems now manage multi-week software and legal workflows with only periodic human review. Yet this leap was shadowed by a sudden cutoff: EU public hospitals, research institutes, and government agencies found themselves locked out of the leading frontier model overnight, with no explanation. The disruption was immediate—cancer diagnostics, regulatory analysis, and infrastructure planning stalled.

In response, the EU invoked emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU, proposing the EU Frontier Access Guarantee as its new priority. The Commission put forward a plan to reserve 15% of compute in major data centres for public use and to pool national high-end hardware into a federated grid. Germany, France, and the Netherlands expressed preliminary support, but Spain and Sweden raised concerns over retaliation from U.S. cloud providers, and formal commitments await further negotiation. Romania’s Gigafactory was formally integrated into the EU compute map, with foreign access vetoed.

The Loss-of-Control Response Protocol concluded, with ENISA publishing after-action reports that highlighted coordination gaps, especially with Central European states. The Tech Sovereignty Package neared final approval, with data-localisation and EU-controlled inference stacks secured. The Mandatory Pre-Release Evaluation Regime continued despite legal challenges, with live evaluations underway.

A major interpretability breakthrough improved model transparency, and new defensive AI tools closed critical cyber gaps. Open-weight models advanced modestly following a public release of improved tooling and training data, reaching broader deployment and better performance, though still trailing the frontier. Preliminary labour data revealed accelerating displacement in administrative and technical roles, stoking quiet unrest.

### The American Choice

The U.S. elected a president who treated advanced AI as a strategic asset, not a commercial product. Access would now be rationed by national interest, confirming European fears of dependence. The posture of the incoming administration will shape the next phase of transatlantic AI relations.
