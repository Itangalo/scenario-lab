### The backstop lands
The emergency guarantee for hospitals and grid operators took full effect in the autumn. Administrators who had faced uninsured closure in spring kept emergency departments open on isolated, re-tested systems, with essential workloads failed over to EU-hosted interim capacity and vetted open models. ENISA field teams cleared queues where they arrived; where they did not, paper workarounds and postponed maintenance continued.

Insurers acknowledged the avoided shutdowns but did not return. The cover remains repurposed money for eighteen months, with slow re-testing and no new funding behind it.

### A coalition, not capacity
With Washington giving no signal on restoring access and new domestic compute sites still idle in hearings and protests, Brussels turned outward. Foreign and competitiveness ministers mandated joint export-licence alignment, pooled procurement bargaining and a shared screening cell for biological risks run with health agencies, offering partners guaranteed failover hosting and joint certification in return for compute access.

The framework was signed quickly — middle powers holding supply-chain chokepoints saw value in bargaining together — but officials privately concede it secures bargaining power, not machines. No new chips or hosting arrived this turn; the separate-deal capital again kept its own foreign contract while studying the offer.

### A darker signal
Late in the turn, a contested genome-design study circulated among biosecurity specialists, suggesting non-experts could reach a viable human-infecting design with model help. Method and publication were fiercely disputed and stayed inside expert circles, but public-health officials asked for detection capacity the Union does not yet have. Evening news paired relief that clinics stayed open with unease that the tools they run on remain borrowed and unrecallable.
