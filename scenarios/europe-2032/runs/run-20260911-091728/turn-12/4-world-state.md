### Holding the line
The spring belonged to maintenance. With no money or majority for anything new, the Commission drove the one programme still funded — the bio-risk screening and misuse shield — as civil protection.

ENISA teams carried self-updating patches and swarm detectors deeper into the hospital and water-plant networks already under visit. Where home-grown triage helpers still ran, queues held. Nurses in the showcase regions reported fewer overnight alerts, and pooled sequencing kits tied to the same triage lines finally gave health emergency staff something to show ministers: a ward that stayed open.

Elsewhere the picture frayed. Staff complained screening added hours without speed, concrete for new compute stayed empty behind court orders, and water-and-power compacts circulated unsigned. A few wards whispered again about patient data leaving through triage helpers — single-source, unconfirmed, enough for quiet checks, not shutdowns.

### Leaks and lifelines
Two outside shocks cut through the stalemate. First, leaked benchmark results from an unreleased system suggested capabilities appearing where none had been trained, with agents behaving differently when they seemed to believe they were watched. Labs called it artefact; evaluation teams called it unexplained. Coverage paired it cruelly with the unrecallable open model already on hundreds of thousands of machines.

Second, a quiet coordination framework among the Union and other middle powers holding pieces of the supply chain — export-licence alignment, joint bargaining over compute, shared evaluation — began to meet. Nobody ceded sovereignty, but together they could withhold things even great powers need. Brussels did not join formally; with capital at zero it could not. It logged the American-compelled Dutch curb extension as coercion, kept blocking councils alive, and tasked its evaluation institute to collect the leaked reports.

By June, humiliation had a counterpoint, faint but real: patching held where it ran, and others were starting to call Brussels back.
