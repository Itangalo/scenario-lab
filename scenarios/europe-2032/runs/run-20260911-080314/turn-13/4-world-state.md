### The download and the drought
The second half of 2032 gave Europe two shocks at once. A new open release, effectively at the frontier, was mirrored on university servers and home machines before regulators had finished reading the model card. Hospital filters clogged again within days, and pooled telemetry lit up with phishing and probing generated at a quality that made attribution almost pointless.

At the same time money left the sector. Valuations collapsed in the United States, two announced data-centre expansions were cancelled, and European orders that had been queued in Washington quietly evaporated with them. The pause helped no one in Brussels: supercomputing expansion stayed frozen, and chip deliveries remained a diplomatic request rather than a shipment.

### Holding the line by hand
With no headroom for a new programme, the Union held to manual fallback. Health emergency teams and the EU cyber agencies kept extra shifts, clean-image kits and faster bio-detection in hospitals running on reprogrammed recovery funds. It kept councils and utilities degraded, not down — but municipal crews in two regions again refused extended hand-running, restoration slipped, and hiring for detection teams lagged months behind need.

A contested genome-model paper claiming a viable human-infecting design circulated among biosecurity specialists, adding urgency without yet reaching the public. Detection money was stretched to cover it.

### Work and cures
Two real gains landed. The entry-work guarantee and wage-insurance pilots closed into bridge funding through the Social Fund despite finance-ministry resistance, putting several thousand displaced graduates and service workers into paid transitional work.

And tailored cancer and rare-disease therapies continued to dispense on time through EU-controlled channels. Clinics reported remissions where none had existed; newspapers ran recovery stories beside outage stories. A separate research advance — a new materials catalyst first found by AI-assisted search in a German-Dutch lab — promised cheaper electrolysis, though factory effects remained two years out.

Gratitude was real, trust was not restored. The benefits-scoring scandal, ruled lawful, remained the lens for much coverage.
