## New measure
**Open-Weight Containment Protocol**  
Establish an emergency evaluation and containment regime for open-weight models that approach or exceed 50.0 in capability, restricting their diffusion within the EU unless they meet baseline safety and security thresholds.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` Halt the uncontrolled spread of open models above 50.0 capability by creating a legal mechanism to delay or condition distribution within the EU, reducing the rate at which `openweight_capability` outpaces `ai_safety`. Aim to stabilise the open frontier at 55.0 or below until sovereign safeguards are operational.  
`Applies to:` Open-weight model publishers, hosting platforms, and AI infrastructure providers operating in or serving the EU market.

## In practice
This turn, we confront a new reality: the frontier is no longer just ahead — it is loose. The openweight_frontier_release event means that systems approaching 60.0 capability are now downloadable on consumer hardware, with no audit, no control, and no recall. We cannot stop every upload, but we can make the EU a jurisdiction that refuses to serve as an open runway. The Open-Weight Containment Protocol will be drafted under Article 16 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU, leveraging emergency powers for internal market regulation. We will task ENISA with rapid evaluation protocols for open models above 50.0, working with the emergent_sovereign_consortium to develop lightweight interpretability benchmarks. Hosting platforms and cloud providers will be required to verify compliance before offering inference services — not a ban, but a circuit breaker. This is not ideal; it risks fragmentation and backlash, but with ai_safety at 5.0, inaction is surrender.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Agent Foundation Stack enters its final phase: DG CONNECT finalises procurement with three consortia — one led by Atos, one by a German-French academic-industrial team, and one by the Nordic Digital Union — to deploy auditable agents on EU-controlled compute by turn 11. We are integrating hardened versions of the best open components from the emergent_sovereign_consortium, but only after air-gapped testing at the EU Cybersecurity Competence Centre. The political challenge is cohesion: Poland and Hungary have raised concerns about fiscal conditionality, and we are offering extended cohesion funding windows tied to compute node contributions, not cash. This is not about building a better GPT — it is about ensuring that when the next black-sky incident hits, the EU still has a command channel that works. Emergency Resilience Surge: Phase II continues its rollout, with the first cross-border cyber drill scheduled for June, linking ENISA, national CERTs, and energy regulators in a live-fire simulation of a coordinated grid attack. We are acting on two tracks: containment to slow the spread of risk, and foundation-building to ensure we can act when it matters — because sovereignty without safety is just faster collapse.
