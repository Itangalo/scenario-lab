## In practice  
This turn, we confront the fact that the frontier is not just loose — it is weaponised, and we are behind. The Open-Weight Containment Protocol has failed to clear legal hurdles; Poland and Hungary have invoked Article 114 TFEU to challenge its proportionality, and cloud providers have filed preliminary injunctions. We cannot enforce upload controls at scale in time. So we pivot: instead of trying to stop distribution, we focus on making it legible. The Joint Research Centre, in coordination with ENISA and the emergent_sovereign_consortium, will stand up a rapid evaluation cell by turn 12 to assess leaked models for autonomy, exploit generation, and alignment failure modes. Results will be published in a public AI Risk Dashboard, updated weekly, with colour-coded alerts akin to pandemic threat levels. This is not regulation — it is information warfare in defence of public sense-making.  

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Agent Foundation Stack has delivered: three auditable agent nodes are now operational — in Lyon, Warsaw, and Helsinki — running on EU-controlled compute with hardened monitoring. They are not general-purpose AI, but narrow tools for verifying medical diagnostics, detecting cyber anomalies, and coordinating infrastructure response. Their real value is command continuity: when the next black-sky incident hits, we will have a channel that does not depend on foreign providers.  

The Emergency Resilience Surge continues: the first cross-border cyber drill revealed critical gaps in energy sector response, particularly in automated grid reconfiguration. We are now mandating that all TSOs integrate AI-assisted incident playbooks by turn 13, with funding from the Digital Europe Programme. The `cyber_major_incident` — a ransomware sweep across municipal services in Belgium and northern Italy — exposed how fast automated attacks can cascade when detection lags. We are accelerating deployment of AI-powered intrusion detection systems in public networks, but only those with human-in-the-loop verification to avoid runaway responses.  

Public sentiment remains fragile. The `backlash_physical` event looms: data centre occupations have begun in rural Ireland and Sweden. We are responding not with force, but with transparency — launching a public AI Impact Monitoring Portal to track job displacement, fraud trends, and system failures in real time. This is not appeasement; it is an attempt to restore the EU’s credibility as a steward, not just a regulator. We are acting on the principle that when you cannot control the fire, you must at least map the smoke.
