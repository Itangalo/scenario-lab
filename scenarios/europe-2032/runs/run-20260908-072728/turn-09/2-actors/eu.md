## In practice
This turn, we close the Frontier Access Restoration Initiative — a diplomatic effort that did not yield access, but which clarified a truth we can no longer ignore: the EU’s strategic vulnerability is structural, not situational. With that chapter closed, we double down on what we *can* control. The Sovereign Agent Foundation Stack moves from proposal to procurement: DG CONNECT activates emergency clauses in the Digital Europe Programme to fast-track tenders for secure agent deployment across three pilot domains — real-time cyber threat correlation at ENISA, validation of AI-generated medical results via the EMA, and autonomous monitoring of energy grid integrity during outages. We unlock €3.8bn in unused RRF digital funds, conditional on member states contributing both compute capacity and cleared personnel; Germany and France have already committed nodes from their national supercomputing reserves, but we are negotiating with Eastern members to ease fiscal concerns through extended cohesion funding windows. Crucially, we are not building a rival to the frontier — we are building a floor beneath collapse.

Simultaneously, the emergent_sovereign_consortium gives us unexpected leverage: a coalition of European firms and institutes, self-organising around on-shore data and compute, proves that demand for autonomy exists beyond Brussels. We are not leading it, but we can channel it — and we will, by integrating its most viable components into the Foundation Stack under a common security framework. This is not charity; it is force multiplication. The Taiwan tension rise reminds us that black-sky scenarios are no longer hypothetical, and with capability_plateau_evidence still unresolved, we cannot assume the frontier will keep lifting us. We act on two tracks: one technical, one political — because without member-state cohesion, even the best stack is just code in a vault.
