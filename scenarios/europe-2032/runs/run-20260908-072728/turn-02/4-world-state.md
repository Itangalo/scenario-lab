### ASML Standoff Escalates  
The EU announces the **ASML Sovereignty Shield**, a proposed large category 4 measure, declaring advanced lithography a strategic asset under Article 21 of the FDI screening regulation. A new European Semiconductor Export Control Authority (ESECA) is proposed, with an estimated cost of €6.5bn in public funding. However, no funding mechanism is identified this turn, and the measure remains in early development. The initiative is framed as defending EU autonomy against extraterritorial pressure, aligning with ASML’s own resistance to U.S. export tightening.

However, the announcement triggers immediate transatlantic friction. Washington signals strong opposition, warning of "unintended consequences" for allied tech cooperation. Legal challenges loom, and member-state unity wavers—some fear retaliation on investment flows. The proposal lacks a financing plan, and ESECA’s creation would require not only legislative approval but also multi-year staffing and integration into existing export control frameworks. The complexity of establishing such a body, especially in a high-stakes geopolitical context, means operational readiness is years away, even if approved. The measure does not advance to implementation this turn and remains in the proposal phase.
