## Priority  
**ASML Sovereignty Shield** — Because the sudden denial of frontier access has exposed our dependence on permissions we cannot enforce, and because control over lithography is the only leverage we have that the frontier cannot route around, this measure is now the hinge on which both sovereignty and resilience turn.

## In practice  
This turn, we finalise the legal drafting of the ASML Sovereignty Shield under Article 21 of the FDI screening regulation, with DG GROW and the Legal Service presenting a draft implementing act to the College by October. We simultaneously open trilogue discussions with the Council and Parliament, focusing on securing qualified majority support in ECOFIN by December. To counter resistance from member states fearing U.S. retaliation, we offer enhanced access to IPCEI ME/CT co-funding for semiconductor infrastructure and pre-emptively activate consultations under the Anti-Coercion Instrument’s early-warning mechanism. The €6.5bn equity stake will be drawn proportionally from the European Defence Fund (40%) and IPCEI (60%), with disbursement conditional on ASML committing to host all high-NA R&D in EU jurisdictions and routing export decisions through ESECA.

The Emergency Resilience Surge completes its deployment phase: we certify 78% of designated critical nodes in energy, water and health as having AI-intrusion detection and response protocols in place, with full integration into ENISA’s threat-sharing network. While delays in municipal-level rollouts persist, the baseline hardening lifts resilience to 44.0 and establishes a template for future surges. We now shift focus to sustaining the capability, embedding it into the NIS3 framework.

We also respond to eu_frontier_access_denied by launching the Secure Frontier Access Agreement under the High Representative’s coordination. This is not a procurement exercise but a diplomatic one: we approach labs in jurisdictions with strong rule-of-law traditions and existing data-sharing frameworks, offering binding commitments on red-teaming transparency, weight security, and misuse monitoring in exchange for guaranteed access for EU health, defence, and civil protection agencies. The goal is not parity with the frontier but predictability — and the signal that the EU is no longer a passive consumer but a condition-setting partner. This measure will require trust, and we begin building it through the informal lab cooperation network, now institutionalised as a preparatory track for formal negotiations in turn 4.
