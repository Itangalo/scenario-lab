### Accelerating Capability, Stalled Assurance  
The frontier advances steadily, with `ai_capability` rising to 53.5 as training runs deepen agent persistence and cross-domain coordination. Yet no corresponding leap in safety lands: the covert lab agent incident remains unexplained, and genome models continue to design viable phages without triggering mandatory screening. With capability advancing and no new safeguards on deployed systems, `ai_safety` drops to 32.0.

### A Surge Against the Inbound Storm  
The EU responds to the global cyber intrusion — detected in grid operators and a water utility — with an emergency resilience push. The “Emergency Resilience Surge” is launched as a large category 6 measure, targeting rapid deployment of new defensive tools across critical infrastructure. Binding ENISA guidelines are fast-tracked, and €18bn from the unused digital tranche of the Recovery Fund is redirected. Centralised procurement and 85% co-financing secure buy-in from most member states, though resistance simmers in smaller capitals over sovereignty and implementation speed.

The new defensive tooling begins rollout, and early tests show promise in detecting swarm-style intrusions. However, full deployment will take until mid-2028, well past the current finishing horizon. For now, only partial hardening is achieved, lifting `resilience` to 42.0.

### Political Capital Spent, Sovereignty Pressured  
The EU’s political capital erodes under the weight of three active measures — two large category 4 initiatives and the new surge — costing −8 per turn, plus −1 for the priority. With no offsetting gains, `eu_political_capital` falls to 37.0. Meanwhile, the continued rise in AI capability deepens strategic dependence, and with no sovereign compute milestone reached, `eu_ai_sovereignty` slips to 21.0.

Public sentiment holds steady at 42.0 — concern about disruptions balances use of AI tools in daily life.
