## New measure  
**Emergency Resilience Surge**  
Launch immediate hardening of critical infrastructure against AI-enabled cyber intrusion, using newly available defensive tooling.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 3`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience from 39.0 to at least 55.0 within 12 months; reduce probability of cascading failure in energy, water and port systems.  
`Applies to:` EU member states with critical infrastructure exposure, coordinated through ENISA and the European Programme for Critical Infrastructure Protection.

## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — Because the cyber_test_shot exposed systemic vulnerability in essential services and the cyber_defence_breakthrough offers a rare window to shift the offence-defence balance, this measure must move faster than the industrial build-out, even though it costs political capital we cannot easily spare.

## In practice  
We are treating the autumn intrusion as a near-miss of strategic consequence. Though no blackouts or contamination occurred, the depth and stealth of access — in transmission grids, a water utility, and a major port — reveal that existing detection regimes are blind to AI-coordinated swarm intrusions. The fact that the breach was found by accident, not design, is unacceptable. We are now acting on the principle that resilience is not a technical add-on but a core condition of sovereignty.

Under Article 12 of the Treaty on European Union and the updated EPICI framework, we are convening an extraordinary Council meeting in December to activate emergency provisions for critical infrastructure protection. ENISA will issue binding guidelines by February 2027 requiring all designated operators to deploy the new swarm-detection and automated patching tools within six months. Funding will come from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused digital tranche (€18bn), redirected via Commission decision under Article 22 of the MFF Regulation. National resistance from smaller member states over cost and sovereignty concerns is expected; we are offering 85% co-financing and centralised procurement to overcome it.

This surge does not replace our industrial strategy — the Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain essential — but it must come first. If we cannot keep the lights on, no amount of compute will matter. We are also laying the groundwork for a broader resilience directive in 2027, but we cannot wait for legislation when the threat is already inside.
