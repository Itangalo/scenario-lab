## New measure
**Secure Frontier Access Agreement**  
Negotiate binding access terms to the most capable AI systems for EU public institutions, backed by mutual security assurances and data sovereignty guarantees.

`Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Stabilise eu_ai_sovereignty by securing predictable access to frontier AI for essential public functions; prevent recurrence of access denial shocks and begin building mutual dependency with allied labs. Aims for +5 to eu_ai_sovereignty through guaranteed access pathways and joint governance mechanisms.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, European Commission, leading AI laboratories (by nationality and hosting jurisdiction), and strategic partners (UK, Japan, South Korea).

## Priority
**Secure Frontier Access Agreement** — Because the sudden denial of frontier access has exposed our systemic vulnerability, and because no amount of domestic compute will close the capability gap in time, we must secure reliable access now: not as a favour, but as a condition of our strategic partnership and shared security.

## In practice
This turn, we finalise the political authorisation for the **Secure Frontier Access Agreement**, with the College of Commissioners mandating the High Representative to open formal negotiations under Article 21 of the Treaty on European Union. We activate a task force within the EEAS, co-located with DG CONNECT and ENISA, to draft the core terms: guaranteed access for EU health, civil protection, and defence agencies in exchange for binding commitments from partner labs on red-teaming transparency, weight security, and misuse monitoring. We leverage the emergent_lab_cooperation network as a trust-building track, institutionalising it into a joint technical working group with observer status from Japan’s AIST and the UK’s AI Office. The agreement will be structured as a mutual recognition framework, allowing EU-certified labs reciprocal access to allied public datasets and testbeds, turning access into a two-way street.

With the **ASML Sovereignty Shield** now complete, we publish the implementing act under the FDI screening regulation, establishing ESECA as the sole authority for reviewing export-related pressure on ASML. The €6.5bn equity stake is disbursed: 40% from the European Defence Fund, 60% from IPCEI ME/CT, with conditions tied to ASML’s commitment to host all high-NA EU R&D within Union jurisdiction and route export decisions through ESECA. We confirm that consultations under the Anti-Coercion Instrument’s early-warning mechanism have been activated with the U.S., not as escalation but as deterrence: the message is clear — economic coercion on strategic technology will trigger a response.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech sovereignty package** remain on track. We finalise the first two site selections under InvestAI, in France and Poland, with grid connection timelines accelerated through NIS3 emergency provisions. Under the Tech sovereignty package, we launch the first cohort of accelerated-permitting zones, streamlining approvals for data centres that commit to 70% EU-based operations and open-weight security audits. Despite low public sentiment, we push forward — not because the politics are easy, but because the window for strategic autonomy is closing.
