## New measure

**Frontier Access Restoration Initiative**  
Launch an emergency programme to re-establish EU institutional access to frontier AI systems through a mix of sovereign fallbacks, coalition bargaining, and conditional partnerships.

`Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Restore `eu_ai_sovereignty` by +5 through binding access agreements with non-US frontier developers (e.g., UK, Japan, Singapore), leveraging semiconductor supply chain position; initiate EU-run fallback agents for critical public functions if access remains denied.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member states, and selected third-country developers, coordinated via the European AI Office and EDA-style joint procurement mechanisms.

## Priority

Frontier Access Restoration Initiative — Because eu_frontier_access_denied has already occurred in practice through the investment collapse, and because the cyber_major_incident reveals that the Union cannot afford even temporary gaps in high-assurance AI support for critical infrastructure, restoring access is now a precondition of both sovereignty and resilience, not a policy option.

## In practice

This turn, we treat frontier access as a matter of critical infrastructure continuity. With the InvestAI Gigafactories and Secure Frontier Access Agreement now complete — though their effects undercut by external shocks — we pivot to damage containment and reconnection. The Frontier Access Restoration Initiative activates under Article 21 of the Treaty on European Union (solidarity clause) and the newly invoked Strategic Autonomy Protocol in the Digital Markets Act review. We dispatch a high-level envoy, co-chaired by the VP for Values and Transparency and the High Representative, to open emergency talks with AIST (Japan), the UK AI Office, and select Singaporean developers, offering mutual recognition of safety evaluations and preferential access to EU semiconductor equipment in exchange for guaranteed API-level access to models above 55.0 capability. Parallel to this, we initiate a fallback track: DG CONNECT and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking begin scoping a minimal sovereign agent stack — not to match the frontier, but to run essential verification, cyber response, and medical diagnostics under EU-controlled infrastructure. We unlock €4.2bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused digital tranche, conditioned on member states joining the coalition approach and refraining from unilateral deals. The European Parliament’s Industry Committee resists, citing sovereignty concerns, but we offer them co-drafting rights on the fallback agent specifications in exchange for support. Without this move, the EU will be locked out of the systems it needs to respond to the very threats it is trying to govern — and that is not autonomy, it is surrender.
