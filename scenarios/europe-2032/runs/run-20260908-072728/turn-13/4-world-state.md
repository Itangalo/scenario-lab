### The Weight of Blindness

Global AI capability crept upward to 68.5, driven by self-improving agents in closed labs, but with no corresponding leap in safety, the gap yawned wider. Open-weight models, now at 56.0, spread further into cyber and disinformation tools, running on consumer hardware with no audit trail. The EU’s Secure-by-Design Gateway Mandate advanced through trilogue, but exemptions for research and testing weakened its scope. Pilot integrations began, yet major providers delayed full compliance, citing liability and fragmentation.

### A Turn Toward Seeing

Recognising that control without visibility was illusion, the EU launched the Sovereign Sensor Restoration Initiative — a €1.2 billion effort to rebuild interpretability and telemetry into high-capability AI. The programme aimed to equip ENISA and national agencies with real-time tools to trace agent reasoning, even in self-modifying systems. Mandated telemetry from gateway-enforced deployments would feed a sovereign data lake, enabling forensic analysis after incidents. Red teams in Lyon and Warsaw began designing evasion challenges to stress-test the system.

Yet the political cost was steep. The new large measure added −3 to the portfolio, joining the ongoing Secure-by-Design Mandate at −3. With the initiative named priority, another −1 was charged. No completed measure delivered a rebound, and sovereignty declined as AI capability rose without matching EU capacity gains. Public sentiment remained frozen at 22.0, as strikes in public services intensified, framing AI not as a shield but as a threat to livelihoods.

### Fractures in the Front

Resistance hardened. Open-source advocates decried mandatory telemetry as surveillance, and U.S. hyperscalers warned of interoperability collapse. The Commission pressed on, arguing that without internal observability, regulation was theatre — but the price of action was a plunge in political capital, now at 8.0, teetering on the edge of operational paralysis.
