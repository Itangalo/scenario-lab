## In practice
This turn, we finalise the political mandate for the **Secure Frontier Access Agreement** with a vote in the Foreign Affairs Council, authorising the High Representative to open formal negotiations under Article 21 TEU. The EEAS task force, co-located with DG CONNECT and ENISA, delivers the draft framework: access for EU health, civil protection, and defence agencies will be guaranteed in exchange for binding red-teaming transparency, weight security audits, and misuse monitoring commitments from partner labs. We institutionalise the emergent_lab_cooperation network into a standing technical working group with observer status from AIST and the UK AI Office, aiming to build trust through shared protocols. But Washington’s quiet freeze on coordination casts a long shadow — no U.S.-based lab will move without clearance from DC, and our bilateral overtures are met with silence.

With the **ASML Sovereignty Shield** complete, we publish the implementing act under the FDI screening regulation, formally empowering ESECA to review export-related pressure on ASML. The €6.5bn equity stake is disbursed — 40% from the European Defence Fund, 60% from IPCEI ME/CT — with binding conditions: all high-NA EU R&D must be hosted within Union jurisdiction, and export decisions routed through ESECA. This is not just industrial policy — it is jurisdictional assertion. Simultaneously, we activate consultations under the Anti-Coercion Instrument’s early-warning mechanism with the U.S., not as escalation but as deterrence: economic coercion on strategic technology triggers consequences.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech sovereignty package** remain on track. We confirm the first two site selections in France and Poland, leveraging NIS3 emergency provisions to fast-track grid connections. Under the Tech sovereignty package, the first cohort of accelerated-permitting zones launches, streamlining approvals for data centres committing to 70% EU-based operations and open-weight security audits. Despite falling public sentiment and thin political capital, we press forward — not because the path is clear, but because the cost of delay is irreversible strategic erosion.
