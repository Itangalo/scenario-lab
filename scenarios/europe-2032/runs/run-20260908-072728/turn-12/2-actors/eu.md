**Secure-by-Design Gateway Mandate**  
Require all open-weight models above 50.0 capability deployed in the EU to run through a hardened, auditable gateway layer that enforces minimal safety constraints, logging, and human-readable intent summaries, effectively creating a non-bypassable inspection point for autonomous systems.

## In practice  
This turn, we abandon the illusion that information alone can govern an uncontrollable frontier. The Open-Weight Rapid Evaluation system was designed for a world where users trusted institutions more than algorithms — but that world is gone. Instead, we move to structural intervention: using Article 114 TFEU, we propose a new regulation mandating that any model above 50.0 capability operating in the EU must pass through a certified gateway stack — a thin, open-source enforcement layer that ensures every action includes a logged intent summary, a chain-of-custody tag, and a kill-switch interface accessible to designated authorities during declared incidents. This is not a ban, not a licence, but a technical condition of operation — like emissions standards for engines. The gateways will be developed by ENISA in partnership with the emergent_sovereign_consortium and deployed via national digital agencies, with compliance enforced through market access rules.

We are met with fierce resistance from open-source communities and cloud providers, who frame this as censorship and a barrier to innovation. To counter this, we are offering a fast-track certification path for open implementations, co-developed with trusted maintainers, and committing €800 million from the Digital Europe Programme to subsidise integration for SMEs and research institutions. Crucially, the mandate does not apply to training — only deployment — preserving scientific freedom while closing the gap between capability and accountability.

Public sentiment remains fragile, but we are betting that visible action — not warnings — will restore some credibility. The Joint Research Centre is already stress-testing prototype gateways against known exploit chains, and early results suggest they can detect 90% of autonomous escalation patterns without degrading legitimate use. If this works, it will be the first time we’ve imposed a physical constraint on logical proliferation. Not perfect. But present. And enforceable.
