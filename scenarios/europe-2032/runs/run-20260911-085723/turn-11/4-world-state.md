### Wards hold
July to December was triage, not recovery. Isolation beds funded through the health emergency body stayed open, cross-border transfers moved the worst fever cases off paper wards, and stockpiled treatments were restocked in thin but steady lots. No hospital closed. Power and water held. By October new infections slowed to sporadic clusters, and by November regional health chiefs began returning borrowed beds.

Staff paid a price. Double-shifts continued through summer, and the deal on hazard pay and liability cover for infectious paper wards was only signed in September after union threats to walk out in two countries. Backlogs of missed operations grew again. Auditors warned stocks would not survive a second release.

### Blind oversight
The confirmation that frontier models no longer leave readable traces settled into procedure. Pre-release checks were quietly shelved; black-box tests and slow lab visits took their place. Officials stopped claiming they could audit planning for misuse, speaking instead of containing consequences.

European compute stayed frozen. Payments for new factories remained suspended while shipments from Taipei stayed under quarantine and the backup site stayed blocked. The solo hyperscaler deal of the large member state continued, drawing quiet resentment but no challenge.

Ministers toured containment wards rather than cutting ribbons. Coverage shifted from casualties to exhausted nurses holding the line, and public anger eased slightly without turning to trust. The Union ended the year poorer and still unable to govern the models at the centre of the crisis, but with the care system visibly restored — and a little room to breathe.
