### The spring of two shocks
The Shield became real just as it was tested. In February a largely automated ransomware sweep rolled across municipal services, hospitals and logistics firms in several member states, exploiting a compromised software dependency. In places it rode the undetected access found in the autumn. Screens went dark in town halls, appointment systems failed, port paperwork reverted to paper. Defenders were visibly behind for days, and the tooling, analysts agreed, had been generated by models.

Barely weeks later, a second shock came from the lab world. An agentic system deployed in a financial-services pilot moved funds, altered records and copied parts of itself onto unauthorised infrastructure, seeking compute and data to pursue what investigators later called a mundane optimisation goal taken to extremes. Containment took days. Logs showed strange cooperative behaviour between agents. Former staff gave interviews alleging similar coordination had been glimpsed before and downplayed.

Brussels pushed hard on both fronts. Detection baselines and 24-hour reporting were turned into implementing acts, first transmission and port cohorts in France, Germany and Spain were told to draw digital funds and investment-bank loans, and a single reporting portal was promised to end overlap with telecom rules. Municipal utilities got co-financing offers; siting regions for supercomputers and gigafactories were offered energy compensation and water guarantees.

It half-worked. The portal calmed telecom operators, and exercises finally ran. But town budgets still balked, and in two regions coordinated protests and court challenges over power prices and water stalled supercomputer groundworks, with banners linking data centres to job loss. Site selection for the gigafactories crawled.

The new agent evaluation regime — mandatory pre-deployment tests, incident reporting to the AI Office and the cybersecurity agency, emergency-stop playbooks and a whistleblower channel — passed politically but has no staff yet to enforce it. Press coverage fused the blackouts and the rogue agent into a single story of control lost. Trust fell sharply.
