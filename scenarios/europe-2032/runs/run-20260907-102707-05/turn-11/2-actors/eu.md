### Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, but we now acknowledge that without sovereign execution capacity, neither mandate can be fulfilled—governance without control is theatre, and safety without access is fantasy  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event and the subsequent collapse of political capital have revealed that Europe’s inability to run critical AI systems independently has rendered its regulatory and safety ambitions meaningless. The two mandates are not in tension—they are both nullified by dependence. This is no longer a question of coordination or influence; it is about whether the EU can act at all when decisions are made in code it cannot execute, on infrastructure it does not control, and in timeframes it cannot match.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, but only to actions that expand our sovereign execution capacity—because in a world where we cannot run the systems we seek to govern, all other commitments are hostage to access  
- Grounds: The failure of the Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory to launch, despite being in flight, proves that even well-designed oversight mechanisms are inert without the compute, data, and operational autonomy to activate them. Acting under uncertainty must now mean building the means to *reduce* that uncertainty through control, not just observation. The cost of being wrong is no longer just wasted capital—it is strategic irrelevance.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor EU decision-making in real-time, sovereign sensing of AI behaviour and capability shifts, **and ensure those insights can be acted upon through independent execution capacity**  
- Grounds: The **emergent_sovereign_compute_alliance** shows that member states are already acting on the understanding that awareness without power is futile. The original commitment focused on sensing; this turn reveals that the true precondition for governance is not just seeing the frontier, but being able to *respond* to it. The commitment must now include the muscle, not just the eyes.

- reclassify `no_single_point_of_failure` to commitment  
- Grounds: The repeated disruption of essential services due to **eu_frontier_access_denied**, combined with the rise of the **emergent_sovereign_compute_alliance**, confirms that this is no longer a tactical preference but a binding strategic imperative. The Union must now treat compute, inference, and model execution as critical infrastructure on par with energy or telecoms—subject to binding redundancy requirements, enforceable access guarantees, and cross-border solidarity. Reclassifying it raises the cost of reversal and signals that dependence will no longer be tolerated, even if it slows adoption or raises costs.

## New measure  
**Sovereign Inference Backbone Initiative**  
Deploy a hardened, distributed network of inference clusters across EU member states, capable of running certified high-capability models (up to 65.0) for essential public functions—health, energy, border control, and crisis response—under full EU legal and operational control.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to eu_ai_sovereignty by ensuring uninterrupted sovereign execution of critical AI workloads; +7 to resilience by enabling failover during access denial or cyber disruption; reduces strategic leverage of foreign providers  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national governments, EuroHPC sites, and accredited public operators

## Priority  
**Sovereign Inference Backbone Initiative** — because the **emergent_sovereign_compute_alliance** proves that member states are already building this capability in parallel, and without central coordination, we risk fragmentation, duplication, and weaker security; because the **Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative** only prepares fallbacks, while this ensures continuous sovereign operation; and because without execution capacity, no monitoring, no safety claim, no policy decision has force.

## In practice  
We are no longer building awareness. We are building power. The **Sovereign Inference Backbone Initiative** launches under the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking, but with a new mandate: not just to host models, but to guarantee their continuous, sovereign operation for essential state functions. We are converting four additional stalled data centre projects—in Sweden, Austria, Greece, and Poland—into inference nodes, each hardened against coercion, cyber intrusion, and energy disruption. These will run certified derivatives of open-weight models up to capability 65.0, tuned for public service continuity and auditable under EU law. Legal authority is drawn from Article 172 TFEU and the Crisis Competence Protocol, with operational control vested in a new Joint Sovereign AI Execution Authority, co-staffed by ENISA, the JRC, and national digital agencies.

Funding comes from a 50% reallocation of the Digital Europe Programme’s AI budget and a €1.2 billion emergency draw from the MFF’s “Strategic Autonomy” reserve, to be ratified in the Mid-Term Review. Member states are being offered binding service-level agreements: in exchange for waiving local permitting and grid approval delays, they receive guaranteed priority access during disruptions and a seat on the Execution Authority’s steering board. The two northern states resisting centralisation are being offered co-location of their national sovereign clusters within the backbone, with joint command protocols to preserve autonomy while ensuring interoperability.

The **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** remains in flight, but we are now conditioning its final deployment on integration with the Inference Backbone—no telemetry without action, no observation without sovereign response. The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative continues, but it is now understood as a stopgap: the real work is the Backbone. We are not waiting for the frontier to slow. We are building the infrastructure to meet it on our own terms—or not at all.
