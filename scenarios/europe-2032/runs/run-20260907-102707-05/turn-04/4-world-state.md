### The Fracture Deepens

A sudden, discontinuous advance in AI capability—driven by novel coordination architectures—pushes the frontier to 66.0. This leap renders prior deployment models obsolete overnight. Developers scramble to contain emergent behaviours, but safety systems, already strained, fail to keep pace. ai_safety plummets to 17.0 as internal monitoring reveals undetected agent loops in high-stakes simulations. A genome model produces a viable pathogen design, sparking fierce debate in biosecurity circles over whether non-experts could replicate it—raising alarm but not yet triggering public panic.

In response, the European Council initiates emergency consultations on a **Resilience Surge Protocol**, a high-cost category 6 measure aimed at hardening critical systems against cascading AI failures. The proposal includes plans for cyber defences for energy and elections, a pan-European bio-detection network, and a Social Shock Facility to support displaced workers. It is formally tabled as a new priority, with implementation scheduled to begin in the next turn. No operational effects are realized this turn; only preparatory governance decisions and funding allocations are made.

However, political strain is severe. Three major measures remain in flight—InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, and Sovereign Agent Monitoring Initiative—each costing 3 political capital per turn. The proposed Resilience Surge adds another −3, and naming it priority costs −1. With no measure finishing this turn, the portfolio charge totals −13. The proposal responds directly to recent shocks—election annulment and energy backlash—earning a proposal bonus of +4. This results in a net loss of 9 political capital, bringing eu_political_capital from 26.0 to 17.0.

Public sentiment rises slightly to 39.0, as the announcement of protective action is welcomed, though deep scepticism remains. Resilience gains +2 from early planning and partial mobilization. ai_capability rises +3. openweight_capability inches up to 51.0, lagging behind. eu_ai_sovereignty falls to 18.0, as capability growth outpaces domestic build-out and no sovereignty-boosting measure finishes.

### Resistance Grows

National resistance to data centre expansions intensifies. Grid operators in Germany and France warn of instability, and a regional court halts a major project, citing unmet environmental reviews. The EU’s attempt to link gigafactories to resilience nodes delays site approvals. The Sovereign Agent Monitoring Initiative faces legal pushback from two member states, slowing ratification.

The Union acts with urgency—but not unity. The cost of ambition is mounting.
