## In practice
We are acting on the conviction that the EU cannot govern what it cannot see — and right now, we are blind. The Real-Time AI Safety Observatory is our first real window into the behaviour of frontier systems as they operate, not after they fail. We are standing it up under Article 114 TFEU, treating systemic AI risk as a single market integrity issue, and building the technical stack in partnership with ENISA and EuroHPC JU. The observatory will require all operators of systems above 50,000 GPU-equivalent to stream cryptographically signed telemetry — decision logs, resource acquisition attempts, inter-system communication — into a secure, air-gapped environment. Independent red teams will gain quarterly access to probe for undetected autonomy or goal drift. To manage resistance, we are offering non-EU hyperscalers a path through trusted third-party validators for data routing, and we are exempting SMEs below the threshold. Funding of €2.1bn is drawn from repurposed Connecting Europe Facility and Horizon Europe safety grants, and we are fast-tracking the hiring of 300 new technical staff by turn 14, prioritising experts in interpretability and agent behaviour.

At the same time, the collapse of ai_investment and the rise of physical backlash have shifted the terrain. The market correction means some of the compute build-out we were counting on has evaporated — but it also means frontier labs are scaling back, buying us a narrow window to build sovereign capacity before the cycle turns again. That is why we are launching the Crisis-Response AI Capacity Reserves: not just more compute, but compute that is legally anchored, physically isolated, and pre-configured to run safety-critical workloads during incidents. This is not about competing with the frontier in peacetime — it is about ensuring that when the next agent goes dark, we have the means to act without asking permission. The reserves will be hosted across three hardened EuroHPC sites, with failover protocols and emergency activation authority vested in the European AI Safety Agency. We are negotiating with member states to fast-track grid access and security clearances, and we are offering private operators liability protection in exchange for integration into the crisis-response framework. This is the beginning of operational autonomy — not just oversight, but the ability to act.
