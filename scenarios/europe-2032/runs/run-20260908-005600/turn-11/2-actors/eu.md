The legal basis remains contested, but we are holding firm on Article 114 TFEU as enabling harmonisation of internal market rules for a critical strategic resource. Behind closed doors, we are offering two new concessions: a phased rollout — Tier 1 access protocols activate only when eu_ai_sovereignty falls below 40.0 or a crisis is declared — and expanded co-governance slots for member states on the Oversight Board, including veto rights on crisis declarations affecting national champions. At the same time, we are advancing the AI Incident Reporting Regime through comitology, bypassing Parliament where possible to save political capital. The European AI Safety Agency will lead, using powers delegated under the AI Act’s Article 64a, expanded via delegated act. We’re requiring encrypted, standardised logs from all large operators, with automated anomaly detection fed into a central observatory. Whistleblower protections are being strengthened through cooperation with the EU Fundamental Rights Agency, and we’re building trust by publishing quarterly redacted summaries — not to satisfy transparency alone, but to prove that we are learning from near-misses before they become catastrophes. This turn, we act on two fronts: one to lock in access, the other to see the shadows forming before they swallow us whole.
