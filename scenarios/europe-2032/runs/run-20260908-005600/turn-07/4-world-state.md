### The Blockade and the Shield

The sudden blockade around Taiwan throws global AI development into chaos. With advanced semiconductor shipments frozen, compute access becomes a zero-sum strategic contest. The EU, home to critical upstream suppliers like ASML and STMicroelectronics, invokes emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU to begin establishing the **European Semiconductor Shield Initiative**. A coordination cell is proposed within DG GROW-DEFIS to manage export licensing for dual-use tools, redirect EU-bound equipment flows, and build a strategic wafer stockpile. State aid waivers are fast-tracked to support retooling at key fabs, but full implementation will require interinstitutional approval and member state coordination. The move signals intent—but no formal proposal is adopted this turn, as political debate continues across capitals. Preparatory work advances, but the measure remains in design and consultation phase.

Despite the crisis footing, the EU completes two major initiatives: the **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme delivers its first exaflop-scale site in Toulouse, and the **AI Safety Evaluation Directorate (ISED)** formally launches. Yet ISED’s authority remains contested—several member states delay compliance, and a major hyperscaler’s legal challenge looms. Meanwhile, the **Critical Services Resilience Surge** progresses: 14 nations submit zero-trust plans, and ENISA runs the first AI-generated red-team exercise.

The EU advances its existing portfolio—InvestAI Gigafactories, Stand up EU Sovereign AI Development Programme, Launch ISED, and Critical Services Resilience Surge—while continuing preparatory work on the Semiconductor Shield. No new measure is formally launched this turn. The perceived urgency boosts coordination signals, contributing to a slight rise in political capital.
