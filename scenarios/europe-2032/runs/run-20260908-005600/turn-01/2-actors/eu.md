## In practice  
We are acting on the intrusion findings from the autumn by invoking Article 13 of the NIS2 Directive to fast-track binding cybersecurity standards for electricity transmission, water, and port operators across the Union. The ENISA rapid response unit is being expanded with €4.2bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused allocations, co-financed by a temporary 0.1% levy on hyperscaler data centre revenues in the EU — a compromise with member states resisting new taxes. This funds automated threat-hunting AI, mandatory air-gapped backups, and quarterly red-teaming exercises starting Q1 2027. Resistance from Germany and Sweden over sovereignty concerns is being met with bilateral assurances on data jurisdiction and a shared operations centre hosted in Estonia.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by finalising site selections in Finland, Spain, and Poland, leveraging the European Investment Bank’s new AI Infrastructure Facility to de-risk private equity tranches. The bottleneck remains grid integration: we are fast-tracking substation upgrades under the TEN-E regulation, overriding national delays through Commission-led project-of-Union-interest designation. The Tech sovereignty package is being renegotiated in the Council’s Digital Agenda formation to include binding national targets for private data centre investment, with penalties redirected into the InvestAI fund. ASML’s position is being defended through a joint Dutch-French legal taskforce preparing a WTO challenge to U.S. extraterritorial servicing restrictions, backed by a €6bn innovation shield to accelerate next-gen lithography R&D within EU jurisdiction.
