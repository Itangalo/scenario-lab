### Acceleration and Anxiety
The global AI frontier advances, with capability rising to 53.5 as leading labs compress release cycles further. Despite underwhelming public results from one major model — a signal some interpret as a plateau — investment and iteration continue apace. Open-weight capability climbs to 41.0, as Kimi K3’s offensive cyber tools circulate widely and no new open release closes the gap.

### The EU Responds to Intrusion
A coordinated cyber intrusion across critical infrastructure in multiple continents triggers alarm. Though detected by chance, the depth of access reveals AI-enabled persistence that evaded automated defences. The EU launches the Emergency Resilience Surge, invoking NIS2 powers to impose binding cyber standards on energy, water, and port operators. A €4.2bn fund, drawn from unused recovery funds and a new levy on hyperscaler revenues, backs AI-driven threat hunting and red-teaming. Estonia will host a central operations hub, easing German and Swedish concerns over data control.

### Sovereignty in Slow Motion
The InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package advance slowly. Site selections are finalised in Finland, Spain, and Poland, but grid integration delays persist. The Council resists binding private investment targets, weakening the Tech Sovereignty Package’s leverage. ASML faces intensified U.S. pressure to restrict servicing of EUV machines abroad. A Dutch-French legal team prepares a WTO challenge, while a €6bn R&D shield is proposed to reduce dependency.

### Political Costs Mount
The new large measure and its priority status strain political capital. The portfolio now carries three measures, two large, and the priority adds further cost. Member-state resistance and slow delivery limit immediate gains, while the cyber incident and ongoing access uncertainty yield no boost to sovereignty.
