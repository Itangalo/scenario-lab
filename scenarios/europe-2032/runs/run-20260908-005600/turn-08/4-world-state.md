### The Denial and the Directive

The abrupt cutoff from the leading AI model sends shockwaves through EU institutions. Hospitals, border agencies and research consortia lose critical support overnight. With no explanation, the dependence on foreign frontier systems becomes a vulnerability laid bare. In response, the European Union activates emergency powers, announcing the **EU Emergency Compute Access Framework**—a binding directive requiring all large-scale compute operators in the bloc to report capacity and prioritise public and sovereign workloads. The move is framed not as expropriation, but as crisis governance: compute is now treated as strategic infrastructure. Implementation will take time, and legal challenges are expected, but the political signal is clear.

### Capability Creeps, Control Crumbles

Global AI capability advances to 62.5, driven by closed-lab agents optimizing training efficiency amid scarce chips. Yet openness stalls—no major release occurs, and export controls tighten further under the **export_control_escalation**, limiting even allied access. Open-weight capability remains at 48.5. With opaque reasoning widespread and no safety upgrades in deployed systems, concerns grow, but no major incident occurs. **Ai_safety** declines moderately to 17.0, reflecting erosion in oversight capacity and growing uncertainty, but not a systemic failure.

### Resilience Rises, Sentiment Sours

The completion of the **Critical Services Resilience Surge** strengthens cyber defences across health and emergency systems, contributing +3 to **resilience**, now at 58.0. However, public backlash grows: media spotlight job disruptions and the humiliation of dependency. The sudden loss of AI services in hospitals and migration offices fuels narratives of European weakness. **Public sentiment** falls to 48.0.

### Sovereignty Gains, Capital Stalls

The **Sovereign AI Development Programme** was expected to complete this turn, but final integration of domestic compute clusters and validation of foundational training runs face delays due to supply bottlenecks and legal uncertainty around emergency access. The programme delivers partial progress: early coordination under the crisis enables a +1 boost to **eu_ai_sovereignty** through improved access to upstream resources via the **European Semiconductor Shield Initiative**, and the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event yields +1 as emergency measures secure limited sovereign compute allocation. However, the full effect of the Sovereign AI Development Programme does not materialise. The +1.0 rise in global AI capability reduces relative standing by −1. These factors result in a net change of +1, bringing **eu_ai_sovereignty** to 23.0.

No boost to **eu_political_capital** occurs. While the emergency action has legitimacy, the failure to deliver the flagship programme on time, combined with falling public sentiment and high implementation costs, prevents any gain. **Eu_political_capital** remains at 14.0.
