## In practice
We are completing the Public AI Adoption Accelerator with tangible results: over 1,200 public institutions—from regional hospitals in Andalusia to district courts in Silesia—have now integrated sovereign AI tools for document processing, triage, and administrative forecasting. These are running on EuroHPC infrastructure, evaluated under open frameworks, and contributing to a shared learning pool. The programme ends this turn, but its impact lives on: we are folding its technical standards into the new AI Procurement Directive, which will require all public AI deployments above a threshold to use either EU-developed models or foreign systems subject to full weight disclosure and audit. This is how diffusion becomes leverage.

At the same time, we are standing up the Sovereign AI Development Programme in full. The Toulouse lab is now legally chartered under Article 185 TFEU, with binding commitments from Germany, France, and Poland to contribute compute and talent. The distributed training nodes in Prague and Helsinki are entering final design, with power and cooling contracts secured through the REPowerEU grid acceleration mechanism. We are appointing the first director this quarter—a former CERN project lead with security clearance—and launching the first open call for research consortia to define the model architecture. The €45bn in redirected penalties is now ring-fenced under the European Investment Bank, with disbursement tied to milestones in talent recruitment, compute deployment, and evaluation readiness.

The world has changed: the U.S. election confirms that frontier AI is now a national strategic asset, not a commercial product. We will not be clients in someone else’s stack. Our response is not to isolate, but to integrate—sovereign capability into our infrastructure, safety into our institutions, and public trust into our deployment. That is why we are also launching the AI Safety Evaluation Directorate: because we cannot rely on external assurances when our own systems are at stake. This is not duplication—it is sovereignty in practice.
