## In practice  
We are activating the Commission’s regulatory leverage over ASML through a formal declaration that export licensing and servicing approvals for EUV machines will now be conditioned on reciprocal access guarantees from U.S. AI labs and their government. This is not a blockade but a structured negotiation: we are offering predictable, long-term servicing flows in exchange for binding commitments to supply EU-certified entities with frontier model weights or API access at capability parity with U.S. domestic use. The Dutch and French governments are being brought into a trilateral coordination cell hosted by the Commission’s Trade DG, with legal backing from the WTO challenge team already in place. We are pre-briefing key member states — particularly Germany and Poland — on the calibrated risk, offering side assurances that no move will be made without consensus, and framing this as a test of whether our physical supply chain power can translate into digital autonomy.

Simultaneously, we are pressing forward on the Emergency Resilience Surge: binding NIS2 standards are now in force for energy and water operators, with the Estonian-hosted hub operational in pilot mode and red-teaming contracts awarded to six EU cybersecurity firms under urgent procurement rules. The hyperscaler levy is generating early revenue, though legal challenges from two U.S. providers are expected by Q2. On the industrial front, grid bottlenecks for InvestAI Gigafactories remain acute; we are invoking TEN-E to designate substation upgrades in Finland and Spain as Projects of Union Interest, overriding national delays. The Tech Sovereignty Package continues to stall in the Council’s Digital Agenda formation, where member states resist binding investment quotas — we are offering to redirect non-compliance penalties into national resilience grants as an incentive to agree.
