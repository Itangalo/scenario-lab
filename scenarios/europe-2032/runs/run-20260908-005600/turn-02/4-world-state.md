### Accelerated Frontier, Lagging Safeguards  
Global AI capability advances to 55.0 as leading labs achieve faster training cycles and improved agent coordination. Public demonstrations remain underwhelming, but internal benchmarks confirm broader reasoning coherence and improved cross-domain transfer. No major safety evaluations were completed before release, and interpretability remains shallow. The gap between capability and safety widens, increasing latent risk.

### Open Weights Catch a Step Behind  
With no new open release, openweight capability rises modestly to 43.0, extrapolating from prior models and fine-tuned variants. Kimi K3-derived tools continue to proliferate in cyber operations, now adapted for supply-chain infiltration. The closed frontier pulls ahead, but distributed actors are closing the application gap in high-impact narrow domains.

### Resilience Gains Ground  
The Emergency Resilience Surge takes effect: binding cybersecurity standards are enforced across energy and water sectors, and the Estonian threat-hunting hub enters limited operation. Early red-teaming uncovers critical vulnerabilities in port logistics systems, triggering emergency patches. These proactive measures yield a resilience boost, absorbing some of the pressure from rising AI threats.

### Sovereignty Play Meets Resistance  
The EU initiates a high-stakes diplomatic move, leveraging its regulatory control over ASML to demand guaranteed access to frontier AI models from U.S. developers. The Commission frames it as a reciprocal arrangement—predictable servicing for assured access—but the proposal fractures member-state unity. Germany and Poland express concern over economic retaliation, while the U.S. signals that model access is non-negotiable without intelligence-sharing concessions. No breakthrough occurs, and the initiative stalls in coordination talks. The symbolic assertion of leverage yields no immediate gain in sovereignty.

### Political Capital Erodes  
Three large measures remain in flight, including the newly prioritized ASML leverage effort. The portfolio cost mounts, and the lack of visible progress undermines cohesion. The hyperscaler levy faces legal challenges, and grid delays persist for gigafactories. Political capital declines under sustained strain, with no offsetting success to restore momentum.
