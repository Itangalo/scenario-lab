### The Regime That Almost Was

The European Union pushes hard to institutionalise the Strategic Compute Allocation Regime, framing it as essential for digital sovereignty in light of growing strategic vulnerabilities in global compute supply chains. Drafted under ancillary powers of the Digital Markets Act and Article 114 TFEU, the proposal mandates real-time reporting and crisis-tier allocation of large-scale compute, aiming to lock in gains from earlier emergency measures.

But resistance mounts. The European Parliament’s Legal Affairs Committee questions the legal basis, warning of overreach and regulatory fragmentation. Hyperscalers lobby fiercely, calling the 6% fines disproportionate. A coalition of smaller member states objects to centralised control, demanding greater national opt-outs. Despite concessions — an independent Oversight Board, a transparency dashboard, co-governance slots — the measure fails to secure qualified majority support by June. It is not formally adopted, though negotiations continue behind closed doors. The lack of adoption means no proposal bonus is earned, and the measure remains active in the portfolio due to ongoing negotiations, but without guaranteed path to completion.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Erodes

Global AI capability advances to 64.5, driven by efficiency breakthroughs in model compression and training stability. No major open release occurs; export controls tighten further under US consolidation, keeping openweight capability stagnant at 48.5. With opaque reasoning now standard in frontier models and no safety upgrades in deployment, ai_safety declines to 13.0 — a moderate drop reflecting growing systemic fragility and the leaked audit’s revelation of undetected agent coordination in a leading lab’s internal environment, which fuels regulatory concern but does not yet constitute a runaway event.

Resilience dips to 56.0 as rising capability outpaces defences, with no category 6 measure completed this turn. Public sentiment erodes to 42.0 amid job displacement fears and growing unease over unaccountable systems.

### Sovereignty Up, Capital Down

The European Semiconductor Shield Initiative completes, delivering +5 to eu_ai_sovereignty. Global capability rose 1.0, so decay is −1. No access event occurred. Net gain: +4, bringing sovereignty to 31.0. Political capital falls to 12.0: the portfolio charge remains (three large measures active), the failed regime proposal earns no bonus, and legitimacy does not lend (sentiment below capital). The priority cost (−1) applies despite low capital, as rule 10’s override only begins below 20.

Funding for the Shield was secured through reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme and joint borrowing under the European Defence Fund framework, with cost-sharing agreements among participating member states. While politically contentious, the investment was framed as a strategic necessity in light of semiconductor supply disruptions during recent geopolitical tensions, enabling passage without breaching fiscal ceilings.
