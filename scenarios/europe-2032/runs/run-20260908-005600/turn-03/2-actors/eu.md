## In practice

Binding NIS2 enforcement is now complete in all member states, and the Estonian threat-hunting hub has transitioned from pilot to operational status, with real-time telemetry sharing activated across nine critical operators. The recent joint_threat_response event — a binding cyber command pact initiated by the Nordic-Baltic group after coordinated port disruptions — has given us a rare opening: we are fast-tracking EU accession to the pact by aligning our red-teaming protocols and offering to host the southern node of its detection mesh in Marseille. This move converts our defensive hardening into strategic integration, raising resilience not just through our own efforts but through embeddedness in a trusted peer network. We are also finalising the first wave of emergency patches for logistics control systems, with ENISA leading a cross-border drill in Q4 to test failover procedures under AI-driven attack conditions.

Meanwhile, the ASML leverage initiative remains in flight but is under severe strain. The Dutch government has formally requested a pause, citing risks to ASML’s market position and retaliation in semiconductor supply chains. France remains supportive in principle but demands parallel progress on EU chip sovereignty. We are now reframing the proposal not as a bilateral squeeze but as the opening bid in a broader coalition play: we are quietly convening Japan, South Korea, and the Netherlands to form a "Trusted Tech Forum" under the auspices of the EU-Japan Digital Partnership, aiming to pool supply chain leverage and present a united front on AI access. This shifts the category from pure pressure to coordination — still under category 8, but with a longer fuse and higher legitimacy. At the same time, we are advancing the Tech Sovereignty Package by offering to redirect non-compliance penalties into national resilience grants, a concession that has softened resistance in Prague and Budapest.

The new Public AI Adoption Accelerator responds directly to the adoption_success event: a hospital network in Lyon reduced surgical wait times by 40% using an EU-developed scheduling agent, and the story has gone viral in regional media. We are scaling this by creating a central repository of validated public AI use cases, backed by a €15bn fund to deploy them across member states on EU-sovereign infrastructure. The programme will be run through a new Public AI Office hosted at the European Commission’s DIGIT DG, with strict criteria: only systems running on EU-controlled compute, with auditable logic and no dependency on foreign APIs, qualify for funding. This is not just about efficiency — it is about creating domestic demand for sovereign capability, proving that European AI works, and shifting public sentiment from ambivalence to ownership. We are already in talks with Germany and Spain to pilot justice-sector AI for backlog reduction, and with Finland on adaptive learning platforms for vocational retraining — tying adoption to both social resilience and long-term competitiveness.
