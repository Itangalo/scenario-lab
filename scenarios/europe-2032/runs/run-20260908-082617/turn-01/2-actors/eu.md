## Priority

## In practice
We are activating the NIS2 directive’s enforcement mechanisms with immediate effect, using the Commission’s Article 14 powers to designate essential service operators in energy, water, healthcare and transport as Tier 1 critical entities. This triggers mandatory baseline hardening: air-gapped backups, zero-trust architecture, and AI-driven intrusion detection systems vetted by ENISA. We are pairing this with a new €30bn Resilience Facility under the Multiannual Financial Framework, co-financed 70% by the EU, to cover the upgrade costs for operators in lower-capacity member states. The legal basis is Article 114 TFEU (internal market harmonisation), which allows us to set binding technical standards across jurisdictions.

Resistance is expected from member states with weak regulatory capacity and from industry lobbies citing cost and operational burden. We are offering fast-track access to InvestAI Gigafactories’ energy allocation for compliant operators, and we are linking future cohesion funds to implementation milestones. ENISA will be expanded by 400 staff and given real-time monitoring authority over designated networks, reporting quarterly to the Cyber Resilience Board in the Council’s Telecommunications configuration. The first stress test — a live-fire exercise simulating an AI-generated supply chain compromise — is scheduled for Q2 2027. This is not optional preparedness; it is the minimum credible response to the fact that offensive capability is now open, distributed, and already in use.
