### Accelerating Frontiers, Widening Gaps  
The second half of 2026 sees AI capability climb to 54.0 as two leading labs complete accelerated training runs, deploying agents that autonomously debug and redeploy critical software stacks. Offensive cyber tools generated by frontier models are now in the wild, evidenced by a global intrusion campaign that compromised grid operators and a major port—detected only by chance. Days later, a new open-weight release, derived from earlier frontier leaks and refined by distributed developer networks, begins circulating. While capable, it lacks full parity with the closed frontier. Improved exploit chaining and penetration testing push openweight_capability to 43.0—reflecting incremental progress grounded in prior diffusion, not sudden leapfrogging.

### EU Responds to Crisis  
In response, the EU proposes Cyber Shield for Critical Operators as its top priority—a large, category 6 measure aiming to harden essential infrastructure. The Commission drafts binding standards under NIS2 and outlines a €30bn Resilience Facility to fund monitoring and upgrades. However, legal challenges from Hungary and Slovakia delay adoption, and German industry resists operational mandates. ENISA’s expanded mandate is approved but faces severe staffing shortages; full deployment will require at least two turns. No funds are disbursed this turn, and the Facility remains unfunded pending a forthcoming EU fiscal review. The measure is in proposal and design phase only.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Shrinks  
Despite ongoing investments in gigafactories and data centres, eu_ai_sovereignty dips to 21.0 as the capability gap with the US widens and no sovereign compute milestone is reached. ai_safety declines to 32.0 due to the offensive capability jump and lack of deployed safeguards. resilience sees only a marginal increase, as Cyber Shield remains in early development. public_sentiment falls to 38.0 amid growing anxiety over job losses in legal and clinical sectors and fears of uncontrolled AI-driven attacks. eu_political_capital drops to 45.0—reflecting the burden of a crowded portfolio and partial progress on new initiatives, but not the full cost of premature implementation.
