## In practice  
This turn, we consolidate two foundational wins: the InvestAI Gigafactories and the European Compute Security Audit Regime are formally complete. The Gigafactories have secured legal jurisdiction over four sovereign compute sites—Finland, France, Spain, Poland—with binding agreements ensuring EU control over training runs and model weights. While full operational capacity remains delayed, the legal anchor is set: no foreign entity can unilaterally withdraw access. The Audit Regime is now law, empowering ANSSI, BSI, and the Dutch DPA to inspect high-performance clusters suspected of feeding the openweight black market. Austria and Poland tied their compliance to cohesion fund disbursements, but the mutual insurance logic held—no member state opted out. These milestones mark real progress toward infrastructure independence, even if the full capability uplift is still deferred.

The Tech Sovereignty Package continues to mobilize private investment, though grid constraints in southern Germany and northern Italy slow deployment. We are redirecting Recovery and Resilience Facility funds to fast-track grid connections at priority nodes, treating energy access as strategic infrastructure. This is not just about speed—it is about proving that EU-controlled compute can scale without reliance on foreign hyperscalers.

But the scandal in Germany’s social insurance system has shifted the axis of crisis from capability to credibility. The public sees AI as opaque, unjust, and unchecked. That is why we are pushing forward with the **Coalition Audit of AI-Driven Public Decisions**, now staffed jointly by the European Data Protection Board, the AI Office, and counterparts in Japan, Canada, and South Korea. We are drafting pilot protocols for reconstructing algorithmic decisions in unemployment benefits (France), predictive policing (Toronto), and visa processing (Seoul), aiming to build a shared standard of post-hoc accountability. Legal bases are being negotiated under Article 16 TFEU and the GDPR’s international cooperation clause, with early alignment from Canada and Japan on data access frameworks. Without this, we risk becoming rule-takers in a world we helped build.

Simultaneously, we act on the **cyber_defence_breakthrough**: offensive capabilities may be advancing, but for the first time in years, defence has leapt ahead. We are launching a new resilience push—**Accelerate Resilience Hardening in Critical Public Services**—to deploy automated patching and swarm-detection systems across healthcare networks, energy grids, and municipal IT systems. This uses newly available defensive tooling to close known exploit windows before they cascade. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme and the RRF’s emergency envelope, coordinated through ENISA and the Joint Cyber Unit. The goal is not perfection—but enough redundancy and detection speed to ensure attacks degrade rather than collapse essential services. This is resilience as political insurance: when the next incident hits, we must be seen protecting people, not just debating oversight.
