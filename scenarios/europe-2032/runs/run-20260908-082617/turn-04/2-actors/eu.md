## New measure  
**Launch Sovereign AI Testbed for Public Services**  
Deploy a secure, EU-controlled environment where public institutions can pilot and evaluate AI systems for healthcare, tax, and social services under real conditions but with strict safety boundaries.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +10 (via operational control of advanced models in public hands), ai_safety +8 (by generating applied assurance data), public_sentiment +5 (by demonstrating safe, useful AI in trusted domains)  
`Applies to:` EU agencies, national ministries in pilot member states (France, Germany, Estonia), and selected EU-certified AI providers

## In practice  
This turn, we complete Cyber Shield for Critical Operators. The Tier 1 designations are now legally binding under Article 114 TFEU, with ENISA’s red-team stress tests concluded and findings compiled into a classified annex for the Cyber Resilience Board. Though German operators secured reduced audit frequency, they remain under continuous logging and mandatory incident reporting—conditions tied to cohesion fund access that held. The results confirm systemic fragility: both energy providers failed to detect AI-driven lateral movement within 72 hours. We are not declaring victory. Instead, we are transitioning Cyber Shield into operational mode under the newly empowered Cyber Resilience Directorate, with permanent red teams and a mandate to expand to Tier 2 operators in turn 5. The measure finishes, but the work does not.

Similarly, the European Labour Transition and Innovation Fund reaches its formal endpoint. Disbursements to Belgium, France, and Germany are unlocked, tied to national job redesign pilots that condition further access on measurable retention and retraining outcomes. We have linked participation to the Tech Sovereignty Package’s permitting zones—not as a reward, but as leverage: no fast-tracking for firms that automate without restructuring. Early results are thin, but the framework is in place. Public sentiment remains low at 35.0, and the campaign_backlash is gaining force. We cannot afford to be seen as indifferent. That is why we are not launching another top-down industrial push, but turning toward visible, controlled public benefit.

The capability_plateau_evidence—underdelivering release, collapsing prices—tells us the frontier is not accelerating. This is not a pause to exploit for more compute or control, but a moment to build understanding where it matters: in public services. We are launching the Sovereign AI Testbed under the Digital Europe Programme’s oversight, hosted in Luxembourg with secure nodes in Estonia and Bavaria. It will run models up to openweight_capability levels (45.0), strictly firewalled, with human-in-the-loop mandates and real-time interpretability tooling. Ministries of health and taxation in three pilot states will test diagnostics support and fraud detection, but only systems that pass pre-vetted safety thresholds. This is not a sandbox. It is a proving ground for sovereign, safe deployment—and a signal that AI can serve, not just disrupt. We are funding it from the unspent balance of the Innovation Fund’s administrative envelope, avoiding new appropriations in this capital-constrained turn. The Dutch remain wary of coercion, the Germans cautious on data, but this is not about export controls or confrontation. It is about demonstrating competence, control, and care—before the next leap comes.
