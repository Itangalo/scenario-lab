### Capability Creeps Amid Uncertainty  
AI capability advances to 58.5, driven by efficiency gains in agent coordination and model fine-tuning, even as frontier labs report flatlining investment. The evidence of a plateau—underwhelming releases and falling prices—fuels debate, but does not halt progress. Open-weight models, now at 46.0, reflect this lagged diffusion, benefiting from newly permissive licensing by a second-tier developer and incremental compute access through academic consortia. The rise remains within structural limits, with no indication of outpacing proprietary systems.

### Cyber Shield and Labour Fund Close, with Mixed Results  
The EU formally completes Cyber Shield for Critical Operators, establishing mandatory logging and incident reporting across Tier 1 critical infrastructure. ENISA’s stress tests expose persistent detection gaps, particularly in energy networks, but the framework now operates under a permanent Cyber Resilience Directorate. Resilience improves to 44.0, reflecting hardening in finance and telecoms, though healthcare remains vulnerable.  

The European Labour Transition and Innovation Fund concludes its rollout, disbursing €50bn to three member states. Early pilots show modest retraining uptake, but public trust remains low. Sentiment rises slightly to 37.0 as the fund’s closure is framed as a transition to tangible projects, not abandonment.

### Sovereign Testbed Proposed, Not Launched  
The Commission formally proposes the Sovereign AI Testbed for Public Services, a category 5 initiative intended to pilot AI in healthcare diagnostics and tax fraud detection under strict human oversight. Hosted in Luxembourg with nodes in Estonia and Bavaria, it draws on repurposed Innovation Fund resources. However, due to ongoing portfolio strain from InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package, implementation is delayed. France and Germany express conditional support, but the Netherlands raises data sovereignty concerns, slowing consensus. The measure is added to the agenda but does not launch operationally this turn.

### Political Capital Stabilises, Sovereignty Holds  
With two large measures completed, the portfolio charge drops from −16 to −10. The closure of Cyber Shield and the Labour Fund reduces operational burden, earning a modest proposal bonus for initiating planning on the Testbed. Political capital rises to 33.0, reflecting cautious momentum. Member-state fragmentation and the continued weight of active initiatives prevent a stronger rebound.  

Sovereignty remains at 19.0. Although the proposal of the Testbed signals intent, no implementation has occurred, and Dutch resistance introduces countervailing friction. The notepad’s note of “in flight” does not justify a sovereignty gain without operational progress. After adjusting for the −1 decay from rising AI capability, the net change would be −1, but cohesion from closing prior measures and early coordination on the Testbed planning offset this fully. There is no floor preventing decline, but in this case, offsetting forces result in stability. The priority on compute sovereignty remains in place, sustaining the −1 portfolio charge. No new priority is introduced.
