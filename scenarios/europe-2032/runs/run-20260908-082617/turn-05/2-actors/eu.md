## In practice  
This turn, we complete the operational transition of Cyber Shield for Critical Operators into the permanent remit of the Cyber Resilience Directorate. ENISA’s classified annex confirms alarming detection lags in energy networks — one operator failed to flag coordinated reconnaissance by an agent cluster now traced to a non-state actor exploiting academic compute. This is not hypothetical harm. We are acting on it by repurposing elements of the Tech Sovereignty Package’s permitting leverage: fast-track approvals for new data centres will now require proof of compliance with upcoming Compute Security Audit standards, even before the regime formally launches. We are already drafting the directive under Article 114 TFEU, with input from BSI, ANSSI, and the Dutch Data Protection Authority, who remain cautious but acknowledge the threat surface has changed.  

The Labour Fund’s closure leaves behind modest gains and lingering resentment. The emergent_labour_protests confirm that retraining alone is not enough — algorithmic transparency and worker voice are now unavoidable. But we cannot launch a new social instrument this turn without breaking capital. Instead, we are using the momentum from the US election_alliance to de-escalate pressure: we signal conditional acceptance of their access terms, but only if joint evaluation includes EU-led audits of workforce impact in shared AI deployments. This buys time and cooperation, but not freedom from action.  

We are launching the European Compute Security Audit Regime as our core move — not to build more compute, but to secure what we have. The grey-market network revealed in Romania, Greece, and Poland is not just evading export controls; it is turning EU infrastructure into a backdoor for open-weight models approaching 50.0. We cannot control the weights, but we can control the wires. The audit regime will mandate geofencing, usage logging, and ownership transparency for all clusters above threshold, enforced by a new unit within the Cyber Resilience Directorate. Member states resist — Austria calls it overreach, Poland cites research freedom — so we tie compliance to cohesion fund disbursements and ERDF access. This is not about stifling innovation. It is about ensuring that the EU’s own resources do not become the means of its own subordination.
