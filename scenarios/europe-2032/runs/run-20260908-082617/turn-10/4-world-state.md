### Sovereignty on Paper, Not in Practice

The EU formally completes the InvestAI Gigafactories and the European Compute Security Audit Regime, marking a symbolic leap in tech sovereignty. Four sovereign compute sites are now under binding EU jurisdiction. Yet enforcement remains partial: Austria and Poland continue to delay full audit access, demanding additional cohesion funding. A deal is tentatively struck—energy grid investments in exchange for compliance—but implementation is deferred to the next turn. The Tech Sovereignty Package mobilises €183bn, short of its €200bn target due to permitting bottlenecks in Italy and Germany. Article 122 TFEU is invoked to fast-track grid upgrades, but legal challenges loom.

### Trust in Crisis

Public trust erodes further as the German social insurance scandal becomes emblematic of systemic failure. An ombudsman confirms the AI system denied benefits without explanation or appeal, triggering widespread anger. The EU accelerates the Coalition Audit and launches the **Post-Deployment Redress Mechanism**, aiming to become an ombudsman for algorithmic harm. Pilots in France and Toronto begin testing forensic logging, but rollout is slow. The narrative of “trusted standards” now competes with visible harm and delayed action.

### The Cost of Ambition

Three large measures remain in flight, including the newly launched Redress Mechanism. With political capital already strained, the portfolio charge overwhelms the system. The priority status on the Coalition Audit yields no acceleration due to low capital. The **automated_decision_scandal** event hits, originating internally—damage is severe. Labour displacement grows, with entry-level roles vanishing in law and administration. Capability growth slows slightly (+1.5), but eval anomalies hint at hidden advances. No openweight leap occurs. Resilience holds, but no gains land.
