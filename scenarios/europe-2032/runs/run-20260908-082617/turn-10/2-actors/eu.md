## New measure  
**Operationalise the Post-Deployment Redress Mechanism for Public AI**  
Launch an EU-level system to reconstruct, review, and correct high-impact algorithmic decisions in real time, using forensic logging, model versioning, and human-in-the-loop review to create enforceable rights.

## In practice  
This turn, we complete the legal and institutional anchoring of EU compute sovereignty: the InvestAI Gigafactories and the European Compute Security Audit Regime are now law and land. Four sovereign sites — Finland, France, Spain, and Poland — are under binding EU jurisdiction, with national agencies empowered to inspect clusters suspected of feeding the openweight black market. Yet Austria and Poland continue to withhold full operational compliance, demanding cohesion transfers as a condition — a reminder that legal victory does not equal enforcement. We are meeting them in the General Affairs Council, offering targeted energy grid investments from the RRF emergency envelope in exchange for full audit access by Q3. Without this, the regime remains a skeleton.

Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package has mobilised €183bn in private capital toward AI infrastructure, falling short of its €200bn target due to permitting delays in Italy and Germany. We are invoking Article 122 TFEU to fast-track grid upgrades at two priority nodes, treating energy access as strategic infrastructure. This is not subsidy — it is survival: if EU-controlled compute cannot scale, we outsource not just capability, but our future.

But the German social insurance scandal has shattered the illusion that oversight ends at release. People were denied benefits by an unexplained algorithm, with no appeal path. That is why we are accelerating the **Coalition Audit of AI-Driven Public Decisions**, now co-located between the AI Office, the European Data Protection Board, and technical teams in Canada, Japan, and South Korea. We are finalising forensic logging standards for unemployment, policing, and visa systems, using GDPR Article 16 and the Digital Governance Act to mandate reconstruction rights. Early pilots in France and Toronto will test whether we can rebuild a decision trail within 72 hours — not for blame, but for correction. If we cannot deliver this, no one will believe our standards are more than theatre.

At the same time, we are launching the **Post-Deployment Redress Mechanism** as a large-scale operational system — not a study, not a proposal. It will sit within the AI Office, with delegated authority to compel data access, freeze high-risk models, and issue binding correction orders. Funding comes from the Digital Europe Programme and the RRF’s AI Resilience window. This is the pivot: from being a regulator of models to being an ombudsman for people. Because if we do not become the institution that fixes AI harm, someone else will — and it won’t be on our terms.
