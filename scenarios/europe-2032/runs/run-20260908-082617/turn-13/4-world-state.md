### Scandal and Response

An ombudsman’s report confirms that AI-supported decisions in social insurance across three member states systematically denied benefits to vulnerable citizens, with no accessible appeal. The ruling lands as a constitutional shock, reinforcing public distrust and validating fears that AI governance has become detached from justice. The European Parliament faces protests, and several national governments face legal challenges.

The EU responds by reactivating the Post-Deployment Redress Mechanism, now embedded within the Emergency Sovereign Compute Continuity Council. The move is framed not as a return to normative idealism, but as a repair of operational legitimacy. Military cyber-forensics units are reassigned to reconstruct flawed decision logs, and legal teams begin fast-track reviews in collaboration with national courts. Germany agrees to lead protocol design, seeing an opening for stricter human oversight.

Yet integration is rocky. The Council’s military and energy focus clashes with the redress mission’s legal and social character. Data from the Compute Security Audit Regime is incomplete—many systems lacked forensic logging—and reconstruction efforts stall. The mechanism restarts, but at a fraction of intended capacity.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

AI capability advances by 1.5 to 72.0, driven by closed-lab progress in agent-based optimization and cyber operations. Open-weight capability rises to 50.7, as leaked models from Eastern networks enable mid-tier autonomous systems in unregulated sectors.

No safety measure fully lands: the redress restart is too recent to yield gains, and the prior pause erodes confidence. With no improvement in shipped systems, ai_safety drops by 2.

Resilience holds steady at 54.0—the Council’s grid interventions in Finland prevent cascading failure, but logistical delays expose fragility. Sovereignty falls to 23.5, as ai_capability rises (−1) and no new compute build offsets dependence.

Political capital plummets to 4.0. Four large measures are in flight (−12), a priority is named (−1), and no completion bonus offsets the cost. The redress restart earns no proposal bonus—it answers a domestic scandal, not an external threat—and the legitimacy deficit deepens.

Public sentiment crashes to 16.0, dragged down by the scandal and perceptions of delayed accountability. The mood turns openly hostile, with protests targeting AI offices and data centres.
