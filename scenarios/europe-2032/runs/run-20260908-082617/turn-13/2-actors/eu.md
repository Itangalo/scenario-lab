## In practice  
This turn, we are reclaiming legitimacy through integration. The automated decision scandal — a ruling that AI-supported denials in social insurance have systematically deprived vulnerable citizens of benefits — has crystallised the cost of our triage: we have secured the wires, but lost the people. We cannot command infrastructure if it is used to inflict harm without recourse. The Post-Deployment Redress Mechanism is no longer a standalone audit project; it is being reactivated as a core function of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Continuity Council, reporting directly to its oversight board and drawing on its technical and enforcement resources.

We are instructing the AI Office to reconstitute the redress team from the Council’s existing personnel, using military cyber-forensics units for model reconstruction and civil service legal experts for case review. The mechanism will focus first on the systems implicated in the scandal, using the forensic logs we mandated under the Audit Regime — even though those audits were never fully implemented, partial data exists. We are engaging the European Ombudsman and national supreme courts to establish fast-track review pathways, and we are offering Germany a lead role in designing the human-in-the-loop protocols they have long demanded. This is not a return to normative ambition — it is a repair of operational credibility. Sovereignty must be both physical and moral, or it will not hold.
