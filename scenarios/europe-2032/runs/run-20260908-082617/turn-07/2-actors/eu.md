## In practice  
This turn, we close out two foundational measures: *InvestAI Gigafactories* and the *European Compute Security Audit Regime*. The Gigafactories have secured four sites — in Finland, France, Spain, and Poland — with binding legal agreements ensuring EU jurisdiction over training runs and weight ownership. This delivers a tangible boost to eu_ai_sovereignty, though the full compute capacity will only come online in the next cycle. The Audit Regime is now law, with ANSSI, BSI, and the Dutch DPA empowered to inspect academic and industrial clusters suspected of contributing to the openweight leak. Compliance is tied to cohesion fund access, and despite Austrian and Polish resistance, no member state has opted out — the mutual insurance logic has held.

The *Tech Sovereignty Package* continues, with private capital responding to the accelerated-permitting zones, though grid constraints in southern Germany and northern Italy are slowing deployment. We are now redirecting emergency infrastructure funds to priority nodes, using the Recovery and Resilience Facility to fast-track connections. This is not just about speed — it is about proving that EU-controlled compute can scale without foreign gatekeepers.

But the real test is legitimacy. The automated_decision_scandal has ignited public fury, and the emergent_labour_protests_coalesce movement is demanding structural accountability. We cannot answer with compute alone. That is why we are launching the **Coalition Audit of AI-Driven Public Decisions** — not as a standalone investigation, but as a new institutional function. Staffed by legal experts from the European Data Protection Board and technical auditors from the AI Office, it will work with counterparts in Japan, Canada, and South Korea to develop methods for reconstructing and contesting AI-influenced decisions, even in the absence of interpretable reasoning. We will pilot it in three domains: unemployment benefit denials in France, predictive policing in Toronto, and visa processing in Seoul. The goal is not just redress — it is to build a shared standard of algorithmic accountability that becomes the de facto benchmark for public-sector AI worldwide. Without this, no amount of sovereign infrastructure will preserve our political agency.
