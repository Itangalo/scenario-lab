### Crisis Command

The EU moves to centralise control over its AI infrastructure amid accelerating threats. The **Emergency Sovereign Compute Continuity Council** is formally established under Article 122 TFEU, granting it binding authority over energy allocation, military deployment, and semiconductor servicing. A joint session of COREPER II, the Energy Council, and the Defence Planning Committee approves its mandate, though only after last-minute concessions to Germany on oversight and Poland on representation. Finland, France, and Spain integrate their civil protection units, but Polish forces remain partially disengaged, citing constitutional limits.

Three category 4 measures — *InvestAI Gigafactories*, *Tech Sovereignty Package*, and *European Compute Security Audit Regime* — complete this turn, delivering physical compute capacity, private investment frameworks, and audit protocols targeting black-market leakage. However, their impact is overshadowed by ongoing fragility. The **Post-Deployment Redress Mechanism** is paused as staff are redeployed, weakening public trust further.

With no external incident this turn, resilience gains slightly from the new Council’s partial activation (+1), though systemic stress persists. AI capability advances by +1.5 to 70.5, driven by closed labs, but safety declines (−2) as oversight falters and no safety-focused measure lands. Open-weight capability creeps up to 49.4, reflecting slow diffusion through Eastern networks.

Sovereignty falls to 24.5: despite completed builds, *ai_capability* rose by 1.5 (triggering −1), and the lingering effects of *member_state_defection* and *supply_chain_coercion* from last turn continue to erode capacity. Political capital drops to 7.0, burdened by three large measures (−9), a named priority (−1), and no completion bonus large enough to offset the cost. The cancellation of the Coalition Audit yields no gain, only avoided further loss.

Public sentiment holds at 21.0 — too low to lift capital, too high to grant protest-driven legitimacy. The mood remains one of emergency, not renewal.

### Triage Deepens

The Council becomes operational, but its authority is tested immediately when ENTSO-E flags instability in the Nordic grid corridor. The Council orders military engineers to secure transformer stations in Finland, but delays in equipment delivery raise doubts about scalability. Meanwhile, intelligence confirms new black-market nodes in Moldova and Serbia, exploiting audit gaps.

The EU is no longer failing — but it is not stabilising.
