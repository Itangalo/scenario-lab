## In practice  
This turn, we finalise the transition of Cyber Shield into the permanent architecture of the Cyber Resilience Directorate, incorporating ENISA’s classified findings on energy-sector detection gaps into updated baseline requirements. While the measure is now finished, its legacy lives on: we are embedding its monitoring protocols into the new Compute Security Audit Regime, extending the same scrutiny from critical infrastructure to high-risk compute facilities. The audit framework is now in draft under Article 114 TFEU, with BSI, ANSSI, and the Dutch DPA aligning on technical standards for geofencing and usage logging. To overcome resistance from Austria and Poland, we link compliance to cohesion fund disbursements — not as punishment, but as mutual insurance. The message is clear: sovereignty means shared rules, not fragmented exceptions.

The Sovereign AI Testbed concludes its pilot phase with successful deployments in three member states, proving that EU-controlled systems can support high-stakes public services. But its closure leaves a gap in public technical capacity just as openweight models creep toward 50.0. That’s why we’re launching the Joint Evaluation Cells — not as a standalone ambition, but as the next phase of our sovereignty strategy. With Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Brazil in the middle_power_coalition, we can pool access to frontier models, co-develop evaluation tooling, and issue joint safety certifications that carry market weight. We’ll staff them with rotating experts from national labs and the newly empowered AI Office, using the Coalition Trust Fund to cover costs. This isn’t outsourcing — it’s scaling.

At the same time, we’re under pressure: public sentiment remains at 35.0, and the streets are restless. Labour protests in logistics and public services grow louder, and with the Labour Fund expired, we have no instrument to answer them directly. We cannot launch a new social measure without breaking capital — not with four large measures still in flight. So we signal through the Joint Evaluation Cells that workforce impact will be part of our joint audit mandate, pushing developers to disclose displacement risks alongside safety metrics. It’s not a job guarantee, but it’s a start — and it’s the only path forward that doesn’t sacrifice either mandate. We act under uncertainty, yes, but we do not act alone.
