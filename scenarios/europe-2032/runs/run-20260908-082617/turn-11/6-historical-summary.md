The EU faces deepening crisis in 2031 as a member state defects to a bilateral AI deal with a non-EU power, triggering a collapse of internal cohesion. Sabotage at data centre sites in Germany and Poland, linked to anti-automation protests and the lingering fallout from the German social insurance scandal, exposes systemic fragility. The Commission halts the Coalition Audit to redirect resources toward emergency assessments of physical and grid vulnerabilities across its four sovereign compute clusters.

External pressures intensify when the US pressures the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s servicing of legacy semiconductor equipment, extending export controls to foundational chip infrastructure and revealing the EU’s strategic dependency. In response, Brussels prepares to invoke the Anti-Coercion Instrument, though legal and political delays prevent swift action.

Emergency measures prioritize compute and grid security: military and civil protection units are deployed to safeguard critical infrastructure in Finland, France, and Spain, but Polish resistance undermines unified implementation. Europol leads cross-border efforts to disrupt illicit model weight transfers, with limited success.

Political capital erodes further as the abandonment of the Coalition Audit symbolizes a retreat from normative leadership. Public trust plummets amid narratives of failure and retreat, while labour displacement accelerates. AI capability rises to 69.0 due to advances in closed labs, but safety and oversight decline. Open-weight capability remains stagnant at 48.7, despite persistent black-market activity. Resilience and sovereignty weaken under combined external coercion and internal fragmentation.
