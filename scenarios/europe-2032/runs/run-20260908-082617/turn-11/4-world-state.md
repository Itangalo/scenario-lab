### Fracture and Fortification

The EU enters 2031’s second half in crisis mode. The defection of a member state to a bilateral AI infrastructure deal with a non-EU power shatters the facade of unity, while sabotage strikes at two planned data centre sites in Germany and Poland. Protesters, some linked to anti-automation movements, disable grid transformers, citing the German social insurance scandal as proof that AI governance is already broken. In response, the Commission abandons the Coalition Audit, redirecting its limited technical teams toward emergency assessments of physical vulnerabilities at the four sovereign compute clusters.

Simultaneously, Washington pressures the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s servicing of older lithography tools, effectively extending export controls beyond cutting-edge chips to foundational semiconductor capacity. The move, justified on national security grounds, exposes the EU’s dependency: its largest industrial chokepoint is being wielded by another power. Brussels activates the Anti-Coercion Instrument in preparation, but legal and political hurdles delay any immediate countermeasure.

The new priority—securing the compute backbone—begins under emergency provisions. Military and civil protection units are assigned to guard grid links in Finland, France, and Spain, though Polish authorities resist full integration, citing sovereignty concerns. Europol coordinates with national agencies to trace illicit model weight transfers through Eastern European networks, but early efforts yield only partial disruption.

Despite the urgency, political capital continues to bleed. The abandonment of the Coalition Audit, once a symbol of normative ambition, triggers institutional disillusionment. Labour displacement accelerates, and public sentiment dips further as media frames the moment as one of retreat, not resilience.

AI capability advances by 1.5 points, driven by closed labs, but safety erodes as oversight falters. No open-weight leap occurs, though black-market diffusion persists. Resilience weakens slightly as systemic stress outpaces hardening efforts. Sovereignty declines under external coercion and internal fragmentation.

### Triage, Not Transformation

The EU is no longer shaping the future—it is trying to keep the lights on.
