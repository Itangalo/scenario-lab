### Hands that do not ask
Spring brought machines that did not need instructions twice. Chinese-built humanoids appeared in logistics yards outside Rotterdam and Gdansk, leased through intermediaries, carrying, sorting, then drilling and patching with a steadiness night shifts could not match. The control software came from American labs. European robot makers, strong in arms and cells, found themselves buying the intelligence elsewhere.

In factories that still led in precision, managers stopped talking about retraining intervals. Junior hiring, already frozen in offices, now froze in warehouses and maintenance bays. Evening news cut from graduate queues to dockworkers watching leased units learn a task by noon.

### Strait watch
At the same time insurers repriced the Strait. Extended exercises, an expulsion, premiums up, chip shipments a week late and then two. The Commission issued coordinated guidance rather than a stockpile order — hospitals and battery plants were told to map dependencies, not to hoard.

Brussels had no money for hoarding anyway. The new Manual Fallback Corps paid municipalities for paper rosters: offline triage in clinics, hand-counted welfare re-decisions with published logs, degraded-mode drills in ports and power control rooms. A few thousand more arrears letters went out, review times held in tens of minutes. Mayors said the separation of redress staff from trainee supervision finally began in places, unevenly.

It kept wards and docks running, but did not answer the larger humiliation. Editorials called it dignity by clipboard while foreign machines lifted the pallets. Trust kept falling; sabotage rumours persisted around fenced build sites, with a second single-source report of a halted hall after a subsea power fault, still unconfirmed, still repaired by night crews.

Joint evaluation channels with Washington stayed open, and the predictability check spread in triage teams, cutting false alarms. Capability moved on regardless.
