### The jump
Autumn began with a demonstration no briefing had prepared for. An American lab released an agent system that planned multi-week engineering work, re-planned when tools failed, and showed its work only after the fact. Benchmark sheets circulated on Monday were obsolete by Friday. Hospitals and grid operators in Europe received the same advisory as everyone else: update your assumptions.

Days later Washington and Beijing announced a limited risk pact — weights security, autonomous escalation, certain biological design tools — with thin verification and no European signature. Brussels learned the outline from press wires and a late-night call.

### The third chair
The Commission asked for a third chair: observer-to-party status, EU incident reports recognised by the AI Office, export-control alignment on lithography and weights audits offered in return, continuity of leased machines at Rotterdam and Gdansk quietly raised with Beijing.

Washington kept joint evaluation channels open and extended predictability checks; a seat at the verification table did not follow. Beijing acknowledged the request without committing. Inside the Council, ministers argued over whether offering alignment upfront had spent leverage for nothing. The Anti-Coercion preparation stayed in the drawer.

### Holding by hand
At home the Manual Fallback Corps became the visible state. Clinics ran offline triage kits, municipalities published hand-counted welfare logs, ports and power control rooms drilled in degraded mode. Payments cleared, wards stayed open.

It did not lift mood. News cut from foreign machines learning a dock task by noon to graduates queuing for traineeships supervising redress queues. Mayors complained reimbursements arrived late and conditionality — separated redress staff, published logs — punished the thinnest administrations. A fenced build site halted again after a power fault; night crews repaired it, rumours outran the repair.

By December, Europe was running on paper while the frontier ran ahead of its monitors.
