### The systems that decide
Winter brought the inquiry every ministry feared. An ombudsman, backed by a court finding, showed a welfare and policing support system had cut benefits and flagged families on faulty scores for months. Caseworkers had approved queues at under a minute a file, logs were complete and never read. Brussels framed it as a breach of high-risk duties, not a gap in the law, but editorials asked why conformity papers had passed at all.

The redress drive opened fast: frozen queues, ordered human re-decisions, arrears promised. Victims' groups called it late and under-staffed. Mayors running placement schemes said the same officers meant to re-hear cases were also meant to supervise juniors.

### Breakthroughs that did not lift
In the same months, two genuine advances landed. A Franco-German materials team, using self-improving lab assistants, published a battery chemistry with immediate industrial interest. Clinicians in three states began tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions, cutting treatment failures visibly.

Both helped, neither rescued. The therapies arrived on foreign models under emergency licences, and procurement on European capacity lagged. Patients were grateful and reminded of dependence in the same broadcast. The materials result drew pride in specialist press and little public notice.

Meanwhile American systems moved again into multi-day autonomous planning. European checklists were rewritten after deployment. Graduate hiring stayed frozen; placements grew from thousands to low tens of thousands but ESF+ clearing still stalled, and firms kept juniors only where wages were largely covered.

Shipping insurers raised Strait surcharges after extended exercises in the Pacific. Stockpiled components absorbed the shock for hospitals and ports, which stayed degraded but running. Fenced build sites stayed under guard, cables cut and repaired as routine.

The Union closed spring with services held, wronged households partly repaid, and a public that used automated help daily and trusted it less than ever.
