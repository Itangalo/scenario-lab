## Priority  
**Priority:** Establish EU Public Trust Corps for AI Incident Response and Transparency  
This remains our dominant effort because the crisis is no longer about capability or access — it is about legitimacy. The Trust Corps is the only instrument in flight that can simultaneously respond to incidents, coordinate redress, and rebuild public trust through visible action, especially as we now shift from transparency as performance to accountability as consequence.

## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: containment and repair. The ombudsman’s ruling on the Dutch social insurance AI has crystallised a truth we can no longer ignore — our own systems are failing citizens under the guise of efficiency. The protests in Utrecht and Lyon are not against AI as such, but against a governance model that treats people as data points. The **Public Trust Corps**, though still months from full deployment, is being activated now in crisis mode. DG HOME and DG CONNECT have agreed to fast-track the **Redress Coordination Unit**, embedding it with national ombudsmen in the Netherlands, France, and Finland by turn 11. This is not symbolic: we are assigning legal officers, data auditors, and trauma-informed communicators to review 500 flagged cases from the Dutch system by December, with compensation pathways open by January. We are also upgrading the **AI Incident Dashboard** to include systemic bias alerts — not just technical failures, but patterns of disparate impact across regions and demographics. This pilot will run in France and Finland, using anonymised audit logs from public-sector AI deployments, cross-referenced with complaint data.

At the same time, the **Public Algorithmic Accountability Boards** are being designed with real teeth. We are working through the Justice Council to grant them subpoena power over model decision logs and internal audit reports, starting with the Dutch case. Member states are resisting — Poland and Hungary have called it an infringement — but we are linking participation to cohesion funding and offering technical support through the Digital Europe Programme. The first board will convene in January, with citizen sortition, academic experts, and civil society representatives. Its mandate: issue binding recommendations on suspension, reform, or decommissioning of the Dutch system within six months. This is not consultation. This is accountability in motion. Meanwhile, the **Sovereign Cyber Shield** audit continues, but we cannot claim external resilience while our internal systems erode public faith. That is why every euro and every political chip is now flowing into making the Trust Corps a living institution — not a framework, not a directive, but a visible, operational force for repair.
