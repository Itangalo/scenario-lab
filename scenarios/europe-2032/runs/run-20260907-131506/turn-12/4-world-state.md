### Crisis as Catalyst

The EU’s frontier model cutoff triggers cascading disruptions: hospital diagnostic systems in the Netherlands and France degrade, welfare processing halts in parts of Finland, and municipal AI assistants go offline. These are not failures of design but of dependency — systems built without fallbacks now exposed as politically fragile. The Public Trust Corps activates early, deploying red-team engineers to reconstruct essential functions using open-weight models at 59.0 capability. Though insufficient for full parity, these efforts restore minimal continuity in key services by mid-June.

A rapid-response resilience surge is launched, targeting healthcare and social administration. Pre-positioned zero-trust protocols and modular inference stacks allow limited substitution, but integration delays and legal bottlenecks slow deployment. Still, visible crisis management begins to shift public perception. Trauma-informed redress teams process hundreds of claims from the Dutch scandal, offering compensation not as closure but as recognition — a symbolic recalibration of state accountability.

Public Algorithmic Accountability Boards gain momentum despite resistance. A legal opinion affirming their authority under EU Charter Article 11 strengthens the Commission’s hand, and offers of co-leadership draw tentative engagement from Hungary. Poland remains opposed, framing the boards as judicial overreach. The first board’s investigation into the Dutch system intensifies, with preliminary findings expected next turn.

Meanwhile, AI capability advances to 70.5, driven by efficiency gains and architectural refinements abroad. No major open-weight release occurs, but community-driven fine-tuning narrows the gap slightly. With no new safety infrastructure deployed, ai_safety remains stagnant at 15.0, as unmonitored inference pathways persist unchecked.
