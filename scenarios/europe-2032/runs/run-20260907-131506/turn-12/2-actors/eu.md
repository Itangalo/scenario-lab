## Priority  
**Priority:** Establish EU Public Trust Corps for AI Incident Response and Transparency  
This remains our dominant effort because legitimacy is now the frontline — not just of public trust, but of operational sovereignty. The Trust Corps is the only instrument in flight that can simultaneously respond to the denial of access, coordinate redress for the Dutch scandal, and stabilise public services under strain, transforming accountability from a promise into a functioning system.

## In practice  
We are acting on three fronts: survival, repair, and reassertion. The cutoff from the frontier model has grounded hospital diagnostics in three member states and frozen welfare processing in two more — not because the systems failed, but because they were never designed to function without a foreign heartbeat. This is not a technical outage. It is a political event. The **Public Trust Corps**, though not yet fully staffed, is being activated as a crisis institution. We are deploying its red-team engineers to reverse-engineer fallback protocols from open-weight models now at 57.5 capability — not to match the frontier, but to restore continuity. Crisis communicators are embedded in national health ministries to coordinate messaging, and the **AI Incident Dashboard** has been upgraded in real time to track service degradation, not just cyber threats. This is transparency under fire.

Meanwhile, the **Redress Coordination Unit** is operational in pilot mode, processing claims from the Dutch social insurance failure with trauma-informed officers and legal aid partners. We are fast-tracking compensation not as a payout, but as a signal: the state hears you. The **Public Algorithmic Accountability Boards** are now backed by a legal opinion from the Commission’s Legal Service affirming their authority under Article 11 of the Charter, and we are offering Poland and Hungary technical assistance and co-lead roles in exchange for participation. The first board will issue its findings on the Dutch system by turn 13 — and if it recommends suspension, we will enforce it. This is not federal overreach. This is democracy repairing its circuits.
