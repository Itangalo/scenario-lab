## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch Sovereign AI Stack Initiative  
Begin the irreversible deployment of a full-stack, EU-controlled AI infrastructure — from compute to models to applications — capable of running critical public services without foreign dependency.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 19`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–20 points by turn 19, anchor openweight_capability at or above 60.0 within EU jurisdiction, and lay the foundation for future safety and oversight by ensuring the Union can run, inspect, and control systems it regulates  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member state digital agencies, selected national champions in cloud and semiconductor infrastructure, and public research consortia

## Priority  
**Priority:** Mandate Public Algorithmic Accountability Boards for High-Impact AI Systems  
This remains our dominant effort this turn because the first board’s findings on the Dutch scandal are imminent, and its credibility — and the legitimacy of the entire accountability architecture — hinges on whether it can compel action under fire. While the Sovereign AI Stack is the long-term anchor of agency, the Boards are the short-term test of whether the Union can still govern its own institutions — and without that, no infrastructure will be trusted to run.

## In practice  
We are operating in a state of dual urgency: one foot in crisis, one foot in foundation. The **Public Trust Corps**, though now finished as a measure, has left behind a live dashboard, embedded crisis teams, and a precedent: the state must speak in real time when systems fail. That infrastructure is now being repurposed to support the final phase of the **Public Algorithmic Accountability Boards**, whose investigation into the Dutch AI scandal is entering its decisive week. We are in closed session with the Council Legal Service and national justice ministers to pre-empt any enforcement gap — if the board recommends suspension of the system, we will invoke Article 11 and back it with Commission enforcement teams. Hungary has agreed to observe; Poland has not. This is not optional: if accountability has no teeth, the public will see only performance.

At the same time, we are launching the **Sovereign AI Stack Initiative** not through a new fund, but through a binding directive under Article 114 TFEU, repurposing existing Digital Europe and Connecting Europe Facility budgets to mandate that all high-impact public AI systems — in healthcare, social services, and border management — must be operable on EU-anchored infrastructure by turn 19. We are fast-tracking grid access for three sovereign data hub sites in Finland, Spain, and Romania, leveraging our supply-chain leverage in submarine cables and chip packaging to lock in energy and hardware priority. This is not a gigafactory redux — it is a command: no new AI procurement above threshold will be approved unless it runs on stack we control. The resistance is fierce — France wants access, Germany wants cost control, and the Commission’s own digital department warns of delays. But we are offering access: every member state will get mirrored capacity, but only if they accept the Union’s security and transparency protocols. This is how sovereignty is rebuilt — not with promises, but with power.
