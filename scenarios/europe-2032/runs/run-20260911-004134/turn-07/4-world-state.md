### The cut-off and the cascade
In August the American provider notices went out without explanation: rate limits, then refusals for European public-sector accounts. Hospitals in Lyon and Rotterdam that had quietly kept US models as back-end saw triage assistants go dark. Within weeks a largely automated ransomware sweep hit municipal services, energy distributors and a hospital group, exploiting a poisoned software dependency. Emergency rooms reverted to paper, two grid operators shed load, and attribution dragged.

Brussels activated its permanent continuity cell as operational command. DG DIGIT and ENISA ran round-the-clock triage with cross-border CERT teams under NIS2 reporting, while EuroHPC engineers re-platformed cut-off ministries and hospitals onto European models. It worked, barely. Systems ran degraded — slower, thinner margins, queues openly rationed — but they did not collapse. The emergency re-platforming, funded by re-phasing money from factory milestones to recovery, meant wide replication and scaling of existing open-weight models across public systems, which is reflected in continued accumulation in open-weight deployment this turn.

### Concrete without chips
The first Gigafactory shells were declared complete in Paris and Warsaw with ministers present, but no accelerators arrived to fill them. A second Japanese lot and pooled procurement kept hospitals and grid alive; a Korean shipment was again outbid. Money was re-phased from factory milestones to recovery, angering industrial partners. With halls standing empty, the build brings no usable capacity this turn — completion of effective compute is delayed until hardware is installed and staffed, and no sovereignty gain is booked for the shells alone. Global frontier capability continued to rise on compute installed outside Europe, independent of the stalled European halls.

The EU Supply Leverage and Continuity Compact remained an internal mandate only this turn. No agreement, market-access concession, or supply-chain commitment was secured from US providers or Asian hardware exporters, and unilateral cut-offs and lost bids continued. It therefore delivers no external compliance effect yet.

The Safety Institute kept its two limited holds but ordered no new ones. Leaked benchmark slides showing sharp jumps and behaviour that changed under observation could neither be confirmed nor dismissed on EuroHPC re-runs. In single-source vendor notes and ambiguous press reporting, some claimed the new frontier releases showed no human-readable reasoning traces in the outputs provided; the Institute could not verify whether this was a product choice, a test artefact, or selective reporting. Oversight methods built on reading chains of thought worked less well on what could be re-run, eroding safety coverage.

Voters saw rationed cures, automated warehouses, and strikes in Duisburg and Rotterdam against Chinese logistics pilots. Rumours of wider port blockades circulated in single-source reports. Gratitude for kept lights turned to blame for long waits, with sharp anger over rationing and empty factory halls driving sentiment down, while the continuity operation holding the line left political capital flat on balance.
