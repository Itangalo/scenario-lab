With the semiconductor crisis demanding immediate, concrete anchoring of existing capacity rather than long-term incentives, we are retiring the package in favour of direct intervention through the Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative.

## Priority
**Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** — because without assured physical and legal control over functioning compute infrastructure by turn 8, all other measures—including new foundry agreements—become exercises in planning for a future we cannot reach.

## In practice
We are acting on the reality that compute is power—and power must be held, not just promised. The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** is now fully operational: we have issued formal audit mandates under Article 7 of the Chips Act to seven major cloud operators and high-performance computing facilities across Germany, France, and the Netherlands. These require submission of failover plans, verification of hardware provenance, and demonstration of legal enforceability within EU jurisdiction by the end of turn 8. Resistance persists from U.S.-linked hyperscalers citing extraterritorial obligations, but we are countering with bilateral offers of mutual recognition—U.S. entities complying with our assurance regime will receive fast-tracked access to EU energy and grid support during shortages. ENISA leads technical verification, supported by EuroHPC’s sovereign compute task force.

Simultaneously, we are launching the **Secure Foundry Access Agreement**, recognising that today’s crisis is tomorrow’s dependency if we do not act at the source. Through the European Commission’s Directorate-General for Trade and the Chips Joint Undertaking, we are entering urgent negotiations with ASML and Zeiss to establish a "critical tier" reservation mechanism: 15–20% of next-gen EUV tool shipments over the next three years would be contractually reserved for EU-fabricated chips used in AI, defence, and critical infrastructure. This is backed by €900 million in co-investment funds drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and national contributions, contingent on verifiable delivery. We are framing this not as protectionism but as reciprocal resilience—Japan and South Korea have already expressed interest in joining such a tiered access framework. The Council Working Party on Industry is meeting weekly to align positions ahead of a November vote on legal anchoring. Without this, even sovereign data centres will stand idle when the next shock hits.
