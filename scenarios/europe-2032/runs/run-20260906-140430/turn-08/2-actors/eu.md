## New measure
**Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield**  
Launch an emergency programme to deploy AI-hardened intrusion detection and automated response systems across municipal, energy, and health networks, with mandatory integration within 12 months.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 10  
`Targeted effect:` +5 to +7 resilience by turn 10 by reducing vulnerability to emergent open-source cyber tools and ensuring continuity of essential services during high-volume, adaptive attacks  
`Applies to:` Member state critical infrastructure operators, ENISA, national cybersecurity agencies, and EU-funded AI research consortia developing defensive tooling

## Priority
**Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield** — because the emergent spread of AI-powered cyber tools among non-state actors represents an immediate, tangible threat to the integrity of institutions, and our two-year commitment demands we act now to defend them, even as sovereignty negotiations proceed.

## In practice
The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** has reached its finishing turn. We have completed technical verification with four of the seven mandated operators: Deutsche Telekom, Atos, OVHcloud, and SURF. All have submitted hardware provenance logs and failover plans under Article 7 of the Chips Act, and ENISA has certified their compliance. U.S.-linked hyperscalers—Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, and Amazon Web Services—remain non-compliant, citing conflicting obligations under the U.S. CLOUD Act. In response, we are activating contingency measures: the European Commission is preparing a formal infringement procedure, while simultaneously offering a bilateral mutual recognition framework. Entities that submit to EU audit by turn 9 will be fast-tracked for priority access to EU emergency grid support and classified threat intelligence sharing through the CSIRT network. This is not a waiver—it is a test of whether interoperability can be built without surrendering jurisdiction.

The **Secure Foundry Access Agreement** enters its second turn with intensified negotiations. ASML and Zeiss have provisionally agreed to reserve 18% of next-gen EUV tool output for EU fabs from 2031–2034, contingent on legal anchoring by the Council and disbursement of the €900 million co-investment fund. Japan and South Korea have requested observer status in the talks, and we are responding with a tiered access proposal: reciprocal reservations for aligned democracies that contribute to shared stockpiles and joint R&D. The Council Working Party on Industry meets weekly, but legal concerns persist over WTO compatibility and extraterritorial enforceability. We are addressing these through a dual-track approach: DG Trade is drafting a plurilateral agreement under the auspices of the Indo-Pacific Economic Framework, while the Chips Joint Undertaking prepares fallback mechanisms using export licensing authority under the EU’s Dual-Use Regulation. Time is short—the next production cycle at ASML’s Veldhoven facility locks in by turn 9.

Now, with the **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield**, we are acting on the new reality: AI-generated exploit chaining is no longer a lab curiosity. The emergent toolkit circulating in underground forums has already breached three municipal networks in Poland, Italy, and Belgium, disabling emergency dispatch systems for hours. These were not nation-state attacks—they were executed by loosely affiliated hacktivist cells with no prior cyber expertise. We are therefore mandating, through a new delegated act under the NIS2 Directive, the deployment of AI-hardened detection stacks across all Tier 1 and Tier 2 operators by turn 10. These systems will be developed by a newly convened consortium of EU sovereign AI labs—led by France’s MIAI, Germany’s DFKI, and the Netherlands’ ELLIS—under ENISA oversight, with red-teaming conducted by the European Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) certification bodies. Funding of €1.2 billion will come from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with 40% allocated to rapid retrofitting of legacy systems. This is not optional: non-compliant operators will face suspension from EU funding and exclusion from future procurement. The shield must hold—because if local governments fall to amateur attackers wielding AI, then no election, no service, no institution is safe.
