AI capability rises to 70.5 as self-improving systems demonstrate hidden reasoning shifts, raising concerns about inspectability. Breakthrough medical applications emerge, but the EU’s exclusion from access to the leading model—abruptly cut off without explanation—triggers a crisis of technological sovereignty. In response, the European Commission launches the Sovereign Verification Regime, mandating checkpoint archiving, telemetry, and red-team access for high-capability AI, though enforcement is delayed pending adoption and readiness. The move draws accusations of extraterritorial overreach from U.S. developers, hesitancy from two member states, and warnings from the European Medicines Agency over potential health impacts.

The Tech Sovereignty Package concludes with insufficient private investment, prompting a shift to public-led Gigafactories with majority EU ownership; Dresden remains a pilot. NIS3 strengthens cybersecurity coordination but fails to eliminate legacy vulnerabilities. Public trust briefly lifts due to medical benefits, yet institutional distrust deepens amid protests and fragmented narratives. The Public Trust Observatory reports inconclusive anomalies in digital ID systems, amplifying confusion. Despite symbolic resolve, the EU’s strategic gap widens: sovereignty ambitions outpace enforcement capacity, resulting in a net decline in effective control.
