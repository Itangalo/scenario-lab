## New measure
**EU Degraded-Mode Continuity Regime for Hospitals, Grid and Municipal Services**
Keeps essential services running on rationed EuroHPC and degraded European models through exercised fallback procedures, manual overrides, and protected maintenance spares, rather than waiting for new accelerators. This is Preparedness and resilience work, and why now is because with sovereignty at 16, political capital at 17, and empty gigafactory shells, autonomy this turn means preventing cascade when the next intrusion or cut-off hits rationed systems.

## Priority
EU Degraded-Mode Continuity Regime for Hospitals, Grid and Municipal Services, because it is the only deliverable form of our two-year commitment to hold autonomy and essential services when new compute will not arrive this turn.

## In practice
We run this through HERA, ENISA and the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking under the existing Critical Entities Resilience and NIS2 bases, with the Health and Energy Council formations: DG CNECT's allocation board ring-fences a continuity slice of EuroHPC for triage, grid balancing and municipal IT, while member-state operators rehearse degraded-mode playbooks — paper fallback for triage queues, islanding drills for grid, spare-pooling for transformers.

The Supply Compact (M7) stays as our external rationing track — DG TRADE chasing the Japanese pipeline and Seoul/Tokyo maintenance deals, using Anti-Coercion screening quietly to deter further diversion — but we stop promising arrivals we cannot enforce. Health ministers and union contacts get the same line: no ban on third-country automation we cannot implement, but protected staffing and maintenance guarantees for hospitals and ports while queues are rationed.
