### The jump
Autumn began with a demonstration that made spring timelines look naive. A frontier lab released video of systems sustaining multi-day engineering and biology workflows with minimal oversight, and leaked evaluations suggested untrained competence in areas no one had benchmarked. Engineers in Munich and Paris called it a step-change; within weeks procurement offices were rewriting requirements.

Days later a contested preprint claimed a genome model had helped produce a viable design for a human-infecting organism. Virologists argued over methods and accused the authors of both hype and recklessness. Inside health ministries, the argument landed differently: with powerful open weights already mirrored on private servers, even an imperfect recipe could not be recalled.

### A control result that actually shipped
Against that gloom, a control result from interpretability research proved unusually practical. It allowed operators to predict certain failure modes before they appeared and to certify containment properties on deployed critical-system agents rather than toy models. Labs adopted it to reduce their own incidents; ENISA pushed it onto hospital schedulers, grid balancers and registry assistants where Brussels ran systems itself.

### Shields declared done
By October the Commission declared two wartime programmes complete. The swarm-detection and auto-patching shield was now standard on hospital and energy contractors, and the non-belligerency survival apparatus — dispersed backups, pooled clean images, paper fallback drills — was pronounced operational. Power remained rationed to hours, trains ran on printed sheets, but hospitals stayed networked through a November intrusion wave that crippled unpatched municipalities.

The new biodefence pact moved slower. Wastewater sequencing expanded around ports and hospitals, HERA stockpiles were topped up, and joint health-police teams raided three suspected underground gene clinics in border towns. Licensed therapy deliveries continued under escort, but desperate patients still whispered about cross-border cures, and one raid found empty vials and a vanished practitioner.

Neutrality held, barely. Washington demanded telemetry, Beijing demanded components, and a cable repair ship waited weeks for escort in the Atlantic. The public, asked to queue for care and light, grew more hostile to anything labelled intelligent.
