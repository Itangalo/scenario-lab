## Priority  
Accelerate Sovereign Compute Corridor — because the cyber_major_incident and the plateau in capability growth reveal a narrowing window: offensive AI is already loose, but the frontier is not accelerating irreversibly, so the EU can still build sovereign capacity *before* it is locked out — but only if it moves now, and only if it clears the bottlenecks that stalled the Tech Sovereignty Package.

## In practice  
We are treating the current moment as a hinge: the capability plateau suggests that today’s ceiling is becoming tomorrow’s commodity, and the open-weight rise to 41.5 means offensive tools are already in circulation. But the attack that just landed was not state-grade — it was model-generated, not model-directed — and that tells us we still have time to build *our own* capability before fully autonomous threats emerge. The problem is not ambition — we have the Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package — but execution. Permits drag, grid access is blocked, and member states still treat compute as real estate, not strategic infrastructure.

So this turn, we are reprioritising. The new measure, *Accelerate Sovereign Compute Corridor*, does not replace the existing ones — it forces them forward. Under the Digital Markets Act and the upcoming Critical Digital Infrastructure Regulation, we are designating two sites — one in the north, one in the east — as EU Strategic Compute Nodes, with automatic permitting, direct grid access from renewable baseloads, and legal requirements that any hyperscaler selling frontier models in the EU must either colocate or license to a node. We are offering Poland and Romania fast-track Cohesion Fund disbursement and permanent hosting rights for a European AI Evaluation Agency outpost in exchange for speed. France will get co-leadership on energy integration. This is not about building faster than the US or China — it is about ensuring that when the next plateau breaks, the EU is not begging for access. The Cyber Resilience Surge continues, but its credibility depends on more than detection — it depends on having systems we control. That is the thread we are pulling now.
