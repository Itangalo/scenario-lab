Despite intensification of the Cyber Resilience Surge, national fragmentation persisted as Germany and Sweden continued autonomous red-teaming, undermining centralized coordination. Evidence of a capability plateau—marked by underperforming models and falling access costs—opened a narrow opportunity for sovereign advancement. In response, the European Commission proposed the *Accelerate Sovereign Compute Corridor*, a Category 4 initiative to build strategic compute hubs in Poland and Romania, conditional on hyperscaler compliance. However, the plan stalled due to unresolved demands on energy commitments, hosting concerns, and disputes over leadership, leaving it in consultation without formal adoption.

Existing initiatives remained gridlocked: the *Tech Sovereignty Package* stalled, and *InvestAI Gigafactories* faced ongoing permitting delays. Cyber defenses saw only partial improvements, while public trust eroded further amid service disruptions and the lingering welfare AI bias scandal, reinforcing perceptions of AI as a source of instability. Political capital declined under the sustained burden of three major initiatives and one priority, with a portfolio charge of −10.
