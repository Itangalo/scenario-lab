### The Fracture Deepens

A major AI-driven cyber intrusion—targeting hospital systems and transport networks—landed with alarming ease, exposing systemic fragility. The attack, generated using accessible model tooling, overwhelmed local defences before detection. This **cyber_major_incident** confirmed fears: offensive AI is operational, and Europe’s resilience lags. In response, the EU intensified the *Cyber Resilience Surge*, but national fragmentation persisted—Germany and Sweden maintained autonomous red-teaming, refusing central coordination.

### The Plateau and the Push

Signs of a **capability_plateau_evidence** emerged as a leading lab’s flagship model underperformed expectations, and prices for top-tier access dropped sharply. This suggested stagnation at the frontier, offering a narrow window for sovereign development. The European Commission drafted plans for a new *Accelerate Sovereign Compute Corridor* initiative—a large category 4 measure aimed at fast-tracking two strategic compute hubs in Poland and Romania, with legal mandates for hyperscaler compliance. The proposal was framed as a sovereignty imperative, not just infrastructure.

However, no final decision was made this turn. Poland demanded binding energy commitments, Romania expressed hesitation over hosting obligations, and France delayed grid integration plans pending co-leadership terms. The measure remains in consultation and has not been formally adopted. As such, it does not yet contribute to portfolio charge or political burden.

Meanwhile, the *Tech Sovereignty Package* remains stalled, and the *InvestAI Gigafactories* continue to face permitting delays. The Cyber Resilience Surge delivered partial hardening but fell short of full coverage.

Public trust eroded further after the cyber incident, with displaced workers and disrupted services dominating headlines. The welfare bias scandal continued to cast a shadow, and the proposed compute push was seen by critics as premature. Sentiment dipped as AI became synonymous with disruption, not progress.

### A Union Strained

Political capital declined due to the ongoing burden of three large measures and the existing priority, reflected in the portfolio charge of −10 (−3 for each active measure, −1 for priority). The draft compute corridor did not add to this cost, as it was not formally launched. 

Sovereignty decreased to 20.0: while `ai_capability` rose by +0.5, no new sovereign capacity came online, and there was no capacity event. Per the metric rule, this triggers a −1 penalty, resulting in a net decrease of −1 from the previous 21.0. This reflects continued dependency despite marginal capability growth. The window for action narrows, but the Union moves cautiously, not all at once.
