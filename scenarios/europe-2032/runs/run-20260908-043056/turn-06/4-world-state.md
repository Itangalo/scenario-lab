### The Weight of Enforcement

The EU doubles down on sovereign infrastructure, treating the stalled Romanian compute node as a national emergency. Invoking Article 122 TFEU, the Commission forces grid integration by Q3, unlocking €1.8bn in cohesion funds. A high-stakes energy swap is offered to Poland—French nuclear and German green hydrogen for hosting rights—but Warsaw remains silent, deepening east-west fissures. The Romanian site remains offline as of June, its symbolic weight now matched by political liability.

Sovereign Runtime Enforcement Zones move from doctrine to regulation, with binding technical standards issued and red-team exercises underway. Major hyperscalers signal resistance, citing operational friction, while some reroute workloads through third countries. The threat of DSA suspensions creates tension but no compliance yet. The Tech Sovereignty Package advances in the Competitiveness Council, but no new data centre reaches shovel-ready status, eroding confidence.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Slips

AI capability climbs as self-improving systems accelerate development cycles. The gap with safety widens: no new safeguards land on frontier models, and leaked reasoning tools enable more sophisticated misuse. Open-source repositories begin hosting stripped-down agents capable of autonomous cyber operations, pushing openweight capability upward despite no major new public release.

Resilience declines further as emergency systems remain vulnerable and no new protective measures take full effect. The recent ransomware wave exposed systemic fragility, and partial fixes do little to restore trust. Public sentiment dips as protests against AI infrastructure intensify, fueled by job losses in public administration and fears of uncontrolled automation.

Political capital erodes under the strain of five large concurrent initiatives. The priority on compute corridor acceleration yields no immediate gains, and the failure to bring Romania online undermines credibility. With capital now below 12, the EU risks losing control of its agenda. The U.S. administration takes office, ushering in a retrenchment posture—Washington turns inward, slowing frontier progress but offering no coordination.
