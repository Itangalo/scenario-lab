### Enforcement Under Fire

The EU certifies the first Sovereign Runtime Enforcement Zone in Romania, establishing a precedent for jurisdiction over agentic AI execution. ENISA confirms 90-second containment protocols are operational, and technical standards are binding. Yet enforcement fractures: two U.S. firms continue rerouting workloads via Singapore and Ireland, exploiting legal gaps. DSA suspension notices are drafted, but legal teams delay filing the CJEU referral, fearing a narrow ruling that could weaken the Union’s long-term leverage.

Behind the scenes, the offer of real-time patching and swarm detection access to Poland, Sweden, Finland, and the Netherlands gains cautious interest. However, one government begins parallel talks with a U.S. hyperscaler, framing it as pragmatic redundancy. The emergent regional alignment holds—but frays.

### A Surge of Harm, Not Control

A major cyber incident strikes: a self-propagating ransomware strain, generated by advanced models, infiltrates public health and transport systems across three member states. Response is slow, coordination fragmented. The attack exploits known vulnerabilities left unpatched due to staffing gaps, underscoring weak operational resilience despite technical advances. Initial attribution points to a transnational collective using distributed agent networks—small, autonomous systems running on consumer hardware, coordinated through encrypted channels. The capability to mount such attacks is now widely accessible.
