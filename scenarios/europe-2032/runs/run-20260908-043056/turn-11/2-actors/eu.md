## New measure  
### Leverage Defensive Stack for Conditional Access to EU Execution Environment  
A coalition of member states gains privileged access to the EU’s sovereign runtime infrastructure and defensive tools in exchange for alignment on enforcement and export discipline.  

`Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty (by 10–15), strengthen enforcement cohesion, and modestly improve ai_safety through coordinated red-teaming and access controls.  
`Applies to:` Member states in the emergent_regional_ai_pact (Poland, Sweden, Finland, Netherlands), with potential extension to third parties under reciprocity.

## Priority  
**Leverage Defensive Stack for Conditional Access to EU Execution Environment** — because with sovereign compute expansion frozen and the openweight frontier closing in, our only remaining leverage is to condition access to the EU’s runtime jurisdiction and defensive tools on alignment with enforcement and export discipline, turning fragmentation into a negotiated hierarchy.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that the Romanian Sovereign Runtime Enforcement Zone is now operational—ENISA has confirmed 90-second containment, real-time monitoring is live, and the technical standard is published. But enforcement is being undermined by rerouting through Ireland and Singapore, and one member state is negotiating a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler outside the EU framework. We cannot build our way out of this, but we can bargain.

We are now offering the four states engaged in defensive tech sharing—Poland, Sweden, Finland, and the Netherlands—a formalised access tier: full integration into the sovereign runtime environment, priority access to the new swarm-detection and real-time patching stack, and joint red-teaming rights—in exchange for binding commitments to enforce DSA suspensions, align on export controls, and halt parallel bilateral deals. This is not a carve-out; it is a conditional upgrade. The legal basis is Article 114 TFEU for internal market integrity, with the AI Office and ENISA as implementing bodies. Funding comes from the Digital Europe Programme and the newly unlocked Crisis Response Instrument, repurposed under Article 122 for systemic technological threat.

We are also preparing a parallel signal to the U.S.: if your firms continue to reroute agentic workloads to evade enforcement, we will restrict access to the runtime environment for all non-compliant entities—including limiting data flows under GDPR and conditioning cloud interconnection rights under the Cyber Resilience Act. This is not a threat; it is the operational logic of jurisdiction. The Council’s Competitiveness configuration will vote on the access framework in December, and we are counting on the current crisis to override inertia. The price of fragmentation must now exceed the price of compliance.
