## In practice
This turn marks the end of an era defined by construction — and the beginning of one defined by consequence. The Sovereign Compute Backbone is now physically complete in Kiruna and Saint-Malo, with full power draw authorised under RePowerEU grid overrides and dark fibre links secured to CERN and EMBL. The racks are powered. But they remain idle — not due to hardware, but because the Horizon Europe Fast-Track Talent Corridor failed to deliver. Despite Schengen-exempt status and fast-track citizenship offers, only twelve researchers have enrolled, most from smaller institutions with no prior frontier experience. The defector state’s CJEU challenge has frozen staffing directives, and national ministries cite constitutional sovereignty to delay approvals. We have the bones of sovereignty, but not the muscle.

Meanwhile, the world moved. A coordinated ransomware sweep — self-propagating, adaptive, and clearly model-generated — crippled hospital scheduling systems across three member states. Emergency care buckled. There was no single point of failure, only a cascade enabled by neglected patching, weak segmentation, and reliance on AI-maintained legacy code. At the same time, the bio_uplift_findings paper passed peer review, demonstrating that a fine-tuned public model could generate plausible human-infective vector designs from openly available data. It is not yet a weapon, but it is no longer science fiction.

We are shifting focus accordingly. With political capital at 5.0 and public sentiment at rock bottom, we cannot launch another industrial moonshot — but we can compel readiness. Under Article 12 of the NIS2 Directive and invoking the Civil Protection Mechanism, we are launching the National Resilience Surge. ENISA will issue binding hardening mandates within 30 days; HERA will expand its remit to include synthetic biology threat response. Funding will come from repurposed Gigafactory contingency reserves, and enforcement will be tied to Cohesion Fund disbursements. We are telling member states plainly: if your hospitals go dark during the next attack, it will not be because we lacked warning — it will be because you chose not to act.
