Global AI capability rises to 57.5, driven by advances in agent-tool integration and model interpretability, which also boost ai_safety and enable open-weight models to reach 47.5 through spillover. A major cyberattack exploiting AI-generated zero-days cripples critical infrastructure in three member states, exposing systemic vulnerabilities and undermining public trust. The Hardened Cyber Shield, still in development, fails to prevent the breach. In response, the European Commission drafts the EU Evaluation and Control Stack (EUCS), a proposed Category 5 oversight system for high-capability AI, but delays its formal launch due to legal and institutional debates. ENISA advances baseline standards for the Cyber Shield, with Germany and Poland agreeing to co-design failover protocols, though Italy’s implementation lags due to leadership gaps and telemetry deficiencies. InvestAI Gigafactories continue under restructured EIB financing, with new ASML-Intel pilot lines and reinforced energy-sharing agreements easing local opposition in Sweden and Brittany, but broader investment remains stagnant. The Tech Sovereignty Package is further weakened—its hyperscaler levy abandoned and private investment targets reduced—yet enforces data sovereignty and mandates onshore storage of model weights. Public sentiment declines sharply amid disinformation, job losses, and backlash against AI expansion, fueling calls for moratoriums. Hungary defects from EU alignment by signing a bilateral AI infrastructure deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, fracturing unity. Despite the crisis, the safety_breakthrough in interpretability provides a marginal resilience gain, improving oversight capacity across deployed systems.
