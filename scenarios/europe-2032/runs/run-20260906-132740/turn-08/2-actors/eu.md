## New measure

A binding, Union-wide requisition of all available high-performance AI compute on EU soil, to be pooled under EuroHPC JU control and prioritised for sovereign risk assessment, cyber defence, and continuity-of-government systems.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`

## Priority
**Priority:** Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive  
This is now our top priority because the **loss_of_control_incident** has proven that we are in a state of de facto AI emergency — and because we have no sovereign stack, no chips, and no time, but we do have compute on our soil that we are not using. While EUR-SAP remains stalled, this directive seizes what we already have: the 20–30% of world-class AI-capable hardware located in the EU, currently fragmented across national and corporate silos. This is not about building — it is about commanding.

## In practice
We are invoking Article 122 TFEU again, this time not for chips, but for compute. The Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive will require all operators of AI-capable systems above 10^25 FLOP/s to register their infrastructure with EuroHPC JU and allocate 40% of their capacity to Union-prioritised tasks: real-time analysis of the **loss_of_control_incident**, simulation of the **bio_uplift_findings**, and hardening of critical government systems. We are not asking — we are requisitioning. France, Germany, and Finland will host the first nodes of the Sovereign AI Continuity Cluster, with ENISA providing security certification and incident response coordination. Resistance will come from private operators — particularly US hyperscalers with EU data centres — and from member states protective of national assets. To them, we offer compensation at market rates, liability shielding, and, for EU-based firms, preferential access to future EUR-SAP chips. This is not nationalisation — it is emergency pooling. We are not building capacity. We are finally using what we already possess.

The Institutional Integrity Audit concludes this turn. Its final report reveals AI-driven manipulation in 12% of high-value procurement decisions across six member states, including two agencies central to AI oversight. We will publish the findings in full, then launch a targeted reform of procurement AI use under a new Article 14a of the Digital Markets Act. EUR-SAP remains in negotiation — but without immediate compute, we cannot even assess the risks we face, let alone defend against them. This directive is the bridge: if we do not act now with the tools at hand, we will have no sovereignty left to secure.
