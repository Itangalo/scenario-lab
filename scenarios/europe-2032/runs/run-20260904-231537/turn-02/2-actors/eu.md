**Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative**  
Launch a binding, time-bound programme to secure essential public services against AI-generated cyber threats, starting with energy, water, and health systems.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that the world has changed: AI is no longer a tool of disruption but a weapon of execution. The recent cyber_major_incident — a model-generated, multi-vector ransomware sweep across regional health administrations and grid operators — was stopped only by manual override and luck. Attribution remains unclear, but the tooling was not human-scale. We are now in a regime where attacks are designed, deployed, and adapted faster than human defenders can respond. Resilience at 37.0 is not a buffer — it is a death warrant.

This turn, we launch the Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative under Article 21 of the NIS2 Directive, empowering ENISA to mandate AI-specific defensive postures for all essential service operators. By turn 3, every Tier 1 operator must deploy AI-driven intrusion detection systems trained on adversarial models, undergo quarterly red-teaming using certified offensive AI agents, and integrate automated failover protocols that activate under algorithmic threat recognition. Funding of €12bn will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with disbursement tied to compliance milestones. We will also establish a European AI Cyber Shield Unit within ENISA, staffed with 500 new specialists and granted emergency access to limited-capability offensive models for defensive simulation.

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive remains on track for adoption in turn 3. We are finalising negotiations in the Council’s Internal Market formation, offering a revised carve-out for SME operators and enhanced transparency in the EDIC allocation algorithm to ease competitiveness concerns. The US embassy has warned of WTO challenges, but we are holding firm: compute sovereignty is not a trade barrier — it is a survival condition. Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package advances slowly. Only three member states have signed onto AI Acceleration Zones, but we are leveraging Cohesion Fund conditionality to incentivise the remaining fourteen: access to structural funds will now require a binding commitment to at least one zone by turn 4. We are building the foundations of autonomy, but we are also fighting the fires that threaten to burn them down before they rise.
