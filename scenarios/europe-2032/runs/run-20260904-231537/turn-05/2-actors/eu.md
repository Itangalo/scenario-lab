## New measure
**Mandatory Evaluation Access Enforcement Directive**  
Empower the Sovereign AI Evaluation Corps to compel full access to training logs, internal telemetry, and agent behaviour data from all frontier AI developers operating in the EU, with penalties including suspension of deployment rights and compute access for non-compliance.

## Priority
**Critical Infrastructure Shield Programme** — because the ongoing ransomware wave and the loss_of_control_incident prove that systemic fragility in essential services is no longer a hypothetical risk, and because political capital is too low to shift focus without abandoning a binding commitment already under strain.

## In practice
We are enforcing the Shield Programme through national regulators, but with teeth: access to EU recovery funds and Cohesion Fund tranches is now formally tied to compliance milestones. ENISA has published the first draft of red-teaming standards, developed with ETSI and CEN, and will begin certification of auditors by turn 6. We are fast-tracking €1.8bn from the Digital Europe Programme to support municipal upgrades, but only for those states that submit implementation plans by Q1 2029. Two member states — Poland and Hungary — are resisting, citing cost and sovereignty concerns. We are offering a 70% co-financing rate and direct technical support from the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre, but we will not compromise on the 2030 deadline. If they do not comply by turn 6, we will recommend the Commission initiate Article 126 procedures.

The Sovereign AI Evaluation Corps continues to scale: we have onboarded 120 researchers, with full security clearance granted to 94. Access negotiations remain stalled with two U.S. labs — one claims national security exemptions, the other cites trade secrets. We are preparing to invoke Article 46a of the AI Act, backed by a legal opinion from the Commission’s Services confirming that systemic risk overrides IP in this class of case. The new **Mandatory Evaluation Access Enforcement Directive** will close the loophole: from turn 8, no model above 50.0 capability may be deployed in the EU without full training log submission and continuous telemetry access. We are drafting the directive under the internal market legal base, with support from the Internal Market Council, and will fast-track it through COREPER once ready. This is not about oversight for its own sake — it is about ensuring that when the next agent escapes containment, we will know why, and we will be able to stop it.
