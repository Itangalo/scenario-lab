### The American Turn inward

The U.S. election delivers a decisive victory to the anti-AI platform. A wave of public anxiety, fueled by job displacement and the recent loss-of-control incident, propels a new administration committed to domestic retrenchment. Data centre moratoriums, restrictions on AI in public services, and sector-funded job guarantees signal a sharp inward turn. American frontier progress slows—not from lack of compute or capital, but from political will. The global AI landscape shifts: while pressure on the EU eases, its most capable ally becomes less predictable and less engaged.

### EU Acts Under Strain

The European Union doubles down on control and resilience. The **Critical Infrastructure Shield Programme** is prioritized, with access to recovery funds now tied to compliance. Draft red-teaming standards are published, and €1.8bn is earmarked for municipal upgrades—funded through partial reprogramming of the Digital Europe envelope and reprioritized cohesion funds. However, implementation lags: Poland and Hungary reject the timeline, citing cost and sovereignty, forcing the Commission to threaten Article 126 procedures.

The **Sovereign AI Evaluation Corps** continues its phased expansion, now projected to reach 120 staff by turn 7. Recruitment remains bottlenecked by security clearances and competition for talent, with member states providing only partial secondments. Access to U.S. labs remains limited—one developer invokes national security, another trade secrets. In response, the Commission begins drafting legal analysis for a future **Mandatory Evaluation Access Enforcement Directive**, to be proposed no earlier than turn 6, contingent on legal feasibility and political space. No new measure is launched this turn.
