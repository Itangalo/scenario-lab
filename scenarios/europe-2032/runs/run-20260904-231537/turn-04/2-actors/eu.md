## New measure  
**Critical Infrastructure Shield Programme**  
Launch a binding, time-bound hardening mandate for all Tier 1 and Tier 2 critical infrastructure operators — energy, water, health, transport, and municipal services — requiring AI-assisted intrusion detection, automated failover protocols, and quarterly red-teaming by certified EU entities.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to resilience by turn 7; reduce systemic fragility exposed by repeated ransomware waves; begin closing the gap between policy and operational security in municipal and regional systems  
`Applies to:` All critical infrastructure operators in member states classified as Tier 1 or Tier 2 under NIS2, with compliance enforced through national regulators and conditional access to EU recovery funds

## Priority  
**Critical Infrastructure Shield Programme** — because the second wave of AI-driven ransomware and the ongoing failure of the Hardening Initiative prove that voluntary measures are insufficient, and because the loss_of_control_incident makes clear that cascading failures in unprepared systems could turn an internal breach into a strategic crisis.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that resilience is not a policy goal — it is a condition of survival. The repeated ransomware attacks on municipal services, now confirmed to use AI-generated exploits tailored to known patch gaps, expose a deadly lag between directive and deployment. The Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative was underfunded, unenforced, and ignored. This time, we are binding compliance to fiscal reality: no Tier 1 operator will receive EU infrastructure grants or recovery funds without certified red-teaming and AI-augmented detection systems in place by turn 7. We are routing enforcement through national regulators, but with central oversight from ENISA, which will publish compliance scores quarterly. Funding of €3.2bn will come from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with technical standards developed in coordination with ETSI and CEN.

The Sovereign AI Evaluation Corps continues to stand up. We have secured hosting agreements with INRIA, Fraunhofer, and CNR, and the first 80 researchers are now onboarded under fast-track security clearance. Access negotiations with two major developers remain tense — one has offered telemetry but withheld training logs, citing IP — but we are preparing to invoke Article 46a on grounds of systemic risk. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive is now law, but its operationalisation is stalled at the ECJ. We are working with the European Commission’s Legal Service and member state representatives in the Internal Market Council to develop an interim framework that allows EDIC to begin allocating reserved compute on a provisional basis, pending final judgment. The Tech Sovereignty Package remains behind schedule; only seven member states have established AI Acceleration Zones. We are now conditioning Cohesion Fund tranches on zone implementation, with penalties for non-compliance starting turn 5. Sovereignty is not one project — it is the sum of our capacity, our control, and our ability to endure. We are rebuilding all three, under fire.
