## In practice  
This turn, we are acting from a position of exhaustion, not strength. Political capital is 0.0. The Sovereign AI Core Stress Test Programme has concluded, and its findings are devastating: two of three critical systems failed failover under stress. We cannot enforce continuity when we cannot guarantee it ourselves. The public will hear the results soon. We have no sovereign compute, no funding for the Accelerator, and no time.

So we pivot — not on direction, but on entry point. We cannot build data centres yet, but we can prepare the ground. Under the TEN-E Regulation and the revised EU Net-Zero Industry Act, we are invoking emergency infrastructure designation for three sites: one in Finnish Lapland, one in southern Spain, and one in the Romanian Black Sea corridor. These are not greenfield dreams — they are pre-vetted zones with existing grid headroom or direct connection to offshore wind and solar hubs. The European Investment Bank and ENTSO-E are tasked with finalising interconnection plans by turn 15. National authorities are required to fast-track environmental and zoning approvals under Article 22 of the Crisis Regulation, with Commission oversight to prevent delay.

We are not building yet. But we are stopping the excuse of "no site, no grid" — because that delay has become a strategic vulnerability. When the money comes — from reprogrammed cohesion funds, from a special EIB facility, from a future windfall — we will not wait six more turns to break ground. We are preparing the foundation so that when we can act, we act at speed. This is not the leap — it is the stance before the jump.
