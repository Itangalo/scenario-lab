### The Shock and the Response

A model-generated ransomware sweep, rapidly adapted and self-propagating, disables emergency dispatch systems in three member states. Recovery is slow, attribution unclear. The attack marks a **capability_jump**, pushing `ai_capability` by +3 to 74.0 — a leap in autonomous offensive planning. Defensive posture lags, and `ai_safety` plummets by −12 as the gap between deployment speed and assurance becomes indefensible.

In response, the EU launches the **Emergency Continuity Response Initiative**, a small category 6 measure under Article 122 TFEU, mobilizing €1.8bn in emergency funds. Cross-border teams deploy to 17 critical sites, repairing misconfigured air-gapped nodes and conducting live drills. In Thessaloniki, command integration improves performance; in two reluctant capitals, cost-sharing unlocks access. The effort stabilizes key systems and delivers visible action — but patchwork fixes do not restore trust.

The Continuity Assurance Framework, though now law, yields only partial operational benefit. With the cyber incident exposing systemic fragility, resilience gains +5 from the new initiative and +2 from ongoing stabilization, offset by no further penalties. Net: +7 → 54.0.

Yet political capital remains frozen at 0.0. The portfolio charge — Frontier Access Guarantee (−3), Emergency Continuity Response (−2), priority (−1) — totals −6. No proposal bonus applies: while the measure answers a recent incident, it is small and reactive, not a new strategic shift. `eu_political_capital` stays floored.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` drops to 21.0: −1 from `ai_capability` rising ≥2, and no capacity event or measure completion offsets it. 

`openweight_capability` rises to 55.0, reflecting intensified diffusion of offensive tooling across state and non-state actors, building on prior gains and exploiting newly demonstrated attack vectors. The increase of +3 aligns with the pace of frontier advancement, consistent with normal spillover following a capability jump, but falls short of a breakout event. No leak, stolen weights, or catastrophic infrastructure failure is observed; the diffusion is rapid but bounded, staying within the expected rate of open replication and adaptation.

Public sentiment ticks up slightly (+2) as emergency response is seen as competent — but not transformative. The mood remains anxious.
