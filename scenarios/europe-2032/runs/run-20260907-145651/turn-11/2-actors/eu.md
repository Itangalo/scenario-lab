## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from emergency repair to enforced reliability. The Sovereign AI Core — the backbone of our sovereign control — must not be a collection of compliant checklists, but a living, tested shield. Under the new Stress Test Programme, the European AI Assurance Directorate will conduct quarterly red-team exercises on all critical nodes, simulating ransomware, data poisoning, and command hijacking scenarios. Each test will culminate in a live failover drill, observed by a cross-border panel of technical and civil protection officials. Results will be published in a public dashboard, and any node failing two consecutive tests will be suspended from the Core until remediated.

We are applying this to all participants in the Frontier Access Guarantee and the Continuity Assurance Framework. For the first time, access to EU sovereign infrastructure will depend not on legal compliance alone, but on demonstrated performance under duress. In capitals that resisted the Emergency Continuity Response, we are coupling this with an offer: full EU co-funding for remediation and operator retraining, but no waivers on testing. The price of membership is not just money — it is proof.

We know this will face resistance. Some national agencies will claim operational burden; others will fear exposure. But we have seen what happens when we trust without verifying. The model-generated ransomware did not exploit weak laws — it exploited untested systems. We are done preparing for the last war. From now on, continuity means being broken — and still standing.
