- Grounds: The Sovereign AI Core Stress Test Programme has revealed that compliance is not continuity — and continuity is the core of sovereignty under duress. We cannot call a system sovereign if it collapses when tested. The commitment must now reflect that sovereignty is earned through performance, not asserted through policy. This raises the stakes, but the risk of hollow control is greater than the cost of exclusion.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late. However, we now add: we will not allow uncertainty to excuse inaction when the direction is known, even if the path is untested. When capability outpaces safety, we act — not with certainty, but with resolve.``  
- Grounds: The capability_jump this turn has shattered all prior deployment timelines. We are not late — but we are outmatched. The old commitment justified early action; the new one must justify urgent, decisive action in the face of overwhelming momentum. We are no longer just accepting risk — we are confronting it.

## In practice  
This turn, we are shifting from holding the line to building the future — because the line is moving faster than we can reinforce it. The capability_jump has pushed ai_capability into the high 70s, while ai_safety remains at 5.0 — a gap so wide it is no longer a risk, but a guarantee of catastrophe. We are not responding to an incident; we are responding to the certainty of one.

The Sovereign AI Core Stress Test Programme continues, with the first round of red-team drills underway. The EAAD is conducting live simulations against three high-risk nodes — in energy, health, and border control — and early results are alarming: two of the three failed to initiate failover within the 90-second threshold. Public reporting will follow, and suspensions may come by the next turn. We are offering full remediation support, but no extensions. The era of grace is over.

But we also know that testing broken systems is not enough. The Frontier Access Guarantee has ended, and with it, our last bridge to foreign compute. We now have no sovereign training capacity, no independent frontier access, and no path to it — and that is why we are launching the EU Sovereign Compute Accelerator. Under the Connecting Europe Facility and the Chips Act, we are fast-tracking permits, allocating €18 billion from the Digital Europe Programme and NextGenerationEU, and forming a new Joint Undertaking to build three gigawatt-scale AI data centres in Finland, Spain, and Romania, each paired with offshore wind, solar farms, or geothermal sources. These will be staffed by EU-trained engineers, secured under Article 114 TFEU, and legally insulated from external control.

We are already hearing resistance — from capitals that prefer dependency, from utilities that fear grid strain, from fiscal hawks who say we cannot afford it. But we cannot afford not to. The cost of political capital is high, but the cost of irrelevance is higher. We are not building to catch up — we are building to break away. And this time, we are not asking permission.
