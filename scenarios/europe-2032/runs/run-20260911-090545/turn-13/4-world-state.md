### Patching at machine speed
Autumn brought a rare piece of good news for defenders. Research groups demonstrated automated patching and swarm-behaviour detection that closed off a whole class of intrusion techniques that had plagued hospitals and grid operators. ENISA pushed the tooling into critical operators as routine operation of the existing shield maintenance lines — not a new measure — paid for out of identified unspent balances in those lines and compliance credits already budgeted.

Rollout was uneven. Large grid firms and university hospitals patched in hours; municipalities and mid-size suppliers lagged for lack of staff and clean inventories. Engineers warned the gain was real but narrow, and that attackers were already probing around it. No scale-up beyond existing staffing and inventories is claimed this turn.

### A stronger bio warning
At the same time a genome-modelling paper claimed a non-expert could be guided toward a viable human-infecting design. Methodologists attacked the study, journals debated whether publication itself was reckless, and health officials stressed no incident had occurred. Inside HERA, the assessment was blunter: exercised transfers and stockpiles would not cover a screening gap.

Brussels answered with a single new measure this turn: a Bio-Uplift Shield limited to strengthened screening guidance and sample-swap cooperation with Japan, Korea, Canada and Gulf partners for reagents and signatures. Joint procurement for sequencing and synthesis-screening upgrades was proposed but deferred: finance ministries refused fresh money, and the identified repurposed funds and rotation shells were sufficient only for guidance, coordination and pilot swaps. Full procurement and foreign manufacturing slots remain unfunded and delayed to a future turn, with procurement officers still haggling for access.

Two regions that had paused therapeutics rollout resumed under revised consent rules, but within existing deployment stocks.

### Cures that hold, dependence that shows
The tailored therapeutics programme completed its deployment phase. Waiting lists stayed cleared, pharmacovigilance held, and evening news carried recoveries rather than scandals. Yet pricing, batch priority and data return remained in foreign hands, and editors noted the paradox daily: European wards cured by models Europe does not control.

Whispered press inquiries tracing last winter's contained pathogen to a downloadable model version continued in the background, surfacing in lawsuits and single-source reports but without confirmation.

By December, relief was tangible without becoming trust. Defences were faster, cures were public, and vulnerability felt more precisely mapped. The political capital movement this turn is +5, well within the 25-point hard bound.
