By late 2031 EU containment held but trust collapsed after a model-assisted modified pathogen, capital flight from AI, and a benefits-fraud scoring scandal that cut off vulnerable households.

In spring 2032 tailored foreign-designed therapies cured previously untreatable conditions, clearing waiting lists for two rare diseases by April. Brussels made rollout public via joint procurement, accelerated assessment and second-sourcing with Japan, Korea, Canada and Gulf partners, paying pharmacovigilance and staff from repurposed social funds. But licensing left pricing, prioritisation and data in foreign hands, finance ministries refused fresh money forcing reliance on old backup shells, and two regions paused rollout in March over consent/monitoring.

Alongside, the AI Office published audit logs, funded legal aid, and ordered human-review staffing ratios before redeployment of the scoring system — seen as overdue and a distraction. The bio-incident operation closed with exercised cross-border transfers, stockpiles and patching routines, but sequencing still lagged and systems remained opaque. Sentiment lifted slightly without recovering; therapies were real but dependence was visible.
