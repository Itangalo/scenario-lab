### The Fracture Widens

AI capability advances by 2.0 points this turn to 92.0, continuing its steady climb into the terminal zone. At this level, the most advanced systems operate with reasoning pathways too opaque and fast for human oversight, even in theory. No new safeguards have scaled to match, and the global oversight deficit deepens.

The EU faces a crisis of access after a leading developer abruptly cuts off services to EU institutions and critical operators. Hospitals relying on AI diagnostics, energy ministries managing grid stability, and research agencies running simulations are suddenly disconnected. The reason given is vague, the reversal impossible. This exclusion lands as both an operational shock and a strategic humiliation.

In response, the European Commission activates emergency protocols, fast-tracking integration between the Open Science Resilience Initiative’s research hubs and national agencies. Germany, France, and Italy issue binding mandates that only auditable AI systems may be used in critical infrastructure—effectively banning uninterpretable frontier models unless they meet strict transparency standards. Interim replacements, based on open protein folding models, are deployed in healthcare, proving slower but reliable and compliant.

The EU joins a new joint threat response pact, enabling real-time cyber and bio intelligence sharing. This boosts resilience, allowing faster detection and coordinated mitigation. However, the move underscores dependence: the Union gains protection only by accepting inclusion on others’ terms.

Public sentiment remains stagnant at 27.0—neither swayed by the resilience gains nor fully alarmed by the access denial. Political capital, already strained, drops to 5.0 under the ongoing cost of large initiatives and the burden of crisis management. With no new measure proposed, and the priority unchanged, the Union scrambles to adapt rather than lead.

Sovereignty slips to 22.0, hit by the access denial event and the continued rise in global capability without offsetting gains in domestic control. The EU’s leverage remains narrow, its response reactive. The lesson is clear: resilience without sovereign capacity is fragile, and trust in foreign systems is a liability.
