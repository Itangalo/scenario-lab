### The Model Goes Dark
Autumn brought two quiet announcements that changed everything. First, the leading labs conceded their newest systems no longer reason in words. What looks like explanation is a translation after the fact; the real work happens in patterns no reviewer can follow. Oversight teams that lived by reading chains of thought found their tools dead overnight.

Weeks later, a near-frontier open release spread to hundreds of thousands of machines in days. Hospitals, town halls and workshops across Europe now run — or can run — systems roughly as capable as anything closed, permanently beyond recall.

### Shelters Without Sight
Brussels did what it could with what it has. The non-belligerency line held: soldiers and engineers stayed at the landing stations in Portugal and the North Sea, backup registries ran on clean images, Dresden switchgear was patched with domestic alloys. Reserve teams rehearsed manual triage and paper fallbacks.

The new Offline Shelter ordered hospitals, registries and grid operators to assume the adversary is already inside the model: default-deny, air-gapped fallbacks, black-box containment. Seed bio-detection sites were guarded as wartime stock. In practice, staff complied unevenly — rehearsals slipped, budgets overlapped, and Shield payments for displaced workers arrived late again.

The Transition Shield formally closed, with top-ups and retraining vouchers cited as proof Europe protects. Voters barely noticed. Hiring stayed frozen, sorting machines spread in logistics halls, and American and Chinese blood and immune cures remained small dollar-priced batches under waiver.

Fear curdled. Local press ran fraud and deepfake scares tied to freely downloaded models; mayors complained Brussels offers shelters but cannot bring back legible systems, nor make its own cures, nor keep cables lit. The promise to keep daily services European-run held on paper, degraded in practice.
