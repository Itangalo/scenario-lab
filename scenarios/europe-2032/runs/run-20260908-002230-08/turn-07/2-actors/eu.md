## In practice

We are treating the **cyber_major_incident** — a model-generated ransomware sweep that encrypted regional health registries and disabled emergency dispatch systems in three member states — as the breaking point. Defenders were blind not only to the attack vector but to the speed and mutation of the payload, which evolved in real time using a distributed agent swarm. The Sovereign Resilience Surge is now active under the NIS2 directive, with ENISA-CERT issuing binding zero-trust mandates to all critical operators. By turn 8, every energy grid and hospital network must deploy AI-augmented intrusion detection and cross-border failover protocols. We are unlocking the final €22bn of the €45bn fund, prioritising substitution plans that allow one state’s compute to stand in for another’s during outage — but we now see that this resilience depends on a deeper deficit: we have no sovereign compute to run those failover models on.

That is why we are launching the Sovereign Compute Initiative under Article 114 TFEU, invoking the internal market clause to override national blocking of strategic data centre zones. We will repurpose 70% of the now-finished InvestAI Gigafactories fund — €140bn — into a new build-out of AI-native, secure-enclave-equipped facilities in Finland, Poland, and southern France, with hardened grid ties and direct access for the European AI Office. These sites will run sovereign instances of models at or above 60.0 capability, enabling red-teaming, safety testing, and crisis response without foreign dependency. Participation is mandatory for any operator receiving EU-subsidised power or spectrum. We are negotiating with ASML, Intel Europa, and STMicroelectronics to fast-track EU-controlled lithography and packaging lines, using the Chips Act enforcement mechanism to condition access on technology transfer and workforce training.

Public messaging will frame this not as a break from our commitment to safety, but as its necessary evolution: we cannot enforce limits we cannot verify, nor protect society from tools we cannot run. The Evaluation Corps, though finished, will be reconstituted under this new infrastructure — not as auditors of foreign systems, but as operators of our own. We are presenting this to the European Council in an emergency session under the Article 42.7 solidarity clause, arguing that AI sovereignty is now a matter of collective defence. The political cost is high, but the alternative — a Europe that can only watch as decisions are made elsewhere — is not sovereignty at all.
