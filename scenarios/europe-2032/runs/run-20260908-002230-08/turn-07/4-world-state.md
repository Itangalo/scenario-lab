### The Breaking Point

A coordinated AI-generated ransomware swarm cripples emergency dispatch and regional health registries across three member states. The attack mutates in real time, evading detection by leveraging distributed agent coordination and zero-day exploits produced autonomously. Response is fragmented, and recovery takes weeks. The incident exposes a fatal gap: the EU can mandate resilience, but without sovereign compute, it cannot run the very tools needed to defend itself.

Global AI capability advances to 64.5, driven by stabilised agent architectures that operate without interpretable reasoning. Open-weight models reach 53.0, as simplified safety techniques are replicated and bioengineering frameworks spread further into non-state hands.

The EU declares a strategic inflection. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Sovereign Evaluation Corps** officially complete, but their impact is overshadowed. The Gigafactories deliver compute capacity, yet it remains unconnected and underutilised due to legal and coordination delays. The Evaluation Corps, though formally established, still lacks access to frontier models — its authority symbolic without execution power.

In response, the EU launches the **Sovereign Compute Initiative**, a large category 4 measure, invoking internal market powers to override national resistance. €140bn is redirected to build AI-native secure facilities in Finland, Poland, and southern France. However, early pushback emerges: two member states challenge the use of Article 114 TFEU, and ASML hesitates on technology transfer, citing U.S. export sensitivities.

The **Sovereign Resilience Surge** continues, but uneven implementation weakens its effect. ENISA-CERT’s mandates face delays, and resilience declines slightly as the cyber incident overwhelms response capacity.

Public sentiment drops to 25.0 amid fear and frustration. The cyberattack is widely framed as a failure of sovereignty, not just security. Labour unrest grows in tech-adjacent sectors.

The U.S. **CONSOLIDATION** posture remains in force, deepening dependency and constraining leverage.

### Strategic Overreach

The EU’s pivot is bold, but political capital erodes. The portfolio charge is heavy, and the new initiative, while justified, stretches credibility and cohesion.
