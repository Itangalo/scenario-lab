## New measure

**Frontier Access Corridor**  
Establish a legally binding, technically secured pathway for the European AI Office to access and run frontier AI models—whether closed or open—on sovereign infrastructure, bypassing foreign-controlled APIs and cloud dependencies.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect: eu_ai_sovereignty +15, ai_safety +10 (via direct red-teaming and verification), eu_political_capital -3 (due to legal friction with developers and third states)`  
`Applies to: EU institutions, the European AI Office, and certified public research entities, with compulsory cooperation from any provider receiving market access under the AI Act or EU subsidies`

## Priority

**Sovereign Compute Initiative** — because without physical, legal, and operational control over the compute that runs frontier models, every other measure—resilience, evaluation, enforcement—is a gesture, not governance: we cannot defend what we cannot run, nor verify what we cannot touch.

## In practice

The Sovereign Compute Initiative is now live, with €140bn redirected from the completed InvestAI Gigafactories fund. We are invoking Article 114 TFEU to fast-track site approvals in Finland, Poland, and southern France, overriding national objections on grounds of internal market integrity and collective security. Legal teams at the Commission are preparing infringement proceedings against the two member states blocking land rezoning, while bilateral negotiations with ASML and Intel Europa are underway to secure EU-controlled lithography nodes under the Chips Act’s national security clause. We are offering STMicroelectronics expanded R&D grants and workforce training subsidies in exchange for packaging-line prioritisation. These sites will host secure enclaves with direct access for the European AI Office, enabling red-teaming of models at or above 60.0 capability without reliance on foreign cloud infrastructure.

The Sovereign Resilience Surge finishes this turn: binding NIS2 mandates are now in force for all critical operators, requiring AI-augmented intrusion detection, zero-trust architecture, and cross-border failover protocols. ENISA-CERT has activated mutual aid across 18 member states, and we have unlocked the final €22bn of funding to deploy substitution plans—ensuring no single point of compute failure can collapse emergency response. But we now see that resilience without sovereign execution capacity is like having fire codes but no fire engines: the rules are sound, but the tools to enforce them are missing. That is why we are launching the **Frontier Access Corridor**—a Category 5 measure that compels cooperation from any AI provider accessing the EU market. This is not a request: it is a condition of operation. Providers must either deploy sovereign instances of their models on EU-controlled infrastructure or risk suspension under the AI Act. We are designing the technical interface now with the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking and the AI Office’s new Red Cell unit, ensuring cryptographic verification and auditability without compromising security. This is not about nationalisation—it is about operational sovereignty. We will not be left blind again.
