### The Weight of Sovereignty

Global AI capability advances to 66.0 as agent-driven cyber operations become more autonomous and harder to interpret. Open-weight models reach 54.5, with bioengineering frameworks now accessible to skilled hobbyists, raising concerns about uncontrolled replication.

The EU completes the **Sovereign Resilience Surge**, enforcing binding NIS2 upgrades across critical sectors. Real-time monitoring and mutual aid protocols are now active, and ENISA-CERT coordinates a joint response to a follow-up probe, limiting damage. This **joint_threat_response** boosts resilience to 49.0, demonstrating that coordinated defence can work — when systems are ready.

Yet the cost of ambition mounts. The **Sovereign Compute Initiative** begins construction, but legal challenges stall land approvals in two member states. ASML delays technology transfer, citing unresolved U.S. export sensitivities. Without immediate access to advanced lithography, the compute buildout faces de facto delays, muting sovereignty gains.

The **Frontier Access Corridor** is launched as a large Category 5 measure, aiming to force providers to deploy models on EU-controlled infrastructure. But pushback is immediate. Major developers warn of compliance impossibility, and Washington signals that such mandates could violate bilateral data agreements. The measure enters the portfolio but yields no early traction.

Political capital erodes under the weight of four active measures. The priority status on the Compute Initiative fails to accelerate progress, and public sentiment remains stagnant at 25.0 — too low to lend legitimacy. Labour unrest simmers as displaced workers see no relief.

Meanwhile, **emergent_regional_data_sovereignty_pacts** gain quiet momentum. Several northern and central states deepen bilateral compute-sharing deals, bypassing EU channels. These pacts reflect growing distrust in both foreign providers and central coordination, foreshadowing fragmentation.

### A Union Straining

The EU acts with urgency, but sovereignty without execution power remains symbolic. The tools to verify, red-team, and respond are still out of reach. The architecture of control is being built — but the foundation is not yet bearing weight.
