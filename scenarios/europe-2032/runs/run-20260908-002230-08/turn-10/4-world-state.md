### Stalled Foundations, Deepening Crisis

The EU marks the formal completion of the Sovereign Compute Initiative, but the victory is hollow. While Finland hosts a partially operational node, Poland’s site remains frozen in legal limbo, and ASML still blocks critical technology transfers under U.S. export constraints. The much-touted compute independence exists more in law than in silicon.

In response, the Commission escalates, framing Poland’s inaction as a breach of solidarity and preparing an Article 7(1) opinion — a move met with backlash from Warsaw and quiet unease in other member states wary of central overreach. Meanwhile, the promised STMicroelectronics acceleration remains on paper, with no confirmed delivery timeline.

The Sovereign Red Line Enforcement Network enters planning, with France and Germany designated as first hosts for the AI Office’s Red Cell. Yet without full compute access, the network’s red-teaming authority lacks teeth. ENISA begins drafting response protocols, but internal assessments warn that simulating autonomous threats in real time remains out of reach.

Public trust plummets after a damning ombudsman report reveals an AI-driven social insurance system in Belgium denied thousands of claims based on flawed risk profiling — a systemic failure echoing across national borders. The **automated_decision_scandal** ignites protests and fuels anti-AI sentiment, with parties across the bloc demanding moratoriums on public-sector AI.

Resilience erodes as health systems remain vulnerable, and the gap between capability and safety widens. Global AI advances slightly, driven by continued refinement in autonomous cyber tools, though the U.S. lab pause tempers expectations of a sharp jump.

### A Union at Breaking Point

The EU acts with urgency, but its foundations are cracking. Sovereignty slips, political capital drains, and the public turns hostile — not because the strategy is unseen, but because it fails to land.
