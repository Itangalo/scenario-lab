Global AI capability reaches 68.5, marginally increasing through refinement of autonomous cyber tools, though U.S. labs pause further offensive demonstrations. The EU formally completes the Sovereign Compute Initiative, but implementation remains fragmented: Finland activates a partial node while Poland’s site stalls due to legal disputes and ASML’s continued withholding of critical technology under U.S. export controls. The Commission responds by initiating Article 7(1) proceedings against Poland, triggering political backlash and exposing fractures in EU solidarity. STMicroelectronics’ promised acceleration fails to materialize, leaving compute capacity shortfalls unresolved.

The Sovereign Red Line Enforcement Network advances to planning, with France and Germany selected as host states for the AI Office’s Red Cell, yet the network lacks operational credibility due to insufficient compute access and unresolved technical gaps. ENISA drafts response protocols but acknowledges that real-time simulation of autonomous threats remains unattainable. Public trust collapses to 18.0 following the **automated_decision_scandal**, in which an AI-driven social insurance system in Belgium wrongfully denies thousands of claims, sparking cross-border outrage, protests, and rising demands for public-sector AI moratoriums. The crisis deepens perceptions of EU overreach and incompetence, further eroding resilience. As health systems remain exposed and the gap between AI capability and safety widens, sovereignty efforts gain legal traction but lose public and political support, revealing a union struggling to translate strategy into functional reality.
