### The Tide Rises

Global AI capability advances to 68.0 as a wave of autonomous cyber operations, driven by agent systems with opaque reasoning, spreads across public health networks in three member states. The attack exploits chained vulnerabilities at machine speed, overwhelming human defenders. Though patches are rolled out through ENISA-CERT coordination, the damage is done: hospitals face days of disruption, and public trust erodes further. This **cyber_major_incident** underscores a fatal gap — the EU can detect and respond, but cannot simulate or counteract frontier models in real time.

A **capability_jump** compounds the crisis. A U.S. lab demonstrates a system that autonomously discovers and weaponises zero-day exploits in under an hour, marking a discontinuous leap in offensive cyber capability. While general competence rises only modestly, the strategic balance shifts. Defenders are now visibly outpaced.

The **Frontier Access Corridor** formally completes, establishing a legal right to model access — but with no sovereign compute to host them, the right is hollow. The **Sovereign Compute Initiative** remains stalled, despite invoking Article 114 TFEU to override land-use objections in Finland and Poland. Legal pushback intensifies, and ASML continues to withhold critical technology transfer, citing unresolved U.S. export controls.

In response, the EU launches the **Sovereign Red Line Enforcement Network**, a large Category 4 measure aiming to build distributed, secure compute enclaves for real-time red-teaming and containment. Funded from emergency reserves and deployed via EuroHPC, it is framed as existential. Yet its ambition strains credibility: six nodes across member states, red-teaming authority, and mandatory integration — all by turn 12.

Public sentiment holds at 25.0. Labour unrest simmers, and the new measure is seen as reactive overreach. Regional data pacts deepen as northern and central states quietly expand bilateral compute-sharing, bypassing EU channels.

### Sovereignty Without Foothold

The Union acts with urgency, but its sovereignty agenda lacks grounding. Legal authority expands, but physical and technical control lags. The tide is rising — and the walls are not yet built.
