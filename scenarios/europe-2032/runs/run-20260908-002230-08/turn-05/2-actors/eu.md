## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI evaluation and enforcement capacity to anchor European strategic freedom``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The global AI frontier has crossed 60.0, enabling systems to execute multi-day professional projects with minimal supervision. At this level, capability outpaces control unless enforcement is independent, technically credible, and legally binding. The export_control_escalation and supply_chain_coercion events confirm that external powers now treat access to AI capacity as a lever of influence — and that sovereignty cannot rest on infrastructure alone. The bio_uplift_findings and emergent_labour_displacement_wave show that harm is no longer theoretical, but public trust cannot be rebuilt through restriction alone: it requires visible, autonomous authority to verify safety. The EU’s current build-up of compute and supply-chain resilience is necessary but insufficient without the power to assess, challenge, and enforce standards on systems that operate in or affect Europe. This shift from infrastructure to authority is not a retreat from industrial ambition, but its necessary counterpart: without sovereign evaluation, we are building roads no one can drive on safely, and bridges that no one will cross.

## In practice
We are treating the end of the ASML Strategic Shield Initiative as a moment of reckoning: the Union has secured a legal framework to resist extraterritorial pressure on its semiconductor exports, but the supply_chain_coercion event proves that framework is not yet power. The Belgian maintenance hub remains a promise on paper, and Washington’s tightening grip on ASML’s entire product line — including legacy tools — shows that Europe’s leverage is not in its laws alone, but in what it can do when those laws are defied. We are therefore shifting emphasis from legal shield to technical enforcement: the new EU Sovereign Evaluation Corps will be the operational arm of our authority, staffed with 300 formally trained evaluators by turn 7, drawn from national cybersecurity agencies, academic verification labs, and the private sector under strict conflict-of-interest rules. It will operate under Article 16 TFEU and the revised AI Act enforcement provisions, with direct access to model weights, training data summaries, and agent-behaviour logs for any system above 50.0 capability.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, with retrofit funding now flowing to three sites under enhanced physical and cyber protections. However, we are conditioning further disbursements on participation in the new Evaluation Corps’ red-teaming programme: any operator seeking EU-subsidised compute must allow pre-deployment testing of its most capable systems. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being renegotiated in light of backlash_physical: we are introducing local benefit clauses — data centres must fund regional retraining hubs and grid resilience upgrades — to counter the narrative that AI infrastructure serves only distant shareholders. In parallel, we are activating the Anti-Coercion Instrument in informal coordination with Japan and South Korea, aiming to form a coalition that can collectively resist unilateral export controls on dual-use technologies. This is not confrontation, but deterrence: we will not be blackmailed into dependency. Public messaging centres on the bio_uplift_findings — not to incite fear, but to show that the same techniques that can design life can also be checked, if we build the institutions to do it.
