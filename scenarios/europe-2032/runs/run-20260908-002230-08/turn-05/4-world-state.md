### A Threshold Crossed, a Union Under Strain

Global AI capability advances to 62.0 as frontier labs integrate goal-stability verification across training runs, enabling longer-horizon agent operations in cyber and materials design. Open-weight models benefit from early adoption of simplified safety techniques, reaching 50.0 — a moderate gain reflecting growing community replication efforts, but still far from the frontier and limited by compute access and fragmented collaboration.

The EU announces the Sovereign Evaluation Corps, a proposed body to assert independent oversight over high-capability AI. The legal framework is under development, but recruitment has not yet begun, and no lab has committed to granting access to model weights. The proposal signals intent, but operational capacity will take time to build, with full implementation expected no sooner than next turn.

Meanwhile, the ASML Strategic Shield Initiative officially concludes — a legal framework exists, but Washington’s pressure on Dutch exports intensifies, now reaching legacy tools. The Union’s leverage remains symbolic. In response, Brussels activates the Anti-Coercion Instrument in quiet coordination with Tokyo and Seoul, but no immediate concessions follow.

Public sentiment sours further. The emergent wave of job losses in legal and administrative sectors fuels union protests, while backlash_physical escalates: data centre construction halts in Bavaria after sabotage. The Commission’s retraining clauses come too late to calm anger.

Resilience dips as cyber defences prove uneven — a near-miss at a Baltic grid operator exposes gaps. No major incident lands, but preparedness is questioned. The Cyber Shield Directive is fully implemented in only half the bloc.

The 2028 U.S. election concludes with a clear outcome: a narrow victory for the candidate advocating strategic AI competition and expanded export controls. The result is certified, and the transition process begins, though the administration will not take office until next turn. The EU updates its strategic assessments accordingly, anticipating increased friction on technology policy.
