### A Framework Without Power

The EU formally cancels the Sovereign Red Line Enforcement Network, acknowledging its enclaves in France and Germany remain inert—dark compute, no red-teaming, no response capacity. In its place, the Conditional Activation Framework is elevated as doctrine: no audit, no activation. The Public-Sector AI Safety Audit Programme remains the Union’s sole priority, with France and Germany nearing final submission, but Italy drags on data access disputes, and Hungary refuses to engage.

To break the deadlock, the Commission launches the **Fiscal Leverage Mechanism for AI Compliance**, withholding emergency fund access and fast-tracked semiconductor benefits from non-compliant states. The tool targets Hungary’s U.S. hyperscaler deal and Poland’s obstruction of coordination efforts. Yet the mechanism faces immediate resistance: Warsaw calls it blackmail, Budapest labels it overreach, and Rome demands legal clarity. No member state folds.

Markets and allies watch closely. The middle-power semiconductor coalition expresses concern that internal fragmentation undermines the EU’s credibility as a strategic partner. Informal bilateral compute-sharing pacts among Northern and Central states grow, bypassing stalled EU mechanisms—a sign of both preparedness and disintegration.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

Global AI capability rises to 71.5, driven by increasingly autonomous cyber tools and self-improving research pipelines. Open weights reach 59.0, narrowing the gap. No safety gains land on deployed systems; assurance remains shallow, and no breakthroughs emerge. Resilience dips slightly as health IT disruptions persist, underscoring systemic fragility.

Public sentiment holds at 19.0—no major incident, but no visible progress either. Political capital erodes further: cancelling a flagship measure costs dearly, even one that failed. The new leverage mechanism offers hope but no immediate return. With political capital below 12, the Union risks losing control of its agenda entirely.
