# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (identity): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign operational AI infrastructure to enforce European red lines in real time, with demonstrable containment of autonomous threats by turn 12 and a public report on systemic risk prevention in public-sector AI by turn 13 — **failed, and now abandoned**
- `resilience_as_foundation` (position): Resilience without sovereign operational capacity is reactive, not strategic — but operational capacity without verifiable safety in public systems and visible harm reduction cannot be sustained
- `sovereignty_through_trust` (commitment): Rebuild EU AI legitimacy from the ground up by ensuring all public-sector AI systems are audited, safe, and accountable before any new sovereign capability is activated
- `enforceable_cooperation` (position): Member state non-compliance with EU AI safety and sovereignty rules cannot be tolerated if the Union is to act at all; we will use fiscal conditionality, access withholding, and coalition-building to enforce common standards, even where unanimity fails

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign operational AI infrastructure to enforce European red lines in real time, with demonstrable containment of autonomous threats by turn 12 and a public report on systemic risk prevention in public-sector AI by turn 13 — **failed, and now abandoned**
  - Grounds: The commitment has formally expired without delivery. Its core objective—real-time enforcement of red lines via sovereign infrastructure—remains unrealised due to blocked compute access, lack of activation authority, and the collapse of cross-member-state cohesion. No containment has occurred; no report has been issued. The premise of top-down sovereignty has failed, and continuing to reference it misrepresents reality.
  - Reason: modified text is identical to the current text
- **modify** `sovereignty_through_trust` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Rebuild EU AI legitimacy from the ground up by ensuring all public-sector AI systems are audited, safe, and accountable before any new sovereign capability is activated
  - Grounds: The commitment still holds, but its scope must expand. It was conceived as conditional activation of infrastructure, but events show that trust is not only about audits—it is also about consequences. Hungary’s unilateral deal and Poland’s obstruction reveal that non-compliance carries no meaningful cost. The commitment must now include enforcement leverage, not just procedural gatekeeping. This shift will be enacted through financial conditioning and coalition diplomacy.
  - Reason: modified text is identical to the current text
- **add** `enforceable_cooperation` — applied
  - Tier: position
  - Text: Member state non-compliance with EU AI safety and sovereignty rules cannot be tolerated if the Union is to act at all; we will use fiscal conditionality, access withholding, and coalition-building to enforce common standards, even where unanimity fails
  - Grounds: The emergence of bilateral data pacts among Central and Northern states shows that member states are already self-organising around continuity, bypassing stalled EU mechanisms. This is fragmentation in motion—but also an opportunity. We must formalise this impulse into a credible enforcement mechanism. Without it, the Conditional Activation Framework risks becoming another empty ritual.
