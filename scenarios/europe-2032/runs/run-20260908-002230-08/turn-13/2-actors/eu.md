## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign operational AI infrastructure to enforce European red lines in real time, with demonstrable containment of autonomous threats by turn 12 and a public report on systemic risk prevention in public-sector AI by turn 13 — **failed, and now abandoned**  
- Grounds: The commitment has formally expired without delivery. Its core objective—real-time enforcement of red lines via sovereign infrastructure—remains unrealised due to blocked compute access, lack of activation authority, and the collapse of cross-member-state cohesion. No containment has occurred; no report has been issued. The premise of top-down sovereignty has failed, and continuing to reference it misrepresents reality.

- modify `sovereignty_through_trust` (commitment): Rebuild EU AI legitimacy from the ground up by ensuring all public-sector AI systems are audited, safe, and accountable before any new sovereign capability is activated  
- Grounds: The commitment still holds, but its scope must expand. It was conceived as conditional activation of infrastructure, but events show that trust is not only about audits—it is also about consequences. Hungary’s unilateral deal and Poland’s obstruction reveal that non-compliance carries no meaningful cost. The commitment must now include enforcement leverage, not just procedural gatekeeping. This shift will be enacted through financial conditioning and coalition diplomacy.

- add `enforceable_cooperation` (position): Member state non-compliance with EU AI safety and sovereignty rules cannot be tolerated if the Union is to act at all; we will use fiscal conditionality, access withholding, and coalition-building to enforce common standards, even where unanimity fails  
- Grounds: The emergence of bilateral data pacts among Central and Northern states shows that member states are already self-organising around continuity, bypassing stalled EU mechanisms. This is fragmentation in motion—but also an opportunity. We must formalise this impulse into a credible enforcement mechanism. Without it, the Conditional Activation Framework risks becoming another empty ritual.

## New measure  
**Fiscal Leverage Mechanism for AI Compliance**  
Withholds access to the AI Emergency Fund and fast-track semiconductor coordination benefits from member states that fail to complete their Public-Sector AI Safety Audit by turn 13 or that enter unauthorised bilateral agreements with foreign hyperscalers.  

- Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)  
- Size: small  
- Finishes on turn: 14  
- Targeted effect: Increase compliance pressure on Italy, Hungary, and Poland; raise `eu_political_capital` indirectly by demonstrating enforceability; support `ai_safety` by accelerating audit completion  
- Applies to: EU member states, European Commission, Council Presidency, middle-power semiconductor coalition

## Priority  
**Public-Sector AI Safety Audit and Remediation Programme** — because it remains the sole foundation for restoring legitimacy, and because without completed audits, neither activation nor enforcement can meaningfully proceed — everything else depends on this being credibly delivered.

## In practice  
We are treating the end of the Sovereign Red Line Enforcement Network not as closure, but as a transfer of function. The enclaves in France and Germany exist, but they are dark. Compute remains blocked by U.S. export controls on ASML servicing, and without activation authority, they cannot run evaluations or red teams. We are preserving the legal and technical architecture, but shifting focus entirely to creating the conditions under which they can one day light up: verifiable safety and enforced cooperation.

The Conditional Activation Framework is now our central lever. We are implementing it through a Council Implementing Regulation under Article 114 TFEU, co-decided with Parliament, which makes audit completion a hard prerequisite for any future sovereign infrastructure activation. ENISA will verify submissions, and the European AI Office will issue compliance certificates. But we now see that certification alone is not enough — there must be consequence. That is why we are launching the Fiscal Leverage Mechanism: no audit, no funding. No adherence to EU sovereignty principles, no access to the emergency fund or fast-tracked chip coordination. This applies immediately to Hungary’s U.S. hyperscaler deal, which violated spirit if not letter — we will not recognise such arrangements as compatible with EU solidarity unless retroactively approved.

We are engaging bilaterally with Italy, Hungary, and Poland, offering technical assistance and extended deadlines — but not indefinite ones. To the middle-power coalition, we say plainly: our ability to coordinate on semiconductors depends on our internal cohesion. If we cannot enforce basic rules at home, we cannot be trusted to hold chokepoints abroad. We are not asking for patience — we are delivering enforceability.
