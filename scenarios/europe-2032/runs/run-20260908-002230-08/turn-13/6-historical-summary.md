Public trust remains flat at 19.0 amid ongoing health IT disruptions and job displacement concerns.

The EU formally cancels the defunct Sovereign Red Line Enforcement Network and replaces it with the **Conditional Activation Framework**, mandating that sovereign AI activation requires prior safety certification through the Public-Sector AI Safety Audit Programme. France and Germany near audit completion, but Italy delays over data access disputes and Hungary outright refuses participation. In response, the Commission introduces the **Fiscal Leverage Mechanism for AI Compliance**, withholding emergency funds and semiconductor benefits from non-compliant states—targeting Hungary’s U.S. hyperscaler deal and Poland’s coordination blockade. Warsaw denounces the move as blackmail, Budapest as overreach, and Rome demands legal clarification; no state yields.

Internal fragmentation deepens: the middle-power semiconductor coalition voices concern over EU disunity, and informal bilateral compute-sharing pacts emerge among Northern and Central member states. Political capital falls below 12, eroding the Commission’s ability to lead, as strategic vision increasingly diverges from operational reality.
