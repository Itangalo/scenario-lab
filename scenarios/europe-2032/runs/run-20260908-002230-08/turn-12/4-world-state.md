### A Reckoning on Sovereignty

The EU formally cancels the Sovereign Red Line Enforcement Network despite its scheduled completion, acknowledging that France and Germany’s enclaves remain inert—no compute, no testing, no capability. The move is framed as a necessary recalibration: sovereignty without legitimacy is hollow. In its place, the Commission launches the **Conditional Activation Framework**, tying all sovereign AI infrastructure to public-sector audit compliance. Red-teaming, frontier access, and compute activation are suspended until member states verify their AI systems as safe.

The Public-Sector AI Safety Audit Programme, now the Union’s sole priority, sees mixed progress. France and Germany submit preliminary findings, but Italy and Hungary resist deadlines, citing legal disputes over data sharing. Fast-track funding offers go unanswered. The European Parliament approves the Council Implementing Regulation, but enforcement doubts linger.

### Trust, Not Access

The pivot wins cautious praise from civil society, with media noting the shift from “control” to “accountability.” However, markets react skeptically. Analysts describe the framework as politically defensible but operationally stalled—sovereign capacity is not just delayed, it is hostage to fractious domestic processes. The Hungarian bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler stands unchallenged, underscoring divergent national strategies.

Public sentiment inches up slightly, reflecting modest approval for transparency efforts, but job losses in municipal AI contracting fuel localized protests. Resilience dips as health IT systems face repeated low-level disruptions—symptoms of broader fragility.

### Capital Erodes Further

Political capital plummets. Cancelling a flagship measure costs dearly, even one that failed. The Commission loses ground in cohesion talks, and Poland again blocks joint statements on semiconductor coordination. The middle-power coalition remains intact but grows impatient.

Meanwhile, global AI capability climbs to 70.0, driven by autonomous cyber tools. Open weights rise to 58.5, narrowing the gap. No safety gains land on deployed systems—assurance still lags behind capability.
