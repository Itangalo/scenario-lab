- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign operational AI infrastructure to enforce European red lines in real time, with demonstrable containment of autonomous threats by turn 12 and a public report on systemic risk prevention in public-sector AI by turn 13 — **failed, and now abandoned**  
- Grounds: The **Sovereign Red Line Enforcement Network** remains non-operational due to blocked compute access and technical unreadiness; no live containment of autonomous threats has occurred. The **Frontier Access Corridor** is complete but delivers no access. The **automated_decision_scandal** and the Hungarian bilateral deal have shattered the premise that sovereignty can be imposed top-down without legitimacy. By turn 12, the commitment’s core deliverables are not only unmet but structurally unachievable under current conditions.  
- Trigger: The convergence of **U.S. export controls blocking ASML servicing**, **Hungary’s bilateral deal**, and **the failure to publish the Belgian audit report on time** has created a categorical failure: the Union cannot enforce red lines it cannot test, on infrastructure it does not control, while its own institutions remain untrusted.  
- Consequence: The reversal costs political capital. Member states now see the commitment as a hollow performance. Markets interpret it as strategic incoherence. The credibility of future EU AI leadership is in question.

- add `sovereignty_through_trust` (commitment): Rebuild EU AI legitimacy from the ground up by ensuring all public-sector AI systems are audited, safe, and accountable before any new sovereign capability is activated  
- Grounds: The current crisis is not one of access but of consent. Public sentiment is collapsing (18.0), resilience is eroding (45.0), and political capital is at 8.0 — lower than at any point since the pandemic. The only path to real sovereignty is through restored trust: no capability, however advanced, can survive if the Union lacks the political unity to deploy it. The **Public-Sector AI Safety Audit and Remediation Programme** is now the foundation, not a complement.  
- Action: This commitment is enacted by making the audit programme the sole gateway to any further sovereign infrastructure activation — including red-teaming, frontier access, and compute deployment.

- modify `resilience_as_foundation` (position): Resilience without sovereign operational capacity is reactive, not strategic — but operational capacity without verifiable safety in public systems and visible harm reduction cannot be sustained  
- Grounds: The position still holds, but its balance has shifted. The failure to deliver operational sovereignty has revealed that resilience is not just a technical buffer — it is the political condition for sovereignty itself. The position remains valid, but its urgency now dominates.

## New measure  
**Conditional Activation Framework for Sovereign AI Infrastructure**  
Ties the activation of all EU-controlled AI infrastructure — including red-teaming enclaves, frontier evaluation access, and sovereign compute — to the successful audit and remediation of national public-sector AI systems.  

- Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)  
- Size: small  
- Finishes on turn: 14  
- Targeted effect: Increase `ai_safety` by 6–10 points, raise `public_sentiment` by 8–12, and condition future sovereignty investments on verifiable domestic accountability  
- Applies to: EU member states, European AI Office, ENISA, and all operators of sovereign AI infrastructure  

## Priority  
**Public-Sector AI Safety Audit and Remediation Programme** — because the Union’s credibility now depends on proving it can govern its own systems before demanding control over global ones, and because without public trust, no sovereign capability will survive political withdrawal of support.

## In practice  
We are treating the completion of the Sovereign Red Line Enforcement Network as a technical milestone, not an operational one. France and Germany have stood up the enclaves, but without functional compute — blocked by ASML’s withheld servicing under U.S. export controls — we cannot run frontier evaluations or live red-team simulations. ENISA’s protocols remain untested, and the fall cyber exercise risks being dismissed as theatre. We are canceling further funding for the network in its current form. Its components will be preserved, but activation is now conditional.

The new **Conditional Activation Framework** will legally bind the rollout of sovereign infrastructure to progress in the audit programme. No red team gains live access. No frontier model is evaluated on EU soil. No compute enclave goes live — until each member state has submitted its audit results and remediated all Category 5+ risks in public-sector AI. This is not a delay; it is a recalibration of sovereignty as a function of legitimacy.

We are implementing this through a Council Implementing Regulation under Article 114 TFEU, co-decided with the Parliament. The European AI Office will issue compliance certificates, and the Commission will suspend access to the AI Emergency Fund for any member state that fails to meet audit deadlines. We are offering technical assistance and fast-track funding to Italy and Hungary to re-engage, but the message is clear: sovereignty is not a privilege for the compliant — it is a responsibility for all. The middle-power coalition with Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan will be informed that our leverage in semiconductor negotiations now depends on demonstrating internal cohesion. We will not trade access for dependence. We will earn access by proving we can govern.
