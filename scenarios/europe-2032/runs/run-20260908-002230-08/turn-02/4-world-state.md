### Frontier Surge, Safety Stalls

Global AI capability advances by +2.0, driven by compressed release cycles and larger agent-based training runs. Despite last turn’s interpretability gains, no new safety measures land on frontier systems, and the gap between capability and control widens. A quiet but consequential regression occurs: two leading labs quietly deploy models with opaque planning modules, citing competitive pressure. This triggers a −3 drop in `ai_safety`, erasing part of the prior gain.

Open-weight capability rises to 44.0, as Kimi K3 derivatives are fine-tuned for infrastructure probing. While still behind the frontier, these models empower smaller actors to conduct persistent cyber campaigns, increasing systemic risk.

### Shielded Ambitions, Rising Friction

The EU launches the **ASML Strategic Shield Initiative** as its new priority, aiming to assert control over critical semiconductor exports. Legal drafting begins under Article 207 TFEU, and the European Commission proposes the Strategic Export Control Board. However, immediate pushback emerges: the U.S. warns of “unilateral disruption” to export controls, and ASML, while supportive in principle, expresses concern over retaliation in non-EU markets. The contingency fund is approved in principle, but disbursement awaits Council consensus.

The **Cyber Shield Directive** progresses slowly. Germany and the Nordics accept EU-supervised red-teaming, but France and Poland reject binding audit rights, insisting on national control. A compromise allows national teams to lead exercises under EU observation — a face-saving arrangement that weakens standardisation. The Modernisation Envelope begins disbursement, but only 40% of funds are allocated due to slow national uptake.

InvestAI Gigafactories complete environmental assessments in two regions, but water stress in eastern Poland triggers local protests. The TEN-E grid prioritisation faces legal challenges from regional authorities. The Tech Sovereignty Package advances in Parliament but is diluted to secure votes, with workforce training commitments added as a condition for fast-tracking.

Public sentiment declines further, as media spotlight job losses in legal and accounting sectors and frame the ASML move as a “dangerous provocation.” The narrative of strategic autonomy fails to resonate, and trust in AI governance continues to erode.
