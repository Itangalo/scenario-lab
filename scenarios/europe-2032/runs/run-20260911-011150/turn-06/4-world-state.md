### The repair trucks
Winter brought the attack everyone had warned about. A wave of machine-written intrusions swept municipal administrations, clinics and a widely used software component at once. Screens went dark in town halls from the Rhineland to Lombardy, appointment systems failed, and backup restores turned out to be months out of date. Attribution teams spoke of tooling assembled at speed no human crew could match.

Brussels answered with what it could afford. Emergency crews under the EU cybersecurity agency were dispatched from existing response rosters, paid for by repurposed digital funds. In the worst-hit towns they worked — isolating compromised systems, rebuilding from clean copies, keeping emergency care on paper. Where they arrived, services flickered back. Elsewhere mayors complained they never came at all, and national computer emergency teams grumbled about confused command lines.

The autumn grid work helped narrowly: power stayed on, but the health and local-government systems left unpatched in December were exactly where the damage landed.

### A solo deal
Midway through the spring, a member state broke ranks. Its government signed its own arrangement for privileged model and cloud access with an American hyperscaler, on pricing and conditions well below the common position agreed with partners in The Hague, Tokyo and Seoul. At home ministers called it pragmatism — guaranteed supply while the five reserved factory sites in Paris, Berlin, Madrid, Stockholm and Warsaw still stood empty.

In Brussels it read as a breach. Paris and The Hague tried to hold the joint licensing line and offered the capital a place in a shared Washington ask, warning that solo deals would forfeit solidarity help. The offer was noted and pocketed. Eastern newspapers that had called the American election result a humiliation now asked why unity applied only to others.

### Washington takes office
In January the new American administration took office on its national-asset doctrine: frontier systems tiered by country, export reviews tightened. European requests for computing capacity were received politely and queued. The middle-power table on lenses, chemicals and packaging kept meeting, but without American supply it was leverage without material.

By June the public mood was bleak. Power-price protests near the empty sites merged with anger over failed clinics and ransomed town halls. Polls showed AI widely used and widely resented, and the Union seen as managing dependence rather than ending it.
