* - modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity to act as a check on uncontrolled frontier advancement and ensure it is operationally integrated into EU governance before autonomous systems outpace human oversight  
  - Grounds: The Sovereign Agent Launch Programme is now two turns in and still lacks auditable decision transparency, while global capability has reached 63.5—deep in the terminal zone. The original commitment underestimated the time required to operationalise sovereignty: it is not enough to build the systems; they must be trusted and usable within real EU processes. This sharpening binds us to delivery, not just deployment.

* - modify `contain_openweight_risk` (commitment): Prevent open-weight models from reaching capability thresholds that enable uncontrolled autonomous harm, and ensure the EU retains sovereign capacity to evaluate and respond to open models regardless of origin  
  - Grounds: The member state defection this turn—accepting a foreign hyperscaler’s open-weight release above 52.0 under bilateral terms—proves that distributed capability can now bypass Union-wide regulation. Our containment strategy fails if evaluation and response capacity remain dependent on goodwill. We must now treat open-weight models not just as a regulatory object, but as a standing operational threat requiring sovereign technical capacity.

## New measure  
**EU Open-Weight Evaluation and Response Capacity**  
We build and legally mandate an EU-level technical capability to independently evaluate, monitor, and if necessary counteract high-capability open-weight models, regardless of origin or hosting jurisdiction.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` +4 to ai_safety (by enabling sovereign detection of emergent risks), +2 to eu_ai_sovereignty (by creating a non-negotiable technical baseline), -1 to public_sentiment (due to perceived militarisation of open research)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member states, and all entities conducting or hosting open-weight model evaluations at or above 45.0 capability within EU jurisdiction

## Priority  
**EU Open-Weight Evaluation and Response Capacity** — because the member state defection proves that regulatory containment alone is futile if the Union cannot independently verify compliance and respond to violations, and because without sovereign evaluation, our entire safety posture becomes reactive and fragmented.

## In practice  
This turn, we are acting on the fracture exposed by the defection: a member state has signed a bilateral agreement with a U.S. hyperscaler, granting preferential access to an open-weight model at 54.0 capability under the guise of academic collaboration. This bypasses the spirit and soon-to-be-letter of the Openweight Release Threshold Regulation and reveals a fatal gap—not in law, but in enforcement capacity. We cannot regulate what we cannot evaluate, and we cannot evaluate what we do not control.  

Therefore, we are launching the EU Open-Weight Evaluation and Response Capacity under the Digital Europe Programme and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking. This will establish two sovereign evaluation enclaves—one in France, one in Poland—equipped with hardened compute and legal authority to ingest, test, and if necessary sandbox or counteract open-weight models above 45.0 capability. The enclaves will operate under ENISA oversight, with interpretability tooling developed from the ongoing €400M investment, and will feed directly into the European AI Office’s incident response framework. We are negotiating with Germany, Austria, and the Nordic states to pool evaluation mandates, offering them early access in exchange for ceding unilateral evaluation rights. Resistance from smaller states fearing loss of research autonomy is being met with a new Tier 3 access protocol: vetted researchers retain open access, but only through sovereign EU evaluation environments.  

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Agent Launch Programme continues under strain. Finland and Spain are now running live health triage and SME compliance pilots, but audit logs remain compressed. We are fast-tracking integration of the new interpretability stack, but full legibility will not land before turn 12. The Openweight Release Threshold Regulation remains in drafting, with the Competitiveness Council deadlocked on enforcement scope. We are now tying its credibility to the new evaluation capacity: you cannot enforce a line you cannot see. This is no longer just about control—it is about coherence. The Union must be able to know, act, and enforce as one—otherwise, we are not a sovereign actor. We are becoming a network of exceptions.
