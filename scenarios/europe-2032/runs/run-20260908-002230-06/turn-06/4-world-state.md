### Strategic Pivot Amid Strain

Global AI capability advances to 59.5 as frontier labs refine agentic workflows, enabling systems to execute longer scientific campaigns with reduced human oversight. The trajectory remains steady, with autonomy thresholds now within clear sight.

The EU cancels the European AI Incident Response Corps, citing unsustainable political capital demands and functional overlap with existing resilience initiatives. While some member states express concern over weakened crisis coordination, the move frees resources for a bold new priority.

The European Commission launches the **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive**, a large category 4 measure aimed at fast-tracking AI data centres as critical infrastructure. Invoking energy security and digital sovereignty, it mandates emergency permitting, grid prioritization, and state-backed power contracts for qualifying facilities in Finland, Sweden, and Spain. €38bn from the recently completed Tech Sovereignty Package is reallocated to fund long-term power agreements via the European Investment Bank.

Despite resistance from Germany and Austria over environmental and sovereignty concerns, the directive gains traction as the US administration formally adopts a **CONSOLIDATION** posture, rationing frontier AI access by alliance tier. This hardens the EU’s strategic rationale: without sovereign compute, autonomy is illusion.

The directive yields early momentum. Pilot sites report accelerated grid connection timelines, and private developers signal interest in co-investment. However, full deployment remains years away.

Resilience rises slightly as hardened infrastructure planning progresses. AI safety dips, as capability growth again outpaces assurance. Sovereignty stabilizes with the new build momentum offsetting dependency pressures.

Political capital holds steady—gains from cancelling a costly measure balance the new portfolio burden and proposal bonus.

### A Fragile Foundation

Public sentiment remains flat. Visible AI benefits in public services are counterbalanced by job displacement anxieties and skepticism about elite-driven infrastructure projects.

The EU acts decisively—but on shifting ground.
