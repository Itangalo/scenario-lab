A binding directive requiring cyber hardening of essential services, with independent audits and public reporting of compliance.

## Priority
Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure — this turn’s cyber_test_shot proves that detection-based defence is already obsolete, and we must harden before we are hit.

## In practice
We are acting on the intrusion revealed this autumn not as an anomaly but as a signal: the frontier has outpaced our defences, and what was found by accident will next go unseen. The Cyber Shield directive will be drafted under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market clause to set minimum security standards across energy, water, transport and health infrastructure. ENISA will lead technical specification, working with national CSIRTs to define baseline configurations resistant to AI-driven zero-day exploits. Member states will resist cost-sharing and implementation timelines, so we are pairing the directive with access to InvestEU’s risk-sharing facility and fast-tracked cohesion funds — conditional on adoption by Q2 2027.

At the same time, we are advancing both inherited measures. The InvestAI Gigafactories fund is now structured, but siting remains contested: we are brokering between regions offering tax incentives and grid operators warning of strain, using the Commission’s energy system adequacy assessments as leverage. The Tech sovereignty package is under pressure from industry to dilute permitting acceleration; we are holding firm, citing the capability_plateau_evidence as proof that today’s investment locks in tomorrow’s leverage. We are also engaging ASML quietly, offering export-control alignment with allies in return for domestic production commitments — a step toward real compute sovereignty.

The safety_breakthrough gives us tactical room: we are pushing for rapid adoption of the new interpretability tool across EU-based developers via the AI Act’s GPAI provisions, even as high-risk rules remain delayed. This is not regulation — it is uptake, through persuasion and procurement. But without hardening, all such gains are fragile. Hence the priority: if we cannot keep the lights on, no assurance will matter.
