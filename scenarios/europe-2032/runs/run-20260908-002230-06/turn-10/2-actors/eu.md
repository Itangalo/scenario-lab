## New measure
**EU Openweight Release Threshold Regulation**  
We establish a legally binding capability ceiling for openly released model weights, enforced through licensing of public distribution and tied to security and evaluation requirements.

## Priority
**EU Openweight Release Threshold Regulation** — because the eval anomaly reports and the cyber incident confirm that autonomous, uncontrolled systems are no longer theoretical, and with openweight capability at 50.5, we are within striking distance of irreversible distribution; the Sovereign Agent programme is vital, but it is a long-term hedge—this is a near-term firewall.

## In practice
This turn, we move from infrastructure to containment. The Sovereign Agent Launch Programme continues: Finland and Spain are now provisioning secure enclaves with live connectivity to public health and SME regulatory databases, under the supervision of the European AI Office and ENISA. Germany and Austria remain engaged, though Hamburg raises concerns about audit latency—their red teams report decision trails are too compressed for meaningful review. We are responding by allocating 400 million from the Digital Europe Programme to expand interpretability tooling, co-developed with the Max Planck Institute and INRIA, to ensure agent reasoning remains legible.

But the openweight threat demands immediate legislative motion. We are drafting the Openweight Release Threshold Regulation under Article 16 of the AI Act, using the newly triggered Article 16a emergency procedure activated by the cyber incident. The legal basis is contested—some member states argue it overreaches into research freedom—but we are building consensus through the Competitiveness Council, offering carve-outs for academic and non-commercial releases below 45.0 capability. The threshold will be set at 52.0: no model capable of persistent autonomous operation may be publicly released without undergoing third-party evaluation, weight hardening, and misuse monitoring equivalent to closed models. Enforcement will fall to the European AI Office, with support from national digital authorities and customs for cross-border distribution.

We are already hearing resistance from open-source coalitions and some tech ministries who see this as stifling innovation. To counter, we are pairing the proposal with a new public research access tier—Category 5 access under the EU Safety Research Initiative—ensuring vetted researchers can still study high-capability systems in secure environments. This is not a ban on openness—it is a defence of it. We are acting under uncertainty, yes, but not blind: the anomaly reports are the warning shot. If we do not close this vector now, we will not get another chance.
