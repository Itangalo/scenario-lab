## Priority  
**EU Open Model Partnership** — because the emergent_lab_cooperation event proves we can catalyse a third pole in AI development, and the emergent_lab_access_breach shows that relying on closed foreign labs is no longer tenable — sovereignty and safety now demand that we build our own open but controlled frontier.

## In practice  
We are acting on a rare alignment: a security shock to the closed frontier and a spontaneous coalition forming on the open one. The breach at the leading lab shatters the illusion that proprietary systems are inherently secure; if weights can be stolen from the most advanced labs, then the EU’s dependence on foreign models is a single point of failure. At the same time, the informal alliance of EU research institutions offers a real alternative — not just open, but *safely* open, with ethical oversight and shared security standards. We are seizing this moment to institutionalise it.

Through Horizon Europe and the European Innovation Council, we are fast-tracking a €30bn partnership to scale this network: shared sovereign compute clusters, secured model repositories, and binding safety protocols that must be met for access. The Commission will draft a legal framework under Article 185 TFEU for joint research initiatives, giving it teeth. National governments will be invited to pool compute and data, with participation tied to cohesion fund eligibility. We are offering the participating labs early access to the Gigafactories’ energy guarantees and export-control exemptions for dual-use research — incentives that bind their success to EU strategic objectives.

This is not a subsidy for open weights; it is a sovereign counterweight. We are setting a condition: openness must come with enforceable safety and security — no unchecked releases. In return, we offer legitimacy, infrastructure, and market access. The model will be auditable, the training data traceable, and the deployment monitored. If we get this right, we do not just raise the floor — we define the field.
