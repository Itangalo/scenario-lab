## New measure  
**Secure Open Weights Initiative**  
A binding safety regime for open-weight models above a defined capability threshold, enforced through access controls to EU infrastructure and markets.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` openweight_capability stabilised (cap uncontrolled diffusion), ai_safety +8 (by embedding enforceable constraints at release), resilience +5 (by reducing attack surface from distributed models)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, developers releasing models above threshold, cloud providers, hosting services, and open model repositories operating in or serving the EU

## Priority  
**Secure Open Weights Initiative** — because the openweight_frontier_release event proves that open models are now within striking distance of the closed frontier, and with them comes an unrecallable attack surface; we must act now to prevent safety from becoming a choice rather than a condition of access.

## In practice  
We are moving fast on a paradox: openness is now a vector of systemic risk. The openweight_frontier_release has landed a model within months of the closed frontier — downloaded hundreds of thousands of times, running on private hardware beyond any jurisdiction. This is not diffusion; it is dispersal. The breach at the foreign lab showed us that closed systems can be compromised. This release shows us that open systems, once released, cannot be secured after the fact. We are no longer choosing between closed dependence and open chaos. We must define a third path: open, but bounded.

Through the European Commission’s Directorate-General for Communications Networks, Content and Technology (DG CONNECT), we are drafting a regulation under Article 114 TFEU to establish a capability threshold—calibrated at 58.0 on the index—above which any open-weight release must comply with enforceable safety constraints to be hosted, distributed, or monetised in the EU. These include model provenance verification, interpretability benchmarks, and real-time misuse monitoring tied to a central incident reporting body. Cloud providers, hosting services, and app stores operating in the EU will be required to enforce compliance as a condition of market access. Non-compliant models will be blocked from infrastructure and payment rails.

We are coordinating with FRONTEX and ENISA to map enforcement pathways, and with the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) to operationalise access controls. Resistance is expected from open-source advocates and some member states who see this as overreach, but the campaign_backlash has shifted the ground: public sentiment now favours guardrails, and we are framing this not as censorship, but as infrastructure security. The measure complements our EU Open Model Partnership by ensuring that the open models we *do* support are not Trojan horses. We are offering compliant developers fast-track access to sovereign compute and audit support through the European AI Board. This is not a ban. It is a boundary. And it is the price of remaining a credible actor in a world where capability no longer waits for law.
