### A Threshold Crossed

Global AI capability climbs to 60.5 as frontier labs deploy the first systems capable of completing multi-day scientific and engineering projects end-to-end. These agents operate with minimal supervision, marking the transition from assistant to autonomous actor. The milestone triggers cascading effects: AI-driven drug discovery accelerates, but so do unauthorised intrusions into industrial control systems using AI-crafted exploits.

The EU sees two major initiatives conclude. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme finishes, delivering foundational compute capacity across four sites. Simultaneously, the **EU Open Model Partnership** wraps up, producing a suite of safety-constrained open models now in early deployment. These completions provide a political boost.

Amid rising strategic pressure, the **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive** is formally proposed and designated as the EU’s top priority. Invoking energy security, it grants AI infrastructure critical status, enabling fast-tracked permitting and grid access in principle. However, implementation remains in early stages: site preparations in Spain, Finland, and Sweden are announced but not yet underway, and the European Investment Bank has only begun drafting power purchase frameworks. Germany and Austria voice strong resistance, citing environmental risks, slowing consensus.

Resilience improves as hardened infrastructure planning matures into binding cross-border protocols. However, AI safety declines further as capability growth outpaces assurance—no new safeguards have landed on frontier systems.

Public sentiment edges upward, buoyed by visible progress on sovereign infrastructure and AI-assisted clean energy breakthroughs. Labour concerns persist, but the narrative shifts toward strategic agency.

### Momentum and Fragility

The conclusion of InvestAI Gigafactories and the EU Open Model Partnership delivers a measured uplift to EU AI sovereignty, contributing +1 from each completed programme. The proposal of the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive adds a further +1 as it enters the pipeline, while the widening gap between global AI capability and open-weight development subtracts −1. Sovereignty thus rises to 21.0, consistent with incremental progress rather than breakthrough momentum.

Political capital increases to 28.0, reflecting gains from completed measures and the symbolic strength of launching a major new priority. The rise respects the constitutional limit: sovereignty contributes no more than +3 to political capital, with the remainder supported by public sentiment growth and the legitimacy of closed programmes. Institutional inertia and unresolved tensions around implementation constrain the full translation of momentum into influence.

Open-weight capability increases modestly, as some frontier advances begin to filter into open ecosystems—offensive cyber tools circulate with greater sophistication, but no coordinated release or catastrophic leakage occurs. The offence-defence balance remains precarious, though whispers of a European-led cyber defence advance grow louder.
