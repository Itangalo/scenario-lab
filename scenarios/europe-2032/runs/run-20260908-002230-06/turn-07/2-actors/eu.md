## New measure
**Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive**  
We mandate emergency permitting, grid prioritization, and state-backed power contracts for AI compute infrastructure on EU soil, directly linking energy and infrastructure policy to strategic AI sovereignty.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +12 (by accelerating domestic compute deployment and anchoring it legally), resilience +3 (through hardened, distributed infrastructure), ai_capability +1 (by enabling EU-run training runs above 60.0 within two years)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, national energy regulators, transmission system operators, and qualifying AI infrastructure developers operating in the EU

## Priority
**Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive** — because the US election outcome and tightening export controls have made it clear that access to frontier AI is no longer a shared resource but a contested strategic asset, and without sovereign compute, the EU cannot run its own agents, enforce its own safeguards, or respond to crises on its own terms — all other measures depend on this foundation.

## In practice
This turn, we act on the core contradiction: we are trying to build sovereign capability while depending on foreign access and foreign infrastructure. That ends now. The Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive will be proposed under Article 114 TFEU, with a legal basis in energy security and digital sovereignty. We will designate AI compute as critical infrastructure, granting it priority grid access and fast-track permitting — overriding national delays in grid connection and environmental review where justified by strategic need. The Commission will work with ENTSO-E and the European Investment Bank to secure long-term power purchase agreements for qualifying facilities, backed by state guarantees where market terms fail. We are targeting three sites — in Finland, Sweden, and Spain — where geothermal and hydro capacity can support massive loads without straining the grid.

We are funding this through a reallocation of €38bn from the now-completed Tech Sovereignty Package, redirecting capital from incentives for private hyperscalers to direct public enablement of EU-controlled infrastructure. This is not a subsidy — it is a reordering of priorities. National resistance, especially from Germany and Austria, will be high; we will counter it by offering co-investment in regional AI hubs tied to retraining programmes, linking compute growth to labour transition. The directive will be negotiated in the Internal Market and Competitiveness Council, with a trilogue expected by turn 7. Without this, every other measure we run — from open-weight safety to sovereign agents — rests on sand. We cannot regulate what we cannot run, and we cannot respond to incidents we cannot compute through. This is the floor, not the ceiling.
