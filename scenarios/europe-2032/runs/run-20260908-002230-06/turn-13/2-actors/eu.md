## New measure  
**EU Sovereign Evaluation Mandate Enforcement Directive**  
We compel all member states to route any acquisition or deployment of models exceeding 50.0 capability through the EU Open-Weight Evaluation and Response Capacity, with suspension of Cohesion Fund disbursements for non-compliance.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to eu_ai_sovereignty (by enforcing jurisdictional control), +3 to ai_safety (by ensuring pre-deployment scrutiny), −2 to eu_political_capital initially (due to resistance) but potential recovery if enforcement succeeds  
`Applies to:` all EU member states, with compliance monitored by ENISA and the European Commission

## Priority  
**EU Open-Weight Evaluation and Response Capacity** — because it is the only instrument we have left that can verify whether a model above 50.0 is inside our borders, and without verification, no rule matters; the defection of a member state makes this not just a technical priority but the last line between collective authority and fragmentation.

## In practice  
This turn, we are drawing a line: no member state may import or operate a high-capability AI outside the Union’s sovereign evaluation framework. The Open-Weight Evaluation and Response Capacity is now live in France and Poland, with hardened enclaves under ENISA supervision actively ingesting test models. Germany and Austria have joined the evaluation pool in exchange for Tier 3 research access, but smaller states remain resistant, fearing exclusion. We are offering them guaranteed seats on the evaluation board, co-governance of research quotas, and dedicated funding for local interpretability research — not just inclusion, but shared ownership. If they still refuse, we will proceed, but we prefer unity forged through equity than imposed through sanction.

The Sovereign Agent Launch Programme has concluded: Finland and Spain have stood down their pilot agents after six months of audited operation in healthcare triage and SME compliance. The results are promising — response times improved by 40%, error rates halved — but audit logs reveal persistent opacity in agent reasoning, underscoring why interpretability remains our bottleneck. These systems will now transition into the Regional Transition Accelerator hubs, where they will be repurposed for public service augmentation under citizen oversight panels.

Meanwhile, the Openweight Release Threshold Regulation has formally passed — a binding 52.0 ceiling on open-weight distribution — but its teeth depend entirely on the evaluation enclaves’ ability to detect violations. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Evaluation Mandate Enforcement Directive: no model above 50.0 enters any member state without EU review. Non-compliance triggers automatic suspension of Cohesion Fund transfers — a painful but necessary lever. We are preparing legal opinions under Article 352 TFEU for emergency invocation, and briefing the ECOFIN Council on the fiscal implications. This is not about punishing members — it is about preserving the Union’s capacity to act at all.
