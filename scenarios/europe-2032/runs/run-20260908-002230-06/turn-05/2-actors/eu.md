## New measure  
**European AI Incident Response Corps**  
We establish a standing, cross-agency unit with authority and technical capacity to detect, assess, and coordinate containment of AI-enabled incidents in real time across sectors and borders.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +8 (by enabling rapid coordinated response to misuse and loss-of-control events), ai_safety +5 (by creating feedback loop from incidents to policy and safeguards), public_sentiment +3 (by demonstrating competence in crisis management)  
`Applies to:` EU agencies, national regulators, critical infrastructure operators, and frontier developers operating in the EU

## In practice  
We are closing the Secure Open Weights Initiative this turn with a final trilogue between the Commission, Parliament, and Council. The enforcement protocol is now locked: models above 45.0 openweight capability will be subject to mandatory interpretability requirements, misuse detection systems, and restricted access to cloud compute within the EU. Germany and France have agreed to support it after we granted both co-leadership of the audit board and pre-access to the first sovereign agent pilots. ENISA will host the compliance dashboard by turn 6, and we are aligning the threshold with the latest observed open releases—now confirmed at exactly 45.0. This is not perfect, but it is live, and it is the first binding regime of its kind.

Meanwhile, the EU Sovereign Agent Framework is accelerating. We have secured €12bn in funding from Digital Europe and Horizon Europe, and EuroHPC JU is standing up the new unit this quarter. The three pilot agents—emergency response, grid management, and medical triage—are being scoped with DG SANTE, ENTSO-E, and the Emergency Response Coordination Centre. We are insisting on formal verification of goal stability and real-time intent logging, using tools developed under the EU Open Model Partnership. The first test environment will go live in Finland by turn 6, with full stress-testing under simulated crisis conditions. This is not about matching the frontier—it’s about proving we can run systems that are both capable and accountable.

The new European AI Incident Response Corps builds on the Cyber Shield experience but goes further: it will integrate threat detection from ENISA, front-line response from national CERTs, and technical analysis from ELI AI and the European Lab for Learning and Intelligence. It will have standing authority to escalate and coordinate during incidents, and we are drafting a new legal basis under Article 114 TFEU for cross-sector mandates. The adoption_success event gives us political cover: the public sees AI working for them in schools and clinics, so we can now argue that resilience is not just defence, but the price of continued trust. We are funding it through a reallocation from the Tech Sovereignty Package, accepting slower private investment in exchange for faster public readiness. This turn, we act on containment, construction, and consequence—all at once, because we have no other choice.
