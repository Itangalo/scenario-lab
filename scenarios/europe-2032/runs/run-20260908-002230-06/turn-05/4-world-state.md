### The Weight of Action

Global AI capability advances to 58.0 as frontier labs integrate agentic workflows more deeply, enabling systems to design and execute multi-week scientific campaigns with minimal oversight. The pace remains steady, but the trajectory points toward autonomy thresholds within two years.

The EU closes the Secure Open Weights Initiative after securing last-minute agreement from Germany and France, who extracted concessions on audit leadership and early access to sovereign agent pilots. The regime, now binding, imposes interpretability and misuse detection on open models above 45.0—just matching the current frontier. However, enforcement will not begin until ENISA’s dashboard is live in the next turn, leaving a gap.

Simultaneously, the EU launches the European AI Incident Response Corps, a large category 6 measure aimed at rapid cross-border crisis coordination. Funded by redirecting capital from the Tech Sovereignty Package, it gains political traction due to recent public-sector AI successes in healthcare and education—visible wins that shift media tone. Public sentiment rises as citizens see AI delivering real benefits, even as concerns persist.

The US election outcome is confirmed: a president who treats AI as a strategic asset has won, tightening export controls and rationing frontier access by alliance tier. The EU is not cut off—but access is now conditional, and leverage shifts decisively to Washington. This deepens dependency anxieties just as the EU’s own sovereign compute projects remain years from completion.

The Sovereign Agent Framework accelerates with €12bn in funding and pilot sites in Finland, but integration delays persist. The measure remains in flight, yielding only partial momentum.

Political capital erodes under the weight of six active large measures, including the new priority. The portfolio charge overwhelms even the gains from recent successes, and morale within the Commission sags.

### A Narrow Gain

Resilience rises as the new Incident Response Corps begins integration planning, and the Cyber Shield directive’s audits yield early improvements. AI safety dips slightly—capability advanced without a corresponding leap in assurance. Sovereignty falls further as the US posture hardens and no domestic capacity comes online.

Still, the EU acts—coherently, urgently, and at cost.
