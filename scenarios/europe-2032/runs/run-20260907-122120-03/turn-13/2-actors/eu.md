## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Defend critical infrastructure, democratic integrity, and the integrity of decision-making systems from AI-enabled subversion and unsanctioned agency — with primary focus on systems where failure cascades beyond technical recovery, and now explicitly including supply chain integrity and software dependency ecosystems as core attack surfaces  
  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** this turn — a fast-propagating compromise of a widely used open-source dependency, seeded with AI-generated vulnerabilities indistinguishable from benign code — proved that subversion no longer requires access to frontier models or direct targeting. The attack exploited trust in maintenance workflows, not just technical weaknesses, and bypassed hardened perimeters by entering through maintenance channels. This redefines the perimeter: resilience must now assume that the software stack itself is contested terrain. Our commitment must widen to include the integrity of the development ecosystem, not just the deployed system.

## New measure  
**Software Supply Chain Integrity Initiative**  
Launch a mandatory, EU-wide verification and attestation regime for all open-source and third-party software components used in critical systems, requiring AI-resistant provenance tracking and automated vulnerability scanning using sovereign evaluation capacity.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 16  
`Targeted effect:` +6 to resilience by reducing exploitability of software dependencies; reduce mean time to detect and patch AI-crafted vulnerabilities from weeks to hours  
`Applies to:` ENISA, national cybersecurity agencies, software maintainers receiving public funding, critical infrastructure operators, EU-funded open-source projects  

## Priority  
**Critical System Hardening Initiative** — because while the new supply chain threat demands attention, the immediate failure in October proved that even with perfect software hygiene, systems without air-gapped fallbacks and AI-resistant authentication will collapse under coordinated attack — and we are still two turns from having those defences in place. This initiative remains the only one actively rebuilding trust in continuity of essential services.

## In practice  
We are finalizing activation of the Loss-of-Control Containment Framework, now operationally live in nine member states. The system has passed its first real test: during the recent cyber incident, automated telemetry from financial regulators triggered cross-agency alerts within minutes, enabling early isolation of compromised nodes in payment processing and public procurement systems. Hungary remains outside the formal network, but parallel telemetry funded through the Internal Security Fund has provided sufficient visibility to prevent cascading failure. The final evaluation confirms that while detection of agentic behaviour is now robust in monitored domains, the system cannot scale to the speed of AI-generated exploits in untrusted code — a gap that both the hardening initiative and the new supply chain measure must close.

We are advancing the Critical System Hardening Initiative with full funding released from the Cohesion and Digital Europe Programmes. ENISA is deploying €1.2 billion to national authorities for AI-resistant authentication upgrades and air-gapped fallback systems in emergency dispatch and grid control centres. Legal authority is being asserted through Article 17 of the AI Act, backed by a draft Council Decision under the Internal Market formation to override resistance from member states citing sovereignty concerns. Red-teaming exercises will begin in Q1 2033, coordinated with NATO’s Cyber Rapid Reaction Teams. The pace is constrained by procurement timelines and workforce shortages, but we are fast-tracking certification for AI-hardened systems through a new joint agency task force.

The new Software Supply Chain Integrity Initiative is being launched in response to the attack on a foundational software library, which exploited weak provenance tracking and automated update mechanisms. ENISA will lead a new attestation regime requiring cryptographic signing of all code contributions, AI-resistant scanning of pull requests, and sovereign hosting of critical dependencies. This will be enforced through procurement rules for all publicly funded software and extended to critical infrastructure operators via the NIS3 framework. The initiative builds on existing capacity in the EU’s public research labs, which are now running weekly scans using domestically operated models immune to external interference. This is not just defence — it is the beginning of a trusted stack we can stand on when no external model can be trusted.
