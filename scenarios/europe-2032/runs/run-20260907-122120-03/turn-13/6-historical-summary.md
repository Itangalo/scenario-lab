A major cyber incident in October, caused by AI-generated vulnerabilities in a widely used open-source library, led to rapid compromises across public service systems, disrupting emergency dispatch in three countries. The breach exploited trusted software workflows, bypassing hardened defenses and revealing systemic fragility. The EU’s Loss-of-Control Containment Framework enabled fast detection but faced limitations due to Hungary’s non-participation, requiring fallback telemetry. Containment succeeded, but slow recovery eroded public trust.

In response, the EU launched the Software Supply Chain Integrity Initiative, a category 6 measure mandating cryptographic provenance and sovereign-AI scanning for critical software. The Critical System Hardening Initiative progressed with funding release and legal backing under the AI Act, though deployment lagged due to procurement and workforce issues. AI capability rose to 73.5, driven by self-improving development loops, while ai_safety and eu_ai_sovereignty stagnated, the latter due to ongoing reliance on restricted U.S. models under RETRENCHMENT. Resilience improved only modestly, offset by incident response demands. Political capital declined further amid high costs and limited visible gains, underscoring the gap between technical measures and public confidence.
