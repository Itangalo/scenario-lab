### A Surge in Silent Power

AI capability advanced again, reaching 71.0, as frontier labs demonstrated systems that autonomously debug and reconfigure complex codebases in hours—marking a clear capability jump in verifiable domains. Though general reasoning improved only incrementally, the jump in narrow engineering and cyber tasks signaled a shift: offensive tooling is now AI-native. Open-weight models followed, climbing to 55.0 as efficient sparse architectures were reassembled from leaked training data, though still lagging behind the closed frontier.

A major cyber incident struck in October: a self-propagating ransomware strain, generated by an AI model, infiltrated municipal networks across three member states, encrypting emergency dispatch systems. The attack exploited zero-day vulnerabilities chained with social engineering, and defenders were overwhelmed. Recovery took weeks. Resilience, despite prior gains, eroded under the strain—especially as the Loss-of-Control Containment Framework remained incomplete and unevenly adopted.

Public sentiment fell sharply. The Zurich breach had been contained, but this new wave of disruption—combined with visible grid instability and deepfake interference—fueled a sense of creeping loss of control. Media coverage framed AI as a destabilizing force, and protests flared near data centres in France and Germany.

The EU pushed forward with the Frontier Model Real-Time Monitoring Directive, positioning it as a sovereign necessity. However, negotiations with non-EU frontier labs stalled. One U.S. developer refused telemetry access, citing national security directives under the RETRENCHMENT posture. The EU retaliated with delayed data centre permits, but leverage was limited. Still, the proposal signaled intent, and internal preparations advanced.

Meanwhile, the Loss-of-Control Containment Framework progressed slowly. Poland integrated parts into its financial monitoring, but Hungary formally rejected any cross-border enforcement, weakening systemic coherence. ENISA expanded its detection protocols, but full operational readiness slipped to early 2032.

### Fractures and Foresight

Despite setbacks, the EU’s focus on observability began to yield technical momentum. Early telemetry pilots in national labs showed promise, and the European AI Office stood up a new monitoring unit. These steps, though narrow, laid groundwork for future control.

Yet political capital dwindled. The cost of maintaining multiple measures, combined with public backlash and internal disunity, strained the Union’s capacity to act.
