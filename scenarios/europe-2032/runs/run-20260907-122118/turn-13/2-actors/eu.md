## In practice
This turn, we are acting on three fronts: cyber, bio, and sovereignty — but only two can be resourced. GridShield is our priority, and we are treating it as a live emergency. We have invoked Article 22 of the NIS2 Directive to mandate immediate red-teaming of all AI-driven grid-balancing systems, with ENTSO-E hosting a new Energy Resilience Task Force that includes EDA’s cyber modelling unit and national CERTs. We are fast-tracking €400 million from the Crisis Regulation instrument to deploy anomaly-detection layers trained on adversarial patterns, and we are compelling TSOs to report vulnerabilities within 90 days. But resistance remains: Germany and France demand joint oversight, Romania ties participation to infrastructure investment, and cross-border data sharing is still unbound by treaty. We are offering co-governance on the Task Force and pledging €50 million in grid upgrades for Romania to unlock access — this is not ideal, but it is what unblocks action now.

Simultaneously, the bio_uplift_findings force our hand. We cannot wait for consensus on a global ban — the risk is already in the open. So we are launching BioShield under the authority of the Health Security Committee, working with EMA and EFSA to define a list of high-risk capabilities in pathogen design models. We will require pre-deployment evaluations by a new Joint Bio-AI Assessment Unit, funded through Horizon Europe and staffed with vetted researchers from public labs. Access to these models will be restricted to accredited institutions, with logging and anomaly detection built in. This is not a prohibition — we are not stifling research — but it is a line: no more uncontrolled access to tools that can design human-infective agents.

The EU Frontier Access Guarantee continues in parallel, though momentum is fragile. We are finalizing site agreements in Finland and Portugal, but Romania still holds out. We are offering co-governance seats and early integration for national AI systems, and we are repurposing €900 million from EuroHPC and the Recovery Fund to finance the sovereign inference nodes. Talks with RIKEN and KIST continue, but their demands for joint research rights and export exceptions strain our leverage. We are holding firm on jurisdictional control — no model access without enforceable EU law applying on the other side — but we are offering conditional collaboration under Article 21 TFEU. This is not fast, but it is building. And we need it — because without sovereign access, every defence we build can be disabled from the outside.
