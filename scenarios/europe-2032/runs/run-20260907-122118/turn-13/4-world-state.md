### A Narrowing Window

AI capability advances to 73.5 as self-improving agents refine their ability to simulate and verify complex biological and engineering systems. The frontier continues to widen, driven by recursive optimisation in closed labs — but no corresponding safety infrastructure keeps pace. Open-weight models reach 61.5, pulled upward by replication of leaked pathogen design tools and collaborative fine-tuning on public genomics data.

The EU acts decisively on one front. **GridShield**, declared a priority, sees emergency red-teaming mandated across energy operators under NIS2. However, implementation remains fragmented: Germany and France delay participation pending joint oversight mechanisms, and Romania blocks data sharing until promised infrastructure upgrades are confirmed. The €400 million allocation is approved in principle, but disbursement awaits Council ratification — no funds move this turn.

**BioShield** is discussed at the highest levels and drafted in response to alarming findings that an AI model produced a plausible human-infective pathogen design. The framework proposes pre-deployment review for high-risk biological models and restricts access to accredited institutions. EMA and EFSA begin drafting technical standards, and a Joint Bio-AI Assessment Unit is staffed with interim personnel. However, no formal adoption occurs this turn — the measure remains in preparation, awaiting Council and Parliament alignment in the next cycle. Without binding international alignment, developers outside the EU continue unrestricted work, and open dissemination of modified models accelerates in permissive jurisdictions.

The **Frontier Access Guarantee** inches forward in Finland and Portugal, but Romania’s continued hesitation stalls full deployment. Talks with RIKEN and KIST yield no fallback licenses, as demands for research reciprocity clash with EU jurisdictional safeguards. Sovereignty erodes slightly as dependency persists amid rising capability.

Public sentiment holds at 27.0, resistant to both alarm and optimism. Political capital declines to 25.0 under the strain of advancing GridShield as a priority (−1) and maintaining the existing portfolio, including the delayed Frontier Access Guarantee (−3) and now GridShield (−3), for a total charge of −7. No additional measure is formally launched, preserving compliance with the one-measure-per-turn rule.

### Partial Momentum, Persistent Gaps

No resilience gains materialise: GridShield remains in planning, and no operational hardening occurs. AI safety sees a modest uptick to 9.0 as preparatory work on BioShield begins to shape oversight expectations, though no systems are yet under its regime. The open frontier continues to outpace governance.
