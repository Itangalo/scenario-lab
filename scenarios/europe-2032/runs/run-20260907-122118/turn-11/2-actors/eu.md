## In practice
This turn, we confront the consequences of exclusion. The eu_frontier_access_denied event landed without warning — a leading model vanished from EU hospital diagnostics, customs automation, and transport logistics systems within hours. No appeal, no explanation. Whether framed as market choice or coercion, the result is the same: critical functions dependent on foreign-controlled AI collapsed mid-cycle. We acted immediately through emergency procurement channels to restore baseline operations using open-weight alternatives, but their capability ceiling — now at 56.5 — falls short of what was lost. The message is clear: reliance on discretionary access is incompatible with autonomy.

We are therefore launching the EU Frontier Access Guarantee under Article 35 of the Treaty on European Union (solidarity clause) and the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions, leveraging the Anti-Coercion Instrument to negotiate binding access terms with non-U.S. aligned developers and repurposing EuroHPC funds to build sovereign inference capacity. This means standing up three new sovereign inference nodes — in Finland, Portugal, and Romania — secured under EU law, with pre-negotiated fallback licenses from Japanese and Korean labs developing outside U.S. export control reach. We are offering member states fast-track integration into these nodes in exchange for harmonising data-sharing rules under the Data Act. Resistance from Germany and France — concerned about duplication and cost — is being met with guarantees of interoperability and co-governance. Without this, every crisis will force us to beg. With it, we begin to close the gap between our mandates: defending against harm requires not just reaction, but the power to act independently in the first place.
