### A Surge in Capability, a Cascade of Crises

AI capability advances to 72.5 as self-improving agents refine their ability to generate verification protocols across clinical and engineering domains. The breakthrough in automated validation widens the frontier, accelerating progress in safety-critical systems — though ironically, no such assurance is applied to the models driving it. Open-weight models rise to 59.5, benefiting from leaked training data and collaborative replication efforts, narrowing the gap with closed systems.

A major cyber incident strikes: a coordinated ransomware campaign, model-generated and highly adaptive, breaches public health and transport systems across three member states. Defenders are overwhelmed, and recovery takes weeks. The attack exploited vulnerabilities first flagged in open-weight simulations — a warning now tragically validated.

Simultaneously, a biosecurity controversy erupts. A genome model produces a plausible human-infective pathogen design. While contested, the mere plausibility triggers alarm. The incident remains contained within expert circles — for now — but signals a dangerous new threshold.

The EU responds with urgency. The **GridShield** programme is proposed as a large Category 6 measure and declared a priority to counter the emergent grid instability surge. Emergency powers under NIS2 are invoked to mandate red-teaming of AI-driven grid systems, and a €400 million allocation is proposed to fund anomaly detection layers. However, no final funding source is secured — discussions on budget reallocation remain ongoing, and borrowing mechanisms face resistance in the Council. Without a confirmed envelope, disbursement cannot begin.

Cross-border coordination through ENTSO-E and the EDA is proposed, but implementation stalls. Some member states delay assessments, citing sovereignty concerns, and refuse to share real-time grid data. Germany and France express support in principle but demand joint oversight; Romania conditions participation on prior infrastructure investment. No binding agreements are reached, and no operational red-teaming commences this turn.

Meanwhile, the **Frontier Access Guarantee** progresses slowly. Site agreements in Finland and Portugal advance, but Romania hesitates, demanding greater infrastructure investment. Talks with RIKEN and KIST yield no binding fallback licenses yet. Germany and France remain lukewarm, despite co-governance offers.

Public trust remains strained after the cyberattack and benefit scandal, but no further collapse occurs — sentiment holds at 27.0. Political capital is not yet depleted, as GridShield has not entered implementation; the proposal consumes planning reserves but avoids full commitment. Sovereignty remains unchanged, as no new access or capacity offsets the ongoing dependence.

### Bottom-Up Momentum, Top-Down Friction

The grassroots **sovereign tech alliance** gains traction, with cities piloting lightweight AI for grid management. Yet without formal support, its impact remains local. The EU acts — but faces gridlock, underfunding, and fragmentation — and no resilience gains are realized this turn.
