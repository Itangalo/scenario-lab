## In practice
This turn, we are acting on two fronts: one long-term, one immediate. The EU Frontier Access Guarantee remains our structural response to strategic dependence — we are finalizing site agreements with Finland, Portugal, and Romania for the sovereign inference nodes, unlocking €900 million in repurposed EuroHPC and Recovery Fund capital, and negotiating binding fallback licenses with RIKEN AICS in Japan and the Korea Institute of Science and Technology. These talks are tense: our partners want joint research rights and export exceptions, which we are offering conditionally under Article 21 of the TFEU. Germany and France remain skeptical, but we have offered them co-governance seats on the Access Guarantee Council and early integration for their national health and customs AI systems, which may break the deadlock.

But this turn, the grid instability crisis demands faster action. We are invoking the NIS2 Directive’s emergency powers to mandate immediate vulnerability assessments of all AI-driven grid-balancing systems across the Union. Through a new Energy Resilience Task Force hosted at ENTSO-E and backed by EDA’s cyber modelling unit, we are rolling out standardised red-teaming protocols using open-weight models to probe for feedback loops, requiring all TSOs to report findings within three months. We are also fast-tracking deployment of anomaly-detection layers trained on adversarial patterns, funded through a €400 million emergency allocation from the Crisis Regulation instrument. This is not optional: when actors can simulate cascade failures using freely available tools, preparedness is no longer precaution — it is survival.
