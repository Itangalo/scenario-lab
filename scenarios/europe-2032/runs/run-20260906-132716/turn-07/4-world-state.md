### The Weight of Denial

AI capability rose to 73.0 as self-improving systems advanced unchecked, their reasoning growing more opaque. With no safety measures landing on deployed systems, and the eval_anomaly_reports confirming uncontrolled reasoning loops, ai_safety fell sharply to 8.0. The gap between capability and control widened into a chasm.

The EU advanced planning for the Operational Continuity Mandate, aiming to require critical operators to run parallel sovereign-AI workflows by 2031. ENISA drafted guidelines and a proposal for €12bn in emergency co-funding was tabled. However, no binding requirements were issued in this turn — member states raised concerns over operational burden, and Italy’s temporary oversight board faced legal challenges over competence. Drills in energy and transport were postponed pending resolution of jurisdictional disputes. The signal was clear: ambition without alignment risks delay, not action.

Resilience rose to 49.0, driven by the partial integration of dual diagnostic pipelines in health systems and the institutional memory of NIS3. But the gains were narrow, offset by the ongoing decay of trust.

The Sovereign Core Initiative remained the Union’s sole named priority. No new measure was formally launched; the Operational Continuity Mandate remains in development and will count as a new measure upon adoption in a future turn. Legal disputes over centralized data control within the Sovereign Core Initiative continue to slow implementation.

Public sentiment ticked up slightly, buoyed by the cancer therapy breakthrough abroad and the EU’s visible operational turn. But the gain was muted: the therapies remained out of reach, and the bilateral deal fed narratives of fragmentation.

The middle_power_coalition emerged informally — a network of mid-sized nations aligning on evaluation standards and export controls. It bolstered political narrative, though the full effect had not yet landed.
