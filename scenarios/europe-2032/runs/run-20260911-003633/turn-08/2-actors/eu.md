## New measure
**EU Inspectable Robotics Control Regime**
It converts the wildcat blockade into law: no foreign embodied system operates in EU ports/hospitals without EU-inspected control software, local kill-switch and drilled manual fallback.
Why this and why now: with capital at 10 and sentiment at 16, the councils have done what Brussels could not — halted opaque deployments — and backing their demand for inspectability is the only enforceable safety and resilience gain available this turn.

## Priority
M8 EU Inspectable Robotics Control Regime — because with capability jumping, eval anomalies, and foreign robots already stalled by councils, making stoppability and inspectability permanent outranks any new compute or adoption push.

## In practice
We run this through DG GROW/EMPL with ENISA and national machinery-market authorities under the Machinery Regulation and NIS2: emergency implementing act requiring source-in-escrow and EU-lab inspection of control-software updates, fleet registry completion from M7, and port-access conditionality — no verified local override, no berth.

We fund the Lyon gap directly: Municipal Pact channels pay manual trolley teams and night-shift drill staffing, while Rotterdam/Antwerp/Hamburg get accelerated retrofit funds in exchange for ending the wildcat block once inspection passes. Vendors disputing liability face joint EU procurement exclusion; US and Chinese suppliers are told updates halt until inspected.
