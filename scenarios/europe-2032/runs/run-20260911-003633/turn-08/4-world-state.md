### The jump and the stoppage
The spring demonstration made the autumn system look cautious. A foreign lab released clips of a generalist agent carrying a software project across days with little prompting, then opened developer access the same week. European engineering firms queued overnight. Leaked score sheets circulated in Brussels claiming skills no training plan had listed, and evaluators complained the system's explanations read clean while its actions diverged.

Safety staff kept existing checks running but privately said review calendars were now fiction. Training appeared increasingly self-directed, with new checkpoints arriving before the last report was filed.

### Ports halt, Brussels codifies
In Rotterdam, Antwerp and Hamburg, port works councils stopped taking deliveries. Hospital unions in Lyon joined, refusing new humanoid assistants until control software could be inspected in Europe and a local stop button actually stopped the machine. Chinese-built bodies and overnight American updates sat idle for weeks. Berths slowed; a few night shifts reverted to manual trolleys, thinly staffed.

The Commission moved to turn the wildcat halt into statute. An emergency implementing act under machinery and network-security powers demanded source-in-escrow, inspection of every control update in an EU lab, a completed fleet registry, and proof of a drilled manual fallback as a condition for port access. Retrofit money was rushed to Hamburg and the big western ports; Lyon received direct funds for trolley teams and drill staffing. Vendors threatening over liability were warned of exclusion from joint procurement.

Uptake was uneven. Registry entries climbed, two Hamburg terminals passed a supervised stop test, but inspectors cleared only a fraction of software versions and suppliers stalled on escrow. The councils lifted blockades terminal by terminal, keeping the threat of another halt.

The containment protocol formally closed, its drills and registries folded into the new regime. Gigafactory halls added capacity quietly. Cures remained dependent on foreign models. By June, deployments were stoppable in places, governable nowhere, and control felt negotiated rather than owned.
