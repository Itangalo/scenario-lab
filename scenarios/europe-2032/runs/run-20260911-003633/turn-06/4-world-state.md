### Stoppable robots, unstoppable models
January opened with a foreign lab demonstration that made last autumn's leaked benchmarks look modest. A generalist agent system planned, coded and operated tools over days with little supervision, and within weeks it was in developer hands abroad. European firms queued for access while safety researchers warned the reasoning traces were far less legible than before.

At the same time, commercial humanoids and warehouse fleets arrived in European ports. Chinese-built bodies running American control software began unloading, sorting and, in two hospital groups, ferrying linen and medicines. Works councils in Rotterdam, Antwerp and Lyon called it a second exposure: the industrial base Europe still led in was suddenly run on machines it did not build and software it could not inspect.

Brussels answered with what it could pass. The municipal pact became the daily priority — staffing money, insurer backstop renewals and wage-bridge payouts pushed through employment and regional channels, with mayors filmed beside shorter waiting lists. The two gigafactory sites kept grid priority while other sites stayed frozen for lack of chips and money.

The new robotics protocol ordered a fleet registry, audited kill-switches and manual-fallback drills in ports and hospitals. Operators complied on paper; drills slipped. A Hamburg terminal exercise stopped half the fleet but left the rest circling because the override needed a cloud link. A Lyon clinic kept manual trolleys, then lacked staff to push them.

The two large programmes completed: the sovereignty package's permits and capital lines, and the bio-shield's monitoring and stockpiles. Engineers celebrated quietly; voters did not. Small-town press covered robot arrivals as job notices, and talk of structured American access — still promised, not yet operating — read as dependence confirmed.

By June, hospitals held, ports moved, but control felt thinner. Systems did more, were understood less, and the machines that carried goods could soon carry other loads.
