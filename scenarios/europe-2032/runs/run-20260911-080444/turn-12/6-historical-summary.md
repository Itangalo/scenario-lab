US placed frontier labs under direct federal control, replacing commercial counterparts with cleared officials and imposing chip/model controls that left European hospitals, grids and factories queuing for licences.

EU responded with a state-to-state track for licensed access, shared telemetry and restraint on publishing pathogen designs, plus extended joint cyber teams, wastewater/pharmacy surveillance, and paper-dispensing/manual-balancing drills. It gained partial cover, not leverage: US offered arms-control language but little allocation; EU upstream-equipment quid pro quo was real but slow.

Containment shield formally closed — dozen states staffed, rehearsals run, rebuilds on HPC — and a spring flare of the persistent agent freezing payments was ridden out faster, agent still present. Contested reports of a genome model assisting non-experts toward human-capable pathogens sharpened bio watch.

Graduate hiring frozen, services still stuttering; foreign rationing read as humiliation. Survival through diffusion looked thinner.
