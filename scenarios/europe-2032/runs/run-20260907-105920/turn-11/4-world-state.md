### The Fracture Deepens

The European Union declares emergency mobilisation under Article 122 TFEU, launching the **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation Directive** to fast-track exascale AI infrastructure in Finland, Spain, and Romania. The proposal grants the Commission authority to suspend national permitting processes and mandates grid priority for designated sovereign compute sites. It also calls for private hyperscalers operating in the EU to contribute 10% of their EU-based training cycles to a newly formed Sovereign AI Reserve.

However, implementation is far from immediate. Legal challenges are filed by two member states at the CJEU, contesting the scope of Article 122 and the Commission’s authority to override national energy and planning regimes. ENTSO-E issues a formal warning about grid stability risks, and no physical infrastructure breaks ground this turn. The directive enters a contested adoption phase, with enforcement suspended pending preliminary rulings — consistent with the two-turn minimum for high-cost measures.

Simultaneously, the **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** begins red-teaming critical systems, but integration with national CERTs remains incomplete. Only three countries fully share data, citing legal and sovereignty concerns. The **Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps**, delayed by unresolved legal disputes, still lacks operational authority, leaving a gap between detection and response.

### A Sovereignty Eroded, Not Built

No new sovereign compute capacity comes online. The legal and political resistance proves too significant for rapid deployment, and the directive remains in early, contested implementation. A **member state defection** occurs: one country finalises a bilateral deal with a foreign hyperscaler, securing exclusive access to advanced models in exchange for data sovereignty concessions — directly undermining the EU’s unified stance.

Public sentiment remains stagnant at 20.0, with growing unease over top-down mandates and perceived overreach. No visible public benefit emerges, and the aggressive legal posture fuels resentment.
