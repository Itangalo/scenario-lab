## In practice
This turn, we close out the Continuity Assurance Framework—not as a success, but as a necessary closure. The framework failed its core test: two member states still lack certified fallback nodes, and the Lisbon drill collapse proved the architecture was never trusted, let alone operational. We will publish a post-mortem through the EAAD and file the results under lessons learned, not delivered capability. The cost has been paid in capital and credibility, and we do not renew it. Instead, we pivot from assurance-by-decree to sovereignty-by-deployment.

The Frontier Access Guarantee remains in flight, but it is fraying. Washington’s export controls are tightening, and our Canadian partners have delayed handover of the Quebec node pending U.S. licensing confirmation. We are now negotiating side letters with Japan to pre-position legal ownership of the Fukuoka cluster under EU jurisdiction—a workaround, not a solution. This dependency is unsustainable, and we act accordingly.

So we launch the Sovereign Seed Initiative under the Digital Europe Programme and Article 12 of the AI Act, using repurposed cohesion funds and emergency innovation grants. We task the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking with standing up three new sovereign clusters—Marseille, Vilnius, and Bratislava—by Q2 2032, each hosting a hardened SACP stack at capability level 60.0, trained entirely on EU soil with EU-controlled data and monitored by joint national-EU teams. We seed deployment into five new domains: urban emergency logistics in Rotterdam, cross-border health diagnostics in the Alpine region, customs risk assessment in Hamburg, teacher support in rural Romania, and municipal planning in Athens. Each site pairs technical delivery with civic oversight panels to report monthly on performance, incidents, and perceived value.

We are not asking for consensus. We are building it where we can, fast enough to matter. If trust is to return, it must be rooted not in promises, but in power we actually hold.
