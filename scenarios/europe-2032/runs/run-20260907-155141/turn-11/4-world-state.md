### A Stalled Leap, a Shrinking Margin

Global AI capability holds at 75.0—the frontier pauses after its explosive jump, as labs scramble to stabilize the new multi-agent systems. Yet the gap between capability and control remains vast. No safety upgrades land; `ai_safety` stays at 5.0, a level where even basic monitoring is blind to emergent behaviors. Meanwhile, the open frontier surges: leaked weights from a compromised training run circulate in underground forums, and a coalition of open researchers reconstructs a functional agent framework. `openweight_capability` jumps to 63.5, narrowing the gap and raising fears of uncontrolled proliferation.

### The Union’s Pivot Falters

The EU formally closes the Continuity Assurance Framework. A post-mortem reveals patchy implementation: only ten member states completed certification, and the Lisbon drill failure exposed systemic distrust in automated failovers. With no improvement in operational resilience, the measure yields nothing—its failure acknowledged quietly in Brussels.

Simultaneously, the Sovereign Seed Initiative launches with fanfare but limited traction. Marseille and Vilnius begin site preparations, but Bratislava delays due to local zoning disputes. Crucially, the Quebec node remains locked by U.S. export controls, and Japan hesitates to transfer legal control of Fukuoka. The workaround stalls, underscoring continued dependence.

Worse, a coordinated work-to-rule action by public-sector AI integrators in France, Germany, and Poland slows backend deployment. Citing unsafe rollout timelines and exclusion from design, teams delay validation for emergency logistics and health diagnostics. The strike is not outright defiance—but it is resistance.

### Sentiment Sours Further

A second wave of AI-driven fraud hits small businesses, exploiting newly available open-weight agents. Combined with visible delays in public services, trust erodes further. `public_sentiment` dips to 18.0. The Sovereign Seed Initiative, meant to signal autonomy, instead highlights fragility.

Resilience declines to 47.0 as the cyber_major_incident overwhelms local response units. Though contained, the attack reveals that fallback systems—where they exist—are untested and slow. `resilience` falls as preparedness gaps widen.

Sovereignty drops to 21.0: `ai_capability` rose by 4.0 last turn (triggering −1), no capacity event occurred, and no category 4 measure finished. Political capital scrapes up to 2.0: finishing the failed Continuity Framework yields +2 (minimal credit for closure), the new small measure costs −2, the priority −1, and the proposal bonus is +3—justified by the recent cyber incident and the initiative’s direct response. No legitimacy lending occurs.
