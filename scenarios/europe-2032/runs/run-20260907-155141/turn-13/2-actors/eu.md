This turn, we formally close the Continuity Assurance Framework. The Lisbon drill collapse is not buried—it is highlighted. The framework delivered no resilience gain, and its cost in credibility was high. We do not renew it. Instead, we shift from structural assurance to active containment.

The Frontier Access Guarantee remains in flight, but it is now clearly a stopgap under strain. Quebec remains blocked by U.S. export controls, and Fukuoka’s legal transfer is stalled in Tokyo. We are not waiting. We accelerate the Sovereign Seed Initiative’s core compute deployment in Marseille, Vilnius, and Bratislava through emergency provisions in the Digital Europe Programme and invoke Article 12(3) of the AI Act to fast-track permitting. We redirect €1.2 billion in unused cohesion funds to cover grid upgrades and security hardening, and task the EuroHPC JU with standing up the first sovereign clusters by Q3. But we know: compute without control is danger. So we act in parallel.

We draft the directive through COREPER II in three weeks, backed by the Commission’s crisis powers. We designate five EU-certified labs—two national, three cross-border—to begin adversarial testing on all high-capability systems in energy, transport, health, and customs. Resistance is expected—Germany’s digital ministry warns of disruption to hospital diagnostics, and France’s AI taskforce demands oversight parity. We offer compromise: joint audit teams, phased rollout over three months, and real-time data sharing with national authorities. But the baseline is non-negotiable: no more blind deployment. If we cannot see what the systems are doing, we cannot allow them to act. This is not sovereignty yet—but it is the first reassertion of control.
