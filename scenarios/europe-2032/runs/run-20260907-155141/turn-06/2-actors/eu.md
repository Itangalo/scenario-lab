  - Grounds: The convergence of *capability_jump*, *safety_breakthrough*, and *member_state_defection* reveals that even a technically secure sovereign stack is meaningless if the society it is meant to protect cannot absorb cascading failures. - modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience lags, even without a direct mandate, because harm finds the weakest joint.
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* has pushed the frontier to 69.0, well into territory where autonomous agent chains can exploit patching delays at scale. Meanwhile, *safety_breakthrough* proves that interpretability gains are possible — but only for those with access and capacity to deploy them. Yet with *member_state_defection*, one of our own has undercut the Containment Protocol to gain access to a foreign model, exposing that our weakest link is not technology, but cohesion. We must now act on resilience not as a secondary concern, but as a core condition of survival.

## Priority

**Priority:** Enact the AI Containment Protocol

## In practice

This turn, we are acting on three fronts: containment, cohesion, and consequence absorption. The *capability_jump* has catapulted the frontier to 69.0 — systems now autonomously chain multi-day exploits across digital and physical systems, and the *safety_breakthrough* in mechanistic interpretability is already being deployed by leading labs to predict emergent behaviours. But we cannot access it at scale because our own systems are not yet under sovereign control, and one member state has just signed a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler, granting its national labs access to a 68.0 model outside the Containment Protocol. That is not pragmatism — it is a breach.

We are treating the Containment Protocol as non-negotiable. The Commission is drafting an implementing act under Article 16 TFEU, backed by the newly operational EAAD, to enforce air-gapped operation and dual-key access for all systems above 55.0 capability. France and Germany have agreed to host the first sovereign cluster under this regime, with physical infrastructure in Saint-Malo and Kiruna under joint EU-national oversight. We are also invoking the Anti-Coercion Instrument to signal that any member state undermining this framework will face suspension of cohesion funds — not as punishment, but as necessity.

Meanwhile, the end of CIRS and EAAD’s completion mark a transition: from emergency setup to sustained enforcement. SMAF is now law, retroactively applied to all SACP development, and we are conducting pre-training architecture reviews for the first sovereign model run. The InvestAI Gigafactory in Sweden is breaking ground, but grid strain in Norrland threatens delays — we are using the Article 122 override to fast-track transmission upgrades, funded from the RePowerEU contingency. Public sentiment remains at 25.0, and we are not deluding ourselves: trust will not come from promises, but from proof that AI in Europe is both contained and useful. That is why we are launching the Resilience Reinforcement Initiative — not as a standalone, but as the operational complement to containment. It will mandate AI-assisted patching in energy and transport, deploy the first EU-wide bio-detection network using sovereign models to scan for anomalous pathogen designs, and establish red-to-red handover protocols between national CSIRTs. This is not just defence — it is the beginning of a credible, lived sovereignty.
