### Placements against the queue
Spring brought no new programme, only the slow clearing of the old one. ESF+ arrears moved, and several thousand more young graduates entered twelve-month posts in wards, town halls and the guarded perimeters of half-built compute sites. Mayors photographed the arrivals. Job boards in Leuven, Bologna and Brno stayed empty all the same, and firms kept subsidised juniors without restoring open hiring.

Hospitals and ports held in their degraded rhythm. Joint response teams patched intrusions quickly; ships waited longer; clinics rationed non-urgent slots. Fenced sites were guarded as routine, cables cut and repaired as routine.

### A lawful wrong
The season's politics belonged to a benefits and policing support system. An ombudsman and then a court found it had systematically downgraded disability claims and flagged fraud risks by neighbourhood, for years. The Commission's review conceded the harder point: the deployment had never fallen inside the high-risk categories written in 2024, so every step had been lawful. Redress was promised, logs ordered re-read, a narrow patch to the rulebook announced.

Editorials called the law badly drawn. Restrictionists gained a cheap argument; administrators froze adoption projects rather than risk the next finding.

### Cures on other people's machines
Two American announcements sharpened the mood. A laboratory reported an algorithmic advance that made a class of materials modelling cheap, with licensing tied to its own cloud. Clinics elsewhere began offering tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions, available to Europeans only through foreign-controlled capacity. Procurement officers in Lyon and Gdansk were told to buy only where the compute sat under European control; elsewhere patients read about cures they could not be offered at home.

Freight insurers raised surcharges as warships exercised around the Strait. Brussels triggered stockpiles and insurer coordination under existing emergency regimes, calming ports without calming politics. Daily use of assistants continued, and daily resentment with it.
