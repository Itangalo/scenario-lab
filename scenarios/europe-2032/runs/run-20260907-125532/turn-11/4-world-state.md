### A Turn of Converging Crises

Three shocks strike simultaneously, reshaping the strategic landscape. A major cyber incident—powered by AI-generated exploit chains—disrupts public services across five member states. Hospitals, transit systems, and local governments face cascading outages. The attack’s speed and scale overwhelm legacy defences, and attribution remains uncertain. ENISA leads a forensic effort under Article 12, with MPAC allies, including newly onboarded Canada, sharing intelligence in real time.

At the same time, a blockade around Taiwan halts advanced semiconductor shipments. The EU’s planned compute expansion, including upgrades to the Sovereign AI Core, grinds to a halt. Without access to cutting-edge chips, frontier progress risks stalling within 18 months. The Commission activates the European Chips Act crisis protocol, assessing whether leverage in lithography equipment can be traded for guaranteed supply.

Meanwhile, a breakthrough in automated verification—specifically in clinical coding—reveals that domains once thought to require human oversight now admit cheap, scalable correctness checks. This accelerates AI deployment in healthcare, but not because systems are safer: because the illusion of control grows. The EAAD launches a fast-cycle evaluation to assess whether this pattern generalises. While promising, the advance remains narrow and has not yet triggered broad model proliferation or open-weight diffusion. The expected spillover into general-purpose capability is delayed by integration bottlenecks and IP restrictions.

The EU responds with triage, not action. No new measures are launched. The EIB pauses disbursements on compute infrastructure. DG GROW and DG SANTE map the ripple effects. Council preparations begin to declare the semiconductor crisis a systemic risk, unlocking emergency flexibilities.

Yet the pause comes at a cost. Public sentiment plummets as job losses in healthcare administration and IT support surge. Media frames the cyberattack as a failure of AI governance. Labour unions intensify calls for deployment moratoria. Resilience erodes as systems prove brittle. Safety declines further as frontier models outpace interpretability. Sovereignty drops as compute ambitions stall. Political capital holds only due to prior gains from MPAC.

The open-weight community makes incremental progress, building on existing models, but cannot leap ahead without hardware or leaked frontier systems. A 1.5-point rise in `openweight_capability` reflects this slow accumulation—consistent with the underlying pace of `ai_capability` growth.

### No Move, But Not No Cost

Inaction is not neutrality. The world moves faster than the Union can assess.
