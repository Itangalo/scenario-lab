Three simultaneous crises—cyberattacks disrupting public services, a Taiwan blockade halting semiconductor supplies, and a narrow breakthrough in automated clinical verification—expose systemic vulnerabilities. The cyberattack overwhelms legacy defenses across five member states; MPAC enables real-time intelligence sharing, with Canada now fully integrated. The chip shortage stalls EU compute expansion and frontier AI progress, risking stagnation within 18 months, prompting activation of the European Chips Act crisis protocol. Automated verification advances in healthcare create an illusion of control, accelerating deployment without improving safety, while broader spillover is delayed by integration and IP barriers.

The EU responds with triage: no new initiatives launched, EIB pauses infrastructure funding, and Council prepares to declare a systemic risk designation for semiconductors. Public sentiment declines sharply as job losses spread into healthcare and IT; media and unions blame AI governance failures. Open-weight models advance slightly (+1.5) through incremental gains, but hardware constraints prevent leapfrogging. Safety and sovereignty decline due to eroding interpretability and stalled compute ambitions, while political capital stabilizes temporarily on prior MPAC achievements. Inaction proves costly—resilience weakens as external shocks outpace institutional response.
