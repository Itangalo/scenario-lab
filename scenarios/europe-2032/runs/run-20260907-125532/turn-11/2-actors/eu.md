## In practice  
We are in a moment of rupture. Three shocks hit simultaneously: a large-scale, model-generated cyberattack has landed across public services in five member states, exploiting automated vulnerability discovery and deployment at speed and scale that overwhelmed legacy defences; the blockade around Taiwan has severed access to advanced-node semiconductors, freezing planned upgrades to the Sovereign AI Core and threatening to stall all EU frontier progress within 18 months; and verification_widens has taken hold in clinical coding — a domain long assumed to require expert human validation now admits cheap, automated correctness checks, accelerating AI adoption in healthcare systems and fuelling pressure to deploy despite safety concerns.

We are not moving. Not yet. Instead, we are convening emergency sessions across three tracks. First, under Article 12 of the Cybersecurity Act, ENISA is leading a cross-border forensic analysis of the attack, with real-time data sharing activated through the MPAC intelligence-sharing tier — Canada has fast-tracked its domestic approval to join. Second, the Commission has launched a strategic review of the semiconductor crisis through the European Chips Act crisis protocol, assessing whether our upstream leverage in lithography equipment can be converted into guaranteed access via barter, co-production, or coercion. Third, the EAAD is running a fast-cycle evaluation on whether the new verification regime in clinical coding generalises — if it does, safety margins shrink not because models are safer, but because the illusion of control grows.

This is not passivity. It is triage. We are directing the EIB to hold disbursements on all new compute infrastructure until the supply chain review reports back. We are tasking DG GROW and DG SANTE with mapping the ripple effects of automated clinical coding — if verification widens here, it will widen elsewhere, and fast. And we are preparing Council conclusions under the Foreign Affairs configuration to declare the taiwan_blockade a systemic risk to EU strategic autonomy, which would unlock emergency procurement and state-aid flexibilities under Article 106a of the TFEU.

We act under uncertainty, yes — but not in blindness. This turn, seeing clearly *is* the action.
