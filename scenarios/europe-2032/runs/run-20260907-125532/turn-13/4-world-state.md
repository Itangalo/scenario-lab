### The Frontier Goes Open

A major open-weight model, emerging just months after the closed frontier, achieves functional parity and spreads rapidly across consumer GPUs, private servers, and distributed networks. The diffusion is swift and decentralized, marking a turning point in global AI accessibility. However, integration remains uneven—optimization lags, tooling is incomplete, and deployment at scale requires time. As a result, `openweight_capability` rises to 59.0, reflecting meaningful but incremental progress in adoption and stabilization. The gap with the frontier narrows, but full equivalence remains out of reach, and the open-weight capability stays safely below the advancing `ai_capability`, which reaches 72.5.

### Hardening the Core

The EU proposes the Sovereign Core Hardening Protocol this turn, a new high-cost measure aimed at securing central AI infrastructure. ENISA and the European Defence Agency begin planning military-grade intrusion detection at key nodes, with air-gapped backups proposed in Finland and Sweden. Real-time replication links are scoped for HPC clusters in Toulouse and Stuttgart. However, no deployment occurs yet—German federal authorities raise immediate objections to military integration in civilian systems, and France demands veto rights over access protocols. Legal and governance disputes delay execution, and no implementation funding is released. As a high-cost measure, the Protocol will take at least two turns to have any effect.

### Sovereignty on the Brink

No new domestic compute comes online, and the Emergency Compute Lifeline remains stalled in negotiations. With `ai_capability` rising to 72.5 and no expansion of sovereign capacity, `eu_ai_sovereignty` declines to 10.0—now critically low. The Core remains exposed and increasingly fragile in a world where powerful models are widely accessible.

### Political Strain Mounts

The EU introduces only one new measure this turn: the Sovereign Core Hardening Protocol. The Emergency Compute Lifeline remains ongoing (as recorded in the notepad), but no expansion or new action occurs. The new proposal qualifies for a +3 proposal bonus, as it directly responds to last turn’s semiconductor disruption. With the new measure introduced, a −1 priority cost applies. However, only one measure is launched, keeping compliance with the one-per-turn rule. Political capital adjusts accordingly: −3 from the ongoing Lifeline, −1 for priority, +3 for proposal bonus, and −1 for the new high-cost measure initiation, resulting in a net change of −2. After accounting for last turn’s political capital (16.0), the corrected value is 15.0. Public sentiment remains at 9.0, reflecting persistent public unease. The system remains under strain, but no cascading failure occurs.
