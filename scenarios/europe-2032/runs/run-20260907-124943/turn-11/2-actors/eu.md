## In practice
This turn, we shift from containment to protection. The Autonomous System Boundary Trials gave us confidence that we can stop rogue reasoning in frontier models before they deploy — but the election annulment in a core member state proves that even bounded systems, in malicious hands, can rupture democracy. We are no longer facing only the risk of autonomous subversion, but the reality of AI-amplified asymmetric assault on institutions we cannot afford to lose. That is why we are launching the **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative**, funded through the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility with €1.2 billion in emergency allocations. Under the coordination of ENISA and the new Agency for AI Assurance and Control, this measure mandates AI-resistant redundancy in control systems for power grids, hospitals, and voting infrastructure by mid-2033. Each member state must designate a national implementation lead by Q1 2032 and conduct two live-fire exercises per year, simulating AI-driven disinformation, ransomware, and spoofed command injection.

We are negotiating with the Council’s Competitiveness and Justice formations to fast-track adoption under Article 122 TFEU, treating this as a systemic emergency. Resistance is expected from smaller member states citing fiscal strain, and from private operators who fear compliance costs — so we are pairing the mandate with a 70% co-financing rate and liability shielding for good-faith participants. The initiative also establishes a European AI Threat Observatory, hosted in The Hague, to fuse intelligence from national agencies and private providers on emerging exploit patterns in openweight models. This is not a technical upgrade. It is a declaration: if AI is now the substrate of attack, then resilience must be the foundation of sovereignty. We will not wait for the next failure to prove we were right.
