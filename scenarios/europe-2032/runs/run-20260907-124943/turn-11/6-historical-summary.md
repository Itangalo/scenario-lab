In 2031, following an AI-driven election annulment and escalating cyber threats, the EU launches the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative—a category 6 response mandating AI-resistant redundancy across energy, health, and electoral systems, supported by a new AI Threat Observatory in The Hague. Despite 70% co-financing, implementation is delayed due to fiscal concerns from smaller member states and resistance from private operators, with full rollout postponed to the next turn. The completed Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme successfully deploys neuromorphic edge systems in five member states, boosting infrastructure resilience and reducing cloud dependency, though integration remains uneven. 

The European Agency for AI Assurance and Control faces setbacks: legal disputes and a major U.S. developer’s refusal to comply with pre-deployment evaluations stall its activation, while a founding member state’s recognition of foreign certifications undermines regulatory unity. AI capability rises to 70.5, driven by advances in AI-aided chip design and limited leakage of exploit logic into openweight models, which see only modest gains. Resilience improves slightly due to partial deployment of hardened systems, but public sentiment stagnates at 24.0 amid ongoing protests and eroding trust. Political capital declines as the EU struggles to manage multiple priorities under constrained capacity, revealing fragility in institutional cohesion despite urgent action.
