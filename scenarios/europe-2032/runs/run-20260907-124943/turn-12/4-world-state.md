### A Breakthrough and a Breach

The **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative** reaches operational status this turn, with national coordinators in place and live-fire exercises launched in Germany and Finland. The European AI Threat Observatory begins receiving real-time data, marking a milestone in cross-border cyber defence. However, testing reveals persistent vulnerabilities in legacy SCADA systems, forcing a revision of retrofit schedules and delaying full resilience gains. Despite this, the partial deployment and new detection protocols yield measurable improvements, slightly hardening the Union’s posture.

Simultaneously, a **safety breakthrough** emerges from frontier labs: a method to certify behavioural consistency in deployed AI agents gains rapid adoption. The EU moves to leverage the innovation through the **European Agency for AI Assurance and Control**, but progress toward full operational status is stalled. The notepad-recorded `emergent_regulatory_defection` — a major member state unilaterally recognizing foreign AI safety certifications — undermines central authority and triggers legal uncertainty. As a result, the Agency cannot yet assert binding control over foreign high-capability models. Its evaluative framework remains under development, and its enforcement reach is contested.

In response, the Commission fast-tracks the **Secure Open-Source Evaluation Framework**, a small category 5 measure enabling vetted researchers to assess open-weight models above 55.0 capability. The platform is announced and initial design work begins, but it does not become operational this turn. Early engagement includes academic and civil society groups, though participation remains limited pending governance finalization.

AI capability climbs to 72.0, driven by advances in autonomous chip design and training efficiency. Openweight models absorb limited exploit logic, rising to 60.0, as underground cyber tools grow more modular and accessible. While no major incident occurs, the erosion of offensive barriers continues, constraining gains in systemic safety.

Resilience improves modestly, aided by the Cyber Shield’s partial rollout. Public sentiment inches upward as oversight efforts gain visibility, though protests persist. Political capital increases moderately, reflecting momentum on the Cyber Shield and early framework development, but is tempered by institutional fragmentation and delayed Agency empowerment. Sovereignty dips slightly as capability growth outpaces domestic compute expansion.

### Fragile Momentum

The EU advances on technical coordination and transparency planning, but institutional cohesion frays under external and internal pressures. Progress is tangible — yet conditional, partial, and vulnerable to divergence.
