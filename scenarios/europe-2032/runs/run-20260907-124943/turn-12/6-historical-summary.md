In 2031, the EU achieves partial operational status for the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative, with live-fire exercises in Germany and Finland and the activation of real-time monitoring through the AI Threat Observatory. However, legacy SCADA vulnerabilities delay full resilience gains. A breakthrough in behavioural consistency certification boosts AI safety efforts, but the European Agency for AI Assurance and Control remains hampered by legal disputes and a key member state’s unilateral recognition of foreign certifications, undermining regulatory unity. The Commission responds by fast-tracking the Secure Open-Source Evaluation Framework for open-weight models, though it does not become operational this turn. AI capability rises to 72.0 due to advances in chip design and training efficiency, while openweight models reach 60.0 amid growing modularity of cyber tools. Resilience and public sentiment improve slightly, and political capital recovers modestly, yet institutional fragmentation and lagging sovereignty constrain systemic progress. Momentum is fragile, marked by technical gains overshadowed by governance delays and divergent compliance.
