### Capability Creeps Forward

Global AI capability advanced to 57.5 as frontier labs pushed larger training runs and refined agent coordination frameworks. Open-weight models reached 45.0, buoyed by widespread deployment of Kimi K3 derivatives and growing accessibility of fine-tuned offensive cyber tools. Despite rising concern, no structural safety improvements landed on deployed systems. With no new evaluation mandates and interpretability research still siloed, ai_safety dropped by 3 points to 24.0.

### Cyber Shield Now Operational

The *Cyber Shield for Critical Operators* directive entered implementation, with ENISA activating central alert coordination among national CSIRTs. Fast-track consultations in Slovakia and Croatia helped shape initial escalation protocols, marking progress in cross-border cyber resilience. The framework is now law, and enforcement begins in Q1 2028, co-financed at 70%. This strengthens the EU’s ability to detect and respond to AI-driven intrusions.

### Sovereignty Push Meets Resistance

The Commission intensified efforts to unblock the *Tech Sovereignty Package*, dispatching envoys and reclassifying AI data centres as strategic infrastructure. The EIB’s fast-track financing window opened, but no applications followed. Member states remained hesitant to commit anchor demand, citing budget constraints and uncertain operational needs. The Joint Research Centre’s demand forecast was delayed again, leaving investment justification in limbo. *InvestAI Gigafactories* remains stalled, with no site selection possible without national commitments.

### Political Capital Holds Steady

With *Cyber Shield* completed, the EU gained +4 to political capital. The portfolio charge for three large measures (−9) and the priority cost (−1) were offset by this gain and a +2 boost from improved resilience. No new measure was launched, avoiding further strain. Public sentiment held at 38.0, as technical progress was overshadowed by visible delays in sovereignty.
