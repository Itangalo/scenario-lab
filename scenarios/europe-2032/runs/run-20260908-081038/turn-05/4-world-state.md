### US Election Sets Harder Tone

The United States elected a new president who treats advanced AI as a strategic asset, not a commercial product. Washington will now explicitly control frontier model exports, rationing access by country tier and aligning distribution with national security interests. For the EU, this means dependence is no longer just a vulnerability—it is a lever now firmly in American hands. The AI Act’s influence weakens as compliance becomes the price of access, not a standard-setter in its own right.

### Cyber Shield Completes, Resilience Rises

The *Cyber Shield for Critical Operators* directive is now fully operational, with ENISA coordinating real-time alerts among national CSIRTs. Fast-track consultations in Slovakia and Croatia helped refine escalation protocols, marking a tangible improvement in cross-border cyber response. The 70% co-funding model under the Digital Europe Programme ensures broad adoption, and resilience gains +2 as a result.

### Sovereignty Efforts Stumble

Despite high-level diplomatic pushes to Helsinki, Sofia, and Dublin, the *Tech Sovereignty Package* remains stalled. Member states hesitate to commit anchor demand, citing fiscal constraints and uncertain operational needs. The Joint Research Centre’s compute demand forecast is delayed again—now not expected until 2029. Without binding commitments, private investors hold back. The EIB’s fast-track financing window opens, but sees no takers. The *InvestAI Gigafactories* initiative remains frozen at the planning stage.

With *Cyber Shield* completed, the EU gains +4 political capital. However, the ongoing costs of three large measures (−9) and the priority penalty (−1) outweigh this. No proposal bonus applies, as no new measure was introduced. Political capital falls to 34.0.
