### Steady Advance, Mounting Pressure

Global AI capability advances to 55.0 as leading labs scale training runs and shorten release cycles. Open-weight models follow, reaching an estimated 43.0, fueled by wider adaptation of Kimi K3 derivatives. Yet safety assurance continues to erode: with no new evaluation regime or transparency breakthrough, and internal opacity growing, confidence in control drops further — ai_safety falls to 30.0.

### Cyber Shield Gains Ground, But Not Without Friction

The EU pushes forward with its *Cyber Shield* directive, now the named priority. ENISA finalizes draft technical standards for AI-powered intrusion detection, informed by the Joint Research Centre’s benchmarking report. A pilot exercise involving Germany, Poland, and Spain tests cross-sector coordination against a simulated multi-vector attack. However, resistance from smaller member states intensifies. Slovakia and Croatia raise constitutional concerns over central monitoring, delaying adoption in the Council’s Internal Market formation. The Commission offers enhanced consultation rights and reaffirms 70% co-financing, but trust remains thin.

### Sovereignty Projects Stumble on Execution

The *Tech Sovereignty Package* faces permitting bottlenecks in Finland and Bulgaria, where local authorities resist fast-tracked environmental reviews. EIB lending remains underutilized. Meanwhile, site selection for the *InvestAI Gigafactories* slips into Q3, with uncertainty mounting about domestic demand absorption. No capacity comes online, and dependence on foreign frontier access deepens as global capability rises.

### Political Capital Erodes

With three large measures in flight and one prioritized, political capital continues to drain. Public sentiment sours slightly after media coverage highlights delays and intergovernmental friction, outweighing modest progress. Labour groups warn of uneven benefits, and civil society legal challenges to open-weight distribution gain momentum.
