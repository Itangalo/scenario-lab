## In practice  
This turn, we advance Cyber Shield through the Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formation, using Article 114 TFEU as legal basis and building on NIS2’s existing framework. ENISA is now operationalising the first phase: drafting minimum technical standards for AI-powered detection systems, with input from national CSIRTs and a pilot group including Germany, Poland, and Spain. The Commission’s Joint Research Centre delivers its benchmarking report in February 2027, which will define what qualifies as “capable” under the directive. Resistance from smaller member states — particularly Slovakia and Croatia — persists over cost-sharing and fears of centralised alert authority. To address this, we reinforce the offer of 70% co-financing from the Digital Europe Programme and establish a fast-track consultation channel in the CSIRT executive board, allowing affected states to challenge EU-level escalation decisions within 12 hours. The first cross-border simulation exercise is scheduled for May 2027, testing coordinated response to an AI-driven multi-vector attack across energy and water sectors.  

Simultaneously, we maintain momentum on the Tech Sovereignty Package, where permitting delays threaten the timeline for new data centre zones. The Commission engages directly with regional authorities in Finland, Ireland, and Bulgaria to streamline grid access and environmental reviews, leveraging the European Investment Bank’s fast-track lending window for greenfield projects. The InvestAI Gigafactories initiative remains on track, with site selection expected by Q3 2027; however, concerns grow about whether domestic demand can absorb capacity at scale without parallel adoption incentives. No new industrial measure is launched this turn — political capital is too constrained, and we must let current instruments land before layering more. Our focus is execution, not expansion.
