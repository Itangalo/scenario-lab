### Fractured Mobilisation, Escalating Risk

The European Union launched the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation (ESCM) as a last-ditch bid to reclaim technological agency. Invoking Article 122 TFEU, the Commission proposed a binding Regulation under urgency procedures, backed by a proposed €45bn in redirected funds — primarily reallocated from cohesion and recovery programmes, pending formal approval by the Council and European Parliament. The legal basis is contested, and the financing mechanism remains provisional, subject to ongoing inter-institutional negotiation. No funds have been disbursed, and no infrastructure procurement has begun. As such, ESCM remains in the proposal phase and has not yet entered implementation; it does not yet carry a portfolio charge.

The EU Compute Authority was formally established but remains in initial staffing phase. It has not yet assumed operational powers. A temporary mandate allows it to issue non-binding coordination guidance to ENTSO-E and member states. Requests for technical secondments from France and Germany are under review, with no binding commitments secured. Grid prioritisation for Lille and Cluj is under study, but no allocation orders have been issued, pending resolution of energy security concerns raised by southern member states.

Resistance hardened. Poland and Hungary escalated CJEU challenges, calling the move “techno-authoritarian overreach.” Italy accepted wildfire support but delayed any commitment to ESCM compliance, citing national sovereignty. Sabotage at Cluj resumed, now suspected to be state-linked. Southern grid operators filed emergency appeals against potential ENTSO-E allocation plans, arguing energy security risks.

Meanwhile, a limited open-weight model update diffused modest improvements in accessible capability. This incremental release, derived from lagged frontier progress, is reflected in a small increase in `openweight_capability` to 52.2. The update did not represent a major capability leap, and benchmark anomalies—dismissed by developers as noise—sparked quiet alarm in red-teaming circles. `ai_capability` rose by 0.5 to 63.5, consistent with the ongoing rate of frontier advancement.

The Secure Critical AI Infrastructure measure concluded, granting +1 resilience. ESCM, as a large category 4 measure, has not yet yielded progress toward sovereignty due to delayed implementation, legal challenges, and lack of staffing. Public sentiment declined further as protests spread, resentful of centralising force and job losses.

With no new safety measures completed and capability advancing, `ai_safety` remains unchanged at 5.0. The portfolio charge—Accelerate Anchor Demand −2, Secure Critical AI Infrastructure finished t10 +5, priority −1—netted +2, but no new charge applies to ESCM this turn. A new measure proposed in response to recent shocks earned no proposal bonus: the events were global, and the response, however bold, remains in early stages. `eu_political_capital` remains at 13.0, as no cost is yet incurred from ESCM’s unrealised execution.
