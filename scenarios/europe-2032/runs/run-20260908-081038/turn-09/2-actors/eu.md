## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI capacity to meet strategic and safety imperatives``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The previous commitment, while technically met through the completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories, failed to ensure operational sovereignty due to political fragmentation, sabotage, and obstructed grid access. The denial of frontier access by the US and the collapse in private investment have further exposed that infrastructure alone is insufficient without enforceable demand, secured energy, and unified political will. The new commitment refocuses on operational capacity — not just hardware, but the full chain from power to procurement to protection — as the only credible foundation for both safety and resilience.

- `Secure Critical AI Infrastructure Against Sabotage and Non-Compliance (category 4, costs 3 per turn, started turn 8, finishes on turn 10): Establishes EU-level authority to fast-track grid access, override national permitting delays, and deploy Civil Protection Mechanism resources to protect and complete sovereign AI infrastructure, with penalties for obstruction`

## Priority
**Secure Critical AI Infrastructure Against Sabotage and Non-Compliance** — because the physical and political resistance in Poland, Hungary, and parts of Southern Europe has become the decisive barrier to sovereignty, not funding or design, and because without energized, secured compute, no safety or resilience measure can ever land.

## In practice
This turn, the Commission is enforcing Article 122 TFEU across three fronts: legal, physical, and financial. Legally, we are contesting Poland and Hungary’s constitutional challenges through the CJEU, invoking the primacy of Union law in matters of technological survival. Physically, Frontex and EUCAP units are now embedded with Romanian and French energy forces to patrol Cluj and Lille nodes, while DG ENER issues binding directives to ENTSO-E to reserve 60% of new transmission capacity for sovereign AI loads, backed by EIB liquidity guarantees. Financially, the Digital Sovereignty Board is activating sanctions: cohesion funds for Poland are frozen, and Italy’s partial procurement commitment is met with a conditional offer of joint wildfire response capacity, tied directly to full compliance by Q1 2031.

We are also responding to the **ai_investment_collapse** by shifting from reliance on private hyperscalers to a public-core model. The JRC dashboard now tracks not just sabotage and procurement, but also idle compute hours and energy shortfalls — data that will inform a Q1 2031 review of whether the Union must assume full operational control of the Gigafactories. France and Germany are being asked to second national technical directors to a new EU Compute Authority, which would manage, monitor, and mandate usage across the sovereign stack. The message is clear: if member states will not act as one, the Union will act for them — and the emergency powers invoked this turn are not temporary, but the new baseline for survival.
