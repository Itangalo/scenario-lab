## New measure  
Accelerate Anchor Demand for Sovereign Compute  
To unlock stalled private investment, the Commission mandates that EU institutions and member states in defence, energy, health, and critical infrastructure commit to binding procurement of sovereign AI compute capacity by Q4 2029, with minimum thresholds set by sector and verified by the JRC.  
- Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
- Size: small  
- Finishes on turn: 7  
- Targeted effect: +10 to eu_ai_sovereignty by turn 8, unlocks private investment in InvestAI Gigafactories, reduces dependence on US-controlled access  
- Applies to: EU institutions and member states, with enforcement through cohesion and Digital Europe funding conditionality

## In practice  
This turn, the Commission finalises the activation of the Cyber Shield’s central coordination layer, with ENISA now receiving live feeds from 18 national CSIRTs and conducting its first automated threat correlation across borders. The fast-track consultation mechanism proves its worth when a coordinated ransomware probe against energy operators in Slovakia and northern Italy triggers an immediate joint response protocol, marking the first operational test of the system. The 70% co-funding model under the Digital Europe Programme ensures near-universal rollout, and resilience holds at 46.0 despite rising threat activity.

Simultaneously, we confront the paralysis in sovereign compute. The Tech Sovereignty Package is now law, but its permitting reforms mean nothing without demand. We issue a formal call to all member states and EU agencies: by September 2029, they must publish binding procurement plans for sovereign AI compute, with minimum thresholds calibrated to national critical infrastructure needs and verified by the Joint Research Centre. The European Defence Agency, EURATOM, and DG SANTE are the first to respond, with draft commitments expected by June. The EIB’s fast-track financing window remains open, but we now tie access to evidence of public demand: no anchor, no grid priority. DG GROW and DG ENER jointly issue guidance confirming AI data centres as strategic infrastructure under the revised TEN-E regulation, enabling immediate permitting in designated zones — but only for projects backed by signed procurement intent.

The new measure applies direct pressure where it is needed: on public buyers. We cannot build capacity if no one commits to using it. By leveraging cohesion funds and Digital Europe co-financing as levers, we make participation mandatory, not optional. This is not a subsidy — it is a circuit closure. The US is about to ration access; we will not wait to be excluded. We are building our own lane, and this turn, we are forcing the first vehicles onto it.
