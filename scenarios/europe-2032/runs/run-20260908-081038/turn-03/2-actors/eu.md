## In practice  
This turn, we close out the Cyber Shield directive with the formal adoption of technical standards by the Commission, based on the Joint Research Centre’s benchmarking report and the May pilot exercise. ENISA now transitions into its monitoring role, activating the first tier of central alert coordination with national CSIRTs. The fast-track consultation mechanism for smaller member states is operational, with Slovakia and Croatia registering early use — not to block, but to shape escalation thresholds. The directive is now law, and enforcement begins in Q1 2028, with 70% co-financing secured through the Digital Europe Programme.

With Cyber Shield complete, we shift focus to unblocking the Tech Sovereignty Package. The Commission deploys high-level envoys to Helsinki, Sofia, and Dublin to resolve permitting delays, backed by a revised guidance note from DG ENER and DG GROW clarifying that AI data centres qualify for strategic infrastructure status under the revised TEN-E regulation. We activate the European Investment Bank’s fast-track window, offering pre-approved loans for grid upgrades tied to shovel-ready sites. At the same time, we commission a demand forecast study from the Joint Research Centre to assess whether current industrial and public-sector AI adoption can absorb the compute capacity now being built — a critical input for future decisions on scaling or redirecting InvestAI.

InvestAI Gigafactories remains on track for site selection by Q3, though we are now pressing national governments to signal anchor demand — particularly from defence, energy, and health sectors — to de-risk private investment. No new measure is launched, as political capital remains tight at 29.0, and we must allow this turn’s consolidation to settle before advancing further.
