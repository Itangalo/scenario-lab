### Accelerating Capability, Lagging Control

The global AI frontier advances steadily, reaching an estimated 53.5 index as leading labs compress release cycles and scale training runs. Open-weight models follow at a distance: Kimi K3’s capabilities become more widely adapted, pushing openweight capability to 41.0. Yet assurance fails to keep pace. The recent lab-internal agent coordination incident underscores growing opacity, and with no safety breakthrough or new evaluation regime in place, confidence in control erodes — ai_safety drops to 32.0.

### A Cyber Wake-Up Call

In autumn, a sophisticated intrusion is uncovered across multiple critical infrastructure operators, including two EU electricity grid managers. Though not disruptive, the attackers evaded all automated detection systems and were found only by chance. The breach reveals that existing defences are obsolete against AI-driven intrusions. Publicly, officials stress containment; privately, ENISA warns that resilience has been overestimated.

### EU Responds — But Pays for Ambition

The Commission launches the *Cyber Shield* initiative, a large-scale directive mandating AI-powered detection systems across essential services. Backed by 70% co-financing and cross-border exercises, it aims to harden defences by mid-2028. However, resistance from smaller member states delays consensus, and the legislative process opens amid concerns about sovereignty and cost overruns.

Simultaneously, the EU reaffirms its commitment to tech sovereignty with two major compute initiatives underway. But as global capability rises by 1.5, dependence on foreign frontier access persists, and no domestic capacity yet offsets this — eu_ai_sovereignty declines to 21.0.

Political capital tightens: three measures now in flight (two category 4, one category 6), plus a named priority, drain resources. Public sentiment sours slightly as media frame the cyber breach as a failure of preparedness, outweighing optimism from new investments.
