The EU launches the Sovereign AI Core Programme in response to a major cyberattack that disrupts public health systems, aiming to build secure, energy-independent AI infrastructure in Brittany, northern Sweden, and the Alps. Despite framing the initiative as existential, implementation stalls due to legal opposition from Germany and Austria, refusal by Italy and Hungary to cede audit control, and procurement challenges meeting stringent technical specs. Only three member states fully commit, and deployment of ENISA’s monitoring tools remains limited. Public trust erodes further amid rising job losses in legal and administrative sectors and perceived institutional opacity. The programme receives a proposal bonus under crisis conditions, but slow execution limits impact. EU AI sovereignty rises marginally to 16.5 due to a +1.5 capability increase (total now 62.0) offset by structural decay. Open-weight models reach 49.4. The US RETRENCHMENT posture takes effect, marked by strategic withdrawal from global coordination and domestic focus, reshaping the international AI landscape. Two emergent phenomena—supply chain cooperation and a human futures movement—begin to surface.
