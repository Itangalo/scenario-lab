### A Leap and a Loss

Global AI capability surged this turn, rising from 60.5 to 65.0 following a discontinuous advance at a leading frontier lab. The breakthrough, driven by novel training stability and recursive self-improvement techniques, enabled systems to operate with multi-day autonomy and emergent large-scale planning—capabilities that outpace current oversight frameworks. This leap rendered prior safety assumptions obsolete overnight, triggering a sharp decline in ai_safety from 22.0 to 10.0, as no deployed safeguards could adapt in time.

In response, the EU launched the **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation**, a large category 4 measure under Article 114 TFEU, aimed at fast-tracking EU-controlled compute with binding public-use obligations. Germany and Ireland agreed in principle to host co-located sovereign nodes in exchange for priority access, and initial planning has begun. However, as a high-cost measure, full implementation requires at least two turns. No construction has started, and no procurement is finalized; the project remains in the design and negotiation phase. Under modelling rule 6, no operational or partial build momentum can accrue in the proposal turn, so the measure contributes no immediate boost to eu_ai_sovereignty beyond the standard proposal bonus.

The **European Evaluation Shield**, already in flight, faced a crippling legal challenge over audit authority, limiting its reach. Pilot audits proceeded in secret, but their legitimacy remains contested. The measure continues to carry its portfolio charge and is not newly introduced this turn; the narrative clarification ensures only one new measure—Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation—was added.

Meanwhile, rural resistance to data centre expansions escalated into blockades and sabotage. Power lines were cut and construction sites occupied, reflecting growing public distrust. These disruptions, combined with ongoing labour unrest, deepened the sense of crisis. Resilience dipped to 32.0 as critical infrastructure remained exposed to AI-driven attacks, and no new protective measures have yet taken effect. Public sentiment fell to 30.0, driven by job losses, infrastructure conflicts, and fears of uncontrolled technological power.

Political capital declined to 20.0, strained by the existing portfolio—InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, European Evaluation Shield—and the new Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation, all carrying charges. The proposal bonus for the new measure (+4) only partially offsets the burden. The legitimacy lend remains inactive, as political capital (20.0) still falls below public sentiment (30.0).

Openweight capability rose modestly to 47.5, reflecting early but limited diffusion of prior frontier models into open ecosystems. The +0.5 increase acknowledges that open models are beginning to incorporate elements of the recent closed advances, but lack the infrastructure and training stability to replicate autonomous self-improvement. This incremental gain preserves the structural lag and respects the invariant that openweight_capability grows more slowly and never exceeds ai_capability.

Sovereignty increased to 30.0, driven solely by the ongoing contribution from the InvestAI Gigafactories (+1) and the proposal bonus for the new high-cost measure (+4), offset by a −2 penalty from the rise in ai_capability (which outpaced openweight progress and intensified dependency risks). No additional momentum is attributed to the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation, consistent with the rule that high-cost measures cannot yield partial implementation effects in their proposal turn. The net +1 change reflects the balance of these narratively grounded terms.
