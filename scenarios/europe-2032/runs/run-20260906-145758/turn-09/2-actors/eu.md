## Priority
**Critical Infrastructure Continuity Directive** — because the *cyber_major_incident* and *emergent_infrastructure_sabotage* have made clear that our most urgent vulnerability is operational fragility: if we cannot keep the lights on or defend physical access, then no amount of sovereign compute or safety evaluation will matter, and this turn is decisive for anchoring continuity standards before the next wave of attacks.

## In practice
This turn, we treat the ransomware attack on health administrations not as an isolated breach but as proof of systemic exposure: offensive AI tools are now accessible beyond the frontier, and they are being used against core public services. We activate ENISA’s expanded mandate under the Cross-Border Cyber Incident Response Protocol to coordinate patching and forensic analysis, while deploying emergency funding from the Digital Europe Programme to restore backup integrity across affected regions. But containment is not strategy. We are now enforcing the Critical Infrastructure Continuity Directive with full urgency, leveraging Article 122 TFEU to override national delays in hardening essential sites. The European Investment Bank has suspended disbursement of sovereign compute grants pending compliance plans from operators, and Frontex is sharing aerial surveillance data with national authorities to detect early signs of coordinated sabotage in rural zones. France, Austria, and Poland are being offered joint command cells and co-funding for regional microgrids — not as incentives, but as conditions of continued support.

At the same time, we are laying the foundation for the Sovereign Evaluation Corps, recognising that even hardened infrastructure fails if the systems running on it cannot be trusted. This agency will not rely on developer self-reporting; it will have legal access to model weights, secure sandbox environments, and the authority to delay deployment where risks exceed thresholds we define. We are negotiating with Germany and the Nordic states to second top-tier interpretability researchers, and preparing draft legislation for the Council’s Internal Market configuration to adopt in early turn 10. The drop in public sentiment and political capital makes bold moves harder, but the adoption_success event — visible improvements in public service delivery credited to EU-led AI use — gives us a narrow window to rebuild legitimacy through competence, not concession. We are using it to shift from reactive defence to layered sovereignty: physical, operational, and cognitive.
