### A Narrow Window of Action

Global AI capability advanced slightly this turn, reaching 66.5 as iterative gains in multi-agent coordination and recursive self-improvement continued. The pace remains high but linear, with no discontinuity. Openweight capability held steady at 49.0, as no new open-weight models were released this turn. While development activity continues in the background, particularly within academic consortia and smaller labs, the anticipated release of a new foundational open model was delayed due to unresolved licensing and compute-access negotiations. The gap with the closed frontier narrowed only slightly, consistent with gradual accumulation.

Despite this, a rare positive signal emerged: AI systems in public-sector trials across several member states delivered measurable improvements in service delivery, from reduced waiting times to more effective teaching tools. These gains were publicly attributed to EU-led initiatives, not foreign platforms, offering a rare boost to legitimacy.

The EU pressed forward with its sovereignty agenda. The **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation** concluded as planned, with new data centre permits fast-tracked and state aid approved. However, no new facilities became operational this turn, and the expected sovereignty boost was offset by rising dependency pressures as global capability advanced. The **Critical Infrastructure Continuity Directive** moved into enforcement, with the European Investment Bank conditioning further funding on compliance plans. France, Austria, and Poland received surveillance support and co-funding offers for microgrids, but implementation remains uneven.

Preliminary discussions began on establishing a **Sovereign Evaluation Corps**, a potential category 5, large measure aimed at independent pre-deployment evaluations. However, legal access to model weights, secure testing environments, staffing, and budget remain unsecured. No formal proposal has been adopted, and the initiative is not yet active. It will be considered for introduction in a future turn once foundational conditions are met. As such, it does not count toward this turn’s one-measure limit, nor does it contribute to portfolio charge.

Public sentiment rose slightly to 27.0, buoyed by visible public-sector benefits. Political capital remains at 15.0, as no new measure has drawn down resources—portfolio charge remains at −6 from existing measures, with no additional burden this turn. The legitimacy gain from public benefits did not fully offset the ongoing cost of the active portfolio, but capital was not further depleted.

Soevereignty fell to 28.0, reflecting the 0.5 rise in global AI capability without sufficient offsetting gains in sovereign capacity or control. The decay term applies in full, as no new sovereign compute came online and no major policy milestone was completed this turn.
