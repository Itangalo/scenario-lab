### A Cascade of Crises

Global AI capability advanced to 66.0 this turn, driven by iterative improvements in recursive self-improvement and multi-agent coordination at the frontier. The pace remains elevated, though no new discontinuity occurred. This progress further widened the gap between closed and open capabilities, as safety frameworks continue to lag.

A large, AI-generated ransomware attack crippled regional health administrations across six member states, exploiting a compromised software dependency. Backup systems failed under coordinated pressure, exposing systemic fragility. The incident, officially classified as a *cyber_major_incident*, overwhelmed response capacity and confirmed that offensive AI tools are now in circulation beyond state-level actors.

In response, the EU activated its Cross-Border Cyber Incident Response Protocol. Forensic teams traced the exploit chain to a widely used open-source library, but attribution remains unresolved. The attack deepened public anxiety, reinforcing narratives of uncontrolled technological risk.

Simultaneously, sabotage of power lines and access roads to data centres intensified in rural regions of France, Austria, and Poland. These acts, decentralized but sharing tactics, suggest informal networks are coalescing around resistance to AI infrastructure. The *emergent_infrastructure_sabotage* trend hardened into a persistent operational threat.

The European Evaluation Shield completed its mandate this turn. While pilot audits were conducted, its legal authority was never fully established, and no binding standards emerged. The measure concluded without delivering systemic assurance gains.

The EU launched the **Critical Infrastructure Continuity Directive**, a large category 6 measure, to harden essential data centres against physical and cyber-physical threats. France and Austria agreed to co-investment in regional continuity clusters, easing initial resistance. However, implementation will take years, and immediate vulnerabilities remain.

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation continued in planning, with no construction begun. Rising dependency costs and the absence of operational resilience infrastructure eroded confidence in the strategy.

Public sentiment fell to 25.0 amid visible disruption and infrastructure attacks. Political capital dropped to 15.0 under the weight of four active measures and declining cohesion. Sovereignty declined slightly as the rise in global capability outpaced EU build momentum.
