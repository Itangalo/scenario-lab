## New measure  
**Critical Infrastructure Continuity Directive**  
A legally binding framework requiring all sovereign and high-capacity AI data centres designated as essential infrastructure to implement redundant power feeds, hardened physical access, and real-time sabotage detection systems, with mandatory coordination between national intelligence, energy, and cyber agencies.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` +5 to +7 resilience by turn 10 through systemic hardening of AI infrastructure against physical and cyber-physical disruption; reduces single-point failure risk from coordinated sabotage; complements Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation by ensuring that sovereign compute remains operational under duress  
`Applies to:` Member states, national energy regulators, transmission system operators, and operators of data centres with >10MW draw or hosting systems above 50.0 capability

## Priority  
**Critical Infrastructure Continuity Directive** — because the emergent_infrastructure_sabotage and cyber_major_incident together reveal that our most immediate vulnerability is not lack of compute, but fragility of operation: if power lines can be cut and sites blockaded without consequence, then every watt we build becomes a target, not an asset.

## In practice  
This turn, we treat the recent ransomware sweep across regional health administrations — exploiting an AI-generated exploit chain in a widely used open-source library — as the wake-up call it is. The attack disrupted patient records in six member states, overwhelmed backup systems, and exposed how little redundancy exists in core digital services. We activate the Cross-Border Cyber Incident Response Protocol under ENISA, coordinating forensic teams and deploying emergency patching via the newly operational Joint Threat Intelligence Cell. But containment is not enough: we now act to harden the underlying conditions that made this possible.  

We launch the Critical Infrastructure Continuity Directive, framing AI data centres not as commercial assets but as dual-use strategic nodes akin to power plants or rail hubs. Under Article 122 TFEU and the solidarity clause, we mandate that all facilities receiving support under the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation must meet minimum resilience standards by turn 10: including geographically separated grid connections, 72-hour uninterruptible power supply, live intrusion detection on physical access points, and monthly joint drills with national civil protection units. The European Investment Bank will tie disbursement of sovereign compute funding to compliance, while Frontex and Europol are tasked with monitoring patterns of rural sabotage and disrupting emerging networks before they escalate. France and Austria, previously reluctant to militarise infrastructure planning, are offered co-investment in regional continuity clusters — shared shielded microgrids serving both AI and emergency government functions — in exchange for dropping objections. This is not peacetime preparedness. This is infrastructure defence in anticipation of sustained asymmetric pressure.
