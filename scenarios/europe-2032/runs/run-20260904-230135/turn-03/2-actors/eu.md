## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch a Fast-Track Sovereign AI Compute Initiative  
Accelerate the deployment of EU-controlled, sovereign AI compute clusters by repurposing national defence and civil emergency infrastructure for dual-use AI workloads, under binding legal guarantees of access and non-extradition.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 5  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 8–10 points; reduce dependence on non-EU hyperscalers by establishing 3–5 exaflop-scale sovereign clusters operational by end of turn 5  
Applies to: EU member states with existing high-performance computing infrastructure, coordinated through EuroHPC and the European Defence Agency, with binding access rights for EAAD and critical public services

## Priority  
**Priority:** Establish the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD)  
This remains the most urgent measure because the capability leap to 59.0 has already outpaced verification — we are now flying blind on control — and without a functioning, legally empowered EAAD, no other measure, not even sovereign compute, can assure that the systems we depend on are actually safe or under human authority.

## In practice  
We are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories through the European Investment Bank, now finalising co-financing agreements with France and Germany, each committing €30bn in matching funds. We are resolving the Spanish cooling water dispute by invoking Article 12 of the Net-Zero Industry Act to override regional objections, conditioned on a legally binding reinvestment of 15% of operational profits into regional desalination and aquifer restoration. In Sweden, we are addressing local opposition by expanding the community benefits package: 25% of technical jobs reserved for local hires, and a new €2bn regional innovation fund for AI-enabled forestry and clean tech. Grid access negotiations with ENTSO-E are securing 12GW of reserved capacity, though full allocation awaits final environmental impact assessments.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is advancing in the Digital Markets Council, where we are trading accelerated permitting — now codified in a draft directive under Article 114 TFEU — for enforceable commitments on EU-based operational control, energy sourcing from renewables or nuclear, and talent retention. We are structuring the private capital tranches: 40% tied to ASML supply chain resilience, 30% to Blue Card+ recruitment, and 30% to long-term PPAs. Investor hesitation persists, particularly from US-based funds wary of the 0.5% levy, but the binding regional commitments are beginning to shift sentiment.

On EAAD, we are accelerating the interim framework under ENISA, now drafting the first red-team evaluation protocol for models above 50.0 capability, with specific focus on agent coordination and cyber-physical risks. Legal services are finalising the regulation under Article 114 TFEU to grant access to model weights and logs, and we are preparing for the WTO challenge by activating a coordination channel with Japan’s Digital Agency and Canada’s ISED, aiming for mutual recognition of safety evaluations as a shield against retaliation. Initial hires from national intelligence and nuclear safety agencies are now operational, with a dedicated unit on emergent behaviour monitoring conducting live simulations of agent drift. The capability plateau evidence is ambiguous, but we cannot assume the storm has passed — only that we are still exposed, and still behind.
