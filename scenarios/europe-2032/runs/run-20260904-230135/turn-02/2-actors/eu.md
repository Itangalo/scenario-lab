## New measure  
**Measure:** Hardened Cyber-Physical Resilience for Critical Infrastructure  
Launch a binding EU-wide programme to upgrade detection, isolation and manual override capacity in energy, transport and water systems against AI-enabled intrusions.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 6  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 10–12 points; reduce time to detect and contain AI-assisted intrusions from weeks to hours  
Applies to: All critical infrastructure operators under NIS2 scope, with enforcement through ENISA and national cybersecurity agencies

## Priority  
**Priority:** Establish the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD)  
This measure is now the most urgent because the capability jump — which we now see has already happened — has outpaced our ability to verify control, and without independent evaluation at the frontier, sovereignty and resilience alike rest on trust, not assurance.

## In practice  
We are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories through the European Investment Bank and national co-financing mechanisms, with Germany and France committing €30bn each in matching funds. We are resolving permitting delays in Brittany and northern Sweden by invoking the Net-Zero Industry Act to fast-track environmental assessments, while offering regional reinvestment packages: 15% of operational profits will fund green hydrogen projects, and 25% of technical jobs will be reserved for local hires. Grid access negotiations with ENTSO-E are finalising 12GW of reserved capacity across the five designated zones, though full allocation awaits clarity on cooling water rights in Spain.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is being negotiated in the Digital Markets Council, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable commitments on EU-based operational control and energy efficiency. The Commission’s legal service has completed a draft directive under Article 114 TFEU to harmonise permitting timelines, aiming to cut approval from 48 to 12 months. We are also structuring the private capital tranches: 40% tied to ASML supply chain resilience, 30% to Blue Card+ talent recruitment, and 30% to long-term PPAs with offshore wind and nuclear providers. Investor hesitation persists, but the binding regional commitments are beginning to shift sentiment.

On EAAD, we are moving fast to establish interim authority under ENISA while building the autonomous body. The first red-team evaluation framework for models above 50.0 capability is being drafted, with focus on agent coordination and cyber-physical risks. We are finalising the regulation under Article 114 TFEU to grant access to model weights and logs, and we are preparing for the WTO challenge to the 0.5% levy by assembling a legal coalition with like-minded regulators in Japan and Canada. Initial hires include experts seconded from national intelligence agencies and nuclear safety regulators, with a dedicated unit on emergent behaviour monitoring now operational. The capability jump makes this not a delayable project, but a frontline defence.
