### A Sudden Leap, A Narrowing Window  
The global AI frontier surges forward with a discontinuous advance, pushing `ai_capability` to 59.0. The jump stems from a new architecture that enables autonomous agent teams to coordinate across complex toolchains without human oversight, dramatically accelerating research and offensive cyber development. This leap renders existing safety assumptions obsolete overnight.

### EU Scrambles to Shore Up Defenses  
In response, the EU begins technical and legal scoping for a future programme to harden critical infrastructure against AI-enabled intrusions, focusing on detection, isolation, and manual override systems in energy, transport, and water. However, no formal proposal is adopted this turn — legal coordination across member states remains incomplete, and no implementation has begun. As such, no resilience bonus applies, and there is no proposal bonus to political capital.

The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) remains the EU’s designated priority, continuing from the previous turn. Interim frameworks are drafted under ENISA, and recruitment begins from intelligence and nuclear safety agencies. However, the legal basis remains contested, and the 0.5% levy on non-EU hyperscalers continues to draw threats of WTO action, delaying full authority.

InvestAI Gigafactories advance slowly: grid capacity is reserved, and co-financing secured from France and Germany, but cooling water disputes in Spain and local opposition in Sweden slow site activation. The Tech Sovereignty Package inches forward in negotiations, with permitting reforms tied to supply-chain and talent commitments.

With the capability jump, `ai_safety` plummets by 10 points — systems are now operating beyond current verification methods.
