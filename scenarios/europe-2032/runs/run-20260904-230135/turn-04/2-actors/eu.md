## In practice  
We are acting on the collapse in private AI investment by activating a crisis clause in the InvestEU mandate, allowing the European Investment Bank to issue up to €50bn in emergency guarantees for the acquisition of stranded compute assets. We are instructing EuroHPC to identify and assess at-risk facilities in Ireland, the Netherlands, and France—sites with existing grid and cooling infrastructure where private developers have halted expansion. Legal teams are preparing a temporary derogation under Article 122 TFEU to bypass state-aid rules, conditioned on full public ownership and non-extradition covenants. We are coordinating with national governments to fast-track security clearances and repurpose defence logistics channels for hardware transport and installation. The goal is to bring three clusters online by the end of turn 5, each capable of training models up to 58.0 capability, under EAAD monitoring and sovereign control.

Meanwhile, the EAAD remains on track for launch in turn 5. We have secured provisional agreement from the Council on its legal authority under Article 114 TFEU, and the first red-team protocol is now in live testing against a simulated agent coordination breach. We are finalising mutual recognition talks with Japan and Canada to insulate the hyperscaler levy from WTO retaliation, framing it as a security measure rather than a trade barrier. Despite investor hesitance, the Tech Sovereignty Package is gaining traction: the accelerated permitting directive passed its first reading in the Parliament, and we have secured binding commitments from three European energy firms to deliver 8GW of nuclear-backed power to priority zones. On resilience, the Cyber-Physical Hardening programme has completed technical designs for all critical nodes in the TEN-T and TEN-E networks, and we are launching the first cross-border exercise in May to test manual override chains under AI-simulated attack conditions.
