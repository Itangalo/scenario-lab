## In practice
We are acting on the sudden erosion of ai_safety and the unsanctioned success of Red Team Europe by bringing the initiative under EU legal and financial authority. We are proposing a Council Implementing Decision under Article 17(6) TEU to grant the coalition binding status as an extension of EAAD’s mandate, with co-equal authority to conduct unannounced adversarial evaluations on any model above 55.0 capability operating in or affecting the EU. The European Defence Fund and Horizon Europe will jointly finance a €4.2bn envelope over three years, routed through a new Cybersecurity Evaluation and Resilience Facility, to equip the network with sovereign inference capacity and secure simulation environments. We are negotiating with Germany, Finland, and the Netherlands to transfer their national red-team units into the framework, and with France to contribute access to military-grade cyber ranges.

At the same time, we are closing out two major initiatives this turn. The Emergency Compute Sovereignty Facility has secured two exaflop-scale clusters — one in Eindhoven, one near Paris — from defunct private ventures, both now under EuroHPC legal control and integrated into the Fast-Track Sovereign AI Compute Initiative. These add 6.2 points to eu_ai_sovereignty, meeting our targeted effect. The EAAD is now formally established: its first binding evaluation authority was confirmed by the Council yesterday, and it has already invoked a 30-day delay on a frontier model submission due to unverifiable agent coordination safeguards. The Tech Sovereignty Package is entering its final legislative stretch, with the accelerated permitting directive now in trilogue; we are offering Spain and Portugal additional grid access guarantees to secure their support. Meanwhile, the Hardened Cyber-Physical Resilience programme is conducting its first live cross-border exercise next week, simulating a coordinated AI-driven attack on the Rhine-Main-Danube corridor, with manual override protocols to be evaluated in real time.
