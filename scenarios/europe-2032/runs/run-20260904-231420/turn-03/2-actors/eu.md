## In practice  
This turn marks the culmination of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive: the trilogue has concluded, the sunset clause is locked in, and urgency procedure has been granted under Article 114 TFEU. The 30% reservation for public-interest use in all data centres above 100 petaFLOPS is now law. We are immediately activating the first tranche of reserved compute, drawn from three operational hyperscale sites in France, Germany, and Poland, with ENISA verifying compliance by turn-end. This is not a paper victory — it is the first time the EU can compel access to frontier-class infrastructure on its own soil, and it ends the era in which our sovereignty depended on the goodwill of foreign providers.

With that foundation in place, we are launching the Sovereign Model Development Programme. EURAI — established this quarter by delegation from the Commission — will lead the training of a model at capability level 58–62 by turn 6, using EU-reserved compute, EU-supervised data pipelines, and a novel safety-by-design architecture co-developed with the EU’s top interpretability teams. The model will not be open-weight, but it will be transparently evaluated and accessible to public institutions, critical operators, and vetted researchers. We are allocating €3.8bn from the Digital Europe Programme and leveraging matched funding from seven member states, including newly committed contributions from Sweden and the Netherlands. This is not duplication — it is the next phase of our two-year commitment: from securing the means to act, to acting.

Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package continues on track. We have now secured grid-access guarantees for nine AI Acceleration Zones, and the European Investment Bank has approved a €12bn blended finance facility to de-risk private investment. But local opposition remains a threat: the campaign_backlash has intensified in two zones, with protests over energy use and land rights. In response, we are expanding the community benefit mechanism to all zones, mandating that 5% of profits fund local digital inclusion and grid resilience — not as charity, but as a social contract for the AI era. We are not slowing down. We are learning how to build.
