## In practice
This turn, we are operationalising the 450 petaFLOPS secured under the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive. ENISA has certified compliance at the Île-de-France, Frankfurt, and Wrocław sites, and the first tranche is now live under EURAI’s control. The model team, drawn from INRIA, the Max Planck Institute, and CWI Amsterdam, has finalised the base architecture — a hybrid transformer with embedded interpretability probes and a constrained self-improvement module trained on EU legal and scientific corpora. Data curation is underway with the European Open Science Cloud, and we are integrating real-time feedback from national cybersecurity agencies to harden against emergent misuse patterns.

The Tech Sovereignty Package continues to mobilise private investment, though the ASML servicing crisis has exposed our vulnerability in hardware supply chains. In response, the Commission is fast-tracking negotiations under Article 21 EC on strategic autonomy in critical technologies, leveraging our market access to secure guaranteed maintenance rights for EU-operated data centres. The European Investment Bank’s €12bn blended facility is now open, but we are tightening conditions: fast-track permitting now requires not only the 5% community benefit levy but also proof of diversified component sourcing by turn 6. This is not protectionism — it is resilience by design.

Meanwhile, the Joint Middle-Power Evaluation Compact has convened its first technical working group in Brussels under Article 207 TFEU, with Japan and South Korea agreeing to co-red-team the latest open-weight model at 50.0. While no binding standards emerged this turn, the exchange of evaluation methodologies has laid groundwork for shared thresholds. Still, with the US now treating AI access as a strategic privilege, coalition-building alone cannot close the gap. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Model Acceleration Initiative: to ensure our model learns as fast as the world changes.
