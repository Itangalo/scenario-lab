### Frontier Leaps, Open Models Gain Traction  
AI capability advances to 56.0 in the first half of 2027, driven by tighter integration of simulation-based training and real-world feedback loops. The leap accelerates research automation, with frontier labs reporting self-correcting training runs that reduce human oversight. However, this progress widens the gap with safety: opaque reasoning and unmonitored agent coordination persist, and `ai_safety` drops to 29.0. A powerful open-weight model is released, attracting rapid interest and hundreds of thousands of downloads. While early adopters begin fine-tuning for specialized tasks, including cyber operations, widespread deployment remains limited by access to sufficient compute and technical expertise. `openweight_capability` rises to 45.0, reflecting growing diffusion but not yet systemic capability escalation.

### EU Responds with Hardening and High Stakes  
The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive advances in trilogue, as the Competitiveness Council demands concessions and the Commission offers a binding sunset clause—review at turn 4, automatic expiry unless reauthorised. Legal threats are withdrawn, and urgency procedure is granted. Momentum builds, but no compute is yet reserved: sovereignty gains remain prospective. With the directive still in flight and `ai_capability` rising by 2.0, `eu_ai_sovereignty` rises modestly to 24.0.

The European Commission initiates planning for a Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative, targeting energy, water, and transport networks. ENISA begins scoping an AI-augmented intrusion detection stack, and outreach starts with 12 critical operators across six member states. However, no implementation occurs this turn; the initiative remains in design phase, with full rollout expected no earlier than turn 4. Early coordination signals alignment but yields only marginal resilience gains: `resilience` increases to 38.0, reflecting private-sector momentum rather than systemic hardening.
