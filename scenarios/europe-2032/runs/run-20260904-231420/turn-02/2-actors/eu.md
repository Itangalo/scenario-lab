## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because without binding control over compute deployed on our soil, all other measures depend on permission from others, and the openweight frontier release proves that capability is now diffusing too fast to govern through labs alone.

## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that compute is power, and that power cannot be outsourced if sovereignty is to mean anything. This turn, the Commission finalises the draft of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive under Article 114 TFEU, locking in the 30% reservation for public-interest use across all data centres above 100 petaFLOPS. We are entering trilogue negotiations with the Council and Parliament, where the Competitiveness Council remains a hard obstacle. To break the deadlock, we are offering a binding sunset clause: the directive will be reviewed at turn 4, and automatically expire unless reauthorised based on demonstrated sovereignty gains. In return, we demand that the Council drop legal threats and allow the directive to proceed under urgency procedure.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the Tech sovereignty package by securing commitments from five additional member states to designate AI Acceleration Zones, bringing the total to eight. These zones now have draft grid-access guarantees and fast-track permitting templates, co-developed with ENTSO-E and the European Investment Bank. We are also piloting a community benefit mechanism in three zones, where 5% of data centre profits will fund local digital infrastructure — a move designed to defuse local opposition and build social licence.

The new Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative responds directly to the stealthy grid intrusion and the alarming pace of openweight offensive capability. We are tasking ENISA with designing the detection stack, using EU-funded models trained under strict data sovereignty conditions. The first phase will cover 12 critical operators across six member states, with red-teaming conducted by a newly established European Cyber Rapid Response Corps. This is not optional modernisation — it is emergency preparedness, and we are funding it by reallocating €4.2bn from unused cohesion funds, conditioned on national co-financing. We are not waiting for consensus; we are hardening the core while the perimeter still burns.
