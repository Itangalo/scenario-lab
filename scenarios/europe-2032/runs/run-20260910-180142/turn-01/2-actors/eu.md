## New measure
**EU Critical Infrastructure Shield**
Hardens electricity, ports, water and telecoms against AI-enabled intrusion with mandatory detection standards, joint exercises and cross-border mutual aid.
This is category 6 Preparedness and resilience, because the autumn grid/port intrusion showed detection would not have caught weeks-long presence, and open-weight frontier cyber capability is now unrecallable.

## Priority
EU Critical Infrastructure Shield, not the Gigafactories, because the intrusion that was found by accident outranks build-out that pays off years hence.

## In practice
We are pushing the Shield through the Transport, Telecommunications and Energy Council with ENISA as operator and ACER and EMSA in support, funded by reprogrammed Connecting Europe Facility and Digital Europe money plus CER/NIS2 enforcement. Grid operators and the affected port authority face binding intrusion-detection and 24-hour reporting duties; interior ministries resisting EU audit get EU-funded SOC capacity in return.

The inherited Gigafactories (M1) and tech sovereignty package (M2) continue on DG CNECT and DG ENER tracks: state-aid clearances for the first two sites, accelerated-permitting zones and grid-connection deals with France, Germany, Spain and the Nordics. We are using ASML leverage quietly — no new export move now — to keep Washington from a repeat of the June switch-off while we build.

We frame this as act-under-uncertainty: we do not know which capability reading is right, but resilience pays under all three, and it buys the political capital to sustain the sovereignty build when the next incident lands.
