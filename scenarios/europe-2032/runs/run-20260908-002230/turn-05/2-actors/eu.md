## In practice
This turn, we are completing the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO) with full integration of the 87 designated operators, including the final three member states whose delayed implementation was resolved through co-financing agreements under the Recovery and Resilience Facility and binding deadlines enforced via the NIS2 Directive. ENISA has issued the first cycle of red-teaming mandates, and the central monitoring dashboard now streams anonymised intrusion data into the EU Cyber Crisis Liaison Organisation Network (EU-CyCLONe), marking a major step in operational resilience. At the same time, the Open-Weight Security Baseline (OWSB) completes its initial audit wave, revealing critical vulnerabilities in widely used open models—particularly in PyTorch-based dependency chains and model-zoo tooling. Emergency patches and developer support are being coordinated through DG CONNECT and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre, with immediate mitigation deployed for models used in public services.

We are advancing the Sovereign Evaluation Consortium (SEC) by securing final legal clearance from the European Data Protection Board for air-gapped audits under GDPR Article 89, enabling access to model weights under strict containment protocols. Recruitment for the first 40 auditors is underway, with priority given to candidates with experience in adversarial testing, agent monitoring, and red-teaming at non-U.S. labs. Infrastructure upgrades at the JRC Ispra site are 78% complete, with secure evaluation pods expected to host pilot audits by turn 6. Parallel legal work is progressing on a binding access framework under the AI Act’s systemic risk provisions, aiming to compel cooperation from all developers placing models above 55.0 capability on the EU market.

Behind the scenes, we are preparing the launch of the EU Sovereign AI Corps—a new measure designed to anchor human capital as decisively as we are anchoring compute. The Corps will offer fast-track residency, salary top-ups, and access to sovereign AI infrastructure in exchange for a five-year service commitment and a legally enforceable duty to report safety-critical anomalies. We are already in quiet talks with national AI hubs in France, Germany, and Finland to align on eligibility and deployment pathways, knowing that without talent rooted in our jurisdiction, all our compute and all our rules will be instruments without hands.
