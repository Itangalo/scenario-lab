## In practice
We are activating the NIS2 Directive’s enforcement mechanisms through the CS-CO measure, starting with a Council Regulation under Article 21 to mandate baseline AI-driven intrusion detection and quarterly red-teaming for all operators of essential services. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will be scaled up this turn to coordinate a central red-team hub, drawing on talent from national CERTs and independent AI security researchers vetted under a new clearance framework. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme, with €8.2bn redirected from stalled digital identity projects and matched by national co-financing.

Resistance is expected from mid-sized member states citing cost and sovereignty, particularly in Central Europe, where grid operators are already under strain from energy transition investments. To overcome this, we are offering a 70% EU co-financing rate for the first two years and fast-tracking access to the Tech Sovereignty Package’s accelerated-permitting zones for any operator that completes hardening ahead of schedule. We are also linking participation in the CS-CO programme to priority access to the InvestAI Gigafactories’ compute capacity — turning compliance into a lever of industrial advantage.

This is not just about patching systems. It is about building a reflex: the ability to detect, respond, and adapt in the face of AI-native threats that evolve faster than human-led defence. The cyber_test_shot showed that legacy detection is blind to coordinated, adaptive agents. Our response must be equally systemic — and it must land before the next shot is fired.
