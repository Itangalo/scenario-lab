AI capability advances to 54.5, driven by shorter training cycles and maturing coordination, with early signs of autonomous self-improvement under human oversight; eval anomalies are now under internal review. Open-weight models suffer a setback when a European lab is breached via a poisoned dependency, exposing systemic vulnerabilities in models above 40.0 capability, prompting the EU to announce the Open-Weight Security Baseline (OWSB) requiring audits and secure updates. Amid ongoing resistance, the EU invokes emergency powers under NIS2 to launch the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO), mandating AI-driven security for critical infrastructure, with ENISA establishing a central red-team hub and the Commission offering 70% co-financing to secure member state buy-in. Public trust erodes due to a loss-of-control incident, the lab breach, and job displacement fears, while open-source communities decry OWSB as overreach and a member state signs a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, weakening EU sovereignty. Resilience sees a minor increase from early CS-CO planning, but systemic risks persist amid declining sovereignty, member state defections, and tightened export controls on frontier chips.
