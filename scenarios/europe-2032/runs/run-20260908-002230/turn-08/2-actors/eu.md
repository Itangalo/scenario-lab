## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that the second mandate cannot be fulfilled without the first, and that sovereignty is not a means among others but the condition of any credible action at all  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event this turn — the abrupt and unilateral denial of access to the leading model, with no appeal or explanation — confirms that dependence on foreign-controlled frontier systems is not a temporary vulnerability but a structural abdication of agency. Hospitals, ministries and firms reliant on this capability were left blind and crippled overnight. This is not a failure of policy but of posture: we cannot govern, audit or defend systems we cannot access, and we cannot access them unless we can credibly threaten to operate without them. The modification reflects a categorical shift: preventing harm now requires that we are the authors of our own AI future, not just its regulator. Without sovereignty, both mandates collapse.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity and control before the frontier escapes collective oversight — but now with the understanding that the frontier has already escaped, and our task is not to prevent autonomy but to reassert political authority over a domain where technical systems no longer wait for permission  
- Grounds: The convergence of **cyber_major_incident**, **eu_frontier_access_denied**, and the ongoing failure to attribute the **loss_of_control_incident** proves that the window for oversight has closed. We are no longer preparing for a future where AI might act beyond human control — we are governing in that future. Our two-year commitment must therefore evolve: it is no longer enough to build capacity in time to matter; we must assume that time has already passed, and that our systems must function *despite* the absence of global coordination or vendor cooperation. This redefinition binds us to a new urgency: sovereignty is not a hedge, it is the only ground from which action remains possible.

## New measure  
**Launch Sovereign AI Core: A Legally Anchored, Air-Gapped Execution Environment for Critical Public Functions**  
We establish a sovereign, EU-operated AI stack — hardware, software, and governance — capable of running essential public functions (evaluation, crisis response, continuity planning) without reliance on any external model or cloud provider, using only EU-controlled weights and compute.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect: eu_ai_sovereignty +15 (by creating an independent execution capability), ai_safety +5 (by enabling secure, observable agent deployment), resilience +6 (by ensuring continuity of critical AI services during external denial or compromise)`  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, Sovereign Evaluation Consortium, AI Incident Response Corps, national crisis agencies, and designated public research laboratories

## Priority  
**Sovereign AI Core** — because **eu_frontier_access_denied** has proven that no evaluation, no audit, no incident response can be trusted if it depends on infrastructure or models we do not control, and because the **cyber_major_incident** shows that the offensive use of AI is already outpacing defence — this is no longer about building capacity for the future, but about restoring agency in the present.

## In practice  
This turn, we are acting in the wake of a denial that has exposed our strategic nakedness. The sudden cutoff from the leading model — the same one we sought to audit under systemic risk powers — has left hospitals unable to process diagnostics, ministries blind to emerging threats, and the Incident Response Corps without the tools to simulate the very attacks now unfolding. We are no longer being excluded as a matter of policy; we are being treated as irrelevant. Our response is no longer incremental: we are building our own core.

We are launching the **Sovereign AI Core** under Article 114 TFEU, combining the compute from the now-completed InvestAI Gigafactories with the air-gapped audit environments at Ispra and the technical talent pooled from the Sovereign Evaluation Consortium. This is not a clone of foreign systems — it is a sovereign stack, starting at capability 58.0 using the best EU-accessible weights, and designed to evolve independently. We are tasking the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) with operational command, and mandating that all EU-critical AI workloads — including those of the SEC and the Response Corps — migrate to this stack by turn 12. The first deployment will be a hardened agent for real-time incident triage, running entirely on EU soil, with no external dependencies.

We are negotiating with France, Germany, and Finland to co-host the core’s execution nodes in geologically stable, energy-secure regions, offering fast-tracked funding for grid upgrades in exchange for binding legal guarantees of jurisdictional immunity. Resistance is expected from member states reliant on US cloud providers, so we are offering transitional support: two years of parallel operation, with EU subsidies covering 70% of the migration cost for critical public functions. This is not a moratorium, not a restriction — it is a declaration of operational independence. We will not wait for permission to defend ourselves.
