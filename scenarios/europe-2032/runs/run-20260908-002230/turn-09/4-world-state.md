### The Fracture Deepens

The EU scrambles to stabilize its digital backbone after the dual shock of a major AI-powered cyber intrusion and the abrupt denial of frontier model access. Energy grid operators in three member states remain on emergency protocols, while hospitals report cascading delays in diagnostics. The public sees not a crisis response, but a system in retreat.

### Emergency Resilience Activation: Partial Gains, Friction Everywhere

The Emergency Resilience Activation launches under the Civil Protection Mechanism, directing urgent funds to harden energy, health, and crisis response systems. Defensive AI agents are deployed in isolated environments, and fallback procedures are mandated. However, implementation is uneven. Two member states delay compliance, citing vendor lock-in and cost. Only partial deployment is achieved by turn’s end, but enough to strengthen incident containment and earn a resilience boost.

### Sovereign AI Core: Momentum Amid Headwinds

Work continues on the Sovereign AI Core, with France, Germany, and Finland finalizing hosting terms. Legal immunities are granted, and the first triage workloads are containerized. Yet, the project’s complexity grows, and no operational capability comes online this turn. The measure remains in flight, contributing no immediate sovereignty gain beyond its initial proposal bonus.

### A Defection Undermines Unity

In a blow to cohesion, one member state quietly negotiates direct access to a U.S. hyperscaler’s latest model, bypassing EU coordination. Framed domestically as pragmatic recovery, it is seen externally as a fracture in the bloc’s stance—worsening dependency while pretending otherwise.

### Public Trust Erodes Further

With visible disruptions and internal disunity, public sentiment declines. Local governments begin informal restrictions on AI use in public services, citing uncontrolled risk. The mood shifts from anxiety to active resistance.
