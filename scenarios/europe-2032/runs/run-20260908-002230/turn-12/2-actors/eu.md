## New measure  
**Launch Civic AI Integration Initiative: Rebuild Public Trust Through Transparent, Localised AI Deployment in Essential Services**  
We deploy EU-sovereign models in visible, high-impact public services—health diagnostics support, emergency response coordination, and vocational retraining—under strict transparency protocols, with real-time performance dashboards and opt-out mechanisms, to demonstrate benefit and accountability.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` public_sentiment +15 (by demonstrating tangible public benefit and restoring agency through transparency), resilience +3 (via improved emergency coordination systems), eu_political_capital +5 (if sentiment recovers, unlocking fiscal and legislative headroom)  
`Applies to:` Member states, regional health and emergency authorities, EuroHPC JU, ENISA, and civil society oversight bodies

## Priority  
**Launch Civic AI Integration Initiative** — because while the Sovereign AI Core finishes its build, it does so without public consent, and with sentiment now at 20.0—deep in hostile territory—our most urgent vulnerability is not technical but political: without legitimacy, no amount of isolated compute can sustain authority. This measure directly confronts that deficit by shifting from seizure to service, from fortress to function.

## In practice  
This turn, the Sovereign AI Core reaches operational completion. Final nodes in France and Finland are certified air-gapped and running containerised workloads for critical infrastructure monitoring and encrypted intergovernmental communication. Legal disputes in Germany and the Netherlands remain unresolved, but the provisional use framework established under Article 213 TFEU allows continued operation under ENISA audit, with data provenance logs made available to national courts on request. The system is secure, isolated, and technically sovereign—but it is also invisible, untrusted, and politically fragile. As protests continue and sentiment plummets, we cannot treat completion as victory.

Instead, we pivot to repair the social contract. The Open-Source Security Partnership expands: the Nordic AI Audit Forum publishes its first public vulnerability scorecard, and the Berlin Interpretability Group releases a real-time attention-flow visualiser now integrated into the Core’s monitoring stack. These tools are not yet mandatory, but their adoption by sovereign labs is rising—and so is quiet confidence in external scrutiny. Still, this is a technical fix for a political wound.

So we launch the Civic AI Integration Initiative. We will deploy sovereign models in three domains: regional cancer screening support in Spain and Italy (processing anonymised imaging with human-in-the-loop verification), flood prediction and emergency dispatch coordination in the Rhine basin (with opt-in data sharing and public dashboards), and AI-guided retraining pathways for displaced manufacturing workers in Poland and Slovakia (linked to active labour market policies). Each deployment includes a local oversight council with civil society representation, real-time performance and error reporting, and a clear off-switch. Funding of €1.2 billion is redirected from the now-concluded Crisis Compute Mobilisation, with implementation led by the European Commission’s DG CONNECT in coordination with national health and interior ministries. We are offering member states fast-track access to EuroHPC compute in exchange for participation—and we are not hiding the risks. Because if we want trust back, we must stop asking for patience and start delivering proof.
