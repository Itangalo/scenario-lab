### A Core Completed, But Not Conquered

The **Sovereign AI Core** reaches operational completion, with air-gapped nodes in France and Finland now running critical infrastructure monitoring and secure intergovernmental communications. Despite unresolved legal disputes in Germany and the Netherlands, a provisional framework under Article 213 TFEU allows continued operation under ENISA audit. Technically, the EU has achieved a milestone in sovereign execution—but politically, the system remains isolated and distrusted.

Public sentiment, though still low, shows faint signs of stabilization. The **Civic AI Integration Initiative** launches with high visibility: cancer screening support in Spain and Italy, flood prediction in the Rhine basin, and retraining guidance in Poland and Slovakia. Each deployment includes real-time dashboards, opt-out mechanisms, and civil society oversight councils. Early feedback is cautious but constructive, with regional officials noting improved coordination and transparency. These tangible benefits nudge public sentiment upward by a narrow margin.

The **Open-Source Security Partnership** gains momentum as the Nordic AI Audit Forum releases its first public vulnerability scorecard, and a real-time interpretability tool is integrated into the Core’s monitoring stack. These contributions, though not yet mandatory, are increasingly adopted by sovereign labs, strengthening external validation and modestly improving assurance. This progress, combined with ongoing red-teaming, lifts *ai_safety* by +2.

Resilience improves slightly as emergency coordination systems are stress-tested during Rhine flood simulations, demonstrating faster response loops. The distributed nature of the new deployments also begins to harden institutional continuity against disruption.

The EU avoids a political capital collapse despite a heavy portfolio: **Sovereign AI Core** finishes (+5), **Open-Source Security Partnership** continues (−2), and the new **Civic AI Integration Initiative** (large, −3) is prioritised (−1).
