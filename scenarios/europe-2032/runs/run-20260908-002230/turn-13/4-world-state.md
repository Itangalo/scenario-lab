### A Fractured Core, a Failing Frontier

The **Sovereign AI Core** completes its technical rollout in France and Finland, now monitoring critical infrastructure with air-gapped systems under ENISA audit. Yet legal disputes in Germany and the Netherlands persist, blocking expansion and exposing a fatal gap: sovereignty without legitimacy is static. The Core functions — but it does not grow.

Simultaneously, the **Civic AI Integration Initiative** launches in Spain, Italy, Poland, and Slovakia, deploying AI for cancer screening, flood prediction, and retraining. Real-time dashboards and oversight councils offer transparency, but regional bottlenecks delay integration, especially in areas with weak digital governance. Public sentiment remains stagnant at 22.0 — too low to shift political momentum.

A major shock arrives: **private AI investment collapses globally**. Valuations crater, hyperscaler build-outs cancel, and several EU compute partnerships evaporate overnight. The frontier’s growth slows — `ai_capability` rises only +0.5 this turn — but the vacuum threatens fragmentation. Smaller labs lose funding; open-weight development risks stalling just as it becomes critical.

In response, the EU proposes the **EU-Regional Evaluation Network**, aiming to formalise and fund the emergent coalition of Nordic, Baltic, and Eastern European red-teaming groups that recently detected anomalous agentic planning. The initiative would embed distributed vigilance into EU structures, leveraging early detection as a new form of sovereign capability.

However, the timing proves treacherous. A **member state quietly cuts a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler**, securing preferential access to high-performance models on terms that undercut the Union’s coordinated stance. The move, framed domestically as pragmatic necessity, is seen externally as defection — weakening collective resolve and complicating efforts to unify evaluation standards.

Despite these pressures, the Open-Source Security Partnership continues, with interpretability tools now integrated into Core monitoring. Still, progress is incremental, and no leap in assurance occurs.

### Political Capital Erodes

The Sovereign AI Core finishes (+5), but the new large measure (−3), ongoing partnership (−2), and prioritisation (−1) strain an already thin political capital. The collapse in private investment offers no relief, as no defensive measure was in place to absorb the shock. Member-state defection counts as an internal-origin negative event, costing −5. No proposal bonus applies — the new measure answers an emergent trend, not a past incident. The net effect is a decline from 30.0 to 29.0.

Sovereignty rises slightly due to distributed coordination gains (+1), offsetting the −1 from capability growth, reaching 37.0.
