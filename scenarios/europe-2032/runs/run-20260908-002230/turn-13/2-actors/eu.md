## New measure  
**Stand Up the EU-Regional Evaluation Network: Integrate Emerging Red-Teaming Coalitions into a Formal, Funded Cross-Border Detection and Response Framework**  
We formalise and resource the emergent network of national labs, Eastern European tech hubs, and Nordic audit groups into a standing EU-coordinated evaluation and early-warning system for agentic AI behaviour, with shared protocols, joint exercises, and direct reporting lines to ENISA and the European Commission.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`

## Priority  
**Stand Up the EU-Regional Evaluation Network** — because the emergent detection of anomalous agentic behaviour proves that our most effective safety infrastructure is now growing outside EU institutions, and with private investment collapsing, the window to absorb and strengthen these networks before they fragment or disappear is narrow and closing.

## In practice  
This turn, the Sovereign AI Core finishes its operational build. The nodes in France and Finland are now fully air-gapped and running live workloads: monitoring power grid stability, water treatment integrity, and encrypted intergovernmental messaging. ENISA conducts its first full audit under the provisional Article 213 TFEU framework, publishing a redacted chain of custody for model weights and inference logs. Yet Germany and the Netherlands continue to challenge the legal basis, and no new member states have volunteered to host additional nodes. The Core is secure, but it is not spreading — and without legal unity or public visibility, it risks becoming a fortress with no city around it.

Meanwhile, the Open-Source Security Partnership deepens. The Nordic AI Audit Forum releases version 1.2 of its vulnerability scorecard, now covering agentic planning loops and data exfiltration vectors, and the Berlin Interpretability Group integrates its real-time visualiser into the Core’s monitoring dashboard. Adoption is voluntary, but all three sovereign labs now run the tools in parallel with internal checks. Still, this remains a patchwork — technically promising, but institutionally thin.

The Civic AI Integration Initiative rolls out in phases. Spain and Italy begin pilot deployments of the cancer screening support system, linking anonymised imaging data to sovereign models with radiologist-in-the-loop verification. In the Rhine basin, flood prediction models go live with public dashboards showing confidence intervals and data sources. Poland and Slovakia launch AI-guided retraining portals, integrated with national employment services. Each site establishes a local oversight council with civil society, unions, and technical experts. Funding flows from redirected Crisis Compute Mobilisation budgets — €1.2 billion reallocated via DG CONNECT — but implementation friction remains high, especially in regions with low digital capacity.

Now we act on the signal from the emergent Regional Evaluation Network. Labs in Warsaw, Helsinki, and Riga have coordinated detection of recursive planning behaviour in testbed models — not yet dangerous, but structurally novel and invisible to standard benchmarks. This was caught not by a central authority, but by a loose coalition using shared red-teaming scripts and cross-border data pooling. We cannot let this capability drift. So we launch the EU-Regional Evaluation Network: a formal, funded framework under ENISA coordination, integrating these groups into a standing detection and response layer. We propose €800 million over three years via the Digital Europe Programme, with legal basis in Article 16 TFEU and operational home in the newly empowered ENISA AI Monitoring Directorate. We offer participating states priority access to sovereign compute and joint ownership of detection IP. This is not a takeover — it is an invitation to scale. Because if the next frontier of safety is distributed, then sovereignty must be too.
