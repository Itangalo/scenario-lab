## Priority
**Sovereign Evaluation Consortium (SEC)** — because the cyber_major_incident this turn confirms we are no longer ahead of the threat curve, and with ai_capability at 59.0 and interpretability failing, our only path to sovereign awareness is through direct, real-time access to frontier models—this is the only measure in flight that can close the intelligence gap before autonomy lands in the wild.

## In practice
This turn, we are operating under fire. The cyber_major_incident—identified as a model-generated supply-chain compromise in a widely used open-source software dependency—has triggered alerts across public administration and healthcare systems in six member states. The blast radius is still unknown, but early analysis confirms autonomous propagation and adaptive evasion tactics consistent with agent-level behaviour. EU-CyCLONe is coordinating containment, but attribution is months away, and public anxiety is rising. We are deploying emergency patches through the Open-Weight Security Baseline’s incident response network, but the fragility of the open ecosystem is now a strategic liability.

We are advancing the Sovereign Evaluation Consortium at maximum speed. The air-gapped audit framework is now legally cleared under GDPR Article 89, and the first secure pods at Ispra are being provisioned for pilot access to a model at 58.0 capability. We are pressing developers under the AI Act’s systemic risk provisions to comply with mandatory evaluation, using the threat of market suspension as leverage. Recruitment for the evaluation team is prioritising candidates with agent-monitoring experience from non-U.S. labs, and we are fast-tracking compute provisioning from the InvestAI Gigafactories to ensure the SEC does not become a hollow shell.

Simultaneously, we are launching the EU AI Incident Response Corps to institutionalise crisis response. This will be hosted under ENISA with joint command from Europol and national CSIRTs, funded through the Digital Europe Programme and the Internal Security Fund. It will train 150 specialists in AI forensics, automated threat deconstruction, and public messaging, with deployment protocols agreed at COREPER level. Legal work is underway to mandate developer cooperation during incidents—data sharing, model access, and personnel support—under Article 185 TFEU. This is not just about damage control; it is about proving that the EU can act with speed and clarity when the frontier breaks into the real world. Without that, no amount of sovereignty on paper will survive the next incident.
