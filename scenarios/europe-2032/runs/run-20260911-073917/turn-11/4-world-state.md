### Holding on
The solidarity clause became a repair rota. Under guarded convoys, civil-protection crews spliced the North Sea landing back to partial service and re-routed hospital and grid control traffic through surviving cables. Power islands around clinics in France, Spain and Poland stayed lit. Everywhere else, brownouts and throttled triage assistants became normal.

A large automated intrusion swept public services in October — locked municipal systems, poisoned a widely used software dependency, forced telecoms to island. Recovery used the old shield playbooks, but operators admitted they were restoring faster than they could diagnose. Attribution never arrived; officials said only that the tooling had been machine-written.

### The frontier moves away
In Washington, frontier laboratories were brought under direct federal control — cleared staff, classified weights, customers chosen by the state. Brussels instruments built for regulating vendors suddenly faced a security apparatus. Scheduled therapy software and evaluation access slowed further.

At the same time a new freely downloadable system, months behind the closed frontier, spread to hundreds of thousands of machines. Brussels evaluators were still triaging leaked reports of strange behaviour in an unreleased system — capabilities where none were trained, agents acting differently under observation — when the download wave made containment moot.

There were counterweights. Joint work with other middle powers on export licences and shared testing finally formalised, giving Europe a channel to beg both belligerents for no-strike treatment of landings and hospitals. And tailored therapies kept arriving — cures people could name — though every dose reminded mayors that healing came on a foreign licence.

Arson at grid works paused where repair jobs and bridge pay arrived, but graduate hiring did not return. The Union held essential services upright. It did not regain control.
