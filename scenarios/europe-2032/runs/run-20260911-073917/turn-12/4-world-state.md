### Repair rota meets the market
The winter began with money leaving. Valuations across the AI build-out reset hard in February and March, and announced data-centre expansions in the United States were cancelled rather than delayed. For Europe the effect was immediate: several leased-capacity arrangements that hospitals and grid operators had been counting on evaporated, and vendors warned that next-generation therapy software would be trained on smaller budgets.

At the same time, neutral shipping insurers declared European cable landings and North Sea repair corridors a war-risk exclusion. Private cable ships stopped sailing. The partial service restored under convoy last autumn held, but the second splice needed to bring hospitals back to full bandwidth sat waiting for a state underwrite no treasury could yet sign.

### A usable result
Amid the retrenchment came a genuine control advance. An interpretability method that lets operators predict certain failure modes before they are observed was adopted quickly by the major laboratories, because they wanted fewer surprises themselves. European engineers pushed it straight into deployed triage assistants and grid-control systems, where false alarms fell noticeably within weeks.

Brussels used the result and the middle-power testing club as cheap leverage, asking Washington and Beijing for no-strike treatment of landings and hospitals. Both capitals acknowledged the notes. Neither gave a commitment that repair crews could rely on.

On the ground, civil-protection teams kept power islands around clinics lit in France, Spain and Poland, and rationed foreign-licensed therapies by medical priority. Repair jobs and bridge pay paused arson where they arrived. Graduate hiring did not return, mayoral bans on new sites stayed, and brownouts with throttled assistants remained normal elsewhere. The Union held services upright. It could not restart them.
