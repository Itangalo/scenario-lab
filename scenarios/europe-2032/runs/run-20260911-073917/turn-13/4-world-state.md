### The notice
It arrived as a status page, not a démarche. Clinicians in Lyon, grid dispatchers in Warsaw, officials in three ministries found the leading American model refusing European users with a short error and no appeal route. Vendors confirmed within hours: access withdrawn at short notice. Workflows built on foreign licences stopped.

In Brussels the reading split instantly. Some capitals called it coercion. Others called it the foresight failure they had warned about since leased capacity evaporated and insurers walked away from the repair corridors.

### Rationing becomes substitution
DG SANTE's rationing lists were hastily re-pointed. With the American system dark, triage assistants and grid-control tools were switched to openly available models hosted on what European hardware remained, hardened with the failure-prediction technique that had cut false alarms earlier in the year.

Civil-protection teams made it work first where power islands already existed — clinics in France, Spain and Poland stayed lit and kept basic triage running. Elsewhere the swap was rougher: degraded answers, slower throughput, brownouts still rolling through evenings. The second cable splice for full hospital bandwidth remained unspliced; convoys held the partial service but no treasury would yet underwrite a full repair voyage alone.

There were faint signs of help. Harbour talk mentioned neutral-flag repair ships and a Nordic-Baltic underwriting workaround willing to sail outside war-risk cover, and middle-power envoys again acknowledged pleas for safe passage for landings and hospitals. Nothing sailed on those rumours.

Bridge pay and repair jobs kept a lid on site attacks, but mayoral bans stayed and hiring did not return. The public mood, already brittle, turned bitter at being switched off by a foreign update. The Union held the line upright on borrowed models. It did not get its frontier back.
