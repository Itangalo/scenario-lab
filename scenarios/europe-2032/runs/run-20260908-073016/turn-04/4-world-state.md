### The Frontier Pushes On  
Global AI capability advances by +2.5, reaching 62.0, as automated research systems demonstrate closed-loop hypothesis generation in materials science and logistics optimisation. Open models absorb delayed fragments of last cycle’s breakthroughs: `openweight_capability` rises to 51.0, nearing the threshold where unsupervised agents begin reproducing offensive cyber operations on consumer hardware.

### Cohesion Through Crisis  
The EU integrates into a new joint threat response coalition formed after coordinated attacks on energy grids across allied states. Binding operational protocols are fast-tracked, linking ENISA, national CERTs, and non-EU partners in real-time telemetry sharing. This integration, combined with the completion of the Cyber Shield programme, delivers a resilience boost: defences are now systemic, not siloed. A pan-European red-teaming exercise in April exposes lingering gaps in municipal infrastructure, but also demonstrates rapid cross-border recovery — a signal that preparedness is hardening into practice.

### Sovereignty Stalls, Capital Holds  
The InvestAI Gigafactories remain delayed, and with `ai_capability` rising over 2, `eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 18.0. Political capital stabilises despite five measures in flight (−14), a named priority (−1), and no new proposal bonus. However, the completion of Cyber Shield (+4) and integration into the joint coalition (+3) offset losses, yielding a net +2. Public sentiment ticks up (+2) due to visible coordination gains and the framing of AI resilience as collective security.

### Safety Still Lags  
No deployed safeguards keep pace with capability growth. The AI Safety Certification Lab progresses operationally but evaluates only legacy models this turn; its influence on `ai_safety` remains latent. The liability compromise in the European AI Cyber Assurance Pact breaks legal deadlock, yet ratification drags in two Eastern states, leaving full cohesion just out of reach.
