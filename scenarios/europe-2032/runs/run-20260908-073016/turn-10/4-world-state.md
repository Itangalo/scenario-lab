### Capability Marches On
Global AI capability advances by +3.0 this turn, reaching 79.0, as frontier systems autonomously refine biological simulations and optimise pharmaceutical supply chains. Human oversight remains nominal in high-impact domains, and no safety breakthrough closes the growing control gap.

### Medical Hope, Political Tension
A wave of individually tailored cancer therapies, developed using frontier AI models, enters clinical use across North America and parts of Asia. While life-saving, the models behind them remain beyond EU access under US consolidation. The Union celebrates the scientific breakthrough but frames it as a stark reminder of strategic dependence. Public sentiment rises on the promise of AI in medicine, yet unease lingers over exclusion from the tools that deliver it.

### Transition Gains Ground
The Labour Transition Acceleration Fund concludes its final disbursements, with retraining programmes in France, Spain, and Italy showing strong placement rates and measurable improvements in public service delivery. The "automation adjustment" clause in procurement is enforced in 17 major contracts, reinforcing the narrative of managed transition. This visible benefit lifts public sentiment into positive territory.

### Containment in Crisis
The Open-Weight Monitoring and Containment Initiative remains operational but toothless. ENISA flags high-risk models, but without enforceable takedown authority—still blocked by Germany’s pending constitutional review—platforms ignore alerts. Developer backlash grows, accusing the EU of stifling open innovation. Fallback talks with decentralised evaluation pilots in Eastern Europe intensify, but no formal pivot occurs.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Holds
US supply chain coercion forces ASML to restrict servicing of mature lithography tools, widening the chokehold. With no EU compute breakthrough and capability rising, sovereignty declines further. Yet, the success of the labour fund delivers a political capital boost, offsetting broader strain.
