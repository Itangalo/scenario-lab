## In practice  
We are treating the healthcare ransomware attack not as a failure of technology alone, but as a systemic breach of preparedness and solidarity. The Cyber Shield programme is being fast-tracked through the Competitiveness Council and the Permanent Representatives Committee, with the Commission invoking Article 13a of the NIS2 Directive to mandate AI-driven intrusion detection systems across all essential operators by end-Q4. To overcome resistance in Eastern member states, we are pairing enforcement with material support: the Digital Europe Programme is releasing €4.2bn in emergency co-financing (70% grant, 30% loan) for detection stack deployment, and Horizon Europe is accelerating the release of open-source monitoring tools developed by the nascent AI Safety Lab. ENISA is conducting weekly coordination calls and will run the first pan-European red-team exercise in November, simulating an AI-coordinated cascade across energy and health sectors.

At the same time, we are converting the emergent EU cyber assurance initiative into a formal pact. The new European AI Cyber Assurance Pact builds on the momentum of that coalition, turning informal cooperation into binding commitments. It will establish a secure, AI-augmented threat intelligence platform operated jointly by ENISA and national CERTs, with automated sharing of exploit signatures and behavioural anomalies. Operators who join will receive limited liability protection for good-faith data sharing and guaranteed access to EU emergency response reserves. The Pact will be negotiated under the Digital Decade framework, with a signing ceremony planned for early turn 5. This is not a substitute for Cyber Shield — it is its operational backbone, designed to ensure that when the next attack comes, we see it earlier, contain it faster, and recover together. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain in deadlock, but we are using the Pact negotiations to reframe compute sovereignty as a security imperative, not just an industrial one — linking access to future EU data centres to participation in the assurance network. Sovereignty must mean not just ownership, but readiness.
