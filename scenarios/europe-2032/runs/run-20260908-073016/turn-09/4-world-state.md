### Capability Surge
Global AI capability advances by +3.0 this turn, reaching 76.0, as frontier systems autonomously design novel materials and optimise complex supply chains. The closed frontier now operates at near-expert level across engineering and life sciences, with human oversight increasingly symbolic. No safety breakthrough occurs, and the gap between capability and control widens further.

### Containment in Legal Limbo
The EU’s Open-Weight Monitoring and Containment Initiative formally completes, but its enforcement remains suspended pending Germany’s constitutional review of emergency powers. While detection systems flag high-risk models, takedown authority is unenforceable, and developer pushback intensifies. The initiative’s symbolic closure does little to slow open proliferation.

### Labour Transition Delivers
The Labour Transition Acceleration Fund concludes with visible success: retraining placement rates exceed 65% in key regions, and public services show measurable improvement. This tangible benefit boosts public sentiment and reinforces the EU’s narrative of managed transition.

### Scandal and Solidarity
An automated decision system in a major member state’s social insurance agency is found to have denied thousands of claims due to biased AI logic, triggering a formal ombudsman investigation. The scandal fuels public distrust, but the EU’s focus on worker support insulates it from the worst fallout. Meanwhile, the Union finalises a middle-power coalition with Japan, South Korea, and Canada, aligning export controls and pooling evaluation resources—a diplomatic win that strengthens political capital.

### Sovereignty Erodes
Despite coalition gains, US consolidation continues to block EU access to advanced compute. With global capability rising over 2.0, dependence deepens, and AI sovereignty slips further.
