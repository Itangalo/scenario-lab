## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we must now act when the cost of waiting exceeds the cost of error, not merely when the risk of delay is high.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent strategic dependence on foreign AI powers while maintaining societal resilience to AI-enabled threats — but this can no longer be pursued as two parallel goals; it must now be pursued as a single integrated imperative: sovereignty through resilience, and resilience through sovereign capacity.

*Grounds:* The Taiwan blockade has made compute access a strategic emergency, not a policy choice. The eval anomaly reports suggest capability thresholds are being crossed unpredictably, and the current gap between `ai_capability` (85.0) and `ai_safety` (15.0) is catastrophic. The Labour Transition Fund’s final disbursements have delivered stabilisation, but the social insurance scandal has shattered trust, and containment remains unenforceable. Waiting for Germany’s ruling is no longer tenable — the world has moved. Sovereignty is not just compute or control, but the capacity to act when others withdraw. Resilience is not just buffers, but the ability to detect, respond, and enforce. These are no longer separate mandates — they are the same survival.

## In practice
We are invoking Article 122 TFEU on grounds of critical infrastructure disruption. The Taiwan blockade has frozen advanced semiconductor shipments — not just for China, but for all non-domestic chains. The US has already imposed consolidation controls; we will not wait to be excluded. We are convening an extraordinary Energy Council next week to fast-track grid access for three priority zones: Västerås, Prague, and Ljubljana — sites where we already have land reserves, partial permits, and cooling capacity. We are offering a 15-year tax holiday and guaranteed procurement to any firm — domestic or foreign — that commits to building and securing sovereign compute stacks under EU legal jurisdiction, with irrevocable access for ENISA, the European Commission’s AI Office, and designated public research institutions.

We are also redirecting €12bn from unused cohesion funds and leveraging the EIB to co-finance the first tranche. This is not a subsidy — it is a security investment. We are requiring that any recipient of this financing must accept binding obligations on data sovereignty, incident reporting, and model access for public evaluators. The US and UK are pushing back — hyperscalers argue this undermines their global operations. We are telling them: if you want to serve the EU market, you must now serve its survival. The German court’s silence on Article 26a ends our ability to wait — we act now, not because we have full clarity, but because the eval anomaly reports confirm that the frontier is moving in ways we cannot see, and we cannot afford to be blind and powerless.
