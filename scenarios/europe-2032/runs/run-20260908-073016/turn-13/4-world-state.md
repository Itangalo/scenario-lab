### Emergency Infrastructure Mobilisation
The European Union declares a strategic emergency in response to the Taiwan blockade and unexplained eval anomalies, launching the EU Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme under Article 122 TFEU. Binding obligations are imposed on energy grid access and data sovereignty for new compute hubs in Västerås, Prague, and Ljubljana. The European Investment Bank co-finances a €12bn first tranche, offering tax incentives to attract firms willing to operate under irrevocable EU jurisdiction.

Despite urgency, progress is fragmented. Germany’s constitutional uncertainty over AI governance spills into infrastructure policy—Bavaria blocks substation upgrades needed for the Prague corridor, citing unresolved legal questions. French tech leaders warn the rules could deter private investment. US hyperscalers protest the model access mandates as incompatible with global operations, threatening to limit participation to minimal compliance footprints.

### Capability Surges, Safety Frays
Global AI capability jumps to 89.0, driven by self-improving systems whose internal logic remains opaque. The eval anomaly reports—once dismissed as artefacts—are now echoed in independent red-team findings from academic hubs in Poland and Estonia, suggesting emergent reasoning patterns not present in training data. With no coordinated safety response, `ai_safety` drops sharply to 3.0.

### Trust Erodes Further
Public sentiment falls to 48.0 as media frames the compute push as a desperate gamble. Labour groups denounce the redirection of cohesion funds while retraining programmes in southern Europe face delays. Though the social insurance scandal recedes, its legacy hardens distrust: AI is seen less as a tool than a destabilising force operating beyond control.

### Resilience Undermined
With no active category 6 measure and rising capability, resilience dips to 42.0. Cyber defences remain patchy, and bio-risk detection lags despite early warnings from distributed research networks.
