### The American Shift
The United States elects a president who treats advanced AI as a strategic asset, tightening export controls and conditioning access for foreign partners on national security reviews. The era of open collaboration ends: allies now face tiered access, and the EU’s leverage through regulation weakens as non-compliance risks not just fines, but exclusion. ### Labour Markets Fracture
Across Europe, entry-level hiring in law, accountancy, software, and administration collapses. Graduates report internships vanishing and junior roles automated. While numbers are contested, the trend is not. Media frames it as a "lost cohort," eroding public trust in AI’s societal benefit.

### EU Responds with Leverage, Not Capacity
The EU completes three key measures: the AI Safety Certification Lab, the European AI Cyber Assurance Pact, and Joint Threat Response Integration. These deliver operational gains — the Lab begins publishing evaluation templates, and the Pact enables real-time coordination after a near-miss in Baltic energy grids. Resilience rises as protocols harden into practice.

Yet sovereignty continues to erode. Despite progress, the InvestAI Gigafactories remain delayed, and with `ai_capability` rising by +2.5, dependence deepens. The new Sovereign Access Framework is launched as a large category 4 measure, aiming to convert infrastructure access into policy leverage. But early bilateral talks with hyperscalers stall — one invokes arbitration under the Digital Markets Act, another delays commitment pending US posture clarity.

Public sentiment falls sharply, reacting to job losses and perceiving the new framework as reactive, not transformative. The political cost of five measures in flight, including a named priority, strains cohesion. The promised Communication on Strategic Digital Access is delayed to December, undercutting momentum.
