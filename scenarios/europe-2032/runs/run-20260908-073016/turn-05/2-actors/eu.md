## Priority
**Sovereign Access Framework** — because the US election outcome has redefined access as a revocable privilege, not a market right, and we must convert our stalled compute build-out into leverage now: this measure turns passive dependence into conditional reciprocity, aligning industrial policy with strategic autonomy.

## In practice
We are treating the completion of the AI Safety Certification Lab and the European AI Cyber Assurance Pact not as endpoints but as foundations: the Lab’s evaluation protocols will now feed directly into the Sovereign Access Framework, giving us technical grounding to set enforceable conditions on foreign operators. With the Lab operational, we can now demand model access under EU legal safeguards as a price of entry — not as a negotiation, but as a rule. The Joint Threat Response Integration has given us a seat at the table, but only if we bring something to it; this measure ensures that what we bring is not just data, but standards.

At the same time, we are reframing the stalled InvestAI Gigafactories not as a failing project but as a bargaining chip: the next phase of the Tech Sovereignty Package will tie permitting acceleration and grid access to compliance with EU safety and reporting rules. We are working with the Council’s Competitiveness configuration to adopt a qualified majority voting path on critical infrastructure access, bypassing the veto risks that delayed the Assurance Pact. The Commission is preparing a Communication on Strategic Digital Access, to be adopted in September, that will frame this not as protectionism but as parity — if Washington decides AI access is a national security matter, then so does Brussels.

Behind the scenes, we are engaging with the three largest foreign hyperscalers through bilateral memoranda, offering fast-track permitting for expansion if they commit to real-time incident reporting, third-party audits, and weight-sharing with the Certification Lab. The threat is clear: without agreement, they face delays, energy prioritisation for sovereign operators, and exclusion from future public procurement. The offer is equally clear: full market access in exchange for verifiable cooperation. This is not about building our own frontier AI tomorrow — that race may be lost — but about ensuring that whatever frontier capability enters our borders, we can see it, control it, and hold someone accountable when it fails.
