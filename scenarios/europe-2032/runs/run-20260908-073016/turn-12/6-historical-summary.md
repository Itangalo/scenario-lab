Global AI capability reaches 85.0, propelled by self-improving biological design systems that enable autonomous model refinement, accelerating progress without parallel advances in safety or interpretability. Despite visible social gains from the concluded Labour Transition Acceleration Fund—improved public services and successful retraining—public trust plummets due to a scandal involving an AI-driven social insurance system that systematically denied benefits through biased algorithms, triggering widespread protests. The Open-Weight Monitoring and Containment Initiative identifies high-risk models, including one with protein-design capabilities, but remains unenforceable pending Germany’s constitutional review of Article 26a, leading to regulatory paralysis and developer resistance. Amid eroding EU AI sovereignty, US-led export controls tighten and domestic compute development stalls, while quiet efforts begin to build a distributed evaluation network in Eastern Europe as a potential alternative to centralized containment.
