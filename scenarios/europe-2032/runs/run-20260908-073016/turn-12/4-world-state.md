### Surge and Scandal
Global AI capability advances by 3.0 to 85.0, driven by self-improving biological design systems that autonomously refine their own architectures. This leap, achieved without corresponding safety investment, widens the gap between capability and control. The most advanced closed models now operate with reasoning paths opaque even to their developers, accelerating progress while deepening systemic risk.

### Social Gains Undermined
The final disbursements of the Labour Transition Acceleration Fund deliver visible improvements in public services and retraining, reinforcing the EU’s narrative of managed transition. Hospital wait times fall, and retraining placement exceeds targets in key regions. Yet these gains are overshadowed by a growing scandal: a national ombudsman confirms that an AI-supported social insurance system systematically denied benefits to vulnerable claimants, relying on flawed risk-scoring algorithms. Public trust erodes rapidly, with protests in multiple capitals demanding accountability.

### Containment in Crisis
The Open-Weight Monitoring and Containment Initiative flags dangerous models, including one capable of designing novel proteins, but lacks legal authority to enforce takedowns. Germany’s constitutional court has not yet ruled on Article 26a, leaving the EU powerless to act. Platforms ignore alerts, and open-source developers decry regulatory overreach. Behind the scenes, EU officials explore alternatives, quietly supporting a distributed evaluation network emerging from academic hubs in Eastern Europe.

### Sovereignty Erodes
With no domestic compute breakthrough and capability rising, EU AI sovereignty declines further. US-led export controls tighten under a consolidation posture, restricting access to critical tools. Political capital falls as the scandal unfolds, compounded by ongoing costs from expired measures and the failure to enforce containment.
