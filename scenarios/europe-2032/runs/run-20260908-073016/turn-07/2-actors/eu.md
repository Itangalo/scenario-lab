## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: one to hold society together, the other to contain the most immediate vector of uncontrolled risk. The Labour Transition Acceleration Fund is now operational under the ESF+ legal base, with the Employment Council fast-tracking bilateral agreements with France, Spain, and Italy. We are releasing 40% of the allocated funds in this turn as emergency disbursements, tied to verified automation in legal, architectural, and junior engineering roles. The EMPL committee has approved the “automation adjustment” clause, which will begin applying to all public procurement contracts above €10 million from January 2030 — a signal that transition costs will not be socialised indefinitely. Our aim is to show results within six months: retrained workers in new roles, waiting times in public services visibly lower, and a narrative shift from loss to adaptation.

At the same time, the rise of openweight models to 54.75 — just 0.25 below autonomous agent viability — demands a response before capability spills beyond containment. The new **EU Open-Weight Monitoring and Containment Initiative** will be coordinated by a newly empowered unit within ENISA, working with national cyber agencies and academic partners to deploy automated model-scanning tools across major code repositories. We are drafting emergency powers under the AI Act’s Article 26a to allow rapid takedown of models assessed above 55.0, based on a fast-track evaluation protocol derived from the now-finished AI Safety Certification Lab’s templates. This will be controversial: we expect pushback from developer communities and free-software advocates, and we are preparing a counter-narrative around “responsible openness” — that unrestricted access must not mean unrestricted risk. Behind the scenes, we are negotiating with major hosting platforms to implement geofenced restrictions on downloads of flagged models within the EU, in exchange for liability shielding under the DSA. This is not a long-term substitute for sovereign compute, but it is the only brake we have left on open proliferation — and if we wait until 55.0 is crossed, it will be too late.
