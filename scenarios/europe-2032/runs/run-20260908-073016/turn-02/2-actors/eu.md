## Priority  
Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure — because the cyber_major_incident this turn proves that our defences are not just insufficient but already outpaced, and resilience is now the most urgent expression of both our mandates: without functioning infrastructure, sovereignty is meaningless, and without containment of harm, legitimacy collapses.

## In practice  
The cyber_major_incident — a cascading ransomware sweep across regional health administrations in three member states, enabled by AI-generated exploit chains and polymorphic payloads — has exposed how far behind we are. The attack bypassed legacy detection systems, encrypted critical patient records, and forced manual fallbacks that crippled services for days. Attribution remains uncertain, but forensic analysis confirms the tooling was model-generated, likely from a closed frontier system now at or above 55 capability. We are accelerating the Cyber Shield rollout: ENISA is deploying emergency detection modules by Q2 under the Digital Europe Programme, co-financed at 70%, and we are invoking Article 13 of the NIS2 Directive to mandate real-time telemetry from all designated operators. Resistance persists in Eastern capitals over cost and capacity, so we are pairing the mandate with fast-tracked access to AI monitoring stacks developed under the future Horizon Europe framework — not as charity, but as solidarity with teeth.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories sit in governance deadlock. The Commission’s golden share proposal has stalled in the Competitiveness Council; member states demand equity and control. We are now offering a phased ownership model: initial Union control for six years, then gradual equity transfer tied to performance and compliance with decarbonisation benchmarks. The goal is not to win every argument, but to break the logjam before the next energy crisis hits. The Tech Sovereignty Package advances slowly through COREPER, but finance ministries remain wary of open-ended energy commitments. We are linking it to the proposed electricity market reform — making clear that sovereignty means not just compute, but stable, green, and affordable power. And now, because we cannot wait for the frontier to slow down, we are building our own eyes: the AI Safety Certification Lab. Hosted jointly by the JRC and a consortium of vetted national labs, it will conduct sovereign evaluations of high-impact systems, starting with those used in critical infrastructure. Access will be mandatory for providers under the AI Act’s highest-risk tier, with weight security enforced to state-actor standards. This is not about catching up — it’s about ensuring that when the next incident comes, we know what we’re facing, and we know it first.
