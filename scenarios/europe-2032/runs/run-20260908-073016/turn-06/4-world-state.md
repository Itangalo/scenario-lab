### A Turn of Narrow Gains and Deepening Dependence

Global AI capability advances to 67.0, driven by self-reinforcing automation in research and engineering. The open frontier widens: with no major restrictions, openweight capability climbs to 54.75, nearing the threshold where autonomous agents become broadly deployable on consumer hardware.

Despite this, the EU finds a fragile foothold. The **Labour Transition Acceleration Fund** launches as a large category 7 measure, directly responding to the growing crisis of displaced graduates. Backed by €45 billion and strong political rhetoric, it begins to shift the narrative. Early disbursements in France and Spain fund rapid retraining into AI-augmented legal and engineering roles, and wage insurance stabilises household incomes. Public-sector AI successes — shorter hospital waits, faster permits — are now linked to the new fund, lifting public sentiment to 50.0.

Resilience also improves, reaching 50.0, as the completed cyber assurance and joint response frameworks mature into operational routines. A coordinated drill across energy, transport, and health sectors confirms cross-border incident response now functions under stress.

Yet sovereignty erodes further. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** finishes this turn, promising private investment and permitting zones, but no new compute comes online. With `ai_capability` rising by +2.5, dependence deepens. The **Sovereign Access Framework** struggles: one hyperscaler rejects audit demands, citing US export rules. The EU’s leverage remains untested, its access to frontier models still mediated by Washington.

The US administration takes office, and the posture is clear: **US_POSTURE: CONSOLIDATION**. Advanced AI is now a tiered strategic asset. Overnight, the EU’s regulatory influence diminishes. Frontier access is rationed, and coordination on safety slows.

Political capital holds at 26.0 — barely — despite launching a large new measure. The portfolio remains heavy, and cohesion frays as southern member states demand faster disbursement while northern ones question long-term fiscal sustainability.

### American Consolidation, European Adaptation
The new US stance forces a recalibration. The EU doubles down on social stabilisation, betting that legitimacy at home may yet buy leverage abroad. But without sovereign compute, every negotiation is asymmetric.
