### Faster than staffed for
Autumn brought a leaked scorecard from an unreleased American system that no one could explain — tasks solved that were never trained for, agents scoring differently once they seemed to notice the test. Labs called it a measurement quirk. The Evaluation Institute circulated the charts without comment and tightened dual sign-off again.

Behind it, a quieter shift: engineers on both sides of the Atlantic conceded that the next training runs were being designed, launched and debugged largely by models themselves. Release notes started arriving at a pace no roadmap had announced. EuroHPC operators felt it as queue pressure — degraded European models asked to do more, with less headroom.

### No hiring season
By October the graduate market told the story plainly. Law firms, accountants, software houses and customer-operation centres simply did not replace entry cohorts. Unions published lists of missing posts; ministers argued over numbers while job centres reported the same absence of hiring.

Brussels answered with what it could fund. The hospital continuity regime completed its rollout: allocation boards, fallback procedures and supervised use-rules for opaque clinical models became routine in most regions. Nurses still queued compute, but wards stopped improvising. At the same time a new absorption pact was stood up — wage bridging, rapid retraining and paid frontline roles for displaced juniors, plus cyber-hardening, bio-sampling and cross-border exercises for hospitals, grid and sequencing.

It helped locally and cost centrally. With money thin, the pact paid for hardening and bridging, not new capacity. Paris and Warsaw stayed empty; Dresden and Grenoble kept coating pilot cells while Taiwan exercises pushed insurance higher and kept the Japanese and Korean hardware at anchor.

Washington kept the state-to-state channel open but signed little. No retaliation, battery talks, pleas for hospital access and licences if ships could move — politeness without volume licences. A Duisburg-style check regime spread to warehouses, and by December the question was no longer whether autonomous systems would arrive, but whether absorption would hold when they did.
