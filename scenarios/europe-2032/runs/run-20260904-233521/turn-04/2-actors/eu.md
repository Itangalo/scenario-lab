## In practice  
We are treating the plateau in ai_safety as a warning sign. The interpretability tools exist, the near-miss in the German grid was real, and yet assurance is not advancing. The problem is no longer technical capacity—it is institutional authority. ENISA has issued standards, but it lacks the mandate, the staffing, and the enforcement teeth to ensure compliance across member states and against powerful foreign developers. That is why this turn, we propose the creation of the European AI Safety Certification Authority (EASCA), a dedicated agency modelled on EASA for aviation safety, with binding pre-deployment evaluation powers for any AI system above 50.0 capability index. EASCA will operate under Article 114 TFEU, with a governance board including national representatives, technical experts, and civil society observers. It will be headquartered in Prague, with satellite labs in Paris and Helsinki, and will draw initial staffing from national cybersecurity agencies and academic AI safety groups.

Simultaneously, we press forward on the Sovereign Chip Access Initiative, now in its second turn. The joint procurement vehicle under the European Defence Agency has completed its first aggregation cycle, pooling demand from seven member states and three Gigafactory operators. Contracts are being negotiated with SK Hynix and a US-based subsidiary of TSMC, with delivery timelines tied to Taiwan stability clauses. The strategic stockpile framework is now codified in Commission Delegated Regulation 2028/EDIC-2, and site preparation begins this quarter at the Lyon and Wrocław Gigafactory hubs. Domestic advanced packaging pilots are launching in Dresden and Eindhoven, supported by €4.2bn in InvestAI co-funding. We are also working bilaterally with Germany to resolve its resistance to defence-channel procurement, offering a veto mechanism on dual-use allocations and a seat on the Chip Security Oversight Board in exchange for full participation. The Competitiveness Council remains divided, but the momentum is shifting: without chips, there is no compute; without compute, there is no sovereignty.
