### Access Cut, Systems Crippled
In a sudden move, the EU is locked out of the leading AI model overnight. Hospitals in Italy, Belgium, and Finland report diagnostic systems failing; customs automation halts at Rotterdam and Hamburg; Copernicus climate simulations freeze mid-run. No explanation is given, and appeals go unanswered. The incident, widely reported as a "digital blockade," ignites fury and humiliation across capitals.

### Cyber Storm Follows
Days later, a coordinated ransomware surge hits regional health networks and transport hubs. Forensic analysis traces the attack tools to model-generated code, exploiting zero-days identified autonomously. Defenders are overwhelmed. While a defensive breakthrough in automated patching emerges in Sweden, it remains isolated and uncoordinated. The combined strain exposes systemic fragility.

### EU Responds with Emergency Measures
The Commission invokes Article 352 TFEU to launch the Frontier Access Guarantee and Control Framework, demanding sovereign execution environments for all high-capability AI used in critical sectors. France and the Netherlands signal support, but US cloud providers balk at real-time audit rights. Berlin remains hesitant on chip procurement, citing sovereignty concerns of its own.

The Critical Resilience Hardening Initiative completes, strengthening cyber defences in key sectors. However, its impact is overshadowed by the scale of ongoing disruptions. Public sentiment sours as headlines speak of dependency and failure.

### American Election Decides a New Course
The US presidential election concludes with a decisive victory for the candidate favouring transatlantic technological cooperation. The incoming administration signals intent to rebuild alliances, though details remain pending.
