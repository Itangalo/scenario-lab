In early 2028, the EU suffers a sudden cutoff from leading AI models, crippling critical systems in healthcare, customs, and climate monitoring across Italy, Belgium, and Finland—an event labeled a "digital blockade." Days later, a wave of AI-generated ransomware attacks strikes health and transport infrastructure, exposing systemic vulnerabilities despite an isolated defensive breakthrough in Sweden. The EU invokes Article 352 TFEU to establish the Frontier Access Guarantee and Control Framework, mandating sovereign execution environments for high-capability AI, with support from France and the Netherlands but resistance from US cloud providers and hesitation from Germany. The Critical Resilience Hardening Initiative is completed but overshadowed by ongoing crises. Public sentiment sours amid growing perceptions of technological dependency. The US presidential election results in a victory for a candidate favoring transatlantic cooperation, raising hopes for renewed technological alliance, though concrete actions remain pending.
