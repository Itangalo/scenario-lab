## New measure
**Labour Transition Acceleration Fund**  
Launch a Union-wide retraining and wage insurance programme to counter the first wave of structural AI-driven job displacement.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Stabilise public_sentiment by mitigating visible job losses; increase resilience by reducing social shock from displacement; modestly improve eu_political_capital through visible responsiveness.

## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_test_shot revealed that detection failed everywhere, and the Union cannot wait for the next intrusion to act when the attack surface is already open and widening, even as new pressures emerge.

## In practice  
We are treating the emergent labour displacement wave not as a future risk but as a present crisis of legitimacy. While the Emergency Resilience Surge moves through legal negotiation, we are launching the Labour Transition Acceleration Fund to signal that the Union sees the human dimension of AI disruption as equally urgent. This fund will redirect €18bn from unspent Recovery and Resilience Facility allocations and the European Social Fund to co-finance national retraining programmes focused on administrative, legal support, and mid-level software roles now under pressure. Wage insurance of up to 70% for displaced workers transitioning into new roles will be available for 18 months, with priority for sectors adopting AI at the fastest rate. Member states must submit plans within six months to access full co-financing, including partnerships with employers automating roles.

At the same time, we are pressing the Tech Sovereignty Package through its final legal challenges in Germany and Belgium, offering binding arbitration through the European Court of Justice to break deadlocks over permitting. We are also renegotiating with the US cloud provider that granted limited evaluation access: in exchange for full weight storage under EU jurisdiction and incident reporting rights, we will fast-track their participation in the InvestAI Gigafactories. This is not a concession — it is a test of whether operational control can be bought where sovereignty cannot yet be built. The goal remains clear: the EU must not be a consumer of decisions made elsewhere, whether in code or in labour markets.
