## New measure
**Emergency Resilience Surge**  
Launch immediate hardening of critical infrastructure against AI-enabled cyber intrusion.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 3`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience from 39.0 to 55.0+ by hardening energy, water and port systems; reduce incident dwell time from weeks to hours.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with binding minimum standards and joint exercises.

## Priority
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_test_shot revealed that detection failed everywhere, and the Union cannot wait for the next intrusion to act when the attack surface is already open and widening.

## In practice
We are treating the cyber_test_shot as a wake-up call: the intrusions were not stopped by detection but found by accident, and they spanned systems too vital to lose. This turn, the Commission will invoke Article 114 TFEU to propose a Directive on Critical AI-Resilient Infrastructure, mandating real-time monitoring, automated anomaly response, and quarterly cross-border red-teaming for electricity transmission, water utilities, and major port operators. The legal base is internal market harmonisation, but the intent is survival: we are no longer asking whether a breach is possible, but whether we can survive it when it comes.

The Council’s Internal Market and Competitiveness formation will lead, but we are already meeting with energy and transport ministers to align implementation. ENISA will be tasked with oversight, and we are redirecting €12bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Recovery and Resilience Facility top-ups to finance rapid deployment. Resistance is expected from member states with underfunded grids and stretched IT teams, so we are pairing the mandate with guaranteed co-financing of up to 70% for the first two years. The goal is not perfection but degradation: systems that keep running even when compromised, and responses that activate before cascading failure begins.

At the same time, we are pushing the InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package through national permitting bottlenecks. We are offering member states fast-track grid access and state-aid flexibility in exchange for designating AI compute zones with environmental impact assessments compressed to nine months. The Gigafactories will not deliver compute until turn 7, but we are using the momentum to renegotiate terms with hyperscalers: any non-EU operator wanting access to these zones must commit to weight storage, model evaluation access, and incident reporting under EU jurisdiction. This is not just about building faster — it is about ensuring that what is built cannot be switched off from outside.
