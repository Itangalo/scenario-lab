## New measure
**Emergency Cyber Shield for Critical Functions**  
Establish a standing, EU-operated defensive layer over energy, water, and health infrastructure using AI-driven anomaly detection and autonomous response protocols under human oversight.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Raise resilience from 40.0 to 55.0+ by end of turn 5; modestly improve ai_safety by enabling faster detection of AI-enabled intrusions; signal operational seriousness to public sentiment.  
`Applies to:` All EU member states, with mandatory integration for operators of essential services, funded through a combination of unused RRF allocations and emergency cohesion funds.

## Priority  
**Emergency Cyber Shield for Critical Functions** — because the Emergency Resilience Surge has failed to clear legal hurdles and deliver on the ground, and with ai_capability now at 58.5 and openweight at 52.0, the window for passive preparation has closed: we must now assume that AI-enabled attacks on critical infrastructure are not imminent but already in motion, and that only an operational, Union-level shield can close the gap between regulation and reality.

## In practice  
We are treating the failure of the Emergency Resilience Surge not as a delay but as a systemic signal: resilience cannot be mandated from the top if it is not operable from the centre. This turn, we are launching the Emergency Cyber Shield for Critical Functions as a direct-execution instrument under the authority of ENISA and the European Commission, bypassing national legal bottlenecks by deploying it as a parallel, opt-out prohibited layer over essential services. We will use €12bn in unspent Recovery and Resilience Facility funds and €8bn in emergency cohesion reserves to build and deploy the shield by turn 5, with real-time monitoring nodes in all member states and autonomous countermeasures pre-authorized for known AI-driven attack patterns. This is not a replacement for national responsibility — it is a floor beneath which no member state can fall.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on the Labour Transition Acceleration Fund, now reframing it as a conditionality for continued access to EU digital innovation grants: member states must submit approved transition plans by the end of this turn or face a 15% clawback on AI-related funding. We are also leveraging the campaign_atlanticist momentum to renegotiate with US cloud providers: in exchange for full data sovereignty and audit rights over their EU operations, we will offer fast-track participation in the InvestAI Gigafactories and joint R&D access through the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking. This is not dependency — it is a bridge. But the bridge must lead somewhere, and that somewhere is an EU that can act, not just react.
