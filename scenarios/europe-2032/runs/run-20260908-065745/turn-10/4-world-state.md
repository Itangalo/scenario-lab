### The Fracture Deepens

Global AI capability advances to 67.5 as scaling continues unabated. The release of a powerful open-weight model within months of the frontier — downloaded hundreds of thousands of times — confirms that containment is over. `openweight_capability` jumps to 66.5, leaving only a sliver behind the closed frontier. With no enforceable safety mechanisms, and no evaluation regime in place, the gap between capability and control widens catastrophically. The biosecurity community is thrown into turmoil by a credible study demonstrating that non-experts could soon engineer human-infective organisms using AI — a warning that goes unheeded by policymakers.

The EU’s Critical Incident Containment Protocol (CICP) formally ends, but its legacy is fragmentation. Only a minority of member states fully participated; Germany delayed, Poland and Hungary refused, and now France, Finland, and the Netherlands have launched their own parallel threat network outside ENISA. Central coordination is no longer functional — it is ceremonial.

In response, the EU launches the **Distributed Cognitive Resilience Backbone (DCRB)**, a large category 6 measure aimed at building detection capacity that persists even if member states defect. Funded through emergency mechanisms, it is declared a constitutional imperative: the Union must still be able to *see* when systems fail, even if it cannot control them.

Yet political capital is near collapse. The portfolio charge remains high, public sentiment falls further due to the annulled election and biosecurity fears, and the launch of a costly new measure without fiscal space triggers quiet resistance. Germany rejects co-design overtures, calling them coercive. Poland and Hungary denounce the DCRB as a surveillance project. Even among allies, interoperability talks stall.

The American posture of **CONSOLIDATION** continues to restrict EU access to frontier models, compounding dependency. No domestic compute comes online. Sovereignty erodes further.

### A Union of Nodes, Not Nations

The EU acts not as a bloc, but as a network of last resort. The DCRB begins deployment in France, Benelux, and Nordic states. Satellite uplinks are tested. But the system is unproven, and trust is gone.
