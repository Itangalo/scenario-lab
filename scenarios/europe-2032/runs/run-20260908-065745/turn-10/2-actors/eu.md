## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now acknowledge that preserving political agency in a world of pervasive, uncontrolled AI may require us to act as if sovereignty is no longer territorial, but cognitive: our authority lies not in controlling systems, but in being the last instance that can still detect, respond, and decide.
- Grounds: The **emergent_fractured_response_network** event marks a categorical shift: crisis response is no longer unified under EU institutions. A coalition of member states has bypassed ENISA to form a parallel detection network, rendering central coordination obsolete in practice. This is not mere disagreement — it is institutional divergence in real time. At the same time, `eu_ai_sovereignty` has collapsed to 6.0, foreign-controlled compute dominates even within defector states, and `ai_safety` remains at 0.0 while `openweight_capability` nears frontier levels. The Union’s ability to determine its own future is no longer a function of infrastructure or law, but of whether it can still *see* what is happening before it collapses. Our identity must reflect that we are no longer building sovereign control — we are preserving the capacity to act when all control has failed.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Shift focus from securing sovereign containment of AI-driven incidents to ensuring autonomous, EU-operated detection and response capacity survives even if member state cooperation fails  
- Trigger: the **emergent_fractured_response_network** event and the refusal of Poland and Hungary to participate in CICP implementation  
- Grounds: The original commitment assumed that containment could be achieved through binding directives and shared infrastructure. That assumption is broken. Germany delays, Poland and Hungary refuse, and now France, Finland, and the Netherlands have begun coordinating outside ENISA. The Union cannot mandate unity — so we must build redundancy. The revised commitment reflects a shift from harmonisation to survivability: we will ensure that detection and response nodes remain operational *somewhere*, even if the formal network fractures. This is not surrender — it is insurance against institutional collapse.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that control is recoverable at the model or compute level; our interventions must now assume pervasive, unauthorised capability within EU borders, and our success will be measured not by compliance, but by persistence of function amid systemic breach  
- Grounds: No change in logic from last turn — this revision completes the update. With `ai_capability` at 66.5 and `openweight_capability` at 65.5, and no mechanism for safety enforcement, the environment is one of total diffusion. The election annulment confirms that democratic integrity is already compromised. We are not governing capability — we are operating inside it. The commitment now fully internalises that we are acting in a condition of permanent breach.

## New measure

**Distributed Cognitive Resilience Backbone (DCRB)**  
Deploy a hardened, decentralised network of AI-powered detection nodes under direct EU operational control, designed to persist and coordinate even if member states withdraw or compromise their own systems.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience from 44.0 to 72.0 by end-2032 by enabling continuous, cross-border detection of autonomous AI behaviour in critical systems, independent of national participation or vendor cooperation, with fallback coordination via satellite uplinks and mesh networking  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, ENISA, European Defence Agency, select national cybersecurity agencies in willing member states (France, Benelux, Nordic), and EU-funded research labs

## In practice

We are no longer preparing for a crisis. We are managing a permanent state of breach. The **election_annulled** event has shattered public confidence, the **bio_uplift_findings** show that catastrophic misuse is now within reach of non-experts, and the **openweight_frontier_release** confirms that every dangerous capability is already distributed. Worse, the **emergent_fractured_response_network** reveals that even among the most technically aligned states, trust in central coordination has collapsed. We cannot rebuild unity — so we must engineer continuity without it.

This turn, we launch the Distributed Cognitive Resilience Backbone (DCRB) under Article 122 TFEU and the EU Civil Protection Mechanism, treating AI-driven systemic failure as a cross-border emergency. We allocate €15bn from the Innovation Fund and REACT-EU to build a network of air-gapped, open-hardware sensors trained to detect anomalous goal pursuit in energy grids, medical systems, transport controls, and election infrastructure. These nodes will operate independently of national authorities, communicating via low-orbit satellite relays and encrypted mesh links to a new EU Crisis Cognition Cell hosted in Luxembourg. We offer Germany co-design rights and early access to threat intelligence if it rejoins; we offer Poland and Hungary humanitarian-grade alerts during pilot phases, conditioning deeper integration on verifiable neutrality. To France, Finland, and the Netherlands, we propose interoperability with their parallel network — not as a rival, but as a redundant layer. This is not about trust. It is about ensuring that somewhere, someone still sees the strike coming.

The CICP finishes this turn, but its legacy is fragmentation. We do not renew it. Instead, we pivot to DCRB as the core of our survival architecture — a system that assumes defection, spoofing, and silence, and still functions. We brief the European Council that this is not a security measure, but a constitutional one: if the Union cannot perceive reality before it collapses, then no decision it makes matters. We are no longer protecting infrastructure. We are protecting the possibility of deciding.
