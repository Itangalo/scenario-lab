### Frontier Creeps, Safety Crumbles

With no safety evaluation regime in place and no transparency on frontier training runs, confidence in system control erodes further. The gap between capability and understanding widens dangerously, dragging `ai_safety` down by 3 points to 3.0.

### Sovereignty, Fractured and Failing
The InvestAI Gigafactories programme concludes, delivering physical infrastructure but falling short of strategic control — most facilities remain under foreign legal jurisdiction. The Mandatory Compute Jurisdiction Framework, intended to close this gap, faces outright defiance. One hyperscaler suspends operations in the EU, citing unacceptable compliance demands. Poland and Hungary deepen their parallel AI access pact with a US provider, bypassing EU authorization entirely. Germany’s constitutional court announces it will hear a challenge to the Enforcement Cyber Shield, freezing further rollout. These developments, combined with rising capability, erode EU leverage over its own AI base, reducing `eu_ai_sovereignty` to 10.0.

### Resilience in Crisis Mode
In response to growing systemic fragility, the EU launches the Emergency Resilience Surge, invoking crisis powers under Article 122 TFEU. ENISA issues emergency directives for energy and health sectors to implement AI-resistant protocols within 90 days. However, rollout is uneven — member states demand exemptions, and procurement bottlenecks delay hardware deployment. The initiative begins to build momentum, but full effects are distant.

### Public Trust Erodes Further
The backlash_physical event escalates: data centre occupations spread to three new sites, one resulting in serious injury. Media coverage frames the state’s enforcement efforts as heavy-handed and disconnected from public concern. Labour groups warn of impending displacement, and local governments resist hosting new infrastructure. Public sentiment drops to 23.0, reflecting deepening alienation.

### Political Capital Exhausted
With five measures in flight and political capital already at 10.0, the EU struggles to maintain coherence. The portfolio charge overwhelms the system, and the new priority yields no acceleration. The Model Authorization Regime formally ends, but its legacy is non-compliance, not control.
