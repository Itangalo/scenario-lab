## In practice  
This turn, we are treating the end of the InvestAI Gigafactories programme not as a victory but as a warning: compute on EU soil means nothing if it is not under EU control, and control means nothing if the systems it runs can collapse society when they fail. The Gigafactories delivered sites, but not sovereignty — too much remains under foreign legal command. That is why the Mandatory Compute Jurisdiction Framework continues into turn 8, now backed by the full weight of the Anti-Coercion Instrument and DMCA enforcement channels. We are no longer negotiating incorporation — we are demanding it, and we are preparing to designate non-compliant providers as systemic risk actors under the Digital Operational Resilience Act.

But the world is moving faster than our legal levers. The backlash_physical event — sabotage, occupations, injuries — proves that enforcement without legitimacy is fuel for chaos. With public_sentiment at 28.0 and Germany still withholding cooperation, we cannot rely on top-down control alone. So we pivot: the EU Model Authorization Regime finishes this turn, but its legacy is not compliance — it is awareness. We now know where the nodes are, but we also know they are vulnerable. So we act on two fronts. First, we launch the Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 122 TFEU, invoking crisis powers to fast-track funding and deployment. ENISA will issue binding hardening mandates within 30 days; HERA will run a live bio-cyber drill in Q1 2030. Member states will receive emergency grants tied to implementation speed, not perfection.

Second, we accept that we are not in control — not yet — and design for failure. The US has closed its frontier, China accelerates, and open weights at 59.0 are already in the wild. Our leverage is not in building faster, but in surviving longer. We are not abandoning sovereignty — we are redefining it: not as parity, but as persistence. The Surge is not about catching up. It is about staying standing.
