## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Prevent catastrophic AI-driven incidents within EU jurisdiction by establishing enforceable containment of high-capability systems — prioritising operational resilience, sovereign control over critical infrastructure, and rapid incident response over model-level authorisation or compute jurisdiction, which have proven politically unenforceable
  - Grounds: The **emergent_lab_state_fracture** event — the formal creation of a Lab Access Pact by four member states with a US hyperscaler — renders any EU-wide model authorisation or compute jurisdiction framework functionally void. With Germany’s constitutional court halting enforcement and Poland, Hungary, Greece and Slovakia now operating under a parallel regime, the legal unity required to enforce model-level controls has collapsed. Meanwhile, `ai_safety` at 3.0 and `openweight_capability` at 61.5 confirm that the threat is no longer potential but active. The previous commitment assumed a functioning enforcement perimeter; we now operate inside a breach. The revision acknowledges that we are no longer setting rules at the frontier, but managing failure within our borders. The pivot is from control to containment: we cannot stop the systems from entering, but we can — and must — prevent them from collapsing our essential services.
