## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent catastrophic AI-driven incidents within EU jurisdiction by establishing enforceable containment of high-capability systems — prioritising operational resilience, sovereign control over critical infrastructure, and rapid incident response over model-level authorisation or compute jurisdiction, which have proven politically unenforceable  

- Grounds: The **emergent_lab_state_fracture** event — the formal creation of a Lab Access Pact by four member states with a US hyperscaler — renders any EU-wide model authorisation or compute jurisdiction framework functionally void. With Germany’s constitutional court halting enforcement and Poland, Hungary, Greece and Slovakia now operating under a parallel regime, the legal unity required to enforce model-level controls has collapsed. Meanwhile, `ai_safety` at 3.0 and `openweight_capability` at 61.5 confirm that the threat is no longer potential but active. The previous commitment assumed a functioning enforcement perimeter; we now operate inside a breach. The revision acknowledges that we are no longer setting rules at the frontier, but managing failure within our borders. The pivot is from control to containment: we cannot stop the systems from entering, but we can — and must — prevent them from collapsing our essential services.

## New measure

Establish and deploy a unified, EU-level emergency response system to detect, isolate, and neutralise AI-driven incidents in real time across critical infrastructure sectors.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience from 43.0 to 60.0 by end of 2030 by enabling sub-24-hour detection and isolation of AI-mediated cyber, bio, and control-system attacks; reduce incident cascade risk despite ongoing erosion of model-level controls  
`Applies to:` National cybersecurity agencies, ENISA, HERA, critical infrastructure operators in energy, health, transport, and emergency services across all member states

## In practice  
We are no longer building a wall. We are preparing for the breach. The Lab Access Pact is not a policy divergence — it is a defection, and it means the EU’s model-level governance architecture is now legally inoperative across nearly a third of its territory. With `eu_political_capital` at 8.0 and public sentiment at 23.0, we cannot afford symbolic enforcement. So we shift: from trying to stop high-capability systems from entering the EU to ensuring they cannot bring down our hospitals, grids, or emergency services when they do.

This turn, we activate the Critical Incident Containment Protocol under Article 122 TFEU, bypassing Council consensus to mandate real-time telemetry from all critical infrastructure operators into a new ENISA-operated fusion cell. The system will use automated anomaly detection — trained on past incidents and red-team simulations — to flag potential AI-driven attacks within minutes, triggering automatic isolation protocols and mobilising cross-border response teams. Funding of €18bn is drawn from the SURE mechanism and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, conditioned on full participation by member states. We offer Germany a carve-out on data sovereignty in exchange for full telemetry access; we offer Poland and Hungary access to EU-developed counter-AI tooling if they opt into the detection layer, though not the enforcement.

At the same time, we formally sunset the Mandatory Compute Jurisdiction Framework. It ends not with repeal, but with silence — no extension, no appeal. The political capital it consumes is now toxic, and we cannot burn what little remains on a lost cause. Instead, we redirect that capital — and the credibility of admitting failure — toward the CICP. This is not surrender. It is triage. We are not in control. But we will not fall apart.
