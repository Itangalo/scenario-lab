## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Enforce physical and legal chokepoints over AI infrastructure and deployment to ensure no high-capability model operates in or affects the EU without explicit authorization, inspection, and enforceable compliance

- Grounds: The failure of the Sovereign Enforcement Framework to achieve full implementation, the abrupt denial of access to frontier models, and the rapid diffusion of open-weight systems at capability 58.5 confirm that sovereignty is not a matter of aspiration but of operational control. The previous commitment assumed that control could be built through coordination and norm-setting; this turn proves it must be enforced through infrastructure, jurisdiction, and binding levers over power, connectivity, and licensing. The new formulation reflects a shift from policy ambition to physical enforcement — not a change in goal, but in the means required to survive.

## New measure
**Mandatory Compute Jurisdiction Framework**  
Require all data centres hosting models above capability 50.0 that serve or affect EU users to be legally incorporated under EU law, with weights, logs, and control interfaces subject to real-time inspection by EU authorities.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by anchoring critical AI infrastructure under enforceable EU jurisdiction; reduce dependency on foreign-controlled compute; create binding legal and physical access for enforcement agencies; slow uncontrolled diffusion of openweight_capability by making hosting contingent on compliance  
`Applies to:` Cloud providers, data centre operators, and AI developers hosting or serving models above capability 50.0 within the EU, including extraterritorial providers with significant EU user base or impact

## Priority
**EU Model Authorization Regime with Physical Enforcement** — because it is the first instrument that turns legal authority into physical reality, and because without the credible threat of power cutoffs and licensing revocation, no other restriction will bind in time.

## In practice
This turn, we are treating the completion of the Sovereign Enforcement Framework not as a success but as a warning: detection without enforcement is surveillance without power. The nodes are in place, but Poland and Hungary still refuse audit access, and Germany’s funding remains frozen. We are not waiting. We are repurposing the Emergency Cyber Shield’s detection layer into an operational enforcement grid, feeding real-time alerts directly to national energy regulators under a new directive drafted under Article 114 TFEU. If a model above threshold 50.0 is detected and unauthorized, the power supply to the hosting cluster can be suspended within hours. We are testing the mechanism this turn with a live drill in the Benelux region, using a decommissioned model as a red team.

The Tech Sovereignty Package finishes this turn, but its legacy is mixed: private capital has flowed, but much of it into facilities where the parent company retains ultimate control and can remotely disable or reconfigure systems. That ends now. The new Mandatory Compute Jurisdiction Framework, launching this turn, will require legal incorporation, data sovereignty, and real-time inspection rights as a condition of operation. We are negotiating with the largest hyperscalers through the Digital Markets Act enforcement channel, making clear that non-compliance will trigger both the Anti-Coercion Instrument and the new Authorization Regime’s enforcement powers.

Public sentiment remains fragile at 31.0, and we are not blind to the risk of backlash. But we are no longer choosing between control and chaos. We are acting: the Gigafactories continue, but now with binding clauses that EU-held weights and training logs are non-negotiable. The US inauguration looms, but we will not base our survival on its outcome. We are building the levers to say no — and to mean it.
