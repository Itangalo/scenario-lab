### Frontier Creeps, Safety Stalls

Global AI capability advances by one point to 63.5, driven by iterative improvements in training efficiency and scaling laws. No structural breakthrough occurs, but closed models inch forward. With no major safety evaluations or transparency measures landing on deployed systems, and capability rising, the gap between power and control widens. Confidence in system predictability erodes further, dragging `ai_safety` down to 6.0.

### Enforcement Drills, Defiance Grows

The EU repurposes the Emergency Cyber Shield into an enforcement pipeline, conducting a live drill in the Benelux region to simulate power cutoffs against unauthorized models. While technically successful, the exercise reveals political fragility: Poland and Hungary formally reject the directive’s applicability to their territory, calling it an overreach of EU authority. Germany delays adoption pending a constitutional review, leaving enforcement patchy at best.

### Sovereignty Undermined

The Tech Sovereignty Package completes, having mobilized private investment in data centres. But many facilities remain under foreign legal control, limiting EU oversight. In response, the EU launches the Mandatory Compute Jurisdiction Framework, demanding legal incorporation and real-time inspection rights for high-capability models. Initial talks with hyperscalers stall, with one major provider warning of operational withdrawal.

Political capital, already strained, plummets to 10.0 as the Model Authorization Regime faces legal challenges and public resistance. The priority yields no acceleration—below 20, it has no effect. Public sentiment drops to 28.0 amid fears of disruption and overreach.

### US Posture Set

The new US administration takes office, adopting a **CONSOLIDATION** posture: frontier access will be rationed by strategic tier, increasing pressure on EU efforts to build independent capacity.
