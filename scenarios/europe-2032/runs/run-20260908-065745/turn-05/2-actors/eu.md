## New measure
**EU Model Authorization Regime with Physical Enforcement**
Establish a mandatory pre-deployment authorization system for all AI models above capability 50.0 operating in or affecting the EU, with enforcement tied directly to compute infrastructure — including power cutoffs and data centre licensing — to ensure compliance is not optional.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` Halt the uncontrolled spread of high-capability models by binding legal authority to physical infrastructure control; reduce openweight_capability growth within EU jurisdiction; strengthen eu_ai_sovereignty by creating a hard, enforceable boundary; begin stabilizing public_sentiment by demonstrating operational control.  
`Applies to:` All model developers, distributors, and cloud providers operating within or serving the EU, with extraterritorial reach for models used by EU entities or residents.

## Priority
**Sovereign Enforcement Framework for Open-Weight Models** — because it is the only active instrument that directly confronts the distributed, unrecallable nature of open models at the enforcement level, and because its success or failure this turn will determine whether the new two-year commitment can even begin on credible ground.

## In practice
This turn, we are finalizing the Sovereign Enforcement Framework’s technical architecture, deploying the first wave of detection nodes into national research networks, cloud exchanges, and municipal IT systems — funded from repurposed cohesion funds and coordinated through ENISA. The nodes will scan for unauthorised model signatures, with real-time reporting to the new Enforcement Unit in DG CONNECT. We are offering Poland and Hungary co-ownership of regional audit hubs and fast-track access to sovereign model exemptions for public services — not because we trust their oversight, but because we cannot afford their obstruction. The Anti-Coercion Instrument remains activated, but its credibility depends on whether we can show enforcement is working, not just declared.

Simultaneously, we are treating the Emergency Cyber Shield’s completion as a pivot point: its detection layer is now being re-tasked as a sensor grid for rogue model activity, turning resilience into early warning. The finished shield will be handed to the EU Cybersecurity Agency with a new mandate to flag unauthorised high-capability deployments as security incidents. Meanwhile, the Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track, though we are now conditioning further disbursements on binding commitments to EU jurisdiction over weights and training runs — no more “sovereign” compute that can be switched off by a foreign parent.

We are launching preparatory work on the EU Model Authorization Regime, drafting it under Article 114 TFEU with enforcement powers tied to energy supply and data centre licensing — the ultimate leverage we still hold. If a provider refuses to comply, the national regulator can cut its power or revoke its operating license. This is not symbolic. We will use it. The US election outcome confirms that access is now a privilege, not a right — and if we do not control what runs here, no one will.
