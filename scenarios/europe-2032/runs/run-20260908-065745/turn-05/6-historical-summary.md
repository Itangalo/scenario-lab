Despite diffusion gains, no structural breakthrough occurs. The EU is abruptly cut off from leading closed models, disrupting critical sectors, while a major member state signs a bilateral AI deal with the US, fracturing collective strategy. The Sovereign Enforcement Framework is finalized, with detection nodes deployed and the Emergency Cyber Shield transferred to the EU Cybersecurity Agency for rogue model monitoring. However, Poland and Hungary block full implementation, Germany delays funding pending legal review, and enforcement remains weak. The EU introduces the costly and controversial Model Authorization Regime, proposing physical enforcement measures, but faces mounting legal, industrial, and public opposition, driving public sentiment down to 31.0. Political capital falls further to 23.0 amid institutional fragmentation, eroding cohesion. US AI policy remains in limbo until the new administration’s January 2029 inauguration, leaving global coordination uncertain.
