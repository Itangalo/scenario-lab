### Frontier Stalls, Then Surges

Evidence mounts that the AI frontier is plateauing—top labs underdeliver, and high-end model prices drop. Markets react, and investors pause. In this environment, a leading open-weight model is released, achieving broad adoption and prompting rapid fine-tuning across academic and industrial clusters. While the model demonstrates strong performance and widespread deployment, its core capabilities reflect incremental optimization of existing architectures rather than a breakthrough. `openweight_capability` rises to 58.5, a moderate gain of 2.5 points, maintaining a clear but narrowing gap behind `ai_capability` at 62.5. The increase reflects diffusion and efficiency gains within the bounds of accumulated progress, not a structural leap.

### Access Cut, Unity Fractured

Midway through the turn, the EU is abruptly cut off from the leading closed model. Hospitals, research institutes, and public agencies relying on it face immediate disruption. No explanation is given. Simultaneously, a major member state signs a bilateral AI access deal with Washington, undermining the Union’s unified stance. The move is framed domestically as pragmatic, but internationally as defection.

### Enforcement Push Meets Resistance

The EU finalizes the Sovereign Enforcement Framework, deploying detection nodes across key networks. However, Poland and Hungary block full integration, rejecting audit authority and refusing hosting rights for enforcement hubs. Germany delays final funding, demanding a constitutional review. The Anti-Coercion Instrument lacks teeth without unity.

The Emergency Cyber Shield completes and is handed to the EU Cybersecurity Agency, now repurposed to detect rogue models. Early warnings spike, but response remains fragmented.

### Political Capital Crumbles

The EU advances the EU Model Authorization Regime, a high-cost regulatory proposal involving physical enforcement mechanisms such as power cutoffs and licensing. The plan is formally introduced and begins its legislative journey, but no enforcement actions occur this turn. The complexity of implementation and legal uncertainties trigger immediate alarm in industry and among member states. Legal scholars question its proportionality, and protests emerge over fears of job losses and overreach, contributing to a dip in public sentiment to 31.0.

The US presidential election concludes in Turn 5, determining the outcome, but the new administration has not yet taken office. As of now, US policy remains in transition. Markets and institutions await the formal inauguration in January 2029, when the posture will be officially established and its implications for AI access and cooperation become clear. Until then, speculation intensifies, but no new operational policy is enacted.
