## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now acknowledge that preserving political agency in a world of pervasive, uncontrolled AI may require us to act as if sovereignty is no longer territorial, but cognitive: our authority lies not in controlling systems, but in being the last instance that can still detect, respond, and decide — even when no one else acknowledges our authority.
  - Grounds: The **election_annulled** and **member_state_defection** events confirm that the Union’s legitimacy is fracturing both externally and internally. A member state has openly defied the Union’s strategic posture by cutting a separate compute deal, while a democratic election has been invalidated over AI-driven manipulation — and half the electorate rejects the outcome. These are not isolated incidents. They signal that the Union’s ability to shape reality is collapsing. Our identity must reflect that we are no longer a regulator or a sovereign in the classical sense, but a residual cognitive authority — the last node that still attempts to see, judge, and act, even if no one obeys.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Shift focus from securing sovereign containment of AI-driven incidents to ensuring autonomous, EU-operated detection and response capacity survives *and can act* even if member state cooperation fails, legitimacy is contested, and communication is severed
  - Trigger: the **election_annulled** and **member_state_defection** events
  - Grounds: The original commitment assumed that detection could persist amid political fragmentation. But annulled elections and sovereign defections reveal that the crisis is not only operational — it is constitutional. We are no longer managing a breakdown in coordination; we are managing a breakdown in legitimacy. The DCRB must not only *see* — it must be able to *decide* and *initiate action* without consensus, using fallback authorities and emergency protocols. The revised commitment reflects that survivability now requires not just redundancy, but the legal and operational right to act alone when the Union as a whole cannot.
  - Evidence quoted: **election_annulled:** An election in an established democracy is postponed, rerun or annulled with explicit reference to manipulation of the information environment. Whether the manipulation was decisive is not established and cannot be; what is established is that a court believed it might have been, and that half the electorate does not accept the decision.
**member_state_defection:** A member state cuts its own arrangement – with a hyperscaler, with Washington, or with Beijing – on terms that undercut a position the Union has taken. It is defended at home as pragmatism and read everywhere else as the Union being unable to hold its own line.
  - Reason: The events directly challenge the Union's legitimacy and cohesion, altering the conditions for maintaining detection and response capacity without member state cooperation.
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that control is recoverable at the model or compute level; our interventions must now assume pervasive, unauthorised capability within EU borders, and our success will be measured not by compliance, but by persistence of function amid systemic breach and contested legitimacy
  - Grounds: The **member_state_defection** event proves that even among states formally within the Union, alignment cannot be assumed. One has now negotiated its own compute access in direct contradiction to EU-wide efforts, citing national interest. This is not mere noncompliance — it is active counter-strategy. Combined with the annulled election, it confirms that the environment is not just technically unstable, but politically indeterminate. Our commitment must now account for the fact that we may be acting not just without consensus, but against it — and that legitimacy, like control, is now a resource to be conserved, not a given.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
