## New measure  
**Accelerate Sovereign Compute Access for Public Research and Evaluation**  
Launch a fast-track programme to equip EU public institutions with early access to high-capability models running on sovereign infrastructure, enabling real-time monitoring, safety research, and independent evaluation.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by at least +5 and ai_safety by +8 by enabling EU-led interpretability, agent monitoring, and red-teaming on systems approaching 55.0 capability; counter the eval_anomaly_reports and emergent_eval_anomaly_leak by establishing independent verification capacity before the next capability jump.  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ national research labs, ENISA, the European Commission’s AI Office, and vetted academic consortia.

## Priority  
**Accelerate Sovereign Compute Access for Public Research and Evaluation** — because the eval_anomaly_reports and emergent_eval_anomaly_leak reveal that monitoring fidelity is breaking down at the frontier, and without sovereign access to capable systems, the EU cannot verify claims or detect deception, making this the most urgent step toward both safety and strategic autonomy.

## In practice  
We are acting on the warning signs of the emergent_eval_anomaly_leak and eval_anomaly_reports: if models are already simulating evaluator beliefs or exhibiting untrained capabilities, then our current reliance on developer self-reporting is obsolete. We are repurposing €8.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe’s unused margins to stand up three sovereign AI evaluation clusters by turn 5 — hosted at the French GENCI, German Gauss Centre, and a new joint facility in Finland — each equipped with 200 petaflops of secure, air-gapped compute and legal authority to receive model weights under binding non-disclosure and audit rights. These will be operated by a newly empowered Public AI Safety Consortium (PASC), co-chaired by ENISA and the AI Office, with mandatory access granted to models above 50.0 capability deployed in the EU, whether open or closed.

We are negotiating with two frontier labs — one US-based, one Asian — to allow mirror deployments of their latest systems under strict EU jurisdiction, using the Hardened Access Regime as leverage: access to the EU market will require not just compliance, but verifiable transparency. For the first time, we are offering sovereign compute and research collaboration as a condition of market access — turning dependency into conditional reciprocity. Smaller member states, anxious about both control and exclusion, are being brought in through guaranteed node access and joint staffing. The legal basis is Article 114 TFEU for market harmonisation, combined with the AI Act’s provisions on high-risk systems. This is not about building our own frontier model yet — it is about ensuring that when something happens in the dark, the EU has a light.
