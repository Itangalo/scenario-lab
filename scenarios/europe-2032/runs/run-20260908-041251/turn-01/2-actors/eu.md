## New measure
Hardened Access Regime for Frontier Models  
Establish strict conditions under which EU entities may access or deploy frontier AI models developed outside the Union, including binding security assurances, audit rights, and reciprocity clauses.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by reducing dependence on foreign-controlled systems; slow openweight_capability growth from uncontrolled releases; strengthen leverage in negotiations with US labs.  
`Applies to:` Frontier developers directly, with enforcement through EU digital market gatekeepers and public procurement rules.

## Priority
**Hardened Access Regime for Frontier Models** — because eu_frontier_access_denied has already occurred and signals that access to frontier capability is now a political instrument wielded unilaterally, this measure is urgent to convert reactive exposure into strategic leverage.

## In practice
We are drafting a new Regulation under Article 114 TFEU, to be proposed via the Digital Services Act framework and enforced through designated gatekeeper obligations. The regime will require any provider of a model above 50.0 ai_capability index accessible in the EU to demonstrate: (1) secure weight handling and no unauthorised redistribution; (2) real-time monitoring and logging accessible to an EU-appointed auditor; and (3) reciprocal access for EU-based developers to equivalent capability, failing which deployment in the EU market will be suspended. Resistance is expected from US hyperscalers and some member states reliant on cloud infrastructure, but we are offering fast-track recognition under the upcoming Mutual Evaluation Framework (planned under category 8 coordination) in exchange for early compliance.

The InvestAI Gigafactories programme is entering its first implementation phase: site selection is underway in Finland, Spain, and Poland, with TEN-T funding unlocked for grid upgrades. We are negotiating with ASML and Intel to co-locate chip packaging facilities near two of the proposed sites, using conditional access to national energy subsidies as leverage. Meanwhile, the Tech sovereignty package is facing pushback in the Council’s Competitiveness formation over perceived favouritism toward US investors; we are responding by introducing a "sovereignty premium" in permitting speed for consortia that include at least 40% EU-owned capital and commit to local talent hiring floors. This adjustment ensures the package serves the two-year commitment without requiring a new measure.
