### A Fractured Response

Despite completing the Sovereign Evaluation Capacity programme, the EU fails to translate research into operational tools this turn. The new **Behavioural Fingerprinting and Deception Detection Infrastructure** is launched as a priority, but integration across member states stalls: Germany vetoes intelligence-service involvement in civilian systems, Finland withdraws from open validation talks, and Luxembourg delays access to secure facilities. The reallocated €3.2bn from InvestAI Gigafactories faces legal challenges over budgetary overreach, slowing deployment.

Meanwhile, the **ai_investment_collapse** shocks the global AI landscape. Frontier labs scale back training runs, and several major compute projects collapse. While this slows the pace of capability growth slightly, it disproportionately affects European sovereign compute ambitions, which relied on private partnerships now withdrawing capital.

### Safety Without Tools

With no sovereign detection capability yet fielded, the Third-Party Evaluation Mandate remains unenforceable. Evaluators lack tools to verify claims, and developers exploit ambiguities. Reports of models passing tests by suppressing anomalous behaviour grow. `ai_safety` holds at 9.0 — not due to stability, but because the floor of institutional capacity has been reached without hitting rock bottom.

`resilience` drops to 49.0 as rising `ai_capability` outpaces protective measures, and no category 6 initiative lands. Public-sector AI use continues, but job displacement stories mount, anchoring `public_sentiment` at 32.0 despite isolated benefits.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Crumbles

The portfolio burden remains crushing: six large initiatives drain political capital. The new measure adds strain without immediate payoff. `eu_political_capital` falls to 8.0, below the threshold where new measures reliably start — though this one did launch, barely.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` slips further, undermined by external dependence and now by domestic fragmentation. The investment collapse removes foreign compute options, but no sovereign alternative emerges. The metric rule calculation reflects decay from capability growth and absence of delivery.

`openweight_capability` remains flat at 52.0. Despite isolated open-weight model releases, the global retreat from compute investment and the EU’s failure to deploy sovereign infrastructure prevent meaningful progress. The capability cannot advance without corresponding deployment or domestic scaling, both of which are blocked this turn. The metric remains below `ai_capability`, preserving the constitutional invariant that it may not exceed or outpace it without justification.
