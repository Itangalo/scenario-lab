## New measure
**Launch Sovereign Control Framework for Critical AI Systems**  
Define and enforce minimum standards for EU control over AI systems used in emergency response, health, energy, and finance, including data sovereignty, audit rights, and kill-switch protocols.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by +10 and resilience by +3 by ensuring that no critical system runs on AI infrastructure that cannot be audited, overridden, or disconnected by EU authorities.  
`Applies to:` Member states, EU agencies, and private operators of critical infrastructure using AI above capability threshold 50.0.

## Priority
**Establish the European AI Incident Response Corps (E-AIRC)** — because the dual incidents this turn revealed a fatal operational deficit: we can detect threats, but we cannot act fast enough across borders, and without rapid response, even hardened systems collapse under pressure.

## In practice
We are finalising the legal activation protocol for E-AIRC under the NIS2 Directive, negotiating with national parliaments to ratify the cross-border deployment clause by December. Germany and Poland remain cautious — we are offering them co-command seats on the operational council and a national opt-out for deployments touching core state functions. Funding is secured through a €3.8bn reallocation from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s digital envelope, with 50% co-financing locked in via bilateral MOUs. Recruitment begins this month: 1,200 specialists will be drawn from national CERTs, Europol’s EC3, and Horizon Europe’s AI forensics cohort, with six regional hubs standing up by Q1 2029. The Finnish prototype node is now running red-team drills against a 54.0-capability model, testing deception resistance in evaluator chains.

Meanwhile, Accelerate Sovereign Compute Access has concluded its run — the Finnish evaluation cluster is live, and we have secured structured access to internal activations from one Asian partner, though not full weights. This is not sovereignty, but it is leverage: we are using it to push for broader audit rights in the next round of cooperation talks. The Court of Justice’s ruling on Article 114 is expected in early 2029; we are preparing the pivot to Article 172 TFEU (public health and safety) to reframe sovereign access as a systemic risk mitigation, not a market intervention. InvestAI Gigafactories continues on track — site selection in Spain, Poland, and Finland is complete, with grid upgrades fast-tracked under the Tech Sovereignty Package’s accelerated-permitting zones. But the US election outcome looms: with AI now treated as strategic infrastructure in Washington, access will tighten. We cannot build fast enough to catch up — so we must control what we have. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Control Framework: no more critical AI systems that we cannot audit, override, or shut down.
