The EU’s push for AI sovereignty faltered amid deepening institutional fragmentation and political exhaustion. The Emergency Cyber-AI Response Corps (ECARC) was formally proposed with pre-authorised crisis powers but stalled due to incomplete legal design and resistance from Germany and France, resulting in a divided system: civilian oversight under the weakened EU AI Trust Authority and a limited ECARC for interventions. The Secure Execution Environment saw partial deployment in Finland and Bulgaria, but lacked legal harmonisation across member states, undermining cohesion.

Sovereign Behavioural Fingerprinting was completed but failed to impact AI safety due to minimal rollout and no enforcement. Cascading failures in healthcare and local government IT following the cyberattack exposed systemic unpreparedness, eroding resilience. AI capability rose marginally through closed-lab advances and lingering open-weight models, while safety remained critically low amid opaque model reasoning and absent coordination.

Public messaging emphasizing "No sovereignty, no safety" failed to stem declining sentiment, as job losses and dependency concerns dominated. With eight major initiatives underway and eu_political_capital strained at 5.0, credibility eroded. Emergency procurement under STEP progressed but faced legal challenges, while sovereignty stagnated and reliance on external AI capacity deepened. The window for effective action narrowed, with no clear breakthrough in sight.
