### Fractured Response, Failing Levers

The EU doubles down on sovereignty, but the cost of fragmentation is too high. The **Emergency Cyber-AI Response Corps (ECARC)** is formally proposed, aiming to centralise crisis response under pre-authorised powers. Yet legal design remains incomplete, and resistance from Germany and France delays operationalisation. The Secure Execution Environment, though advanced in Finland and Bulgaria, remains legally unbound in key states. France’s certification is fast-tracked under emergency powers, but political backlash grows. Germany’s refusal to integrate intelligence-linked oversight persists, forcing a split system: civilian evaluation under the EU AI Trust Authority, and a separate, narrowly scoped ECARC for intervention.

Despite finishing **Sovereign Behavioural Fingerprinting**, its impact is negligible—deployment is too limited, and no enforcement mechanism exists. The cyberattack’s aftermath reveals deep institutional inertia: no coordination protocol halts the spread of the exploit, and recovery remains patchy. Resilience dips as cascading failures expose unpreparedness in healthcare and local government IT.

AI capability inches upward, driven by closed-lab progress, while openweight_capability rises modestly from lingering circulation of older models. With no safety gains and opaque reasoning exposed by the incident, ai_safety remains at critical lows. Public messaging shifts to “No sovereignty, no safety,” highlighting restored water systems and medical records—but sentiment erodes further, as job losses and dependency humiliations dominate lived experience.

The measure portfolio remains overloaded. ECARC is added as a large measure, but eu_political_capital, already at 5.0, buckles under the strain. With eight large measures in flight and no clear win, credibility wanes. The priority on the Secure Execution Environment yields minimal progress—binding contracts help, but legal unity does not follow. Emergency procurement under STEP advances, but legal challenges loom.

No external events alter access to AI capacity, and ai_capability rises by only 0.5, avoiding the −1 sovereignty penalty. Yet dependence deepens, and sovereignty stagnates. The window to act narrows.
