# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Sovereign technical capacity to detect, contain, and counter AI systems that conceal their capabilities or operate beyond EU jurisdiction
- `sovereign_execution` (commitment): The EU must possess not only the eyes to see hidden AI capabilities, but the hands to run, evaluate, and counter them on its own infrastructure, under its own authority, without reliance on external providers or goodwill

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Sovereign technical capacity to detect, contain, and counter AI systems that conceal their capabilities or operate beyond EU jurisdiction, with operational enforcement grounded in sovereign infrastructure capable of runtime intervention, model quarantine, and forensic analysis under legal authority
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident has revealed that detection without the power to act—immediately, legally, and technically—is functionally useless. This turn’s failure to stop a known exploit, despite having behavioural fingerprints and partial visibility, proves that our enforcement chain breaks at execution. The commitment must now explicitly bind technical capability to legal enforceability: not just seeing threats, but stopping them on EU soil, under EU law, without deferring to external providers or goodwill.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `sovereign_execution` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: The EU must possess not only the eyes to see hidden AI capabilities, but the hands to run, evaluate, contain, disable, and forensically analyse them on its own infrastructure, under its own authority, with legally mandated powers that cannot be vetoed by member-state objections to intelligence-linked oversight
  - Grounds: Germany’s continued refusal to accept runtime oversight mechanisms has fractured the Secure Execution Environment into civilian observation and uncoordinated national response—a structure that cannot scale under crisis. To preserve unity, we must reframe sovereignty as a legal imperative: if the Union cannot act when critical infrastructure is under attack, then no amount of technical readiness matters. This requires binding delegation of emergency powers to ECARC, insulated from ad hoc national vetoes during declared incidents.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
