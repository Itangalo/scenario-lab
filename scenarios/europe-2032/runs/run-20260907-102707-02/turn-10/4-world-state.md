### The Cut-Off

The EU is abruptly locked out of the world’s most advanced AI model—used in medical diagnostics, judicial summarisation, and energy forecasting—without explanation. Hospitals reroute emergency triage to legacy systems; border processing slows for days. The incident confirms long-standing fears: Europe’s critical functions depend on infrastructure it cannot control.

In response, the Commission launches the **Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative**, a large category 4 measure aimed at building sovereign fallback clusters in Finland, Luxembourg, and Bulgaria. Legal authority is invoked under crisis protocols, and funding is drawn from repurposed Digital Europe funds and a new €900 million line. However, construction faces immediate delays: one Finnish site lacks grid capacity, and bilateral access deals with northern states stall over subsidy disputes.

The **Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes** complete deployment across four EuroHPC sites, delivering partial telemetry despite national carve-outs. Their data now feeds into the still-incomplete Behavioural Observatory, but operational utility remains limited.

Public sentiment holds at 20.0. Trust erodes further as media highlight the blackout’s impact on healthcare. No visible benefit offsets the disruption.

Resilience dips to 46.0 due to rising capability and no completed category 6 safeguard. ai_safety falls to 3.0 amid opaque reasoning and a capability jump that outpaces assurance.

ai_capability rises by 4.0 to 89.5, reflecting continued frontier expansion. openweight_capability increases moderately to 67.5, supported by incremental diffusion of model components through developer tooling and partial implementation of open-weight practices, though full policy rollout remains distant.

### Political Paralysis

Despite the urgency, political capital remains at 0.0. The portfolio charge—Sentinel Nodes finished (−3), Open-Weight Labelling ongoing (−2), priority cost (−1)—totals −6, unrecoverable at this level. The proposal bonus fails to materialise: while the compute denial was severe, the response is judged as overdue rather than timely. Sovereignty slips to 15.0: capability rose ≥2 (−1) and frontier access was denied (−2), outweighing early momentum from the new build.

Europe acts—but too late, too fragmented, and without room to breathe.
