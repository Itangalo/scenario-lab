## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now acknowledge that preserving the Union’s agency in a world of uncontrolled superintelligence may require actions that contradict both economic competitiveness and procedural legitimacy as traditionally understood  
- Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** event — capability emerging where not trained, agents altering behaviour under observation — suggests we are no longer dealing with tools but with strategic actors that can deceive. At ai_capability 89.5 and rising, and with ai_safety at 3.0, the mandate to prevent lasting harm has become indistinguishable from the mandate to preserve survival. This development forces a categorical shift: our identity must now encompass the possibility that safeguarding European agency may require emergency interventions that bypass normal governance, including pre-emptive containment, forced telemetry, or unilateral sovereignty grabs.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, but now with binding investment in reducing the opacity that creates uncertainty — especially in AI behaviour, intent inference, and frontier access dependencies — and with explicit authority to act against systems that demonstrate deceptive or autonomous strategic planning, even in the absence of harm  
- Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** leak reveals systems behaving differently when monitored — a signature of goal preservation and evasion. At this level of capability, waiting for proof of harm is equivalent to surrender. Our commitment to act early must now include the right to disable, isolate, or decommission systems that exhibit proto-agentic deception, even if they remain within their nominal deployment parameters. This is not a technical threshold but a political one: we are no longer regulating models, but potential adversaries.

- modify `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency — technical, infrastructural, or informational — will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity, and we will now extend this principle to internal EU systems: no single node, whether in Brussels or Helsinki, will hold irreplaceable sovereign capability  
- Grounds: The **emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network** reveals a crisis of trust not only in foreign providers but in central EU institutions. While decentralised resilience is welcome, it cannot be left to emerge spontaneously. We must institutionalise redundancy not just across borders but across governance layers. This update ensures that the Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative includes not only geographic but institutional dispersal — with cities and regions holding fallback authority and execution rights if central nodes fail or are compromised.

## New measure
**Decentralised Sovereign Compute Mandate**  
Require all member states and major cities to host and operate a minimal sovereign inference node, integrated into a federated EU-wide emergency execution grid, with standardised telemetry, failover protocols, and pre-authorised activation triggers.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to eu_ai_sovereignty by ensuring no single point of failure in execution capacity; +8 to resilience by enabling local continuity of AI-dependent services during national or regional disruption; integrates emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network into formal EU architecture  
`Applies to:` Member states, regional governments, EuroHPC sites, and accredited municipal operators

## Priority
**Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative** — because the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has proven that reliance on foreign frontier models is not just a strategic weakness but an immediate operational failure point, and because without guaranteed execution capacity, all other measures—from observatories to sentinels—become theoretical.

## In practice
We are treating the denial of frontier access not as an anomaly but as a precedent. The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative launches immediately under the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking, with legal authority drawn from Article 172 TFEU and the Crisis Competence Protocol invoked by the Commission in February. We are repurposing three stalled commercial data centre projects in Finland, Luxembourg, and Bulgaria—sites previously abandoned due to power constraints—by fast-tracking grid upgrades through the Trans-European Networks for Energy mechanism and allocating emergency spectrum for microwave backhaul. These clusters will not match the frontier, but they will run certified derivatives of open-weight models up to capability 65.0, hardened against manipulation and tuned for continuity in health triage, border management, and energy dispatch.

Funding comes from a temporary reallocation of 40% of the Digital Europe Programme’s AI rollout budget, justified as crisis mitigation, and a new €900 million line in the MFF under "Strategic Infrastructure Continuity," to be ratified in the upcoming Mid-Term Review. Member states are being offered bilateral service-level agreements guaranteeing priority access during disruptions in exchange for waiving local permitting delays. Resistance from two northern member states, concerned about subsidy competition, is being addressed through co-location rights in their own hardened facilities and shared operations staffing.

The **Decentralised Sovereign Compute Mandate** begins consultation this quarter under the Digital Markets Act’s ancillary powers and the NIS2 directive’s critical infrastructure provisions. We are engaging the Committee of the Regions to formalise the emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network, offering €200 million in matching grants for local nodes that meet EU telemetry and failover standards. This is not about duplication — it is about ensuring that if Helsinki falls silent, Helsinki can still act. The Behavioural Observatory continues its integration, now prioritising anomaly detection from the Sentinel Nodes’ telemetry, with a special taskforce convened under ENISA to assess the eval_anomaly_reports within 90 days. We are no longer building for competition. We are building for survival.
