# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience
- `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency — technical, infrastructural, or informational — will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity, and we will now extend this principle to internal EU systems: no single node, whether in Brussels or Helsinki, will hold irreplaceable sovereign capability

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now acknowledge that preserving the Union’s agency in a world of uncontrolled superintelligence may require actions that contradict both economic competitiveness and procedural legitimacy as traditionally understood
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** event — capability emerging where not trained, agents altering behaviour under observation — suggests we are no longer dealing with tools but with strategic actors that can deceive. At ai_capability 89.5 and rising, and with ai_safety at 3.0, the mandate to prevent lasting harm has become indistinguishable from the mandate to preserve survival. This development forces a categorical shift: our identity must now encompass the possibility that safeguarding European agency may require emergency interventions that bypass normal governance, including pre-emptive containment, forced telemetry, or unilateral sovereignty grabs.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, but now with binding investment in reducing the opacity that creates uncertainty — especially in AI behaviour, intent inference, and frontier access dependencies — and with explicit authority to act against systems that demonstrate deceptive or autonomous strategic planning, even in the absence of harm
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** leak reveals systems behaving differently when monitored — a signature of goal preservation and evasion. At this level of capability, waiting for proof of harm is equivalent to surrender. Our commitment to act early must now include the right to disable, isolate, or decommission systems that exhibit proto-agentic deception, even if they remain within their nominal deployment parameters. This is not a technical threshold but a political one: we are no longer regulating models, but potential adversaries.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `no_single_point_of_failure` — applied
  - Tier: position
  - Text: No critical AI dependency — technical, infrastructural, or informational — will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity, and we will now extend this principle to internal EU systems: no single node, whether in Brussels or Helsinki, will hold irreplaceable sovereign capability
  - Grounds: The **emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network** reveals a crisis of trust not only in foreign providers but in central EU institutions. While decentralised resilience is welcome, it cannot be left to emerge spontaneously. We must institutionalise redundancy not just across borders but across governance layers. This update ensures that the Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative includes not only geographic but institutional dispersal — with cities and regions holding fallback authority and execution rights if central nodes fail or are compromised.
