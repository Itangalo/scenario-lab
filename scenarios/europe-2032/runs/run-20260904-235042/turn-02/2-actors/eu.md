## Priority  
**Priority:** Hardened Critical Infrastructure Access Protocols  
This turn, the cyber intrusion revealed that our most urgent vulnerability is not in what we build, but in what we already run — and with a capability jump now confirmed, we cannot assume future attacks will be detectable by design; we must assume they will be persistent, adaptive, and aimed at cascading failure.

## In practice  
We are activating the Critical Infrastructure Access Protocols through binding amendments to the NIS2 Directive, to be adopted under Article 114 TFEU with a fast-track procedure in the Council and Parliament. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will issue technical standards within four months, mandating three layers of protection: AI-driven anomaly detection trained on adversarial patterns from recent intrusions, human-operated override switches with monthly live verification drills, and air-gapped control systems for emergency fallback. Compliance will be tied to eligibility for Cohesion Fund disbursements and post-incident recovery support, giving member states both incentive and urgency. We are deploying a €15bn Rapid Resilience Facility from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused margins to co-finance upgrades, prioritising grid operators, water treatment plants, and rail signalling systems.

At the same time, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by resolving the land access impasse in Brittany and Norrland through binding regional benefit agreements: 30% of construction jobs reserved for locals, 10% of operational energy allocated to green hydrogen projects for regional industry, and a €2bn Innovation Co-Development Fund to link gigafactory AI capacity to regional universities and SMEs. The European Investment Bank is now structuring non-recourse loans backed by future data centre revenue streams to accelerate private participation. On the Tech Sovereignty Package, we have secured provisional agreement in the Digital Markets Council to harmonise permitting timelines to 18 months by default, with a fast lane of 12 months for projects that meet energy-recycling and talent-localisation benchmarks. The Commission is finalising the Blue Card+ proposal to fast-track 50,000 AI and semiconductor specialists by 2028, with relocation bonuses and housing support funded jointly by member states and private partners.

The EAAD continues its setup under ENISA’s interim governance, with its first 200 hires focused on agent behaviour monitoring and interpretability tooling. We have issued draft implementing acts under the AI Act to require weight access for any model above 50.0 capability index deployed in the EU, and we are preparing for legal challenges from non-EU hyperscalers by building a coalition of national competition and data protection authorities to co-enforce. The 0.5% levy on non-EU hyperscaler revenue is now tied to a broader Digital Resilience Contribution mechanism, which we will propose as part of the next MFF revision to ensure long-term funding. Quietly, we are also seconding intelligence analysts from national agencies to model adversarial use cases — including supply chain sabotage and election interference — to stress-test the EAAD’s evaluation framework before its first live deployment review.
