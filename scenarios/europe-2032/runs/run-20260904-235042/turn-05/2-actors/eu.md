## In practice
We are treating the US election outcome as tactical relief, not strategic resolution. The new administration’s offer of structured access is welcome, but it reinforces our core vulnerability: sovereignty cannot be outsourced, even to allies. We are accepting the terms for now — aligning on export controls and joint evaluations — but we are not slowing the Foundational Compute Access Initiative. Instead, we are using the breathing room to deepen domestic capacity. The Norrland and Brittany nodes are now live: Norrland runs AI-assisted diagnostics for the Nordic-Baltic cancer screening network, processing cross-border data under GDPR-compliant federated learning; Brittany manages real-time load-balancing for the Iberian and French grids during peak stress, with EAAD red teams probing for emergent behaviour weekly. These are not pilots — they are permanent services, with fallbacks but no fallback plan.

The redirection of €4.5bn from the stalled resilience fund to secure interconnects is now operational, with low-latency, quantum-resistant links being laid between sovereign nodes under classified EuroDefense telecom contracts. Legal challenges to the use of Article 114 TFEU and Schengen-associated provisions persist, but we are bypassing resistance by offering member states early access to sovereign AI services in exchange for dropping objections — a trade of capability for consent. Meanwhile, the EAAD completes its mandate this turn: it will now shift from establishment to operation, with legal authority to audit and delay. We are embedding its red teams into the new Sovereign AI Resilience Corps, which begins recruitment next month through Erasmus-style partnerships with technical universities and national cyber agencies. This is not about catching up — it is about building a self-sustaining stack, from silicon to service, that no external power can disable.
