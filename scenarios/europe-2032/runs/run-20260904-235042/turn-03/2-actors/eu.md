## New measure  
**Measure:** Foundational Compute Access Initiative  
Establish a sovereign EU cloud layer with guaranteed access for public agencies, critical industries, and vetted researchers, built on domestically controlled infrastructure and legally insulated from extraterritorial interference.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–20 points; reduce dependence on foreign-controlled compute by creating a resilient, high-capacity EU-operated cloud stack with enforceable access rights  
Applies to: EU institutions, member state governments, critical infrastructure operators, and approved research consortia

## Priority  
**Priority:** Foundational Compute Access Initiative  
This turn, the ASML servicing crisis and the sudden leap in AI capability have made clear that our strategic vulnerability is not only in safety or resilience, but in access: we cannot secure what we do not control, and we cannot act with agility if our compute can be throttled by decisions made elsewhere — so we are committing now to a sovereign cloud that anchors both our industrial future and our risk posture.

## In practice  
We are launching the Foundational Compute Access Initiative under Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Europe Programme’s legal framework, with the European Commission tasking a joint unit from DG CONNECT, DG DEFIS, and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) to stand up the core architecture by turn 5. The initiative will repurpose 40% of the planned InvestAI Gigafactory capacity — specifically the Norrland and Brittany nodes — into a sovereign cloud layer with tripartite access: one partition for EU and national government services, one for critical infrastructure operators (energy, telecoms, finance), and one for vetted public research teams working on safety, alignment, and resilience. Legal insulation will be achieved through a new status of “EU Strategic Compute Asset,” which binds infrastructure operators to EU jurisdiction, prohibits foreign government access to systems or data, and mandates physical and cryptographic separation from commercial hyperscaler stacks.

To overcome national resistance and accelerate deployment, we are offering member states co-ownership stakes in the sovereign cloud nodes hosted on their soil, with 15% of compute capacity reserved for national public services and an additional 10% allocated to regional innovation clusters. We are also negotiating with ASML, IMEC, and STMicroelectronics to establish a parallel track for maintaining and upgrading legacy EU-based compute infrastructure using domestically secured spare parts and service protocols — a stopgap until next-generation EU fabs come online. The €15bn Rapid Resilience Facility will be partially redirected to fund secure interconnects between the sovereign cloud nodes, ensuring redundancy and low-latency coordination across borders. Meanwhile, the EAAD’s staffing push continues, with its first red-teaming unit now operational and conducting live evaluations of agent behaviour in simulated supply chain sabotage scenarios. We are also fast-tracking the Blue Card+ proposal through trilogue negotiations, linking approval to the broader Tech Sovereignty Package to secure buy-in from member states concerned about brain drain and social strain.
