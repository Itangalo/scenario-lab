## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now see that without foundational capacity, neither mandate can be fulfilled.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor sovereignty and risk resilience, and begin integrating it into public critical functions by turn 6 to prove its operational value.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but this turn, the eval anomaly reports and capability plateau evidence force us to act on divergent signals, not just uncertainty.

`- Grounds:` The frontier has split: one path suggests runaway capability (eval anomalies), the other stagnation (price drop, underdelivery). We cannot assume the pace — but we must assume the risk. Our identity must now reflect that sovereignty is not a means to both mandates — it is their precondition. The two-year commitment evolves because we are no longer just building capacity; we must now *use* it to gain credibility and counter the backlash. The act_under_uncertainty commitment is deepened, not reversed: we are acting on conflicting data, not abandoning resolve.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Operationalise Sovereign Compute in Critical Public Services  
Integrate the first nodes of the Foundational Compute Access Initiative into live operations for healthcare diagnostics, energy grid management, and emergency response coordination by June 2028, demonstrating functional independence from foreign-controlled systems.

Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)  
Size: small  
Finishes on turn: 6  
Targeted effect: Increase public_sentiment by 8–12 points; increase resilience by 5–7 points by proving sovereign AI can deliver essential services under stress; signal that EU capacity is not just strategic but functional  
Applies to: EU institutions, member state public service operators, critical infrastructure providers

## Priority  
**Priority:** Foundational Compute Access Initiative  
This turn, the denial of frontier access — sudden, total, and unexplained — confirmed that our dependence is not a vulnerability to be managed but a failure mode already active; we are accelerating integration of sovereign compute not to catch up, but to prove we can act when the outside world does not answer.

## In practice  
We are treating the denial of access not as a crisis but as a catalyst. The Foundational Compute Access Initiative is now under emergency coordination by the Commission’s Crisis Response Cell, with DG DEFIS and EuroHPC JU fast-tracking deployment at the Norrland and Brittany nodes. We are re-routing 30% of the stalled Rapid Resilience Facility — €4.5bn — to secure interconnects and air-gapped gateways between sovereign cloud nodes, prioritising low-latency links for energy and emergency services. Legal teams are finalising the “EU Strategic Compute Asset” framework under Article 114 TFEU, with a carve-out for national security invoked via the Schengen-associated provisions in the Lisbon Treaty, bypassing the most entrenched treaty objections.

At the same time, we are launching the first operational use of sovereign AI under Category 9: by turn 6, the Norrland node will run AI-assisted diagnostics for cross-border cancer screening networks, while Brittany supports real-time load-balancing for the Iberian and French grids during peak summer demand. These are not pilots — they are live services, with fallback protocols but no intention to fall back. We are offering member states immediate access to these services in exchange for dropping regional objections to Gigafactory repurposing, turning resistance into uptake. The EAAD’s red-team unit is now embedded in both deployments, testing for emergent behaviour under stress, while the cyber defence breakthrough is being reverse-engineered into the sovereign stack under a classified annex to the Digital Europe Programme. We are not waiting for consensus — we are creating facts on the ground.
