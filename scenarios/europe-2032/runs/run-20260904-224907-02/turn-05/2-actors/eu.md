## In practice
We are closing the gap between law and reality. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive is now on the books, but we are not activating it — not yet. This turn, we are enforcing a hard linkage: no compute allocation under its authority will proceed until the Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework completes its first audit cycle and demonstrates real-time monitoring of agent trajectories. We are amending the Directive via delegated act under Article 68a of the AI Act to codify this dependency, and ENISA is finalising the technical standards for model interpretability that will underpin the audit. The message to Germany and other legal sceptics is clear: we are not bypassing oversight — we are making it the gatekeeper.

At the same time, we are completing the Critical Infrastructure AI Shield with the November red-team exercise, which will stress-test healthcare defences against AI-driven intrusion. The results will feed into the next phase of resilience building, but for now, we are treating the current 42.0 as a baseline, not a finish line. The `ai_investment_collapse` has frozen several foreign compute projects the Tech Sovereignty Package relied on, so we are renegotiating with domestic providers to redirect stranded capital into grid resilience and cooling infrastructure for the Gigafactories. The Polish defection remains a wound, but we are using the `election_retrenchment` in the US to reframe the narrative: this is not a time to chase scale, but to build control. The world’s two largest powers are turning inward — our task is to become the jurisdiction that can be trusted to act, because we have chosen to be able to stop.
