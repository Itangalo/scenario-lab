## New measure  
**Critical Infrastructure AI Shield**  
A coordinated programme to deploy AI-driven intrusion detection, automated response systems, and red-teaming across essential services in energy, healthcare, and transport.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 5  
Targeted effect: +12 to +15 resilience by turn 5; begins narrowing the gap between advancing offensive capability and defensive readiness, particularly in response to eval_anomaly_reports and the recent cross-continental cyber intrusion  
Applies to: EU jurisdiction, with mandatory adoption for operators of essential services under NIS2, funded through the Digital Europe Programme and co-implemented with ENISA and national CSIRTs

## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because despite the mounting costs and resistance, binding control over compute remains the linchpin of sovereignty: without it, every other measure, including this turn’s new resilience push, depends on the goodwill of providers who are not bound to our security or our future.

## In practice  
We are acting on the understanding that capability without control is surrender. This turn, the Commission advances the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive through trilogue negotiations with the Council and Parliament, using Article 114 TFEU to frame compute access as a single-market condition rather than a national concession. The text now includes the tripartite offer: fast-track access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund for compliant operators, a liability shield for actions taken under EU scheduling, and the creation of a transparent, EDIC-run utility model for allocating reserved capacity. We are targeting adoption by turn 3, but the path is narrow — the Competitiveness Council remains divided, and Germany and Poland have threatened legal challenges. To hold cohesion, we are offering bilateral side letters on early access to sovereign compute for national resilience projects, tying their domestic interests to the Union’s strategic frame.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Critical Infrastructure AI Shield in direct response to the eval_anomaly_reports and the recent intrusion. ENISA will issue binding guidelines by mid-year requiring all NIS2-covered entities to deploy AI-powered detection stacks vetted for robustness and interpretability, with red-teaming conducted quarterly by a new Joint Cyber Defence Cell. Funding will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme, with top-up allocations for member states below the 50 resilience threshold. This is not optional modernisation — it is a condition of continued operation for essential services. The measure acknowledges that even as we build sovereign capacity, the frontier is already inside our systems, and we cannot wait for full control to begin defending what we have.
