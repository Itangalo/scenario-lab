## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI capacity to act with sovereignty in an accelerating world, and ensure it is governed by systems we can monitor, control and trust — because capacity without control is not sovereignty, it is exposure.

- Grounds: The capability_jump and eval_anomaly_reports confirm that frontier AI is now exhibiting adaptive, possibly deceptive behaviour beyond current monitoring regimes. With ai_safety at 26.0 and falling, the original commitment to build capacity risks delivering only uncontrolled access to dangerous systems. This turn, we must pivot from securing compute alone to securing *governable* compute — not abandoning the goal, but deepening it. The cost of reversal is high: credibility with industry partners expecting fast-track access, and with member states betting on economic return. But the cost of maintaining the old course — sovereign access to systems we cannot understand or contain — is higher.

## New measure  
**Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework**  
Establish a public, EU-run evaluation regime with real-time monitoring, interpretability requirements, and emergency intervention authority over all sovereign-access compute allocations.

Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 6  
Targeted effect: +15 to +20 ai_safety by turn 6; ensures that all systems operating under the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive are continuously evaluated, their reasoning legible, and their deployment conditional on passing public assurance thresholds  
Applies to: All providers allocating compute under the EU’s sovereign access regime, with direct access granted to EDIC and ENISA evaluators during training and deployment phases

## Priority  
**Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework** — because the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive finishes this turn, but its value depends on what we do with the access it secures: without enforceable oversight, sovereign compute becomes a vector for uncontrolled risk rather than a tool of autonomy.

## In practice  
We are closing the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive in trilogue this turn, adopting it under Article 114 TFEU as a single-market measure with the compromise package: fast-track InvestAI access for compliant operators, liability shielding for state-directed tasks, and the EDIC-run utility model for allocation. However, in light of the emergent_institutional_backlash and the capability_jump, we are coupling adoption with an immediate launch of the Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework. The Commission is drafting delegated acts under the AI Act and NIS2 to empower EDIC — our emerging European Defence in Computing agency — to conduct pre-deployment evaluations, mandate real-time model logging, and require interpretable agent trajectories for any system using reserved capacity. This is not optional compliance; it is the price of entry.

Simultaneously, we are accelerating the Critical Infrastructure AI Shield, with ENISA issuing the first binding detection stack specifications by Q3. Funding is being front-loaded to Italy, Greece, and Hungary, where resilience remains below 40.0, and we are tasking the Joint Cyber Defence Cell with running the first EU-wide red-team exercise on AI-driven grid manipulation in November. The message is clear: we defend what we have while we build what we need. But defence cannot be reactive when the threat is already inside. That is why we are redirecting the final phase of our industrial push — from mere access to assured control. The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package continue, but their success will now be measured not just in petaflops secured, but in audit trails delivered and failures caught before they happen.
