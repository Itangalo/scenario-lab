### Sovereignty Under Siege

The EU formally adopted the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive, reserving 30% of frontier compute for public use. But the victory was hollowed by the **member_state_defection**: Poland signed a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, exempting its AI workloads from EU reservation rules in exchange for cloud investment. The move, justified as economic pragmatism, shattered the common front. With `ai_capability` rising by 3.0 and access now demonstrably fragmented, `eu_ai_sovereignty` fell to 17.0.

But the measure faces immediate headwinds: Germany questions its legal basis, and industry warns of chilling investment. The portfolio now carries four large measures, draining political capital.
