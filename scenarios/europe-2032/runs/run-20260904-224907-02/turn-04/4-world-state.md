### A Pause in the Rush

The EU enacts a strategic recalibration. Alarmed by the collapse of oversight following the AI capability leap, Brussels proposes the **Compute Access Moratorium Trigger**, a small but binding mechanism that would halt sovereign compute allocations if safety falls below 20 or open models reach dangerous levels. Framed as responsible sovereignty, the proposal is fast-tracked under NIS2 and gains provisional support—debate is minimal, but formal adoption and integration into enforcement protocols will take at least one full turn. Full effect is delayed pending technical specification and audit alignment with the stalled **Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework**.

The **Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** formally completes, delivering a +4 bonus to political capital. However, its implementation remains suspended in practice, awaiting audit protocols from the **Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework**. Without enforceable monitoring, no allocations proceed. Germany continues to challenge the framework’s legal basis, delaying technical standards.

Meanwhile, the **Critical Infrastructure AI Shield** advances, with detection systems deployed in healthcare networks and red-teaming scheduled for November. The partial delivery yields +2 to resilience, now at 42.0, reflecting incremental hardening despite sectoral delays.

### Shifting Grounds

The US-China agreement on limited AI risk controls, though thin on verification, shifts global expectations. Restraint gains legitimacy, and the EU’s pivot is seen less as weakness than foresight. Public sentiment rises slightly to 45.0, buoyed by visible public-sector AI successes in reducing administrative delays—credited to EU-led integration, not foreign tools.

Open-weight capability increases moderately to 50.0, driven by continued diffusion of existing open models and incremental fine-tuning activity across European research labs and startups. This growth reflects sustained grassroots adoption and optimization, not a breakthrough release. The emergent **EuroShield** toolkit gains traction in municipal networks, contributing to defensive capacity without altering the strategic balance. The rise is consistent with organic accumulation and remains safely below `ai_capability`.

Yet sovereignty erodes further. With `ai_capability` rising by 3.0 and no sovereign compute actually operational due to the suspension, the Union’s access remains theoretical. The earlier Polish defection continues to undermine cohesion, and no new capacity comes online. `eu_ai_sovereignty` drops to 15.0.

Political capital, strained by four large measures and the priority cost, barely absorbs the +4 completion bonus. Ongoing costs outweigh gains, ending the turn at 20.0—still above the crisis threshold, but fragile.
