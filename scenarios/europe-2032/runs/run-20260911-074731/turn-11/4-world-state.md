### The cutoff
In August the notices arrived without explanation. Calls to the leading American model from European hospitals, ministries and logistics firms began returning refusals. Licences that Lyon oncology units and Gdansk port planners had rented by the hour stopped renewing. Helpdesks cited export terms. No appeal channel answered.

Wards that had prepared tailored-therapy protocols reverted to paper and waiting lists. Benefits offices already stained by the triage scandal lost their drafting assistants. Insurers repriced Strait freight again.

Brussels called it a failure of foresight, not an outrage to negotiate. Under emergency continuity powers, DG CNECT and ENISA ordered reserved domestic compute and openly available models reallocated to triaged health, benefits and port workloads. Joint cyber teams kept systems degraded but running.

### Holding, barely
The fallback stopped cascade but not pain. Switched systems were slower, less capable, and staff distrusted them. Mayors who received fallback slots and extra guards for fenced construction sites welcomed the help and demanded ten times more. Sabotage at fenced sites continued — cut fences, burned cable, night clashes with guards — now framed by local press as defence against foreign dependence.

The entry-level placement corps formally closed its funding round, credited with a few thousand posts in hospitals and town halls. Graduate hiring stayed frozen. Editorial pages linked the cutoff to the spring of cures that never arrived: hope licensed abroad, then withdrawn.

Washington offered structured access talks on published terms, and joint incident reporting resumed. In Brussels that read as thin comfort while clinics improvised. By December services had not collapsed, but the Union was visibly running on substitutes, and voters noticed.
