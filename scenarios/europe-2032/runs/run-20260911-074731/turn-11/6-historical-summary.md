US materials and therapy breakthroughs remained foreign-licensed and compute-dependent, then in August US model access from Europe was cut without appeal — Lyon oncology and Gdansk port licences stopped, wards reverted to paper and waiting lists, benefits offices lost drafting assistants, Strait freight repriced.

Brussels invoked emergency continuity powers: DG CNECT and ENISA reallocated reserved domestic compute and open models to triaged health, benefits and port workloads, with joint cyber teams keeping degraded systems running. Freight had earlier been kept moving via stockpile releases and insurer coordination despite higher premiums; automated benefits/court triage had been found to have systematically downgraded vulnerable groups for over a year despite being lawful, prompting redress and log-review mandates.

The fallback prevented collapse but was slower, less capable and distrusted. Mayors welcomed fallback slots and guards for fenced sites but demanded far more; sabotage continued, now framed as defence against foreign dependence. The ESF+ placement corps closed with a few thousand posts against tens of thousands missing, graduate hiring frozen. Washington offered structured access talks and resumed incident reporting, seen in Brussels as thin comfort. By December the Union ran visibly on substitutes.
