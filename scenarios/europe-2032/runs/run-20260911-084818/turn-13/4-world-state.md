### Cut off
In early autumn hospitals in three large member states found the leading foreign model refusing queries. No explanation, no appeal channel. Triage assistants, radiology summarizers and ministry drafting tools built on it simply stopped. Staff fell back to older EU-hosted systems and to paper. HERA and disease-control doctors triaged the caseload, keeping urgent wards running but at slower throughput.

Brussels called it coercion. Washington commentators called it a commercial compliance decision. For patients and clerks the cause mattered less than the queue.

### The coalition answer
The Commission convened holders of fibre, lithography parts, specialty chemicals and spare compute in a standing secretariat, using trade and anti-coercion powers to align export licences and bargain jointly for replacement capacity. Reimbursed health and ministry workloads were ordered onto coalition-logged infrastructure, paid from existing investment envelopes.

It worked in part. By November two replacement pipelines were flowing, enough to restart most hospital workloads. Vendors outside the public sector largely ignored the order, and joint procurement secured far less frontier capacity than promised. Ministers still claimed proof that denial would be answered by withholding bottlenecks others needed.

### No hiring
At the same time graduate hiring froze. Law firms, accountants, software houses and customer centres confirmed they were not replacing entry-level leavers, attributing it openly to automated handling. Universities reported cohorts without placements. Retraining vouchers tied to restored access reached only hundreds.

The redress guarantee formally completed — retention rules, human re-review rights and interim payments now law. But review desks cleared a fraction of backlogs, and anger over unreadable systems and lost starts hardened into hostility toward AI itself. Certification of the new checking method continued in reached hospitals, and synthesis screening caught paperwork errors, but neither touched the mood.
