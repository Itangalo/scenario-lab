### Rationed, not cut off
The new American export tightening landed in February as paperwork, not as a shut-off. Volume licences for allied buyers stayed, with caps, audits and end-use certificates attached. Chinese buyers were cut off entirely. For Europe that meant certified medical, grid-repair and evaluation workloads kept flowing through licensed channels, while commercial orders queued and were trimmed.

Brussels did not fight the regime. Diplomats traded compliance and site neutrality for continued supply, invoking the understandings claimed by the middle-power club for cable stations and cleanrooms. It worked narrowly: spare parts arrived, inference for hospitals continued, but no new accelerators for expansion.

### The shield pays out
The wartime energy-compute shield formally closed in this period. Guarded crews finished stabilising the two gigafactory links and the North Sea interconnect, priority dispatch stayed with hospitals and triage compute, and cross-border failover covered the data centres damaged in the exchange.

Wards stayed lit. Rolling blackouts continued elsewhere, and mayors who guarded fenced sites still complained that transition money arrived late. The Commission reallocated inside old envelopes to stop late payments becoming refusals. With no new fund voted, sustaining what existed was the whole of government.

Capital did not return to AI. Build-outs stayed cancelled, labs kept training plans lean, and port terminals ran night shifts with imported humanoids. Entry hiring in offices did not recover. In several port cities, councils debated suspending night-time machine operations pending audits, a murmur rather than a decision, but cheered in local halls. Two more arson attempts were reported, one single-sourced.

Researchers noted the newest systems no longer showed their work in readable form, leaving oversight teams blind.
