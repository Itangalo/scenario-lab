By mid-2029, a major AI-driven cyberattack—**cyber_major_incident**—crippled critical infrastructure across three EU member states, exposing systemic vulnerabilities despite the belated completion of the **Hardened Cyber Shield**. The breach, attributed to a foreign actor using model-generated exploits, underscored the fragility of current defences and intensified pressure on the EU’s sovereignty agenda.

In response, the EU proposed **Harden and Expand Emergency Override Protocols**, integrating prior interpretability advances into automated kill-switches and anomaly detection for sovereign runtime nodes. While development began, deployment was delayed by integration challenges and member-state coordination, with no operational impact this turn. The **Sovereign Compute Reserves** came fully online, providing a foundational resilience boost, but **InvestAI Gigafactories** achieved only partial operation due to permitting and energy grid delays, postponing full benefits.

A breakthrough in design verification—**verification_widens**—enabled AI to autonomously validate structural engineering plans, marking a shift of frontier capabilities beyond software into physical infrastructure, weakening containment arguments. Meanwhile, a new open-weight model release, widely distributed and functionally advanced, lifted `openweight_capability` to 55.5, though it remained behind closed models in real-time control and integration. `ai_capability` rose to 65.0 due to closed-model progress.

Political capital increased to 14.0: +3 from delivering one major initiative, +3 proposal bonus for addressing systemic risks (downgraded from +5 due to the measure’s limited scope), −2 for new initiative cost, and −1 for priority expenditure. Sovereignty gains remained muted due to stalled node deployment, Polish energy-related hesitations, and Bavarian construction protests.

The **emergent_regional_evaluation_network** formalized into a parallel audit authority, enhancing cross-national monitoring and red-teaming among EU audit units, while the **emergent_middle_power_compute_pact** deepened informal compute-sharing ties between mid-sized EU states, Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan—bypassing formal governance. Public trust stayed low, with no perceived benefits outweighing fears of disruption and dependency.
