### Enforcement Without Power

The EU declares *Enforce Emergency Audit Compliance* a success, publishing its first findings and threatening emergency actions against non-compliant AI developers. The Sovereign Evaluation Corps issues access demands under Article 23, targeting U.S. labs with high-capability models in health and energy systems. Yet no major developer fully complies. American firms cite conflicting federal regulations and assert that real-time audits compromise proprietary integrity. Behind closed doors, diplomats warn of retaliation if enforcement proceeds unilaterally.

With no binding agreements in place, the EU’s authority falters. No emergency declarations are issued — too many member states hesitate, fearing disruption to essential services reliant on foreign AI. The Corps publishes only redacted summaries, lacking technical depth or consequence. Publicly, the Commission calls this “a necessary first step”; privately, officials admit it is a bluff without leverage.

Meanwhile, the **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme officially concludes. Despite €200bn in funding commitments, only two sites have secured land and permits; legal disputes over environmental reviews and local opposition delay construction. The broader **Tech sovereignty package** ends with mixed results: private investment in data centres rose, but most new capacity remains tied to U.S. cloud providers, not sovereign infrastructure.

Resilience erodes further. Though the **Hardened Cyber Shield** is now technically complete across 68% of critical systems, healthcare networks remain vulnerable due to fragmented implementation and understaffed response teams. A follow-up breach hits regional hospitals in Greece and Portugal, exploiting known vulnerabilities left unpatched — proof that detection alone is not defence.

Public sentiment dips as protests grow near planned data centre zones. Slogans like “No Backdoor, No Trust” appear in Bavaria, while Polish activists disrupt fibre-laying operations, accusing the EU of building dependency under the guise of control.

In Brussels, political capital tightens. With three large measures still active — including the stalled **Sovereign Compute Reserves** — the budget strain deepens. The new audit push fails to shift the balance: declared a priority, it finishes on time but lands without force.

### Shadow Networks Rise

Amid central paralysis, national audit units in France, Germany, and Poland quietly expand coordination outside EU channels, sharing threat assessments and testing joint evaluation protocols. This emergent network operates in parallel to the official Corps — more agile, less visible, and increasingly sceptical of Brussels’ ability to enforce what it promises.
