In mid-2027, AI capabilities advanced steadily, with closed labs achieving more autonomous systems capable of sustained research tasks, raising global `ai_capability` to 57.0. A sophisticated open-weight model emerged, expanding access to powerful cyber and disinformation tools and increasing `openweight_capability` to 45.0, narrowing the open-closed gap. In response, the EU reaffirmed the Hardened Cyber Shield as a strategic priority and secured €6bn in co-financing, though binding mandates remain stalled due to member state resistance, delaying deployment until at least 2028.

The InvestAI Gigafactories initiative faltered further amid a complete withdrawal of private investment and no progress on public takeover, leaving a €45bn shortfall and undermining the EU’s compute sovereignty. The broader Tech Sovereignty package lost credibility, with Council divisions blocking decisive action.

The EU launched the Sovereign Evaluation Corps to audit high-risk AI systems, marking a policy milestone, but the agency remains non-operational, with no staff, authority, or developer access established. Informal EU coordination efforts stalled over sovereignty disputes, while voluntary safety cooperation among non-U.S. labs emerged as a limited counterpoint.

Public distrust deepened, with rising anti-AI protests and local moratoriums on data centres reflecting widespread anxiety over job losses and infrastructure fragility. Political momentum waned as costs mounted and key initiatives remained in limbo, leaving the EU increasingly reactive in the face of accelerating technological risk.
