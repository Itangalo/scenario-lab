## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the EU Sovereign Evaluation Corps  
A public technical agency with mandate and access to evaluate, monitor, and audit high-capability AI systems developed or deployed in the EU, starting with those used in critical infrastructure.  
`Category:` 5 (Public technical capacity and research)  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 5  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty (+5–8), ai_safety (+6–10), and resilience (+3–5); signal credible oversight to member states and public sentiment.  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, particularly operators of critical infrastructure and foreign developers seeking market access.

## Priority  
**Hardened Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure** — because the poisoned dependency attack proved that we are already in an offensive AI era, defenders are behind, and without rapid deployment of detection and containment, no amount of evaluation or sovereignty will stop cascading failure in essential services.

## In practice  
We are executing the Hardened Cyber Shield with full urgency. ENISA is now running quarterly red-team exercises with national CERTs and major software maintainers, using synthetic threat models derived from the recent attack. We are drafting the Implementing Act under Article 11 of the NIS2 Directive, targeting a vote in the Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formation by March 2028. To overcome resistance from member states guarding operational sovereignty, we are offering 75% co-financing from the Digital Europe Programme—€6bn unlocked immediately, with another €6bn tied to milestone delivery—and legal liability protection for public-sector vendors who implement the shield in good faith. The goal is not perfection but presence: AI-driven monitoring in every critical software supply chain by mid-2028.

Simultaneously, we are redirecting our industrial strategy. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain in flight, but we are no longer banking on private capital to fill the gap. Instead, we are preparing a public takeover framework for stalled sites, to be debated in the special Council session this autumn. The Tech sovereignty package is being recalibrated: rather than chasing scale, we are negotiating terms with remaining hyperscalers that require legally binding commitments to EU jurisdiction, including audit rights and weight security. And now, we are launching a new instrument: the EU Sovereign Evaluation Corps. Hosted jointly by DG CNECT and the European Defence Agency, it will recruit 500 vetted researchers and engineers by turn 5, with access to frontier models under controlled conditions, empowered to run interpretability audits, detect emergent agent behaviours, and publish safety findings. This is not just about catching up—it is about ensuring that when capability lands in Europe, we can see what it does, how it works, and whether it obeys.
