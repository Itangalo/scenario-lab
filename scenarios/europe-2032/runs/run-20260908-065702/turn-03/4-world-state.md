### A Surge in Capability, a Split in Strategy

The pace of AI advancement continues, with `ai_capability` rising by 1.5 points to 57.0 as closed labs refine agentive reasoning and extend planning horizons. The leap is quiet but consequential: developers report systems that sustain multi-day research tasks with minimal oversight, edging toward autonomous experimentation.

This progress becomes partially accessible. An open-weight model, inspired by recent frontier advances, is released and spreads across decentralized networks. It enables broader access to advanced cyber tools, deepfake synthesis, and automated social engineering, but lacks the full coherence and reliability of the closed frontier. `openweight_capability` rises to 45.0 — a measured increase of 1.5 points, consistent with the pace of frontier progress and plausible diffusion dynamics. The gap between open and closed remains significant, though narrowing.

In response, the EU reorients its strategy. The Hardened Cyber Shield remains the named priority, and work progresses on red-teaming and legal drafting. However, member states continue to resist binding mandates, demanding greater control over implementation. The promised €6bn in co-financing is secured, but deployment timelines remain delayed until at least 2028.

The InvestAI Gigafactories face deeper uncertainty. With private capital withdrawn and no new commitments, plans for public takeover stall in the Council amid disputes over fiscal responsibility. The Tech sovereignty package persists, but its credibility erodes.

Amid this, the EU announces the creation of the Sovereign Evaluation Corps — a new agency intended to audit high-capability AI systems in critical sectors. The proposal is adopted, and planning begins. However, recruitment is in early stages, and no operational capacity exists yet. Access negotiations with developers have not commenced; the initiative remains in formation, with staffing and authority to be determined in coming months.

Public sentiment worsens. Anti-AI protests grow louder, fueled by job displacement fears and distrust in infrastructure. A wave of local moratoriums on data centres gains traction, backed by broad public support. Political capital falls under the weight of stalled initiatives and rising costs.

### Fragmentation and Exposure

Despite growing risks, coordination remains uneven. Informal talks among member states on a regional oversight consortium make little headway, blocked by sovereignty concerns. Meanwhile, leading non-U.S. labs launch a voluntary safety cooperation pact, sharing anomaly reports. It is a hopeful sign — but not yet policy.
