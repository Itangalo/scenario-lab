### A Crisis of Authority

The EU closes two major initiatives: the Tech sovereignty package, which unlocked €180bn in private AI infrastructure investment, and the Hardened Cyber Shield, now operational in 68% of critical systems. Yet the recent ransomware sweep across public health networks exposed fatal gaps — detection worked, but response failed under load. The attack, powered by AI-generated exploits, overwhelmed understaffed IT teams and revealed that resilience is only as strong as its weakest link.

In response, the EU launches *Enforce Emergency Audit Compliance*, a large Category 1 measure aimed at forcing foreign developers to submit to real-time audits by the newly forming Sovereign Evaluation Corps. The move is declared the EU’s priority, with emergency powers threatened for non-compliance. However, U.S. labs continue to delay access, citing conflicting domestic regulations. No findings are published by mid-year, and operational capacity remains below target — the Corps is underfunded and understaffed, with only two member states having fully appointed auditors. The measure is underway but not yet effective.

This sharp capability gain feeds closed-model progress, pushing `ai_capability` to 61.5. Offensive tools evolve in parallel, and with no new resilience measure in force, systemic fragility deepens.

The Sovereign Compute Reserves initiative stumbles. Legal disputes over Article 114 TFEU persist, and two member states refuse ratification without additional concessions. Momentum stalls, and with `ai_capability` rising significantly, `eu_ai_sovereignty` erodes further due to unmet dependency risks.
