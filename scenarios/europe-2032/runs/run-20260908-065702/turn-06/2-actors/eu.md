## In practice
We are closing the Tech sovereignty package and the Hardened Cyber Shield this turn. The Shield is operational in 68% of critical supply chains, with ENISA and national CERTs now running coordinated detection. But resilience remains uneven — healthcare systems were overwhelmed — and we are accelerating integration into the remaining 32% through co-financing offers tied to audit compliance. The Sovereign Evaluation Corps has transitioned from setup to enforcement: 200 auditors are now active, drawn from DG CNECT, the European Defence Agency, and national agencies. We have issued emergency access requests under Article 23 to all developers with systems above 50.0 capability in health, energy, transport, and digital infrastructure. The U.S. labs are stalling, citing jurisdictional conflict — we will not allow that to become precedent.

The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep across public health systems — has hardened resolve. Recovery was slow, patching chaotic, and public anger is real. We are now integrating the new predictive control method from the safety_breakthrough into audit protocols, allowing us to assess emergent behaviour risks before deployment. But that is not enough: we must act. We are preparing emergency declarations for non-compliant entities and will publish the first findings by mid-2029. This is not a request — it is a demand for access, and a warning that silence will be treated as refusal.

Simultaneously, we are standing up the Sovereign Compute Reserves under Article 114 TFEU, designating 10% of EU-anchored compute as legally protected infrastructure. The EuroHPC-AI Authority will govern it, with nodes distributed across five member states and hardened against extraterritorial pressure. Legal disputes persist — two member states threaten to block — but we are offering energy and grid priority in return for early ratification. This is not about matching the frontier; it is about ensuring that when access is cut, we do not go dark.
