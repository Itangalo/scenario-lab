## In practice  
We are closing out the Hardened Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure this turn. The Implementing Act under NIS2 has passed through the Internal Market and Telecommunications Council, secured by the offer of 75% co-financing and liability protection for public vendors. ENISA, in coordination with national CERTs, has conducted four red-team exercises simulating AI-driven supply chain attacks, and the results have informed the final configuration of the shield. By June 2028, AI-driven intrusion detection is now live in 68% of designated critical software supply chains, with full coverage expected by Q3. The shield is not perfect — some member states still resist full integration — but it is present, and that presence is already deterring opportunistic actors.

Simultaneously, we are accelerating the EU Sovereign Evaluation Corps beyond its original mandate. The safety_breakthrough event has given us a real tool: a certified method for predicting emergent agent behaviour before deployment. We are deploying it now. DG CNECT and the European Defence Agency have jointly activated the first 200 auditors, and we are issuing emergency access requests to developers of systems above capability 50.0 operating in critical sectors. We are invoking Article 23 of the AI Act’s high-risk provisions and preparing emergency declarations for non-compliant entities. This is not optional review — it is binding audit. The first findings will be public by turn 5, and they will include not just safety gaps but weight-security assessments and verification of human oversight claims. We are also integrating the verification_widens insight: where automatic checks now exist in law, medicine, and engineering, we are expanding audit scope to include those domains immediately. This is how we close the loop — capability grows, but so does our ability to see it, challenge it, and contain it.
