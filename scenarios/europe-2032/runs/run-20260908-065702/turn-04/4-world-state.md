### Breakthroughs and Building Capacity

The frontier advances again. With automatic verification now possible in law, medicine and engineering, developers rapidly train systems to exploit these domains autonomously. `ai_capability` rises by +1.5 to 58.5, as AI-driven contract analysis, clinical coding and structural assessments become self-validating — a shift that erodes the last assumption that human oversight is necessary in complex professional work.

Simultaneously, a major safety breakthrough lands: a control method that predicts emergent agent behaviour before deployment is adopted across leading labs. This directly strengthens assurance on live systems, not just research prototypes. The improvement contributes to `ai_safety`, which increases by +5.0 to 33.0 — significant progress, but short of full systemic integration.

In response, the EU accelerates its Sovereign Evaluation Corps. Drawing on the new interpretability tools, the agency expands staffing and begins initial training for audit teams. Binding audits of high-capability AI systems in critical infrastructure are planned, with pilot assessments scheduled for early next turn. Emergency access requests under Article 23 of the AI Act are drafted, but no foreign developer has yet received or responded to them. Legal preparations are underway, but enforcement capacity remains nascent.

The Hardened Cyber Shield finishes deployment this turn. Detection systems are now active in 68% of critical software supply chains, with full rollout nearing. Red-team exercises confirm improved resilience against AI-driven intrusions, contributing to systemic robustness.

Public sentiment ticks up slightly as visible action counters narratives of helplessness. However, protests persist at data centre sites, and distrust remains high. Political capital improves due to completed measures and credible response, though costs remain steep.

### Fragmentation Persists

Despite progress, coordination gaps remain. Some member states delay full integration into the Cyber Shield, citing national sovereignty. Developer compliance with upcoming audit orders is uncertain, and no U.S. lab has confirmed participation. The Union’s leverage depends on enforcement follow-through — which has not yet been tested.
