In early 2027, a widespread AI-facilitated attack exploiting poisoned software dependencies crippled municipal services across five EU member states, disrupting healthcare, taxation, and social payments. The incident underscored the urgent threat of offensive AI and triggered emergency measures, including the formal prioritization of the Hardened Cyber Shield. ENISA launched red-team exercises with national CERTs, and the EU moved to unlock €6bn in emergency funding, proposing mandatory AI-driven intrusion detection for public-sector software supply chains with 75% co-financing incentives. However, legal approval remains pending due to member state resistance over sovereignty and control, delaying full deployment until at least mid-2028.

Simultaneously, the collapse of private AI investment derailed the Tech Sovereignty package, leaving two of three hyperscaler partnerships defunct and creating a €45bn shortfall in the InvestAI Gigafactories, jeopardizing the EU’s compute strategy. A deeply divided Council debated public intervention, reflecting broader uncertainty about AI’s economic trajectory amid market volatility and underwhelming model releases. Despite signs of slowed advancement, offensive AI tools remain widely accessible, widening the gap between capability and governance.

Public trust declined sharply as disruptions and failed initiatives fueled skepticism and protests. Political capital eroded under rising costs and fragmented coordination. While resilience improved incrementally through planning and partial deployments, systemic vulnerabilities persist. The EU has set a strategic course—but remains reactive, with long-term stability still in doubt.
