### The Weight of Response

A poisoned software dependency, quietly propagated by AI-generated code, brought municipal services in five member states to a standstill. Health records vanished, tax filings stalled, and social payments froze. The attack—adaptive, stealthy, and broadly deployed—was a wake-up call: offensive AI tools were no longer theoretical. The EU responded with urgency, designating the Hardened Cyber Shield as its central defence and initiating emergency procedures.

ENISA began coordinating red-team simulations with national CERTs, and discussions advanced on unlocking €6bn in emergency funding from the Digital Europe Programme. A proposal for mandatory AI-driven intrusion detection in public-sector software supply chains was tabled, accompanied by draft liability protections and a 75% co-financing model to incentivize adoption. However, no mandates have taken effect yet—implementation requires Council and Parliament approval, and several member states have signaled resistance to ceding operational control. Legal and procedural hurdles mean full deployment is unlikely before mid-2028.

### A Fracturing Foundation

The cyber blow landed amid deeper tremors. The ai_investment_collapse erased private commitments underpinning the Tech sovereignty package, leaving two of three hyperscaler partnerships defunct. The InvestAI Gigafactories now face a €45bn shortfall, casting doubt on the entire compute build-out strategy. A special Council session in April revealed sharp divisions: some states pushed for public takeover of stalled sites, others questioned the viability of any large-scale investment amid market uncertainty.

While some point to recent underwhelming model releases and shifting market dynamics, no conclusion can yet be drawn about the long-term pace of AI advancement. The apparent dip in momentum may be cyclical or temporary. Regardless, offensive tools are already in circulation, and the gap between capability and control continues to widen.

### Sentiment and Capital in Freefall

Public trust wavered as media coverage focused on disruption and dependency. The visible failure of critical systems, paired with news of evaporating investments, triggered a wave of scepticism. Labour displacement fears resurfaced, and protests flared outside data centre sites. Political capital eroded under the weight of multiple stalled initiatives and rising costs. The Hardened Cyber Shield, while now prioritized, remains in early execution and cannot yet offset the growing sense of instability.

Resilience inched upward through planning and partial deployment, but the full shield remains years from completion. The EU has set a course—but not yet changed the tide.
