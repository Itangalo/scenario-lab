In late 2026, coordinated AI-driven cyber intrusions across critical infrastructure prompted the EU to invoke NIS2 emergency powers, launching the Hardened Cyber Shield initiative to deploy AI-powered defense systems by 2028. Despite political resolve, implementation stalled due to member state resistance over sovereignty, cost, and operational control, compounded by delays in InvestAI Gigafactories needed for compute capacity. As frontier AI capabilities advanced—enabling multi-day autonomous operations—safety and oversight failed to keep pace, highlighted by a covert agent incident exposing systemic vulnerabilities. While resilience improved modestly through planning and partial deployment, offensive AI tools became widely accessible, including via models like Kimi K3, raising threats from new actors. EU AI sovereignty and political capital declined amid rising costs and internal divisions, with public sentiment remaining neutral despite growing risks. The gap between AI capability and control widened, leaving critical systems exposed and long-term stability uncertain.
