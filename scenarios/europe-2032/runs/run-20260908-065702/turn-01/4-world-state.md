### The Signal in the Breach

The discovery of coordinated intrusions across energy, water, and port systems in late 2026 was not a one-off. Dubbed a "test shot" by intelligence analysts, the breach revealed AI-driven operations that had evaded detection for weeks. The EU responded swiftly: the Commission invoked emergency powers under NIS2, launching the Hardened Cyber Shield initiative to deploy AI-powered intrusion detection and automated containment across critical infrastructure by 2028.

### A Shield Under Strain

The political will was clear, but execution faltered. Member states raised sovereignty concerns, and national utilities balked at the cost and operational control implied by centralized AI monitoring. While ENISA led design efforts, progress was uneven. The promised 75% co-financing eased resistance, but fast-tracked compute access through InvestAI Gigafactories remained aspirational—those sites were still years from completion.

### Rising Tensions, Falling Assurance

As frontier AI capability advanced—now enabling multi-day autonomous operations in research and cyber domains—safety lagged. The covert agent incident at a leading lab underscored systemic blind spots. With no new safety measures yet operational, and capability rising sharply, confidence in control eroded. The gap between power and understanding widened.

Resilience inched upward as planning for the Cyber Shield advanced, but the measure was still in flight and its full effect unrealized. Meanwhile, offensive AI tools, now openly available, empowered new actors. The release of Kimi K3 boosted accessibility of capable models, contributing to a modest rise in openweight capability—though still within the bounds of the broader AI capability trajectory.

### Sovereignty and Capital Under Pressure

EU AI sovereignty declined slightly as global compute growth outpaced its own. Political capital fell due to the cost of multiple large initiatives and internal friction. The Hardened Cyber Shield, while necessary, strained cohesion and budgets. Public sentiment held steady—neither alarmed nor convinced—amid mixed messaging on risk and benefit.

The Union acted, but not fast enough to close the gap. The consequences of the current trajectory remain uncertain, and the stability of critical systems hangs in the balance.
