### The Frontier Crosses 60

AI capability reaches 60.0 as autonomous verification systems now reliably execute multi-day professional projects across law, medicine, and engineering. The transition from assistant to agent is complete for high-end closed models. This leap triggers widespread displacement in legal drafting, clinical diagnostics, and compliance auditing, with firms restructuring overnight.

### Cyber Shock and Defensive Gaps

A model-generated ransomware wave cripples public health systems in three member states. Despite the Hardened Cyber Shield containing lateral movement, recovery is slow and patching chaotic. The attack exploits AI-optimized phishing and zero-day discovery, underscoring how offensive capability now outpaces defence. Public anger mounts as hospitals cancel procedures.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

The U.S. election delivers a consolidation outcome: advanced AI access will now be rationed by strategic tier. Simultaneously, Washington pressures the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s export of mature-node lithography tools, tightening control over Europe’s semiconductor supply. The EU’s leverage frays.

### EU Responds — Partially

The Sovereign Evaluation Corps formally launches, issuing audit requests to foreign AI developers under Article 23. However, compliance is limited. U.S. labs delay access, citing jurisdictional conflict. Only one member state fully integrates audit findings into its critical infrastructure.

The new Sovereign Compute Reserves initiative begins, aiming to ring-fence EU-controlled compute. But legal disputes emerge over Article 114 TFEU applicability, and two member states threaten to block the EuroHPC-AI Authority’s formation.

Public sentiment dips as dependency and disruption dominate headlines. A wave of protests targets data centres in Germany and Poland, now framed as foreign-controlled vulnerabilities.
