## New measure
**Measure:** Establish Sovereign Compute Reserves and Access Lockdown  
Create a legally ring-fenced, EU-controlled compute infrastructure reserved for emergency access by the Evaluation Corps and critical public functions, with physical and legal protections ensuring uninterrupted operation during geopolitical disruption.  
`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty (+8–12), ai_safety (+4–6), and resilience (+3–5); ensure that even under external supply or access denial, the EU retains operational control over evaluation, auditing, and crisis response systems.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national governments, and designated critical infrastructure operators; binding on all foreign AI providers operating in the EU.

## Priority
**Accelerate Sovereign Evaluation Corps Access and Audits** — because the launch of binding audits this turn is the first real assertion of EU authority over foreign-developed AI in critical systems, and its credibility depends on visible, timely execution: without immediate access and public findings, the Corps becomes a paper tiger, and our leverage evaporates just as the U.S. tightens its own grip through election_consolidation.

## In practice
We are closing two major programmes this turn: the Hardened Cyber Shield and the initial phase of the Sovereign Evaluation Corps. The Shield is now operational in 68% of critical software supply chains, with ENISA and national CERTs running coordinated detection and automated containment. Full coverage will be achieved by Q3 2028, but the real test comes now — whether member states accept binding integration and whether developers comply with audit requests. We are using the co-financing offer and liability shield to pressure holdouts, but sovereignty concerns remain sharp, especially in Central Europe.

Simultaneously, the Sovereign Evaluation Corps is transitioning from setup to enforcement. We have activated 200 auditors, drawn from DG CNECT, the European Defence Agency, and national cybersecurity agencies, and are issuing emergency access requests under Article 23 of the AI Act to all developers with systems above capability 50.0 operating in health, energy, transport, and digital infrastructure. The new predictive control method from the safety_breakthrough is being integrated into audit protocols, allowing us to assess emergent behaviour risks before deployment. We are preparing emergency declarations for non-compliant entities — including U.S. labs — and will publish the first findings by early 2029. This is not a request for cooperation; it is a demand for compliance.

The cyber_major_incident this turn — a model-generated ransomware sweep across public health systems in three member states — has exposed how far offensive capability has outpaced defence. While the Hardened Cyber Shield contained the lateral spread, recovery took weeks, and public trust eroded further. At the same time, the emergent_open_source_biological_toolkit and bio_uplift_findings signal that the bio risk gate is opening, and open-weight models are nearing dangerous capability. Meanwhile, supply_chain_coercion and election_consolidation confirm that our dependence on U.S. goodwill is now a strategic vulnerability — access to advanced AI is being rationed, and our leverage is thinning.

That is why we are launching a new measure: Sovereign Compute Reserves. We are designating 10% of all EU-anchored compute — including capacity from the InvestAI Gigafactories and partner hyperscalers — as legally protected infrastructure, immune to foreign jurisdictional override and reserved for public evaluation, crisis response, and continuity of essential services. This will be governed by a new EuroHPC-AI Authority, with physical nodes distributed across at least five member states and hardened against extraterritorial pressure. We are drafting the legal act under Article 114 TFEU, with emergency override provisions under the Digital Crisis Protocol. This is not about matching the frontier — not yet — but about ensuring that when access is cut, we do not go dark.
