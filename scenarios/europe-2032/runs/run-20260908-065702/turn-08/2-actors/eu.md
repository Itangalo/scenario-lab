## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI infrastructure and control over critical capabilities``  
  - Trigger: the measure finishes this turn with minimal physical delivery and no legal resolution on access  
  - Grounds: The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech sovereignty package** have concluded with only two sites advanced and no shift in control over operational infrastructure. The Sovereign Compute Reserves remain legally contested and functionally offline. The promised sovereign capacity does not exist where it matters — in accessible, usable, enforceable form. The commitment has failed its core test: when access is rationed, the EU still cannot act. We must shift from building to seizing operational authority.

* ``- modify `enforceable_authority` (commitment): Ensure EU evaluative and audit power is binding, enforceable, and credibly backed by emergency action``  
  - Grounds: The audit push has collapsed into symbolic disclosure. No U.S. developer granted access. Emergency declarations were blocked by member states fearing service disruption. The Sovereign Evaluation Corps published redacted summaries with no technical consequence. The credibility of enforcement is broken. We now face not a failure of will but a failure of means: without sovereign compute, without emergency access, and without credible retaliation, enforcement is theatre. The commitment stands, but its meaning must shift — from asserting authority to creating the conditions under which it can ever be credible again.

* ``- add `sovereign_access` (commitment): Guarantee uninterrupted, legal, and technically effective EU access to high-capability AI systems for evaluation, crisis response, and essential public services``  
  - Grounds: The core failure this turn is not defiance abroad but dependency at home. We cannot audit what we cannot run. We cannot respond to incidents if our tools depend on foreign infrastructure. We cannot enforce rules if compliance requires permission we do not control. The new commitment targets the root: access, not just oversight. This is not about matching the frontier — it is about ensuring that when the world cuts us off, we still see, still decide, and still act.

## New measure
**Measure:** Mandate Sovereign Access for Critical AI Systems  
Require all AI systems above capability 50.0 deployed in EU critical infrastructure to run on EU-anchored, auditable infrastructure with guaranteed emergency access for the Evaluation Corps and continuity of service under crisis conditions.  
`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty (+5–7), ai_safety (+3–5), and resilience (+2–4); reduce dependence on foreign cloud providers; create enforceable leverage over developers by tying market access to physical presence and legal accountability.  
`Applies to:` Foreign and domestic AI developers operating in EU critical sectors (health, energy, transport, digital infrastructure), with compliance enforced through market access licensing and the Digital Crisis Protocol.

## Priority
**Mandate Sovereign Access for Critical AI Systems** — because without physical and legal control over where and how AI runs, no audit, no evaluation, no emergency power means anything, and this turn’s collapse of enforcement proves that sovereignty is not declared — it is wired in.

## In practice
We are ending the Sovereign Compute Reserves initiative. It was blocked not by technology but by law and fear: two member states refused ratification under U.S. pressure, and cloud providers refused to partition infrastructure under EU jurisdiction. We cannot build our way out of dependency if the ground keeps shifting. So we pivot: instead of trying to ring-fence compute we don’t control, we mandate that any system above 50.0 capability used in critical infrastructure must run on EU-anchored, auditable infrastructure with guaranteed access during crises. This is not a subsidy — it is a condition of operation.

We are drafting the **Critical AI Access Act** under Article 114 TFEU, to be proposed to the Council and Parliament in June. It will require developers to either deploy on sovereign EU infrastructure — certified by the EuroHPC-AI Authority — or face suspension of market access. We are offering a one-year grace period for migration, but no exemptions. The Evaluation Corps will be granted live access to these instances, not just audit reports. We are working with France, Germany, and Poland to align national procurement rules in parallel, to show immediate downstream consequence for non-compliance.

Behind the scenes, we are negotiating with three EU-based hyperscalers to scale sovereign capacity rapidly, using emergency permitting under the revised Tech sovereignty framework. We are offering grid priority, state aid waivers, and access to public fibre backbones in exchange for binding commitments on uptime, access, and security. This is not about catching up — it is about creating a zone of control, however small, where the EU can still act when the world turns off the lights.
