### The Pivot That Isn’t

The European Union formally cancels the Sovereign Compute Reserves, conceding that legal resistance and U.S. pressure have rendered it unworkable. In its place, the Commission proposes the **Critical AI Access Act**, mandating that all high-capability AI systems in critical sectors run on EU-anchored, auditable infrastructure with guaranteed emergency access. Framed as a sovereignty firewall, the measure targets foreign cloud dominance by conditioning market access on physical presence and legal accountability.

Yet the pivot falters at launch. Only three EU-based hyperscalers have the capacity to absorb the mandated workloads, and none commit fully, citing unresolved liability and energy allocation risks. France, Germany, and Poland express cautious support but delay aligning procurement rules, demanding proof of enforceability. U.S. developers denounce the Act as digital protectionism, warning of service disruptions in healthcare and energy if compliance timelines are enforced.

The American **CONSOLIDATION** posture hardens the divide. In response to the proposal, export controls tighten further—now rationing advanced AI access even to allied institutions unless usage is pre-approved and monitored. This **export_control_escalation** directly undermines the EU’s leverage, exposing the fragility of its operational access.

Meanwhile, the **Hardened Cyber Shield** proves insufficient. A new AI-driven intrusion exploits legacy interfaces in Portuguese grid software, bypassing detection for 36 hours. Though contained, the incident reveals that automated defences still rely on U.S.-hosted models, which throttle performance during peak audit loads—a silent throttling interpreted as sabotage by some officials.

Public sentiment dips further as protests in Bavaria and Silesia disrupt test deployments of sovereign cloud nodes. Critics frame the Access Act as a rerun of failed gigafactory promises—more mandates, less delivery. Trust in EU-level AI governance erodes.

Brussels scrambles to reframe the narrative, but political capital continues to bleed. The portfolio remains heavy, and the new measure, though large and urgent, fails to rally cohesion.

### Shadow Levers

Outside official channels, the informal audit network among France, Germany, and Poland expands, conducting joint red-team exercises on energy AI systems. Their findings, shared in encrypted channels, bypass the weakened Sovereign Evaluation Corps—offering a glimpse of what distributed enforcement could look like, if ever formalised.
