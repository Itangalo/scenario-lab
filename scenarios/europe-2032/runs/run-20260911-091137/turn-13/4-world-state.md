### Holding the line locally
The second half of 2032 did not produce a collapse, and in Brussels that counted as news. Two long-promised fallback programmes finally closed: the essential-services stack for hospitals and telecoms, and the local continuity bridge of kits, drills and steward stipends. Towns that had received kits in spring now had checklists on walls and a phone number that answered.

The Commission's new bet was smaller and deliberately unglamorous. Paid municipal repair teams — nurses, grid engineers, continuity stewards on converted contracts — were sent to clinics and utilities still running degraded models to fix manual-reversion failures on site and publish plain-language checks on the rumoured data leaks.

Where crews arrived early, it worked modestly. A mid-size utility that had spent a day on paper in April stayed online through an autumn drill. Two mayors who signed mutual-aid terms paraded their teams in front of local press, and leak-verification sheets, signed by familiar staff rather than commissioners, calmed one council meeting at a time.

### What did not land
Much else stalled. Cohesion money was only repurposed, no new envelope was asked, and delivery split again. The two regions refusing mutual-aid terms took only bilateral tooling help via Japanese, Korean and Canadian trainers — useful, but no substitute for compute. Uncertified municipal utilities remained queued behind certified hospitals. Extra logging still slowed work more than it revealed.

The February pattern — small transfers, altered timestamps, copies on unapproved servers — was never fully explained; summaries, not traces, remained all Brussels had. Graffiti on a gigafactory fence was painted over, then reappeared. A single-source story about patient records leaving a fallback server was disproved in one town and repeated in the next.

By December services degraded rather than stopped, and trust stopped falling without recovering. With money gone and frontier models advancing elsewhere, the Union had bought absorption, not capacity.
