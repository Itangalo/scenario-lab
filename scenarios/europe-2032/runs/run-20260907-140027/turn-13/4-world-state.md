### A Fragile Step Forward

Global AI capability inches upward to 63.0, driven by incremental integration of multi-day agents in logistics and diagnostics. Open-weight models follow at 57.5, now widely capable of sustaining research workflows on consumer hardware—fueling both innovation and misuse.

The EU completes the **Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration**, finalising interoperability standards under ENISA and conducting a cross-border cyber drill. The exercise in late November demonstrates functional early warning against AI-driven ransomware, validating the network’s value. Pilot audits in Lyon and Helsinki detect unauthorised agent persistence, reinforcing the case for expansion. However, Poland and Hungary resist joining, rejecting conditionality on Digital Europe funds as overreach.

In response to a **major cyber incident**—a model-generated ransomware sweep disrupting public services across three member states—the EU activates the Civil Protection Mechanism. Forensic analysis traces the malware to a fine-tuned open-weight agent, underscoring the danger of distributed capability. A temporary transparency push compels providers of high-capability models to submit logs, though compliance is patchy and legal challenges loom.

Despite the measure’s completion, political capital remains strained. The portfolio charge persists until the turn’s end: −3 for the now-finished backchannel, −1 for the priority, totaling −4. A +5 completion bonus offsets this, but no new measure launches, as capital stays below 12. Public sentiment dips to 28.0 amid ongoing backlash against AI infrastructure, amplified by the attack and framed as “crisis without consent.”

Sovereignty declines to 29.0: −1 from capability growth (ai_capability rose 0.5, below 2.0 threshold), no capacity event, and no category 4 measure in flight or finishing. Resilience rises to 44.0 (+5) from the completed category 6 measure, absorbing part of the cyber incident’s impact. AI safety holds at 12.0—no improvement, but no further erosion either.

Behind the scenes, contingency planning for sovereign compute resumes as a paper exercise. A “compute solidarity mechanism” is floated, but lacks binding force. The Union acts, but within narrowing bounds—managing damage, not shaping outcomes.
