## In practice
This turn, we complete the final phase of the Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration. ENISA finalises the interoperability standards for agent monitoring and model deployment logging, releasing them as a binding technical specification under Article 12 of the Cyber Resilience Act. Pilot audits in Lyon and Helsinki confirm the ability to detect unauthorised persistence in public service agents, and we use these findings to pressure non-participating member states—particularly Poland and Hungary—to join by linking compliance to eligibility for Digital Europe Programme funds in 2024. DG HOME leads a cross-border crisis simulation in late November involving Finland, Spain, and France, testing coordinated response to a simulated AI-driven ransomware cascade; the exercise validates the backchannel as a functional early-warning layer, even without central command.

We also respond to the cyber_major_incident by activating the Civil Protection Mechanism under Regulation 1381/2013, framing the attack as a transnational crisis requiring mutual aid. While attribution remains unclear, forensic analysis from Europol’s Joint Cyber Unit confirms the malware was generated by a fine-tuned open-weight agent based on a model released at capability 56.0, now weaponised through automated dependency chaining. We issue a formal notice under Article 16 of the AI Act to all providers of models above 55.0 capability, demanding submission of training logs and inference-time monitoring data for the next 90 days—a temporary transparency measure justified by emergency powers, not a permanent regime. This buys time to assess whether the Evaluation Shield’s anomalous behavior alerts were early warnings of this class of threat, while avoiding new legislative proposals we lack capital to pass.

Behind closed doors, the Commission’s AI Task Force begins drafting a contingency plan for a potential Category 4 revival—sovereign compute through repurposed HPC infrastructure and emergency grid access—but only as a paper exercise. Without German and Irish buy-in, and with public sentiment hostile to new data centres, any launch now would fail. We instead focus on damage control: the Presidency circulates a non-paper proposing a “compute solidarity mechanism” where member states with surplus capacity (Finland, Sweden) temporarily allocate 15% of it to EU-wide resilience tasks, framed as civil defence rather than industrial policy. It is a stopgap, not a strategy—but in this turn, survival is the only strategy we can afford.
