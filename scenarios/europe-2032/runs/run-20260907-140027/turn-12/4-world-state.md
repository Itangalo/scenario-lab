### Stalled Sovereignty, Shrinking Capital

Global AI capability advances to 62.5, with steady integration of multi-day reasoning agents into logistics and diagnostics, though no breakthroughs emerge. Open-weight models track closely, rising to 57.0, now capable of running sustained research workflows on consumer-grade hardware, amplifying accessibility but also misuse risks.

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation formally expires with no infrastructure secured. German constitutional resistance and Irish grid constraints remain insurmountable, leaving the EU without sovereign access to frontier-scale compute. The failure drains credibility and accelerates doubts over the Union’s strategic coherence.

Political capital, already at 5.0, declines to 3.0. The sole active measure, Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration, continues at full cost (−3), and the named priority adds a further −1. With no new measure launched and no portfolio gain, the charge stands at −4. No proposal bonus applies. Public sentiment, at 30.0, remains below political capital, so no legitimacy lending occurs.

EU AI sovereignty drops to 30.0: last turn’s 32.0, minus 1 due to capability rise (ai_capability increased by 0.5, below the 2.0 threshold, so no decay), but no capacity event occurred. However, the expired category 4 measure yields no boost, and no replacement is in flight—leaving the Union more exposed as dependency deepens.

Resilience inches up to 39.0 (+1) from partial progress in cross-border drills and standardisation work, but without funding or mandate, gains remain preparatory. The backchannel functions as an informal network, not an operational shield.

The European Evaluation Shield’s redacted alert on “anomalous agent behaviors” triggers developer backlash. Firms accuse the EU of opaque enforcement, and several delay compliance, citing legal uncertainty. While technically a step toward oversight, the move fractures trust further.

Public sentiment falls to 30.0 as grassroots opposition to data centre expansions spreads across mid-sized cities. Legal challenges and local referenda, framed as democratic resistance to unaccountable AI infrastructure, gain traction. Media coverage emphasizes “AI without consent,” reinforcing public anxiety.
