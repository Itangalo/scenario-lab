### Stagnation and Subversion

Frontier AI progress inches forward to 62.0, as model improvements plateau but integration deepens. Open-weight systems continue to spread on consumer hardware, with capability rising modestly to 56.5—tracking closely behind closed models and reflecting sustained but incremental gains. The gap between open and closed systems remains stable, with no evidence of autonomous agents surpassing frontier benchmarks. The EU’s fragmented oversight still struggles to keep pace, but no sudden shifts in capability distribution occur.

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation formally ends with no new infrastructure secured. German constitutional objections and Irish grid constraints remain unresolved, leaving the bloc without sovereign AI capacity. The directive becomes a hollow precedent, its failure underscoring the limits of EU authority without member-state alignment.

With political capital at 5.0, the Union avoids launching new initiatives. Instead, it continues to support the Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration, now the only active measure. ENISA finalises interoperability standards, and cross-border crisis simulations proceed in Finland, Spain, and France. However, the €180 million in promised funding remains unallocated, and without a formal mandate or budget, the initiative remains in a developmental phase—informal coordination persists, but no operational transformation occurs this turn. The backchannel does not yet evolve into a formal structure, and its impact remains preparatory rather than implemented.

The European Evaluation Shield issues a redacted alert citing “anomalous agent behaviors” and suspends market access for two systems pending remediation. While technical watchdogs welcome the move, developers accuse the EU of opacity and overreach. The enforcement action fractures relations further, with no unified compliance mechanism across member states.

Public sentiment dips slightly to 32.0 as concerns about AI governance persist, but no wave of local legal challenges or referenda materialises this turn. Labour unrest simmers, with mid-tier professionals citing AI-driven deskilling in public services, but no coordinated escalation occurs.

AI safety declines modestly to 15.0, reflecting the slow erosion of safeguards relative to capability growth, consistent with the fixed pace of world movement. No new frontier model release or systemic failure event accelerates risk dynamics beyond this gradual trend. Resilience sees a minor uptick from ongoing backchannel work, but remains uneven. EU AI sovereignty holds steady at 32.0, as capability growth is matched by proportional tracking in open-weight systems, with no unexplained divergence. Political capital remains unchanged at 5.0, as no new measure is launched and no major capital expenditure occurs.
