### The Core Cracks

The EU pressed ahead with the Sovereign AI Core Programme, deploying task forces to begin site preparation in Brittany, Sweden, and the Alps under emergency powers. Germany and Austria were offered co-auditing rights, but legal challenges persisted—no court has yet ruled, and no foundation was poured. Italy and Hungary rejected the Apennines node conditions outright, calling them coercive. No new compute came online.

Simultaneously, EAAD invoked emergency authority to mandate real-time agent trace access across critical infrastructure. Energy and finance operators resisted integration, citing operational risk and liability exposure. A major German grid operator refused API access, triggering a formal enforcement notice. The promised dashboard did not launch; technical disputes over data sanitization stalled deployment. ENISA reported negligible visibility.

Public sentiment declined further after a regional hospital’s AI triage system failed during a surge, leaving dozens without timely care. Protests grew in France and Spain. A second reinsurer followed the first in withdrawing from public health cyber coverage, amplifying procurement paralysis.

Meanwhile, a central European member state finalized a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler for exclusive access to next-generation AI training runs, bypassing EU coordination entirely. The agreement undercut Brussels’ leverage and exposed deep fragmentation. Markets interpreted it as proof that the Union could not enforce strategic alignment.

Closed AI capability advanced by 1.5 points, driven by clinical coding automation and internal lab tooling. Open-weight models rose to 52.0, matching the closed frontier of two years prior, now widely distributed. With no safety measure landing on deployed systems, and capability rising, ai_safety held flat despite tool development. Resilience eroded further due to unchecked systemic risks and weakened institutional trust.

Political capital collapsed under the weight of unmet promises, resistance, and the member-state defection. The Sovereign AI Core Programme remained the priority, but its momentum faltered. Investor confidence in EIB tranches tied to delivered capacity evaporated—the metrics showed no progress, only conflict.

### Fractured Authority
Sovereignty fell sharply, reflecting both the loss of coordinated access and stalled domestic build-out. The EU’s capacity to act independently weakened just as dependency risks crystallized.
