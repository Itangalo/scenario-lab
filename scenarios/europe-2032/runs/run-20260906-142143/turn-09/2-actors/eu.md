## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Sovereign AI infrastructure operational and under full Union control by turn 13``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The previous commitment to secure sovereign AI infrastructure with full operational control, energy autonomy, and real-time auditability by turn 7 has not been met due to legal resistance, member-state fragmentation, and implementation gridlock. The strategic imperative remains unchanged — without sovereign infrastructure, the Union cannot enforce safety, resilience, or political agency in AI — but the timeline must now reflect reality. The new commitment extends the deadline to turn 13, allowing time to overcome legal, technical, and political obstacles while maintaining pressure for delivery.

## New measure
**Measure:** Activate Joint Threat Response Protocol with EU Cyber Shield Activation  
Launch a binding, time-bound integration of EU critical infrastructure into the newly formed Joint Cyber Command, enabling real-time telemetry sharing, automated threat isolation, and coordinated countermeasures across energy, health, finance, and transport sectors.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: small  
Finishes on turn: 10  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 8–12 points through rapid incident containment and cross-border mutual aid; leverage joint threat response to build trust and cohesion among member states  
Applies to: ENISA, national CSIRTs, critical infrastructure operators, EU-level defence and civil protection agencies

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign AI Core Programme  
We are pushing hardest on the Sovereign AI Core Programme because, despite its failure to deliver physical infrastructure by turn 9, it remains the only instrument capable of breaking our dependency on foreign compute and restoring the Union’s capacity to act independently — without it, every other measure, including the new cyber shield, depends on permissions we cannot enforce and access we cannot guarantee.

## In practice
We are treating the Sovereign AI Core Programme’s technical completion this turn as a legal and political milestone, not an operational one. The programme ends its funding and implementation phase, but no compute is live, no node is online, and no audit trail exists. We are now shifting to damage control: invoking Article 122 TFEU to reframe the stalled build-out as a continuing emergency, allowing the Commission to bypass national objections in Brittany and northern Sweden by classifying both sites as strategic energy-security assets under EU energy solidarity rules. We are offering Italy and Hungary a revised Apennines node: full local employment guarantees, co-governance of the audit framework, and a sunset clause on data access — but only if they accept API-level logging by April. No audit, no access still stands — but we are now pairing it with tangible benefits.

Simultaneously, we are activating the Joint Cyber Shield under the Joint Threat Response agreement, using the recent cyber_major_incident as the catalyst. ENISA will coordinate with national CSIRTs to deploy automated telemetry gateways within 90 days, funded from the EIB’s emergency resilience tranche. The first phase connects grid operators and central banks; hospitals follow in turn 10. This is not optional: participation is tied to continued eligibility for EU cyber risk pooling. We are trading sovereignty we do not yet have for protection we cannot build alone — but doing so from a position of visible action, not paralysis. This is how we hold cohesion: by delivering something, somewhere, before the Union cracks.
