### A Fractured Inflection

The EU declares a strategic pivot, launching the Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme with binding energy allocations and redirecting SCAP funds to nuclear and geothermal AI infrastructure. Yet momentum falters: Germany and Austria escalate legal challenges, and ENTSO-E refuses to treat power ring-fencing as binding without unanimous consensus. The Commission presses ahead under Article 122 TFEU, but implementation remains partial, delaying physical deployment.

The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) becomes operational, certifying its first models using new interpretability tools. Automatic verification in contract and clinical coding is mandated for public-sector AI, a direct response to recent capability gains. However, real-time logging for critical systems stalls in COREPER II—Italy and Hungary reject audit access, weakening enforcement despite ENISA offers of technical support.

Public trust erodes further. Labour displacement accelerates in legal, accounting, and administrative sectors, with hiring frozen at entry levels. Protests by displaced professionals grow, amplified by media framing the EAAD as symbolic. A new communication push around Emergency Resilience Corridors fails to land, as the public sees no visible protection.

Pre-exercised cyber response protocols for finance, energy, and health were considered for fast-tracking, but interagency coordination delays and legal reservations prevent formal launch this turn. ENISA continues preparatory integration work, though full rollout remains years away.

AI capability crosses 60.0 as automated code generation and verification widen in law and engineering. Open-weight models see moderate gains, reaching 48.5, supported by increased EU access to non-US frontier weights amid tightening American export scrutiny—though this effect remains nascent and does not outpace global frontier advancement.

The US election concludes with a decisive anti-AI mandate. The incoming administration signals sweeping restrictions, moratoriums, and sector-funded job guarantees. The implications for global AI development will become clearer once the new administration takes office.
