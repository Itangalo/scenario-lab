## In practice
This turn, we are driving the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* through the final stages of the European Parliament’s legislative process, anchoring it firmly in Article 114 TFEU as an internal market measure to prevent fragmentation. The Legal Service has locked the red lines: no model above 60.0 capability operates in the EU without sovereign access to weights, real-time telemetry, and verified kill switches. We are preparing to invoke Article 155(2) if Parliament attempts to dilute these conditions under lobbying pressure — this is not a negotiable safeguard but a constitutional minimum for technological sovereignty.

ENISA, in coordination with national agencies in France, Germany, and the Netherlands, is expanding the pilot detection grid to monitor for inference signatures of unauthorized models. The first EAAN gateway in Brussels is now technically ready and will go live next turn, enabling initial certification runs. We are also advancing contingency planning under the DMA: legal teams are drafting the classification of certain foundation models as core digital services, which would bring them under existing gatekeeper obligations and allow enforcement through fines and operational restrictions. At the same time, we are offering a carrot: early-submitting labs gain fast-track audits under the revised AI Liability Directive and liability shielding for verified compliance, creating a clear path to market access for those willing to cooperate.

Behind the scenes, transatlantic tensions are rising. US hyperscalers have suspended new deployments in the EU, calling the rules extraterritorial and unenforceable. We are holding firm but not closing the door: a working group under the Trade and Technology Council will convene in December to explore mutual recognition of evaluation standards, though only if baseline sovereignty conditions are met. The message is consistent — we do not seek isolation, but we will not outsource control of systems that can reshape reality on our soil.
