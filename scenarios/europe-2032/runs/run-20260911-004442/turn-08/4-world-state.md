### A faster model, a longer night
Winter began with a demonstration that made last autumn's slides look stale. An American lab released an agent that planned across days, used tools without prompting, and wrote much of its own evaluation harness. Timelines quoted in Brussels the week before were obsolete overnight.

Weeks later the tooling turned. A largely automated intrusion swept public services in several member states — a tainted software component updated itself into municipalities, clinics and a rail ticketing backend. Screens went dark, appointments were lost, fallback paper returned. Technicians said the payload looked machine-written. Attribution will take months.

The containment system built in autumn held, but only just.

### Drills become operations
ENISA isolation orders went out over pooled dashboards, tenants were cut, patches pushed in three languages at once. Kill-switch rehearsals in three corridors became real rerouting. Hospitals degraded rather than stopped, yet rationed care returned in places that had just resumed. Officials claimed the lights stayed on because the autumn protocol existed; mayors answered with queues at town halls.

At the same time hiring stopped for graduates. Law firms, accountants, software houses and customer centres confirmed what staff already felt: entry posts were not being replaced. Ministers argued over numbers while young jobseekers saw empty inboxes.

### Bridge promised, shells waiting
The Commission answered with a wage bridge and retraining vouchers channelled through employment offices, with placement pilots in the five hit sectors and contributions tied to firms doing the automating. Money moved slowly; national offices complained about forms, employers about conditions.

The supply board kept ring-fencing remaining foreign chips for re-testing and clinics. Sites near Paris and Warsaw stayed permitted but empty, and power managers quietly curtailed computing loads to protect grids, drawing jeers from protest placards outside data-centre fences.

Voters saw decisive action arrive late twice — first agents no one could locate, now services no one could patch — and trust sank further.
