### The attack everyone expected
In February, automated ransomware swept through municipal services, hospital IT and two port logistics systems. The tooling was model-generated, the blast radius widened by an unpatched software dependency, and defenders spent weeks isolating systems. Emergency crews from the autumn drive were recalled, spares bought time, but clinics again went to paper. Attribution remained open into June.

Brussels put emergency coordination first. Daily technical guidance was published, mutual aid was activated, but there was no new shield to stand up and no money behind it. Coverage called the response orderly and behind.

### A capital goes its own way
Mid-spring, a member state signed its own compute and model-access arrangement with an outside supplier on preferential terms, undercutting the common Washington ask and the joint licensing line. The capital called it pragmatism; Paris called it a breach of solidarity.

The Commission opened a cohesion case, offered the joint terms as a return path and threatened to move joint evaluation without the holdout. The file stalled. The five reserved factory sites stayed reserved and unbuilt, permits kept warm with no cash.

### Paper leverage
The tech sovereignty package formally closed, its permitting zones and capital pledges booked as delivered. Bankers stayed absent. The middle-power compact became the only funded track, but with the Union unable to fund builds and unable to hold its line, polls read the half-year as managed dependence tipping into exposure.
