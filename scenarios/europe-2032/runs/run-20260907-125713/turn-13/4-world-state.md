### Frontier Acceleration and Openweight Lag

Global AI capability advances to 72.0, driven by autonomous engineering loops and multi-agent coordination now managing full product lifecycles in semiconductor design and logistics. The closed frontier pulls ahead sharply, compressing release cycles and embedding AI deeply into industrial innovation.

Despite urgent efforts to replicate gains, open-weight models remain constrained. A prototype matching key components of the new frontier capability emerges, but its deployment is fragmented and partial. GPU bottlenecks and export controls severely limit training and distribution; only mid-tier implementations in research and defensive cyber applications achieve partial parity. Openweight capability rises to 63.0, reflecting slow diffusion and infrastructure deficits—still trailing the frontier by 9.0 points.

### Cyber Catastrophe and Sovereignty Crisis

A sophisticated ransomware attack, leveraging AI-generated exploits, cripples emergency dispatch systems in three EU member states. The breach originated in a compromised software library, autonomously weaponized and adaptive to local configurations. Recovery takes weeks; public trust plummets. Resilience inches up slightly due to emergency interventions, but systemic fragility remains.

In response, the EU initiates planning for a resilience surge, mandating air-gapped backups, human-in-command protocols, and rapid red-teaming for critical infrastructure. ENISA begins drafting emergency toolkits, and discussions start on unlocking €4.1 billion for hardening. However, implementation is not yet approved—some member states resist, citing cost and complexity, and governance delays prevent swift action. The measure remains in preparation, not activation.

Meanwhile, the European Frontier Initiative struggles. “Prometheus,” the sovereign training run, remains offline as energy negotiations stall and GPU deliveries slip. A senior agency reroutes another high-priority workload to US cloud infrastructure, deepening institutional distrust in the EU stack. Sovereignty drops to 11.0: −1 from capability growth and no offset from in-flight measures.

Political capital declines to 8.0. The notepad reflects a carryover charge from prior unresolved measures (−3 for sovereign access, −3 for monitoring, −1 for priority) = −7. No new measure is added this turn; the resilience surge remains in early planning and does not enter the portfolio. The political cost reflects existing burdens and crisis response, not new overcommitment.

Public sentiment crashes to 9.0 amid visible chaos, job losses in public IT, and protests branding the EU’s strategy as both reckless and ineffective.

The US posture remains undetermined. Transition activities continue, but no commitments or policy directions have been formally established.
