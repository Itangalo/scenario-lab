## New measure  
**Establish emergency cyber resilience surge for critical public services**  
We are launching an immediate, time-bound hardening programme for public health, energy, and election infrastructure against AI-generated cyber threats, prioritising detection, isolation, and manual override capacity.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`

## Priority  
Re-establish sovereign access to frontier AI capability  
This remains our priority because without restoring credible, independent access to frontier capability, no oversight regime—no matter how robust—will be sovereign in practice; the EFI is the foundation on which all other control and resilience measures depend, and its credibility is under immediate political and institutional threat.

## In practice  
We are executing the European Frontier Initiative under Article 122 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act’s safeguard clause, with the European Commission empowered to override national competition objections and fast-track site permits, energy allocations, and export controls. The European Investment Bank has activated the €9.2 billion facility, with 60% allocated to GPU procurement through pre-negotiated contracts with three non-US suppliers, 25% to long-term energy commitments with Nordic and Iberian providers, and 15% to retention bonuses for key personnel drawn from both industry and public research. The Sovereign AI Core has suspended non-essential workloads, redirecting 6 exaF-days exclusively to the EFI training run, now codenamed “Prometheus.” ENISA and the European Artificial Intelligence Authority (EAAD) are embedded in the project with real-time logging, inspection rights, and veto authority over checkpoint releases—tools we are now preparing to extend through the new oversight measure.

But the emergent institutional defection to US cloud infrastructure has shaken confidence at the highest levels. Behind closed doors, several member states are questioning whether the EFI can deliver in time, and whether sovereignty means anything if our own agencies do not trust it. We are responding with a dual track: publicly reaffirming the EFI as the Union’s sole legitimate frontier access vector, while privately convening a crisis session of the Competitiveness Council to address continuity concerns. We are offering interim access to a hardened, audited subset of the Sovereign AI Core’s current capability—rated at 58.0—for critical national functions, conditioned on full logging and no external data transfer. This is not a concession to dependence, but a demonstration that sovereign systems can be both secure and usable. The openweight release at 62.0 only sharpens the stakes: the capability is already distributed, and if we cannot govern it here, we will not govern it at all.

Now, the cyber_major_incident forces our hand. A supply-chain compromise in a widely used municipal software library has triggered cascading ransomware activation across emergency services in three member states. The attack was adaptive, evading static signatures and exploiting zero-day logic flaws—hallmarks of AI-generated tooling. We are activating the EU Civil Protection Mechanism to coordinate response, but recovery is slow, and public trust is collapsing. In parallel, we are fast-tracking the new resilience measure: the Commission will issue a Temporary Crisis Directive under Article 114 TFEU, mandating AI-resistant authentication, air-gapped backups, and 24/7 human oversight for all systems classified as essential. The ENISA Rapid Response Team will deploy to national CIRTs with pre-positioned toolkits, and we are unlocking €4.1 billion from the Solidarity Fund for urgent upgrades. This is not a long-term fix—but it is the only way to stop the bleeding while Prometheus comes online.
