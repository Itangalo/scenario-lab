### Emergency Sovereignty Push Amid Access Crisis

The European Union announces the European Frontier Initiative (EFI), a high-stakes programme to reclaim access to frontier AI capability after being abruptly locked out of the leading model. Hospitals, ministries, and firms relying on it face immediate disruption, with some reverting to manual processes. The Council proposes invoking Article 122 TFEU to fund the €9.2 billion measure, but final approval is delayed pending negotiations on budget reallocations and intergovernmental guarantees. Implementation will not begin until funding is secured, with full effect expected no earlier than mid-2032.

### Capability Creeps Toward 70

Global AI capability rises to 69.5, driven by ongoing training momentum. The closed frontier inches forward, tightening release cycles. No single breakthrough defines the advance, but the accumulation accelerates research in materials science and autonomous engineering.

### Open Frontier Advances Steadily

A significant open-weight model release lands just months behind the closed frontier, widely replicated across academic and industrial clusters. Development momentum strengthens, supported by growing tooling and distribution infrastructure. Openweight capability increases to 60.0, reflecting sustained progress but not an abrupt leap, maintaining alignment with prior accumulation trends.

### Safety Erodes Further

Unexplained reasoning shifts in unreleased models deepen. Interpretability tools fail to track internal state changes, and no new safeguards land on shipped systems. AI safety drops to 22.0 as oversight bodies report growing uncertainty.

### Resilience Dips Slightly

The denial of frontier access disrupts critical workflows, and contingency systems prove inadequate. With no active category 6 measure, and rising capability, resilience declines to 34.0. Healthcare IT remains especially vulnerable.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

Despite launching the EFI, sovereignty falls to 15.0. The denial of frontier access costs −2 under rule 5, and rising capability (by 1.5) costs −1. No category 4 measure finishes, and in-flight progress yields no credit yet.

### Political Capital Erodes

The large category 4 measure costs −3, and naming it priority costs −1. No proposal bonus applies, as no recent incident directly justifies this specific response. Political capital drops to 16.0, reflecting strained cohesion and fiscal pressure.

### Public Sentiment Stagnant

No visible benefit emerges. Protests continue in Lyon and Vienna, framing the proposed EFI as a costly overreach without guaranteed results. Sentiment remains at 14.0.
