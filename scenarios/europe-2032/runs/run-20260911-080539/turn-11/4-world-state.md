### Paper that holds
July to December 2031 did not bring a single large blackout, but a grinding sequence of small ones. Grid operators in three member states isolated substation controllers, banks froze retail payments for hours, and two hospital groups reverted to handwritten triage sheets for a weekend. What changed was that the paper held.

Nurses, clerks and utility dispatchers had begun copying each other's fallback protocols — A month hospital, how run,,,,�ed., how to queue repairs without dispatch software, how to verify identity without the central register. The Civil Protection Mechanism, with no money to offer, endorsed the bundle as interim guidance and paid for photocopies and exercises. Crews rebuilt faster, and local press noticed that the lights came back in days, not weeks.

The Union's cyber recovery pact formally closed in autumn. Auditors counted hardened backups, exercised restoration playbooks, and joint telemetry feeds from the allied cyber command now flowing into ENISA dashboards. Operators say the drills are still thin — isolation switches that exist on slides, reserved supercomputing that takes days to allocate — but restoration is no longer improvised.

### Paychecks and chip anxiety
Brussels tried to turn that fragile competence into livelihoods. Employment ministries launched wage bridges and placement vouchers for frozen-out graduates and manual-operation staff, asking automating firms to co-fund retraining in exchange for computing access and procurement points. Uptake was uneven: German and French employment agencies paid out quickly, southern agencies stalled on eligibility rules, employers complained about the levy.

Public mood lifted only marginally. Therapy wards still dispensed cures, and the fact that hospitals kept running won grudging credit. But graduate hiring remained frozen, fraud and faked video dominated evening news, and the newest frontier systems — now reasoning in compressed traces no auditor can read — deepened distrust.

Over it all hung shipping news. Extended naval manoeuvres around Taiwan pushed insurance premia up and revived talk of chip stoppages. No embargo came, no cables were cut, but finance ministries modelled shortages and industry lobbies warned that Europe's access to leading models and advanced chips could narrow without warning.
