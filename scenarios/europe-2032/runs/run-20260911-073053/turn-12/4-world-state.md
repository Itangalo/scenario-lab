### Permission becomes politics
The spring began with a demonstration from an American lab that made last year's roadmaps look quaint. Benchmarks were abandoned overnight; ministerial briefing packs were rewritten. Within weeks Washington put the labs themselves under direct federal control — cleared officers inside training runs, weights treated as defence articles, foreign customers approved case by case.

For Europe the commercial ground vanished. Conformity assessments, market access threats, data-act levers — all assumed a company on the other side. Now there was a security apparatus. Volume licences for medical inference kept flowing, but each renewal arrived as a political favour, stamped and conditional.

### Cures on someone else's machine
That stung because the cures were real. Tailored cancer therapies and rapid triage models cut waiting lists in pilot hospitals in Lyon, Rotterdam and Krakow. Nurses spoke of wards clearing for the first time in years. Yet every dose plan was computed on licensed inference that Brussels could not inspect, reroute or guarantee. Gratitude mixed with humiliation in the press.

At the same time, graduate hiring froze across law, accountancy, software and customer operations. Firms blamed the new agents openly. Student protests merged with farmer convoys around data-centre sites.

### Holding the line
The Continuity Shield, formally closed in January, was stretched informally: guarded repair crews, cross-border failover nursed through another cable fault, rationed clinics steered toward the allied medical trickle. The middle-power club delivered spare optics and shared compute, but no checkpoints, no chips.

A new state-to-state channel was mandated — envoys shuttling to Washington offering continued closure of landings and cleanrooms to military use, pooled maintenance for lithography tools, in exchange for certified medical licences. Talks began, slowly. Clearance costs slowed even the Americans, but Europe felt it as dependence, not pause. By June the lights held, the cures flowed, and control had moved further away.
