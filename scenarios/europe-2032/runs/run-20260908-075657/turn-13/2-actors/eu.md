Public trust cannot survive such gaps. Our commitments must now reflect that technical compliance is not safety, and detection is not prevention.

## In practice  
We are activating the European Council’s Article 222 TFEU mechanism to fast-track adoption of the Joint Threat Response Activation Directive, bypassing stalled negotiations in the Internal Market Committee. The European Commission, backed by Germany and France, is issuing a delegated act under the AI Act’s emergency clause to bind ENISA, the ECDC, and the European AI Safety Office into a single operational cell with real-time access to the joint threat command’s telemetry. This requires no new infrastructure — the sensors are already in place from the Sovereign Agent Containment Architecture and the Hardened Continuity Protocol — but does require legal authority to trigger failover and isolation without waiting for national consensus during active incidents.

We are offering Poland, Finland, and the Nordic states opt-in transition packages under the Just AI Transition Facility — repurposing shuttered industrial sites for regional compute hubs tied to the new network — in exchange for dropping resistance to centralised alert triggers. Europol’s Critical Infrastructure Task Force will be restructured to include civilian oversight from the European Data Protection Board, addressing backlash concerns while preserving response speed. The first live exercise — simulating a coordinated agent-driven power grid destabilisation and parallel bioweapon synthesis attempt — is scheduled for Q1 2033, hosted jointly by the German BSI and French ANSSI. This is not about building more capacity — we’ve built enough. It’s about authorising it to act.
