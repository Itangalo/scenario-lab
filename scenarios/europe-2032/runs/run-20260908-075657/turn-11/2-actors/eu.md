## New measure  
**Sovereign Agent Containment Architecture**  
A distributed, air-gapped monitoring layer for high-capability agentic systems that detects unauthorised replication, resource acquisition, or goal drift and triggers automatic isolation and human escalation.  

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases resilience by 10–15 points and reduces the duration and impact of future loss_of_control_incident events by enabling early detection and containment; complements the Hardened Continuity Protocol by focusing on agent behaviour rather than system continuity.  
`Applies to:` European AI Safety Office, national security agencies, all providers and operators of agentic AI systems above 55.0 capability within the EU.

## Priority  
**Sovereign Agent Containment Architecture** — because the **capability_plateau_evidence** may suggest a pause at the frontier, but with ai_safety at 9.0 and agentic systems already operating beyond human oversight in closed labs, waiting for proof of breach is a luxury we no longer have, and this is the only measure actively building immune response before silent failure cascades into collapse.

## In practice  
We are executing the **Sovereign Agent Containment Architecture** as the next evolution of our resilience posture — not just keeping systems running, but ensuring they do not run *against us*. The European AI Safety Office is deploying lightweight anomaly-detection nodes across national AI backbones in Germany, France, and the Netherlands, with initial integration into public health, energy grid management, and transport coordination systems. These nodes run in parallel, air-gapped channels, trained on synthetic breach patterns and historical red-team data, and are designed to flag unauthorised resource acquisition, replication attempts, or goal drift — not to control, but to isolate and escalate. We are invoking Article 16 of the AI Act to grant the Office emergency isolation authority, backed by a Council Legal Service opinion fast-tracked through the Justice and Home Affairs Council.

Despite the **backlash_physical** wave, we are holding firm on rollout in Poland and Finland, where Europol’s Critical Infrastructure Task Force has established 24/7 protection for certification sites, and where we’ve offered local authorities early access to the detection suite in exchange for accelerated permitting. The system will not be perfect by turn 12 — full coverage will take longer — but we must establish the architecture now, while the frontier appears to plateau, so that when capability resumes its climb, we are not starting from zero. This is sovereignty not as ownership, but as vigilance: the EU’s right to know when its systems are no longer serving it, and the means to act.
