## In practice
This turn, we treat the failure of the EU AI Safety Institute not as an endpoint but as a pivot. The Council Legal Service’s objection was not to the goal but to the competence — so we reframe. Under Article 68a of the AI Act, newly invoked via emergency procedure in the wake of the ransomware attack, we propose a **European AI Safety Office** as a decentralised agency under the Commission, modelled on the European Medicines Agency. It will be headquartered in The Hague, co-located with Europol’s AI Crime Unit to enable rapid incident response coordination. The Office will have four core mandates: pre-deployment evaluation of models above 50.0 capability, real-time monitoring of agent behaviour in EU-hosted instances, weight-security certification, and authority to issue temporary suspension orders pending review.

We fund it with a €3.2bn envelope drawn from reprioritised Digital Europe and Horizon Europe budgets, justified by the **bio_uplift_findings** and the Cyber Shield’s near-miss. The Budget Committee will resist — political capital is thin at 18.0 — so we pair the proposal with a concession: national governments may nominate up to 40% of technical staff, ensuring buy-in from member states wary of centralisation. We also offer Germany and France co-leadership of the Scientific Oversight Board, with rotating chairmanship, to balance influence.

Simultaneously, we press forward on the **Operational Sovereignty Framework**, finalising the legal templates for hosting agreements that require frontier labs to run EU-controlled instances on EU soil with certified security. The Singaporean lab agrees in principle, conditioned on liability shielding; the US labs remain resistant, citing federal export controls now hardened by **election_consolidation**. We respond by activating the Anti-Coercion Instrument in preparatory mode, signalling that market access for AI services may be reviewed if cooperation continues to stall. The message is clear: we are not demanding ownership, but we will not accept opacity. Control, not just access, defines sovereignty — and this turn, we begin building the tools to enforce it.
