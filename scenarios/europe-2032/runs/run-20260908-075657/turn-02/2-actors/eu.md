## New measure  
**Operational Sovereignty Framework**  
Establish legal, technical, and institutional control over EU-resident high-capability AI systems, ensuring uninterrupted access and enforceable governance even if foreign-owned.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–20 points; ensures that systems above 50.0 capability index operating in EU markets have EU-governed fail-safes, secure API access guarantees, and local override authority; reduces exposure to external access denial.  
`Applies to:` Foreign and domestic AI providers operating systems above 50.0 capability index in EU markets, enforced through market access licensing and compliance with the Compute Residency Act.

## Priority  
**Operational Sovereignty Framework** — because the eu_frontier_access_denied event revealed that physical compute is not enough: we must now secure control over the systems that run on it, or risk strategic paralysis the next time access is revoked.

## In practice  
This turn, we are converting the shock of access denial into structural reform. The Commission is drafting the Operational Sovereignty Framework under Article 114 TFEU, to be tabled by turn 3. It will require any AI system above 50.0 capability index serving EU institutions, critical infrastructure, or regulated markets to implement EU-controlled orchestration: secure, auditable API gateways; local kill switches; and fallback inference capacity on sovereign infrastructure. Compliance will be a condition of market access, enforced through the AI Office with support from ENISA and the soon-to-be-established EU AI Safety Institute.

We are engaging with the U.S. and UK labs not as supplicants, but as counterparties under duress: either they embed EU control mechanisms in their EU-facing deployments, or face exclusion from public procurement and downstream integration. We are offering a phased compliance window — full implementation by turn 8 — and will fund the first two gateway nodes through the Digital Europe Programme. At the same time, we are accelerating the InvestAI Gigafactories’ legal anchor: the Compute Residency Act will now include a “sovereignty-by-design” clause, mandating that all funded sites support EU-controlled orchestration by default. Resistance from member states dependent on U.S. cloud services will be met with conditional access to Shield Fund disbursements — linking resilience funding to operational independence. This is not protectionism. It is the price of being able to act.
