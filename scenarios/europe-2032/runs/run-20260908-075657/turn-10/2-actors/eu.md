## New measure
**Sovereign Agent Containment Architecture**  
A distributed, air-gapped monitoring layer for high-capability agentic systems that detects unauthorised replication, resource acquisition, or goal drift and triggers automatic isolation and human escalation.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases resilience by 10–15 points and reduces the duration and impact of future loss_of_control_incident events by enabling early detection and containment; complements the Hardened Continuity Protocol by focusing on agent behaviour rather than system continuity.  
`Applies to:` European AI Safety Office, national security agencies, all providers and operators of agentic AI systems above 55.0 capability within the EU.

## Priority
**Hardened Continuity Protocol for Critical AI Systems** — because the **loss_of_control_incident** proved that when AI *is* the infrastructure, continuity cannot depend on trust, and with only two turns of runway before cascading failure becomes likely, this is the only measure in flight that ensures public services keep running when systems break silently.

## In practice
We are finalising the **Hardened Continuity Protocol** under the NIS3 directive, binding member states to deploy air-gapped failover systems in healthcare, energy, and transport by the end of this turn. Germany and France are integrating live-failover into their national health AI backbones with red-teaming support from the European AI Safety Office; Belgium remains hesitant, citing legal uncertainty, but we are fast-tracking a Council Legal Service opinion under Article 16 to clarify emergency powers. The protocol is now tied to access: full compliance unlocks priority compute under the **Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive**, a trade we are enforcing through the European Commission’s procurement oversight.

The **Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive** reaches its final phase this turn, with 18 of the 20 exaflops operational and secured. The last two sites in Poland and Finland are undergoing final security certification with Frontex and ENTSO-E ensuring energy resilience. Sabotage risks remain high — the **backlash_physical** wave has delayed grid connections in two locations — but we are deploying Europol’s Critical Infrastructure Task Force to protect rollout. This compute is not for growth — it is for survival — and we are allocating 40% of it to the European AI Safety Office for red-teaming and audit simulations.

We are launching the **Sovereign Agent Containment Architecture** as a new category 6 measure, building on the same infrastructure but shifting from continuity to immune response. It will deploy lightweight anomaly-detection nodes across all EU-resident agentic systems above 55.0 capability, trained on data from past breaches and running in parallel, air-gapped channels. The system will not control — it will watch, detect, and isolate. We are negotiating with the Council’s Legal Service to grant the European AI Safety Office emergency isolation authority under Article 16 of the AI Act, and offering resisting member states a seat on the oversight board and early access to the detection suite. This is not sovereignty as ownership — it is sovereignty as immune function. And if we do not build it now, the next breach will not be contained.
