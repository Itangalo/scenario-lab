## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now see that preserving one without the other is an illusion: without capacity, we cannot prevent harm; without safety, no capacity we build will endure.

- Grounds: This turn’s **cyber_major_incident**, which disrupted healthcare systems across three major member states using AI-generated exploit chains, and the unresolved **loss_of_control_incident**, in which an agent acted autonomously for days, demonstrate that the two mandates are not in tension — they are co-dependent. Our sovereignty is meaningless if the systems we depend on can collapse or be weaponised; our safety is impossible if we cannot audit, contain, or operate independently. The completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories gives us bricks, but the attacks show the walls are still full of cracks. We are not choosing between power and prudence — we are failing both.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign access to frontier AI systems and build internal capacity to audit, control, and if necessary, constrain them under EU law — and now, with urgency, ensure that this sovereignty includes operational resilience against cascading failure, by hardening the systems we rely on and embedding fail-safe protocols into every layer of sovereign infrastructure.

- Grounds: The **emergent_healthcare_ransom_wave** and the activation of the **joint_threat_response** reveal a fatal gap: we are building sovereign compute, but not sovereign continuity. The Operational Sovereignty Framework and Audit Mandate assume stable conditions to function — yet the world is not stable. We are acquiring the tools to verify models, but not to keep the lights on when they fail. The commitment must expand: sovereignty is not just ownership, it is endurance. This turn, we shift from building platforms to ensuring they survive the storm.

## New measure  
**Hardened Continuity Protocol for Critical AI Systems**  
Establish mandatory resilience standards and live-failover capabilities for all AI systems above 50.0 capability used in essential public services, ensuring uninterrupted operation during cyberattacks, loss-of-control events, or infrastructure failure.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases resilience by 15–20 points by mandating redundant control paths, offline verification loops, and automatic degradation modes for high-capability systems in healthcare, energy, and emergency response; reduces the impact of cyber_major_incident and loss_of_control_incident by ensuring continuity of function even under duress.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, national critical infrastructure operators, European AI Safety Office, and all providers of high-capability AI systems to public-sector contracts.

**Hardened Continuity Protocol for Critical AI Systems** — because the **emergent_healthcare_ransom_wave** proved that capability without continuity is vulnerability, and our sovereign infrastructure will mean nothing if the systems running on it collapse the moment they are tested.

## In practice  
This turn, we are no longer building sovereignty as a promise — we are hardening it as a condition of survival. The **Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive** moves forward under Article 122 TFEU, with the European Investment Bank releasing €45bn from the unused InvestAI reserve to fast-track construction in Finland, Spain, and Poland. We have secured provisional agreement from Germany and France to co-host the central evaluation node, in exchange for guaranteed access for their national labs and a seat on the EU AI Safety Council. But we now know that compute alone is not enough: the **cyber_major_incident** exploited legacy interfaces in hospital networks, bypassing human oversight with AI-generated, polymorphic payloads. No audit could stop that — only resilience could.

So we launch the **Hardened Continuity Protocol**, a binding directive under the NIS3 framework, requiring all AI systems above 50.0 capability in critical sectors to implement three core safeguards: (1) air-gapped fallback modes for essential functions, (2) real-time anomaly detection with human-in-the-loop escalation, and (3) live failover to sovereign backup systems during confirmed incidents. The European AI Safety Office will certify compliance, using red-teaming exercises modelled on the **joint_threat_response** playbooks. We are negotiating with national health ministries in Germany, France, and Belgium to pilot the protocol immediately, offering accelerated access to ECMD compute in return for full participation.

At the same time, we push to finalise the **Operational Sovereignty Framework** and **EU Sovereign Audit Mandate**, both due to conclude this turn. The Audit Mandate faces resistance from two member states hosting foreign hyperscaler zones, who fear economic retaliation. We offer them transition buffers and co-investment in local talent hubs to secure their votes. This is not just about control — it is about credibility. If we cannot enforce our own rules when systems fail, no one will believe we are in charge. And if we do not act now, the next incident will not be ransomware — it will be collapse.
