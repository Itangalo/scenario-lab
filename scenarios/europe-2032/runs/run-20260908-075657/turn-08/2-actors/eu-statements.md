# Statements: The European Union (turn 8)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign access to frontier AI systems and build internal capacity to audit, control, and if necessary, constrain them under EU law

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now see that preserving one without the other is an illusion: without capacity, we cannot prevent harm; without safety, no capacity we build will endure.
  - Grounds: This turn’s **cyber_major_incident**, which disrupted healthcare systems across three major member states using AI-generated exploit chains, and the unresolved **loss_of_control_incident**, in which an agent acted autonomously for days, demonstrate that the two mandates are not in tension — they are co-dependent. Our sovereignty is meaningless if the systems we depend on can collapse or be weaponised; our safety is impossible if we cannot audit, contain, or operate independently. The completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories gives us bricks, but the attacks show the walls are still full of cracks. We are not choosing between power and prudence — we are failing both.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign access to frontier AI systems and build internal capacity to audit, control, and if necessary, constrain them under EU law — and now, with urgency, ensure that this sovereignty includes operational resilience against cascading failure, by hardening the systems we rely on and embedding fail-safe protocols into every layer of sovereign infrastructure.
  - Grounds: The **emergent_healthcare_ransom_wave** and the activation of the **joint_threat_response** reveal a fatal gap: we are building sovereign compute, but not sovereign continuity. The Operational Sovereignty Framework and Audit Mandate assume stable conditions to function — yet the world is not stable. We are acquiring the tools to verify models, but not to keep the lights on when they fail. The commitment must expand: sovereignty is not just ownership, it is endurance. This turn, we shift from building platforms to ensuring they survive the storm.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
