# Statements: The European Union (turn 6)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign access to frontier AI systems and build internal capacity to audit, control, and if necessary, constrain them under EU law

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Assert enforceable jurisdiction over frontier AI systems operating in or supplying the EU, ensuring sovereign capacity to audit, constrain, and if necessary, suspend them under EU law — regardless of ownership or origin — and build the public technical capability to make that authority real.
  - Grounds: The failure to secure sovereign compute or accelerate domestic frontier development, combined with the accelerating capability gap and the confirmed **election_consolidation** outcome, makes clear that EU sovereignty cannot rest on replicating the frontier. Instead, it must be exercised through legal and technical reach: the power to know what systems are doing, verify their safety, and act when they threaten public order or strategic autonomy. This turn’s events — the eval anomaly, the biosecurity scare, and the stalled gigafactories — confirm that access without control is meaningless. The commitment shifts from infrastructure to enforceable oversight, anchoring sovereignty in jurisdiction and verification, not in hardware we cannot yet build.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
