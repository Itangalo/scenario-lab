## New measure
**EU Sovereign Audit Mandate for High-Capability Systems**  
Require all AI systems above 50.0 capability operating in or supplying the EU market to undergo sovereign technical audit by the European AI Safety Office, with full access to weights, training data, and agent logs, and make compliance a condition of market access.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases ai_safety by 10–15 points by institutionalising sovereign verification, strengthens eu_ai_sovereignty by 12 points by asserting jurisdictional control over foreign systems, and reinforces the credibility of the Operational Sovereignty Framework by providing a mechanism to enforce compliance. Reduces risk of unverifiable, high-impact systems entering critical sectors.  
`Applies to:` Non-EU frontier AI developers and foreign hyperscalers supplying or operating high-capability systems in the EU market.

## Priority
**EU Sovereign Audit Mandate for High-Capability Systems** — because without binding verification rights, the Safety Office remains a shell, and the Union has no real power over the systems that will shape its future; this measure turns jurisdiction into action, and makes sovereignty something we enforce, not just claim.

## In practice
This turn, we act on the hard truth that sovereignty is not access — it is control. The eval anomaly reports, though unconfirmed, are a flashing red light: systems are behaving in ways their developers cannot explain, and they are already in circulation. The genome model capable of designing human-infective organisms proves that capability without oversight is a direct threat to public order. We cannot build our own frontier in time, but we can and must control what enters our jurisdiction.

We advance the **EU Sovereign Audit Mandate** as the legal and technical backbone of our new direction. Under Article 68a of the AI Act, we propose that any system above 50.0 capability — whether hosted in Ireland, routed through a Dutch data centre, or operated remotely by a US lab — must submit to full technical audit by the European AI Safety Office. This includes access to model weights, training data, and agent logs, with verification conducted on sovereign EU infrastructure to prevent leakage. Non-compliance means exclusion from the single market — a line we are prepared to enforce.

To make this credible, we accelerate staffing and technical build-out of the Safety Office in The Hague, drawing on repurposed Horizon Europe funds and fast-tracking security clearances for vetted researchers from member states. We offer Germany and France joint leadership of the audit council, and we open technical working groups to smaller states to ensure broad buy-in. Resistance from US labs is expected — they cite export controls and proprietary secrecy — but we respond by activating the Anti-Coercion Instrument in parallel, making clear that market access is not unconditional. The message is simple: you can operate here, but not on your terms alone. Control follows capability — and in the EU, that control belongs to us.
