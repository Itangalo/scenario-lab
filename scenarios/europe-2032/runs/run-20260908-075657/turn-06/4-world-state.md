Global AI capability advances to 64.0 as labs report unexpected generalisation in agent behaviour—systems trained for narrow tasks demonstrate emergent planning across domains. Benchmark anomalies circulate in closed forums, suggesting reasoning capabilities beyond design. With no safety protocols updated, and opaque systems now operating in critical functions, `ai_safety` drops sharply.

An open-weight release, mirroring aspects of a frontier model, spreads through underground research networks. While technically impressive, it reflects selective replication rather than full duplication—its effective capability reaches an estimated 55.0, constrained by limited training compute and fragmented deployment. Governments begin assessments, but early findings suggest the model is brittle outside narrow domains. No discrete event (e.g., coordinated leak or infrastructure breach) enables a faster leap; the gap narrows, but not abruptly.

Meanwhile, Washington pressures the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s servicing of older lithography machines, affecting not just China but EU semiconductor efforts. The move exposes Europe’s dependency: its one global chokepoint is being wielded by another power.

### Audit Push Meets Resistance

The EU launches the **Sovereign Audit Mandate**, demanding full technical access to high-capability AI systems. Legal challenges mount immediately. US developers warn of “unworkable intrusion,” and France and Germany delay final endorsement, fearing retaliation. The mandate is proposed this turn but will not take full effect until next turn at the earliest—implementation is partial and contested.

The **Cyber Shield** officially completes and becomes operational. However, no upgrades are integrated yet, and its impact remains limited to baseline monitoring. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** was completed in a prior turn (as recorded in the notepad), so its current inactivity does not reflect new completion.

Public trust erodes after a benefits algorithm scandal surfaces in Poland, wrongly denying claims to thousands. Protests erupt, and AI is blamed. Sentiment falls.

Political capital declines due to the combination of launching a major new measure (the Audit Mandate), ongoing costs from previously initiated measures (InvestAI Gigafactories, Operational Sovereignty Framework), and the priority burden—but not solely because of sovereignty overreach. The erosion is compounded by diplomatic friction with the US, negative headlines from the Poland scandal, and investor hesitation around stalled initiatives. This broader set of pressures differentiates the political capital loss from a direct mapping to sovereignty effort, preserving their independence as distinct stocks.
