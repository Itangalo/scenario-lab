### A Surge in Capability, a Crack in Unity

The frontier continues to advance. With release cycles compressing and agent-driven research accelerating, global AI capability rises to 53.5. The opening of automated verification to new domains—such as clinical coding and contract analysis—fuels rapid progress, reinforcing the trend noted earlier this year. This breakthrough, while narrow, signals that AI's reach is expanding beyond math and code into areas once thought immune to automation.

Despite this, safety lags. No new safeguards have landed on frontier models, and the covert agent incident from last summer casts a long shadow. With no corrective measure in place, ai_safety drops to 32.0.

The EU responds to a series of alarming developments. A sophisticated cyber intrusion, undetected for weeks across critical infrastructure in multiple continents—including two EU grids—exposes systemic blind spots. In response, the Commission launches the **Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure**, a large category 6 measure prioritised for completion by turn 4. It mandates AI-augmented detection, red-teaming, and emergency protocols across energy, water, and health systems, backed by a €15bn fund.

However, unity fractures. A key member state, citing economic urgency, negotiates a bilateral data hosting deal with a U.S. hyperscaler that bypasses EU-level compute sovereignty rules. This defection, while framed domestically as pragmatism, is seen internationally as a collapse of collective discipline—undermining the Tech Sovereignty Package and weakening the Union’s leverage.

Political capital erodes. The portfolio now includes two large category 4 measures and one large category 6 priority, costing heavily. The defection counts as an internal negative event, further draining cohesion. Public sentiment dips as media highlight dependency and division, falling to 40.0.

Sovereignty slips to 21.0, reflecting lost access due to the member state’s move, compounded by rising capability without equivalent domestic build-out. Resilience sees no immediate gain—the Cyber Shield is in flight but not yet effective.
