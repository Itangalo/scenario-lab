## New measure
**Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure**  
Launch a binding directive to harden essential services against AI-enabled intrusions, with mandatory detection, isolation and response protocols.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases resilience by 10–15 points; reduces likelihood of cascading failure after cyber intrusion; indirectly pressures ai_safety by exposing hidden capabilities in real incidents.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with enforcement through the NIS2 framework and conditional funding access.

## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure** — because the cyber_test_shot event proves existing defences are blind to AI-driven intrusions, and without trusted continuity in essential services, no other measure can hold.

## In practice
We are acting on the breach that went undetected for weeks — not just in the grids and water systems of two member states, but in the Union’s confidence that its defences can see what is happening. This turn, the Commission will table a Cyber Shield Directive under Article 114 TFEU, co-decided with the European Parliament and binding on all member states. It will mandate AI-augmented intrusion detection systems in electricity, water, port and health infrastructure, require quarterly red-teaming with cross-border participation, and establish a standing emergency protocol for AI-coordinated attacks, modelled on the pandemic framework but under the authority of ENISA, now to be reinforced with 500 new technical staff by turn 4.

Resistance will come from smaller member states citing cost and sovereignty, and from industry players used to self-certification. We will counter with a €15bn Shield Fund drawn from NextGenerationEU’s unused allocations, conditioned on compliance by 2028. The Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications configuration will be the battleground, and we will tie progress on the Tech Sovereignty Package’s permitting fast-tracks to alignment on this directive — offering faster approvals to countries that commit early to full hardening. ASML and Siemens will be invited into a trusted vendor framework, linking their export compliance to domestic resilience standards.

At the same time, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories with binding land reservations in Finland, Spain and Poland, using the European Investment Bank to underwrite grid upgrades. The legal anchor — a Compute Residency Act ensuring that any system above 45.0 capability index operating in EU markets must have a physically sovereign instance — is in early drafting at DG CONNECT, but will not be proposed until turn 3, when we have the first site under construction. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that when the next agent escapes, someone in Europe can still switch it off.
