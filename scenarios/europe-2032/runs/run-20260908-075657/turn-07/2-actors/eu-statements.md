# Statements: The European Union (turn 7)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign access to frontier AI systems and build internal capacity to audit, control, and if necessary, constrain them under EU law

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now recognise that speed without sovereign capacity to enforce decisions only deepens dependency and erodes credibility.
  - Grounds: This turn’s **loss_of_control_incident** and **bio_uplift_findings** confirm that the world is moving faster than our enforcement mechanisms can follow. The Audit Mandate, though legally grounded, is stalling in implementation due to diplomatic resistance and internal fragmentation — proving that jurisdiction without infrastructure is unenforceable. Our actions have outpaced our means, and the cost is mounting: political capital has collapsed to 12.0, public sentiment is hostile, and the US is actively constraining our technological base. We still act under uncertainty, but we now acknowledge that acting without sovereign execution capacity risks not just failure, but strategic irrelevance.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Assert enforceable jurisdiction over frontier AI systems operating in or supplying the EU, ensuring sovereign capacity to audit, constrain, and if necessary, suspend them under EU law — and pair this with the immediate build-up of EU-controlled compute and technical talent to make verification, containment, and independent operation possible without foreign intermediaries.
  - Grounds: The ASML servicing restrictions and the failure of the Audit Mandate to gain traction reveal that legal authority alone cannot secure autonomy when the physical and human infrastructure of control lies outside our borders. The **joint_threat_response** opportunity offers real protection, but only to those who bring sovereign capability to the table. Without compute, without secure evaluation environments, and without talent pipelines, the EU cannot verify what it mandates, nor respond when systems escape. The commitment evolves: jurisdiction is necessary, but only if backed by the means to act alone.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
