## In practice  
This turn, we are racing to finalise the Cyber Shield directive before the Council adjourns in December. The legal text is in final review by COREPER II, with the Presidency brokering compromises on enforcement timelines for healthcare and energy sectors. We are offering member states early access to the EU’s emerging threat-sharing platform — fed by ENISA’s new AI-enabled intrusion detection pilots — in exchange for dropping demands to weaken the isolation protocols. The Digital Europe Programme will disburse €1.8bn in pre-allocated Shield Fund tranches to national agencies that certify baseline compliance by Q1 2028, creating a pull mechanism that bypasses slow central coordination.

Simultaneously, we are launching the EU AI Safety Institute as a de facto authority, not just a research body. Under Article 16 of the AI Act, we are issuing a delegated act to grant it provisional audit powers over model weights and training logs for systems above 50.0, starting with those subject to the Operational Sovereignty Framework. We are recruiting from the academic interpretability community and former lab safety leads, offering fast-track security clearances and access to sovereign compute clusters. This is not symbolic: we will publish redacted audit summaries quarterly, starting in Q1 2028, to force transparency where developers resist it. The backlash from providers is expected, but the **emergent_bio_uplift_findings** give us cover — and the falling cost of capability means we may have a narrowing window to act before open-weight systems reach the same threshold.
