### Cures hold, code escapes, control gets testable
The second half of 2032 was defined by medicine working and models leaking.

The Trusted Cures programme finished its first procurement cycle. University hospitals in France, Germany, Spain and the Nordics moved tailored infusions and validated diagnostic agents onto certified European hosting, with provenance checks and local data custody. Waiting lists shortened. For families who got a prescription that had not existed a year earlier, Brussels earned rare credit. Mayors who had fought data-centre siting now cut ribbons on infusion wards.

At the same moment, reassurance on safety evaporated. A new openly downloadable system landed within months of the best closed systems. Hundreds of thousands of copies spread in days to private servers, clinics, consultancies and basements. Whatever containment the municipal shield offered, the capability itself was now unrecallable.

Against that, two technical results mattered. Laboratories in Europe demonstrated hospital-validated diagnostic agents that held up under independent checking — specialists called the validation protocol a landmark because results could be scored automatically against outcomes. Separately, researchers produced an interpretability and control advance that worked on shipped systems, not demos: behaviour that could be predicted and certified rather than argued over. Labs adopted it quickly because it reduced their own incident load.

Brussels tried to bolt the second result onto the third reality. The AI Office and ENISA re-tasked certification teams to push the new control tooling onto already-deployed hospital and municipal systems, making it a condition for continued procurement. Adoption was uneven. Large hospital federations complied for fast-track recertification; smaller communes complained of audit backlogs and frozen updates, and a payroll freeze repeated in one district.

Diplomacy disappointed. Envoys seeking real verification access to the Washington-Beijing understanding on secured weights and bio-tools returned with briefings, not inspection rights. Editorials that had praised the cures turned sharp: Europe could dispense, diagnose and patch, but still could not produce the underlying capacity on its own terms.

Trust rose from rock bottom, but remained anxious and divided.
