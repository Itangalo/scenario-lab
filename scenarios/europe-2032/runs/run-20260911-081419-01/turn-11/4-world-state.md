### Dark screens, then patch trucks
Autumn brought another largely automated assault on town halls, hospitals and a widely used contractor update. Appointment systems froze, registries went manual, and in districts without isolation gates payment desks stayed dark for weeks. Where gates and backups held, crews restored in days. Attribution slipped away into months of contradictory claims, but every forensic note pointed to machine-written tooling.

This time the clean-up crews carried something new in use, not new in mandate. A defensive advance in automated patching and swarm-behaviour detection — proven first in bank and telecom networks — was rushed into municipal use as a widened deployment scope of the existing EU Sovereign Fallback Stack (M11). No new measure is proposed this turn, and no M12 or other new measure is introduced. M11 remains the sole priority. French and Dutch containment teams that had spent winter mopping up stayed on to install patch agents and isolation gates, with recovery payouts made conditional on telemetry-sharing and deployment. ENISA inspectors certified the tooling; autumn fallback drills became live rollout tests.

It worked unevenly. Large cities and connected hospitals stabilized quickly; smaller communes struggled with staffing, false positives that blocked legitimate updates, and resentment of Brussels-mandated software. Containment held, but confidence did not return. The EU Sovereign Fallback Stack (M11) remains in flight and is not yet finished — deployment widened this turn, pushing full completion out, with no completion boost claimed this turn.

### Money leaves, cranes stop
While engineers patched, finance fled. Valuations across AI firms reset hard. Announced data-centre expansions were cancelled, not delayed, and two co-financing arrangements European compute plans had counted on evaporated. The single gigafactory under construction slowed, EuroHPC partitions reserved for public-interest inference stayed reserved but half-empty. A landmark result — machine-assisted proof work cracking a long-open combinatorial problem, published from a US-European collaboration — made headlines in science pages and changed nothing in council budgets.

Brussels chose hardening over building. No new money was asked for, because none would have been given. Digital Europe cyber lines were reprogrammed by cutting and delaying planned compute build-out, emergency cover was stretched thinner over municipalities, and health buyers were steered to European-hosted models for the patch controllers. The trade-off was explicit: patch rollout now, slowed sovereign build later.

The public noticed the outages more than the fix. Town-hall anger remained raw, trust in automated systems stayed near rock bottom, though nurses in patched hospitals quietly noted systems that stayed up.
