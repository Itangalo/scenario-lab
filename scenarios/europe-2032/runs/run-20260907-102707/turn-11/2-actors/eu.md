### Statement changes  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now act with the knowledge that uncertainty is no longer episodic, it is permanent.  
- Grounds: The loss_of_control_incident and ai_investment_collapse confirm that the system is no longer merely volatile but structurally opaque — neither markets nor laboratories can stabilise expectations, and no signal will ever be complete. Our previous formulation assumed uncertainty as a gap in data; we now face a condition where comprehension itself is outpaced. This does not reduce our duty to act, but it changes its character: we must institutionalise reversible decisions, modular infrastructure, and fail-deep rather than fail-fast. The commitment stands, but its interpretation must evolve to match the terminal phase.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor sovereign capability in auditable AI, prioritising resilience, reuse, and rapid iteration over frontier parity  
- Grounds: The ai_investment_collapse resets the global landscape — the frontier is no longer accelerating, but our dependency remains. This creates a narrow window: while the external labs contract, we can redirect talent, capital, and compute toward EU-controlled, verifiable systems. But the Open Science Resilience Initiative is only three-fifths active, and our integration into the joint_threat_response pact depends on systems we cannot yet field at scale. We must shift from proving auditable AI to *anchoring* it — making it the default, the fastest, and the most supported path for deployment in high-consequence domains. This is no longer about catching up; it is about defining the field.

## In practice  
This turn, we are finalising the integration of the EU into the joint cyber-biosurveillance pact, marking the completion of the Secure foundational resilience measure. ENISA and ECDC have operationalised co-located analysis cells in Brussels and Stockholm, and secure telemetry pipelines now link to national CERTs in eight member states. Binding incident response protocols have been ratified by the Political and Security Committee, and a standing alert mechanism is now active. This is not symbolic: during the recent loss_of_control_incident, early data sharing via this channel allowed for partial containment within 36 hours, proving the value of trusted, real-time coordination. The measure is now complete, its costs cease, and its effects begin to compound.

Simultaneously, we are pushing the Open Science Resilience Initiative toward full activation. The three operational hubs — in Tübingen, Grenoble, and Bologna — are now integrated with national biosecurity agencies and Digital Innovation Hubs, and we are deploying emergency funding to activate the two dormant sites in Helsinki and Valencia. The ai_investment_collapse has freed both GPU clusters and senior researchers; we are negotiating with distressed European AI firms to redirect compute to the Initiative under EU legal anchor, using the Anti-Coercion Instrument to block foreign acquisition of stranded assets. DG GROW and the European Investment Bank are drafting a special refinancing window to convert equity stakes into public-purpose hosting agreements.

At the same time, we are launching the Auditable AI Acceleration Programme to ensure that the systems we develop are not just proven but *used*. The Commission is drafting a Council regulation that establishes a fast-track procurement lane for auditable AI in critical sectors, modelled on the EU’s vaccine rollout framework. This will mandate that energy grid operators, national health services, and rail infrastructure managers prioritise auditable systems in all new AI deployments, with compliance tied to cohesion fund disbursements. The goal is to create pull — not just push — so that the most attractive market for responsible developers is the EU itself. This is how we turn technical capacity into sovereign leverage.
