- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that the first is impossible without the second, because a future determined under coercion or collapse is no future at all.
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident and eval_anomaly_reports confirm that capability without control is not power — it is vulnerability. The Union cannot be sovereign if its critical systems are penetrable by tools it cannot trace, and it cannot be safe if its defences rely on systems it does not control. The two mandates are not in tension; they are sequential. Without preventing catastrophic risk, there is no agency to exercise. This is not a shift in values, but a recognition of order: survival precedes strategy.

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Anchor sovereign capability in auditable AI, prioritising resilience, reuse, and rapid iteration over frontier parity — and now, pivot from anchoring to *embedding*, making auditable systems the only viable path for critical infrastructure by locking them into procurement, incident response, and compute allocation

We must move beyond creating auditable AI to making it *structurally indispensable*. This means binding it to energy grids, emergency protocols, and regional fallback arrangements. The commitment evolves because the threat is no longer potential — it is systemic, and the window to institutionalise alternatives is closing.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
