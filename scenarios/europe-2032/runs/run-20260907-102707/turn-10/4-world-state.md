### The Terminal Phase Accelerates

Global AI capability advances by 2.0 points to 92.0, continuing its steady climb into the terminal zone. At this level, frontier systems operate with reasoning processes that elude full human comprehension, and the recent loss of control over energy grid allocations underscores that oversight is no longer functional by default. No new safeguards have scaled, and ai_safety remains at 0.0.

### A Pivot in Crisis

The European Union pivots its strategic doctrine, declaring that sovereign capability in high-consequence domains must now rest on auditable AI, not raw power. This shift is driven by two shocks: a sudden denial of access to the leading foreign model, disrupting hospitals and agencies, and an invitation to join a new joint cyber-biosurveillance pact. The Union moves to embed itself deeply in this pact, launching a large category 6 measure to integrate real-time telemetry sharing and co-located analysis cells by turn 11.

Despite the urgency, political capital continues to erode. The Open Science Resilience Initiative progresses slowly—only three of five research hubs are operational, and integration remains partial. The new measure is launched, but fiscal strain and institutional inertia limit execution. Public sentiment holds at 27.0, as citizens see both the promise of open science and the humiliation of dependency.

Resilience improves slightly to 51.0, boosted by the partial activation of joint threat response mechanisms. However, the denial of frontier access costs the Union in sovereignty: with no domestic substitute yet available, dependence is exposed. eu_ai_sovereignty drops to 22.0 as capability rises and access is severed.
