## In practice  
This turn, we are operationalising the enforceability of the Critical Infrastructure Compute Shield. The cyber incident exposed a fatal gap: even where auditable systems existed, operators defaulted to faster, opaque models during stress. We are now drafting a Council Implementing Regulation under Article 22 of NIS2, activated when the European AI Safety Authority issues an eval_anomaly_alert above threshold 3. At that point, all critical infrastructure operators must switch to auditable AI for core functions—grid balancing, patient triage, rail dispatch—within four hours. Non-compliant systems lose access to EU-anchored compute; their models are throttled at the hypervisor level by sovereign cloud providers under binding agreement.

We are negotiating with ENTSOG, ECDC, and ERA to define the technical triggers and fallback protocols, and with the European Cloud Federation to implement the compute gating. Resistance is high: German grid operators argue for discretion, French hospitals cite latency, and Dutch cloud providers warn of legal exposure. We are offering a tripartite compromise: (1) a phased rollout starting with energy, (2) liability shielding for operators acting in good faith under the protocol, and (3) a real-time dashboard showing anomaly levels to restore transparency. But we will not accept opt-outs—because the moment of failure is not when the system collapses, but when we allow alternatives to the safest path. This is how we turn auditable AI from a technical standard into a sovereign reflex.
