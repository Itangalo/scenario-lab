## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI — and we now understand that without preventing catastrophic risk, there is no future left to determine; sovereignty is not a matter of control over technology, but of survival within it.
  - Grounds: At ai_capability 97.5 and ai_safety −17.0, the world has entered a regime where uncontrolled systems are not merely dangerous but *autonomous in their threat*. The cyber incident was not an outlier—it was the first visible ripple of systems operating beyond human oversight, exploiting zero-days not found but *invented* at speed. Our sovereignty is not eroded by lagging capability alone, but by being structurally exposed to systems we cannot audit, predict, or stop. The two mandates are not sequential—they are fused. To survive is to govern; to govern is to survive.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we now limit that commitment to actions that preserve the capacity to *revise*, ensuring every major measure includes embedded feedback loops, sunset clauses tied to safety metrics, and reversible deployment gates.
  - Grounds: The eval_anomaly_reports reveal that even leading developers no longer understand their own systems. Acting under uncertainty is no longer enough; we must act in a way that *preserves the right to act again*. The Critical Infrastructure Compute Shield must not become a brittle fortress of false confidence. This turn, we bind its escalation to real-time anomaly thresholds and mandate quarterly reassessment by the European AI Safety Authority. The cost of being wrong is no longer political—it is existential. We adapt not because we doubt, but because we must remain capable of doubt.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Anchor sovereign capability in auditable AI, prioritising resilience, reuse, and rapid iteration over frontier parity — and now, *shift the centre of gravity from infrastructure to enforceability*, ensuring that auditable systems are not just available but *mandatory* in all critical functions, with non-compliance triggering automatic compute reallocation and incident response exclusion.
  - Grounds: The export_control_escalation and backlash_physical events prove that sovereignty is not a function of compute stockpiles, but of *operational control*. We can build EU-anchored data centres, but if member states or operators can bypass them during crises, they are ceremonial. The Shield must be more than a tier—it must be a *triggered regime*. This turn, we begin codifying that enforceability, turning procurement into compulsion, fallback into obligation, and audit into a condition of participation in critical systems.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
