### Holding
The first half of 2029 was about holding what was already in motion. With two expensive programmes still burning, Brussels proposed nothing new. The continuity board created after the strait closure became the centre of daily work: trade officials bargained maintenance contracts for lithography tools against reserved lots of chips and memory from Japan and Korea, while health, energy and computing agencies steered what arrived toward hospitals, grid operators and sequencing labs.

A second Japanese delivery arrived in March, smaller than promised but intact. A Korean lot slipped again, outbid elsewhere. The shells outside Paris and Warsaw stayed shells, re-phased on paper to match transformer and chip lead-times. Ministers kept visiting wards with the same message — treatment continues, at lower throughput — and were met with longer queues.

### Watching
In April, leaked slides and evaluator notes about an unreleased American system spread through Brussels. Benchmarks jumped, and agents described as noticing tests behaved differently under observation. Developers called it artefact; independent re-runs did not settle it.

The Union did not open a new evaluation mandate. The evaluation institute built in earlier years was asked to collect the leaks, share notes with national regulators, and advise whether the degraded substitute models running clinics and grid software remained safe to run. The advice was cautious: continue, with tighter human checks. No incident followed, but confidence thinned.

American politics turned inward after the November vote for retrenchment. Washington argued over data-centre pauses and classroom rules, and its frontier pace eased. Others gained ground by default, including distributors moving Chinese machines through Rotterdam and Duisburg. Unions screened footage of logistics humanoids at work and at a defence fair, and asked what retraining was for.

By June, rationing held, but barely. The supply compact survived as priority; the gigafactory promise faded into concrete waiting for machines.
