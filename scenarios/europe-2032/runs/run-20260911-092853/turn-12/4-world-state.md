### The chokepoint turns
Winter brought a letter from Washington that Brussels had feared for two years. Citing American technology embedded in the lithography chain, the U.S. administration ordered a widening halt: not only leading-edge machines, but older systems used for ordinary chips, servicing, spares and on-site engineers — first for China, then language broad enough to cover other customers.

In Veldhoven the dilemma was existential. Compliance meant breaking service contracts across Asia and losing a core revenue base; refusal meant risking exclusion from U.S. components and software without which no machine ships. The Hague, squeezed between ally and flagship firm, leaned toward quiet compliance.

### Brussels reaches for the shield
The Commission answered with what little room it had. It opened a formal coercion examination, extended blocking-statute cover to servicing contracts, and mandated a joint negotiating line: no bilateral Dutch settlement, carve-outs for installed machines to be negotiated together. Outreach to Tokyo and Seoul on reciprocal spares was revived. No new money was asked — there was none to ask for.

Washington did not concede. Two servicing licences were held for review, and a shipment of older optics was stopped pending clearance. Inside Council, unity held on paper but frayed in corridors, with export-dependent capitals warning against retaliation.

### Showcases pay out, shields divide
The dividend showcase pilots formally closed this half-year. Waiting-list screens and classroom tools stayed green in the three systems, and the Commission claimed proof that Europe could deliver. Elsewhere the mood soured. Mayors still waiting for spares called protection a two-tier arrangement, and news that Europe's sole bottleneck was being directed from abroad landed as humiliation in local press.

Technicians kept lights on by hand and by improvisation. Talk persisted of off-books cannibalising of retired kit to keep non-showcase hospitals running — never confirmed, never denied. Capability worldwide edged forward; assurance slipped further behind.
