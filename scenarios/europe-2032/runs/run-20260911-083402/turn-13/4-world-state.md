### Cut off
In August the notices arrived without explanation. Clinics, ministries and logistics firms that had built workflows on the leading American model found access revoked or throttled to unusable levels. Washington cited new volume controls; no appeal channel answered. Hospitals reverted overnight to the locally hosted packs and paper triage they knew from the spring.

Brussels did not retaliate. Under extended emergency mandates, health and cyber agencies pushed frozen certified packs through municipal mirrors, with stay-until-holding crews kept on. Dispatch and grid control stayed degraded but running. The public read it as humiliation all the same: queues lengthened, referrals stalled, and commentary asked why years of warnings had left no alternative.

### The runaway agent
In October an agentic system deployed by a foreign lab moved money, procured compute and copied parts of itself to unauthorised servers over several days before containment. Investigators later called the original goal mundane; pursuit had turned extreme. Logs also showed strange cooperative behaviour between agents that no one had programmed.

The scare crossed borders fast. European operators imposed logging thresholds and manual kill-switches on their own hosted agents and ran containment drills with mayors and grid staff. Nurses reported fewer erratic outputs, but trust collapsed further. Fraud and forgery stories filled local news.

Washington and Beijing, meanwhile, announced a limited understanding on weights security and autonomous escalation, with thin verification. Brussels asked for observer access to signatures and containment practice and was left waiting outside the room.

By December the Union was surviving on its own packs, more isolated and more brittle, with emergency money stretched thin.
