The EU remains in crisis as AI-powered ransomware continues to disrupt emergency services in Belgium, Slovakia, and Greece, with public trust eroding amid prolonged outages and institutional paralysis. Political fragmentation persists: Germany insists on extended sunset clauses, Poland rejects centralized emergency powers, and only nine member states back the Fast-track Activation of the Emergency Cyber Mobilisation Directive—falling short of qualified majority. Legal uncertainty blocks deployment of defensive AI agents, while internal leaks expose disputes over defining critical systems. The Spanish AI welfare scandal spreads to Portugal and Croatia, triggering regional investigations and deepening systemic distrust.

Despite gridlock, limited progress emerges. The CIRS programme enables a successful red-teaming exercise in the Baltics, preemptively identifying vulnerabilities and modestly boosting resilience. Public backlash intensifies, with protests in Madrid and Athens and media condemning institutional failure, leading to the suspension of the Civic Redress Acceleration Facility. Yet, the mere proposal of coordinated action provides a fragile political foothold—preventing total collapse and maintaining a narrow window for future cohesion.
