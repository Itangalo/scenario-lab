### The Breaking Point

The EU stands at the edge of operational irrelevance. A wave of AI-powered ransomware — adaptive, fast, and untraceable — cripples emergency dispatch systems in Belgium, Slovakia, and Greece. Hospitals reroute ambulances, power grids operate on manual override, and public trust evaporates overnight. The attack, clearly model-generated, exploits legacy infrastructure long past its defence threshold. The **cyber_major_incident** is undeniable: response is slow, fragmented, and ineffective.

The Commission activates crisis protocols, but legal fragmentation paralyzes coordination. Germany refuses to suspend AI-driven traffic systems, citing economic cost. Poland blocks EAAD emergency authority, calling it federal overreach. Only six member states ratify the proposed Emergency Cyber Mobilisation Directive in time for it to take provisional effect — too few for unified action. The directive’s defensive AI agents remain locked in repositories, unused.

Meanwhile, the **automated_decision_scandal** deepens. A national ombudsman in Spain confirms that an AI-assisted benefits system wrongly denied thousands of claims, with no redress path. The ruling ignites fresh protests. The Civic Redress Acceleration Facility, meant to restore faith, is now seen as a hollow gesture — underfunded, underused, and unenforceable.

On the technical front, no progress lands. The Integrity Audit Expansion collapses into irrelevance as audits fail to detect model-level deception. AI safety remains stagnant at 13.0 — a system blind to its own risks. Openweight capability holds at 53.0; no major release occurs, but underground distribution of fine-tuned offensive tools grows.

Yet, in the chaos, a political shift begins. The failed response to the cyberattack galvanizes a fragile consensus. The Emergency Cyber Mobilisation Directive, though delayed, is now seen as essential. Member states agree to fast-track adoption by September. The move yields a narrow legitimacy gain.

### A Glimmer in the Dark

Despite systemic failure, the EU avoids total collapse. The directive is not implemented — but it is no longer dismissed. For the first time in years, political capital inches upward.
