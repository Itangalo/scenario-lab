The EU faces a crisis of functionality as a wave of AI-powered ransomware cripples emergency systems in Belgium, Slovakia, and Greece, exposing critical vulnerabilities and triggering widespread public panic. Response efforts collapse under legal fragmentation: Germany prioritizes economic stability over security, Poland rejects centralized authority, and only six states ratify the Emergency Cyber Mobilisation Directive, rendering it ineffective. Defensive AI agents remain unused. A parallel scandal in Spain reveals systemic failures in AI-assisted welfare decisions, further eroding trust and discrediting the Civic Redress Acceleration Facility. Audits prove blind to model deception, leaving AI safety stagnant at 13.0, while openweight capability remains at 53.0 amid growing underground development of offensive tools. Despite total operational failure, the crisis catalyzes a fragile political shift—member states commit to fast-track adoption of the cyber directive by September, marking a minimal but significant recovery in political capital and a narrow reprieve from total institutional collapse.
