Global AI capability reaches 96.0, advancing deeper into the terminal zone of untraceable model reasoning without corresponding improvements in safety or oversight. Despite a collapse in private investment slowing frontier training runs, algorithmic and architectural progress sustains rapid capability growth, widening the gap between performance and verifiability. The Open Science Resilience Initiative concludes with mixed results: auditable models for biosecurity and climate resilience are deployed but face operational failures under real-world load, delaying critical healthcare services. Germany rejects full integration on legal and technical grounds. In response, the EU launches the Accelerated Sovereign Deployment Programme, establishing optimisation clusters in France, Poland, and Finland to enhance model performance while preserving auditability, with ENISA and data protection authorities embedded in the process. Political capital continues to erode as member states resist binding funding conditionality, Italy threatens legal action, and public sentiment remains stagnant at 27.0. Energy-related protests against AI data centres spread to ten member states, framed as democratic resistance to unaccountable technological expansion. Systemic fragility persists despite reduced foreign leverage from global investment declines. No progress is made on AI safety, open frontier capability, or biosecurity integration.
