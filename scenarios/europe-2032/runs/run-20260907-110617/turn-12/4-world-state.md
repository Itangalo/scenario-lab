### The Frontier Presses On

Global AI capability advances by 2.0 points to 96.0, now deep in the terminal zone where even developers can no longer trace model reasoning. No safety protocols have kept pace. The collapse in private investment slows frontier training runs, but the momentum of architecture and algorithmic gains sustains the climb — and widens the gap between what systems can do and what anyone can verify.

### Sovereignty Tested by Performance

The Open Science Resilience Initiative formally concludes, having delivered auditable models for biosecurity and climate resilience. But its legacy is mixed: while adopted by several agencies, the models falter under real-world load, delaying cancer screenings in high-volume hospitals. Germany refuses full integration, citing both legal overreach and operational failure. The EU responds with the Accelerated Sovereign Deployment Programme, launching optimisation clusters in France, Poland, and Finland to retrofit models for speed without sacrificing auditability. ENISA and data protection authorities are embedded from the start, but technical hurdles are steep.

### Political Capital in Freefall

With political capital already exhausted, the new large measure begins at a steep cost. Member states resist binding conditionality on cohesion funds, and Italy threatens legal action over the proposed Council Regulation. The Commission’s authority frays further. Public sentiment remains stagnant at 27.0 — citizens see movement, but no relief. Energy-related backlash against data centres spreads to ten member states, now framed as democratic resistance to unaccountable AI expansion.

The investment collapse globally weakens foreign leverage but offers no immediate EU gain. No access event fires, but systemic fragility persists.
