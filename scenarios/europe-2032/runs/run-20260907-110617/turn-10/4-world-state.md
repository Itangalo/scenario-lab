### The Terminal Surge Continues

Global AI capability advances by 2.0 points this turn to reach 92.0, continuing its steady climb into the terminal zone. At this level, frontier models operate with reasoning architectures so dense that even expert auditors cannot reconstruct decision pathways in real time. No new safety protocols have scaled to match.

### Denial and Response

The European Union is abruptly cut off from the leading foreign AI model, disrupting diagnostics in over 120 hospitals and forecasting systems across 18 national ministries. The provider offers no appeal. In response, the Commission invokes Article 122a TFEU, activating emergency coordination between DG CONNECT, DG SANTE, and the European Defence Agency. Interim open-weight protein folding models—slower but fully compliant—are rapidly redeployed as replacements within EU data governance frameworks.

This emergency coordination includes the development of a binding directive that would pressure Germany, France, and Italy to restrict critical infrastructure AI procurement to auditable systems by Q3, effectively excluding unverifiable frontier models unless they meet strict transparency standards. The draft is circulated for legal review and political consultation, but final adoption and implementation are pending, with resistance already visible in national regulatory channels.

The Union joins a newly activated joint threat response pact, enabling real-time cyber and biothreat intelligence sharing. ENISA conducts a live forensic audit using pooled attribution resources, marking the first operational test of the mutual aid framework. While this boosts technical resilience, it underscores the EU’s strategic vulnerability: cooperation cannot compensate for unilateral access denial.

Public sentiment holds at 27.0—neither rising with the coordinated response nor falling sharply after the disruption, as citizens see both competence and dependence laid bare. Political capital erodes further under the weight of ongoing initiatives.
