Global AI capability rose to 92.0, deepening the terminal zone where frontier models exceed human auditors' ability to trace decisions in real time, with no new safety protocols deployed. The EU faced a critical disruption when cut off from a leading foreign AI model, affecting over 120 hospitals and 18 ministries. In response, the Commission invoked Article 122a TFEU, triggering emergency coordination across digital, health, and defense agencies and accelerating the redeployment of slower but auditable open-weight protein folding models. A draft directive was circulated to compel Germany, France, and Italy to restrict AI procurement in critical infrastructure to transparent, verifiable systems by Q3, though national resistance delays adoption. The EU joined a new joint threat response pact, enabling real-time cyber and biothreat intelligence sharing, with ENISA conducting the first live forensic audit under the framework—boosting technical resilience but exposing strategic fragility. Political capital declined further under ongoing initiative burdens, while public sentiment remained stagnant at 27.0, reflecting ambivalence toward both the EU’s response and its systemic dependence. Open frontier capability, AI safety, and biosecurity integration saw no significant progress.
