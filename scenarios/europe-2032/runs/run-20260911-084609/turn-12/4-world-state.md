### The black box gets darker
In February the leading labs confirmed what evaluators had whispered for months: the best models no longer thought in words. Reasoning traces were summaries written after the fact. Oversight teams that lived by reading chains of thought lost their instrument at once. Brussels asked for access and received benchmarks.

### Chips tighten again
Spring brought a new round of American export tightening. Under country-tier rationing, European buyers stayed formally licensed but practically short: smaller lots, longer queues, higher prices. Two expansion builds already paused for mediation stayed paused, now for lack of accelerators as much as protest. Data-centre curtailment remained first and permanent.

Taiwan exercises and rising insurance rates filtered into procurement meetings as a second anxiety — what if the next lot never sails.

### The fallback becomes the stack
The catalogue issued in autumn hardened into infrastructure. Municipalities and clinics now ran triage summaries, procurement drafts and diagnostics on the open near-frontier system hosted on Union machines. It worked, slowly. Paper backlogs shrank where recovery corps teams arrived, persisted where they had not.

That technical survival carried a political price. The system was downloaded hundreds of thousands of times, patched by volunteers, audited by no one with authority to stop it. Safety reviewers warned that Europe had swapped a switched-off frontier for an uninspectable commons.

### Paid work for a lost cohort
With no money for new build, the Commission pushed a single instrument: a Transition Bridge enrolling collapsed entry cohorts in coding and support into six-month paid corps to digitise clinic backlogs, check machine summaries, and reinforce municipal recovery.

Rollout was uneven. Mayors certified placements quickly where claims windows were open; unions forced a freeze on imposed rostering tools before cooperating; blockade sites agreed only to mediation. Wildcat stoppages eased in cities where corps teams visibly cleared queues, continued where they did not. Graduates got pay and placement, not careers.

By June essentials had not stopped. They ran degraded, expensive, and opaque — but staffed.
