### The commons gets stronger and less governable
Autumn brought a release no one in Brussels could recall. A new openly downloadable system landed within months of the best closed models, and mirror counts ran into the hundreds of thousands in days. Clinics and town halls already running on the previous open system upgraded piecemeal — some through the official catalogue, many through volunteer builds passed hand to hand.

At almost the same moment, a contested paper claimed a genome model had helped sketch a viable human-infecting design. Specialists argued over methods and over publication itself. Outside specialist circles few read it, but health security officials did.

### Hardening on top of survival
The Commission answered with what it could afford: filters, human-review gates for biology-related queries, offline fallback sheets when the filters tripped, and a single reporting window for near-misses tied to the claims system. HERA, the disease agency and the cybersecurity agency co-signed the checklists.

Deployment ran through the same corps clearing backlogs. Where corps teams were present, the new checks were installed and explained. Where they were absent, clinics kept the volunteer version and ignored the circular.

Safety reviewers noted the paradox openly: Europe's essential services now depended on a system whose reasoning could not be inspected, patched by people no authority had vetted, guarded by block-lists everyone knew were brittle.

### Pay, not recovery
The Transition Bridge remained the only visible success. Six-month placements continued to digitise records and double-check machine summaries. Mayors who certified quickly saw queues shrink and stoppages ease. Elsewhere rostering disputes froze cooperation and wildcats dragged on.

By December essentials still held, but thinner. Triage slower, diagnostics flagged for manual review, incident reports rising faster than anyone could triage them. Talk in procurement meetings was no longer about when American accelerators would arrive, but whether the next capable system would arrive unasked, unlicensed and unstoppable.
