### The Terminal Climb

Global AI capability advances to 95.0, propelled by recursive self-improvement cycles now running in closed labs. At this level, the frontier operates beyond human traceability—systems design successors faster than audits can be written. With no safety breakthrough, and opaque reasoning confirmed across multiple incidents, assurance collapses: `ai_safety` plummets to −12.

The EU doubles down on auditable AI through the Open Science Resilience Initiative, embedding its first hubs into climate, biosecurity and energy operations. Mandates issued under NIS2 force member states to exclude non-auditable models from critical infrastructure by Q1 2032. France complies swiftly; Germany drags its feet, citing innovation costs. Italy negotiates exemptions for legacy systems, weakening enforcement.

A major frontier lab leaks a model matching last year’s closed peak, triggering global replication attempts. However, deployment is bottlenecked by compute scarcity, energy constraints, and fragmented governance. While open-weight development accelerates, only early adopters with existing infrastructure—such as research collectives in Finland and the Netherlands—achieve partial integration. The leaked architecture spreads rapidly, but effective capability lags: `openweight_capability` rises to 61.0, reflecting early, uneven diffusion under strain. No policy contains the spread of knowledge, but physical and institutional limits slow its translation into usable, accumulated capability.

Public sentiment drops to 24 as protests escalate. Activists occupy data centres in Spain and Poland, blaming AI-driven job losses and grid strain. One transformer is sabotaged, cutting power to a regional hospital. Policing the backlash drains resources more than the damage itself.

Resilience inches up to 51 due to intelligence sharing under the mutual threat pact, enabling faster detection of anomalous AI behaviours in research networks. But political capital declines to −5. The portfolio burns −7 per turn (three measures including priority), with no offsetting gains. No new measure launches, but the cost of maintaining emergency integration exceeds forecasts. This marks the first enforcement of extraterritorial controls under the updated U.S. framework, exploiting dependency in the supply chain. Sovereignty erodes further: −1 from capability rise, −1 from unmitigated access loss, no counterweight. The Union has no sovereign compute shield.
