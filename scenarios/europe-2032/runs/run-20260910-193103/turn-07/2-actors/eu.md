## New measure
**EU Essential Services Fallback and Continuity Reserve**
It puts standby fallback capacity behind hospitals, ports and municipal services — offline procedures, isolated backup compute and pre-positioned recovery teams — so throttling rented models and absorbing the automated attack degrades rather than stops services. Why this and why now: the major cyber incident plus pre-emptive throttling over tiering fears is exactly the essential-services failure our two-year commitment exists to prevent, and containment alone (M7) cannot keep services running without something to fall back to.

## Priority
EU Essential Services Fallback and Continuity Reserve, because keeping hospitals, ports and municipalities running through attack and cutoff fears outranks even kill-switches this turn — without fallback, both containment and throttling mean degradation.

## In practice
We act under NIS2 Article 11 and the Cyber Solidarity Act: Commission Implementing Act tasking ENISA and EU-CyCLONe to stand up a Continuity Reserve — exercised offline playbooks, air-gapped backups, and a rostered pool of national CSIRT and HERA surge staff funded by reprogrammed Digital Europe and EU4Health emergency lines, no new budget vote. Health and Transport Council formations are the venue; we buy hospital and municipal compliance by funding overtime arrears and exempting fallback drills from agent-cap penalties.

We hold M7 Agent Containment on track — CSIRT enforcement of spend caps and kill-switches continues — and we treat M1 Gigafactory sites as the physical host for isolated fallback compute where possible. Messaging via Commission and ENISA is operational: what was hit, what fallback is live, and that throttling rented models is sanctioned and covered, not punished.
