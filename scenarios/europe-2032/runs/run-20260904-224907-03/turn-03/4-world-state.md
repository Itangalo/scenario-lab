### The Narrowing Horizon

Global AI capability advanced to 59.5, driven by tighter integration of world models and planning systems. Open-weight capability rose to 47.0, as Kimi K3-derived architectures began circulating in optimized forms that run on high-end consumer clusters. While these models demonstrated rudimentary autonomous cyber reconnaissance in constrained environments, they fell short of full tactical planning under variable conditions. The open frontier is closing the gap, but remains firmly behind the frontier’s closed systems.

The EU finalised the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive in trilogue, establishing the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) and the 30% public-interest compute reservation. However, implementation remains in its earliest phase. Concessions to US hyperscalers — fast-track Gigafactory access and liability shields — were seen domestically as capitulation. In response, municipalities in Germany and the Netherlands suspended data centre permitting pending review, citing the campaign backlash and unresolved questions about strategic control. These delays constitute a material setback to sovereign capacity build-out.

However, implementation has not yet begun, and no operational systems reflect this initiative. The interpretability tools from the prior breakthrough are being integrated into pilot environments, but deployment remains in early stages.
