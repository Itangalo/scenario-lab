## New measure  
**Sovereign AI Testbed and Safety Corridor**  
A publicly governed, high-assurance testing environment where EU-cleared developers can train and evaluate frontier models under strict monitoring, using sovereign compute and vetted data, with full model access granted to independent evaluators before deployment.

## In practice  
We are acting on the narrowing window between capability and control. This turn, the Commission finalises negotiations on the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive in trilogue, anchoring it in Article 114 TFEU as a single market measure for digital infrastructure resilience. The final text retains the 30% reservation for public-interest use but introduces the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) as an independent allocator, using a transparent auction mechanism to assign time slots for critical infrastructure resilience, public health modelling, and verified safety research. To break the deadlock with US hyperscalers, we are pairing this with fast-track access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund and a liability shield for compliance — not a gift, but a trade: market access for enforceable sovereignty.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Sovereign AI Testbed and Safety Corridor as a next-generation instrument for public technical capacity. Hosted on sovereign compute within the EDIC framework, the testbed will allow EU-approved developers to train models under real-time monitoring, with full interpretability tooling and third-party evaluator access baked in from the start. This is not a sandbox — it is a sovereign development lane, designed to produce models whose safety claims are not self-attested but independently verified. We are funding it through a reallocation of €12bn from the Tech Sovereignty Package’s regional acceleration zones, narrowing those zones to France and Finland only, where grid readiness and social acceptance are highest, and redirecting the rest toward this higher-leverage capability.

The Resilience by Design programme is now in implementation: national cybersecurity agencies are updating NIS2 guidance to mandate zero-trust architectures and AI-driven red-teaming for all essential service operators, with audits beginning in Q1 2028. We are using the recent interpretability breakthrough to tighten the definition of “AI-resistant” defences, requiring anomaly detection systems to be tested against agent-generated attack vectors. Public sentiment remains ambivalent, but the convergence of left and right on data centre moratoriums forces us to act decisively — not by retreating from infrastructure, but by embedding it in a framework of public control, safety, and resilience that answers the demand for limits with credible sovereignty, not just restriction.
