The new commitment binds control, capability, and independence into a single objective — not as parallel tracks, but as co-requisites for political agency in an AI-shaped world.

## New measure
**Sovereign AI Assurance Framework**  
Establish a legally mandated, EU-wide certification regime for all AI systems used in public administration or essential services, requiring proof of internal control, continuous monitoring, and sovereign fallback capacity.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 8  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to +14 to ai_safety by turn 8; +5 to eu_ai_sovereignty by ensuring fallback systems are sovereign and auditable; reduces recurrence of automated_decision_scandal-type failures  
`Applies to:` All EU member states, public agencies, and operators of essential services deploying AI above capability index 45.0

## Priority
**Sovereign AI Assurance Framework** — because the eu_frontier_access_denied event proves that reliance on foreign models is no longer viable, and the automated_decision_scandal proves that domestic systems without rigorous assurance are politically and ethically unsustainable; this measure closes both gaps at once by mandating control, transparency, and sovereign redundancy where it matters most.

## In practice
We are acting under the weight of two simultaneous shocks: the sudden denial of access to the leading global AI model, which disrupted hospitals, ministries, and research institutions across the Union, and the ongoing fallout from the automated decision scandal, which has shattered public confidence in AI-assisted governance. These are not isolated incidents — they are symptoms of a deeper failure: we have built neither the sovereign capacity nor the assurance infrastructure to operate independently and safely in a world where AI is foundational.

This turn, we finalise the Sovereign AI Testbed and Safety Corridor as a completed measure. The Marseille node is now operational under EDIC governance, with real-time logging, checkpointing, and evaluator access protocols in place. We have broken the deadlock with Germany and the Netherlands by granting binding seats to national safety agencies on the allocation board and enshrining parliamentary review for any capability above 60.0 — a red line that holds. The testbed is no longer a promise; it is a functioning instrument of control.

At the same time, we launch the Sovereign AI Assurance Framework under Article 114 TFEU, to be implemented by the European AI Office with technical support from ENISA and national audit authorities. This will require all public-sector AI deployments above 45.0 to undergo certification proving they are continuously monitored, internally interpretable to a human-in-the-loop standard, and backed by a sovereign fallback system that can be activated within 72 hours. Certification will be renewed annually, with unannounced audits and public reporting of all deviations. We are redirecting €12bn from the Tech Sovereignty Package to fund sovereign fallback systems in health, social services, and border management — not to replicate frontier capability, but to ensure continuity of state function when external systems fail or are withdrawn.

The EU-Wide AI Incident Reporting Framework continues in implementation, now integrated with the new assurance regime: incident data will feed directly into certification reviews. We are also preparing for the political consequences of export_control_escalation. With Washington tightening controls, we must decide whether to accept rationing under allied status or to accelerate full independence. Our answer will be clear by turn 6: we are no longer building access — we are building agency.
