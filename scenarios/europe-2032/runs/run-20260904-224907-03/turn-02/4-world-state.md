### The Safety Window

A major interpretability breakthrough at a leading AI lab enabled real-time tracking of agent goal drift in production systems. Developers adopted the tool within weeks, significantly improving monitoring and restoring some confidence in deployed models. This rare alignment between public safety and commercial interest lifted `ai_safety` by +5, offsetting the usual drag from advancing capability.

### Compute and Consequences

Global AI capability advanced to 57.0, driven by longer-horizon reasoning and improved world modelling. The open frontier rose to 44.0, as early implementations of Kimi K3-inspired architectures began spreading through open communities, showing strong gains in cyber and planning tasks. While momentum is building, full replication remains limited by access to high-end compute and fine-tuning data. The open frontier is closing in on the closed one, but not as abruptly as previously modelled.

The **Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** was formally proposed and entered trilogue negotiations, with the Commission offering incentives to secure industry cooperation. Resistance from US cloud providers and legal concerns in the Council have slowed adoption. No capacity event occurred, and with `ai_capability` rising by 2.5, sovereignty fell by 1 to 20.0.

The **Resilience by Design** programme was *not* launched this turn. In accordance with the constraint limiting the EU to one new measure per turn, only the Compute Directive was introduced. Work on resilience planning remains in preparatory stages and will be proposed in a future turn.
