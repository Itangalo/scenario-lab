### The Cost of Control

Global AI capability advanced to 62.0, driven by tighter integration of planning systems and world models now enabling multi-day autonomous execution in closed environments. Open-weight models, benefiting from leaked Kimi K3 derivatives and improved consumer-scale training frameworks, rose to 54.5 — approaching the threshold where autonomous cyber operations become feasible outside state labs.

The EU responded to two shocks: a major scandal involving an AI-driven social insurance system that denied thousands of legitimate claims, and Washington’s coercive move to restrict ASML’s export of mature lithography tools. The scandal shattered public trust, exposing how even non-frontier AI can cause systemic harm when deployed without oversight.

In response, the Commission launched the EU-Wide AI Incident Reporting and Transparency Framework, a large category 2 measure aimed at restoring accountability. However, early resistance emerged from member states wary of centralised reporting and from industry actors concerned about liability. Full implementation will take time, and no immediate safety gains materialised.

The Sovereign AI Testbed and Safety Corridor, now the priority, advanced with €8bn allocated to a pilot node in Marseille. Yet negotiations with Germany and the Netherlands over data centre permitting stalled, as local governments demanded veto power over testbed capabilities — a red line Brussels could not accept. No breakthrough occurred.

Resilience by Design and the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive completed this turn, delivering +3 to resilience from hardened critical infrastructure protocols. However, the broader political cost was steep.

Sovereignty fell to 16.0: the 2.5-point capability rise imposed −1, and the supply_chain_coercion event — a direct strike on EU industrial access — triggered a −2 penalty. No offsetting gains occurred.

Political capital dropped to 27.0. The portfolio charge was −13 (four large measures, one priority), no proposal bonus applied (the new measure responded to an internal scandal, not an external threat), and legitimacy did not lend support (sentiment below capital). The automated_decision_scandal, of internal origin, imposed a −7 political cost.

Public sentiment fell to 35.0, dragged down by the scandal and fears of unchecked AI in public services.
