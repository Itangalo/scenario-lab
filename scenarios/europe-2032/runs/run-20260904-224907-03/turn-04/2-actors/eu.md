- Trigger: the automated_decision_scandal  
- Grounds: A high-profile failure of an AI-supported public system has made clear that capability without assurance is not just risky — it is politically untenable and institutionally corrosive. The scandal reveals that even non-frontier systems, operating within current capability bands, can inflict systemic harm when deployed at scale without transparency, oversight, or recourse. This changes the calculus: sovereignty is not served by building powerful systems we cannot govern. Our commitment must now bind capacity to control, or it becomes self-defeating.

## In practice  
We are acting on the dual shock of the automated_decision_scandal and the US-led supply_chain_coercion. The scandal has shattered the fiction that we can treat AI deployment as a routine administrative upgrade — it is a governance frontier, and we are behind. The US move on ASML confirms that our industrial sovereignty is not just a matter of investment, but of leverage and autonomy. We cannot afford to build powerful systems we do not understand, nor depend on supply chains we do not control.

This turn, we are advancing the Sovereign AI Testbed and Safety Corridor by finalising its governance structure under the European AI Office, with binding access rights for national safety agencies and independent researchers. We are allocating €8bn from the redirected Tech Sovereignty Package funds to stand up the first node in Marseille, co-located with one of the InvestAI Gigafactories, using EDIC-managed compute. The testbed will require real-time logging, model checkpointing, and full evaluator access — not post-hoc audits, but continuous assurance. We are negotiating with German and Dutch authorities to lift the data centre permitting freeze by offering binding local oversight seats on the EDIC allocation board and guaranteeing that no testbed deployment will exceed 60.0 capability without prior parliamentary review.

Simultaneously, we are launching the EU-Wide AI Incident Reporting and Transparency Framework to institutionalise learning from failure. Drafted under Article 114 TFEU as a single market measure for digital service safety, it will be implemented by the European Data Protection Board with support from ENISA. The framework mandates reporting of all incidents involving harm to rights, safety, or essential services, with penalties for non-compliance tied to market access. We are pairing this with a public communication campaign — "AI We Can Challenge" — to restore trust, emphasising that reporting is not punishment, but the foundation of control. The Resilience by Design and Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive measures are now complete; their effects will persist, but their political charge is discharged. We carry forward only those that remain in flight, and we focus our thin capital where control and capacity converge: the testbed.
