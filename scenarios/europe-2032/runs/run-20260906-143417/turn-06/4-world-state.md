### The Breach and the Response

A coordinated, AI-generated cyber intrusion sweeps across European health and energy systems, exploiting a poisoned software dependency in widely used grid coordination tools. The attack bypasses human review chains, propagates autonomously, and cripples real-time response capacity. Public health records are encrypted, and emergency grid adjustments fail in multiple member states. Attribution remains unclear, but forensic analysis confirms the tooling was model-generated — a sign that offensive capabilities have decisively outpaced defences.

In response, the European Union declares a state of emergency under Article 122 TFEU and launches the **Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative**, a large-scale resilience measure targeting Tier 1 critical infrastructure. The European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) begins deploying AI-hardened defence stacks featuring swarm detection, zero-trust code verification, and real-time model monitoring. Funding of €45bn is redirected from the stalled SCAP envelope and the completed Tech Sovereignty Package.

However, implementation stumbles. Legal resistance to centralized grid control persists, and ENISA lacks authority to mandate integration across all operators. Only three of the eight targeted sectors commit to full deployment by turn 7. The Commission advances a Council Regulation under Article 194 TFEU to enforce power ring-fencing for sovereign compute, but Germany and Austria threaten legal action, calling it an overreach.

Public sentiment deteriorates sharply. The cyberattack confirms fears of AI-driven instability, and displaced professionals protest in Brussels. A rebranded communication campaign framing the Cyber Shield as a "digital immune system" fails to gain traction. Labour displacement in legal and administrative sectors accelerates, with no clear path to retraining at scale.

The US administration takes office, adopting a restrictive posture that limits access to frontier models. While not formally blocking the EU, export scrutiny tightens — a development felt more in atmosphere than in immediate policy.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

Despite finishing the Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme, physical deployment lags. The 5GW target is not met, and sovereign compute remains fragmented. The InvestAI Gigafactories continue, but integration with security layers is partial. With ai_capability rising by 1.5 and no compensating gains in safety or resilience, the Union’s strategic position weakens.
