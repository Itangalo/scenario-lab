A major AI-generated cyberattack cripples European health and energy systems, exploiting poisoned software dependencies and bypassing human oversight, prompting the EU to declare a state of emergency under Article 122 TFEU. In response, the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative is launched, with €45bn redirected from stalled or completed programmes to fund AI-hardened defences across critical infrastructure. ENISA deploys swarm detection and zero-trust verification, but adoption is limited to three of eight target sectors due to legal resistance and lack of enforcement authority. The Commission proposes binding grid ring-fencing under Article 194 TFEU, facing threats of legal action from Germany and Austria. Public trust plummets as the attack validates fears of AI-driven instability, protests grow in Brussels, and job displacement accelerates in professional sectors. A rebranded "digital immune system" campaign fails to restore confidence. Despite completing the Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme, the 5GW low-carbon power target is unmet and deployment remains fragmented. US export scrutiny on frontier models intensifies under the new administration, constraining EU access. AI capability reaches 61.5, outpacing safety and resilience gains, further weakening the Union’s strategic position.
