## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from infrastructure to authority. The Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative has reached its final deployment phase and is now technically complete: energy transmission, air traffic control, and emergency medical dispatch systems in 22 member states are running hardened defence stacks with zero-trust verification and swarm response protocols. However, Germany and Austria still withhold full grid and health IT integration, citing constitutional concerns over centralised command. We are responding with a dual track: legal, through the Commission’s invocation of Article 114 TFEU to assert internal market necessity, and political, by offering joint oversight councils with national representation and guaranteed data sovereignty. The EIB has formally tied future digital infrastructure grants to compliance, creating tangible leverage.

The Binding Auditability Mandate is now in final drafting, with the Legal Service resolving jurisdictional conflicts between GDPR, the Cyber Resilience Act, and the Digital Markets Act. We are insisting that any AI system operating in or affecting EU critical infrastructure — regardless of origin — must provide real-time audit access to EAAD, including model weights, runtime state, and adversarial test results. Non-compliance will trigger automatic decommissioning orders and suspension of market access. US hyperscalers are mobilising legal challenges, but we are preparing countermeasures: conditional data flow authorisations under GDPR and restrictions on model hosting via the AI Act’s high-risk provisions. EAAD’s red-team unit is conducting live probes on test systems, and we are fast-tracking legislation through COREPER and the JURI committee with a targeted plenary vote scheduled for December.

Public sentiment remains at 10.0, and the Right to Repair and Reclaim movement is gaining traction in local elections. We are reinforcing the €10bn community cyber resilience programme, now rebranded as Local Sovereignty Hubs, to train displaced workers as auditors and stewards. This is not just technical deployment — it is legitimacy-building. If we cannot show that sovereignty means control, not just capital, then no amount of infrastructure will hold.
