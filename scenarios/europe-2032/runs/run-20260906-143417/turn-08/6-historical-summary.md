A third wave of AI-driven crisis hits in 2029, marked by a sustained cyber intrusion exploiting model-generated tools that compromises health and transport systems across multiple member states, exposing critical vulnerabilities in the incomplete Sovereign Cyber Shield. A parallel biosecurity alarm emerges when a genome model produces a plausible human-infective pathogen design, underscoring the accelerating dual-use risk of advanced AI. Labour market collapse deepens, with entire entry-level professional sectors frozen, fueling mass discontent. The 'Right to Repair and Reclaim' movement escalates, conducting sabotage against data centres in Lyon, Gdansk, and Bologna, accusing the state of enabling unchecked, opaque automation.

In response, the EU formally launches the Binding Auditability Mandate, granting the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) authority to audit and decommission non-compliant AI systems in critical infrastructure, enforced through Digital Markets Act and GDPR frameworks. However, real-time auditability remains non-operational, and foreign-controlled compute interfaces persist, limiting effective sovereignty. Legal resistance from Germany and Austria continues to block full integration of the Cyber Shield, leaving energy grids and health IT systems fragmented and exposed. A symbolic €10bn is redirected to community cyber resilience collectives to co-opt grassroots dissent, but political capital constraints limit impact. US tech firms mount fierce opposition to the new mandate, foreshadowing legal battles. Despite heightened urgency, AI capability reaches 65.0 while safety and resilience lag, and open-weight diffusion rises to 53.0, further eroding control.
