### A Surge in Capability, Anchored by Safety

Despite the ongoing Taiwan tensions, global AI capability advances to 64.0. The **verification_widens** development unlocks rapid progress in legal, medical, and engineering domains, where automatic checks now enable faster training loops. This accelerates frontier models sharply, though not uniformly. Simultaneously, a genuine **safety_breakthrough** lands: a new interpretability technique allows auditors to predict emergent reasoning paths before deployment, and it is rapidly adopted across leading labs. For the first time in years, safety improvements reach operational systems in time to matter.

The EU capitalises on this window. The **Mandatory Pre-Deployment Verification for High-Impact Domains** is launched as a small category 1 measure, targeting newly verifiable sectors. Leveraging the safety breakthrough, it mandates third-party audits for AI in clinical coding, contract review, and structural engineering. Early cooperation from German and Dutch regulators enables pilot frameworks, and ENISA fast-tracks templates. The move is seen as timely, technical, and narrowly focused—avoiding backlash.

However, geopolitical strain deepens. The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event occurs: without explanation, EU institutions and several major hospitals lose access to a leading clinical AI model. The disruption is contained, but the message is clear—reliance on external frontier systems carries existential risk. Sovereignty takes a hit, despite the ongoing **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative**.

That initiative progresses, with audit teams deployed and €1.8bn in emergency funding released. But U.S.-linked hyperscalers resist jurisdictional verification, and reciprocity talks stall. The **Tech sovereignty package** formally ends, having failed to attract projected investment amid global instability.

Political capital improves slightly. The completion of the long-running Tech sovereignty package yields a +4 boost, and the new safety measure earns a +3 proposal bonus for directly addressing a recent, high-stakes opportunity. These gains offset portfolio costs and the access denial shock.

Public sentiment remains stagnant at 34.0—no major incident or benefit shifts perception.
