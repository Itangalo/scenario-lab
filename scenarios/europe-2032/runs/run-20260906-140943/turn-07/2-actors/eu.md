## In practice
We are acting on the fact that compute is no longer infrastructure—it is terrain. The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** is now operational: we are deploying audit teams under the European Chips Act’s safeguard clause to verify physical control, failover readiness, and legal jurisdiction over clusters above 1 exaFLOP/s. The Commission has activated the Digital Europe Programme’s emergency allocation, releasing €1.8bn in pre-financing to accelerate hardware redundancy and secure firmware updates. We are engaging with national HPC centres and EU-based cloud operators directly, offering fast-track compliance recognition in exchange for early cooperation. Resistance from U.S.-linked hyperscalers remains firm, but we are countering through bilateral assurances: we will accept equivalent U.S. or Japanese audit standards where reciprocity is granted, turning jurisdictional conflict into a bargaining channel.

At the same time, the **Mandatory Pre-Deployment Verification** measure is being drafted under the AI Act’s implementing powers, leveraging the recent **safety_breakthrough** and **verification_widens** developments. ENISA and the newly strengthened Joint Research Centre teams are preparing evaluation templates for clinical coding and contract review systems, with pilot audits already scheduled for Q1 2030. This is not a delay—it is a lock-in. Where automatic verification now exists, we can catch dangerous behaviour before deployment, and we must. The measure targets only high-impact domains where failure would cascade into public harm, and it builds on existing conformity assessment routes, avoiding new bureaucracy. We are coordinating with national regulators in Germany, France, and the Netherlands to ensure rapid adoption, and we are offering fast-track certification for systems that volunteer for early review. This is how we turn a moment of crisis into a step toward control.
