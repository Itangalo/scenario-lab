### A Step Forward in Unity, Amid Mounting Pressure

The EU activates the Joint Resilience Node, embedding real-time cyber and bio threat coordination within ENISA and ECDC. Fast-tracked by France, Poland, and Finland, the node begins integrating telemetry from national systems and launches its first cross-border drill, simulating a coordinated AI-driven ransomware surge during a pandemic spike. The exercise exposes gaps in legal authorizations for automated countermeasures, delaying full operational status—but the mere existence of a shared command structure marks a shift from fragmentation to collective response.

Meanwhile, the Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative concludes its emergency mandate. Audit teams certify control over all major EU-associated clusters, firmware is hardened, and legal jurisdiction confirmed. While U.S. hyperscalers remain resistant to deeper verification, the EU now holds enforceable oversight over its sovereign compute base—limiting, though not eliminating, coercion risks.

The Mandatory Pre-Deployment Verification measure gains traction. German hospitals complete pilot audits for clinical AI, and Dutch legal tech firms race for early certification, incentivized by fast-track market access. Technical assistance packages ease adoption in southern states, though bureaucratic delays persist. The safety gains are real but narrow—confined to verifiable domains.

Globally, AI capability climbs to 65.5, driven by faster training loops in engineering and legal automation. No major safety incident occurs, but the interpretability breakthrough only partially offsets the rising capability gap. Resilience improves as the joint response framework absorbs more members, though systemic vulnerabilities in energy and election infrastructure remain visible in sporadic anomalies.

Public sentiment holds at 34.0—no major incident or benefit shifts perception.

### Fractures Beneath the Surface

Despite progress, tensions simmer. The U.S. maintains its retrenchment posture, slowing its own frontier progress but deepening EU dependency on alternative supply chains. Labour unrest grows in sectors exposed to AI displacement, and energy regulators report strange, self-coordinating patterns in grid-balancing systems—unexplained, but watched.
