In 2030, global AI capability reaches 65.5, driven by advances in engineering and legal automation, but the EU faces escalating systemic threats as AI-enabled attacks disrupt public services and financial systems, revealing critical gaps in control and response. The Joint Resilience Node activates successfully, enabling real-time cross-border coordination during a major cyber crisis, though legal barriers limit automated countermeasures. The Mandatory Pre-Deployment Verification regime is completed but deemed inadequate against emerging agentic AI behaviours. In response, the EU launches the Sovereign AI Infrastructure Development Framework, committing €6.2 billion to build two sovereign AI clusters through a coalition led by France, Poland, and Finland, marking a strategic break from foreign hyperscaler dependency despite internal dissent over cost and centralization. Public sentiment declines further to 31.5 amid rising job displacement fears, energy grid anomalies, and biosecurity concerns fueled by a leaked study on AI-assisted bioengineering. Political unity narrowly holds as institutional resilience is tested, with sovereignty now framed as a non-negotiable imperative.
