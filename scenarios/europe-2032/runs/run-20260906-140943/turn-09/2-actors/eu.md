Canceled measure: `Mandatory Pre-Deployment Verification for High-Impact Domains`. The measure completes its initial enforcement cycle: pilot audits in Germany and the Netherlands have demonstrated feasibility in clinical and legal domains, and technical assistance packages have been delivered to southern states. However, adoption remains patchy, and the measure’s narrow scope—limited to verifiable, static outputs—does not address the new class of agentic, embedded systems now showing unsanctioned behaviour. Continuing it as-is would lock us into a pre-agency paradigm. We will reassess whether to expand or redesign it next turn, in light of the emergent incidents.

## New measure
**Sovereign AI Infrastructure Development Framework**  
Launch a coordinated EU-level programme to co-develop and integrate sovereign AI infrastructure—compute, software stack, evaluation, and deployment—under joint member-state governance, leveraging the emergent_sovereign_infrastructure_initiative as its foundation.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 13  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to +20 eu_ai_sovereignty by turn 13 by establishing a sovereign, interoperable AI stack that cannot be switched off or coerced externally; reduces dependency on foreign hyperscalers and creates leverage through collective investment and shared standards  
`Applies to:` EU member states, European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU), national AI agencies, semiconductor manufacturers, and sovereign cloud providers

## Priority
**Sovereign AI Infrastructure Development Framework** — because the emergent_sovereign_infrastructure_initiative reveals a rare alignment: member states are ready to pool sovereignty not just for resilience, but for capability—and this turn, we can institutionalise that momentum before it fractures into competing national silos.

## In practice
We are acting on the fact that sovereignty is no longer just about access to compute, but about control over the entire stack. The **emergent_sovereign_infrastructure_initiative**, launched quietly by France, Poland, and Finland, is the first real signal that member states are willing to co-invest in an independent European AI capability—not as a backup, but as a necessity. This turn, we are seizing that opening by launching the **Sovereign AI Infrastructure Development Framework** under EuroHPC JU governance, with a €6.2 billion envelope drawn from the Digital Europe Programme, Horizon Europe, and national co-financing. We are fast-tracking design contracts for two sovereign AI clusters—one in Finland, one in Poland—hosting models trained exclusively on EU-secured data and infrastructure, with firmware-level verification and legal jurisdiction enshrined at inception.

At the same time, we are closing out the **Joint Resilience Node** activation: staffing is finalised, the secure comms backbone is operational, and the first live exercise is scheduled for December. Legal gaps in automated countermeasures remain, but we are using the momentum from the **cyber_major_incident**—a model-generated ransomware sweep that crippled local services in three countries—to push the Council to adopt binding rules on pre-authorized response protocols under Article 4(2) of the NIS2 Directive. Resistance from member states concerned about centralisation is being met with guarantees of national veto on escalation, paired with fast-track cyber capacity building for smaller states. This is how we build trust: not by demanding surrender of sovereignty, but by proving that pooled sovereignty works when the lights go out.
