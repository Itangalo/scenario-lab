### Concrete poured, code accelerated
The first mediated gigafactory site reached final investment decision in autumn, with grid connections signed and piling started under the InvestAI banner. The second site remained in court. Brussels claimed a first domestic anchor; opposition papers called it one building against a flood. Officials acknowledged the build remains under construction and will not deliver usable sovereign capacity until a later turn, with no completion credit taken this turn.

That flood sharpened. A new release in verifiable work — code generation, mathematics, intrusion tooling — markedly changed what a skilled attacker could do within weeks, running multi-hour operations from private hardware. General assistants improved only modestly, with commentators disagreeing sharply about what the uneven results meant for evaluations.

At the same time a genome-modelling paper showed a non-expert team reaching a viable human-infective design with model help. Methodologists attacked the study and editors were accused of printing a recipe. Inside health-security circles it was read as a categorically new signal.

### Patch prioritized, other proposals deferred
The Commission's single new measure this turn was its exploit-containment effort, named as the priority. Through ENISA and the joint cyber command, backstop payouts were conditioned on live telemetry, offline kits and reserve licences were audited in the municipalities hit in spring, and backups were pre-positioned around hospitals. Officials dropped demands to read model reasoning — with the new unreadable architectures, only black-box behaviour and telemetry could be enforced. Coverage widened but staff shortages left smaller communes waiting, and two regional outages degraded care for days without collapsing it. No surge capacity was available to scale further this turn, so wider rollout was explicitly delayed.

No other new measures were introduced this turn. Work on a Middle-Power Trusted Compute Compact remained exploratory talks with no decision, and the defecting member state's separate hyperscaler deal continued to undercut the common line, with Japanese and Korean servicing offers paraded as the alternative. A synthesis screening and medical countermeasure effort was not launched as a measure; only scoping discussions through HERA within existing funds took place, with wider rollout, sequencing integration, and broad industry enforcement deferred for lack of staffing, money and governance, and several low-cost providers continuing to offer unscreened synthesis offshore pending a full proposal in a later turn.

Washington stayed inward, its frontier work slowed by domestic moratorium politics, leaving Europe to absorb both shocks alone. Limited delivery amid shortages and deferred ambitions yielded only a modest political return.
