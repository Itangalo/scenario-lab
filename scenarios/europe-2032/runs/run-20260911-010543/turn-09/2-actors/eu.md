Stands up EU-funded rapid restoration teams, pre-imaged backups and cross-border mutual aid for public services hit by the largely automated ransomware/dependency attack, because defenders are visibly behind and hospitals already showed degraded-but-operating is the difference between shock and cascade. This is Preparedness and resilience work, category 6, and why now is that the major incident plus the verifiable-domain capability jump means the next weeks decide whether disruption becomes lasting loss of trust.

## In practice
We execute under the old commitment this turn: finish M9 screening stocks into the hospitals holding M8/M5 kits, and run M10 telemetry and isolation playbooks through ENISA and the joint cyber command with live exercises with operators, conditioning restoration aid on reporting compliance. The new Surge is funded by reprogramming Civil Protection and Digital Europe money via the Competitiveness Council formation, with ENISA dispatching restoration teams to the worst-hit municipalities and health systems first.

We do not start a new compute build while gigafactory sites are disputed; we keep the finished Trusted Compute Compact as servicing leverage with Japan/Korea against the carve-out hyperscaler deal, and we speak plainly that productivity gains without entry-level recovery plus dark public mood leave us no room for a large new spend. Small, visible restoration is how we buy back standing to pursue the next two-year direction.
