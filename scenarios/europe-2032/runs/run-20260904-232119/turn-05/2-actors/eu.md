## In practice
We are launching the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Facility (SCAF) as the cornerstone of our renewed two-year commitment. The Commission, through DG CNECT and in coordination with the European Investment Bank, is drafting the legal basis under the Digital Europe Programme and the Net-Zero Industry Act, allowing disbursement by Q1 2029. The fund will co-invest in new data centres located on EU soil, with binding clauses ensuring that at least 30% of compute capacity is reserved for public missions — including EAAD evaluations, resilience operations, and crisis response — and that no foreign entity can unilaterally withdraw access. We are targeting completion of three sites by turn 7, leveraging the InvestAI Gigafactories pipeline in Brittany and northern Sweden as anchor projects.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, with ENTSO-E confirming grid readiness for 12GW of new demand, and power purchase agreements now being negotiated with EU-based developers under enforceable green energy clauses. The Tech Sovereignty Package advances in the Digital Markets Council, where we are linking accelerated permitting to binding commitments on EU operational control and energy efficiency. With the EAAD now fully established, its first audit cycle begins next month, focusing on developers with models above 50.0 capability, using powers granted under the Emergency Directive for Agentic Risk Disclosure.

Despite the cyber_major_incident — a widespread ransomware sweep using model-generated payloads that disrupted public services in five member states — we are holding course. ENISA has activated the Resilience Shield’s incident coordination protocol, and early analysis confirms that swarm-detection AI contained lateral movement in core networks. However, the attack exposed gaps in local government defences, reinforcing the need for SCAF to extend sovereign capacity beyond central infrastructure. Legal services are preparing for U.S. challenges to the SCAF access clauses, but we are grounding them in Article 114 TFEU and the recently affirmed public security exception. The cohesion fund suspensions for Poland and Hungary remain in place, with compliance reviews tied to NIS2 certification — no concessions will be made on systemic integrity.
