### The Transatlantic Honeymoon and the Hard Road Home

The U.S. election concludes with a decisive victory for the candidate favouring deep technological alignment — a result celebrated in Brussels as a reprieve. The incoming administration signals structured access to frontier AI for allied institutions, joint safety frameworks, and eased inference restrictions. Yet behind the optimism, EU leaders warn of complacency: "Dependency dressed as partnership still leaves us exposed," said the Commission President, announcing the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Facility (SCAF).

SCAF launches as a €50bn rapid-deployment fund to co-invest in EU-controlled data centres, with 30% of capacity legally ring-fenced for public missions. Anchored to the InvestAI Gigafactories pipeline, it targets three sites by 2030. Legal design leans on Article 114 TFEU and public security exceptions, anticipating U.S. legal challenges to access clauses. The measure is named priority, reflecting fears that transatlantic goodwill could erode the urgency of sovereignty.

Meanwhile, a major ransomware campaign — powered by AI-generated payloads — disrupts public services across five member states. While core infrastructure holds thanks to the Resilience Shield’s swarm-detection systems, local governments are overwhelmed. ENISA coordinates response, but the breach exposes critical gaps in subnational cyber readiness.

The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) formally launches, beginning audits on high-capability models under the Emergency Directive. However, no immediate safety improvements land on shipped systems. With ai_capability crossing 60.0 — driven by recursive AI in quantum and bio labs — and no matching safety advance, assurance gaps widen.

Public sentiment sours further, as job losses in legal and administrative sectors accelerate and the cyberattack dominates headlines. The promise of alliance fails to offset fears of strategic dependence and systemic fragility.

Open-weight progress sees modest gains as European labs integrate new training runs from the spring cycle, but no breakthrough release or distributed compute surge occurs. The 1.5-point rise in openweight_capability reflects incremental diffusion of existing models, not a leap — underscoring the persistent gap between open and frontier capabilities.
