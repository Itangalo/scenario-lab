## In practice  
We are activating the Critical Infrastructure AI Resilience Shield under Article 122 TFEU, treating the AI-driven cyber intrusion as a cross-border crisis threatening essential services. ENISA, in coordination with the European Defence Agency and national CSIRTs, is issuing binding deployment orders to operators of essential services in energy, rail, and health, mandating integration of the new AI patching and swarm-detection stack by the end of this turn. Funding of €15bn is being drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with co-financing waived — a move already challenged legally by Poland and Hungary, but we are prepared to escalate to the Commission’s crisis powers under the NIS2 Directive to enforce compliance. Biweekly progress reports will go to the Crisis Coordination Mechanism, with operational dashboards shared with the European Council.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track for site finalisation, though resistance in Brittany and northern Sweden persists. We are offering binding energy reinvestment packages — €500m for regional smart grid upgrades and green hydrogen production — in exchange for accelerated permitting, leveraging the Net-Zero Industry Act to fast-track environmental assessments. ENTSO-E has confirmed 12GW of reserved grid capacity, pending national energy plans. The Tech Sovereignty Package advances through the Digital Markets Council, where we are linking accelerated permitting to enforceable commitments on EU-based operational control and energy efficiency. The legal service is finalising the Article 114 TFEU directive to harmonise permitting timelines, aiming for a June trilogue.

The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) continues forming despite the CJEU challenge to the hyperscaler levy. We are proceeding with initial staffing via secondments and fast-track recruitment offering private-sector parity pay, focusing on agent monitoring and interpretability. A shadow directorate under ENISA is drafting evaluation protocols for models above 50.0 capability, with red-team scenarios for autonomous coordination and cyber-physical risks. We are preparing a revised funding mechanism — a temporary solidarity contribution from large digital service providers — to be proposed if the levy remains blocked by turn 4.
