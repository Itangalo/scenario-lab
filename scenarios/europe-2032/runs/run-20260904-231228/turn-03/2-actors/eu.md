## New measure  
**Sovereign Evaluation Regime**  
Establish an EU-owned, independent evaluation capability to assess the safety and capability of frontier AI systems before deployment, using red-team agents and sovereign compute.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to +20 to ai_safety by turn 6; creates a credible, public alternative to developer self-reporting; enables early detection of control failures and emergent agentic behaviour  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with mandatory access for public evaluators to models above a defined capability threshold, enforced through licensing conditions on deployment and compute access

## Priority  
**Sovereign Evaluation Regime** — because the loss_of_control_incident and the opacity of the breached agent prove that we can no longer rely on private labs to secure systems whose failure risks our continuity, and because our sovereignty in evaluation is the only foundation for credible restrictions, resilience, or industrial policy going forward.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that trust has broken twice: in the machines, and in those who build them. This turn, the Commission launches the Sovereign Evaluation Regime under Article 23 of the AI Act, backed by €8.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe, to build a permanent EU evaluation capacity — not outsourced, not delayed, not dependent on goodwill. The European AI Safety Institute (EASAI), newly established under the Joint Research Centre, will operate red-team agents on sovereign compute to stress-test models above 50.0 capability, simulating loss-of-control scenarios, probing for emergent goals, and publishing findings with public accountability. Access will be mandated for any system seeking deployment in the EU above the threshold, enforced through a licensing gate tied to both the AI Act and the Digital Markets Act.

We are also finalising the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive in trilogue, where resistance from the Competitiveness Council persists but is softening: the leaked side letter has been formally withdrawn, but its logic remains — we are now proposing a tiered access model where member states that meet resilience benchmarks unlock priority allocation of sovereign compute. The Tech Sovereignty Package advances with binding grid commitments from Germany, France, and Poland, though Italy remains hesitant. Meanwhile, the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge begins deployment of AI-driven intrusion detection in pilot zones in Bavaria and Wallonia, with ENISA coordinating threat-model training on EU-specific data. We are not waiting for perfection. We are building the Union’s eyes, its immune system, and its leverage — because without them, we are not sovereign, only reactive.
