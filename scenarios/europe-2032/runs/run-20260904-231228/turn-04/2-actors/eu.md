## New measure  
**European AI Safety Institute (EASAI) Operational Launch**  
Stand up the core red-teaming and evaluation teams of the European AI Safety Institute, deploy sovereign compute clusters to support them, and begin mandatory pre-deployment assessments for all models above 50.0 capability entering the EU market.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to +18 to ai_safety by turn 6; establishes EU as a credible independent evaluator of frontier models; enables enforcement of safety thresholds under the AI Act; forces transparency from developers via binding access mandates  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with mandatory participation for any provider deploying models above 50.0 capability, enforced through licensing gates tied to compute access and market entry

## Priority  
**Sovereign Evaluation Regime** — because our credibility in preventing harm depends on seeing what others would rather we didn’t, and because without sovereign evaluation, every restriction, resilience measure, and industrial policy we build rests on trust we no longer have.

## In practice  
We are turning promise into presence. This turn, the Commission finalises the legal foundation for the European AI Safety Institute (EASAI), embedding it within the Joint Research Centre but with operational independence and direct reporting lines to the College. €3.1bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe is now committed to procurement of sovereign GPU clusters—secured under EU export controls and physically hosted in Finland and Luxembourg—while recruitment begins for 400 core staff: red-team leads, interpretability researchers, and agent-behaviour specialists. We are not outsourcing scrutiny. We are building it in-house, on EU soil, under EU law.

The first binding assessments under the Sovereign Evaluation Regime will target three incoming models above 55.0 capability, all from non-EU developers. Access is mandated via amendments to the AI Act’s conformity assessment process, backed by the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions—no model above threshold enters the single market without evaluation. Resistance from the US and UK providers is expected; we are preparing counter-leverage through the Anti-Coercion Instrument and coordinated scrutiny with Japan and Canada. Domestically, the Competitiveness Council remains uneasy, but the campaign_atlanticist has shifted the tone: dependency without reciprocity is now seen as strategic weakness, not pragmatism. We are using that opening to lock in evaluation as the baseline of sovereignty—not just for safety, but for credibility, for leverage, and for the right to act.
