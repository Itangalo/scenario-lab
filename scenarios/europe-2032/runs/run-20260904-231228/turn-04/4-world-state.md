### Capability Creeps Forward
AI capability advances by 1.5 points to 58.5, driven by tighter training loops and marginal gains in efficiency. The frontier remains closed, but labs report improved agent persistence and multi-step reasoning in controlled environments. No breakthroughs are confirmed, yet the trajectory suggests the 60.0 threshold may fall within a year.

### Open Weights Follow, Quietly
Openweight capability rises to 47.5, sustained by community-driven optimizations of last year’s architectures. While no new frontier model is released openly, adaptations enable broader deployment of semi-autonomous agents in cyber reconnaissance and localised disinformation. Detection remains patchy, especially in under-resourced municipalities.

### EU Builds Evaluation Muscle
The European AI Safety Institute (EASAI) launches operationally, with legal foundations ratified and €3.1bn committed to sovereign GPU procurement in Finland and Luxembourg. Recruitment begins for red-teaming and interpretability staff. The first mandatory assessments are scheduled for three non-EU models above 55.0 capability, enforced via AI Act conformity rules and DM Act leverage.

However, pushback emerges swiftly. US and UK developers challenge the access mandates as extraterritorial, and legal teams prepare cases under bilateral trade frameworks. Domestically, the Competitiveness Council voices concern over retaliation risks. While the *Sovereign Evaluation Regime* is named priority, its pull-in effect fails—governance delays prevent acceleration.

### Resilience Stalls, Capital Erodes
The *Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge* remains delayed in Italy and parts of Eastern Europe. With no active deployment, resilience dips slightly as capability grows unchecked. Political capital declines: the portfolio’s cost—four large measures and a priority—drains 13 points, and the proposal bonus for EASAI is modest (+1), given the lack of recent major incidents. Sentiment holds steady at 37.0, unmoved by technical initiatives.
