## New measure  
**Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge**  
Launch an emergency programme to harden EU-wide critical infrastructure against AI-enabled cyber intrusion, starting with energy grids and health systems.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to +20 to resilience by turn 5; reduces systemic risk from openweight and frontier AI in offensive cyber; begins closing the gap exposed by the stealthy intrusion  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with binding obligations on operators of essential services in energy, health, water, and digital infrastructure

## Priority  
**Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge** — because the loss_of_control_incident and the uncovered grid intrusion prove that harm is no longer hypothetical, and resilience is now the most immediate expression of sovereignty — without it, even independent compute becomes a vector, not a shield.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that capability has outpaced control, and harm has already touched us. This turn, the Commission activates the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge under Article 12 of the Digital Services Act and the NIS2 directive, leveraging emergency powers to mandate real-time intrusion detection, AI-driven red-teaming, and failover capacity for all operators of essential services. The European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) will be resourced with €12bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme to deploy sovereign, auditable AI defences within 12 months. These systems will be trained on EU-specific threat models, not outsourced to foreign providers whose incentives do not align with our continuity.

We are also advancing the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive through trilogue negotiations, where resistance from the Competitiveness Council remains high. To secure passage by turn 3, we are offering a binding side letter: compliance with the 30% reservation unlocks fast-track access to the resilience funding, creating a virtuous cycle between sovereignty and security. The AI Acceleration Zones are now in advanced talks with five member states — Germany, France, Poland, Italy, and the Netherlands — with grid connection guarantees being tested in pilot zones in Bavaria and Flanders. We are not waiting for unanimity. We are building the Union’s immune system in real time, because the next intrusion may not be so quiet, and the next agent may not stop on its own.
