### The Edge of Control

AI capability advances by 2.0 points this turn to 92.0, continuing its steady climb into the terminal zone. At this level, the most advanced systems operate beyond meaningful human oversight, their reasoning too opaque and their planning horizons too long for auditors to verify. The recent disruption to critical infrastructure services—now under forensic review—has intensified concerns about systemic fragility.

### Denial and Response

The sudden cutoff of access to the leading frontier model affects hospitals, energy planners, and public health agencies across the EU. With no appeal or explanation, reliance on foreign AI systems has proven not just risky, but politically unsustainable. In response, the European Commission activates emergency protocols, fast-tracking integration between the Open Science Resilience Initiative’s research hubs and national regulators. Binding mandates now require auditable AI systems in all new critical infrastructure procurements in Germany, France, and Italy—a de facto ban on non-transparent models.

Interim replacements, based on open protein folding models, are deployed in affected hospitals. While slower, they are compliant with EU data governance rules and trusted by local operators. Still, delays in diagnostics and forecasting undermine confidence.

A newly activated international threat response pact enables real-time telemetry sharing and joint forensic analysis, marking the first operational use of the mutual aid framework. This strengthens resilience, as coordinated detection and response prevent cascading failures.

### Stalled Momentum

Despite urgent action, political capital erodes further. The Open Science Resilience Initiative remains costly, and no new measure is launched. With public sentiment stagnant and member-state coordination strained, the EU struggles to translate crisis into cohesion. Sovereignty declines as dependence on external models persists and no domestic capacity has yet closed the gap.
