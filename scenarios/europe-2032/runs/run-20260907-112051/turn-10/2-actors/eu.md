## In practice
This turn, we are accelerating the integration of the Open Science Resilience Initiative with operational agencies, treating it not as a research programme but as an emergency sovereign capability build. The Commission has activated the Crisis Coordination Protocol under Article 122a TFEU, directing DG CONNECT, DG SANTE, and the European Defence Agency to fast-track interoperability between the three operational research hubs and national biosecurity and energy regulators. Binding mandates have been issued to Germany, France, and Italy to designate auditable AI systems as the sole permitted class for any AI deployed in critical infrastructure procurement by Q3 2031—a de facto moratorium on frontier models in these domains unless they meet full interpretability and logging standards.

We are responding to eu_frontier_access_denied by convening an emergency COREPER session to coordinate legal and technical countermeasures. The denial affects over 120 hospitals and 18 ministries relying on the blocked model for diagnostics and forecasting. We are deploying interim replacements from the open protein folding models, which, though slower, are already integrated into EU health data environments and legally compliant. The European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) is conducting a forensic audit of the incident chain, with real-time data sharing activated under the newly formed **joint_threat_response** pact—marking the first operational use of the mutual aid framework. This gives us attribution capacity we could not have built alone, but it also exposes how little leverage we have when access is unilaterally withdrawn. The message is clear: resilience without sovereignty is temporary. Our next move must close that gap.
