# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act on assumptions about safety that the evidence has falsified, nor on assumptions about political capital that reality has exhausted, and we will not again invoke emergency powers without first securing the means to enforce them.
- `restore_legitimacy_first` (commitment): We will halt the expansion of AI in public services until confidence in fairness, redress and labour impact is restored — but we now recognise that legitimacy cannot be rebuilt through control alone, nor through inaction. With public services collapsing and access to frontier AI severed, we must now prioritise survival of function over procedural purity. The pause has become indistinguishable from surrender.

  - Trigger: the **cyber_major_incident**, **ai_investment_collapse**, and **eu_frontier_access_denied**
  - Grounds: The emergency has failed. We invoked powers we did not possess, issued directives we could not enforce, and promised action while lacking staff, energy, capital, and cohesion. The world has now delivered three shocks in one turn: a major cyberattack lands while defences are unmanned, the investment base vanishes beneath our feet, and we are cut off from the very systems our hospitals and ministries depend on. These are not failures of will — they are failures of material capacity. We still reject paralysis, but we now see that legitimacy is not restored by decree. From this point forward, no commitment to act will be made without a credible path to enforcement — not because we value caution, but because we have learned that unenforced action destroys what little capital remains.
  - Evidence quoted: **cyber_major_incident:** A large, largely automated attack lands. Its form is chosen at the time – a ransomware sweep across public services, a compromised software dependency with an unknown blast radius, or disruption of infrastructure directly – and severity scales with how far offensive capability has outrun defence. Damage is real and public, defenders are visibly behind, and attribution takes months. Whoever ran it, the tooling was model-generated.
**ai_investment_collapse:** Capital flees the sector. Valuations reset hard, announced build-out is cancelled rather than delayed, and several of the arrangements European compute was depending on evaporate with it. What the frontier laboratories can afford to train shrinks for the first time.
**eu_frontier_access_denied:** The Union is cut off from the leading model at short notice, wholly or by nationality of user. No detailed reason is given, there is no appeal, and the immediate practical effect lands on hospitals, ministries and firms that had built on it. Whether this reads at home as an outrage or as a failure of foresight depends on what the Union had done about it beforehand.
  - Reason: The developments expose the EU's lack of enforcement capacity, directly challenging its ability to act on assumptions about political capital and emergency powers.
