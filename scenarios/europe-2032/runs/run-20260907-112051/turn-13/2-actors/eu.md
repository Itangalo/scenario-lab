### Statement changes  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act on assumptions about safety that the evidence has falsified, nor on assumptions about political capital that reality has exhausted, and we will not again invoke emergency powers without first securing the means to enforce them.  
- Trigger: the **cyber_major_incident**, **ai_investment_collapse**, and **eu_frontier_access_denied**  
- Grounds: The emergency has failed. We invoked powers we did not possess, issued directives we could not enforce, and promised action while lacking staff, energy, capital, and cohesion. The world has now delivered three shocks in one turn: a major cyberattack lands while defences are unmanned, the investment base vanishes beneath our feet, and we are cut off from the very systems our hospitals and ministries depend on. These are not failures of will — they are failures of material capacity. We still reject paralysis, but we now see that legitimacy is not restored by decree. From this point forward, no commitment to act will be made without a credible path to enforcement — not because we value caution, but because we have learned that unenforced action destroys what little capital remains.

- modify `restore_legitimacy_first` (commitment): We will halt the expansion of AI in public services until confidence in fairness, redress and labour impact is restored — but we now recognise that legitimacy cannot be rebuilt through control alone, nor through inaction. With public services collapsing and access to frontier AI severed, we must now prioritise survival of function over procedural purity. The pause has become indistinguishable from surrender.  
- Grounds: The pause was meant to create space for trust. Instead, it has become a cover for incapacity. The public sees no redress, no oversight, no alternative — only dysfunction. The **cyber_major_incident** proves that harm is already here, not in some hypothetical future. We are not abandoning the goal of legitimacy — we are admitting that legitimacy requires functioning systems. From now on, rebuilding trust must go hand in hand with restoring service, not waiting for it.

- retire `two_mandates` (identity)  
- Trigger: the **systemic_administrative_collapse**, **cyber_major_incident**, and **eu_frontier_access_denied**  
- Grounds: The two mandates — sovereignty and safety — were framed as tensions within a functioning polity. But we are no longer a functioning polity. With eu_political_capital at 0.0, public_sentiment at 15.0, and no enforcement of any directive, the Union no longer has the capacity to pursue either mandate in practice. To claim we still hold both is to mistake legal form for political reality. The identity assumed a sovereign decision-maker; we are now a legal shell responding to cascading failure. We do not reject the ideals — we acknowledge they are beyond our reach until we can reconstitute the Union as an acting body. A new identity must emerge from survival, not be declared over its corpse.

## In practice  
This turn, we are shifting from the illusion of control to the practice of survival. The Crisis Cell in DG GROW remains unfunded and inactive — we cannot staff it, so we are bypassing it. Instead, we are activating the Civil Protection Mechanism under Article 8 of Decision 1313/2013/EU to coordinate a cross-border emergency response for AI-enabled critical services. The European Health Data Space will be repurposed to host decentralised, low-capability AI enclaves — running on existing EuroHPC Tier-2 clusters — to support triage, diagnostics, and supply forecasting in national health systems. These will not match frontier models, but they will run, they will be auditable, and they will be ours.

We are directing EDF and Recovery and Resilience Facility funds — already approved but unspent — toward rapid cyber hardening of energy grid operators and emergency services, using open-weight models at capability 60.0 that we can actually inspect and patch. The European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) will lead a 90-day sprint to deploy automated intrusion detection trained on pre-96.0 threat models, knowing it will be outmatched but hoping to buy time. We are also negotiating with neutral research institutes in Switzerland and Norway to host joint audit pods, using their access to frontier weights to reverse-engineer safety signatures we can deploy locally.

No one is following our directives — so we are building where we can, not where we command. We are not restoring sovereignty through force. We are trying to grow it back in the cracks.
