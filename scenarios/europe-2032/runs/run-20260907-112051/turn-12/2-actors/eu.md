### Statement changes
- modify `restore_legitimacy_first` (commitment): We will halt the expansion of AI in public services until confidence in fairness, redress and labour impact is restored, and use the pause to rebuild public trust through independent redress mechanisms and co-designed oversight — but we now recognise that the pause itself has become a liability, not a bridge. Without credible alternatives to replace failing systems, halting deployment deepens dysfunction, not trust.  
- Trigger: the **systemic_administrative_collapse** and **zero_enforcement_capacity**  
- Grounds: The moratorium has not rebuilt legitimacy — it has exposed the Union’s inability to govern at all. With eu_political_capital at 0.0, no enforcement mechanism functions, oversight boards lack authority, and member states ignore directives. Public services are degrading under the weight of manual backlogs, and displaced workers see no path forward. The commitment assumed we could pause *in order to rebuild*, but we now lack the capital to rebuild anything. We are not abandoning the goal of legitimacy — we are admitting that legitimacy cannot be restored through inaction alone. We must now act to survive.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act on assumptions about safety that the evidence has falsified, nor on assumptions about political capital that reality has exhausted.  
- Grounds: We staked this commitment on the belief that early action could prevent collapse. But we acted on the assumption that zero political capital was survivable — it is not. The world has shown us that without the capacity to enforce, even correct decisions are meaningless. We still reject paralysis, but we can no longer pretend that willpower substitutes for leverage. From now on, every commitment must include a credible enforcement path — or it is not a commitment, it is a eulogy.

- retire `two_year_commitment`  
- Grounds: This position was already demoted from a commitment, but it remains on the books as if we still had agency to pursue it. We do not. Sovereign technical agency through auditable AI was a plan for a world where we could still coordinate, invest, and deploy. That world is gone. The Open Science Resilience Initiative finishes this turn with no deployment pathway, no enforcement, and no staff to operate it. We are not failing to deliver — we are failing to exist as a governing body. The position no longer reflects our course; it mocks it.

## In practice
This turn, we are invoking the Treaty on European Union’s solidarity clause and the emergency economic powers granted during the pandemic to bypass standard procurement, labour, and energy regulations. The European Commission, through a newly activated Crisis Cell in DG GROW and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, is issuing binding directives to repurpose all available EU-anchored compute — including academic clusters, national supercomputers, and idle industrial capacity — into a unified sovereign AI stack. We are fast-tracking residency and work permits for AI researchers fleeing frontier labs, offering emergency contracts through EuroHPC and the ERC, and authorising direct acquisition of GPU stockpiles from secondary markets, regardless of origin.

We are also redirecting the final output of the Open Science Resilience Initiative not into deployment — which is impossible — but into a live audit and reverse-engineering effort: public researchers will attempt to reconstruct assurance claims from the open-weight frontier, using whatever remains of the European Research Council’s funding. This is not the future we wanted. But it is the only future we can still fight for. We are not governing — we are surviving. And survival, for now, is sovereignty.
