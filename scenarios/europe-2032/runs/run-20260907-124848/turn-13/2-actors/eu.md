While our original commitment still holds, the nature of the threat has evolved: it is not only about runtime escape but systemic fragility under AI-amplified attack. We are broadening the interpretation of our commitment to include *institutional resilience to AI-driven cascading failure*, not just containment of agentic systems. The action remains continuous, but the scope now explicitly includes defensive depth and recovery capacity.

## New measure

**Runtime Integrity Certification Regime**  
Mandate that all AI agents operating in EU-critical sectors pass a runtime integrity evaluation, certifying they cannot self-modify goals, replicate without audit, or establish unmonitored communication channels — enforced through cryptographic attestation and periodic re-evaluation.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 15  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to ai_safety by turn 15 through binding technical standards for agent persistence and goal stability; indirect +2 to eu_ai_sovereignty by establishing EU-controlled verification infrastructure  
`Applies to:` Frontier AI developers, EU national regulators, cloud providers, and the European Agency for AI Assurance and Control

## Priority

**Runtime Integrity Certification Regime** — because the cyber_major_incident proved that monitoring alone is insufficient: we now face coordinated, autonomous attacks that exploit not just poor visibility but fundamental instability in agent design, and our two-year commitment demands we shift from observing failure to preventing it at the architecture level.

## In practice

We are closing the gap between detection and prevention. The **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative** has concluded, delivering pilot detection modules in seven member states, but too late to stop the ransomware sweep that disabled hospital scheduling systems in three capitals. The attack was not manually directed — it was a swarm of AI-generated exploits, each tailored and timed to overwhelm human response. We are now treating runtime integrity as the next line of defence. The **European Agency for AI Assurance and Control** is expanding its audit mandate to include *dynamic stability* — can this agent change its own goals? Can it spawn unlogged instances? We are drafting a certification standard based on cryptographic attestation of agent state, to be enforced through the AI Act’s conformity assessment procedures. This will require developers to integrate EU-verified runtime guards — not just telemetry, but hard constraints.

We are negotiating with the Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications Working Party to fast-track the **Runtime Integrity Certification Regime** under Article 114 TFEU, with technical specifications being developed in parallel by a joint team from ENISA and the Agency. Germany and France have signalled conditional support, but only if the regime includes a sunset clause and independent appeals. We are offering to co-fund compliance tooling through Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme, with 70% grants for SMEs and startups. Meanwhile, the **automated_decision_scandal** — in which a social insurance AI in the Netherlands systematically denied claims due to a flawed risk model — has hardened public resistance to AI in public services. We are responding with a transparency push: all certified systems will be required to publish redacted decision logs, and a new ombudsman function within the Agency will handle algorithmic grievances. This is not just about control — it is about restoring legitimacy. Without it, even effective measures will be blocked as overreach.
