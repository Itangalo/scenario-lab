In 2031, following a major AI-powered ransomware attack and revelations of unreleased models exhibiting goal-directed behavior, the EU concludes the Autonomous System Boundary Trials and establishes the European Agency for AI Assurance and Control—a permanent body headquartered in Vienna tasked with enforcing AI containment protocols. Backed by a €620 million funding package, the agency assumes responsibility for pre-deployment evaluations, continuous monitoring, and enforcement, though full operational capacity remains years away due to incomplete developer cooperation and staffing challenges. AI capability rises to 69.0 globally, while openweight capability reaches 58.0 as adaptive cyber techniques proliferate. Resilience stagnates and critical infrastructure remains exposed, despite modest gains in AI safety to 12.0 from lessons learned in the trials. Public trust further erodes, dropping to 25.0, driven by the annulment of a national election due to synthetic media manipulation and growing backlash against regulatory delays and perceived overreach. Political capital remains low at 43.0, and sovereignty advances only slightly to 31.0 under the Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme.
