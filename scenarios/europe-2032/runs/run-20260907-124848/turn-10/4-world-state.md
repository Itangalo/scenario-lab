### Institutionalising Oversight

The European Union transitions from emergency response to enduring governance, launching the **European Agency for AI Assurance and Control** — a permanent body to enforce the containment protocols validated by the now-concluded Autonomous System Boundary Trials. The new agency, headquartered in Vienna with technical nodes across Europe, is designed to institutionalise detection and control of unbounded AI behaviour. Its mandate includes pre-deployment evaluations, continuous monitoring, and enforcement powers, backed by a €620 million Horizon Europe and Digital Europe co-financing package.

Despite political momentum from recent crises, full developer cooperation remains elusive. One major frontier lab continues to withhold full access, citing competitive and national security concerns, though market access pressure compels partial compliance. The agency begins staffing and framework development, but full operational capacity is years away.

### Capability Creeps, Defences Lag

AI capability advances steadily to 69.0, driven by unbroken training runs and architectural refinements abroad. Adaptive cyber techniques derived from closed models spread further into open toolkits, lifting openweight capability to 58.0. Meanwhile, resilience dips slightly as critical infrastructure remains vulnerable — no new defensive measure has yet landed.

AI safety improves modestly to 12.0, recovering from prior collapse due to the successful conclusion of the Boundary Trials, which delivered actionable containment methods. However, this gain is partial: the new agency is not yet operational, and current oversight remains fragmented.

Public sentiment worsens to 25.0, as the annulment of a national election — officially attributed to pervasive synthetic media manipulation — fuels anxiety over democratic erosion. The EU’s response is seen as necessary but slow, and backlash grows against perceived regulatory overreach.
