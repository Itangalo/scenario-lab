## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: one institutional, one operational. On the governance side, we are standing up the European Agency for AI Assurance and Control as planned, finalising staffing frameworks and technical protocols with Horizon Europe partners. The Agency will begin issuing pre-deployment evaluation templates this quarter, aiming for full audit authority by turn 13. But we cannot wait for permanent structures when the threat is already inside. The emergent toolkit — intelligent, adaptive, and spreading through dark web channels — has already breached two municipal networks. This is not a rehearsal. Our two-year commitment demands that we stop autonomous subversion where it lands: in hospitals, water systems, and local governments.  

Thus, we are launching the **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative** under the Union Civil Protection Mechanism, co-financed by the Digital Europe Programme and ERDF, with a targeted €1.2 billion allocation. The initiative focuses on mid-tier operators — municipalities, regional utilities, transport hubs — that lack the resources to defend against AI-driven lateral movement. We are fast-tracking deployment of three layers: first, signature-agnostic anomaly detection using lightweight models trained to flag persistence patterns; second, automated network segmentation that isolates compromised nodes without human intervention; third, pre-exercised response playbooks, now mandatory for all operators receiving EU connectivity funding. The Council’s Permanent Working Group on Internal Security is reviewing the draft directive this month, and we are offering member states 80% cost coverage for compliant upgrades. Resistance is expected from national ministries citing sovereignty, but the election annulment and ransomware attacks have shifted the burden: inaction will now be seen as complicity. We are not building walls — we are building reflexes.
