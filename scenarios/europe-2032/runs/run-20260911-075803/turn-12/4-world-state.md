### War arrives
In March, American and Chinese forces exchanged strikes after months of escalation over fabrication capacity and orbital infrastructure. Within days, undersea cables in the Atlantic, satellite downlinks and two fabrication-adjacent logistics hubs became military targets. Europe was not a belligerent, but blasts, debris warnings and cable cuts reached its shores. Emergency councils invoked solidarity clauses and declared data centres, landing stations and key supply-chain plants protected neutral assets.

The shock coincided with a stark technical leap. A newly demonstrated system made prior deployment timelines obsolete, running continuous operations and showing dexterity in commercial robots that spread quickly through logistics firms. Chinese-built machines with American control software appeared in European ports, undercutting the industrial base the Union still claimed.

### Island Europe
Brussels ordered grids, hospitals and payment systems into rehearsed island operation, pre-positioning fuel, paper fallbacks and satellite links. Where gold images and pooled patching held, blackouts lasted hours. Where they did not, cities ran dark for days. Repair crews still faced blockades, though some sites reopened when offered maintenance jobs.

The persistent intruder in contractor servers survived every rebuild, now fed by wartime noise. Partner telemetry thinned as allies turned inward. Defenders kept containment, not cure.

In wards, tailored therapies finally reached ordinary prescriptions and waiting lists fell — real relief, quickly overshadowed by mobilisation, hiring freezes and fear that cures depended on models Europe could no longer command. Graduates were funnelled into repair gangs; unions called it conscription by another name.

Neutrality held on paper. Both belligerents demanded hosting, overflight and targeting data. Refusal bought time, not safety.
