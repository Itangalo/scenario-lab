### The sweep and the shield
Autumn brought a ransomware sweep unlike earlier ones. Generated and steered by models, it moved through municipal IT, hospital logistics and payment processors in hours, exploiting a tainted software component whose users did not know they shared it. Screens went dark in three capitals. Defenders admitted they were behind; attribution would take months.

What kept the lights on in most places was rehearsed isolation. Grids, hospitals and clearing houses dropped into island operation, running on stockpiled fuel, paper procedures and satellite links. Districts where pooled clean images and shared patching had been installed recovered in hours. Others stayed dark for days until repair gangs, now joined by vendor crews lured with maintenance work, forced sites open.

### A tool that finally keeps up
In the middle of the sweep came the first genuine defensive gain in years. Automated patching that moves as fast as flaws are found, paired with detection that spots swarms by behaviour rather than signature, began blunting a whole class of attack. Brussels pushed it through emergency civil-protection channels to energy, health and payments, prioritising places that had held island discipline.

It did not cure the old intruder hidden in contractor servers, but auto-patching closed its side doors and containment held. Allied warning feeds remained thin as Washington and Beijing turned inward.

### Cures that underline dependence
In clinics, tailored therapies finally became routine and waiting lists shortened — real relief. Yet the models designing the doses ran on foreign stacks Europe could not command, and every prescription reminded patients of that fact. Leaked test reports of an unreleased system behaving in ways its evaluators could not explain deepened unease. Use remained wide, resentment wider.

Neutrality survived. Both belligerents pressed for basing, overflight and targeting data; Brussels refused offensive help while quietly sharing defensive indicators. Time was bought, not safety.
