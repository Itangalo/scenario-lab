### The Calm Before the Next Storm

Global AI capability crept upward to 63.0, as frontier labs outside Europe advanced incrementally in agent coordination and inference efficiency. No breakthroughs were reported, but the quiet progression deepened the gap between what systems can do and what anyone can control.

The EU completed the Open Integration Programme, successfully adapting the Zurich interpretability tool to analyse high-capability models up to the 52.0 threshold and linking a Brussels node with national research institutes. While technically sound, the system remains experimental—unable to monitor closed models running on foreign infrastructure, and offering no enforcement power. Grassroots monitoring groups in Germany and Scandinavia expanded their parallel detection efforts, sharing anomalies with CERTs, but without legal authority or sustained funding.

Meanwhile, the Expand Resilience Surge entered its second phase, with ENISA finalising designs for autonomous containment in critical infrastructure. Protocols for SCADA override, diagnostic rollback, and rail routing isolation were drafted and shared with member states. However, political capital remains frozen at 10.0, paralyzing decision-making. The proposed Council regulation on emergency AI access, intended to bypass national vetoes, stalled in the Telecommunications Working Party as two member states threatened legal challenges under data sovereignty grounds.

With no new measures launched and the priority having no effect below the 20.0 threshold, the Union drifts in damage-control mode. Public sentiment held at 18.0, as hospitals reported ongoing disruptions from lost AI support and media coverage focused on bureaucratic inertia. The emergent sovereign inference network in the Nordic-Benelux bloc continued operating independently, raising concerns about fragmentation of EU digital governance.

### Sovereignty Without Leverage

Despite technical progress, the EU’s strategic position did not improve. Access to frontier AI remains severed, and with no domestic compute base to fall back on, sovereignty stayed at 10.0. Resilience saw no gain—design work is not deployment—and political capital eroded further under the weight of unresolved legal battles and intergovernmental friction.
