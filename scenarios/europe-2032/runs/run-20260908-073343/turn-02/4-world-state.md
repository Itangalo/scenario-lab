### A Narrowing Window

The EU doubled down on operational resilience, finalising legal empowerment of the European Cyber Resilience Board and releasing €12bn in conditional funding to accelerate adoption of AI intrusion detection across critical sectors. ENISA advanced detection templates targeting slow-burn, multi-vector attacks, and incentives were tied to digital modernisation funds. Yet progress remained uneven: only six member states met early milestones, and industrial resistance delayed open-sourcing of key monitoring tools. The Cyber Shield, while symbolically potent, showed strain in execution.

Behind the scenes, technical scoping began for the Sovereign Deployment Corridor, aiming to bind EU-controlled compute, networking, and governance into a single trusted stack. However, legal anchoring under the Digital Markets Act faced early challenges from U.S. legal teams, raising doubts about enforceability against foreign override.

### The Frontier Pulls Ahead

AI capability advanced to 54.5 as agent coordination and training efficiency improved. No corresponding safety upgrades landed on deployed systems. With the gap between capability and control widening—and no new safeguards in force—*ai_safety* dropped by 3 points, reflecting growing unease in technical circles.

Meanwhile, *openweight_capability* rose to 43.0, as slightly improved open models circulated, narrowing the gap with the closed frontier. No major release occurred, but incremental diffusion continued.

### Fractures Deepen

The earlier member state defection continued to erode cohesion. With no new sovereignty-building measures completing, and *ai_capability* rising by 1.5, *eu_ai_sovereignty* fell by 1 under rule 5. The new Sovereign Deployment Corridor, though large and ambitious, is still in flight and yields no immediate benefit.

Political capital deteriorated sharply. Three large measures remained in flight, costing −9 per turn, and the named priority added −1. No proposal bonus applied, as the new measure responded to structural gaps, not a recent incident. With *eu_political_capital* now at 30.0, the EU can pursue only one major initiative at a time.

Public sentiment dipped further, as protests near data centres grew and media coverage linked AI to both security failures and job displacement. No visible public benefit offset the narrative.
