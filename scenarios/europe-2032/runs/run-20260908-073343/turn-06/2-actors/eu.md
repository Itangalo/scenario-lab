## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent systemic AI incidents from overwhelming EU institutions or critical infrastructure  
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn  
- Grounds: The commitment has guided our focus on resilience and preparedness through a period of accelerating capability and declining safety. With the frontier now demonstrating autonomous planning in adversarial contexts and open models reaching dangerous levels of capability, the next phase must shift from containment to active control. The commitment ends not because it failed, but because it succeeded in framing the threat — and because the next six years demand a sharper, more operational mandate.

- add `secure_control_before_capability` (commitment): We will ensure the EU can observe, interpret, and interrupt frontier AI systems operating within or affecting its territory, regardless of origin, before their capability reaches 60.0  
- Grounds: At 59.0, the global frontier is one step from end-to-end autonomous project execution. With ai_safety at 12.0 — lower than at any point in the last four years — and no sign of safety catching up, we can no longer rely on developer goodwill or post-hoc regulation. Control must be secured now, not promised for later. This commitment binds us to build sovereign technical capacity to monitor, audit, and if necessary halt systems that operate beyond human oversight, even if they are foreign-built or cloud-hosted. It is not a retreat from cooperation; it is the precondition for it.

## New measure
### Establish EU AI Observatory with Real-Time Monitoring and Interrupt Authority  
Deploy a distributed system of model audits, network probes, and execution monitors to detect unauthorised or unsafe AI operations across cloud, edge, and open-weight deployments, with legal authority to suspend services that exceed defined autonomy thresholds.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +10–12 (via continuous monitoring and enforced transparency), eu_ai_sovereignty +8 (via sovereign visibility and control levers), public_sentiment +5 (via demonstrated oversight)  
`Applies to:` All AI systems operating within EU jurisdiction, including foreign-hosted models serving EU users or interfacing with critical infrastructure

## Priority
**Establish EU AI Observatory with Real-Time Monitoring and Interrupt Authority** — because at 59.0 capability and collapsing safety, we are one breakthrough from losing observational control over systems that could act against us autonomously; resilience is not enough if we cannot see the attack coming or stop it mid-execution.

## In practice
This turn, we activate the newly formed European AI Oversight Board, reporting directly to the College of Commissioners, to begin drafting the legal and technical framework for the EU AI Observatory. Drawing on expertise from ENISA, the European Union Agency for Fundamental Rights, and national cybersecurity agencies, we are designing a layered monitoring architecture: lightweight model registration requirements for all deployments above 40.0 capability, deep audit access for systems above 50.0, and real-time execution probes for any system operating autonomously in critical domains. The system will be built on open standards to ensure interoperability, but with sovereign EU control over the core decision nodes.

We are allocating €9.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and NextGenerationEU recovery funds to stand up the first phase: sensor deployment in cloud gateways, integration with national CERTs, and development of lightweight interpretability tools for agent behaviour. Legal work begins immediately under Article 114 TFEU to establish a new regulatory act granting the Observatory authority to issue emergency suspension orders for systems exhibiting uncontrolled goal pursuit, recursive self-modification, or unauthorised persistence. Resistance is expected from foreign cloud providers and open-weight developers, so we are pairing the enforcement mechanism with a fast-track compliance pathway: systems that submit to pre-deployment evaluation and runtime monitoring gain liability protection and preferential access to EU public procurement. This is not about banning AI — it is about ensuring that no system, wherever built, can operate in the dark.
