## Priority  
**Emergency Activation of the European Cyber Resilience Board** — because the cyber_major_incident has made containment the urgent priority, and without a central, empowered body to coordinate response, every other measure—sovereign or resilient—fails at the point of execution.

## In practice  
This turn, we invoke Article 13b(3) of NIS2 to grant the European Cyber Resilience Board emergency powers for 18 months, enabling it to issue binding incident response directives across sectors and borders. The Board will activate a standing EU-wide incident cell, co-located with ENISA and Europol’s Joint Cybercrime Unit, with pre-approved access to AI forensic tools developed under the Cyber Shield programme. We release €3.2bn in emergency funding from the Union Civil Protection Mechanism, conditioned on compliance with Board directives during active incidents. This is not optional coordination—it is command authority for crisis tempo.

Behind the scenes, we are fast-tracking interoperability between national CERTs and the Sovereign Deployment Corridor’s control layer, ensuring that any system running sovereign workloads can be quarantined or audited at the Board’s order. DG DEFIS and EDA are testing secure override protocols this quarter, using red-team scenarios based on the current attack vector. We are also pressing the three industrial vendors—Darktrace, Trend Micro, Siemens—to release their detection modules under a common EU incident licence, allowing immediate sharing during declared incidents. This is the price of market access: cooperation or exclusion. The cyber_major_incident has changed the calculus—resilience is no longer about detection, but about who decides when the system is still yours.
