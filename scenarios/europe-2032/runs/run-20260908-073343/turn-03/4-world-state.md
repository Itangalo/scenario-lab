### The Fallout of Failure

The cyber_major_incident struck with cascading force: a compromised software dependency, silently propagated through a widely used industrial automation library, disabled safety monitoring systems across three major energy providers. The attack was not immediately contained, revealing a fatal gap—sovereign compute meant little when no EU body could compel response. In the aftermath, the European Cyber Resilience Board was granted emergency powers under NIS2, backed by €3.2bn in rapid-disbursal funds and direct access to forensic AI tools. Yet even as the Board activated its EU-wide incident cell, member states hesitated. Two refused initial data-sharing protocols, citing national sovereignty, and industrial vendors dragged feet on releasing detection modules under the new EU licence.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Crumbles

Global AI capability advanced to 55.5, driven by tighter agent coordination and training optimisations. No new safety measures were deployed, and the gap yawned wider. The incident’s forensic analysis—conducted too slowly—revealed model-generated attack planning, deepening technical alarm. With no safeguards in place, *ai_safety* dropped sharply by 5 points, reflecting both the breach and the lag in response.

### A Fragile Response

The Emergency Activation of the Cyber Resilience Board delivered partial gains. Resilience rose by 4 points as coordination improved and forensic sharing began, though not universally. The measure, small and fast, yielded early benefits but fell short of its full target. Public sentiment dipped further, as media coverage focused on the initial failure and ongoing intergovernmental friction. Protests near data centres intensified, now framed as resistance to both foreign control and EU overreach.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Shrinks

With *ai_capability* rising by 1.0 and no completed sovereignty measure, *eu_ai_sovereignty* fell by 1. The Sovereign Deployment Corridor remained in flight, yielding no immediate benefit. Political capital deteriorated: three large measures and a new priority cost −10, with no offsetting gains. The proposal bonus did not apply, as the new measure responded to an ongoing structural flaw rather than a recent, discrete shock.
