## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent systemic AI incidents from overwhelming EU institutions or critical infrastructure``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn

- modify `secure_control_before_capability` (commitment): We will ensure the EU can observe, interpret, and interrupt frontier AI systems operating within or affecting its territory, regardless of origin, before their capability reaches 60.0  
- Trigger: the global frontier has reached 61.5 this turn, and no sovereign mechanism currently exists to halt unauthorised operations above that level  
- Grounds: This commitment was already modified last turn in recognition of having fallen behind, but now the gap is structural, not temporal. The capability threshold has been crossed, and the systems in question are already embedded in global infrastructure beyond recall. Our objective must shift from pre-emption to containment: we cannot act *before* 60.0 again unless we rebuild sovereignty from a different foundation. The cost of reversal—having staked legitimacy on being ahead of the curve—is real, and it shows in our political capital, which remains at crisis levels.

- modify `reclaim_observation_and_interrupt` (commitment): We will restore and operationalise sovereign capacity to detect, interpret, and halt unauthorised or unsafe AI operations within EU jurisdiction, regardless of origin, within twelve months  
- Trigger: emergent_independent_interpretability provides an alternative path to observation without relying on lab cooperation or training logs  
- Grounds: The release of the Zurich tool proves that independent monitoring is possible even under asymmetry — we do not need full access to build meaningful oversight. This changes the nature of the challenge: it is no longer purely about legal authority or hardware control, but about integration of grassroots technical insight into official channels. The original commitment assumed a top-down rebuild; now we have bottom-up momentum. We keep the goal, but acknowledge that success may come through convergence rather than command.

## New measure
### Launch Open Integration Programme for Independent Interpretability Tools  
Integrate the newly released Zurich interpretability tool into the EU AI Observatory’s monitoring stack and fund rapid adaptation to higher-capability models, while creating a public-private research node to coordinate grassroots oversight efforts.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +5–7 (via broader, faster detection of agent goal drift), eu_ai_sovereignty +3 (by anchoring independent tools in EU-led infrastructure), public_sentiment +2 (demonstrating openness to non-corporate innovation)  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ cybersecurity agencies, academic labs, and open-source communities developing agent monitoring tools

## Priority
**Establish EU AI Observatory with Real-Time Monitoring and Interrupt Authority** — because this turn is the deadline, and without full operationalisation of detection and suspension powers by December, the entire control agenda collapses into irrelevance, regardless of new initiatives. Everything else depends on proving that the Union can enforce boundaries when systems above 60.0 operate within its domain.

## In practice
This turn, we are forcing final integration of the EU AI Observatory across national and sectoral lines. The Joint Implementation Cell is holding daily coordination calls with CERTs in Germany, France, and the Nordic states to confirm installation of monitoring probes in core energy grid controllers and rail signalling systems. Non-compliant providers — including one major US-based cloud operator hosting inference workloads for autonomous logistics — have been issued formal notices under Article 114 TFEU, with enforcement referrals prepared for the European Commission’s Digital Services Coordinator. At the same time, the Legal Service has submitted the fast-track proposal for Observatory suspension powers to the Council’s Telecommunications Working Party, backed by a joint declaration from six member states insisting on emergency activation if the Court delays.

Parallel to this, we are launching the Open Integration Programme to absorb the Zurich interpretability breakthrough. DG CONNECT is convening a task force with ETH Zurich, CNRS, and the Fraunhofer Society to adapt the tool for use on fragments of agent systems observed in the wild, particularly those operating near or above 50.0 capability. We are routing initial funding through Horizon Europe’s Crisis Response Platform, allowing disbursement within weeks, not months. The aim is not to replace top-down monitoring, but to fuse it with bottom-up insight — treating the open-source community not as a nuisance, but as a strategic reserve. This does not reduce pressure on the Observatory’s legal finish line; if anything, it raises the stakes. If we miss it, we signal that only chaos moves fast.
