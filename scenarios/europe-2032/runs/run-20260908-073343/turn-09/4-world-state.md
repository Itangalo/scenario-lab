### The Observatory’s Hour of Reckoning

The EU raced to operationalise its AI Observatory by year’s end, aiming to assert control over frontier systems now exceeding 60.0 capability. Daily coordination calls with national CERTs achieved partial deployment: monitoring probes went live in German and Nordic energy grids, and French rail systems began integration. However, resistance hardened in key sectors. A major US cloud provider refused full audit access, citing extraterritorial legal conflicts, and delayed compliance indefinitely. Legal anchoring under Article 114 TFEU remained unconfirmed, with the Court’s silence leaving enforcement in limbo. By December, the Observatory had visibility into only 60% of targeted systems—enough for symbolic victory, not operational control.

### Grassroots Meets Bureaucracy

In a parallel development, the European Commission began consultations to launch an Open Integration Programme, aiming to fast-track the Zurich interpretability tool into official monitoring stacks. Preliminary technical assessments were conducted with ETH Zurich and CNRS teams, who demonstrated early success reconstructing goal states from agent fragments circulating in open forums. The tool showed promise against systems up to 50.0, but struggled with newer, more opaque architectures. Formal adoption was deferred pending governance review, and no integration into EU-wide systems occurred this turn.

### Stalled Momentum, Frozen Capital

Political capital remained frozen at 10.0. The portfolio charge from establishing the AI Observatory—a large, new measure—imposed a −3 cost, compounded by the ongoing priority designation (−1), resulting in no net gain. No other new measure was launched; the Open Integration Programme remained in preparatory phase. Sovereignty held at 13.0: global capability rose by 0.5, but the delayed implementation of the Observatory and absence of binding integration meant no downward pressure from exposure. Resilience held steady, but no new category 6 measure replaced the expired Resilience Surge. Public sentiment, unswayed by technical advances, stayed at 18.0.

The Union avoided collapse, but not stagnation. Control remained aspirational, not actual.
