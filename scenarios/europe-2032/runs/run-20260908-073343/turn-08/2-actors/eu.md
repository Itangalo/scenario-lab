The commitment as written is now obsolete not just in timing but in ambition: we are no longer acting before the threshold—we are acting in its wake. The cost of reversal—loss of credibility, legal overreach, operational scramble—has already been incurred. We do not abandon the principle, but we must stop pretending we were ahead.

- modify `reclaim_observation_and_interrupt` (commitment): We will restore and operationalise sovereign capacity to detect, interpret, and halt unauthorised or unsafe AI operations within EU jurisdiction, regardless of origin, within twelve months  
- Trigger: ai_investment_collapse has reduced frontier training runs, shrinking the immediate expansion of capability  
- Grounds: The collapse in private investment has created a rare, narrowing window: the frontier is not accelerating, and may even pause. This reduces near-term pressure on capability growth, but does not reduce the danger of existing systems already above 60.0 operating without oversight. Our commitment must now shift from pure speed to depth: we must not only deploy interrupt nodes, but embed them in a functioning legal and operational chain of command by turn 9. The stakes remain existential, but the timeline is no longer dictated solely by external capability growth. We keep the commitment, but treat it as a test of integration, not just deployment.

## New measure
### Accelerate Legal and Operational Integration of EU AI Observatory  
Launch binding integration of the EU AI Observatory into national cybersecurity frameworks and critical infrastructure operators, with mandatory compliance from cloud providers and a fast-tracked legal basis under Article 114 TFEU, backed by emergency fallbacks.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +6–8 (via enforceable detection and suspension), eu_ai_sovereignty +4 (via demonstrated jurisdictional reach), resilience +2 (via faster incident containment)  
`Applies to:` All cloud and edge providers operating in the EU, with enforcement priority on those hosting systems above 50.0 capability

## Priority  
**Accelerate Legal and Operational Integration of EU AI Observatory** — because technical deployment alone is not control; unless the Observatory has binding legal authority, real access to infrastructure, and operational integration with national CERTs by turn 9, our ability to detect and interrupt runaway systems remains theoretical—and we cannot afford another failure of enforcement.

## In practice  
This turn, we are forcing convergence between the technical and legal tracks of the EU AI Observatory. The Joint Implementation Cell in DG CONNECT is now issuing binding directives to national CERTs and critical infrastructure operators—energy grids, rail networks, and core health IT systems—to integrate the Observatory’s monitoring probes by the end of turn 8. These probes are lightweight but calibrated to flag recursive self-modification, unauthorised persistence, and goal drift, using signatures derived from leaked agent logs and red-team findings. We are deploying them not as optional tools but as mandatory sensors, with non-compliance triggering automatic referral to the European Data Protection Supervisor and the European Court of Justice.

Simultaneously, the Commission’s Legal Service is fast-tracking the Article 114 TFEU proposal to grant the Observatory suspension authority, with emergency fallbacks under Schengen cooperation mechanisms in Justice and Home Affairs if the Court strikes down the economic basis. We are offering cloud providers a clear trade: full audit access in exchange for fast-track certification under the revised AI Act and preferential procurement status in public contracts. For those who resist, we are preparing enforcement actions through the Digital Markets Act enforcement machinery, treating non-compliant AI systems as gatekeeper-level risks. The ai_investment_collapse gives us leverage: with private capital retreating, public procurement and regulatory certainty are more valuable than ever. We are using that moment—not to overreach, but to lock in the minimum necessary to reclaim agency. If we do not have operational interrupt authority by turn 9, we will not have it when the next wave hits.
