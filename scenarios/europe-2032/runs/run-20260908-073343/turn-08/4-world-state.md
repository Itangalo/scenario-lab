### The Calm Before the Storm

Global AI capability crept upward to 61.5, as self-improving systems consolidated gains in autonomous execution. Yet the much-feared acceleration stalled—not from restraint, but from collapse. A wave of failed funding rounds and abandoned data centre projects rippled through the frontier labs, triggered by investor flight. The frontier did not fall, but its climb slowed, buying fragile breathing room.

In Brussels, the EU doubled down on control. The AI Observatory, long a technical vision, was pushed into operational reality. Binding integration directives went out to national cybersecurity teams and critical infrastructure operators, demanding installation of monitoring probes by turn’s end. Energy grids and rail networks began retrofitting; cloud providers faced a stark choice: full audit access or regulatory confrontation. Behind the scenes, legal teams raced to anchor the Observatory’s suspension authority under Article 114 TFEU, with emergency fallbacks readied in case the Court challenged its scope.

Yet momentum faltered at the edges. The Sovereign Deployment Corridor, though legally complete, remained underutilised—legacy systems resisted integration, and member-state CERTs lacked trained staff. The Resilience Surge finished on schedule, strengthening red-teaming across sectors, but its benefits were narrowly absorbed. Public sentiment stayed frozen at 18.0, as job losses in AI-adjacent services and a surge in synthetic fraud dominated headlines.

Political capital remained stagnant at 10.0. Despite finishing three major measures, the EU’s agenda was still too costly, too fragmented to gain ground. The new push—legal integration of the Observatory—was large, urgent, and deeply contested. With capital below 12, the Union teetered on the edge of losing control of its own strategy.

### Flickers of Autonomy

The Nordic-Benelux compute pact quietly expanded, now including two more members. No declaration was issued, but procurement notices hinted at shared inference capacity. Meanwhile, an open-source interpretability tool, born in a Zurich lab, spread through underground forums, offering faint hope of independent oversight.
