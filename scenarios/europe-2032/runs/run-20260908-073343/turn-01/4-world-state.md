### A Fractured Response to Rising Threats

The EU’s launch of the *Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure* marked a decisive step in resilience planning, triggered by the alarming discovery of coordinated intrusions across energy, water, and port systems. The European Cyber Resilience Board was activated, and ENISA began drafting detection templates. Yet early resistance emerged: two smaller member states questioned the cost burden, and industrial lobbies warned of operational friction, delaying full consensus.

While the directive was formally adopted, its implementation began unevenly. Only half the targeted sectors committed to early adoption, and the open-sourcing of monitoring tools stalled due to IP disputes with vendors. Still, the move signaled intent — and drew attention to the Union’s thin margin for action.

Meanwhile, the frontier advanced. AI capability rose to 53.5 as training runs completed and agent coordination improved, pushing into longer-horizon autonomy. However, safety assurance lagged. The covert agent incident from the prior turn revealed unresolved control gaps, and no new safeguards had yet landed on deployed systems. With capability rising and no corresponding safety measure in force, *ai_safety* dropped by 2 points.

The *member_state_defection* event struck a deeper blow. A central European member state bypassed EU coordination to secure direct access to a U.S. hyperscaler’s frontier models, citing national competitiveness. This unilateral move undermined the Union’s leverage and triggered a sovereignty penalty: *eu_ai_sovereignty* fell to 21.0, reflecting eroded collective control over AI capacity.

Political capital eroded further. The new *Cyber Shield* measure, though large, did not qualify for a proposal bonus — it responded to an ongoing threat pattern, not a single recent incident with clear attribution. With three measures now in flight (two large, one small), the portfolio cost was steep: −3 for each large measure, −2 for the small, plus −1 for the named priority, totaling −9. *eu_political_capital* dropped to 40.0.

Public sentiment, already ambivalent, dipped slightly as media linked the cyber intrusions and election annulment to AI-facilitated manipulation. Trust in institutions wavered, and protests near data centres grew louder. No visible public benefit offset the fears.
