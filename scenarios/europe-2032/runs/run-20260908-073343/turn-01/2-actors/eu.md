## New measure
### Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure
A binding directive requiring all critical infrastructure operators in the EU to implement AI-specific intrusion detection and response protocols, with funding and technical support from ENISA and the newly empowered European Cyber Resilience Board.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 4  
Targeted effect: resilience +15–20 points, ai_safety +5–8 via reduced downstream misuse exposure  
Applies to: EU member states, with enforcement through national regulators and conditional access to EU cohesion funds

## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure** — because the cyber_test_shot proves existing defences are blind to AI-coordinated intrusions, and without trusted detection across energy, water and ports, no level of AI capability can be safely used or governed.

## In practice
This turn, we activate the European Cyber Resilience Board under Article 13b of the revised NIS2 Directive, empowering it to issue binding technical standards for AI-enabled threat detection in critical sectors. We allocate €12bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme to co-finance deployment, with 70% grants for member states that adopt the protocols by Q2 2027. ENISA will publish baseline detection templates by December, drawing on lessons from the recent intrusion — particularly the use of slow-burn, cross-system coordination that evaded siloed monitoring.

Resistance will come from smaller member states citing cost and administrative burden, and from industry actors who fear operational disruption. To counter this, we tie compliance to continued eligibility for EU infrastructure bonds and offer fast-track digital modernisation funds for early adopters. Behind the scenes, we are negotiating with three major industrial cybersecurity providers to open-source lightweight versions of their AI-monitoring stacks, ensuring interoperability and reducing vendor lock-in. This is not just about defence — it is about proving that the EU can set operational tempo in preparedness, even as it lags in raw capability.
