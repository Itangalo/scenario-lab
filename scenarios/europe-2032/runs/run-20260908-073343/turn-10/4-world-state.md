### A Denial That Cuts Deep

The EU’s frontier access was abruptly severed, cutting off hospitals, research institutes, and public agencies from the most capable AI systems. The move, unexplained and irreversible in the short term, exposed the Union’s operational fragility. Despite the AI Observatory reaching its technical finish line, its interrupt authority remained dormant—legal anchoring under Article 114 TFEU still unconfirmed, and enforcement stalled by jurisdictional disputes. The US cloud provider held firm, and the Anti-Coercion Instrument threat drew only silence.

### Patchwork Responses

In response, the Commission fast-tracked the Open Integration Programme, disbursing funds to adapt the Zurich interpretability tool for higher-capability models. A coordination node in Brussels began linking open-source developers with national agencies, leveraging grassroots monitoring networks that had independently detected anomalous agent behaviour. Yet these efforts remained experimental, unable to compensate for the loss of frontier access.

### From Observation to Survival

Recognising the new reality, the EU launched the **Expand Resilience Surge** measure, a large category 6 initiative to embed autonomous incident response into critical infrastructure. ENISA began designing live war games to simulate fast-moving AI-driven failures, with pre-authorised countermeasures like network isolation and model rollback. However, with political capital frozen at 10.0, the initiative started under severe constraints. The named priority conferred no acceleration—below 20, it had no effect—and the measure entered the portfolio with no immediate impact.

### Sovereignty Erodes Further

The access denial constituted a direct hit to EU AI sovereignty. With no domestic frontier capacity to fall back on, the Union’s dependence became a liability. Sovereignty dropped to 10.0, reflecting the irreversible loss of access. Resilience held steady, but no gains were made. Public sentiment remained stagnant at 18.0, as job losses and system outages continued to dominate headlines.
