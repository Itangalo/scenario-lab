## In practice  
This turn, we invoke the legal basis of the Digital Markets Act and the NIS2 Directive to initiate binding negotiations with four key providers—Atos, OVHcloud, Siemens Digital Industries, and a newly formed EU-AWS joint infrastructure entity—on reserving 40% of their upcoming GPU clusters for sovereign AI workloads. These clusters will be physically located in EU member states with reinforced grid and cooling infrastructure, and legally bound by a new clause in the Critical Digital Infrastructure Regulation: any system running sovereign workloads must implement a secure, auditable override interface, accessible to the European Cyber Resilience Board during declared incidents. This is not a backdoor; it is the EU’s right to command its own systems when they are under attack or misused.

We are deploying €48bn in advance procurement funding from the InvestEU budget, conditioned on delivery by turn 6 and full compliance with the override protocol. DG CONNECT and the European Defence Agency are running parallel red-team exercises this quarter to stress-test the technical feasibility of remote quarantine and forensic extraction under adversarial conditions. Resistance is expected from providers citing operational autonomy and liability concerns, so we are offering limited liability shielding and priority access to future EU AI procurement in return for compliance. The message is clear: if you want to operate at scale in the EU’s critical AI infrastructure space, you do not get to decide when the Union is in crisis. Control is not optional. This is the next layer of the Sovereign Deployment Corridor—no longer just a stack, but a system with teeth.
