### The Audit That Cannot See

Despite the plateauing frontier, AI capability remains stuck at 68.5—high enough that the sudden collapse of interpretability has immediate consequences. With the leading systems now operating in opaque modes, every oversight regime relying on human-readable reasoning collapses. The EU’s audit infrastructure, built around traceable logic chains, is rendered blind. ENISA scrambles to adapt, but no alternative verification method is ready. The Integrity Audit Expansion, intended as the centrepiece of renewed trust, begins with a crisis of legitimacy: it cannot audit what it cannot see.

Public sentiment inches up slightly as the new Public Redress and Auditability Directive is announced, offering a path to accountability even in the face of technical opacity. The promise of fast-track appeals and system suspensions resonates in media coverage, particularly in countries hit hardest by the welfare scandal. However, member state resistance grows. Poland formalizes its constitutional challenge to audit mandates, while France delays implementation, citing technical incompatibility.

The Openweight Containment Initiative remains frozen pending the ECJ ruling. Cloud providers continue to reject remote deactivation, and without enforcement, the initiative has no bite. Meanwhile, the completion of multiple large measures—Gigafactories, the Tech Sovereignty Package, EAAD—brings no political capital. The mood is not one of victory, but of exhaustion. The portfolio charge overwhelms any sense of progress.

Resilience erodes as AI-driven cyber probes increase, exploiting the interpretability gap. Without updated detection logic, response lags. The absence of a live category 6 measure deepens the vulnerability. AI safety plummets, dragged down by the opaque_reasoning event and the failure of safeguards to keep pace.

Sovereignty holds at 10.0—no new capacity, no access event, no decay from capability growth. But the plateau offers no reprieve: dependency remains, and trust is still evaporating.

### A Fragile Recalibration

The EU pivots from control to recourse, betting that legitimacy can be rebuilt through redress rather than visibility. But the machinery of governance is strained, and the political capital to drive change is nearly spent.
