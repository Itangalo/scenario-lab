## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public and institutional trust in sovereign AI systems by ensuring auditable decision-making, enforceable redress, and resilience to both technical compromise and operational misuse  
  - Grounds: The **opaque_reasoning** event has rendered auditable decision-making technically unachievable in the short term, and the Integrity Audit Expansion cannot deliver on its promise without a breakthrough in verification. Meanwhile, the **backlash_physical** event shows that public trust is now actively eroding through direct confrontation with AI infrastructure. Continuing to frame trust as dependent on auditability risks anchoring our commitment to a failing technical premise. This turn, we must shift from *auditable* to *accountable*: trust must be rebuilt not through visibility into systems we can no longer see, but through institutional responsiveness when they fail. The revised commitment reflects that pivot—away from technical proof, toward political and procedural legitimacy.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as synonymous with agency, and we will not mistake motion for progress when the ground has shifted beneath us  
  - Grounds: The **capability_plateau_evidence** suggests the frontier may have stalled, but the **opaque_reasoning** collapse shows that capability is no longer the only axis that matters—control has fractured independently. Our past actions assumed that moving fast on audit and containment would secure sovereignty, but they have not. With `eu_political_capital` at 5.0 and `public_sentiment` at 21.0, further acceleration risks breakdown, not breakthrough. We must now act with restraint, choosing depth over speed, and legitimacy over motion.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Establish the EU Rapid Redress Mechanism  
A standing, fast-track administrative tribunal empowered to suspend AI systems in public services upon evidence of systemic failure, with binding authority across member states and direct access for citizens and civil society.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `public_sentiment` by 10–15 points and `ai_safety` by 3–6 points by institutionalizing a visible, citizen-accessible pathway to challenge and halt harmful AI, even when technical understanding is absent  
`Applies to:` All AI systems used in EU public administration, enforced by EAAD in coordination with national ombuds institutions and the European Data Protection Supervisor

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch the Integrity Audit Expansion  
We are pushing this hardest this turn because, despite the **opaque_reasoning** collapse, it remains the only instrument actively attempting to reconstitute oversight in a world where traditional audit has failed—by layering procedural, operational, and institutional checks even where technical traceability is lost, and by binding findings to continued operation.

## In practice  
This turn, we are governing in the dark. The **opaque_reasoning** event has blinded our primary audit mechanisms, and the Integrity Audit Expansion is now our last chance to build accountability without visibility. EAAD and ENISA are co-developing new audit templates that shift focus from reasoning chains to decision consequences: can a system explain *why* it denied benefits, not through internal logic, but through observable inputs and documented policy alignment? We are piloting these in three member states—Germany, Italy, and Estonia—with funding from the Digital Europe Programme to upgrade legacy case management systems for log compatibility. The Justice and Home Affairs Council is reviewing a proposal to make audit findings a condition of continued deployment under the AI Act, but France and Poland remain resistant, citing judicial overreach.

The Openweight Containment Initiative remains frozen pending the ECJ ruling, and cloud providers continue to ignore deactivation mandates. We are now preparing a fallback: a voluntary certification scheme for compliant hosts, to be branded through the Digital Services Act transparency framework. But our focus is shifting. With the **backlash_physical** event escalating, we cannot afford to appear as enforcers of unaccountable systems. That is why we are launching the EU Rapid Redress Mechanism: not to audit, but to answer. It will sit within EAAD, with standing delegated to national ombuds bodies, and will have the power to issue immediate suspensions—backed by the Court of Justice if needed. This is not control, but it is recourse. And in a time when we cannot see, recourse is the only legitimacy we have left.
