### The Audit That Cannot See, and the Recourse That Must

The frontier remains stagnant at 68.5, but the damage from opaque reasoning deepens. With no technical path to auditability, the Integrity Audit Expansion falters. Pilots in Germany, Italy, and Estonia struggle to retrofit legacy systems, and France and Poland block binding enforcement, citing sovereignty and judicial overreach. The audit regime becomes a patchwork of intent without teeth.

Public trust, briefly lifted by promises of redress, erodes further as physical protests intensify. Data centre occupations in Spain and the Netherlands, coupled with sabotaged grid links, signal a shift from dissent to disruption. Policing the backlash drains resources and fuels resentment, painting the EU as protector of opaque systems rather than public interest.

The EU pivots to accountability through the new Rapid Redress Mechanism, aiming to empower citizens to halt harmful AI in public services. But with political capital at 5.0, momentum is weak. The measure is seen as reactive, not transformative, and its small size limits immediate impact. Member states question its enforceability, especially where national ombuds institutions resist EU delegation.

Meanwhile, resilience slips as cyber defences fail to adapt. AI-driven probes exploit the interpretability gap, and without active category 6 measures, critical systems grow more exposed. The absence of technical safeguards and the paralysis of audit regimes leave institutions reactive and vulnerable.

A rare bright spot emerges: a coalition of middle powers, including Japan, South Korea, and the EU, aligns on export controls and joint evaluation standards. This coordination strengthens the EU’s leverage and signals a shift in global dynamics. However, the benefits are structural, not immediate, and do not offset domestic erosion.

The Openweight Containment Initiative remains in legal limbo. Cloud providers ignore deactivation mandates, and the ECJ ruling delays further. A voluntary certification scheme is proposed but gains little traction.

Despite completed megaprojects, sovereignty remains at 10.0—no new capacity, no access secured. The plateau offers no reprieve; dependency persists, and legitimacy frays.

### A Fragile Recalibration, Now Fraying

The EU governs not from strength, but from memory of it. The pivot to redress is necessary, but arrives too late and too weak to stem the tide.
