### A Shift in Focus, Not Fortune

With AI capability stagnant and defensive breakthroughs offering a fragile reprieve from cascading cyber risks, the EU pivots from containment to care. The launch of the **EU AI Labour Absorption and Transition Fund** marks a desperate bid to restore legitimacy not through control, but through solidarity. Designed to cushion displaced workers in law, accounting, and software, the fund offers wage insurance, retraining, and regional renewal, tying public procurement to retraining obligations. Germany and the Netherlands endorse the move as essential stabilisation; France embraces it as a reassertion of social sovereignty. Poland, however, resists binding commitments, demanding opt-outs for its eastern regions.

The AI Emergency Suspension Authority limps forward under Article 114 TFEU, its implementing act delayed by legal wrangling. To appease France and Poland, the Commission offers co-chairing rights on its review panel, diluting supranational authority. The mechanism remains symbolic—no system has been suspended, and no court has ruled—yet it persists as a legal placeholder.

Public sentiment inches upward, not due to trust in institutions, but because the fund acknowledges lived hardship. Media spotlight stories of graduates retraining as AI auditors and paralegals, offering a narrative of inclusion. Yet data centre occupations continue in rural France and Wallonia, now met with EU-wide coordination among activist networks. Police crackdowns fuel further resentment, even as sabotage incidents decline slightly—attributed more to the cyber defence breakthrough than policy.

Resilience holds steady. Though the Labour Fund targets social absorption, its effects are too distant to register yet. No category 6 measure has landed, and cyber defences remain brittle outside pilot zones. The eval anomaly reports spark quiet alarm in closed briefings, but with no visible incident, no corrective action follows.

### Political Capital Erodes Further

The EU’s political capital slips to 2.0. Three large measures remain in flight, including the new Labour Fund, draining −3 each. The named priority adds −1. Despite the fund’s social intent, the cost is immediate and unrelenting. With capital below 12, the risk of further measure collapse looms.
