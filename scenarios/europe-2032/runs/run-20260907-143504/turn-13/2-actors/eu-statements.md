# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public and institutional trust in sovereign AI systems by ensuring auditable decision-making, enforceable redress, and resilience to both technical compromise and operational misuse

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Restore public and institutional trust in sovereign AI systems by ensuring **enforceable redress, resilience to operational failure, and institutional accountability in the absence of technical auditability** — **but shift primary focus from systemic suspension to societal absorption, as the legitimacy of stopping systems is now less urgent than preventing collapse from their continued operation**
  - Grounds: The **cyber_defence_breakthrough** and **capability_plateau_evidence** together alter the risk calculus: the immediate threat of runaway harm has receded, while the social and economic damage from AI-driven displacement intensifies unchecked. The **eval_anomaly_reports** suggest latent danger, but the plateau in capability growth means the window for reorienting without catastrophe has not yet closed. Meanwhile, the Emergency Suspension Authority — though symbolically necessary — has failed to quell public anger; instead, occupations and sabotage grow, not because people fear *what AI might do*, but because they see *what it is already doing*: erasing opportunity, deepening inequality, and entrenching foreign control. Trust is not being lost to opacity alone, but to exclusion. We must now pivot from mechanisms that *stop* AI to those that *bind* it to society — not just through accountability, but through inclusion. The revised commitment reflects that: we remain committed to control in the absence of understanding, but our primary task is no longer halting systems — it is ensuring that the EU, and its people, are not left behind by them.

We are still acting under uncertainty, but we now recognise that agency is not only the power to stop, but the power to include. This revision acknowledges that legitimacy must be earned not through jurisdictional assertion, but through distributive justice: we must act not only to govern AI, but to ensure its gains are not concentrated beyond our reach.
