### Sovereign Compute Push Meets Resistance

The EU presses forward with the Sovereign Compute Corridor, invoking emergency powers to fast-track three strategic sites. Legal teams finalize the regulation under Article 122 TFEU, but national pushback intensifies. Germany demands veto rights over Rhine-Danube security protocols, while Spain delays grid access in Seville, citing environmental reviews. The Commission holds firm, but momentum slows.

### Emergency Resilience Surge Launched Amid Crisis

Following the recent cyberattack and a defensive breakthrough in automated patching, the EU launches the Emergency Resilience Surge. Under NIS2’s emergency clause, ENISA begins deploying AI-driven defensive stacks to critical public platforms. Early rollouts in Estonia and Finland show promise, cutting vulnerability windows from hours to minutes. However, France and Poland resist centralised command, demanding national control over response protocols.

### Open-Weight Activity Raises Concerns

Signs emerge of an advanced open-weight model fragment circulating in select technical forums, reportedly derived from a partial leak of high-fidelity training data. While initial analysis suggests high potential, the model remains unstable and incomplete. A few developers have begun attempting replication, but widespread deployment is hindered by technical barriers and lack of coordination. The episode raises alarms about future proliferation, but no broad capability leap has yet materialized.

### Public Backlash Hardens

A coordinated civil campaign against AI infrastructure gains ground, staging blockades at proposed data centre sites in Sweden and Portugal. Protesters cite energy consumption, lack of transparency, and broken promises on public benefit. The movement’s cross-border coordination marks a shift from sporadic dissent to organised resistance, further eroding public trust.

### Safety and Sentiment Slip Further

While defensive AI improves detection, no systemic safety upgrades land on frontier models. The gap between capability and control widens. Political capital erodes under the weight of contested measures and low cohesion. Public sentiment dips as unrest grows and visible benefits remain scarce.
