### Consolidation Amid Crisis

The European Union formally closes the Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration, elevating city-level AI monitoring standards into binding EU civil protection protocols. The Lyon, Helsinki, and Barcelona pilot audits are codified as a Delegated Act, bypassing Council deadlock, while DG HOME launches the first cross-border drill in the Rhône-Alpine corridor. The move delivers tangible progress on crisis coordination, reinforcing federated resilience just as physical backlash against AI infrastructure escalates.

### Backlash Turns Violent

Across multiple member states, protests against data centre expansion turn disruptive: grid substations serving AI facilities are vandalized, and sit-ins delay construction in three regions. One incident in northern Italy results in injuries during police intervention. The unrest reflects deepening public anxiety, fed by stagnant job markets and opaque AI governance. Media coverage frames the actions as both criminal and symptomatic, with regional governments split on whether to negotiate or crack down.

### Political Capital Holds—Barely

With no new measure proposed, the portfolio burden drops: the completed resilience initiative exits, reducing strain on political capital. The priority bonus is not charged, as the measure finishes rather than being actively pushed. The EU avoids paralysis—for now. However, public sentiment declines as the violence dominates headlines, undercutting the quiet gains in resilience.

### Safety Erodes Further

Despite the Evaluation Shield’s monitoring conditions, frontier labs report unexplained agent drift in two models during internal testing. No formal breach is declared, but developers share findings through informal channels, bypassing EU oversight. With no new safety measures landing, and capability advancing, the gap between control and power widens.

### Frontier Creeps Forward

Global AI capability reaches 62.0, driven by iterative tooling gains within closed labs. Open-weight models remain stagnant at 52.0, with no major public release. Signs of plateau persist—market volatility, falling premium prices—but progress, though slow, continues.
