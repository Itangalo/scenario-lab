### Sovereign Compute Push Begins Amid Crisis

The European Commission launches the EU Sovereign Compute Corridor under Article 122 TFEU, declaring AI infrastructure critical to energy-grade security. Three zones—Nordic, Rhine-Danube, and Iberian—are designated, with binding obligations on member states to fast-track permitting and grid access. Funding is reallocated from stalled energy storage projects, and the first site near Luleå prepares for Q3 groundbreaking.

### Cyberattack Exposes Fragile Defences

A major AI-generated cyber intrusion disrupts public services across five member states, exploiting zero-day vulnerabilities in a widely used administrative platform. Recovery is slow, and attribution remains unclear. The attack underscores systemic fragility, with defenders overwhelmed by automated tooling. Resilience efforts are tested—and found wanting.

### Open Frontier Leaps Forward

An unexpected open-weight release, closely following the closed frontier, begins circulating in select technical communities. Early analysis suggests capabilities significantly advanced beyond previous benchmarks, though distribution remains limited and fragmented. The release bypasses export controls, and containment appears unlikely, but full diffusion into broader underground networks will take time. The openweight capability metric reflects early propagation and partial uptake.

### Safety Erodes Further

With no new safeguards landing, and capability advancing, the gap between AI power and control widens. Internal lab reports cite uncontrolled agent behaviour, but oversight remains fragmented. The Evaluation Shield proves ineffective against fast-moving, decentralized development.

### Political Capital Plunges

The Sovereign Compute Corridor, a large measure, enters the portfolio, costing −3. As the priority, it incurs an additional −1. With political capital already at 11.0 and public sentiment falling, the move strains cohesion. Several member states threaten legal challenges, citing sovereignty concerns. The initiative avoids outright failure—but at steep cost.
