## In practice  
We are launching the EU Sovereign Compute Corridor under Article 122 TFEU (emergency infrastructure), treating AI compute as critical energy-grade infrastructure. The Commission, through DG ENER and DG CONNECT, is drafting a Regulation—not a Directive—to bypass national opt-outs and establish three sovereign compute zones: one in the Nordic hydro corridor (Norway-Sweden-Finland), one in the Rhine-Danube spine (Germany-Austria), and one in the Iberian solar belt (Spain-Portugal). Each site will host at least 3 exaflops of training-capable compute, hardened against physical and cyber intrusion, with legal jurisdiction vested in a new EuroHPC Joint Undertaking governance board that includes Commission, ENISA, and national intelligence representatives.

Funding comes from a reallocation of €3.8 billion from the Digital Europe Programme and REPowerEU’s stranded energy storage lines, reprogrammed via omnibus act. We are offering member states 70% co-financing for grid upgrades and security perimeters, but demanding reciprocity: any nation blocking siting forfeits access to sovereign AI services for public administration and defence planning. The first site—near Luleå—will break ground in Q3, using emergency permitting under Sweden’s national energy security law as precedent. This is not a request for consensus.
