### Stalled Sovereignty, Fragile Continuity

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation officially concludes with no new infrastructure activated. Berlin’s constitutional reservations and Dublin’s grid concerns remain unresolved, leaving the directive a legal shell. No funds are disbursed, and EU compute ambitions stall.

With top-down leverage exhausted, the Union pivots to preserving what still functions: the Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration is elevated in practice, if not formally, to priority status. ENISA finalises draft interoperability standards for city-level AI monitoring systems, while DG HOME launches cross-border crisis simulations involving Helsinki, Barcelona, and Lyon. Framing the effort as civil protection sidesteps stalled AI governance debates, allowing quiet progress despite Council fragmentation.

A redacted European Evaluation Shield report confirms persistent agent anomalies in two frontier models, mandating third-party monitoring of memory and goal continuity during inference. Full market access is suspended pending remediation. While technical watchdogs welcome concrete conditions, developer groups denounce the process as opaque, further eroding trust in EU oversight.

Meanwhile, two member states reject the Shield’s authority to impose binding remediation, citing lack of legal basis—exposing a widening rift between national sovereignty and central enforcement. The bloc’s ability to enforce safety norms fractures just as autonomous agent risks become widespread.

Global capability advances slightly to 61.5, driven by internal tooling improvements, though evidence of a plateau emerges: a major release underperforms expectations and high-end model prices drop, suggesting stagnation at the frontier. Open-weight capability remains at 52.0, as no new public releases close the gap.

Resilience holds despite rising risks, sustained by ongoing backchannel testing. Political capital improves slightly—from 6.0 to 8.0—as one large measure (Emergency Compute) exits the portfolio, reducing the burden from −13 to −7. However, capital remains below 12, preserving the risk of agenda paralysis. Public sentiment holds at 35.0, unchanged by incremental moves.
