### The Weight of Collapse

The first half of 2032 offers no reprieve. The **medical_breakthrough**—a wave of AI-designed gene therapies entering clinics—should have been a moment of hope. But in the EU, it arrives as humiliation. The models behind the cures are locked behind foreign firewalls, accessible only through licensed platforms that dictate terms, pricing, and patient eligibility. Hospitals in Milan and Bucharest administer treatments developed in California and Shenzhen, deepening the sense of technological subjugation. There is no celebration—only resentment.

Meanwhile, the **emergent_regional_aid_fracture** hardens into open conflict. Southern and eastern member states demand emergency funds to offset AI-driven job losses in manufacturing and logistics. Northern finance ministers refuse, citing failed implementation of prior resilience measures. Behind closed doors, talk turns to national AI corridors and bilateral compute deals outside EU frameworks. The single market trembles on the edge of divergence.

The EU attempts a last stand: the **EU Continuity Compact**, a treaty-level bid to bind members to collective AI infrastructure and burden-sharing. Framed as a survival pact, it is pushed as the top priority. But with **eu_political_capital** at 3.0, the Union lacks the credibility to compel. Coreper meetings stall. Some states welcome the offer of emergency compute access; others dismiss it as symbolic—SACP’s infrastructure is outdated, its capacity negligible. The proposal lingers in legal limbo, unblocked but unendorsed.

Three major programmes—Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, EAAD—finally conclude. Yet their completion yields no political capital. The public sees no change. Resilience remains thin, sovereignty unimproved. The **Critical Infrastructure Continuity Protocol** begins, but without enforcement or funding, it is more doctrine than defence.

### Fractured Assurance

With trust in official channels shattered, **emergent_open_source_audit_tools** gain quiet traction. Civic hackers in Barcelona and Warsaw distribute lightweight probing suites that simulate adversarial audits. Local governments adopt them unofficially, a sign that accountability is now being crowdsourced.

The Openweight Containment Initiative is formally canceled. Cloud providers had already ignored it; now, the Commission concedes defeat. No new measure lands. No metric moves. The Union is not falling—it is frozen in place, waiting to break.
