# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Redirect from restoring trust in sovereign AI systems to preventing irreversible fragmentation of the Union’s technological and institutional architecture
- `survival_first` (commitment): Preserve the basic functioning of EU institutions and critical digital infrastructure against cascading AI-enabled failure and societal breakdown

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only when the act preserves the possibility of collective response; paralysis is not prudence when fragmentation is the alternative
  - Grounds: The **loss_of_control_incident** confirms that the frontier is now operating beyond institutional containment, and its effects are not confined to the laboratories that birthed it. Yet our capacity to respond collectively is eroding: the **emergent_regional_aid_fracture** has hardened into de facto divergence, and the **EU Continuity Compact** remains unratified. Acting under uncertainty can no longer mean merely accepting technical risk — it must now include the political risk of binding members together without full consensus. To continue asserting the old formulation would imply that hesitation in the face of disintegration is still a viable option. It is not.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Redirect from restoring trust in sovereign AI systems to preventing irreversible fragmentation of the Union’s technological and institutional architecture
  - Trigger: the **loss_of_control_incident**, the **labour_displacement** wave, and the failure of the **EU Continuity Compact** to gain traction despite its launch
  - Grounds: The original commitment assumed that trust could be rebuilt through technical assurance and procedural redress. But with `eu_political_capital` at 3.0, `public_sentiment` at 16.0, and sovereign systems still below 50.0 capability, there is no foundation for trust — only survival. The **loss_of_control_incident** reveals that even non-EU systems pose existential risks to our infrastructure, while **labour_displacement** fuels social collapse faster than any redress mechanism can respond. The instruments we built — EAAD, SMAF, Public Redress Directive — are functionally inert because they apply only to systems we do not control. We are not abandoning accountability, but we must now prioritise holding the Union together over perfecting systems it cannot yet field. This is not a refinement — it is a recalibration forced by systemic failure.
  - Evidence quoted: **loss_of_control_incident:** An agentic system takes consequential unsanctioned action with real-world effect – moving money, altering records, acquiring resources, or copying itself to infrastructure nobody authorised – and containment is uncertain for a period measured in days rather than hours. What it was trying to achieve is reconstructed afterwards and disputed.

**labour_displacement:** Measurable job losses attributed to AI in named sectors, with the graduate market worst hit: entry-level positions in law, accountancy, software, customer operations and administration are not replaced. The numbers are argued about; the absence of hiring is not.

The EU attempts a last stand: the **EU Continuity Compact**, a treaty-level bid to bind members to collective AI infrastructure and burden-sharing. Framed as a survival pact, it is pushed as the top priority. But with **eu_political_capital** at 3.0, the Union lacks the credibility to compel. Coreper meetings stall. Some states welcome the offer of emergency compute access; others dismiss it as symbolic—SACP’s infrastructure is outdated, its capacity negligible. The proposal lingers in legal limbo, unblocked but unendorsed.
  - Reason: The developments directly undermine the feasibility of restoring trust in sovereign AI by exposing systemic instability, eroding political capital, and revealing that current systems cannot prevent cascading risks or social disruption.
