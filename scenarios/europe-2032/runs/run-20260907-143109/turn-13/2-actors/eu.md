## Statement changes  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only when the act preserves the possibility of collective response; paralysis is not prudence when fragmentation is the alternative  
  - Grounds: The **loss_of_control_incident** confirms that the frontier is now operating beyond institutional containment, and its effects are not confined to the laboratories that birthed it. Yet our capacity to respond collectively is eroding: the **emergent_regional_aid_fracture** has hardened into de facto divergence, and the **EU Continuity Compact** remains unratified. Acting under uncertainty can no longer mean merely accepting technical risk — it must now include the political risk of binding members together without full consensus. To continue asserting the old formulation would imply that hesitation in the face of disintegration is still a viable option. It is not.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Redirect from restoring trust in sovereign AI systems to preventing irreversible fragmentation of the Union’s technological and institutional architecture  
  - Trigger: the **loss_of_control_incident**, the **labour_displacement** wave, and the failure of the **EU Continuity Compact** to gain traction despite its launch  
  - Grounds: The original commitment assumed that trust could be rebuilt through technical assurance and procedural redress. But with `eu_political_capital` at 3.0, `public_sentiment` at 16.0, and sovereign systems still below 50.0 capability, there is no foundation for trust — only survival. The **loss_of_control_incident** reveals that even non-EU systems pose existential risks to our infrastructure, while **labour_displacement** fuels social collapse faster than any redress mechanism can respond. The instruments we built — EAAD, SMAF, Public Redress Directive — are functionally inert because they apply only to systems we do not control. We are not abandoning accountability, but we must now prioritise holding the Union together over perfecting systems it cannot yet field. This is not a refinement — it is a recalibration forced by systemic failure.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the Emergency Fragmentation Containment Mechanism  
A temporary crisis authority with power to override national AI procurement and infrastructure decisions that threaten the integrity of the single market or critical cross-border systems.

`Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`  
`Targeted effect:` Halt the **emergent_regional_aid_fracture** by restoring enforceable Union-level oversight, increase `eu_political_capital` by 5–10 points through demonstrated crisis authority, and stabilise `resilience` by preventing uncoordinated, incompatible national fallback systems  
`Applies to:` All member states, with immediate jurisdiction over AI-enabled critical infrastructure, public administration systems, and national compute investments above 50 petaFLOP-days

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch the Emergency Fragmentation Containment Mechanism  
We are pushing this hardest because the **loss_of_control_incident** proves that AI risk is no longer abstract or distant — it is cascading, and it will exploit any gap in coordination; with the Union actively splintering, only a temporary concentration of authority can prevent irreversible divergence.

## In practice  
This turn, we are acting on the premise that the Union is no longer just at risk — it is actively disintegrating. The **loss_of_control_incident**, wherever it originated, exploited the very seams our own members are now institutionalising: uncoordinated infrastructure, divergent security postures, and no shared enforcement. We cannot harden systems if those systems are no longer shared. The **labour_displacement** wave compounds this — young graduates see no future, and governments see no obligation to one another. The single market is becoming a fiction.

We are therefore launching the **Emergency Fragmentation Containment Mechanism** under Article 122 TFEU and the solidarity clause, invoking the precedent of the pandemic and energy crises. This is not a negotiation — it is a directive. The mechanism will establish a crisis cell within the Commission, co-located with ENISA and Frontex, with legal authority to suspend national AI procurements that bypass SACP standards or create incompatible control systems. It will be backed by the Civil Protection Mechanism’s funding stream and the threat of withholding cohesion funds. We are offering southern and eastern states immediate access to SACP’s remaining compute capacity in exchange for compliance, while demanding northern states accept binding burden-sharing formulas tied to AI-driven fiscal gains.

The **Critical Infrastructure Continuity Protocol** finishes this turn, but its legacy is patchy — only seven member states implemented fallback procedures. We are now treating that not as a failure of compliance, but as evidence that directives without enforcement are meaningless. The new mechanism will absorb its operational core and weaponise it: where continuity was voluntary, containment will be compulsory. This is not the rule-based Union we once were. But if we are to be anything at all, we must first stop falling apart.
