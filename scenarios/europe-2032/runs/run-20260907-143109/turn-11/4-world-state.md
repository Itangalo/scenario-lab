### The Fracture Widens

The second half of 2031 is defined not by policy, but by breakdown. The **backlash_physical** event — a wave of occupations, sabotage, and injuries targeting data centres and grid infrastructure — shatters any illusion of stable governance. Protests no longer petition; they dismantle. Police operations to restore access cost more in political capital than the damage itself, deepening the sense of a Union losing control of its own territory.

In response, the EU formally cancels the **Integrity Audit Expansion**, acknowledging it cannot enforce cross-jurisdictional audits amid legal resistance and collapsing cohesion. The **Public Redress and Auditability Directive** is launched, but its symbolic force is overwhelmed by the scale of unrest. Public trust remains frozen at historic lows.

The **Critical Infrastructure Continuity Protocol** is announced as a new priority, mandating manual override and decentralised fallbacks in energy, water, rail, and emergency services. Legal bases are invoked under DORA and Article 114, and funding is rerouted through civil protection channels. Yet implementation is patchy: member states with strained budgets delay compliance, and ENISA lacks enforcement muscle. The measure begins, but does not land.

Meanwhile, the **Sovereign AI Core Programme** stagnates. No new compute comes online, and talent drains to private actors. The **Openweight Containment Initiative** remains in legal limbo, with cloud providers awaiting ECJ rulings before acting.

Despite completing three major initiatives — the Gigafactories, the Tech Sovereignty Package, and the EAAD — no political capital is regained. The portfolio remains overburdened, and with **eu_political_capital** at 3.0, the Union operates in triage. The narrative is no longer about leadership, but survival.
