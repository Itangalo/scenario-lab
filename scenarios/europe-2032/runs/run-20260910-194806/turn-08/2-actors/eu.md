## Priority
No priority named this turn, because with zero measures in flight and political capital at 2.0 any new push would fail unenforced — holding the finished Municipal Continuity and Trust Pact in place outranks starting anything new.

## In practice
We spend this half-year enforcing what is already on the books rather than legislating. ENISA teams seconded under the Municipal Continuity Pact stay embedded with the moratorium cities, running manual-fallback drills for utilities and hospitals and certifying human-in-the-loop triage before any reconnection. The joint cyber telemetry and biosurveillance feeds accepted last turn are operated via ENISA and ECDC under NIS2 Article 11 mutual aid, with incident data from the ransomware and welfare-scoring cases as our contribution — no new fund is asked from ECOFIN.

We use the Health and EPSCO Councils to hold the line politically: a time-limited pledge of no new automated triage procurement without Provenance Clearinghouse audit, EIB maintenance cover for degraded essential services, and pressure on the defecting capital to route its side-deal US capacity through common allocation. We frame the August US refusal shock publicly as the case for EU-controlled fallback workloads from the completed gigafactories, while quietly accepting those workloads remain insufficient — rebuilding hiring and usable sovereign capacity is the next commitment period's work, not this turn's.
