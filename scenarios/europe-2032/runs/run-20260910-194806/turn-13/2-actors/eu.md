## Priority
M8, the EU Joint Threat Telemetry Accession, because it is the only instrument still delivering shared detection and patching to keep essentials degraded-but-running while we cannot fund or permit anything else.

## In practice
We keep the essentials pledge alive on existing law only. Under NIS2, the Cyber Solidarity Act and Health Union bases, ENISA and HERA/ECDC run the outward telemetry feed with seconded national SOC analysts and hospital lab focal points; automated patching from the spring breakthrough is applied strictly inside already-certified water, grid and hospital-intake sites under human supervision, with paper-forms-and-human-check remaining the sole reconnection test.

We do not seek a seat at the US-China risk agreement this half-year and do not take a side on export-control rationing — with no frontier capacity and no build to bargain with we would be informed afterwards in either case. On the automated-decision scandal we treat it as the second, more damaging reading: a lawful-but-harmful deployment in a gap the 2024 Annex III categories do not reach, and we log it for AI Act review rather than pretend enforcement effort can answer it, because restriction is now cheap and adoption politically impossible for years.
