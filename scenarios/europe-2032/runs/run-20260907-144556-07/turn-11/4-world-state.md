### A Response to Displacement, Amid Deepening Fragility

The EU launches the **EU Civilian Redress and Reintegration Facility**, a large-scale social intervention aimed at stabilising communities hit by AI-driven job losses in law, accountancy, software, and administration. Framed as an act of solidarity, the measure passes through an emergency EPSCO session, but only after tense negotiations over co-financing burdens and conditionality. Germany and France accept pilot roles; smaller states with tight budgets resist mobility mandates, threatening delays.

Despite political approval, implementation falters immediately. Fiscal hawks in several capitals slow disbursement, and legal challenges arise over whether the Union has competence to coordinate retraining pipelines. The facility exists on paper, but its benefits do not yet reach workers. No material improvement in labour absorption occurs this turn.

Meanwhile, the **Integrity Audit Expansion** formally concludes. While some audits clear systems for continued use, others are suspended indefinitely due to unverifiable model reasoning — a direct consequence of the opaque_reasoning event. Public trust in oversight erodes further; auditors are seen as certifying process, not safety.

But `ai_safety` drops by 3: capability advanced without corresponding assurance, and no finished measure improves shipped-system safety. The gap between power and understanding widens.

Resilience remains stagnant. Though the new facility aims to strengthen societal absorption, it delivers no benefit this turn. The **labour_displacement** wave deepens public resentment, while **backlash_physical** escalates — data centre occupations spread, and grid sabotage in Belgium triggers blackouts. The **election_annulled** event in a northern member state fuels conspiracy narratives, amplified by AI-generated disinformation.
