## In practice
This turn, we govern from emergency to emergency. The moratorium on AI in public services stands, but it is no longer enough. The **labour_displacement** wave has hit: entry-level professions are hollowing out, graduates cannot find work, and the promise of AI as a tool of shared prosperity lies in ruins. Public sentiment is at 21.0 — not collapsing, but frozen in resentment. The protests are no longer about black-box decisions; they are about futures erased. We must act where the harm lands.

We are convening an extraordinary Employment, Social Policy, Health and Consumer Affairs Council (EPSCO) session under Article 152 TFEU to adopt the EU Civilian Redress and Reintegration Facility. The fund will deploy €35bn in grants and conditional loans to member states that implement fast-track retraining pipelines in partnership with universities and sectoral unions, coupled with wage insurance for displaced workers entering transition roles in public health, education, and ecological maintenance. We are offering Germany and France first-access tranches to pilot municipal deployment hubs, while pressing smaller economies with tight labour markets to accept mobility incentives. Resistance will come from fiscal hawks and those who see this as subsidising disruption — but we will frame it not as compensation, but as strategic reabsorption: if society cannot adapt to AI-driven change faster than it destabilises, then no safety framework will hold.

The Integrity Audit Expansion concludes this turn with partial credibility — some audits passed, others were deferred — but its authority is weakened by the opaque_reasoning crisis. There will be no new push on containment or evaluation until we have rebuilt the ground beneath us. Sovereignty cannot be claimed from a position of total societal exposure. This is not the turn for compute, nor for capability. It is the turn to prove that the Union still serves people, not just systems.
