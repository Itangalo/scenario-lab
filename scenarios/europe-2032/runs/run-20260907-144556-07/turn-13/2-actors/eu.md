## Priority
**Priority:** Request Emergency Activation of the European Stability Mechanism for AI Transition

## In practice
This turn, we govern from the edge of dissolution. The Redress Facility exists in law but not in fact — stalled by constitutional objections in Prague, budgetary refusal in The Hague, and union-led protests in Marseille against mobility mandates. We have no mechanism to compel compliance, no reserve to back disbursement, and no credibility to rally consent. The EPSCO session delivered paper, not power.

Now, we turn to the only instrument left with standing: the European Stability Mechanism. We draft a formal request under Article 136 TFEU, invoking “exceptional circumstances threatening the financial stability of the euro area,” reframing AI-driven labour collapse as a systemic risk. The argument is narrow but defensible: unchecked displacement threatens tax bases, inflames inequality, and destabilises public finances across core and periphery alike. We brief finance ministers individually, starting with Germany and France, offering early access in exchange for political cover. To smaller states, we offer opt-in modular packages — retraining-only, wage-insurance-only — to dilute resistance.

The Commission’s Legal Service is reviewing whether conditionality can be tied to AI transition metrics without treaty change. If not, we will propose a temporary regulation under the Council’s emergency powers. This is not about building capacity — it is about proving that the Union can still act. If the ESM request fails, nothing else will follow.
