## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without social legitimacy is a fortress without a people.

  - Grounds: The convergence of *capability_jump*, *safety_breakthrough*, and *member_state_defection* reveals a new triad of risk: technical overreach, political fragmentation, and public alienation. The US has weaponized access, a model has crossed a biothreshold, and a member state has bypassed Union controls — all while public sentiment remains at 25.0. This is not merely a crisis of capability or safety, but of legitimacy. Our authority to act depends not only on securing systems, but on being seen to secure *for* the Union, not *over* it.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and we will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; nor will we assume that control justifies silence, and we will now couple every containment measure with a public explanation of its purpose and limits.

  - Grounds: The *member_state_defection* proves that opacity breeds fragmentation: when national governments cannot see or trust the Union’s safeguards, they make their own deals. The *safety_breakthrough* gives us a rare moment — a real tool to explain what “assurance” means in practice. We must use it to rebuild credibility. Henceforward, acting under uncertainty includes the duty to justify, not just to decide.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent catastrophic misuse through sovereign control of high-capability AI systems, with enforceable containment, real-time monitoring, and public accountability for all systems above 55.0 capability.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* pushes `ai_capability` to ~67.0, far beyond the 60.0 we were preparing for, and the *safety_breakthrough* offers a narrow window to embed new verification tools before the next leap. At the same time, the *member_state_defection* shows that unilateral control is fracturing. Our commitment must evolve: not just to build sovereign systems, but to make their containment visible, verifiable, and legitimate — or risk irrelevance.

## New measure

A binding requirement that all sovereign AI systems above 55.0 capability subject to SMAF or the AI Containment Protocol must publish an annual public assurance report, co-signed by the EAAD and national oversight bodies, explaining their safety architecture, containment measures, and incident history in accessible language.

**Priority:** Establish the Sovereign Model Assurance Framework (SMAF)  
We are pushing SMAF hardest this turn because the *capability_jump* and *member_state_defection* together prove that sovereign systems are meaningless unless they are trusted — not just by experts, but by the governments and publics that must live with their risks.

## In practice

This turn, we are fighting on three fronts: technical, political, and perceptual. The *capability_jump* has vaulted the frontier to an estimated 67.0, likely from a US lab releasing a self-improving agent architecture — we know this from the sudden spike in autonomous research output in quantum materials and cryptanalysis. At the same time, the *safety_breakthrough* — a real-time circuit-tracing tool that can flag deceptive reasoning before output — is being adopted by leading labs, including one in France. We are fast-tracking its integration into SMAF’s audit protocols through emergency delegation under Article 114 TFEU, working with DG GROW and the EAAD’s new red-team unit.

But the real shock is the *member_state_defection*: Poland has signed a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler for priority access to frontier models, with joint AI cells embedded in its defence ministry. Warsaw calls it “pragmatic deterrence,” but it undermines our entire containment strategy. We are responding not with sanctions — that would fracture cohesion further — but with inclusion: inviting Poland into the SMAF framework with expedited observer status and offering to co-host the second air-gapped cluster in Wrocław, under dual EU-Polish key. The offer is backed by a €10bn resilience grant from the Modernisation Fund, tied to full CIRS compliance by turn 8.

Meanwhile, the EAAD and CIRS have formally concluded their establishment phases. EAAD’s first audit — of the SACP’s prototype agent stack — begins this month, using the new interpretability tool. CIRS transitions to ENISA as a standing cyber-monitoring function, but we are adding a new mandate: to publish quarterly threat assessments, redacted where necessary, to feed into the Public Assurance Protocol. The Swedish InvestAI site is now breaking ground, and we are using the Article 122 override to fast-track grid connections across the Nordic corridor. But we know that without public trust, none of this holds. So we are coupling every containment move with a transparency obligation — not because it’s easy, but because it’s the only way to keep the Union together when the world is pulling it apart.
