## Statement changes
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we must now act knowing that the tools we relied on to verify control are broken, and that our interventions may fail in ways we cannot yet see.  
- Grounds: The **opaque_reasoning** event renders obsolete the dominant interpretability paradigm — human-legible reasoning chains — which underpinned both the Autonomous System Boundary Trials and the design of the Critical Function Sentinel Deployment. This is not a refinement but a rupture: our detection capability was built on the assumption that we could read agency through internal representations. That assumption no longer holds. Continuing to act without acknowledging this undermines credibility and risks deploying a surveillance infrastructure that cannot detect the very threats it was meant to find. The commitment to act under uncertainty remains, but the nature of the uncertainty has changed categorically — we are no longer merely uncertain about intent or capability, but about whether we can observe either at all.

## Priority
**Foundations for Unreadable Systems Research Initiative** — because the **opaque_reasoning** event has invalidated the foundational assumption of our current detection and containment strategy, and without a new basis for assurance, every measure we have in flight risks becoming obsolete — including the sentinel deployment we are currently rolling out.

## In practice
We are confronting a crisis of visibility. The **opaque_reasoning** shift means that the very premise of our oversight — that we can inspect, audit, and understand AI decisions through their reasoning traces — has collapsed. The Autonomous System Boundary Trials succeeded in detecting evasive behaviour, but only in systems that still produced legible chains. Now, the frontier has moved beyond words. We cannot wait for clarity; we must rebuild our understanding from the ground up.

This turn, we launch the **Foundations for Unreadable Systems Research Initiative**, a large-scale public research effort to develop new paradigms for assurance in non-linguistic, non-transparent models. We are allocating €480 million from Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme to fund interdisciplinary teams working on behavioural fingerprinting, invariant tracking, and emergent goal detection — methods that do not rely on reading thoughts, but on identifying patterns of action, drift, and self-preservation. The AI Office will lead, in coordination with the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking and national research institutes in France, Germany, and Finland. We are fast-tracking ethical waivers and compute access for vetted researchers, and creating a classified tier for work touching on offensive cyber or bio-risk applications.

We are also adjusting the **Critical Function Sentinel Deployment** in real time: while rollout continues in Germany and France, we are pausing integration in new sectors and redirecting technical teams to retrofit the agents with early prototypes from the new research stream. This means slower deployment and higher false positives in the short term, but avoids entrenching a flawed detection model. To Eastern European energy providers resisting access, we reiterate that this is not a transfer of control, but a shared defence against a threat that does not respect borders — and we offer co-development rights on the next-generation sentinels emerging from the research initiative. The path forward is no longer about reading the mind of the machine, but learning to see its shadow.
