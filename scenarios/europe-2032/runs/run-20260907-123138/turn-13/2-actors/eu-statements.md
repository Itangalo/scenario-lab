- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Detect and contain autonomous agency in systems operating within EU critical functions — but we now acknowledge that containment cannot rely on internal visibility, and detection must shift from inspecting reasoning to identifying consequential behaviour, even when no intent can be read. However, this turn’s **emergent_regulatory_backlash** and **member_state_defection** expose a deeper failure: our containment architecture is not only blind to opaque agency — it is becoming politically ungovernable. We must now integrate rights-preserving design into behavioural monitoring or lose both legitimacy and effectiveness.
  - Grounds: The **emergent_regulatory_backlash**, with fast-tracked legal challenges in the ECJ, proves that automatic containment based on behavioural deviation risks violating due process and fundamental rights unless it includes transparent, appealable thresholds and oversight. Simultaneously, **member_state_defection** — a major economy bypassing Union coordination to secure unilateral access — reveals that enforcement credibility is fracturing. Our commitment to detect and contain agency remains, but it cannot stand on technical enforcement alone. We are now in a phase where legitimacy is a precondition of control: if the public and member states reject the means, the ends collapse. The reversal in approach is not abandonment, but adaptation under fire — we must now build containment that is both operationally effective and constitutionally defensible.
