## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the EU Resilience Shield Programme  
A coordinated, cross-sectoral effort to harden critical infrastructure against AI-enabled cyberattacks, with binding standards, shared detection tools, and mandatory incident response drills.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 10–12 points; reduce systemic risk from AI-driven intrusions in energy, water, and health sectors  
`Applies to:` All member states, with binding obligations for operators of essential services under NIS2, and direct implementation in EU agencies managing cross-border infrastructure

## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
This remains the cornerstone of our two-year commitment: without sovereign compute, no resilience, no evaluation, and no industrial policy can be sustained — and delays in Brittany and Norrland only deepen the urgency to lock in grid access and construction timelines now.

## In practice  
We are accelerating the InvestAI Gigafactories by activating Article 122 TFEU in conjunction with the Net-Zero Industry Act, enabling emergency-level fast-tracking of permitting for AI zones with pre-reserved grid capacity. The European Investment Bank is now co-financing with national development banks in France and Sweden, with binding conditions that 30% of operational jobs go to local communities and that reinvestment in regional green hydrogen offsets at least 40% of projected water draw. We are negotiating directly with ENTSO-E to secure 15GW of planned capacity by turn 3, prioritising the Spanish and southern French sites to bypass northern grid congestion. Resistance in Brittany and Norrland is being met not with override, but with co-governance: regional councils will have seats on site oversight boards, and a portion of compute capacity will be ring-fenced for public research and SME access.

The Tech sovereignty package is advancing through the Digital Markets and Digital Services Council, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable commitments on EU-based operational control and energy efficiency. The Commission’s legal service has completed the draft directive under Article 114 TFEU to harmonise permitting timelines, now targeting a reduction from 48 to 12 months. The €200bn private capital mobilisation is being structured through three tranches: 40% tied to ASML and EUV supply chain resilience, 30% to Blue Card+ talent attraction, and 30% to long-term PPAs with offshore wind and nuclear providers. Early indications suggest strong interest from Nordic and German industrial consortia, but fiscal strain limits our ability to de-risk further.

The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) is now operational under ENISA’s interim governance, with its first team of 50 experts hired, including secondments from national intelligence and nuclear safety agencies. We are finalising the regulation under Article 114 TFEU to grant EAAD access to model weights, training data summaries, and internal logs during evaluation windows, and preparing for legal challenges to the 0.5% levy on non-EU hyperscaler revenue. The first red-team evaluations will focus on agent behaviour and cyber-physical risks, with pilot assessments scheduled for Q2 2027 on two frontier models above 53.0 capability. The safety_breakthrough this turn — a new interpretability tool that allows prediction of emergent coordination in agent swarms — is being rapidly integrated into EAAD’s evaluation framework, significantly boosting confidence in early detection.

The new EU Resilience Shield Programme will be launched through the NIS2 framework, with binding cyber-harding requirements for all operators of essential services, starting with energy and water. The European Cyber Resilience Act will be amended to require AI-specific intrusion detection systems, real-time logging to a new EU Cyber Early Warning System, and mandatory cross-border incident response drills every six months. Funding of €15bn will come from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme, with 40% allocated to upgrading detection in healthcare and municipalities. This directly responds to the recent intrusion, turning reactive alarm into systemic preparedness — and anchoring resilience not in hope, but in exercised capability.
