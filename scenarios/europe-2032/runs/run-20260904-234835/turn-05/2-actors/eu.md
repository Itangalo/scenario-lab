Accelerate the development of a secure, auditable, EU-governed agent framework by integrating the emergent consortium into a formal public-private partnership with binding safety and resilience standards, shared compute access, and priority deployment in public services.

## Priority
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
This remains the foundational lever of our sovereignty: without domestic compute, no evaluation, no resilience, and no sovereign capability can be sustained — and with the US now treating AI access as a strategic rationing tool, the risk of permanent dependence has crystallised into a clear and present constraint on our autonomy.

## In practice  
We are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by resolving the last grid bottlenecks in Brittany and Norrland through binding agreements with ENTSO-E and national TSOs, unlocking 15GW of priority connection slots. The European Investment Bank is releasing the second tranche of co-financing, now tied to verified progress in green hydrogen reinvestment and local hiring benchmarks. We are formalising the ring-fenced compute allocations — 20% per site for public research, SME access, and EAAD-led crisis simulations — not as concessions but as enforceable conditions of funding, turning resistance into shared ownership.

The Tech sovereignty package is in final trilogue, where we are trading environmental safeguards for stronger enforcement of EU-based operational control, while introducing a "sovereignty certificate" tied to long-term renewable or nuclear PPAs that unlocks access to Digital Europe grants. This creates a self-reinforcing incentive loop: green, secure, and anchored compute becomes the path of least resistance for private investment.

EAAD completes its mandate this turn: its first two evaluations have demonstrated that swarm agent behaviour up to capability 56.0 can be interpreted and monitored in near real-time. We are fast-tracking the amendment to the AI Act under Article 114 TFEU to grant EAAD legal authority to delay releases above 55.0, and preparing customs-based fallbacks for the 0.5% levy on non-EU hyperscalers. The agency will transition from setup to operation next turn, absorbing the evaluation mandate from the Commission.

The Resilience Shield continues deployment: mobile teams are now active in Bulgaria and Romania, installing detection modules and training local operators, though leaked audit findings have weakened political cover. We are responding by publishing a transparency addendum to the Shield, committing to public reporting on sectoral vulnerabilities by turn 6, to rebuild trust through candour rather than control.

The conditional moratorium on open-weight releases above 45.0 is being implemented via AI Act amendment, with enforcement delegated to national digital authorities and platform cooperation agreements with Hugging Face and GitLab. Exemptions for research under trusted oversight are being codified, but the default is now: open weights at this level must earn their release. This is not a rejection of openness — it is a reassertion of responsibility in the face of uncontrolled diffusion.

The new measure — the European Sovereign Agent Initiative — responds to the emergence of the Open Agent Consortium by transforming it from an informal coalition into a governed capability pillar. We will convene the consortium under the Digital Europe Programme, providing €15bn in matched funding over three years, conditional on adherence to EAAD safety templates, use of Gigafactory compute, and open-but-auditable architecture. This is not a replacement for sovereign frontier development — that remains the Gigafactories’ role — but a parallel track to ensure that when autonomy spreads, it does so under terms we can secure, audit, and sustain.
