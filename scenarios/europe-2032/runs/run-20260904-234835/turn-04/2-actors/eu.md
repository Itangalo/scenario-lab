## New measure  
**Measure:** Introduce a conditional moratorium on open-weight releases above capability 45.0  
Establish a legal threshold: no open-source model weights capable of autonomous multi-day task execution may be released in the EU unless accompanied by a certified safety case from an accredited evaluator and subject to post-release monitoring obligations.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Slow the rise of openweight_capability to preserve governability; increase ai_safety by anchoring open releases to verified safeguards; signal regulatory seriousness to public and developers  
`Applies to:` Model developers, open-source platforms, and research institutions releasing AI weights publicly within or into the EU

## In practice  
We are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by finalising grid access agreements with ENTSO-E for 15GW across Spain, southern France, and the Baltic, prioritising sites where local co-governance councils have already approved construction. The European Investment Bank is releasing the second tranche of co-financing, contingent on verified progress in green hydrogen reinvestment and local hiring. In Brittany and Norrland, we are formalising the ring-fenced compute allocations: 20% of each site’s capacity will be reserved for public research, SME access, and crisis simulation runs under EAAD supervision. These are not concessions — they are commitments that turn resistance into ownership.

The Tech sovereignty package is now in trilogue negotiations, with the Council pushing for stronger enforcement of EU-based operational control, while the Parliament demands clearer environmental safeguards. We are holding firm on the 40% ASML supply chain linkage and the Blue Card+ talent corridor, but offering a new compliance mechanism: a "sovereignty certificate" tied to long-term PPAs with renewable or nuclear providers, which will unlock access to the Digital Europe Programme’s innovation grants. This creates a positive incentive without expanding fiscal exposure.

EAAD has conducted its first two pilot evaluations using the new interpretability tool for agent swarm behaviour, and the results — classified but shared with COREPER — confirm early detection is now feasible up to capability 56.0. We are fast-tracking the legal authority to delay releases by amending the AI Act under Article 114 TFEU, with a carve-out for emergency evaluations on models above 55.0. The 0.5% levy on non-EU hyperscaler revenue remains under legal review, but we are preparing fallback mechanisms using customs data and digital service declarations to ensure funding continuity.

The Resilience Shield is being implemented in phases: Germany, the Netherlands, and Denmark have already installed the AI detection systems and connected to the EU Cyber Early Warning System. For southern and eastern states, we are deploying mobile technical assistance teams from ENISA and leveraging the newly adopted cyber toolkit — now under review for EU endorsement — to close capability gaps without waiting for full centralisation. This is not top-down imposition; it is coordination at the speed of risk.

The new measure — the conditional moratorium on open-weight releases above 45.0 — responds directly to the momentum behind open models reaching general-purpose competence. While the grassroots cyber toolkit has strengthened resilience, its underlying architecture is now being repurposed for more general agentive functions. We cannot allow the open frontier to become a backdoor for uncontrolled capability diffusion. The threshold will be implemented via amendment to the AI Act, with enforcement through national digital authorities and cooperation with platforms like Hugging Face and GitLab. Exemptions will exist for research under trusted oversight, but the default is now: open weights at this level must earn their release. This is not a rejection of openness — it is a reassertion of responsibility.
