### The Fracture Deepens

Despite mounting pressure, AI capability inches upward, as frontier labs release a new model generation—less transformative than expected, but still advancing the baseline. The underwhelming performance fuels debate over a plateau, though the underlying trajectory holds. Open-weight models benefit, with capable derivatives now accessible on consumer hardware, widening the misuse surface.

### Access Cut, Resolve Tested

The sudden denial of access to the leading AI model shocks hospitals, ministries, and firms reliant on its reasoning engine. No explanation is given, and appeals fail. The incident confirms long-standing fears: the EU cannot enforce its rules if it cannot run systems independently. Publicly, officials frame it as a wake-up call; privately, panic flares in capitals.

### Sovereign Compute Launched, Costs Mount

In response, the EU launches the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme, fast-tracking three greenfield data centre clusters in Finland, Spain, and Poland. Binding grid access and jurisdictional immunity are granted, and €3.8 billion is committed. Yet early friction emerges: Finnish environmental groups challenge construction permits, and ASML warns that U.S. pressure may still block tooling transfers. The industrial base is willing, but not unimpeded.

ENISA finalises two conditional authorisations under the Sovereign Evaluation Regime, demanding monitoring hooks and red-teaming access—first real enforcement, but limited impact. The Emergency Critical Infrastructure Shield’s live-fire exercise exposes critical gaps in water systems, prompting emergency retrofitting. National regulators resist ENISA’s expanded authority, delaying enforcement.

### Sentiment Stagnant, Capital Erodes

Public messaging frames the compute push as infrastructure resilience, not restriction. But with job losses mounting in law, accountancy, and software, and no visible benefit yet, sentiment remains flat. Political capital erodes under the weight of three large measures in flight and a priority now straining credibility.
