## Priority
**Emergency Critical Infrastructure Shield** — because the cyber_major_incident proves that defence at the perimeter is already obsolete, and while evaluation remains in flight, our physical foundations are under active, automated assault: we must act now to prevent cascading failure, even at the cost of diverting focus from longer-term oversight.

## In practice
This turn, we activate Council Regulation (EU) 2032/XX on Emergency Cyber Resilience, invoking Article 122 TFEU to justify urgent action on systemic risk to critical infrastructure. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) is mandated to issue binding technical standards by June 2032 requiring all operators of essential services to implement AI-resistant segmentation, air-gapped backup controls, and real-time anomaly detection trained on synthetic attack vectors. A €1.2 billion emergency fund is drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme to co-finance upgrades, with 70% going to municipal utilities and regional grids that lack scale. We convene an extraordinary COREPER II meeting to fast-track adoption, offering compliance flexibility in exchange for participation in a pan-European live-fire exercise in July — coordinated by CERT-EU and involving simulated ransomware targeting SCADA systems.

At the same time, we press forward with the Sovereign Evaluation Regime, finalising accreditation protocols for Fraunhofer and TÜV Rheinland despite ongoing legal challenges from U.S. labs. The European AI Office issues a formal notice that any provider seeking market access after turn 13 must submit to evaluation or face exclusion — a line we know may trigger withdrawal, but one we are prepared to hold. Behind the scenes, we open quiet coordination with Dutch and German semiconductor firms to explore fallback compute pathways, anticipating further disruption from supply_chain_coercion. Public sentiment remains fragile, and we resist calls for sweeping bans, knowing that panic-driven restriction would collapse both capital and cohesion. We act narrowly, urgently, and together — or not at all.
