## Statement changes  
* - modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now add: we will not act alone when collective verification is available. If an independent, technically credible body offers evidence that we cannot produce ourselves, we will treat its findings as actionable, even if they challenge our assumptions or disrupt our sequencing. The cost of waiting for full sovereignty in understanding must not exceed the risk of acting blind.  
- Grounds: The emergent_global_ai_watchdog has demonstrated forensic capacity and technical credibility, publishing verifiable analyses of model leaks and supply chain risks that neither the EU nor any single member state could have produced at this pace. To ignore this capability — because it is not ours — would be to let pride compound uncertainty. We are politically bankrupt, technically dependent, and socially fraying. If we are to act under uncertainty, we must now do so with eyes open to sources beyond our borders — and our control.

* - modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now affirm that capability without continuity is not sovereignty, and continuity without legitimacy is not survival. Our two mandates converge not in control, but in endurance: the ability to absorb shocks, adapt to shifts, and hold together across crises. We are not merely a regulator or an industrial power. We are a polity that must persist.  
- Grounds: The metrics confirm a system in freefall: ai_safety at 5.0, eu_political_capital at 0.0, public_sentiment at 22.0. Yet we continue to build compute, pass directives, launch programmes. But infrastructure without political capacity is inert. Control without trust is coercion. The two mandates — self-determination and harm prevention — do not merely conflict; they collapse into a single imperative: endurance. If we cannot hold the Union together through the storm, nothing else we build will matter.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Join and conditionally empower the emergent global AI watchdog  
We will formally recognise the non-aligned AI monitoring body as a source of verified intelligence on systemic risks, grant it observer status in EU technical forums, and commit seed funding in exchange for binding access to its forensic pipelines and early warnings.

`Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 5–8 points through external verification of model leaks and misuse risks; improve resilience by +3 via early warning of supply chain and cyber threats; modestly raise public_sentiment by demonstrating responsiveness to independent accountability.  
`Applies to:` The emergent global AI watchdog, EU technical agencies (ENISA, AI Office), and member-state intelligence services.

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch the Sovereign AI Core Programme  
We are prioritizing this because, despite our political paralysis, the physical and legal anchoring of compute on EU soil remains the only foundation that can eventually restore both control and credibility — without it, every other measure, including this turn’s outreach to the watchdog, becomes a temporary patch on a system we cannot secure.

## In practice  
This turn, we are advancing the Sovereign AI Core Programme through binding contracts under the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, with the first procurement round for EU-fabricated accelerators now closed. The €7.2bn from the Strategic Autonomy Facility is being released in tranches tied to domestic lithography milestones, with 40% conditional on chip production at the new Île-de-France fab. We are using the Anti-Coercion Instrument to secure access to ASML tools, framing it not as a trade dispute but as a survival imperative. Host states — France, Germany, Poland — are being offered not just infrastructure, but governance: a seat on the Core Council and guaranteed emergency access in any cutoff scenario. This is not charity; it is covenantal infrastructure.

Simultaneously, we are operationalising the Social Continuity Compact. The European Labour Authority is finalising verification protocols for retraining plans, and the €1.8bn Transition Equity Facility is being unlocked for Málaga and Thessaloniki pilots. We are amending the Digital Public Services Directive under the European Semester, making retraining plans a condition for all AI-driven modernisation funds. Negotiations with BusinessEurope continue, but we are holding firm: no deployment without co-signed transition pathways. The message is clear — automation must not mean abandonment.

And for the first time, we are reaching outward. We are initiating diplomatic contact with the emergent global AI watchdog, offering €200m in structured funding over three years — disbursed quarterly, conditional on full access to their forensic pipelines and early warnings. We will grant them observer status in the AI Office’s technical working groups and ENISA’s threat coordination cell. This is not surrender of sovereignty, but strategic interdependence: we cannot build full understanding alone, not at ai_safety 5.0. But we will not be passive recipients — our funding comes with demands for transparency, and our participation comes with the right to challenge. If this body is to shape our decisions, it must be accountable to our institutions, even as it remains independent. We act under uncertainty — but not in isolation.
