## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the Sovereign AI Core Programme  
A unified, EU-operated infrastructure stack — from chips to models to fallback nodes — ensuring uninterrupted access to critical AI functions under duress, with full legal and physical anchoring in the Union.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–20 points by establishing sovereign compute at scale, hardening against geopolitical cutoffs; support resilience by ensuring continuity nodes run on trusted infrastructure; begin closing the gap with ai_capability through coordinated investment.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, national digital agencies, selected hyperscalers operating in the EU.

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Sovereign AI Core Programme  
We are prioritizing this because the Emergency Continuity Response Initiative proved we can fix nodes — but not if the infrastructure beneath them is foreign-controlled and recallable; we acted under crisis, but the crisis exposed that our sovereignty is still rented. We must now build what we cannot lose.

## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from emergency response to sovereign foundation. The Continuity Assurance Framework has formally concluded — its nodes are now certified, its audits recorded — but in Thessaloniki, Málaga, and Riga, we found fallback systems that were compliant on paper but failed under stress. The framework was not wrong; it was insufficient without the infrastructure to sustain it. We cannot air-gap systems that run on foreign compute.

So we are launching the Sovereign AI Core Programme under Article 172 TFEU, mobilising €7.2bn from the Strategic Autonomy Facility and leveraging the Anti-Coercion Instrument to secure chip supply. This is not a research project. It is a binding directive: by turn 15, the EU will operate its own integrated stack — from EU-fabricated accelerators to sovereign data centres to audited models — capable of running critical services without external dependency. The Joint Undertaking will lead deployment, with binding milestones for compute availability, energy resilience, and model access.

We are already negotiating with ASML and STMicroelectronics to fast-track lithography access, and with France, Germany, and Poland to site three sovereign clusters under EU legal jurisdiction. Resistance remains: some capitals still prefer the certainty of American access to the uncertainty of our timeline. So we are offering co-ownership, not just compliance — technical seats on the Core Council, priority access for national emergencies, and cost-sharing for transition. We are not banning foreign systems; we are making ours indispensable. The lights must stay on — and this time, they must stay on because they are ours.
