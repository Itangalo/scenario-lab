### A Fragile Recovery, Under New Pressure

The EU’s emergency response to the ransomware crisis stabilised critical nodes, but confidence remains thin. In Thessaloniki and Málaga, fallback systems certified under the Continuity Assurance Framework failed stress tests — compliant on paper, brittle in practice. The revelation undermines trust in procedural resilience and confirms a deeper truth: sovereignty cannot be outsourced.

Amid this fragility, the European Union launches the **Sovereign AI Core Programme**, a binding initiative to build an integrated, EU-controlled AI stack from chips to models. Invoking Article 172 TFEU, Brussels mobilises €7.2bn and fast-tracks negotiations with ASML and STMicroelectronics for lithography access. Three sovereign clusters are slated for France, Germany, and Poland, under joint EU jurisdiction. The move is framed not as exclusion, but as indispensability — offering co-ownership and emergency priority to reluctant capitals.

Yet the moment is strained. Labour markets fray as AI displaces entry-level roles in law, accounting, and customer operations. Graduates face a hiring freeze, fuelling quiet resentment. Public sentiment, already low, dips further to 20.0 as unions warn of a "two-tier state": one with access to AI power, and one serving it.

Meanwhile, export controls tighten globally. Under the US posture of **CONSOLIDATION**, allied access is preserved — but only barely. The EU finds itself rationed, its frontier access contingent on political alignment. This deepens dependence even as sovereignty efforts begin.

The new measure launches without fanfare. Internal resistance simmers: some member states fear delays, others dependency on unproven infrastructure. The commitment to no more free rides is softened — enforcement now requires consultation and a viable alternative — acknowledging that coercion fractures cohesion.

### Signals in the Fog

Rumours grow of a global AI watchdog, independent of US and EU influence, gaining traction among non-aligned states. Worker strikes over retraining demands in key digital hubs slow deployment of new continuity systems, exposing the tension between security and social equity.

The world moves fast. AI capability remains at 75.0, but safety stays at 5.0 — a dangerous plateau. No open weights breach the firewall. The gap holds, and the stakes rise.
