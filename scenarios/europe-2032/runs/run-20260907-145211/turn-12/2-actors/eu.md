## Statement changes  
* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse — but we now define "sovereign control" not only as technical independence, but as *operational continuity under duress*, meaning that any control regime must ensure uninterrupted access to critical AI functions for public services, even during geopolitical cutoffs or internal enforcement actions. However, we now add: this continuity must be socially sustained. A system that secures infrastructure but fractures cohesion is not sovereign — it is brittle. Therefore, sovereign control must include enforceable retraining pathways, equitable access to AI productivity gains, and binding commitments from automating institutions to reinvest displaced labour capacity into public resilience.``  
- Grounds: The strikes in digital hubs and the continued erosion of public sentiment to 20.0 confirm that operational continuity is not only technical — it is political and social. The Sovereign AI Core Programme cannot run on public trust that has been hollowed out by job losses and perceived exclusion. We cannot claim to control AI if we lose the consent of those it displaces. The commitment must expand: sovereignty now means not just keeping the lights on, but ensuring who gets to be in the room when they’re switched on.

* ``- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement will now be conditional on the operational readiness of the Sovereign AI Core Programme, and we will not cut off a member state from critical capabilities unless we can offer a viable, secure alternative within 90 days. We act to defend the regime, not to punish into helplessness. However, we now extend this principle: no enforcement will proceed without prior consultation and co-design of the alternative, and no member state will be left without access during transition — even if that means temporary dual use of foreign systems under EU supervision. Furthermore, we now apply this same standard internally: no EU institution or public agency may deploy AI that displaces workers without a verified retraining and redeployment plan, co-signed by national labour authorities and the European Labour Authority.``  
- Grounds: The same logic of conditional enforcement that applies to member states must now apply to our own institutions. If we demand continuity and equity from others, we must embody it. The strikes over retraining are not a side effect — they are a signal that our legitimacy is fraying. The commitment evolves: control is not only about who runs the stack, but who bears the cost of running it.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Establish the Social Continuity Compact  
A binding framework requiring all public AI deployments that displace more than 50 full-time equivalent roles to include a retraining and redeployment pathway, co-designed with national labour authorities and funded from a central EU transition pool.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase public_sentiment by 10–15 points by anchoring AI adoption in social equity; support resilience by reducing labour shocks that cascade into institutional failure; begin closing the legitimacy gap that undermines enforcement of sovereign control.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national public agencies, recipients of EU cohesion and digital transition funding.

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch the Sovereign AI Core Programme  
We are prioritizing this because it is the only measure that simultaneously builds the technical, legal, and industrial foundation without which no other control — social, safety, or strategic — can be sustained; everything else rests on whether we can run AI on infrastructure we cannot lose.

## In practice  
This turn, we are operationalising the Sovereign AI Core Programme through the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, with binding milestones set for turn 13: lithography access secured via the Anti-Coercion Instrument, land rights finalised in Île-de-France, Bavaria, and Małopolska, and a first procurement round launched for EU-fabricated accelerators. The €7.2bn from the Strategic Autonomy Facility is being disbursed under Article 172 TFEU, with 40% tied to domestic chip production and 30% to energy resilience. Resistance persists in capitals reliant on US cloud access, so we are offering not just co-ownership of clusters, but a seat on the Core Council for each host state — and emergency priority in any future cutoff. This is not a subsidy; it is a covenant.

At the same time, we are launching the Social Continuity Compact to address the growing fracture in public legitimacy. Under the European Semester framework, we are amending digital transition funding rules: no disbursement for AI-driven modernisation unless a retraining plan, verified by the European Labour Authority, is in place. The €1.8bn Transition Equity Facility will co-fund upskilling in AI-adjacent public roles — cyber monitors, model auditors, continuity drill coordinators — creating a ladder, not a cliff. We are negotiating with the ETUC and BusinessEurope to codify this in the upcoming Digital Public Services Directive. The message is clear: sovereignty is not just about who controls the stack — it is about who gets to build it, and who gets to benefit.
