### Claiming a win while the stores burn
The half-year opened with money leaving AI. Funds that had underwritten data-centre expansions and cloud credits pulled back, valuations halved, and two private compute deals European hospitals and universities had quietly relied on were cancelled. Frontier labs talked openly about training less, later.

Washington tightened again. Even allied buyers went onto volume quotas for advanced chips and hosted model access, with older tool servicing approved case by case. The Hague signed under protest. In Brussels the decision was to manage the quota, not fight it: no new concessions on lithography, keep spares talks with Tokyo and Seoul alive, freeze any forced migration and live off what was already on European soil.

That made the one bright spot matter more. In three health and justice administrations, EU-funded scheduling and case-triage assistants cut waiting lists and decision times by weeks. The Commission moved to brand it: dashboards, EU labels, visits, and — crucially — first call on black-start cover for the showcase clinics and courts.

The containment protocol agreed two years earlier finally paid out. Cross-border mutual aid, isolation playbooks and manual fallback drills were now formal procedure, and when the spring brought sabotage, it helped. Small groups opposed to automation broke into generator compounds, drained fuel stores and smashed breaker-log cabinets, calling them collaboration. Several off-books pools the towns had boasted about were wrecked.

Engineers restored by hand where the protocol existed; where pools had stayed off-scheme, there was nothing to reimburse and no spares to draw. Police guards went up around showcase sites, which protected the pilot but fed the story that Brussels guarded computers better than neighbourhoods.

By June the showcase survived, the fallback held in patches, and the Union's standing was thinner than ever. Quotas metered from outside, investors gone, stores sabotaged from inside — Brussels could point to waiting lists falling, and few believed it would last.
