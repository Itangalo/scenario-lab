### Desks through winter
The town-hall desks stayed open into winter. Where fuel and breaker books had been registered, civil-protection payments arrived the same day and joint patrols of stores held. Two more attempted thefts were turned back in paired towns. Where pools stayed off the books, refusal hardened further. A drained store in November left an unregistered village waiting two nights for heat; engineers restored it by hand, without reimbursement.

Clinics kept running on the European scheduling assistant alone. American copilots remained dark without explanation. Waiting times stayed weeks lower than in 2030, dashboards stayed public, and the failure-mode playbooks bought hours again during two autumn intrusions that moved across grid operators.

### The far-away handshake
In October, Washington and Beijing announced a limited risk pact on securing model weights, restraining autonomous escalation tools and controlling a class of biological design software, with thin verification. Brussels learned the outline afterwards, from press briefings and side conversations in Tokyo and Seoul.

The High Representative sought a seat at the verification table, offering European incident logs, bio-surveillance data and the certified playbooks as a contribution. Co-sponsorship from Japan and Korea produced polite encouragement, no invitation. American and Chinese officials praised the European material in public and continued meeting without it. The bridge track became correspondence and a small liaison presence, not participation.

Capability growth eased slightly in the half-year, the one relief from the pact. Safety work did not get easier at home: systems grew more opaque, assurance slipped, and municipal services absorbed more strain. Investors did not return.

By December, lit wards beside dark streets remained. The pact slowed the frontier a little; it did not bring capacity back, and Europe watched it from outside.
