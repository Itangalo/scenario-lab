### Cut off
The notice arrived on a Tuesday, short and without reason. American frontier access that hospitals, ministries and firms had built on under quota went dark for European users. Bookings, triage support, procurement copilots that ran as metered fallback behind the EU default stopped answering. In two showcase hospitals the screens fell back to the European scheduling assistant alone.

Brussels called it managed. Under the quota-management line there would be no new concessions on lithography tools, spares talks with Tokyo and Seoul would continue, and clinics would live off what was already on European soil. In practice engineers spent the autumn re-routing queues by hand.

That same autumn brought two counterweights. Researchers published an interpretability result that actually held on deployed systems — failure modes that could be predicted and certified rather than argued over. ENISA and the crisis network took it straight into playbooks, and DG REFORM pushed it into the showcase triage assistants with new audit labels.

And the showcase kept delivering. In the three health and justice pilots, waiting lists stayed down by weeks, decisions arrived in days. Dashboards stayed published, ministers visited, showcase clinics and courts kept first call on black-start cover.

### Stores and guards
The sabotage did not stop. Fuel stores were drained again in November, breaker-log cabinets smashed, one generator compound fire-damaged. Where the black-start and manual fallback network now formally existed, restoration by hand worked: mutual aid, isolation, paper logs. The measure completed in December, and engineers could point to wards that stayed warm because drills had become procedure.

Where pools had stayed off-books, there was still nothing to reimburse. The Commission offered to bring them on-scheme with payment for fuel and spares, to remove the target painted on them. Some towns accepted; others refused, calling registration control. Police guards stayed on showcase sites. The protection held, and the resentment hardened — guarded clinics in lit streets beside dark neighbourhoods.

By December the Union could show certified assurance and falling waits, and could not show independent capacity. The cut-off read less as outrage than as predicted failure. Investors did not return. Whispers grew of technicians quietly pooling leftover quota and cancelled private capacity to keep machines running outside allocation rules, unconfirmed and persistent.
