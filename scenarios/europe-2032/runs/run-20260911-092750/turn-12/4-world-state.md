### Registration desks
The Compact started as a desk in town halls: bring the fuel log, the spare transformer, the paper breaker book, get a receipt and same-day payment from civil protection money. DG ECHO teams paired willing towns with refusing ones, ENISA issued the standard log.

Uptake was partial. Perhaps a third of off-books pools came on-scheme by June. Where they did, joint guarding replaced showcase-only guards and two attempted drains were stopped. Where they did not, refusal hardened — registration as control, reimbursement as a leash.

With American fallback copilots still dark, engineers kept living off European soil. Showcase clinics ran on the EU scheduling assistant alone; waits stayed weeks down, dashboards stayed public. The certified failure-mode playbooks held in two more intrusion attempts, buying hours for manual isolation.

But capacity did not return. Tokyo and Seoul talks produced sympathy and spare parts brochures, no volume. Whispers of technicians pooling leftover quota and cancelled private capacity grew louder — a van seen behind one hospital, a meter running after hours — never confirmed, never denied.

Sabotage continued at lower intensity: smashed cabinets in February, a drained store in April in an unregistered town that then waited three days for heat. Police protection held, resentment held with it. Lit wards beside dark streets remained the image.

The showcase formally closed as a project this spring, becoming routine operation. Ministers stopped visiting.
