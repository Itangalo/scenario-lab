### Holding the line
July to December 2029 was a sustainment semester. With no budget for new builds, Brussels lived off what was already installed.

Grid crews closed out the segmentation programme begun in 2027. Control rooms received the last breaker kits, hospitals and water operators rehearsed islanding, and ENISA kept the pooled telemetry from the spring containment running. When autumn phishing waves hit municipal networks, they degraded rather than stopped — a quiet vindication officials cited in every Council.

The political energy went to hospitals. The joint EPSCO-Health pact funded locum cover, paid for site-level fixes to referral software, and promised no forced migration until tools passed clinician acceptance tests. Joint review boards with unions met in the five walkout states. Most wards stayed staffed, but doctors described the tools as merely less broken, and national news still ran images of handwritten discharge notes.

Washington's tiered rationing hardened into routine paperwork: licences, queues, downgraded models for European public workloads. Domestic data-centre capacity from the spring tranche absorbed the load without new failures, but without expansion either.

Frontier capability crept forward elsewhere while Europe patched and negotiated. Open weights narrowed the gap again, and safety assurance slipped further behind. By December, ministers could claim essential services had held — and little more.
