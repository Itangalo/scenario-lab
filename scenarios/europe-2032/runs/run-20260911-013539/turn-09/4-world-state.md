### Cut off and hit at once
Autumn brought two shocks in quick succession. Washington providers suspended the leading American model for Union users at short notice, with no reason given and no appeal channel. Hospitals, ministries and firms that had built workflows on it found outputs blocked overnight. Weeks later a large automated attack swept public administration — ransomware locking municipal systems, a poisoned software dependency forcing hurried audits, and brief disruptions to energy dispatch desks.

Defenders were visibly behind at first. Then the new ENISA tooling began to bite: automated patching pushed at the speed vulnerabilities were found, and swarm-behaviour detection caught lateral movement that signatures had missed. EU-CyCLONe exercises ordered fallback to EU-hosted downgraded models and, where those failed, to paper-manual procedures.

### Holding the line, barely
The fallback held essential services running but degraded. Two regions kept clinician-fixed referral pilots alive on domestic models, and joint review boards with nurses certified the fallback triage tools as legible. Elsewhere waiting lists lengthened, discharge-note errors returned, and water-permit fights sharpened as utility managers in two member states quietly held back new data-centre permits while siding with hospital staff in public meetings.

The lithography pool survived on life-support. Trade officials kept the initialled spares-for-slots understanding alive without new concessions, but no chips flowed and burden-sharing quarrels continued.

Robots changed nothing. Vendors offered fixed-task machines for controlled warehouses, defence ministries kept carrying and mine-clearance trials as trials, and manufacturers explained missed deliveries.

Brussels ended December with services upright, legitimacy thin, and dependence exposed in public.
