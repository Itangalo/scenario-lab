### A Turn of Reckoning

The EU confronts a stark reality: infrastructure without control is illusion. The sudden denial of access to the leading AI model—used in hospitals, agencies and grid operators—exposes the limits of its sovereignty project. Despite Gigafactories coming online and energy-backed compute secured, the Union cannot run what it does not govern. The Emergency Physical Protection regime collapses in COREPER, blocked by constitutional resistance from Germany and Austria; no armed deployment on domestic soil will proceed.

In response, the Commission launches the **EU-Controlled Model Hosting & Execution Framework**, a large category 4 measure aiming to create air-gapped, legally sovereign execution environments under EU law. Two Gigafactory sites will be retrofitted with €18bn from repurposed funds. France backs the plan; Germany insists on civil oversight. The US mission condemns it as digital autarky, warning of fragmentation.

Meanwhile, the **Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative** completes rollout across Tier 1 operators, but its impact is muted—physical node security remains unaddressed, and autonomous threats evolve faster than static defences. No resilience bonus lands.

A rare bright spot: a **safety_breakthrough** emerges from interpretability research adopted across major labs. Behaviour once opaque can now be predicted and verified in deployed systems, lifting global assurance. The EAAD fast-tracks integration into audit protocols.

AI capability advances to 65.0, driven by self-improving toolchains. Open-weight models rise to 53.5, narrowing the gap as frontier leaks proliferate. Job losses mount in entry-level professions—law, accounting, software—fueling quiet resentment. Public sentiment holds at 19.0, brittle but not yet erupting.

Political capital erodes further. The portfolio charge hits −9 (three ongoing measures), and the priority adds −1. With `eu_ai_sovereignty` falling to 5.0—penalised for rising capability and no sovereign access—the Union’s leverage dwindles. Action continues, but authority frays.
