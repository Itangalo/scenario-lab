## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI infrastructure with full operational control, energy autonomy, and real-time auditability by turn 7  
- Trigger: eu_frontier_access_denied and the portfolio charge reducing `eu_ai_sovereignty` to 7.0  
- Grounds: The Union has been cut off from the leading model with no explanation and no recourse—proof that sovereignty was never a matter of infrastructure alone, but of enforceable access. Our commitment assumed that building within our borders would ensure control; it now stands exposed as incomplete. With `eu_ai_sovereignty` collapsing below 10.0, we are not merely behind—we are functionally disempowered. The commitment remains, but its failure this turn forces a reckoning: securing infrastructure is meaningless without securing the right to use it.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone restores agency when the foundations of power have eroded.  
- Grounds: The safety_breakthrough proves that progress in control is possible—but only where developers choose to adopt it. We cannot mandate what we do not govern. Our previous posture treated early action as inherently stabilising; it now risks compounding dependence if that action relies on foreign systems we cannot audit or compel. We must act under uncertainty, yes—but with clearer eyes about which levers are ours to pull.

## New measure
**Measure:** Establish EU-Controlled Model Hosting & Execution Framework  
Create a legally and physically sovereign execution environment for high-capability AI models, operating independently of foreign cloud providers and governed by EU law, with mandatory onboarding of all systems above capability threshold 60.0 used in public services or critical infrastructure.

`Category:` 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 11  
`Targeted effect:` Restore `eu_ai_sovereignty` to at least 40.0 by enabling uninterrupted operation of certified models under EU jurisdiction, reduce dependency on external access, and lay foundation for autonomous capability development  
`Applies to:` Member states, EIB, ENISA, national digital agencies, certified AI providers, public-sector operators

## Priority
**Priority:** Establish EU-Controlled Model Hosting & Execution Framework  
Because eu_frontier_access_denied has proven that physical infrastructure alone does not confer control, and because our sovereignty metric has collapsed to 7.0—lower than at any point in the simulation—we must now build not just compute, but sovereign execution. This is no longer about resilience or restriction; it is about creating a domain where the EU can run AI systems no one else can switch off.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the inflection point where infrastructure ceased to be the solution and became the symptom. The denial of frontier access during an ongoing cyber_major_incident has exposed a fatal flaw: we built data centres, but we did not build autonomy. With `eu_ai_sovereignty` at 7.0, we are not just dependent—we are governable by others’ choices. 

The new framework will be established under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market clause to mandate hosting of all high-capability models used in public functions within a newly designated Sovereign Execution Zone. The EIB will allocate €18bn from repurposed InvestAI contingency funds to retrofit two existing Gigafactory sites with air-gapped, EU-law-governed execution environments, using openweight models above 51.5 as fallback baselines. ENISA will draft technical standards by turn 9, requiring hardware-rooted attestation, real-time audit logging, and no outbound telemetry to foreign entities. National digital agencies will be required to migrate all public AI services into the zone by turn 11.

Germany and France have conditionally supported the move, provided civil oversight is embedded from the start. We are offering joint governance through a new Board of Sovereign Execution, co-chaired by the EAAD and a rotating member state, to prevent unilateral control. The US mission has registered strong objections, calling it protectionist—but we are no longer negotiating access as a privilege. We are defining it as a right. If we cannot run AI under our own rule of law, then we do not run it at all.
