## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI execution and control under EU law``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The previous commitment assumed that infrastructure—compute, energy, physical security—was the path to sovereignty. But we have been denied access to systems running on our own soil, governed by foreign terms. Infrastructure without jurisdiction is vulnerability. The new direction shifts from building capacity to enforcing authority: we must not only host AI, but control it—legally, technically, and politically—within a domain no external actor can override.

## New measure
**Measure:** Mandate Real-Time Auditability for All High-Capability Models in Public Use  
Require all AI systems above capability index 60.0 deployed in public services or critical infrastructure to expose internal reasoning traces, decision logs, and training provenance to EAAD auditors in real time, using hardware-enforced attestation to prevent obfuscation.

`Category:` 1 (Evaluation and oversight)  
`Size:` small  
`Finishes on turn:` 10  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by at least +5.0 and strengthen `eu_ai_sovereignty` by enabling active enforcement of compliance within the Sovereign Execution Zone  
`Applies to:` Member states, certified AI providers, public-sector operators, EAAD, ENISA

## Priority
**Priority:** Establish EU-Controlled Model Hosting & Execution Framework  
Because the denial of access during a live cyber incident proved that compute without control is not sovereignty—it is dependency—and because `eu_ai_sovereignty` has collapsed to 5.0, we must complete the shift from hosting to governing. This framework is the only measure that creates a domain where the EU can run, verify, and compel behaviour from AI systems without deferring to foreign jurisdictions.

## In practice
We are executing the final phase of the EU-Controlled Model Hosting & Execution Framework under Article 114 TFEU, mobilising the EIB’s €18bn contingency reserve to retrofit two Gigafactory nodes—Luxembourg and Marseille—with air-gapped execution zones governed exclusively by EU law. ENISA has drafted the technical standards: no outbound telemetry, hardware-rooted attestation via trusted platform modules, and mandatory logging of all model inputs and outputs to an EAAD-monitored audit chain. National digital agencies are instructed to begin migration planning immediately, with full cutover required by turn 11. France supports immediate adoption; Germany demands civil society representation on the Board of Sovereign Execution. We are offering Berlin co-chairmanship of the board’s oversight panel and guaranteed veto rights over any military-linked application.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the new auditability mandate through the Council’s Competitiveness configuration, leveraging the EAAD’s expanded authority post-safety_breakthrough. The rule will bind all models above 60.0 used in healthcare, justice, energy, or transport to expose interpretable decision pathways in real time. We are working with openweight developers like Mistral and Aleph Alpha to implement lightweight verification layers compatible with performance constraints. Resistance is expected from US-based providers whose architectures resist introspection—we will respond by barring non-compliant systems from the Sovereign Execution Zone. This is not about transparency alone; it is about enforceability. If we cannot see what a system is doing, we do not control it. And if we do not control it, it is not ours.
