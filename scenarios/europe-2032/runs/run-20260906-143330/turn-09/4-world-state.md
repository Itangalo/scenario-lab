### A Sovereignty of Paper

The EU pushes forward with legal authority as its last line of defence. The **EU-Controlled Model Hosting & Execution Framework** advances physically—retrofitting begins in Luxembourg and Marseille—but politically, it fractures. Germany refuses co-chairmanship without civil society veto power over model deployment criteria, not just use. Talks stall. France threatens unilateral adoption under national security provisions.

Simultaneously, the Commission fast-tracks the **Mandate for Real-Time Auditability**, banking on post-safety_breakthrough tools to enforce compliance. EAAD drafts technical standards requiring hardware-attested logging of reasoning traces. But US providers signal non-compliance: their models, they argue, cannot operate under such constraints without performance collapse. One major vendor suspends updates to its EU-facing systems, citing “unworkable verification demands.”

Behind the scenes, the **export_control_escalation** bites hard. Under the standing US consolidation posture, allied access is rationed—not cut, but throttled. Two Gigafactory-linked clusters lose scheduled upgrades to next-gen accelerators. The Union’s compute pipeline wobbles, not from blockade, but from scarcity masked as volume licensing.

AI capability climbs to **66.5**, driven by self-improving training loops now embedded in core development pipelines. Open-weight models rise to **55.0**, narrowing the gap as frontier techniques leak through academic spin-offs and developer forums. Autonomous cyber agents evolve faster than the **Sovereign Cyber Shield** can adapt, especially in mid-tier infrastructure.

Resilience erodes further. Without physical node protection, hardened networks are bypassed via insider-abuse vectors. Grid operators report near-misses. Public sentiment dips to **18.0** as a wave of AI-driven layoffs hits legal support and municipal services. Protests grow in Paris and Berlin, echoing the quiet spread of the human futures movement.

Political capital collapses under the weight of four active measures and a failing priority. Sovereignty, starved of both access and cohesion, slips again.
