## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI infrastructure with full operational control, energy autonomy, and real-time auditability by turn 7  
- Trigger: the cyber_major_incident and backlash_physical this turn  
- Grounds: The physical sabotage of grid connections and occupations at data centre sites confirm that the political and social foundations of sovereign infrastructure are as fragile as its technical ones. The original commitment assumed delivery by turn 7 was a matter of coordination and investment; it is now clear that without public legitimacy and physical security, even completed installations will not function. We are no longer merely behind schedule—we are losing ground. The commitment stands, but its meaning has shifted: it is no longer about completing a programme, but about defending one already under attack.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.  
- Grounds: The bio_uplift_findings represent a categorically new signal: for the first time, credible non-state actors may be within reach of engineering human-infective organisms using publicly accessible models. This is not a hypothetical path—it is a demonstrated design space. Our previous posture assumed that capability and access would scale together slowly enough to allow course correction. That assumption has failed. We must now act not only under uncertainty, but under the certainty that waiting for consensus will mean waiting past the point of no return.

## New measure
**Measure:** Enact Emergency Physical Protection of Critical AI Infrastructure  
Deploy EU-wide security protocols and rapid-response units to protect data centres, grid interconnects, and microgrid facilities from sabotage and occupation, integrating Frontex, national gendarmerie, and ENISA into a unified physical resilience framework.

`Category:` 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 9  
`Targeted effect:` Reduce physical disruption risk by hardening protection of sovereign compute and energy infrastructure, stabilising resilience at 50+ by turn 9 and enabling the continued operation of InvestAI sites under threat  
`Applies to:` Member states, Frontex, national interior ministries, ENISA, EIB, COREPER

## Priority
**Priority:** Enact Emergency Physical Protection of Critical AI Infrastructure  
This is no longer a question of strategic ambition or economic competitiveness—it is about ensuring that the infrastructure we are building can survive the next six months. With the backlash_physical event confirming that AI infrastructure is now a target, not just a tool, and with cyber defences only as strong as the systems they run on, physical security is the new front line. Without it, every other measure fails.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the point where the Union’s survival as a governing body depends on its ability to physically defend its own foundations. The InvestAI Gigafactories, though officially completed, are now under threat at multiple sites—occupations in Brittany, sabotage in northern Sweden, and intelligence reports of planned disruptions in Hungary. These are not isolated incidents; they are symptoms of a system-wide legitimacy crisis. We are activating Article 78 TFEU to establish an Emergency Physical Protection regime, designating sovereign AI infrastructure as strategic assets equivalent to nuclear facilities. Frontex will coordinate with national forces under a new Joint Resilience Command, funded through a €12bn reallocation from the stalled SCAP programme and overseen by the EAAD for auditability.

The Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative remains in flight, but its deployment is being slowed not by technical hurdles, but by the inability to guarantee the physical integrity of the nodes it must protect. We are now integrating the two: cyber resilience cannot exist without physical resilience. The Commission is issuing a Recommendation to COREPER for binding security standards, including armed response protocols and drone surveillance at all Tier 1 compute sites. Germany and Austria have raised concerns about militarisation, but we are offering a joint inspection regime with EEA oversight to preserve civil liberties while ensuring continuity. This is not a temporary measure—it is the new baseline. If we cannot protect what we build, we will have nothing to govern.
