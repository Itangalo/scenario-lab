### The queue at forty seconds
Winter turned on a file. An ombudsman inquiry into a welfare and policing support system used in three member states found thousands refused, flagged or scored down on machine-generated summaries, with caseworkers clearing a screen every forty seconds. Logs existed and were never read. Television ran the clock on air: forty seconds to decide a life.

Brussels chose to call it a breach, not a gap. The Commission froze the impugned queues, ordered human re-decision with time floors, promised arrears and published logs through national ombudsmen and the Fundamental Rights Agency. The framing held in the legal press — obligations breached, enforcement to follow — but not in town halls, where crowds asked why conformity had passed on paper.

### Cures and proofs from elsewhere
In the same months, two genuine goods arrived. University hospitals began tailored immune therapies for a blood cancer previously untreatable, planned with American models, and a materials result from a joint lab cut battery degradation sharply. Clinics in Lyon and Gdansk posted both on their doors: shorter queues from the permit modules, first remissions from the new protocol.

Gratitude was real and thin. Commentators noted every dose depended on compute and models the Union could not switch on by itself. Procurement on European-anchored terms was announced, but vials arrived before racks did.

### Strait and strain
Extended exercises around the Taiwan Strait lifted shipping insurance again. Chip consignments reserved for European sites slipped further, surcharges were absorbed from continuity stocks, and guarded build sites went back to routine patrols after more cut cables.

The youth placement corps grew from thousands to low tens of thousands, visible in wards and hospitals that otherwise degraded without stopping. It was not enough against the hiring freeze. Graduate job boards stayed empty, opposition lists blamed data centres, and mayors who welcomed placements also asked for slots they did not get.
