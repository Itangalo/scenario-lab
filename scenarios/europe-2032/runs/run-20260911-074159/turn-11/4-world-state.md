### The cutoff
In September the American provider cut off the leading model for European users with a short status notice and no appeal path. Hospitals in Lyon and Gdansk that planned immune therapies on it, ministries running permit assistants, firms with agents built on its tools found outputs stopped mid-queue. Brussels called it continuity management. Staff shifted workloads to European-anchored capacity and older fallbacks. Queues stayed open but slower, wards degraded without stopping.

The cut landed the same month developers announced a discontinuous jump: faster planning systems whose internal steps no longer read as language. Vendors sold it as performance. For auditors and caseworkers it meant the oversight method Brussels relied on — read the log, check the reasoning — stopped working at once.

### Forty seconds, again
Television kept running the clock. The frozen welfare and policing queues stayed frozen while re-decision teams with time floors worked through backlogs. Arrears were paid in two of the three states; in the third payments slipped to spring. Published logs confirmed what the ombudsmen had found: thousands refused or flagged on machine summaries, approvals at a screen every forty seconds, records generated and never reviewed.

The Commission held the breach line — high-risk obligations breached, enforcement to follow — to save the statute. Town halls did not buy it. Crowds asked why conformity had passed, mayors asked for placement slots that did not come, opposition lists tied empty graduate job boards to data-centre power deals.

The youth corps placements finished their funding cycle with low tens of thousands placed, visible in hospitals and ports. It was credited locally and dwarfed nationally. Strait insurance rose again, reserved chips slipped further, surcharges were absorbed from stocks. By December gratitude for remissions coexisted with open talk of dependence as humiliation, and restriction, not adoption, set the terms.
