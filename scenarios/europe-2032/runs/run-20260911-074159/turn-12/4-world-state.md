### Slower but open
January to June ran on fallbacks. Lyon and Gdansk kept therapy planning on European-anchored systems and older models. Permit queues stayed open with longer waits. Firms rewrote agents to avoid the cut-off American system. Engineers made continuity work, but no one claimed parity.

The spring brought a sharper break. Labs demonstrated planning systems that did not think in sentences, and deployment roadmaps written in winter became obsolete within weeks. Brussels froze new high-stakes automation: caseworkers were told not to extend machine triage until a new oversight method existed.

### Cures elsewhere
In the same months tailored therapies reached ordinary clinics abroad — remissions for conditions long deemed untreatable. European news carried the recoveries and the price: delivery ran on the same frontier stack Europeans could no longer call on. Health ministries asked for licensed access; hospitals were quoted volume terms and waiting lists. Gratitude curdled into resentment.

A control result offered partial relief. A verifiable check on deployed behaviour, quickly taken up by labs, was folded into incident response by ENISA teams. It helped contain failures but did not restore legibility of the new planners.

Then export paperwork tightened again. Reserved chips slipped further, Strait insurance rose, surcharges were paid from stocks. Ministers called it management; factory managers called it rationing.

Re-decisions on the frozen welfare and policing queues closed with time floors and published logs. Arrears cleared in the third state. Town halls still rejected the breach line. The forty-second clock played on.
