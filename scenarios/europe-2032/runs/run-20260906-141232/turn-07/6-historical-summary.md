The EU advanced the Sovereign AI Core Programme by invoking emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU, overriding national grid objections and securing provisional capacity allocation from ENTSO-E despite legal challenges from Germany and Austria. Site development proceeded in Brittany, northern Sweden, and the Alps, backed by a €30bn EIB bond tranche tied to audit readiness. The Commission leveraged access to Core-hosted AI tools to secure Italian and Hungarian agreement to phased implementation of the Real-Time Logging Mandate, though full compliance remains uncertain.

Global open-weight model diffusion accelerated, narrowing the gap with closed frontier systems, while the EU was abruptly cut off from the leading frontier model—disrupting healthcare and administrative functions and exposing critical dependency risks. In response, the EU joined a NATO-Nordic cyber intelligence pool, enabling early detection and containment of follow-up attacks. The Gigafactories measure concluded with limited sovereignty impact, and while political capital saw partial recovery due to crisis management and programme advancement, it remains constrained by ongoing initiatives. Public sentiment stagnated, reflecting widespread resignation amid persistent instability and unmet expectations.
