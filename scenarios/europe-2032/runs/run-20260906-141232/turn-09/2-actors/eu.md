## In practice
We are delivering the Core. The Commission has activated Article 122 TFEU to override national objections, treating sovereign compute as emergency infrastructure akin to vaccine production during the pandemic. Germany and Austria remain legally opposed, but their technical representatives are now embedded in the oversight board, and ENTSO-E has provisionally stabilised grid integration through dynamic load isolation. The EIB has released the final tranche of its €30bn bond: 18 exaF-days of verified compute are now under Union control, auditable in real time by EAAD, and physically disconnected from external hyperscalers. This stack runs the Real-Time Logging Mandate’s detection engine, hosts MPAC’s shared evaluation sandbox, and will soon run the first EU-governed training run above 65.0 capability.

Internationally, the MPAC summit concluded with provisional alignment: Japan will join export controls on AI-specific chip designs, South Korea commits to mutual recognition of safety evaluations, and Norway offers Arctic data centre co-location for joint redundancy. Canada remains cautious, demanding parliamentary review, but has agreed to share intelligence on dual-use model leaks. This is not a treaty—it is a network of enforceable side-payments: market access for compliance, exclusion for defection. The cyber_major_incident, which exploited an auto-generated exploit chain in hospital admin systems across three member states, proved why this matters: the attack was traced to a compromised open-weight model derivative, but because logging was active, EAAD reconstructed the chain within 72 hours and pushed countermeasures through the newly formed MPAC alert channel.

Public sentiment remains at 14.0, and the streets of Lyon and Vienna saw protests against “black box governance” last week. But we are no longer reacting—we are anchoring. The Core is live. The Coalition is moving. And for the first time since the run began, the Union has something the frontier cannot ignore: a switch it can flip.
