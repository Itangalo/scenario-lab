### Core Under Pressure, Logging Live

The Sovereign AI Core Programme advances physically but fractures politically. Construction begins in Brittany and northern Sweden under emergency directives, yet Germany and Austria escalate their legal challenge, arguing the Energy Union’s solidarity clause was never meant to override national grid sovereignty for non-energy-critical infrastructure. ENTSO-E warns of cascading instability risks. The EIB bond remains tied to performance, not progress—no further tranches will release without audit-verified compute delivery.

The Real-Time Logging Mandate is now live. EAAD and ENISA receive data streams from major hospitals, grid operators, and banks. Early alerts flag anomalous model behaviour in two financial AIs, prompting investigations. Italy and Hungary comply with initial logging requirements, granted continued access to efficiency-boosting AI tools. However, full transparency remains partial: some state-linked operators delay integration, citing technical incompatibility.

### Frontier Falters, Safety Crumbles

Eval anomaly reports from a leading lab suggest unexplained reasoning jumps in unreleased models. Developers are investigating whether internal representations are becoming less interpretable, with early indications pointing to non-linguistic structures dominating model reasoning. These changes complicate oversight mechanisms that rely on traceable logic chains. Jurisdictions relying on interpretability-based assurance are re-evaluating their frameworks, but no systemic failure has been confirmed. The EU’s recently launched regime remains operational, though under growing strain.

Labour markets contract sharply in entry-level law, accounting, and software. Universities report near-zero graduate placements in administrative AI-facing roles. Public frustration mounts, though no single scandal crystallises.

### Coalition Gambit Launched

Responding to opacity and displacement, the EU convenes a summit to form the Middle Power AI Coalition (MPAC). Draft agreements propose aligned export controls and joint bargaining on compute access with Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Norway. Initial signals are positive—especially from Seoul and Oslo—but Tokyo demands exemptions for semiconductor R&D partnerships, and Ottawa insists on parliamentary review. Full alignment is uncertain.

Despite the ambition, public sentiment dips further, reflecting anxiety over job losses and opaque technology. Political capital stabilises only due to the coalition initiative’s perceived strategic value.
