## In practice
We are acting on three fronts: building the Core, enforcing runtime control, and reshaping the international balance. The Sovereign AI Core Programme is now in full legal and logistical motion. The Commission has issued implementing acts under Article 122 TFEU, treating the Core as a matter of public health and security. Binding directives have fast-tracked permitting in Brittany, northern Sweden, and the Alps, overriding national objections through a qualified majority under the Energy Union’s solidarity clause. ENTSO-E has provisionally allocated grid capacity under protest, but the EIB’s €30bn output-linked bond tranche is now active—tied to audit readiness and delivered compute, not construction milestones. Germany and Austria have been offered full environmental transparency and joint technical oversight, but the project will not wait.

The Real-Time Logging Mandate has now concluded its implementation and is operational. EAAD and ENISA are receiving live feeds from critical infrastructure operators in health, energy, and finance. Automated anomaly detection is flagging irregularities, and escalation protocols are being tested in joint exercises with national CERTs. Italy and Hungary have begun phased compliance, incentivised by continued access to Core-hosted AI tools that have demonstrably reduced administrative backlogs. This is not just transparency—it is the first real-time nervous system for AI governance in the world.

And now, we are acting internationally. The eval_anomaly_reports and opaque_reasoning events confirm that the frontier is becoming unpredictable and unobservable. We cannot rely on understanding systems we do not control. The labour_displacement crisis deepens public anger, but also strengthens our hand: the middle_power_coalition opportunity is real, and we are taking it. The Commission is convening a summit in Luxembourg next month to formalise the Middle Power AI Coalition, with draft agreements on export licence alignment, joint compute access negotiations, and shared evaluation infrastructure. This is not a bloc against anyone—it is a network that makes unilateral coercion costly. If the US or China want to cut us off, they now have to cut off Japan, Korea, Canada, and Norway too. That changes the game.
