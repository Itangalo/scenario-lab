### The Denial and the Response

In January 2031, the European Union’s access to the most advanced AI models was abruptly severed. Hospitals relying on diagnostic agents, ministries running policy simulations, and research teams training next-generation systems were locked out overnight. No explanation was given. The cutoff was not framed as coercion but as an administrative realignment—yet its effect was strategic: Europe’s most sensitive AI-dependent operations stumbled in real time.

The EU responded with resolve, but not unity. The **Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes**, deployed across Jülich, CINECA, Prague, and Bologna, came fully online, feeding telemetry into the new EU AI Situational Awareness Hub. However, two member states withheld full integration, citing national security, weakening the network’s coherence. Still, the nodes marked a tangible step toward technical autonomy.

Simultaneously, the **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** began staffing in Seville, Stockholm, and Bucharest. Fast-tracked recruitment under emergency protocols brought in specialists, though delays in security clearance slowed deployment. The facility aims to transform raw data into strategic insight—a necessity now that external intelligence is unreliable.

The new **Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps** was launched as a joint EEAS-Europol initiative, designed to neutralise synthetic crises within 72 hours. It inherits urgency from the German election postponement but starts from a deficit: tools are untested, coordination protocols fragile, and public trust low.

### Capability and Fragility

AI capability rose to 87.5, supported by incremental gains in validation efficiency and limited self-improvement cycles, operating within existing compute constraints. The growth reflects sustained optimization rather than recursive breakthrough, consistent with the fixed pace of the world.

Open-weight capability increased to 68.5, aided by partial assimilation of leaked techniques and improved fine-tuning pipelines. While progress is tangible, it remains bounded by integration delays and the lack of full-scale training runs, preventing a steeper rise.

Resilience inched up to 50.0 as the Sentinel Nodes finished, delivering partial gains under metric rule 10. But political capital remained at 0.0, crushed by ongoing portfolio costs and the failure to act decisively before the cutoff. Sovereignty fell to 15.0, penalised for rising capability and the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event, which exposed irreplaceable dependencies.

No sentiment shift occurred. The public sees no victory—only institutions scrambling after the fact.
