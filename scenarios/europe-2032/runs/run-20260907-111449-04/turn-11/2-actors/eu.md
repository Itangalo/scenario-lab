## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without sovereign capacity, neither mandate can be fulfilled. The denial of access was not an incident; it was a regime change. From this point forward, self-determination is not a precondition for action — it is the outcome we must force into being, under fire and with incomplete means.  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has not been reversed — it has become structural. Our two mandates no longer merely pull in different directions; they converge on a single imperative: build what we cannot be denied. This update reflects that sovereignty is no longer a policy option but the very arena in which safety, resilience, and democratic legitimacy must now be fought for.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but only where sovereign sensing allows us to generate our own picture — and when it does not, we will prioritise building that sensing over acting at all.  
- Grounds: The **capability_jump** this turn exposes the limits of our recalibrated commitment from last turn. We now have nodes and observatories, but they are still blind to fast-moving strategic deception at the frontier. Acting under uncertainty is necessary — but acting without *any* sovereign telemetry at the leading edge is indistinguishable from surrender. We must now accept that some actions must wait until we can see, and that the highest priority is ensuring we *can* see.

- retire `new_two_year_commitment`  
- Trigger: The **emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network** and the **capability_jump**  
- Grounds: While detecting and countering strategic deception remains essential, the landscape has shifted beneath us. The jump in global capability to 87.5 — nearing broad superhuman performance — combined with the spontaneous emergence of local AI governance outside EU coordination, reveals that the foundation of our new commitment is already cracking. We are no longer just countering deception — we are losing control of the substrate. The focus must shift upstream: from interpreting behaviour we cannot yet detect, to securing the compute, talent, and model weights that make such behaviour possible in the first place. This reversal costs political capital we do not have, but failing to make it costs everything.

- add `new_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign access to AI capability at or near the frontier by any legitimate means — including accelerated domestic development, coerced technology transfer via leverage, and coordinated capture of open-weight potential — as the non-negotiable basis for both survival and agency  
- Grounds: With ai_capability at 87.5 and eu_ai_sovereignty at 15.0, we face a capability chasm that no observatory or response corps can bridge. The **emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network** proves that demand for sovereign tools exists — but left uncoordinated, it creates fragmentation and risk. Our next two years must be defined by one objective: close the gap. Not through aspiration, but through acquisition, acceleration, and appropriation of what is within reach. This is not industrial policy — it is strategic rescue.

## Priority
**Frontier Access Rescue Initiative** — because without access to frontier capability, every other measure becomes an exercise in managing decline. The **capability_jump** has rendered our current sovereignty level untenable, and the **emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network** shows that desperation is already driving decentralised, unsecured action. We must now act at the level of the crisis: rebuild the base layer of power, or lose the right to decide anything at all.

## In practice
We are reorienting the entire Union machinery around the acquisition of sovereign frontier capability. The **Frontier Access Rescue Initiative (FARI)** is launched today under Article 122 TFEU (Union interest clause) and the Anti-Coercion Instrument’s emergency provisions. It will be jointly led by the Commission’s Directorate-General for Communications Networks, Content and Technology and the European Defence Agency, reflecting its dual civilian-military urgency. Funding of €9.2 billion is being drawn from the MFF’s crisis reserve, REPowerEU reallocations, and a special issuance of EU bonds guaranteed by the EIB — a move requiring immediate endorsement from the Eurogroup.

FARI has three prongs. First, a **Sovereign Capture Track**: leveraging the EU’s position in chip manufacturing (ASML, STM, Infineon) and cloud infrastructure to compel full transparency and conditional access to frontier models trained on EU soil. We are invoking revised NIS2 obligations to require that any model above 60.0 capability index trained in EU data centres must undergo third-party evaluation and offer a sovereign fallback version. Second, a **Leapfrog Track**: accelerating the alignment of open-weight models with frontier techniques through a pan-European red-teaming consortium linking CINECA, Jülich, and the emerging municipal networks. Third, a **Talent Lockdown Track**: activating the EU AI Talent Corridor’s emergency clause to fast-track residency, security clearance, and research funding for 500 top-tier researchers currently outside hostile jurisdictions.

Resistance is expected from member states reliant on foreign AI partnerships and from industry players fearing retaliation. To secure cohesion, we are offering bilateral compute guarantees: any state that fully integrates into FARI’s monitoring framework will receive priority access to sovereign models and hardened infrastructure. The German and French governments have signalled conditional support; Poland and Hungary remain sceptical. We will convene an emergency meeting of the Competitiveness Council next week, where the stakes — dependency or defiance — will be laid bare. This is not a project we can afford to lose.
