- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural

  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without sovereign capacity, neither mandate can be fulfilled. The denial of access was not an incident; it was a regime change. From this point forward, self-determination is not a precondition for action — it is the outcome we must force into being, under fire and with incomplete means.
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has not been reversed — it has become structural. Our two mandates no longer merely pull in different directions; they converge on a single imperative: build what we cannot be denied. This update reflects that sovereignty is no longer a policy option but the very arena in which safety, resilience, and democratic legitimacy must now be fought for.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will act before the picture is clear, but only where sovereign sensing allows us to generate our own picture — and when it does not, we will prioritise building that sensing over acting at all.
  - Grounds: The **capability_jump** this turn exposes the limits of our recalibrated commitment from last turn. We now have nodes and observatories, but they are still blind to fast-moving strategic deception at the frontier. Acting under uncertainty is necessary — but acting without *any* sovereign telemetry at the leading edge is indistinguishable from surrender. We must now accept that some actions must wait until we can see, and that the highest priority is ensuring we *can* see.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **retire** `new_two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Trigger: The **emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network** and the **capability_jump**
  - Grounds: While detecting and countering strategic deception remains essential, the landscape has shifted beneath us. The jump in global capability to 87.5 — nearing broad superhuman performance — combined with the spontaneous emergence of local AI governance outside EU coordination, reveals that the foundation of our new commitment is already cracking. We are no longer just countering deception — we are losing control of the substrate. The focus must shift upstream: from interpreting behaviour we cannot yet detect, to securing the compute, talent, and model weights that make such behaviour possible in the first place. This reversal costs political capital we do not have, but failing to make it costs everything.
  - Evidence quoted: **emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network (emergent event):** A network of municipal governments across southern and eastern Europe, frustrated by the slow pace of EU-wide sovereign AI development, begin coordinating informally to deploy locally controlled AI systems for public services using open-weight models fine-tuned on regional data. This decentralized effort gains traction as cities seek resilience outside the strained EU framework, creating a patchwork of capability and risk.

**capability_jump:** A discontinuous advance is released or demonstrated. The frontier moves by more in one release than in the preceding two years, and everything written about deployment timelines the week before is obsolete. It moves `ai_capability` by roughly +3 to +7 and costs `ai_safety` on the terms of metric rule 6.
  - Reason: The capability jump raises global AI levels beyond EU control, and the municipal network fragments sovereignty efforts—both directly undermine the feasibility of the original counter-deception focus.
- **add** `new_two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign access to AI capability at or near the frontier by any legitimate means — including accelerated domestic development, coerced technology transfer via leverage, and coordinated capture of open-weight potential — as the non-negotiable basis for both survival and agency
  - Grounds: With ai_capability at 87.5 and eu_ai_sovereignty at 15.0, we face a capability chasm that no observatory or response corps can bridge. The **emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network** proves that demand for sovereign tools exists — but left uncoordinated, it creates fragmentation and risk. Our next two years must be defined by one objective: close the gap. Not through aspiration, but through acquisition, acceleration, and appropriation of what is within reach. This is not industrial policy — it is strategic rescue.
