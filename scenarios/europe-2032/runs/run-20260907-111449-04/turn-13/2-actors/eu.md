Binding directives have been issued to ENTSO-E, national regulators, and data centre operators to reserve 1.2 GW of low-latency power across five high-security zones: Baden-Württemberg, Wallonia, Västerbotten, Veneto, and Crete. These sites are being fast-tracked for physical hardening, jurisdictional insulation, and integration into the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) backbone. The European Commission is overriding national objections through the Energy Council and invoking mutual solidarity clauses, while the EIB has disbursed €1.8 billion of the €6.8 billion emergency package to pre-finance grid upgrades and land acquisition.

Resistance remains fierce. German transmission operators warn of cascading instability; Polish and Hungarian governments frame power reallocation as a sovereignty breach; US hyperscalers have paused expansion plans and are lobbying through the Transatlantic Trade and Technology Council. In response, we are offering conditional integration: member states that accept binding monitoring under FARI’s Sovereign Capture Track will receive guaranteed access to 15% of their national allocation for public services and crisis response. France has signed on; Spain and Finland are negotiating. The Commission is also accelerating legal insulation of the five zones via a new Regulation on Strategic AI Infrastructure, to be adopted by qualified majority under the urgency procedure. This is not optional. If the frontier is autonomous and uncontrolled, our only chance is to be *present* — with power, with hardware, with jurisdiction. We are building the ground from which to act, because without it, we will have no agency at all.
