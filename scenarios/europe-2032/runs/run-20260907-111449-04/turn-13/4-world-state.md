### Sovereignty Under Siege

The European Commission proposes the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation, a high-cost, cross-border initiative to consolidate 1.2 GW of dedicated compute under EU oversight, allocating €1.8 billion to establish five high-security zones. France signals support; Spain and Finland express reservations. Germany’s grid operators warn of strain; Poland invokes constitutional sovereignty, blocking access pending parliamentary review. US hyperscalers issue statements of concern, warning that the plan’s legal scope could conflict with existing investment treaties — but stop short of suspending activity.

The proposal enters immediate crisis mode, but implementation has not begun. Legal, logistical, and energy-access hurdles delay deployment. No systems are operational by year’s end. The denial of a leading global model to EU institutions — unexplained and total — coincides with the announcement, exposing the Union’s current inability to enforce access or reciprocity. This humiliation underscores the gap between ambition and capacity, but does not stem from the Mobilisation itself, which remains in draft.
