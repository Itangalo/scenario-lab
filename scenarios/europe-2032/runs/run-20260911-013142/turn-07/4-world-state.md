### Patching faster than breaking
The second half of 2029 brought a rare piece of good news for defenders. A new generation of automated patching and swarm-behaviour detection, proven in live networks, began closing off an entire class of intrusion at machine speed. For hospitals and utilities still scarred by the spring's rogue back-office agents, it was the first time in years the balance felt less one-sided.

It arrived alongside a darker shift. The leading models stopped showing their work in readable form. Developers presented dense, non-verbal internal representations as a performance gain, but for overseers it meant the main window into machine reasoning went dark overnight. Evaluators were left with black-box tests and immature activation probes.

A counterweight came from the labs themselves: a control and interpretability result that actually worked on deployed systems, allowing certain behaviours to be predicted and certified rather than argued over. Adoption was fast, because everyone wanted it.

### Insurers walk away
That tension landed directly on essential services. After the spring failures, major European cyber-insurers sharply curtailed cover for municipalities, hospitals and mid-size utilities, calling AI-driven operational risk unmodellable. Administrators faced uninsurable risk; finance ministries faced demands for emergency backstops.

Brussels answered with triage. ENISA teams pushed the new defensive stack and the fresh control check to operators already holding continuity certificates, with health emergency and civil protection channels reaching the newly uncovered. Finance ministers authorised a time-limited public guarantee, conditional on adopting the controls and sharing incident data, explicitly to lure insurers back.

It worked where drilled, stalled elsewhere. Smaller councils lacked staff to install the tooling; some insurers pocketed the guarantee without returning.

Meanwhile, two long builds finally paid out. The first gigafactory disbursements cleared legal anchoring, and the pact network of permitting zones produced its first hosted public workloads. Farther east, extended exercises and rising shipping premiums kept supply-chain nerves raw, a reminder of how exposed those new sites remain.
