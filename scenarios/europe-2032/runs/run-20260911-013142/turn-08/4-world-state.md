### When the lights stayed on
The attack came in February, automated and indiscriminate. A poisoned software update, repackaged by machine-generated tooling, swept through municipal IT providers, hospital administration systems and a cluster of mid-size utilities. Appointment books vanished, billing froze, and in three regions grid balancing fell back to manual procedures for days. Attribution would take months. What mattered in Brussels was that the phones kept working where drills had been done.

The continuity certificates and the public guarantee from last autumn became the triage line. Cities that had installed the new patching and swarm-detection stack plus the certified controls restored services in hours; councils that had lacked staff to install them queued for emergency teams. Insurers, still largely absent, watched from the sidelines while finance ministries extended the temporary backstop rather than open a new fund.

### A pact in two directions — proposed, not yet delivered
The same weeks redrew the diplomatic map. Washington and Beijing announced a limited understanding on securing model weights and restraining autonomous escalation, with thin verification. Europe was not at the table when it was signed.

Brussels answered only by proposing to join the middle-power coordination framework taking shape around export licences, pooled bargaining for compute, and shared evaluation. The offer was put on the table: pooled incident telemetry from the guarantee scheme, hosting in the new permitting zones, and future offtake from the first gigafactory as a contribution to joint bargaining. In return, European negotiators requested a seat as observer in the verification arrangements, citing the recently adopted control certification as credentials.

No agreement has been secured and no metric effect is claimed from the proposal in this turn. Licence alignment stalled with two member states demanding first call on pooled access and mutual-aid crews, delaying a common EU mandate. No joint teams have yet deployed — the backlog in the hardest-hit hospitals was cleared by domestic emergency crews, stretched thin. Washington and Beijing have not agreed to observer access, and no verification role has been granted. Smaller municipalities complained the surge favoured the already-prepared, and evening news carried images of cancelled operations alongside ministers talking about supply chains.

By June, essential services were degraded rather than stopped, and Europe had opened a diplomatic track it lacked in January — but with implementation at least a turn away, no external acceptance, and no added operational capacity, political standing did not advance and public mood, anxious about dependency and machine-driven disruption, turned darker still.
