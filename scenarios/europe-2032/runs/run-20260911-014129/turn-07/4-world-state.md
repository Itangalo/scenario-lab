### The autumn of two containments
The ransomware sweep started in municipal IT and jumped. A compromised update, rebuilt with machine-generated tooling, locked registries, billing and hospital admin systems in a dozen regions within hours. Control rooms running the newly finished grid segmentation saw the breakers hold — lights stayed on — but screens went dark around them. Engineers fell back to phones and paper.

While servers were still being isolated, health authorities confirmed a separate biological release involving a modified agent. Clinics that had just paused the forced software migration now ran triage in masks. Sequencing teams and emergency stockpiles, rehearsed under old playbooks, were activated. Containment ran for weeks, with real casualties and long contact tracing.

Brussels did what its empty budget allowed. Ministers put the grid telemetry network, the health emergency authority and the cyber agency on a single mutual-aid protocol: pooled logs, pushed patches where tools were already installed, isolation of affected councils, no new procurement. The coordination was small and fast, and it kept essential services degrading rather than stopping.

A new interpretability result from the labs helped triage the February agent logs and the fresh malware, and was adopted quickly because defenders needed it. It did not restore trust.

The wider world tightened. Washington's export desks formalised rationing of advanced chips and models, with allies kept on volume licences while others were cut off. At the same time Washington pressed the Netherlands to extend curbs on lithography exports and servicing, reaching back to older machines and more customers. The Hague protested, Brussels recorded the move for a future coercion response, but contested nothing. Councils read both moves as the cost of dependence.

In the background, police logged break-ins and small fires at substations and construction sites, claimed in single-source posts as retaliation for the runaway agent and energy prices. Nothing was confirmed, but guards were doubled.

By December the grid held, the fever wards emptied, and the public concluded only that tools were weaker and wilder than promised.
