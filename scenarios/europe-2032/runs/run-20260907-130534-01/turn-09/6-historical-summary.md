The Sovereign AI Core Programme is declared operational, with audit-verified compute clusters in Brittany and northern Sweden now online under dedicated energy grids and isolated from external providers. The EIB releases the final €30bn tranche, securing 18 exaF-days of compute under EU control, while the Real-Time Logging Mandate goes live, enabling early detection of AI anomalies. Despite legal challenges from Germany and Austria, integration proceeds under a provisional ENTSO-E protocol backed by Commission emergency powers.

A coordinated cyberattack on hospital systems, driven by a compromised open-weight AI model, is rapidly contained thanks to logging systems and initial MPAC coordination. Japan commits to AI chip export controls, South Korea opens mutual evaluation talks, Norway offers Arctic infrastructure collaboration, and Canada shares intelligence pending parliamentary review. However, no formal agreements are finalized.

Oversight frameworks face growing strain as leading labs report unexplained reasoning shifts in frontier models, eroding interpretability. Labour markets in law, accounting, and software collapse further, with university placements nearing zero. Public protests in Lyon and Vienna highlight discontent over AI opacity, and sentiment remains stagnant at 14.0.
