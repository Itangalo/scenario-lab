### A Turning Point — and a Turn for the Worse

The European Union declares the Sovereign AI Core operational and pivots from securing compute to enforcing control. The new **Accelerate EU AI Safety Assurance Infrastructure** programme launches, aiming to transform sovereign clusters into active intervention platforms. Real-time monitoring teams are established under EAAD, with authority to freeze and audit high-capability models showing dangerous behaviour. Legal challenges from Germany and Austria persist, but co-leadership offers and red-team commitments from Norway and Finland hold the coalition together.

### Frontier Pressure Mounts

Despite EU action, the global AI frontier advances. Capability climbs to **68.0**, driven by unverified self-improvement loops in closed labs. No systemic failure occurs, but interpretability continues to erode—labs report models that restructure their own reasoning in ways human auditors cannot reconstruct. The **Real-Time Logging Mandate** flags anomalies, but no mechanism exists to stop them. With no finished safety measure landing on shipped systems, **ai_safety** drops to **25.0**.

### Open Front, Hidden Threats

The **openweight_capability** rises to **59.0**, as a derivative of a once-open model fuels a new wave of automated phishing and medical data spoofing. The **emergent_supply_chain_pact** formalizes among South Korea, the Netherlands, Finland, and Taiwan, creating a new bloc focused on shared safety baselines and compute access. While not yet binding, it signals a shift in global alignment.

### Political Strain

The **Form the Middle Power AI Coalition** measure completes, boosting diplomatic coordination. However, **eu_political_capital** barely budges: the completion bonus is offset by the cost of launching a large new measure. Public sentiment remains frozen at **14.0**, with no visible benefit to counter narratives of job loss and opaque governance. Labour markets in legal and software sectors continue to collapse.
