### The Foothold

The EU formally launches the **Sovereign Frontier Compute Initiative**, a bold bid to reclaim agency through infrastructure. Groundbreaking begins at Jülich and CINECA, repurposing exascale systems for AI training under EU legal control. A draft Regulation on AI Compute Sovereignty is tabled, invoking Article 114 TFEU to frame sovereign compute as critical infrastructure. Bilateral talks with Taiwan and Japan advance, but US-aligned semiconductor firms quietly resist supply diversions.

Despite the ambition, political capital remains frozen at 0.0. The portfolio charge holds: −3 for the Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory, −3 for the new large measure, and −1 for prioritisation — a total of −7. No proposal bonus applies; while recent shocks were severe, this measure answers systemic collapse rather than a single event. Capital cannot fall below zero, nor rise without headroom to spend.

Sovereignty dips to 13.0: −1 from `ai_capability` rising ≥2, and no offset from early-stage buildout. Though construction starts, rule 5 grants no gain until completion. The denial of frontier access lingers as structural damage, not acute event.

`ai_capability` climbs to 92.5 (+3), driven by recursive self-improvement now operating beyond full human interpretability. The **verification_widens** shift accelerates automation in law and engineering, expanding the frontier’s economic reach. The **us_china_agreement** introduces modest coordination on biological design tools, slightly tempering open proliferation — but excludes the EU entirely.

`openweight_capability` rises modestly to 74.5 (+3), reflecting incremental diffusion of existing tooling and compressed checkpoints. It remains strictly below `ai_capability` at all times, consistent with the frontier constraint. The narrative of widening access continues, but no unannounced breakthrough enables sudden leaps.

`ai_safety` increases only slightly to 9.0 (+1), as limited interpretability improvements circulate among open labs. The prior turn's signals of progress do not resolve into a breakthrough this turn; the **safety_breakthrough** gate remains unopened, and no unearned advancement is narrated. The EU has not yet adopted the new methods, and deployment remains patchy.

Resilience holds at 49.0; the completed Counter-Deception Corps yields no immediate boost, as operational delays persist. Public sentiment remains stagnant at 20.0 — too low for mobilisation, too high for revolt.

In city halls from Barcelona to Helsinki, quiet procurement talks deepen among mayors bypassing Brussels. The signal grows: sovereignty is draining downward.
