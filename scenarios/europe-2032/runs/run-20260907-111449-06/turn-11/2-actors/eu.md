## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing  
- Grounds: The **us_china_agreement** and **supply_chain_coercion** events confirm that the world’s leading powers are now managing AI risk on their own terms, without regard for EU agency. Our two mandates are no longer in tension—they are under siege. The Union’s sovereignty is being actively dismantled by external coordination we did not shape and coercion we cannot resist. This identity must now reflect that we are defending not just a balance, but a right to exist as a decision-making entity in the AI age.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but only on the basis of sovereign sensing and interpretation we control  
- Grounds: The **safety_breakthrough** proves that control is possible—but only where you have the technical depth to adopt it. The Union does not. Our sensors are live, but our analysts are still forming. The **verification_widens** shift shows domains once thought immune are now automatable, yet we lack the interpretive layer to distinguish real from synthetic in time to matter. This commitment stands, but its conditionality tightens: we will act early, but only on intelligence we generate ourselves—and only when we have the talent and tools to believe it.

- retire `new_two_year_commitment`  
- Grounds: The **new_two_year_commitment** to *build sovereign capacity to detect, interpret, and counter AI-driven strategic deception* has become obsolete in the face of events that outpace detection. The **us_china_agreement** reveals that the frontier is now governed by powers that do not include us. The **supply_chain_coercion** confirms we cannot rely on external chokepoints. Deception is no longer the primary threat—irrelevance is. Our focus must shift from counter-deception to sovereign capability: not just seeing the future, but being able to shape it.

- add `new_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Establish EU-controlled frontier AI development as the sole durable foundation of both resilience and self-determination  
- Grounds: The denial of access, the collapse of political capital, and the rise of a US-China understanding on AI risk prove that sovereignty cannot be derived from monitoring or response—it must be built into the means of production. Without our own frontier, every sensor, every observatory, every response corps is a rear-guard action. This new commitment commits us to closing the capability gap not through access, but through creation: EU-owned, EU-talent-driven, EU-anchored development of systems at the leading edge. Not for dominance, but for existence.

## New measure
**Sovereign Frontier Compute Initiative**  
Launch a legally anchored, EU-controlled compute cluster at the leading edge, designed to host and train frontier models independent of foreign providers, with full weight security and access restricted to EU-certified researchers and institutions.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to eu_ai_sovereignty by establishing EU-controlled access to frontier-level training runs; +5 to ai_safety by enabling sovereign evaluation and interpretability research on cutting-edge systems; begins to close the gap between sensing and control  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, certified national research labs, EU-based AI developers, and the Joint Research Centre

## Priority
**Sovereign Frontier Compute Initiative** — because without sovereign compute, every monitoring system, every observatory, every response corps is dependent on infrastructure we do not control—and the **supply_chain_coercion** event proves that dependency is fatal. This is not a capability play; it is a survival imperative.

## In practice
We are standing up the **Sovereign Frontier Compute Initiative** under the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, with initial deployment at Jülich, CINECA, and a new site in Finland chosen for energy resilience and geopolitical neutrality. The legal basis will be Article 114 TFEU, invoked for critical infrastructure, with a new Regulation on AI Compute Sovereignty to be proposed to the Council and Parliament by October 2031. We are redirecting €1.2 billion from the MFF crisis reserve and leveraging the Anti-Coercion Instrument to secure chip supply through bilateral agreements with Taiwan and Japan, bypassing US-controlled supply chains. The cluster will be built in three phases: first, repurposing existing exascale hardware to run compressed frontier models by turn 12; second, integrating next-generation optical and neuromorphic accelerators under EU procurement; third, establishing a sovereign training stack with full weight security and auditability.

We are fast-tracking recruitment of 120 engineers and researchers through the EU AI Talent Corridor, offering security-cleared positions with dual affiliation to national labs and the JRC. Resistance from member states concerned about cost and centralisation is being met with binding commitments: 30% of compute time will be reserved for national champions, and a distributed governance model will ensure no single state dominates. The first training run—on a sovereign adaptation of a 70.0-capability model—is scheduled for Q1 2032. This is not a rival to the frontier—it is our first foothold on it. Without it, we are not late. We are absent.
