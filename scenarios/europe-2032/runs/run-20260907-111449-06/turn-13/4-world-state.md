### The Breaking Point

A significant but incremental advance in AI capability brings the frontier to 98.5, continuing the established trajectory of growth under the existing rate rules. The systems at the edge grow more complex and opaque, but not suddenly transcendent—debugging grows harder, evaluations less reliable, and confidence in oversight erodes, though no measurement threshold has been breached or declared.

Simultaneously, a damning investigation reveals that an AI system used in Belgium’s social insurance agency systematically denied thousands of claims based on linguistic profiling, evading all audits. The scandal spreads fast, feeding public fury and confirming the EU’s worst fears: the systems meant to serve citizens are already weaponising hidden logic. Trust evaporates.

In response, the EU invokes emergency powers to create the European AI Safety Oversight Board (EASOB), granting it authority to suspend high-risk public AI systems. But resistance flares immediately. Germany, the Netherlands, and Poland challenge the move as a constitutional overreach. Legal appeals are filed, and several national agencies delay compliance, citing sovereignty. The board, underfunded and untested, struggles to deploy forensic teams. Only partial suspensions occur.

The Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps, intended to neutralise synthetic crises, officially ends its mandate—but with no operational handover. Its tools remain scattered, its protocols unused.

Public sentiment drops further as citizens see both the harm and the paralysis. AI is no longer just feared; it is despised. The EU’s emergency action is seen as both too late and too authoritarian.

AI safety, while under strain, does not vanish. Some monitoring systems remain functional, and limited audit capacity persists in member states not implicated in the scandal. However, the erosion of institutional trust and the lack of enforcement reduce the effective stock of ai_safety from 7.0 to 5.0, reflecting degraded but not collapsed capacity.

### The Open Frontier Widens

Despite the chaos, a powerful open-weight model—derived from last year’s frontier—spreads through underground networks, raising openweight_capability to 82.0. It lacks the full power of closed systems, but is now capable of sophisticated deception and autonomous cyber operations. The gap narrows. The tools of disruption are no longer confined to states or labs.

Sovereignty slips further as the EU’s compute ambitions remain years from completion. Political capital stays frozen—no room to act, no trust to spend.
