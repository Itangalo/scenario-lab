## New measure
**Emergency Resilience Mobilisation Directive**  
A binding, time-limited mandate to harden all EU member states’ critical public services against AI-driven cyber attack, with immediate funding, shared threat models, and enforceable benchmarks.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to resilience by enforcing baseline cyber hardening, incident response protocols, and cross-border mutual aid in healthcare, energy, and public administration within 18 months; begins to close the gap between EU-wide policy and local implementation  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national governments, critical infrastructure operators, and municipal service providers

## In practice
We are invoking Article 4(2) of the Treaty on European Union and the Crisis Protocol of the Digital Operational Resilience Act to issue the **Emergency Resilience Mobilisation Directive** as a binding instrument, effective immediately. We are redirecting €800 million from uncommitted MFF allocations and unlocking emergency disbursement mechanisms under the Crisis Regulation to fund rapid upgrades to public-sector cyber defences. The European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) will issue baseline hardening standards within 30 days — multi-factor authentication, zero-trust architecture, and AI-driven anomaly detection — with compliance required within 12 months. Non-compliant member states will face automatic withholding of cohesion funds and exclusion from EU-wide digital infrastructure projects.

We are deploying a rapid assessment team from the Joint Research Centre to map vulnerabilities in real time, starting with healthcare in Spain, energy grids in Poland, and municipal services in Finland — the first targets of the recent attack. The directive includes a mutual aid clause: any state hit by a major incident can trigger automatic support from other member states’ cyber corps, modelled on the EU Civil Protection Mechanism. Resistance from smaller states citing cost and sovereignty is being met with binding offers: 70% co-financing, pre-approved procurement templates, and technical assistance from the newly reconstituted EU Cyber Rapid Response Pool. This is not optional. The frontier has already moved. We are not building the future — we are defending the present. And if we cannot defend it, we will not survive to build anything else.
