### Holding the line by hand
The autumn brought no new Brussels law, only triage. When registries froze and appointment systems went dark across a dozen cities, and a tainted software update forced administrators to hunt its spread file by file, municipal staff worked from printed backups while national response teams sorted cases by hand. Power and core networks flickered and degraded but did not fail, thanks to telemetry and patching left from the earlier hardening effort. Clinics ran paper queues for days. Officials admitted privately the attack tooling looked machine-written; who sent it remained unclear at Christmas.

Almost at the same time, a freely downloadable model close to the frontier spread to hundreds of thousands of machines in days. Students celebrated. Security chiefs said the same thing in every capital: what it could do was now unrecallable.

Dependence bit harder. Washington tightened chip and model licences again, keeping close buyers on quotas while others were cut off. The Hague, under heavy American pressure, extended controls on lithography machines and servicing to older tools as well. Then, with a few days' notice, European hospitals, ministries and firms found the leading American model withdrawn or throttled for their users, with no reason and no appeal. Teams that had built workflows on it scrambled to licensed fallbacks and half-ready European substitutes, pausing migrations where referrals broke.

Police guarded the halted data-centre and interconnector sites hit by coordinated sabotage in the summer. Labour ministers stretched existing social funds to young office workers whose entry jobs had vanished.

One bright spot was claimed loudly: in a few regions where European-run backups and assisted triage kept waiting lists moving, commissioners presented continuity itself as a European decision that worked. It barely moved a hostile public mood.

### A Commission that would not build
With coffers empty and trust at rock bottom, the Commission proposed nothing, prioritised nothing, and contested neither the Dutch curbs nor the Washington-Beijing understanding on weights and bio tools. Survival, not sovereignty, was the doctrine.
