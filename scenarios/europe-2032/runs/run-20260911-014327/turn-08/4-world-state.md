### The winter everything leaked at once
The first half of 2030 did not produce a single crisis so much as a pile-up Brussels had no tools left to separate.

A large automated attack swept public services in January — in some cities encrypted registries and appointment systems, in others a poisoned software component whose spread took weeks to map. Municipal IT staff restored from backups while national response teams triaged by hand. Grid operators, running the telemetry and automated patching left from the earlier hardening programme, kept power and core networks degrading rather than collapsing, but clinics, town halls and back-offices queued paper forms for days. Investigators said privately the tooling was model-written; attribution would take months.

As recovery dragged, a new openly downloadable model family appeared, within months of the closed frontier and fetched hundreds of thousands of times in its first week. University labs celebrated; security officials noted dryly that whatever it could do was now permanently beyond recall.

That frontier itself was being re-divided. Washington tightened chip and model controls again, with allied buyers kept on volume licences while others were cut off. Almost simultaneously, under American pressure, The Hague was forced to extend curbs on lithography exports and servicing — beyond leading-edge tools to the older machines used for ordinary chips. For the Commission it was a bitter demonstration: Europe's one real chokepoint used, and not by Europe.

Washington and Beijing did reach a limited risk pact on weights security and certain biological design tools, with thin verification. Brussels was informed afterwards, not consulted.

Then construction sites were hit. Coordinated physical sabotage against data-centre builds and grid interconnects in two member states, claimed by anti-technology groups, forced temporary halts. Police guarded halted sites; ministers kept paused migrations to European substitutes where referrals still broke, foreign systems on standby.

With no money and no mandate, the Commission opened nothing new. It lived off standing incident reporting, black-box probes and existing social funds. Dependence, rationing and empty entry-level desks coloured every shortfall.
