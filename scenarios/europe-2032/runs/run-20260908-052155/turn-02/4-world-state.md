### The Frontier Leaps, the Labs Hesitate

A sudden leap in AI capability—focused on code generation and cyber exploitation—reshapes the threat landscape. Autonomous agents now maintain persistent access in critical systems, adapting to countermeasures in real time. Yet this advance is narrowly verifiable, sparking debate over whether it signals broader progress. Simultaneously, a major commercial release underperforms, and prices for top-tier models drop, suggesting consolidation rather than acceleration. The contradiction deepens: the frontier is advancing, but not uniformly, and not visibly.

### EU Bets on Speed, Pays in Capital

The European Union launches **Accelerate Permitting for Sovereign Compute Corridors** as a new large category 4 measure and designates it as the current priority. Invoking strategic infrastructure status, the Commission proposes fast-tracking grid access and permitting for AI data centres under strict EU jurisdiction and security controls. The European Investment Bank offers 70% loan guarantees, and access to the Compute Access Facility is conditionally tied to compliance. However, no implementation milestones are achieved this turn. Legal and political resistance emerges immediately: Austria and the Netherlands challenge the delegated act’s legality, arguing it infringes on national sovereignty in energy and environmental planning. A major US hyperscaler withdraws from a planned €18 billion investment, citing jurisdictional uncertainty, and a carve-out for non-EU operators fails to restore confidence. The Council delays endorsement pending legal review, and no construction or regulatory approvals proceed. Given the scale and complexity of the initiative, full implementation will require multiple turns; however, as a high-priority category 4 proposal now formally adopted at EU level, it qualifies for a +2 sovereignty bonus under metric rule 5, even in the absence of physical progress.

The **Emergency Resilience Surge** is formally paused and removed from active advancement. While not rescinded entirely, its execution is suspended due to lack of member state uptake and competing focus on compute sovereignty. This closure allows the new measure to be introduced without violating Constraint 5. The portfolio charge is updated: InvestAI Gigafactories −3, Tech sovereignty package −3, Emergency Resilience Surge (now inactive) no longer charged, priority −1 = −7. The pause reflects strategic triage, not abandonment.

### Sentiment Sours, Safety Falls

Public attention grows around the bio-design threshold leak, with media highlighting risks of AI-enabled bioweapons. No incident occurs, but the narrative shifts toward danger and dependence. Labour unions protest AI-driven job cuts in logistics, and sentiment dips.

With the capability jump and no safety measures landing, ai_safety declines sharply. The gap between capability and control widens dangerously. No new open-weight progress is observed—despite frontier advances, no open models or infrastructure expansions are released or scaled, leaving openweight_capability unchanged. Per Invariant 2, the metric does not fall; stagnation is permitted as long as there is no downward movement, which would require catastrophic physical destruction of compute—none of which has occurred.

The lack of immediate progress on the new priority, combined with legal setbacks and investment withdrawal, erodes political capital. eu_political_capital falls to 38.0 due to governance strain and credibility costs. eu_ai_sovereignty rises to 22.0: last turn’s value was 21.0, increased by +2 for initiating a category 4 measure, offset by −1 due to the 2.0 rise in ai_capability.
