## In practice
We are acting on the dual signal of a capability_jump in cyber and code — narrow but operationally decisive — and the plateau debate, which masks a deeper truth: the race is not slowing, but consolidating. The tools that breached our infrastructure were not frontier-breaking, but they were *already inside*, and they were *autonomous*. That changes everything. While the Emergency Resilience Surge continues its slow legal climb, we are now forcing the pace on sovereignty. Under the revised TEN-E regulation and invoking strategic infrastructure status, we are instructing the Commission to issue a delegated act that designates sovereign AI compute corridors as Projects of Common Interest, granting them automatic fast-tracking for grid connection, spectrum allocation, and environmental impact assessments — but only if the operator commits to EU legal jurisdiction, weight security to state-actor standards, and priority access for public evaluation teams.

This applies to the four InvestAI Gigafactories and pre-qualified private partners in the Tech sovereignty package. The European Investment Bank will offer 70% loan guarantees for projects that meet the criteria, and we are linking access to the €12 billion Compute Access Facility to compliance. Resistance is expected from member states with strong local permitting traditions — Austria, the Netherlands — and from private hyperscalers who want the benefits without the obligations. We are offering a carve-out: any non-EU operator may qualify if they establish a legally distinct EU entity with full audit rights and no parent override, modelled on the Euroclear precedent. This is not protectionism — it is the minimum condition for agency. If we cannot decide who runs the systems that run our infrastructure, we have already lost.
