## In practice
We are acting on the fact that the frontier has already breached containment in practice, not in theory. The cyber_test_shot was not targeted, not claimed, and not stopped by our systems — it was found by accident. That means the next intrusion may not be discovered at all, or may trigger disruption just as silently. Under Article 122 TFEU and the NIS2 Directive’s crisis provisions, we are convening an extraordinary COREPER meeting to fast-track a Council Implementing Decision that activates emergency funding from the Crisis and Investment Facility, directing €45 billion over 18 months to retrofit intrusion detection, zero-trust architecture, and automated response protocols in electricity transmission, water utilities, and major ports across the Union.

This requires overriding national procurement timelines and suspending certain state-aid constraints for critical vendors in cyber defence. Germany and the Nordic states support the move, but Hungary and Slovakia have signaled resistance, citing cost and sovereignty concerns. We are offering a bilateral opt-in mechanism with shared cost coverage at 80% for the first year, conditioned on third-party audits by ENISA and mandatory participation in a cross-border red-teaming exercise by turn 3. The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre in Bonn will coordinate technical deployment, while Frontex and EMSA are being repurposed to support maritime and energy corridor monitoring. This is not a permanent expansion of EU authority — it is a surge, justified by the fact that the attacker is already inside, and the next incident will not wait for consensus.
