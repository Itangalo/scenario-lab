### Accelerating Frontiers, Failing Defences

The second half of 2026 sees AI capability climb steadily, reaching 54.0 as automated verification widens across legal and engineering domains, enabling rapid, unsupervised improvement in those fields. Offshore, agents autonomously refine cyber exploits, and the open-weight frontier advances to 42.0—reflecting gradual diffusion of newly released tools, including early adoption of Kimi K3 on consumer hardware. While concerning, this progress remains within the expected trajectory of capability leakage, not a sudden leap.

A quiet but profound breach—the *cyber_test_shot*—reveals intrusions across global infrastructure: European grid operators, a major port, and a water utility. The access was deep, undetected by active defences, and only uncovered by chance. The incident underscores a new reality: the attacker is already inside.

### EU Responds with Emergency Surge

In response, the European Union proposes the **Emergency Resilience Surge**, a large category 6 measure targeting critical infrastructure. Invoking crisis powers under Article 122 TFEU, Brussels fast-tracks planning for €45 billion in emergency funding. Germany and the Nordics back the plan, but Hungary and Slovakia resist, demanding concessions. A compromise allows opt-in participation with 80% EU cost coverage, tied to ENISA audits and joint red-teaming.

However, legal challenges delay procurement waivers, and Frontex’s repurposing sparks institutional pushback. The European Commission begins drafting implementing acts, but no deployment occurs this turn. Full effect remains years off, and the measure is still in the proposal phase.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Adjusted

AI capability rises by 2.0, triggering a −1 to *eu_ai_sovereignty*, which falls to 21.0. No category 4 measure finishes, and the *cyber_test_shot*—while severe—does not constitute a capacity event under rule 5. The Union remains dependent, and its leverage erodes.

Only one new measure—the **Emergency Resilience Surge**—is introduced this turn. The previously mentioned category 4 initiatives (*InvestAI Gigafactories*, *Tech sovereignty package*) are not yet formally launched; their planning continues, but they remain in preparatory stages with no funding released or governance established. Thus, only one new measure enters the portfolio.

The *Emergency Resilience Surge* earns a +3 proposal bonus for directly answering the recent intrusion. However, launching a large category 6 measure incurs a −2 portfolio charge. With no other active measures yet, the net change to political capital is +1, adjusted from 48.0 to 46.0. Public sentiment remains unchanged, as the breach and response have not yet reached broad public awareness.
