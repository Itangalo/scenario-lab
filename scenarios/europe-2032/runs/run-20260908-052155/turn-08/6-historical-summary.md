AI capability reaches 65.0 as self-improving agent frameworks enable recursive refinement in cyber and bio domains, outpacing safety and control efforts. Interpretability remains unattainable, and the control gap widens. The European AI Evaluation Agency (EAA) releases a redacted report linking a high-risk genome model to a non-EU frontier lab, but its limited access and delayed disclosures further damage its credibility. In response, the EU pushes centralized authority through a mandatory ENISA-developed kill switch directive for AI systems in essential services, invoking Article 114 TFEU to override national vetoes. Backed by an €800m Digital Europe allocation, the measure mandates cryptographic override capabilities activatable by the EAA without host-state consent, targeting implementation by turn 10. Strong resistance emerges, with France denouncing it as a “constitutional bypass,” Germany warning of legal fragmentation, and Nordic states challenging its proportionality, prompting a joint legal review by six member states and delaying rollout.

The stalled Emergency Empowerment proposal continues to drain political capital, now compounded by the contentious kill switch directive. Public trust erodes further as protests against AI infrastructure spread, targeting data centres in France and Sweden, while sabotage incidents rise. Resilience declines as adaptive threats outpace defences, and sovereignty weakens amid persistent lack of sovereign compute. Political capital drops sharply due to high-cost initiatives, institutional deadlock, and growing intra-EU friction. Amid fragmentation, France and Germany advance bilateral AI integration in military cyber defence, while the Global AI Safety Forum meets without major power leadership, underscoring its limited reach. EU coordination falters, deepening systemic instability.
