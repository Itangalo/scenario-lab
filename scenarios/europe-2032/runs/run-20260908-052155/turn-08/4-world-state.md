### A Hardening Divide

AI capability climbs to 65.0 as self-improving agent frameworks achieve recursive refinement in cyber and bio domains, accelerating frontier development. Despite growing risks, no meaningful safety improvements land: interpretability remains out of reach, and control systems lag. The European AI Evaluation Agency (EAA) publishes a redacted analysis linking a high-risk genome model to a non-EU frontier lab, intensifying pressure for enforceable safeguards — but the report also reveals gaps in its own access, further undermining credibility.

The EU doubles down on institutional authority, launching a new large category 1 measure: mandatory ENISA-developed kill switches in all AI systems across essential services. Framed as a technical necessity, the directive invokes Article 114 TFEU to bypass national vetoes, mandating cryptographic override modules activatable by the EAA without host-state consent. A €800m Digital Europe fund supports retrofits, targeting completion by turn 10. Yet resistance hardens — France condemns the move as a “constitutional bypass,” Germany warns of legal fragmentation, and Nordic states question proportionality. The proposal triggers a joint legal review by six member states, delaying implementation.

Meanwhile, the stalled Emergency Empowerment measure remains in flight, its political cost ongoing. With no progress, the EU’s credibility erodes further. Public sentiment dips as protests against AI infrastructure grow, now targeting data centres in France and Sweden. Sabotage incidents increase, and trust in both AI and EU coordination wanes.

Resilience declines slightly as threats evolve faster than defences. Sovereignty falls due to rising capability and no domestic compute gains. Political capital drops sharply under the weight of multiple high-cost measures, the failure to break deadlock, and rising internal friction.

### Fragmentation in Motion

The emergent EU cyber defence initiative gains quiet momentum, with France and Germany integrating AI-driven threat detection into joint military exercises. The Global AI Safety Forum meets again, but without EU or U.S. leadership, its influence remains limited.
