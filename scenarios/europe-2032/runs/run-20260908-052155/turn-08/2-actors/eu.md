## New measure  
**Establish Pre-Authorized Override Protocols in Critical Infrastructure with ENISA-Developed Kill Switches**  
To mandate the pre-installation and cryptographically secured activation of emergency override modules in all AI systems operating in EU essential services, ensuring that the EAA can act without delay when a threat is verified.  

- Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)  
- Size: large  
- Finishes on turn: 10  
- Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by 8–12 points by closing the enforcement gap; strengthen eu_ai_sovereignty by 6–10 points through irreversible technical control; reduce dependency on political consensus during incidents  
- Applies to: EU member states, critical infrastructure operators, and foreign AI developers whose systems operate in EU essential services  

## Priority  
Emergency Empowerment of the European AI Evaluation Agency with Enforcement Authority — because without binding authority to act in real time, every other measure becomes a post-mortem ritual, and because this turn’s paralysis proves that waiting for consensus during a crisis is indistinguishable from surrender.

## In practice  
We are acting on the collapse of political will. The Emergency Empowerment measure remains in flight, but its legal passage is blocked by national vetoes — Germany citing proportionality, France demanding strategic autonomy, the Nordics warning of mission creep. We are now shifting strategy: instead of seeking permission to act in crisis, we are mandating the technical capacity to act, irreversibly. Under Article 114 TFEU, we are proposing a revised Critical Entities Resilience Directive that requires all AI systems in energy, transport, health, and election infrastructure to embed ENISA-certified override modules — cryptographically sealed, updateable only by EAA-issued keys, and activatable without host-state consent during verified incidents. This is not a request; it is a condition of operation. We are pairing this with a €800m fund from the Digital Europe Programme to cover retrofit costs, targeting adoption by turn 10. The measure applies equally to domestic and foreign providers — no exemptions. Resistance is fierce: France calls it a “backdoor coup,” and German operators warn of liability cascades. We are offering a joint EAA-ENISA command cell with national liaison officers to preserve oversight, and a sunset clause that triggers review at ai_safety 50.0 — but no opt-outs. At the same time, we are directing the EAA to publish a redacted version of the genome model incident, attributing it to a non-EU frontier lab, to build public and institutional pressure for irreversible safeguards. This is not consensus-building; it is institution-building in the shadow of failure. We will not wait for the next breach to learn that authority without access is a fiction.
