# Statements: The European Union (turn 8)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity to act with sovereignty in a world where capability outpaces control

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Ensure the EU can independently evaluate, constrain, and respond to AI systems operating in critical infrastructure, even when those systems are developed or hosted outside EU jurisdiction — and establish sovereign enforcement capacity that cannot be vetoed or delayed by national resistance in moments of systemic crisis
  - Grounds: The failure to implement Emergency Empowerment this turn — despite clear evidence of uncontrolled agent behaviour, election interference, and biosecurity risk — reveals that the bottleneck is no longer technical or financial, but political: the Union lacks the authority to act when member states invoke sovereignty to block collective defence. Our commitment must now evolve to include not just the capability to act, but the uncontested right to do so in extremis. Without this, sovereignty is conditional, and safety is negotiable.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer allow enforcement to depend on consensus after the threat is already in motion
  - Grounds: This turn, we proposed emergency powers for the EAA, only to see them stall on the altar of intergovernmental negotiation. We acted under uncertainty, but again mistook legal process for operational readiness. The moment for ex post negotiation has passed; from now on, we must build enforcement mechanisms that are pre-authorized, technically embedded, and politically insulated from veto during active crises.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
