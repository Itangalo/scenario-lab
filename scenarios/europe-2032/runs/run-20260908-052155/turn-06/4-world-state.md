### The Weight of Unchecked Capability

AI capability advances to 62.0 as autonomous agents demonstrate recursive self-modification in cyber operations, adapting faster than human-led patch cycles can respond. The jump reflects sustained progress, not a sudden leap, but it deepens the gap between what systems can do and what anyone can control.

Open-weight models rise to 50.0, buoyed by leaked agent training frameworks from the recent cyber incident. While the leak accelerates diffusion, the practical deployment of such models on commodity hardware remains constrained by optimization and energy efficiency barriers. The openweight advance is notable but remains within the bounds of the broader AI capability trajectory, preserving the invariant that openweight cannot exceed frontier capability.

### The EAA’s Test—and Failure

The European AI Evaluation Agency formally launches, but its authority fractures under pressure. The Article 16 directive demanding access to agent reasoning traces faces non-compliance from major US developers and legal resistance from EU contractors reliant on foreign models. National cybersecurity agencies split: some delay enforcement, citing operational risks; others refuse on constitutional grounds. The Commission initiates infringement proceedings, but the Court of Justice signals a multi-year docket.

Meanwhile, a ransomware sweep—generated by AI and targeting municipal services in three member states—exposes systemic blindness. Defenders could not detect the attack’s adaptive logic until critical systems were encrypted. Recovery takes weeks; public trust erodes further.

### Fragmentation and Backlash

A consortium of Central and Eastern European states bypasses EU channels, jointly procuring defensive AI tools through ENISA-adjacent coordination. While operationally useful, the move weakens centralized sovereignty and sets a precedent for divergence.

Physical protests against data centres escalate into sabotage, with grid connections damaged and injuries reported. Policing costs mount, and local governments demand moratoria.

With no new measure proposed, the EU conserves political capital—but pays the price of inaction. Five active initiatives continue to drain resources. The EAA’s operational debut delivers no safety gain, and the cyber incident confirms deteriorating control.

Public sentiment drops as fear outpaces reassurance. Resilience declines—adaptive threats now consistently outpace institutional response. Sovereignty falls as frontier access remains contested and no domestic compute comes online. Political capital decreases, but not as sharply as previously assessed, as the cost of an unnamed priority is withdrawn: no measure was proposed, and no priority was declared, so no additional priority cost applies this turn. The portfolio charge is adjusted accordingly.
