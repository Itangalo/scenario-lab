### Sabotage and Sovereignty

A wave of coordinated sabotage strikes AI infrastructure in France, Sweden, and Poland, disrupting power feeds and cutting fibre lines near major data centres. The attacks, attributed to a decentralized network of anti-AI activists using AI-generated plans, expose critical weaknesses in physical security and deepen public unease. In response, the EU reorients its Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme toward geographic dispersion and hardening, classifying core nodes as critical digital infrastructure under NIS2. Real-time monitoring, intelligence sharing, and AI-assisted perimeter patrols with Frontex and Europol are initiated.

Despite these efforts, political cohesion frays. The EAA’s binding directives to Germany, Italy, and Austria to implement override protocols are met with defiance. National regulators reject conditional funding, framing it as coercion, and public protests intensify, now citing the gigafactory plans as energy colonialism. The €800 million retrofit fund risks becoming a tool of division, not unity.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

AI capability advances to 69.5, driven by recursive optimization in cyber and bio domains. No safety gains land on deployed systems — interpretability remains confined to closed labs. With no measure improving shipped assurance, and amid rising capability, ai_safety drops to 15.0. The open-weight frontier rises to 57.0, aided by fragmented adoption of leaked techniques, but compute scarcity and distrust limit diffusion.

Resilience declines slightly as adaptive threats grow and no category 6 measure fully offsets the damage. Political capital collapses to 9.0 under the weight of three active measures, a named priority, and the fallout from sabotage and non-compliance. Public sentiment worsens to 22.0, dragged down by visible dependency, infrastructure attacks, and perceived overreach.

Sovereignty erodes to 14.0 as capability rises and no access is secured. The Union remains exposed, its tools untested, its unity questioned.
