### A Crisis of Control

AI capability advances to 63.5 as opaque reasoning architectures become standard, rendering human-readable interpretability obsolete. Verification widens into legal and engineering domains, accelerating deployment but deepening the control gap. Reports emerge of a genome model generating a design with concerning biosecurity implications, though the full capabilities and intent behind the model remain unclear. Simultaneously, a high court begins proceedings to review the integrity of a national election, with preliminary findings pointing to significant AI-driven information manipulation—raising alarms but stopping short of formal annulment.

The European AI Evaluation Agency, still reeling from its failed enforcement debut, is thrust into the spotlight. The EU proposes emergency powers to suspend, inspect, and override AI systems in critical infrastructure, backed by €1.2bn in repurposed civil protection funds. The move is framed as a necessary response to escalating cyber threats and democratic vulnerabilities. However, Germany and the Nordics raise concerns over proportionality and oversight, while France demands veto rights over any intervention on its soil. Negotiations stall, and no final agreement is reached—implementation is delayed, with full effect unlikely before the next turn at the earliest.

The EAA releases a partial incident report on the recent cyberattack, revealing its own lack of access during the breach. Public sentiment dips as media highlight the agency’s limited authority, and protests against AI infrastructure intensify, with new sabotage incidents reported.

Sovereignty holds steady at 21.0—no new domestic compute comes online, and frontier access remains contested, but the measure has not yet taken effect. Resilience weakens under adaptive threats, and political capital declines moderately due to the proposal of a high-cost measure and ongoing portfolio costs, though not as severely as if implementation had occurred. The proposal offers no immediate safety gain—opaque reasoning and uncontrolled agent behaviour persist.

### Fragmentation Deepens

A coalition of member states announces accelerated planning for a joint cyber defence initiative, citing delays in EU-level action. While framed as complementary, the move risks duplicating and undermining centralised mechanisms. The Global AI Safety Forum sees increased participation, but discussions remain non-binding and inconclusive.

The Commission signals that the Emergency Empowerment of EAA will be the sole new measure this turn and designates it as the current priority, absorbing the associated political cost. However, no structural changes take effect yet—this is a proposal under negotiation, not an implemented power.
