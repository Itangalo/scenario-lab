## In practice
We are acting on two simultaneous realisations: first, that we cannot secure what we do not own, and second, that we cannot trust what we do not understand. The leap to 56.0 in ai_capability was not a general explosion but a targeted breach—autonomous agents operating inside critical infrastructure, adapting in real time. That means sovereignty is not about catching up in raw performance; it is about controlling the substrate on which such systems run. We are doubling down on **Accelerate Permitting for Sovereign Compute Corridors**, instructing the Commission to defend the delegated act before the Court of Justice as a matter of Union competence under Article 114 TFEU, arguing that fragmented national permitting regimes now threaten the internal market’s integrity when AI-driven cyber dependencies cross borders. We are offering Austria and the Netherlands a side letter: full transparency on environmental impact modelling and joint oversight of grid load distribution, in exchange for dropping their challenge. For the US hyperscaler that withdrew, we make no concessions—but we signal clearly: the carve-out remains open, but only for entities willing to be bound, not just hosted.

At the same time, the eval_anomaly_reports event forces our hand. If capabilities are emerging unpredictably—even if only in one lab, even if called a measurement artefact—we must assume they could land anywhere, anytime. The current level of ai_safety at 25.0 is indefensible: voluntary testing, opaque weights, no real access. So we are launching the **European AI Evaluation Agency**, modelled on EASA and EURATOM, with headquarters proposed in Hamburg or Prague, and initial staffing drawn from national cybersecurity agencies, ENISA, and vetted academic researchers. It will have legal power to demand evaluation access before any model above 50.0 is deployed in the EU, using red-teaming, sandboxed agent monitoring, and weight inspections. We will fund it with €4.2 billion over three years from the Horizon Europe contingency line and tie participation in the emergent_eu_compute_pact to full cooperation. This is not a barrier to innovation—it is the condition for trusted scale. Without it, every advance becomes a threat by default.
