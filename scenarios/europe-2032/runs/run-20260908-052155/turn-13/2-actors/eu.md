## Priority  
Launch Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme: Gigafactories for AI — because without physically and legally anchored compute, every enforcement authority we claim is a fiction, and because the cutoff has proven that access is conditional, but infrastructure we build and harden ourselves is not.

## In practice  
The Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme has reached its planned finish line. The gigafactories are now live: three core nodes in Finland, Poland, and Portugal are operational under full Union legal control, hardened under NIS2, and running early workloads for public safety evaluation and crisis simulation. Mobile fallback units have been tested in border regions, and dual power and encrypted telemetry are standard. This is not yet scale, but it is sovereignty with teeth — the first time in years the EU can run capable models without asking permission.

But political capital is at 5.0, barely enough to sustain routine operations, let alone launch new ones. Germany, Italy, and Austria remain in open resistance: Berlin refuses observer appointments, Rome has suspended audit cooperation, and Vienna threatens litigation over funding conditionality. We are not escalating. Instead, we are consolidating. The Commission is preparing a communication to the Council arguing that the gigafactories’ completion marks a threshold: from now on, any member state that rejects EAA verification must fund its own sovereign compute node, under its own liability, outside the Union’s security perimeter. This is not punishment — it is a choice. Either you are in the system, or you are outside it, but you cannot block it and expect to share its protection.

The leaked anomaly detection research from the frontier lab is now being tested on early open-weight models at the EU’s new safety research cluster in Leuven. We are directing a fraction of the new compute to support this work — not to chase frontier capability, but to explore whether transparency can become a European comparative advantage. If open models become more auditable than closed ones, that changes the balance of power. But none of this can move without legitimacy, and legitimacy is draining. We are not acting further this turn. We are holding, testing, and preparing to argue — not for more power, but for the right to keep what we have built.
