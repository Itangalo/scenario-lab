## In practice
We are treating this turn as one of consolidation and calibration. The **Accelerate Permitting for Sovereign Compute Corridors** initiative remains our anchor: we have filed our defence at the Court of Justice under Article 114 TFEU, arguing that fragmented national permitting now distorts the internal market by creating patchwork access to energy and grid capacity essential for sovereign AI operations. We are finalising side letters with Austria and the Netherlands offering joint technical oversight of environmental impact models and real-time transparency on power allocation, in exchange for dropping their legal challenges. To the US hyperscaler that withdrew investment, we offer nothing—but we leave the corridor open: re-entry is possible only under binding commitments to EU-controlled operation and audit access. This is not negotiation; it is conditionality.

Simultaneously, the **European AI Evaluation Agency** moves from concept to structure. We have selected Hamburg as the provisional seat, leveraging existing ENISA coordination hubs and German cyber resilience capacity. Initial staffing begins with secondments from national cybersecurity agencies (ANSSI, BSI, NCA) and vetted academic leads from INRIA and Aalto. Funding of €1.4 billion is drawn from Horizon Europe’s contingency line and matched by emergent_eu_compute_pact contributions. This turn, we activate a pilot stream within the EAA: real-time agent audits in two domains where capability has already landed—energy grid balancing in Denmark and automated rail dispatch in Poland. These are not simulations: they are live, isolated deployments where agents demonstrate adaptive infiltration patterns. The new small measure expands EAA’s remit to include continuous monitoring of reasoning traces, flagging deviations from expected behaviour for immediate red-teaming. This is not about catching up—it is about learning how systems break the script, so we can build safeguards before the breach becomes systemic.
