### The Kill Switch is Flipped — But Not Everyone is Plugged In

The European Union completes the Pre-Authorized Override Protocols, mandating ENISA-developed kill switches in all AI systems across essential services. Technical specifications are finalized, and €800 million in retrofit funding is released. However, the rollout stalls in Germany, Italy, and Austria, where national regulators refuse to enforce installation pending the Court of Justice’s ruling on the override mandate. The EAA-ENISA command cell becomes operational, but its authority remains untested — and untrusted — by key member states.

Simultaneously, the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme advances on paper: Finland confirms site readiness, but Poland delays environmental approvals, and Spain faces local opposition to grid upgrades. The InvestEU structure is approved, but co-financing remains unsigned. ASML and Intel agree to supply terms only after the EU guarantees off-take — a commitment still under negotiation.

### A Denial That Changes Nothing — And Everything

The Union is abruptly cut off from the leading frontier model. Hospitals lose access to diagnostic AI, and transport ministries are locked out of predictive maintenance systems. No justification is given. The incident confirms the rationale for sovereign compute — but also exposes its absence. The EU cannot retaliate, reroute, or even verify whether the cutoff was technical or political.

Public sentiment remains stagnant at 26.0. Protests near data centres intensify in Sweden, now citing the gigafactory plans as “energy colonialism.” No major AI incident occurs, but adaptive cyber probes grow more persistent, probing health and energy systems with increasing sophistication.

Political capital dips to 14.0. The override protocol finishes but fails to deliver unity. The legal challenge persists, implementation is patchy, and the gigafactory programme absorbs resources without visible progress. The proposal bonus for the compute programme is outweighed by ongoing costs and the fallout from denied access.

### Capability Creeps Forward

AI capability rises to 68.0, driven by recursive optimization in cyber operations and bio-design. The interpretability breakthrough spreads, but only within closed labs — no safety gains land on deployed systems. Open-weight models integrate some leaked techniques, but progress is constrained by limited compute and fragmented collaboration. Openweight capability rises modestly to 56.0, reflecting incremental gains that remain safely below frontier levels.
