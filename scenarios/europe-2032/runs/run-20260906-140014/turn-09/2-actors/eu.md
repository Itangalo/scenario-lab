## In practice
This turn, we are acting under the weight of two major shocks: the cross-border grid failure and a large-scale, model-generated ransomware sweep that crippled regional health and transport systems in three member states. The **Critical AI Interoperability Directive** is now our immediate shield. We are fast-tracking the legal basis through the Internal Market Council using a combination of DORA and the AI Act’s high-risk provisions, with ENISA drafting technical standards for real-time telemetry and coordinated override protocols by the end of turn 9. To overcome resistance from national operators, we are offering partial compensation from the REPowerEU contingency fund and pairing compliance with access to a new EU-wide AI stress-testing platform. The Energy Council has agreed to a joint declaration, but full adoption in the Council of the EU will require concessions to federalist members on enforcement — we are offering a phased rollout with opt-in early adoption for willing states, knowing that momentum is our best ally.

At the same time, the **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative** remains our long-term anchor. Legal negotiations under Article 122 TFEU are nearing conclusion, and we have secured a political truce with smaller member states by formalising the tiered access model: full red-teaming and deployment rights for those contributing energy quotas, audit-only access for others. France and Germany have committed to co-finance the first 4 exaflops, and the European Investment Bank is finalising a blended finance structure to de-risk private participation. We are also in quiet talks with ASML and Infineon to embed sovereign verification layers into next-gen chiplets, ensuring that even imported hardware can be audited for backdoors. But progress is slow, and the lack of a unified procurement trigger means we are still months from groundbreak.

In response to the cyber_major_incident, we are launching the **European AI Incident Response Corps** — not as a standalone agency, but as a mandate layered across ENISA, Europol, and national CERTs, with pooled analysts, shared playbooks, and pre-authorised intervention rights during declared AI emergencies. The legal basis will be built on the NIS2 Directive and the upcoming Crisis Coordination Regulation, with a pilot activation planned for turn 10. This is not about building a new bureaucracy — it is about making sure that when the next attack comes, someone is already in the room, already trusted, and already empowered to act.
