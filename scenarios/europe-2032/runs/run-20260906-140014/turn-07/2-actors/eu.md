## In practice  
This turn, we close the **Systemic Integrity Watch**, a small but urgent step toward closing the detection gap in AI-driven systemic risk. While it delivered only partial integration due to national data-sharing disputes, its pilot phase confirmed the feasibility of cross-sector telemetry: anomalous load patterns in two regional grids were flagged within minutes, and a rogue automation script in a health data processor was halted before escalation. The lesson is clear — we can build nervous systems, but they are useless without organs to act. That is why we now pivot decisively: the integrity of our institutions cannot be defended from a position of technological vassalage.

We are launching the **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative** under the legal umbrella of the Chips Act and the Digital Markets Act’s interoperability provisions, with €9.2bn in initial funding drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused allocations and the European Defence Fund’s emerging AI clause. The project will be housed in a new European AI Infrastructure Agency, hosted in Lyon and staffed jointly by DG CNECT, ENISA, and national security technocrats, with a mandate to procure, deploy, and govern compute that no third country can legally compel or physically disrupt. We are already in talks with France and Germany to co-locate the first cluster within hardened energy grids, using SMR-backed power to guarantee uptime. Resistance is expected from member states reliant on US cloud providers — we are offering them guaranteed access tiers and joint oversight to secure buy-in. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that when the next threshold is crossed — in bio, in cyber, in cognition — the EU has a voice not because someone lets us speak, but because we have the capacity to act.
