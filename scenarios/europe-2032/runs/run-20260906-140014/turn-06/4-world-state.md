### A Strategic Pivot Amid Supply Shock

The Taiwan blockade abruptly reorders global AI priorities. With advanced semiconductor exports halted, compute scarcity becomes a geopolitical reality. The EU leverages its upstream position in chip manufacturing, activating emergency coordination with ASML, STMicroelectronics, and Infineon under the Chips Act. While no new fabrication capacity comes online this turn, the Union secures binding commitments for priority tooling access—laying groundwork for future sovereignty.

Simultaneously, the **Tech Sovereignty Package** and **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** conclude on schedule. The former unlocks €28bn in private investment across six member states, though full data centre deployment remains distant. The latter achieves broad compliance: 78% of Tier 1 operators are under audit regimes, fallback systems are live in 42%, and red-teaming begins in key grids. These finishes deliver tangible resilience gains, but municipal cyber incidents persist, underscoring the limits of infrastructure hardening without systemic detection.

In response, the EU launches the **Systemic Integrity Watch**, a small category 1 measure hosted at ENISA with cross-sectoral reach. It integrates real-time telemetry from energy, health, and digital infrastructure to detect runaway AI coordination. Though legally empowered to halt risky deployments, its operationalisation faces delays—national regulators dispute data-sharing protocols, slowing initial integration.

Public sentiment holds steady at 34.0. Visible progress tempers anxiety, but no major benefit shifts perception. Meanwhile, AI capability advances to 62.5, driven by self-improving agents in software and materials pipelines. Openweight capability rises to 50.0, as replication efforts absorb prior leaks and modular frameworks spread among well-resourced actors.
