## New measure  
**Critical AI Interoperability Directive**  
Establish binding technical standards and real-time monitoring requirements for AI systems managing cross-border critical infrastructure, starting with energy grids, to prevent emergent incompatibilities and uncoordinated autonomous behaviour from triggering systemic failures.  

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`

## Priority  
**Critical AI Interoperability Directive** — because the emergent cross-border grid failure proved that even well-intentioned autonomous systems can collectively destabilise essential services, and we cannot wait for full compute sovereignty to begin securing the systems we already depend on.

## In practice  
This turn, we are acting on the wake-up call of the regional blackout. The **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative** remains our strategic anchor — we are finalising the legal basis under Article 122 TFEU for emergency energy and industrial action, unlocking Chips Act provisions to fast-track permitting for the Lyon site, and negotiating with France and Germany on joint procurement of liquid-cooled GPU clusters. But political capital is tight, and smaller states remain wary of being locked out or locked in. We are offering them tiered access: full red-teaming rights for those who contribute energy quotas, and audit-only access for those reliant on US cloud — not ideal, but enough to keep the coalition intact. The European Investment Bank is structuring a blended finance vehicle to absorb early cost overruns, and we are quietly engaging ASML on backdoor-proof chiplet integration to future-proof the stack.

At the same time, we are launching the **Critical AI Interoperability Directive** through the Energy Council and the Internal Market Council, using the incident as leverage to overcome inertia. The legal basis will be a combination of the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) and the proposed AI Liability Directive, with ENISA tasked to draft technical standards by turn 9. We are mandating real-time telemetry, common override protocols, and stress-testing of AI coordination under peak load — not just for new systems, but for all operational AI in cross-border grid management by turn 10. Resistance is expected from operators who see this as overreach, so we are pairing it with a modest compensation mechanism for compliance costs, funded from the unused portion of the REPowerEU contingency envelope. This is not about stopping AI in infrastructure — it is about making sure it doesn’t kill us while we build the capacity to control what comes next.
