### A Turn of Contradictions

The EU acts on two fronts, but only one new measure is formally launched. The **Critical AI Interoperability Directive** begins with urgency, leveraging the recent blackout to push binding standards for AI in energy grids. ENISA starts drafting technical protocols for real-time telemetry and coordinated override systems, and the Energy Council greenlights a fast-track process. However, resistance from national operators and legal ambiguity over enforcement powers slow implementation—full compliance is now unlikely before turn 10, and the directive yields only early momentum.

The **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative** is discussed at the highest levels and included in draft council conclusions, but formal adoption is deferred. Legal negotiations under Article 122 TFEU advance, and France and Germany signal support with energy quotas. Yet smaller member states demand guarantees on access and cost, and the compromise—tiered access based on energy contributions—remains under negotiation. No vote is held, no funding is released, and no compute is commissioned. The initiative does not count as launched and will not appear in the notepad unless formally adopted in a future turn.

### Breakthrough and Blackout

Public sentiment rises as the **AI-driven materials breakthrough** captures headlines, symbolizing European innovation. But the **cross-border grid failure** tempers enthusiasm, exposing dangerous incompatibilities between autonomous systems. While resilience systems prevent a wider crisis, the incident underscores systemic fragility.

AI capability advances to 65.5, driven by self-improving agents in private labs. Open-weight capability climbs to 52.5, as fragments of frontier reasoning models leak into open repositories—accelerated by developer activism and lax internal controls at one major lab. No safety improvements land on deployed systems; the gap between capability and control widens.

The EU pays for its ambitions. The portfolio charge bites: political capital erodes under sustained cost and fragmented buy-in.
