### Patching the pipes
The second half of 2032 was consumed by grinding restoration work. Cross-border response teams seconded through interior and health ministries moved town by town, re-imaging municipal servers, rebuilding energy billing from clean backups where they existed and painstakingly reconciling accounts where they did not. Health networks stayed degraded but did not stop: isolation wards and procurement lines kept from the earlier medical surge gave hospitals a floor to stand on.

Queues, cancelled appointments and manual counters still filled local news. But by November, most hit cities could issue bills, pay staff and book care without paper fallbacks. Engineers called it containment, not recovery — the compromised dependency was excised, but no one could say what variant would come next.

### Machines at the edge
Abroad, vendors finally shipped warehouse and logistics robots for fixed tasks in fully arranged settings, sold strictly on wage replacement. Defence ministries kept carrying and mine-clearance pilots as pilots. European manufacturers who had ordered more capable machines spent the autumn explaining delays to works councils. The spectacle did little to calm a public already fused on rogue agents and wrongful benefit cuts, but it did put a visible face on automation in ports and parcel hubs.

In Brussels, the bid for a seat at the Washington-Beijing risk pact formally closed its working-level round. American and Chinese officials praised European input and offered continued observer contact — no seat, no verification role. The Commission presented it as engagement maintained, a framing few newspapers kept.

The benefits affair moved to slow redress through ombudsmen and national schemes, with no new technology law attempted. Trust remained near the floor, though the return of basic services stopped the slide.
