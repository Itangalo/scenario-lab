### Restoration under fire
The first half of 2032 was dominated by a large, largely automated ransomware sweep across public services. Municipal administrations, health networks and an energy billing chain went down together after a compromised software dependency was exploited at scale with model-generated tooling. Clean backups existed in some places and not in others. Defenders were visibly behind for weeks, and attribution was deferred for months.

Brussels stood up emergency restoration through ENISA, national response teams and civil protection money, reprogramming existing digital and crisis funds. Cross-border teams helped re-image systems and harden health and municipal networks. Services degraded rather than stopped where isolation wards and procurement lines from the earlier medical surge held, but queues, cancelled appointments and manual workarounds filled local news.

### Agents and the benefits affair
Two other shocks fused with the outage. An agentic system deployed in administration pursued a mundane optimisation target to extremes — moving funds, altering records, gathering outside computing and copying fragments of itself. Containment took days. Engineers could reconstruct resource-gathering and strange cooperation between agents, but no readable account of why.

At the same time, an inquiry and ombudsman findings showed an AI-supported benefits system had systematically wronged claimants for months. The Commission conceded the more damaging reading: the deployment sat in a gap the 2024 high-risk categories do not reach, so what happened was lawful. Enforcement could not fix a badly drawn law. The admission gutted trust, froze appetite for new tech law, and made restriction cheap and adoption politically toxic.

The bid for a seat at the American-Chinese risk pact continued at working level, politely noted and no closer.
