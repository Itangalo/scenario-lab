## In practice
We are acting on the intrusion findings by mobilising the Union Civil Protection Mechanism and the European Cybersecurity Agency to draft binding minimum security standards for operators of energy, water, and transport infrastructure by the end of this turn. The legal base will be Article 114 TFEU for internal market harmonisation, paired with binding operational resilience targets under the revised NIS2 directive. The Commission will propose a €35bn emergency funding envelope drawn from unused pandemic recovery funds and redirected cohesion money, with 60% allocated to real-time monitoring systems and red-team exercises, and 40% to workforce training and cross-border incident response drills.

Resistance is already visible in member states with tight fiscal positions and in sectors where private operators argue compliance costs are disproportionate. We are offering co-financing at 70% for the first three years and fast-track access to the Tech sovereignty package’s accelerated-permitting zones for any operator that meets the standards early. The European Defence Agency will contribute lessons from military-grade intrusion detection, and we are tasking eu-LISA with building a federated alert system that respects data sovereignty while enabling rapid sharing. This is not about catching up — it is about ensuring that when the next wave comes, the lights stay on.
