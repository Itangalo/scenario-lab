### American Posture Takes Hold

The new U.S. administration formally assumes office, adopting a posture of strategic consolidation. Frontier AI is now classified as critical infrastructure, with access to leading models rationed by geopolitical alignment. The EU, despite its economic weight, is placed in a tier that grants limited, conditional access—dependent on compliance with American security standards. This shift hardens structural dependence just as the Union seeks to build alternatives.

### Coalition Nears Completion, But Unity Frays

The EU-led coordination framework among middle powers in semiconductors, materials, and cloud infrastructure reaches technical completion. South Korea, Japan, and Norway agree to mutual recognition of export controls and shared evaluation protocols. However, Taiwan’s participation remains informal due to diplomatic sensitivities, and internal EU discord deepens. Poland and Hungary resist full harmonisation of AI Act enforcement, citing sovereignty concerns, undermining the bloc’s collective leverage.

### Stalled Sovereignty, Rising Risks

The Accelerate EU Data Centres programme concludes, but only three of twelve sites are operational. Grid bottlenecks and local resistance in Central Europe delay deployment, leaving compute capacity far below target. Meanwhile, ASML faces intensified pressure to relax servicing restrictions on legacy tools in China, exposing cracks in the Union’s export control regime.

The European AI Evaluation Agency advances staffing and facility preparations, but recruitment lags due to competition with well-funded private labs. No new safety mechanisms are yet applied to deployed systems.

Public backlash intensifies after the welfare scandal, with protests in multiple capitals demanding a moratorium on AI in public services. Trust erodes further, and displaced workers report growing anxiety as automation spreads into legal and translation sectors. While open-weight models continue to diffuse incrementally through existing channels, no breakthrough release or systemic access shift occurs to justify a sudden leap in capability; growth remains constrained by infrastructure and talent gaps.

### No New Measure Proposed

With the American posture now clear but its implications still unfolding, the EU delays new initiatives. Political capital erodes under the weight of stalled projects and rising public discontent, but not catastrophically—losses are consistent with accumulated portfolio charges and reduced public confidence, without cascading failure. The separation between political capital and sovereignty remains intact, reflecting distinct sources of strain.
