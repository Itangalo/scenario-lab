The EU entered late 2030 in deepening crisis, closing the Worker Transition Fund amid collapsed retraining efforts and escalating protests over "digital triage" in Thuringia, Silesia, and Wallonia. Automation in legal services surged ahead of policy responses, eroding public trust further. The European Cybersecurity Agency confirmed AI-generated exploits from open models as standard in ransomware, exposing ongoing dependence on foreign developers for threat intelligence. A minimal task force under DG CONNECT was launched to explore rebuilding AI evaluation capacity, but remains unfunded and non-operational until at least mid-2031.

Semiconductor export unity fractured as ASML’s servicing of Chinese legacy equipment gained tacit approval, with Poland and Hungary pushing for formal exceptions, weakening the coalition. The Accelerate EU Data Centres programme stalled with negligible expansion. Relations with the U.S. deteriorated under restricted access to frontier AI models, compounding strategic dependency.

Global AI capability reached 61.5, driven by incremental gains in agent autonomy, while open-weight models advanced to 52.0 through diffusion and fine-tuning. No breakthroughs occurred, but the widening gap between capability and safety governance points to mounting systemic fragility by 2032.
