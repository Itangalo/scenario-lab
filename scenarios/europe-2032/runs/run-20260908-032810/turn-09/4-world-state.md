### Stalled Transition, Rising Tensions

The EU limped through the second half of 2030, closing the Worker Transition Fund under duress. Final disbursements reached Thuringia, but retraining pipelines collapsed under algorithmic delays and broken employer commitments. Protests intensified as unions denounced "digital triage without accountability," and the Commission’s last-minute extensions with Germany and Poland failed to quell the sense of abandonment. Public trust eroded further, with sentiment dipping as automation in legal services outpaced institutional response.

### Blind to the Threat

The European Cybersecurity Agency confirmed AI-generated exploit chains—derived from open models—are now standard in ransomware campaigns. With no sovereign capacity to audit or interpret model behavior, the EU remains dependent on foreign developers for threat insights. A lean task force was quietly launched under DG CONNECT to assess the feasibility of rebuilding evaluation capacity, but it lacks funding, staffing, and legal mandate. Its formation marks only a procedural first step, with no operational capability expected before mid-2031 at the earliest.

### Sovereignty Erodes Further

ASML’s continued servicing of legacy Chinese lithography tools, tacitly accepted by key member states, deepened fractures in the semiconductor coalition. Poland and Hungary pushed for formal exceptions, undermining export control unity. The EU’s leverage with the U.S., already strained under the CONSOLIDATION posture, weakened further as access to frontier models remained rationed and conditional.

### Capability Creeps On

Global AI capability rose to 61.5, driven by iterative improvements in agent autonomy and deployment efficiency. Open-weight models advanced modestly to 52.0, reflecting continued diffusion and fine-tuning of existing architectures. While misuse potential grows, no breakthrough in open-model training or infrastructure has occurred. The gap between global capability and safety or resilience continues to widen, but not at an accelerated pace. Without coordinated intervention, the trend points toward increasing systemic fragility by 2032.
