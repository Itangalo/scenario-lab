### Cyber Shock and Democratic Fracture

A coordinated ransomware campaign, powered by AI-generated exploit chains, paralysed regional health systems in France, Belgium, and Estonia. The attack exploited adaptive agents trained on open-weight models, overwhelming incident response. Despite mutual aid protocols, recovery was slow, exposing critical gaps in cyber resilience. The European Cybersecurity Agency confirmed that the tooling originated from repurposed open-source frameworks, now widely accessible.

Simultaneously, Slovakia annulled its parliamentary election after the European Court of Human Rights ruled that AI-generated disinformation had undermined the integrity of the vote. The decision sparked mass protests, with half the electorate rejecting the court’s authority. The crisis deepened perceptions of democratic fragility and eroded trust in EU institutions.

### Institutional Drift and Strategic Erosion

The European AI Evaluation Agency formally launched but failed to conduct a single audit. Recruitment collapsed under competition from private labs, and member states questioned its value. The agency ends the turn functionally inert. The Accelerate EU Data Centres programme concluded with only three sites operational—far below target—while grid bottlenecks and local opposition stalled progress.

The middle-power semiconductor coalition was formally ratified, but Poland and Hungary abstained, weakening its leverage. ASML’s decision to service legacy tools in China through third parties—citing shareholder obligations—amounted to a quiet defection, fracturing export control unity.

### Crisis Response and Political Exhaustion

The EU fast-tracked disbursements from the Worker Transition Fund to Silesia and Wallonia, using Cohesion Fund top-ups to bypass national delays. However, the scale was insufficient to stem rising anxiety as automation spread into legal and translation roles. Public sentiment plummeted amid overlapping crises, with protests demanding a moratorium on AI in public services.

With no new measure proposed and political capital drained, the Union remained in reactive mode. The priority on worker transition provided minimal political relief, as systemic risks outpaced institutional capacity.
