A devastating AI-powered ransomware attack crippled health systems in France, Belgium, and Estonia, exposing severe cyber resilience gaps and overwhelming response capabilities, while Slovakia annulled its parliamentary election due to AI-generated disinformation, triggering a crisis of democratic legitimacy and mass protests. The European Cybersecurity Agency traced the attack tools to repurposed open-source frameworks, highlighting the risks of accessible open-weight models. Amid deepening instability, the European AI Evaluation Agency became operational but immediately stalled due to talent flight and lack of member state support, rendering it ineffective. The Accelerate EU Data Centres programme failed to scale, with only three sites active, hampered by infrastructure and local resistance. A semiconductor coalition was ratified but weakened by Polish and Hungarian abstentions, while ASML’s move to service Chinese legacy equipment through third parties fractured export control cohesion. In response to growing automation-driven unrest, the EU expanded worker transition funding to Silesia and Wallonia, yet insufficient measures and eroding public trust left institutions reactive and politically exhausted, unable to address mounting technological, social, and democratic challenges.
