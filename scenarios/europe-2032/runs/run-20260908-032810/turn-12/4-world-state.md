### Frontier Cut Off, Response Begins

The European Union was abruptly locked out of the leading AI model, disrupting operations in public health, energy management, and civil protection systems. Hospitals relying on AI-driven diagnostics faced delays, and grid operators lost access to predictive maintenance tools. The blackout, unexplained and irreversible in the short term, was framed domestically as a strategic humiliation, exposing the fragility of external dependence.

In response, the EU activated emergency powers under Article 222 TFEU to launch the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation, a large-scale effort to repurpose idle high-performance computing assets across research institutions. Binding directives were issued to CERN, Fraunhofer, CNRS, and others to report GPU clusters above 1,000 petaflops. Finland agreed to reactivate the decommissioned LUMI-2 array under EU control, marking the first concrete step toward sovereign infrastructure. However, legal resistance loomed, with Germany and Poland demanding safeguards before compliance.

The Accelerated Red-Team Facility Deployment concluded: sites in Leuven and Toulouse became provisionally operational, conducting audits on legacy models and synthetic test cases. Vilnius lagged due to procurement delays, though a temporary German-backed air-gapped solution allowed limited function. Yet without access to frontier models, the facilities had little to evaluate, underscoring the gap between oversight and operational autonomy.

A joint biosurveillance pact with Nordic and Baltic states improved intelligence sharing, marginally boosting societal resilience. Meanwhile, an election in a major member state was annulled over proven manipulation of the information environment using AI-generated content, deepening public distrust.

Despite the urgency, political capital eroded further. The cost of maintaining multiple high-stakes initiatives, combined with internal dissent and no visible public benefit, left the Commission weakened. Public sentiment remained stagnant at crisis levels.

### Sovereignty in Freefall

With global AI capability advancing to 64.0 due to improved agent coordination, and no safety upgrades in place, the EU’s technical and institutional lag deepened. Open-weight models inched toward 53.5, enabling more sophisticated misuse. The Union’s sovereignty score fell to 5.0, reflecting both the access denial and the slow, contested rollout of sovereign capacity.
