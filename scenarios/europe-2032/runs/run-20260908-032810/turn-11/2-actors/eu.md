Behind the scenes, we are pre-positioning staff and compute: the €3.2 billion envelope from Digital Europe and NextGenerationEU is being split, with €800 million fast-tracked to secure facilities and hire 150 red-team specialists by Q1 2032. We are bypassing normal procurement to lease secure GPU clusters from ASML and Siemens, using anti-coercion instrument clauses to ensure they remain under EU legal jurisdiction.

The openweight_frontier_release has changed the threat landscape overnight — 52.0 is no longer a lagging shadow of the frontier, but a live mirror. We cannot wait for the full agency to stand up. That is why we launched the Accelerated Red-Team Facility Deployment: using emergency powers under Article 222 TFEU (civil protection), we are cutting through permitting in Belgium, France, and Lithuania to have evaluation teams running live audits by March 2032. These sites will not wait for full legal clarity — they will operate under a provisional mandate from the Commission, backed by EDPB oversight, and focus first on AI systems managing hospital triage and grid load balancing. We know this risks legal pushback, but the joint_threat_response has given us cover: the biosurveillance pact invites sovereign evaluation as a condition of participation. We are using that to argue that what was once optional is now a condition of collective security. This is not overreach — it is the minimum required to see what is already loose.
