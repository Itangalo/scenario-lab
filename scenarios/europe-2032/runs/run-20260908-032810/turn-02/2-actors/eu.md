## In practice
We are advancing the AI Security Shield by finalising the delegated acts under Article 114 TFEU that will set binding cyber hygiene standards for critical operators, with the European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) publishing the first draft by mid-March. The €35bn emergency fund is being restructured to front-load 40% of disbursements for real-time monitoring and automated patching systems, capitalising on the cyber_defence_breakthrough that now allows defensive AI to patch vulnerabilities faster than they are exploited. We are pairing this with mandatory red-teaming exercises under the NIS2 framework, to be conducted quarterly starting in Q3, and building a federated alert system through eu-LISA that enables cross-border detection without centralising sensitive data.

At the same time, we are launching the Worker Transition Acceleration Fund in response to emergent_labour_displacement_signals. The Commission will propose a €12bn envelope, co-financed at 60% by the EU, to support national retraining programmes in the hardest-hit sectors. Firms that commit to reskilling displaced workers will receive accelerated access to the Tech Sovereignty Package’s permitting fast-tracks and partial tax rebates. This is not just social policy — it is strategic: if public_sentiment turns hostile, the political capital needed to sustain our sovereignty build-out evaporates. We are therefore acting pre-emptively, using the stability of the current 42.0 sentiment as cover to build absorption capacity before the wave hits.

Resistance to the Security Shield persists in member states wary of cost and sovereignty, so we are offering bilateral implementation pathways: those that adopt the standards into national law by Q4 gain priority access to the new defensive tooling and joint cyber-reserves. The eval_anomaly_reports remain unconfirmed, but we are not waiting — because when the first sign of uncontrolled capability appears in the wild, it will not announce itself twice.
