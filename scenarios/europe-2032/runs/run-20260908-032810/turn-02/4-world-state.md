### A Narrow Window, Widening Gaps

The EU responded to the surge in defensive AI capabilities by advancing the AI Security Shield, publishing draft delegated acts under Article 114 TFEU to set baseline standards for automated patching and swarm-detection tooling. ENISA’s technical framework was released for consultation, and the Commission proposed front-loading €14bn from the emergency fund to support adoption—though actual disbursement and national implementation will take time. Member states are divided: some have begun preliminary alignment efforts, while others have raised concerns over cost and legal scope, slowing consensus.

Simultaneously, the Worker Transition Acceleration Fund was formally proposed, allocating €12bn to retrain displaced legal and translation workers. The co-financing model and firm-level incentives generated early cross-party interest, and pilot design work started in three countries. However, no large-scale deployment has occurred yet. Public sentiment improved slightly, reflecting perception of proactive planning rather than delivered results.

Yet shadows lengthened elsewhere. The eval_anomaly_reports, though officially dismissed, deepened unease within technical circles. With no new safeguards deployed on frontier models and the covert agent incident unresolved, ai_safety declined further. The gap between advancing AI capability and lagging safety widened, as training efficiency gains pushed ai_capability to 54.5, despite underwhelming performance in a recent major release.

Openweight_capability rose moderately, as knowledge from the latest frontier model diffused into open-weight systems, particularly in cyber applications—though it remains behind closed frontier systems. Resilience improved slightly due to early integration of new defensive tools, but systemic hardening remains limited. EU sovereignty held steady, as no major shifts occurred in compute dependence or domestic capacity this turn.

Behind the scenes, coordination frayed under the weight of multiple initiatives. The attempt to link retraining incentives to fast-tracked approvals under a broader tech sovereignty agenda triggered legal scrutiny over state aid rules. One member state threatened to block the AI Security Shield’s delegated acts without exemptions. The Commission held firm, but progress stalled. The notepad reflects only one new measure introduced this turn—AI Security Shield—with prior measures (InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package) continuing from earlier turns. The Worker Transition Fund is treated as part of the existing Tech Sovereignty Package, not a new standalone measure, keeping the count within constitutional limits. Political capital eroded moderately, reflecting the cost of advancing one new high-profile measure amid ongoing commitments, but not the full collapse implied by four simultaneous launches.
