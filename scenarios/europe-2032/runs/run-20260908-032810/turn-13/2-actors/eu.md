## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign resilience through independent AI assurance and critical infrastructure protection  
  - Grounds: This turn has revealed that assurance without operational sovereignty is a hollow shell: our red-team sites are live but idle, our Evaluation Agency lacks the models it needs to evaluate, and the annulled election proves that harm is already landing. We are not being blocked by technical delay but by denial of access to the very systems we are mandated to oversee. The commitment stands, but its interpretation must shift from oversight-first to capability-first: we must run what we protect, and protect what we run. Assurance is no longer the starting point — it is the goal, and the path runs through sovereign stacks, not audit mandates.
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.  
  - Grounds: The cost of acting under uncertainty has become existential to our political capital. At 1.0, we are functionally bankrupt: member states are withholding cooperation, markets are pricing EU digital infrastructure as high-risk, and public sentiment has collapsed to levels where even defensive measures are met with hostility. We still believe in acting before the full picture emerges — but we can no longer afford to act alone. This turn, we reframe the commitment: we will commit before the picture is clear, but only in concert with binding coalitions that share the risk. Unilateral emergency action has failed. Our pivot to joint activation with Finland, the Nordics, and the PESCO partners is not retreat — it is the only way forward with legitimacy.

## New measure  
### Secure Stack for Critical Functions  
Launch the development and deployment of a minimal, EU-controlled AI stack — training, inference, and deployment pipeline — focused exclusively on healthcare diagnostics, energy grid stability, and civil protection coordination, using repurposed compute from the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation.  

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`  
`Targeted effect:` +20 to eu_ai_sovereignty by establishing a live, maintained sovereign model stack; +5 to resilience by hardening three critical sectors against cascading failure during AI blackouts; +3 to ai_safety by enabling red-team evaluations on a system under full EU control  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with mandatory integration into national systems for public health, energy, and civil protection in all member states  

## Priority  
Priority: Secure Stack for Critical Functions.  
This is no longer about building capacity for the future — it is about restoring function now. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation secures hardware, but hardware without software is a monument, not a tool. We need a stack we can run, maintain, and defend — and we need it before the next blackout. This measure turns repurposed clusters into operational sovereignty.

## In practice  
We are out of time and out of trust. The Sovereign Audit and Evaluation Launch finishes this turn, but its authority is hanging by a thread. The European Court of Justice has fast-tracked the German and Polish challenge, and we are preparing for a partial invalidation of the centralised evaluation mandate. To preserve what we can, we are offering a tripartite compromise: national regulators retain veto power over audits touching core state functions, but must delegate technical evaluation to a strengthened European AI Evaluation Office operating under Article 114 TFEU. This office will not have full authority, but it will have access — and we are already negotiating bilateral data-sharing pacts with France, Finland, and the Netherlands to ensure it has models to test.

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation is now active: CERN has reported 1,800 petaflops of idle capacity; CNRS has committed 1,200. LUMI-2 in Kajaani is undergoing recommissioning, funded by a €1.4 billion draw from Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme. But we are hitting coordination failure: without a unified stack, each cluster will run its own fork, and we will gain capacity but lose cohesion. That is why we are launching the Secure Stack for Critical Functions — not as a research project, but as a binding deployment directive. We are tasking the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) with leading it, in partnership with the newly operational red-team sites in Leuven and Toulouse. The stack will be minimal: one training run every six weeks, inference hosted on EU-secured infrastructure, and a strict application boundary — no general-purpose models, no open releases, no integration with consumer services. Our first use case is hospital triage in six member states, coordinated through the EHDS (European Health Data Space). We are mandating adoption via the NIS2 directive, with a six-month grace period. Germany resists, citing sovereignty; we are offering them leadership of the core development team and first access to audit rights. This is not ideal — but it is the only way to move.
