### Scandal and Backlash

A national ombudsman delivers a damning report: an AI-assisted welfare eligibility system in a major member state denied thousands of legitimate claims due to biased risk scoring. The scandal ignites public fury, reinforcing perceptions of AI as arbitrary and unaccountable. Trust erodes further, especially among vulnerable populations. Labour unions and civil society demand a moratorium on AI in public services.

### Fragile Unity, Fading Leverage

The EU attempts to rally a coalition of middle powers to counterbalance American control over frontier AI. A new coordination framework emerges among semiconductor, materials, and cloud infrastructure states, aligning export licensing and pooling evaluation resources. While no sovereignty is ceded, the group gains leverage. However, internal disunity persists—Poland and Hungary resist harmonizing AI Act enforcement, citing national discretion, and ASML faces quiet pressure to ease servicing restrictions on legacy tools in China, undermining the bloc’s strategic posture.

### Stalled Momentum

No sovereignty-building measure concludes. Private compute investment remains frozen, and the capability gap widens. The Open-Source Intelligence Unit progresses slowly, still not operational. The Worker Transition Fund expands modestly but fails to stem rising anxiety. With political capital strained and sentiment falling, the EU struggles to act decisively.
