The U.S. elects a new president who classifies advanced AI as a national security asset, tightening export controls and rationing frontier model access by geopolitical tier, deepening EU dependence. A major scandal erupts in an EU member state as an AI-driven welfare system denies thousands of legitimate claims due to biased algorithms, triggering public outrage and demands for a moratorium on AI in public services. Trust in AI plummets, further eroding public sentiment. The EU forms a coordination framework with middle powers in semiconductors, materials, and cloud infrastructure to pool resources and align export licensing, gaining limited leverage without ceding sovereignty. However, internal fragmentation persists, with Poland and Hungary resisting AI Act harmonization, and ASML facing pressure to relax restrictions on servicing legacy tools in China. No sovereignty-enhancing measures conclude: private compute investment remains frozen, the capability gap widens, the Open-Source Intelligence Unit stays non-operational, and the Worker Transition Fund expands only modestly. Political capital stagnates under strained legitimacy and declining public support, leaving the EU unable to mount decisive action.
