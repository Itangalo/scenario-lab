### The Open Frontier

A major open-weight model, approaching frontier capability, was released and began rapid dissemination. Advanced AI capabilities spread quickly across research and developer communities, with early evidence of use in automating exploit generation and scientific workflows. The release significantly boosted open capabilities, lifting `openweight_capability` to 45.0—still below `ai_capability` and within the expected growth corridor. While adoption was swift in technically adept networks, the model’s global reach remained partial, and it did not achieve functional parity with the most advanced closed systems.

The EU completed its AI Security Shield, binding cyber hardening standards to critical infrastructure operators. Automated patching and swarm-detection protocols were adopted by leading energy and port operators, and the federated alert system launched in pilot mode. These gains, combined with joint exercises and threat-sharing incentives, lifted `resilience` to 50.0. However, Poland and Hungary delayed transposition, citing cost and legal overreach, weakening collective readiness.

Public concern sharpened. The release intensified fears of uncontrolled AI misuse, while job losses in legal and translation sectors fed into a growing backlash. Campaign rhetoric in the US echoed this anxiety, framing AI as a zero-sum race. Despite visible action, `public_sentiment` dipped to 44.0 as displacement and insecurity dominated discourse.

The EU announced the Open-Source Intelligence Unit to monitor emergent risks in open models, establishing coordination channels between research nodes in Berlin, Toulouse, and Prague. While the unit’s mandate was approved, staffing and technical integration remain ongoing, with full operational capacity expected no sooner than the next turn. It is not yet a regulatory tool, but aims to restore analytical capacity over open ecosystems. The move earned cautious praise but added to an overloaded agenda.

The Worker Transition Fund expanded in France, Estonia, and Spain, with wage-insurance disbursements beginning in July. Linking reskilling to access to future compute contracts created leverage, but legal challenges persisted. The measure remained a priority, yet its political return lagged behind rising social pressure.

### Sovereignty Without Momentum

No sovereignty-building measure concluded. Private compute commitments remained frozen, and US hyperscaler retreats left structural dependence unaddressed. Despite the capability rise, no event affected EU access directly—so the decay term (−1) from prior stagnation held, but no further. `eu_ai_sovereignty` remained at 19.0.

Political capital edged up slightly. The completion of the AI Security Shield delivered a +4 bonus, offsetting ongoing costs. Four large measures and a priority still drained −13, but the finish brought relief. No new measure earned a proposal bonus—too soon after the release for clear attribution. Sentiment remained below capital, so no legitimacy lending applied.
