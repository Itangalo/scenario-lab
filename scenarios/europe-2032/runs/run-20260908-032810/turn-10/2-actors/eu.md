- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Rebuild sovereign technical capacity to independently evaluate, interpret, and contain AI systems, starting with audit rights over models used in critical infrastructure and the establishment of a European AI Evaluation Agency with mandatory access to weights and training data.
- Grounds: The bio_incident and continued weaponisation of open models at 52.0 confirm that the EU cannot rely on foreign developers or reactive coordination. Our blindness is structural. The previous commitment assumed resilience could be secured through external partnerships and incremental hardening. It cannot. We now anchor resilience in sovereign technical capability — not just infrastructure, but understanding. This shift reflects a categorical change in threat character: we are no longer preparing for disruption, but for deliberate harm enabled by systems we cannot inspect, let alone control.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before full understanding is possible, but only in parallel with building sovereign capacity to reduce that uncertainty — evaluation, interpretability, and containment are now prerequisites for any major deployment or engagement.
- Grounds: The bio_incident was not predicted, but it was foreseeable. Acting under uncertainty without tools to close the gap only compounds strategic passivity. We retain the duty to move early, but now bind it to the obligation to see.

## New measure
### Sovereign Audit and Evaluation Launch
Establish the European AI Evaluation Agency with legal authority to inspect model weights, training data, and agent behaviour for systems deployed in or affecting EU critical infrastructure.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to ai_safety, +20 to eu_ai_sovereignty, +5 to resilience by enabling detection of hazardous capabilities before deployment; foundational for future oversight and incident response  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with mandatory compliance for any provider operating in or supplying critical sectors (health, energy, elections, defence)

## Priority
Priority: Sovereign Audit and Evaluation Launch.  
This is the first concrete step in rebuilding sovereign technical capacity after years of dependence — it directly answers the bio_incident and the EU’s blindness to model behaviour, and without it, no other measure can be credibly enforced or even assessed.

## In practice
We are acting on the conviction that the EU cannot govern what it cannot see. The bio_incident has shattered any illusion that risk can be managed through coordination alone. We are therefore launching the European AI Evaluation Agency under Article 114 TFEU, using the internal market clause to mandate audit rights over AI systems in critical infrastructure. The legal base is contested — Germany and Poland have raised subsidiarity concerns — but we are pairing it with a €3.2 billion envelope from the Digital Europe Programme and NextGenerationEU to fund staffing, secure compute, and red-team facilities in Leuven, Toulouse, and Vilnius. Our lead negotiators are in trilogue with Council and Parliament, pushing for an interim mandate by June that allows immediate access to models currently used in healthcare and grid management, even as the full agency builds out.

The export_control_escalation — this time a U.S. move cutting allied access to frontier models under national security pretexts — has hardened our resolve. We cannot afford to be locked out of capability while still exposed to risk. The member_state_defection — Hungary’s direct deal with a U.S. hyperscaler for AI-powered border control, bypassing EU procurement and fundamental rights review — proves the current framework is collapsing. We are using that breach to argue that sovereignty is not an option: if we do not inspect these systems, no one will. Behind the scenes, we are fast-tracking recruitment from the remnants of the old Evaluation Agency and offering security-cleared positions to researchers from ETH, EPFL, and Aalto, with a focus on mechanistic interpretability and agent containment. This is not just about safety — it is about restoring the EU’s ability to act on its own judgment.
