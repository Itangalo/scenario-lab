### Crisis and Resolve

The EU opened 2031 reeling from a biological incident involving an AI-designed pathogen, with containment efforts stretching across weeks and global AI governance discourse reset overnight. The European Commission responded by launching the Sovereign Audit and Evaluation Launch, a large Category 5 measure aiming to establish a European AI Evaluation Agency with full access to model weights and training data. Citing the bio_incident and Hungary’s unilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler on AI-powered border systems, the Commission framed the move as existential: the Union cannot govern what it cannot inspect.

### Fractures Deepen

The U.S. tightened export controls, restricting even allied access to frontier models under national security rationales — a move felt as punitive within Brussels. This, combined with Hungary’s defection, was interpreted as a dual blow to access and cohesion. Yet the political cost was severe. Germany and Poland challenged the legal basis of the new agency under Article 114 TFEU, stalling trilogue talks. With public sentiment collapsing further due to fears of unchecked AI-enabled threats, protests flared near proposed red-team facility sites. The Worker Transition Fund’s closure continued to haunt the Commission, now seen as emblematic of broken social contracts.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

Global AI capability advanced to 62.5, driven by incremental gains in agent coordination and deployment efficiency. Open models remained at 52.0, with no new releases but persistent misuse in cyber and disinformation. Despite the bio_incident, no safety improvements landed on deployed systems. Resilience eroded as critical infrastructure remained exposed, and the EU’s delayed response deepened perceptions of institutional drift.
