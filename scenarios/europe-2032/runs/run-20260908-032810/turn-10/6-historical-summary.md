The EU began 2031 in crisis following a biological incident involving an AI-designed pathogen, triggering a fundamental shift in AI governance. The Commission launched the Sovereign Audit and Evaluation Launch, a Category 5 initiative to establish a European AI Evaluation Agency with full access to model weights and training data, framed as essential for sovereignty after Hungary’s unilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler on AI border systems. However, legal challenges from Germany and Poland under Article 114 TFEU stalled trilogue negotiations, while public protests near proposed red-team sites reflected eroding trust. The U.S. tightened export controls, restricting allied access to frontier AI, exacerbating strategic dependency and deepening EU fragmentation. Global AI capability rose to 62.5 due to gains in agent coordination, but open models stagnated at 52.0 with ongoing misuse in cyber and disinformation. No safety improvements were implemented post-incident, leaving critical infrastructure exposed and reinforcing perceptions of institutional failure. The closure of the Worker Transition Fund remained a symbol of broken social promises, compounding political and societal fragility.
