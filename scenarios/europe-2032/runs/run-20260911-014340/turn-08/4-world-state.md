### Holding the line
January to June 2030 was a semester of maintenance. Grid control rooms kept segmentation and pooled telemetry running on existing mandates. When spring probing brushed substations again, operators isolated quickly. No blackout followed. Bio-surveillance sequencing standards stayed in force. On paper, what had held kept holding.

Politics did not hold.

### The benefits ruling that broke trust
In March an ombudsman and then a court found that an automated scoring system used in social insurance and policing triage had systematically cut benefits and flagged families for years. Caseworkers had approved queues in seconds. Logs existed and were never read.

Then came the worse finding: the deployment had never been classified as high-risk at all. The categories written in 2024 did not reach it. Everything done had been lawful.

Editorials called the rulebook obsolete. Talk shows replayed families describing lost payments. Ministers promised redress and an audit of logs under existing funds, and paused forced switches to European-hosted tools where clinics said referrals were breaking. It read as apology without repair. Trust in Brussels' ability to govern AI collapsed further. Municipal councils citing water use and blackout fears kept permitted data-centre grid connections frozen; police files on arson and break-ins at construction compounds grew, reported only as open investigations.

### Distant strait, near prices
Extended exercises around the Taiwan Strait lifted shipping insurance and revived chip-supply memos in trade and defence directorates. No new build was triggered; officials mapped exposure from existing envelopes. The capital that had signed its own supply deal outside the common line kept its cheaper inference, while continuity offers conditional on returning to the common position went unanswered.

By June, frontier models abroad crept forward and openly mirrored weights narrowed the gap again. The Union ended the semester more brittle, poorer in cohesion, and with no room to start anything new.
