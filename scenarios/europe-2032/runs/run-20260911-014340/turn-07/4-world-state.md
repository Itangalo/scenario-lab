### Holding what held
The second half of 2029 was the semester Brussels stopped building and tried to keep what it had built running. The Grid Shield hardening work formally closed, and control-room crews kept segmentation kits and pooled telemetry live through the autumn peak. When a fresh wave of automated phishing and grid-probing hit in October, operators isolated substations in hours rather than days. No blackout followed. Engineers called it vindication; ministers called it relief.

Relief did not travel far.

### The side deal
In late summer a capital broke ranks. Facing hospital backlogs and industry pressure over rationed access to advanced American models, its government signed its own supply arrangement with a US hyperscaler — cheaper inference, faster permits, outside the common rationing and permitting line. At home it was sold as pragmatism. In Brussels and in Washington it read as proof the Union could not hold a line.

The Commission offered continuity funds and mutual aid if the capital returned to the common position, and paused forced switches to European-hosted helpers where doctors said referrals were breaking. Union negotiators got a return to shifts in two regions, but not a return to the line. Licensed foreign workflows stayed on standby in the worst-hit hospitals, quietly becoming the workflow again.

### Thin trust
Public mood soured further. Television debates replayed mangled referrals and water-use fights around data-centre sites. Two municipalities deferred new grid connections for permitted sites after protests. Police logged arson attempts and break-ins at two construction compounds and a telemetry depot in single-source reports, linked in press commentary to anti-AI networks. Officials would confirm only that investigations were open.

By December, Brussels had spent no new money because there was none to spend. The safety inquiry into the February agent incident leaked interim findings on voluntary testing and loosely held weights without assigning blame. Frontier capability crept forward abroad; openly mirrored models narrowed the gap again. The Union ended the year more resilient in its grids, poorer in cohesion, and with almost no room to act.
