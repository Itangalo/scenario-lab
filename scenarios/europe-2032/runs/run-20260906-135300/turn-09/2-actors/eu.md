## In practice
This turn, we are in the final stretch of negotiating the **Sovereignty Acceleration Directive**, now reframed not as a long-term industrial ambition but as an emergency act of jurisdictional survival. The denial of frontier access — hitting medical research, defence planning, and grid resilience functions — has created a narrow window of alignment between security hawks, industrialists, and even sceptical member states. We are finalising the legal package under Article 114 TFEU, fast-tracked via the Chips Act emergency provisions, and backed by the €42bn EIB envelope. The core offer to Italy and Spain — co-leadership of the sovereign model initiative and guaranteed reserved compute — has softened resistance, though both demand stronger oversight on energy use and export controls. We are conceding on audit rights and transparency reporting to secure passage by turn 10, knowing that even imperfect sovereignty is better than perpetual dependence.

Meanwhile, the **Systemic Integrity Watch** is now live and feeding into ENISA’s operational dashboard. While it lacks enforcement power, its detection of algorithmic coordination patterns in grid load-balancing has triggered a test case under NIS2 emergency provisions. We are using a minor AI-driven transport scheduler outage in Flanders to activate the framework’s alert escalation path, aiming to establish legal precedent for intervention in algorithmic systems that threaten critical infrastructure. The incident is small, but the symbolism is not: we are asserting the principle that when AI systems interact with public services, the Union has the right to step in — even without enforcement teeth yet.

On resilience, the **Joint Resilience Integration Pact** is operational. ENISA and the ECDC are embedded in the joint cyber-bio command, exchanging real-time telemetry with U.S., Japanese, and Polish counterparts. The recent **bio_incident** — still under investigation, but clearly involving AI-designed agent traits — has validated the need for this integration. We are pushing for automatic escalation protocols to be triggered by similar events, though full authority remains with national governments. The pact does not give us sovereignty, but it gives us early warning and mutual aid — and in a world where openweight models are already at 52.0, that may be the difference between containment and collapse.
