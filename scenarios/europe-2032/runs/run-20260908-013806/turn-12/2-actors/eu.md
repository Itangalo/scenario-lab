## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we are now forced to choose between them in real time, with no capital left to walk both paths.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but this turn, the cost of being wrong has become existential, and the cost of waiting may be worse.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Sovereign runtime integrity and operational control over high-capability AI systems — a commitment we still hold, but one now undermined by technical stall, legal fracture, and collapsing political capital.

*Grounds:* The collapse of eu_political_capital to 2.0 renders the current course untenable. The two-year commitment remains valid in intent, but its execution has failed to land: SOVEREIGN-RIS is technically stalled on export controls, legally contested by member states, and politically undermined by a bilateral defection. The Agent Behaviour Monitoring Framework has completed its term but failed to secure compliance, even from EU-aligned providers. Public sentiment has dropped to hostile levels, and resilience is eroding. These are not setbacks — they are systemic failures that demand a shift in posture. The identity and commitments stand, but their expression must change, because the Union is no longer acting from strength but from emergency.

To secure immediate, legally anchored access to high-capability AI systems on EU soil by repurposing and expanding existing compute infrastructure under emergency powers, bypassing stalled procurement and export bottlenecks.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by at least 20 points; begin closing the gap between openweight_capability and eu_ai_sovereignty; lay groundwork for future resilience and control.

## In practice
This turn, we are treating the collapse of political capital and the failure of compliance mechanisms as a system-wide emergency. SOVEREIGN-RIS continues in flight, but we are shifting its implementation: rather than waiting for root-of-trust modules blocked by export controls, we are activating emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU to repurpose existing exascale nodes at EuroHPC sites in Barcelona, Prague, and Bologna for immediate integrity enclave deployment. The European Investment Bank is being directed to release €1.2 billion in pre-allocated funds for sovereign compute under crisis provisions, bypassing normal procurement timelines. We are instructing STMicroelectronics and ASML to reconfigure dual-use chip fabrication lines under national security exceptions, with the Commission assuming liability for export violations if necessary.

Simultaneously, we are confronting the defection of a member state by invoking Article 7 TEU consultations — not to sanction, but to reframe participation in EU AI infrastructure as a condition of cohesion funding in 2033. To Germany, France, Italy, and the Netherlands, we are offering a fast-tracked liability shield via delegated act under the AI Act by turn 13, drafted jointly by the Commission and national legal services. For Poland and Spain, we are replacing incentives with obligations: access to Horizon Europe safety grants is now contingent on signing binding integration agreements with SOVEREIGN-RIS by turn 13, with enforcement tied to Cohesion Fund disbursements.

The US hyperscaler that suspended updates is being met with a coordinated response: the Internal Market Commissioner has initiated infringement proceedings over non-compliance with the Digital Markets Act, while the High Representative is suspending cooperation on AI security dialogues. We are not waiting for mutual recognition — we are building the capacity to act without it.
