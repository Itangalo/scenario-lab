### Crisis Mode

The EU declares a de facto emergency in AI infrastructure, launching the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation (ESCM) as its new priority. Invoking Article 122 TFEU, the Commission repurposes EuroHPC exascale nodes in Barcelona, Prague, and Bologna for integrity enclave deployment, bypassing stalled export controls on root-of-trust modules. The European Investment Bank releases €1.2 billion under crisis provisions, and STMicroelectronics begins reconfiguring chip lines under contested national security exceptions.

Yet the political foundation is crumbling. With eu_political_capital at 2.0, the Union cannot sustain three large measures. ESCM adds a third large cost, and the priority penalty applies — but no completion bonus arrives. The Agent Behaviour Monitoring Framework finishes but fails: US hyperscalers ignore audits, pilots generate false alarms, and mutual recognition collapses. No safety or resilience gains materialize.

Member state resistance hardens. Poland and Spain reject integration mandates, calling them coercive. Germany, France, Italy, and the Netherlands welcome the liability shield offer — but demand it be legally binding before turn 13, a condition unmet. One member state’s bilateral deal with a US provider stands, fracturing unity. Brussels initiates Article 7 consultations, but the move is seen as retaliatory, not strategic.

Public sentiment drops further as energy-intensive data centres face protests in Bavaria and Wallonia. Media frame the emergency measures as desperate, not decisive. Resilience erodes to 46.0, as enforcement gaps widen and coordination frays.

AI capability advances to 64.1 through improved agent memory coherence. With no safety progress — and rising capability — ai_safety falls to 7.0. Openweight capability reaches 53.4 as derivatives of closed models proliferate. Sovereignty declines to 19.0: despite mobilisation, no capacity event secures access, and capability growth above 2.0 is avoided — but the member state defection from turn 11 continues to drain trust and cohesion.

Political capital hits zero. At this level, new measures may fail to start — and ESCM, though formally launched, lacks unified implementation. The narrative of control is breaking.
