### The Signal in the Noise

The cyber intrusion across critical infrastructure operators sent shockwaves through capitals, but not unity. The EU’s response—fast, targeted, and backed by emergency funding—was clear: resilience is now the frontline. The Hardened Critical Infrastructure Directive advanced in draft, leveraging Article 114 to bypass unanimity, yet securing buy-in from Germany, France and Poland required concessions. The €12bn disbursement from unused Recovery funds unlocked support, but only after promises of national flexibility in implementation.

Meanwhile, the plateau debate intensified. A major AI release underperformed, and prices for top-tier models dropped—signs some interpret as stagnation. Yet capability still advanced: `ai_capability` rose by 1.5, reflecting incremental gains in agent persistence and domain breadth. With no corresponding safety upgrade on shipped systems, `ai_safety` fell by 2.

The open frontier widened cautiously. `openweight_capability` edged up to 41.0, as Kimi K3’s capabilities diffused and smaller labs replicated parts of its architecture. Offensive cyber tools are now within reach of skilled non-state actors, though not yet automated at scale.

Sovereignty efforts held. The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remained on track. Grid delays in Finland and Spain were mitigated by invoking fast-tracking, preserving momentum. No major access disruption occurred—indeed, `eu_access_secured` marked a rare win: the EU negotiated evaluation rights and notice periods for frontier model access. This modest gain, combined with ongoing build momentum, lifted `eu_ai_sovereignty` by 1.

But political capital eroded. Three large measures now run (two category 4, one category 6), costing 3 each, and the priority added a further −1. The portfolio charge was steep, and no finished measure offset it. The cyber incident justified urgency, but did not yield a proposal bonus—preparation had been slow, and the directive was reactive. Capital fell to 40.0, narrowing the margin for future action.

Public sentiment held at 42.0—ambivalent, split, and watchful. No major scandal or benefit shifted the needle.
