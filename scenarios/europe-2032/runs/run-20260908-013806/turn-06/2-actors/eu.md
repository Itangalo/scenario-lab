## In practice
This turn, we are finalising the Tech Sovereignty Package: the Council has adopted the accelerated-permitting zones, and national regulators in France, Finland, and Poland have begun fast-tracking grid connections for the InvestAI Gigafactories. But the collapse in global AI investment has exposed a fatal flaw — our compute build-out relied on private capital that no longer exists. Promised investments from US cloud providers have been withdrawn, and European firms are freezing expansion. We cannot afford to wait for sentiment or markets to recover. That is why we are launching the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation Directive: using emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU, we are instructing the European Commission to identify all stalled or underutilised high-performance computing sites within the Union, and to offer state-backed purchase guarantees for GPU clusters deployed under EU legal jurisdiction, with priority given to facilities that integrate into the Gigafactories network. We are also negotiating with energy regulators to reclassify sovereign AI compute as critical infrastructure, granting it priority grid access and capped energy prices. To hyperscalers, we offer a clear deal: anchor your hardware legally in the EU, submit to weight-security audits, and gain guaranteed market access and power allocation. Refuse, and we will redirect public funds to domestic champions or seize option rights on idle capacity. This is not nationalisation — it is survival. The window to act is narrow, and we are using it to lock in the physical foundation of autonomy before the world closes again.
