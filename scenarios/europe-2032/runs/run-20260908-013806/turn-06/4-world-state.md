### The Investment Winter Deepens

Global AI investment collapses as valuations crater and major cloud providers cancel planned expansions. The EU’s compute ambitions, heavily reliant on private capital, face sudden exposure. Promised GPU clusters vanish from contracts, and European firms freeze AI infrastructure plans. The Union acts swiftly, declaring an emergency under Article 122 TFEU to launch the **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation Directive**, aiming to secure 150,000 H100-equivalent GPUs on EU soil through state-backed purchase guarantees and binding legal agreements.

### Sovereignty Gains, Capital Costs Rise

The directive, now the named priority, accelerates access to stalled sites and reclassifies sovereign compute as critical infrastructure for energy allocation. France and Finland fast-track grid access, and early negotiations with domestic operators show promise. However, hyperscalers resist audit demands and legal anchoring, stalling key deals. The completed **Tech Sovereignty Package** delivers its final momentum, contributing +1 to sovereignty, while rising AI capability (up 0.3) imposes a −1 decay. With no access event, the net effect is +2, lifting sovereignty to 24.0.

### Political Strain Mounts

The EU’s political capital is strained. The portfolio charge is steep: three large measures (−9), plus the priority (−1), totaling −10. The new category 4 measure earns a strong proposal bonus (+5) for directly countering the recent investment collapse — a severe, external shock. However, this is offset by ongoing fiscal pressure and scepticism over emergency powers. No sentiment boost applies, and legitimacy lending is absent. Net change: +5 −10 = −4, reducing political capital to 26.0.

### Safety Erodes, Open Models Spread

Despite regulatory efforts, no safety upgrades land on deployed systems. The gap between capability and safety widens as autonomous agents advance, and a new open-weight model adapted for cyber intrusion circulates. With no mitigating measure completed, ai_safety drops by −3. Openweight capability rises to 48.5, closing the gap further. Resilience holds at 55.0, as existing frameworks absorb the increased threat volume without systemic failure. Public sentiment remains stagnant at 30.0, with no visible benefit to counter job loss narratives.
