## New measure
**EU Open-Weight Monitoring and Containment Framework**  
Establish a pan-European detection and response system to monitor the diffusion and misuse of open-weight AI models, particularly those approaching frontier capability.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by +5–8 points through early detection of misuse vectors and containment of high-risk open models; indirect pressure on ai_safety by creating a feedback loop between deployment incidents and developer accountability.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, open-source hosting platforms, cybersecurity agencies (ENISA, CERT-EU), and academic and independent developer communities.

## In practice  
This turn, we are closing the EU Joint Threat Response Protocol: the Council has adopted the binding decision under Article 202 TFEU, the €4.2bn is committed from the Civil Protection Mechanism and Digital Europe Programme, and the Threat Fusion Cell is now operational in Brussels with live feeds from NATO and partner biosurveillance networks. The Joint Research Centre has integrated the first wave of telemetry protocols, and HERA is standing up rapid-response protocols for dual-use biological threats. Poland has accepted co-lead status on bio-detection, with a new node in Warsaw to coordinate screening of synthetic DNA orders across EU labs. This is not perfect—coverage gaps remain in non-EU jurisdictions—but it is live, fused, and faster than any single state could have built alone.

At the same time, we are pushing through the final national implementations of the Hardened Critical Infrastructure Directive. Poland’s delay on funding is now the critical bottleneck. We are offering a 70% co-financing top-up from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, conditional on full adoption by turn 4, and deploying Commission task forces to assist national regulators in rolling out detection systems and red-teaming protocols. ENISA is running a live exercise in Q4 with energy and water operators across six member states to stress-test readiness. The cyber_major_incident makes this no longer optional: the attack vector was model-generated, and defences were too slow. We must harden before the next wave.

On the industrial front, the InvestAI Gigafactories are progressing: Finland and Spain have secured grid access, and construction begins in Q4. In Poland, we have invoked the Tech Sovereignty Package’s accelerated-permitting clause to override regional objections, citing the new threat environment. The Environment Committee’s threat to block Digital Europe funding is serious, so we are fast-tracking the PUE review: ENERDATA will deliver binding efficiency benchmarks by December, and we will publish them as a condition of future site approvals. We are not backing down on compute—we are anchoring it in accountability. The openweight_frontier_release proves that what is loose cannot be recalled; our sovereignty must include the ability to monitor, detect, and respond to what runs beyond our control.
