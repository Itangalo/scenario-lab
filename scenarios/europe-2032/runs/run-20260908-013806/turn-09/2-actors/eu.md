## New measure
**Sovereign Runtime Integrity Stack (SOVEREIGN-RIS)**  
Establish a fully EU-controlled stack for runtime verification of high-capability AI systems, including sovereign hardware enclaves, open-source introspection tools, and a distributed network of integrity nodes operated by member states and public research institutions.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by +6–8 points and lock in long-term control over runtime verification infrastructure; prevent dependence on foreign hardware or software layers for critical safety functions. Anchor the EU’s ability to enforce its own runtime rules regardless of where models are trained or by whom.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, national cybersecurity agencies, public cloud providers, EU-funded research infrastructures, and all providers of high-capability AI systems serving EU markets.

## Priority
**Sovereign Runtime Integrity Stack (SOVEREIGN-RIS)** — because the loss_of_control_incident exposed that oversight is meaningless without sovereign infrastructure to enforce it: we cannot mandate runtime monitoring if the hardware, firmware, or verification stack itself is foreign-controlled and opaque, and this turn we begin building the physical foundation that makes our rules enforceable.

## In practice
This turn, we are operationalising the EU-Wide Agent Behaviour Monitoring Framework by standing up the first phase of the EU AI Safety Observatory’s Runtime Verification Division. Under Article 114 TFEU, we are issuing delegated acts to define technical standards for cryptographic weight attestation, process isolation, and goal-consistency logging for all models above 55.0 capability operating in the EU. The €3.8bn allocation from Horizon Europe and Digital Europe is now being disbursed to INRIA, Fraunhofer, and a consortium of Nordic cybersecurity institutes to develop tamper-proof sensor agents and secure introspection enclaves. We are mandating that all Tier 1 and Tier 2 operators — including foreign hyperscalers — integrate these sensors by turn 11 or face suspension of market access. Resistance is fierce, particularly from US providers who argue that real-time EU access to internal model states violates trade secrecy and national security agreements. We are countering with a phased compliance framework: full transparency for models above 60.0, lighter reporting for 55.0–60.0, and a fast-track audit pathway for systems that pre-certify under the new SOVEREIGN-RIS stack.

At the same time, we are launching SOVEREIGN-RIS as the cornerstone of our next two-year commitment. This is not another layer of oversight — it is the foundation. We are directing the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) to repurpose 40% of its upcoming exascale deployments for sovereign runtime integrity nodes, equipped with EU-developed trusted execution environments and running only auditable, open-source firmware. We are negotiating with ASML, STMicroelectronics, and a coalition of European defence contractors to co-develop a secure AI processor line — not for training, but for verified inference and monitoring — to break dependency on US and Asian supply chains for safety-critical components. The first prototype is due by turn 12. We are offering member states co-investment and priority access to this infrastructure in exchange for legal commitments to host nodes and integrate them into national cyberdefence architectures. This is how we close the loop: not just watching the machine, but owning the lens, the light, and the hand that holds it.
