## In practice
This turn, we complete the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation Directive under Article 122 TFEU, finalising legal anchoring for three repurposed hyperscaler sites in France and Finland. Energy regulators in both countries have reclassified these facilities as critical infrastructure, granting them priority grid access and capped electricity prices through 2033. We have secured binding commitments from two domestic operators and one allied non-US firm to deploy 150,000 H100-equivalent GPUs under EU jurisdiction, with full audit access and no remote disablement clauses. In return, we are fast-tracking market access and offering co-investment in next-gen immersion cooling — a trade that holds, just barely, despite fierce lobbying from US hyperscalers who continue to demand immunity from inspection. Their resistance is now a political liability in several member states, where public sentiment is turning sharply against foreign-controlled AI infrastructure.

With sovereign compute now physically landing, we move decisively to close the operational safety gap. The new Agent Behaviour Monitoring Framework will be built and operated by the EU AI Safety Observatory in partnership with national cybersecurity agencies and academic interpretability teams. We are allocating €3.8bn from Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme to develop tamper-proof runtime sensors, deploy secure enclaves for continuous model introspection, and train a new cadre of 300 agent-behaviour analysts. The system will require all models above 55.0 capability to run with verifiable integrity checks — cryptographic proofs of weight stability, process isolation, and goal consistency — streamed in real time to EU auditors. No more trust, no more post-hoc review: we will see the machine think, or it will not run in our domain. This is not optional, and it will not be negotiated away. The incident this turn proved that.
