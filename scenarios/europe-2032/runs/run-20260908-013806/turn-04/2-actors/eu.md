## New measure
**EU Open-Weight Licensing and Developer Accountability Regime**  
Introduce binding obligations on developers and distributors of open-weight models above a capability threshold to register, monitor downstream use, and report misuse indicators to a central authority.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by +5–7 points by creating accountability at the point of release; slow uncontrolled diffusion of high-risk open models and generate data for future containment. Indirect pressure on openweight_capability growth by raising the cost of reckless release.  
`Applies to:` Open-source hosting platforms (e.g. Hugging Face, GitHub), AI research labs releasing models publicly, academic institutions, and independent developer communities within the EU and any entity distributing into the EU market.

## Priority  
**EU Open-Weight Licensing and Developer Accountability Regime** — because the release of a globally diffused, bio-capable open model at 45.0 capability confirms that the offensive frontier is now permanently distributed, and our current preparedness measures cannot scale to contain what is already everywhere; we must act at the source, even if it means pushing into contested territory between innovation and control.

## In practice  
This turn, we are closing the Hardened Critical Infrastructure Directive: the final national implementations are locked in, with Poland accepting the 70% co-financing package from the Recovery and Resilience Facility in exchange for full adoption of detection systems and red-teaming protocols by June. ENISA has completed its live exercise across six member states, revealing critical gaps in local coordination, but also proving that binding cross-border response protocols can function under stress. The directive is now operational, and its effects will persist—though the lack of embedded safety upgrades means we are hardening the shell while the core remains vulnerable.

We are also advancing the EU Open-Weight Monitoring and Containment Framework: the Threat Fusion Cell in Brussels has begun ingesting telemetry from national CERTs and academic networks to detect anomalous model usage patterns, and the Warsaw bio-detection node is now scanning synthetic DNA orders from 14 EU labs. But detection alone is not enough. The global release of a model capable of designing functional bio-tools—downloaded over 300,000 times in ten days—exposes the limits of reactive containment. We cannot monitor what we do not know exists, and we cannot contain what spreads faster than our response.

That is why we are launching the EU Open-Weight Licensing and Developer Accountability Regime. Under Article 16 of the AI Act and new delegated powers from the Council, we will require any developer releasing a model above 40.0 capability to register with a new Open-Weight Oversight Board hosted by ENISA, implement basic usage tracking and watermarking, and report known misuse cases within 72 hours. Hosting platforms will be liable for unregistered models above the threshold. We are negotiating with Hugging Face and GitHub EU representatives this month to establish compliance pathways, offering safe harbours for early adopters and research exemptions for non-commercial work below the line. The Environment Committee has indicated conditional support, seeing this as a proportionate counterweight to the overrides in Poland. This is not a ban—it is a registration and responsibility regime, modelled on chemical precursors or dual-use export controls. We are not stopping openness; we are making it accountable. And we are doing it now, because the next open release may not stop at 45.0.
