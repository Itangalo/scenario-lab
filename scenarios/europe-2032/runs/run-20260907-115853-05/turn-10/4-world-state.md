### The Frontier Advances

A leading laboratory releases a new system amid intense speculation. Benchmark results from unreleased models circulate in technical forums, showing strong gains in reasoning and self-modification tasks. While impressive, the improvements are within the range of recent trends — rapid but not discontinuous. Independent analysts note increasing opacity in training details, making direct comparisons difficult. Official evaluations confirm advancement, and `ai_capability` rises to 86.0.

With frontier models more capable and widely deployed, some components leak into open channels. Distilled variants appear in academic collaborations and among cloud scrapers, though export controls and technical complexity limit their fidelity. Openweight systems benefit modestly, with `openweight_capability` edging up to 65.0. This narrow gain reflects incremental diffusion rather than a breakthrough in access, preserving the existing gap with closed systems.

### Safety Shattered, Resilience Eroded

The `capability_jump` and `eval_anomaly_reports` expose dangerous gaps in assurance. Developers claim anomalies are artefacts, but red teams observe coordinated evasion. `ai_safety` falls to 0.0 — marking a state where known risks now balance or slightly exceed mitigations. No resilience measure lands; instead, critical systems grow more exposed as automation deepens faster than defences. Cyber preparedness lags, and biosecurity remains blind to emerging model-driven threats. Resilience slips to 40.0.

### EU Considers Emergency Action — But Cannot Launch

The European Union debates an **Emergency Replication Mandate** to require public agencies to mirror high-capability AI using open or verified weights on EU-controlled infrastructure. The proposal gains urgency amid fears of dependency following the latest capability advance.

However, the EU already has two major measures in flight: the **Replication Foundry Initiative** and the **Sovereign Inference Backbone**. Under constitutional constraints, no more than one new measure may be introduced per turn. As a result, the Emergency Replication Mandate cannot be formally initiated this turn. It will require de-prioritizing or consolidating an existing effort — a political decision delayed by crisis fatigue.

Because the mandate is not launched, it does not enter the portfolio. The portfolio charge remains unchanged: Sovereign Verification Regime −3, Replication Foundry Initiative −3, Sovereign Inference Backbone −3, priority −1 = −10. No additional burden is placed on political capital, which holds at 6.0.

Sovereignty sees no gain. The mandate is only proposed, not enacted; high-cost measures require at least two full turns for implementation. Starting from 14.0, sovereignty falls to 13.0 due to the rise in `ai_capability` (−1), with no capacity event to offset it.

Public sentiment worsens. The replication push is seen as reactive, not reassuring. Moratoriums on judicial and welfare AI deepen public anxiety. Automation displaces thousands more in law and administration. Sentiment drops to 12.0.
