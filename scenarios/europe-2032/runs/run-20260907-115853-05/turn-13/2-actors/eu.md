## New measure

**Measure:** Bio-Risk Red Line and Rapid Screening Mandate  
This measure establishes a legally binding, EU-wide prohibition on the use of AI for synthetic pathogen design above a defined risk threshold, and mandates real-time screening of all high-capability AI systems used in life sciences for dual-use bio-risk signatures.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 5–7 points by blocking the most dangerous misuse pathways; slow openweight proliferation in bioengineering by raising detection and enforcement costs; signal credible red lines to developers and member states  
`Applies to:` EU member states, public and private research institutions, AI developers operating in or serving the EU market

## Priority

**Priority:** Bio-Risk Red Line and Rapid Screening Mandate  
This is now our top priority because the **bio_uplift_findings** represent a categorical shift: we are no longer facing theoretical risk, but demonstrated capability within reach of non-experts. With `ai_safety` at 0.0 and openweight models at 75.0, the bio gate is open. We must act before the first incident — and we must act with law, not just with tooling. Sovereign assurance is necessary, but it is not sufficient: we must also draw a line.

## In practice

We are treating the bio_uplift_findings as a wake-up call that cannot be met with research alone. The European Commission will draft a new AI Act amendment under Article 5a, introducing a prohibition on AI-assisted design of pathogens with pandemic potential, defined by a risk matrix co-developed with the ECDC, EMA, and independent biosafety experts. The regulation will enter the ordinary legislative procedure immediately, fast-tracked through the Internal Market and Environment Council formations, with a target adoption date by March 2023 (turn 14). Enforcement will be tied to the Digital Services Act and AI Act compliance mechanisms: any platform or model provider enabling such design will face deplatforming, fines, and loss of access to EU compute.

Simultaneously, we are mandating that all AI systems above capability level 70 — whether open or closed, public or private — used in life sciences must integrate real-time bio-risk screening modules developed by the EAAD+ and the Replication Foundry network. These modules will flag and log high-risk queries, with alerts routed automatically to national biosecurity authorities and the ECDC. Funding for deployment comes from the emergency reserve of the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe, redirected from stalled adoption pilots. We are offering German and Polish authorities a carve-out for academic freedom in exchange for full audit access and on-site monitoring — a compromise to break the deadlock, not a surrender.

The **Sovereign Assurance Acceleration** programme continues, with mobile audit teams now expanding to Warsaw and Berlin to overcome the current bottlenecks. We are leveraging the emergent **AI Refugee Network** by establishing a formal, encrypted liaison channel with its core nodes, offering legal protection under the EU Whistleblower Directive in exchange for verified data on unlogged model coordination. This is not endorsement — it is triage. We are building eyes, yes — but we are also building walls.
