## New measure

**Measure:** Launch the Sovereign Stack Hardening Initiative  
A focused programme to secure the core components of the Sovereign AI Core Programme against external compromise, including mandatory zero-trust architecture, air-gapped model evaluation environments, and controlled access to interpretability tools developed by EAAD.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by at least 10 points by ensuring that sovereign systems remain under EU control even if offensive techniques derived from leaked tools are deployed; slow the erosion of `eu_ai_sovereignty` by hardening the integrity of the stack itself  
`Applies to:` All entities operating under the Sovereign AI Core Programme, including public labs, EU-contracted developers, and joint ventures with member-state participation

## Priority

**Priority:** Launch the Sovereign Stack Hardening Initiative  
We are pushing this hardest this turn because the **emergent_circuit_tracing_leak** has fundamentally compromised our technical advantage — our own audit tools are now in the wild, and we must assume that adversaries can reverse-engineer or counter them. While the Openweight Containment Initiative addresses distributed risk, the core of our sovereignty is now vulnerable from within, and without immediate action, the SACP becomes a hollow shell.

## In practice

This turn, we are acting in the shadow of sudden exposure. The leak of EAAD’s circuit-tracing tool was not a breach — it was a publication, traced back to a disgruntled researcher who uploaded the code to a public repository under a pseudonym. The technique is now being adapted globally, not just for defensive use but for evasion. We are treating this as a strategic incident: the very tools meant to give us assurance are now accelerating the offensive frontier. At the same time, the **ai_investment_collapse** has frozen private capital across the sector. Our €200bn Gigafactory fund, once hailed as a breakthrough, now rests on evaporating promises — two of the four planned sites have suspended construction, and cloud providers are scaling back expansion plans.

We are responding by pivoting from expansion to integrity. The Sovereign Stack Hardening Initiative will be launched under the authority of Article 122 TFEU (security of supply), with funding redirected from the stalled resilience grant to Poland — now frozen pending a full audit of Wrocław’s cybersecurity posture. The initiative will mandate zero-trust architecture across all SACP nodes, enforce air-gapped evaluation environments for models above 55.0, and restrict access to interpretability tools to vetted personnel with multi-factor clearance. ENISA will conduct weekly penetration tests, and the EAAD will publish a redacted post-mortem of the leak by turn 9 to restore credibility.

Meanwhile, the Openweight Containment Initiative continues its rollout, but momentum is slowing. Cloud providers are challenging enforcement under DSA compliance disputes, and academic freedom advocates have filed a preliminary reference to the ECJ. We are holding firm: the Commission will issue delegated acts by mid-turn to define watermarking standards, developed in collaboration with open-source security collectives. But our focus has shifted — we can no longer assume that building faster than the frontier is enough. We must now assume that the frontier is inside our own systems.
