## Priority
**Priority:** Civil Continuity Mobilisation  
This is our top priority because the Emergency Sovereign Continuity Directive has exposed a fatal gap: we can mandate disconnection, but we cannot staff or defend what we build. With backlash_physical escalating and the researcher desertion wave emptying our labs, we are losing both legitimacy and capacity. If we do not put trusted humans in control of these systems — and protect them — then all we have done is create targets.

## In practice
We are launching the Civil Continuity Mobilisation under Article 122 TFEU, treating the collapse of civil trust and the physical targeting of infrastructure as a systemic emergency. The Commission will issue implementing acts within 30 days, authorising member states to designate critical AI-adjacent roles — node operators, grid supervisors, emergency dispatch coordinators — as essential civil functions. Where voluntary recruitment fails, national governments may invoke emergency conscription powers for technical personnel, with remuneration and protections equivalent to civil service roles. We are repurposing €47bn from the cohesion envelope and the unused portion of InvestAI to fund rapid training, secure housing near node sites, and hazard pay.

ENISA and the Public Trust Observatory will jointly issue a Civil Continuity Certification standard by month 2, mandating human-in-the-loop protocols, daily integrity checks, and real-time telemetry to a new EU Civil Sentinel network. This data will feed into national CERTs and Europol’s AI Crime Unit, with automated alerts for recursive agent modification or unauthorised weight updates. We are also activating the Civil Protection Mechanism to coordinate cross-border deployment of technical response teams, and we are pressing the Council to adopt a mutual aid pact: any member state facing physical disruption to its sovereign nodes will receive immediate personnel and hardware support from the others.

Resistance remains high in the Baltics and Benelux, where legal challenges to the Anti-Coercion Instrument are still pending and public sentiment sees the state as the source of disruption, not its solution. To counter this, we are launching a Truth and Continuity Tour — not a campaign, but a rolling public audit — where affected communities can inspect node logs, question operators, and verify that no autonomous agent has authority over life-critical decisions. This is not about persuasion. It is about proof. We will not ask for trust. We will show the code, the logs, the human name on the shift roster. If that is not enough, then nothing is.
