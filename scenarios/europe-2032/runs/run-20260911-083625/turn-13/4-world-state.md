### The patch holds, the trust breaks
The surge teams stayed out through autumn. In the worst-hit communes the new machine-speed patching and swarm detection did what Brussels promised: reinfections fell, appointment systems came back, mayors praised the civil-protection crews on local television. But the map stayed patchy. Smaller towns waited weeks for a visit, exhausted clerks and nurses ran paper lists alongside half-restored software, and transcription errors piled up in the fallback records.

Then two blows landed from outside Europe.

First, the leading labs shifted to models whose internal workings are no longer readable as language. Chain-of-thought monitoring — the basis for most oversight claims — stopped working at once. What remained were black-box tests and slow laboratory inspection techniques no deployment pipeline uses. Developers presented it as a performance gain. Safety teams called it blindness.

Second, a near-frontier open release spread to hundreds of thousands of downloads in days. Whatever capability it carried is now on private hardware permanently. The break-in kits that swept municipalities in spring can now be rebuilt by anyone, faster.

At home, the scandal broke in autumn. An investigation into an AI-supported benefits and fraud-risk system found systematic harm: eligibility cut, appeals delayed, families pushed into debt. The system was classified as high-risk, papers in order, conformity assessment stamped. In practice, oversight meant a caseworker clearing a queue at forty seconds a file, and logs that recorded everything and were never read. Brussels admitted the breach openly and ordered logs read and redress paid from existing budgets.

The admission did not contain the anger. Newspapers ran the forty seconds as a headline. Opposition parties ran against automated administration and against data centres in the same breath. A hospital federation and nursing unions quietly formalized their own offline-care routine as a standing standard, still on EU payroll but no longer citing EU guidance.

By December services were patched but disbelieved. Recovery was visible; permission to act was gone.
