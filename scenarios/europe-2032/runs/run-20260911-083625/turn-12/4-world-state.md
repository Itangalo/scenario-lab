### The sweep and the patch
Winter turned into a municipal ransomware wave. Automated break-in kits, assembled in hours from widely shared tooling, swept across communes, hospitals and a handful of utilities. Screens went dark in registries, appointment systems and dispatch desks. Recovery took days where backups held, weeks where they did not.

Brussels had almost nothing left to spend. The fallback care corps finishing its mandate became the triage line again — paper forms, offline lists, nurses and town clerks holding queues together. Into that gap came a new defensive toolkit developed outside Europe: patching that moves at machine speed and detection that spots coordinated swarms rather than single signatures. The Union reprogrammed leftover health and digital funds to push it to the worst-hit towns first, with shared recovery teams under civil protection coordination.

It helped, visibly. Reinfected machines dropped where the tooling landed, and mayors who had been openly improvising their own offline care routines credited the surge teams. But coverage was patchy, staff exhausted, and errors in the degraded fallback systems kept rising.

### Jobs and machines
At the same time, entry-level losses hardened in coding, standardized analysis and first-line support. Firms did not rehire juniors; they kept the automated drafts and a thinner senior layer. The broader collapse once forecast did not arrive, but in the affected offices the change felt permanent. Coverage of layoffs sat beside coverage of outages, deepening mistrust.

### The Hague pressed
Washington then tightened its grip on lithography. Under threat to its American supply chain, the Dutch supplier was forced to cut servicing and exports of older chip-making equipment as well as leading-edge machines, widening the ban to ordinary-chip customers. The Hague protested, Brussels shelved retaliation it could not fund, and factory sites stayed warm but unbuilt. Dependence, already daily in clinics, now reached the industrial core.

By June services were still up, but thinner, slower, and openly borrowed.
