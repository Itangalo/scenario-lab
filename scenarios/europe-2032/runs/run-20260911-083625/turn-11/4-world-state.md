### Holding the line without a vote
July to December 2031 was a long repair shift. With no new money voted, the Union ran its single funded instrument — the fallback care and bio-response corps — as the operating line for everything.

Repair teams stayed embedded in clinics and communes. Paper triage, offline backups and dispatch workarounds became routine rather than emergency improvisation. Sequencing and wastewater sentinels continued feeding case-finding after the spring deliberate pathogen release, and containment held without a second wave. Overtime for nurses and municipal staff was paid from reprogrammed health and digital funds.

Ministries and hospitals stayed on the cleared list of European-hosted models, with scarce inference rationed to triage, dispatch and lab work. It worked after a fashion: services degraded but did not stop. Mayors and nursing unions, paid and publicly credited, defended the arrangement.

But the costs compounded. The fallback systems were slower and more error-prone than the cut-off foreign frontier services. Queues lengthened, administrative errors rose, and the new generation of intrusion-automation tools circulating widely kept municipal IT staff in permanent incident response. Factory sites were kept warm but not built, and retaliation over chip-tool curbs stayed shelved.

By December exhaustion was the dominant mood. The corps held continuity together, yet dependence was now visible in daily life, and trust in official information remained low. Brussels had avoided a second front it could not fund, but it had also spent its last political margin doing so.
