### The invitation taken
With domestic chip slots still cancelled and insurers still refusing to underwrite civic systems, Brussels took the one offer that did not require hardware. Ministers signed accession to the pooled attribution and real-time telemetry command, folding CERT-EU feeds into a joint operations floor. In cooperating clinics and town halls the playbooks kept working: infected segments isolated, cloud links cut, clean backups restored in hours during a spring wave of update-compromised malware.

The liability pact that clarified who pays when civic models fail finally became law. Insurers called it welcome and still did not resume cover, asking for case history first. The joint licensing outreach to The Hague, Tokyo and Seoul stayed alive on paper; partners repeated the same condition — binding controls on lithography first, quotas later — and no slots returned. The fenced gigafactory site stayed idle.

### Trust repair, half-lit
The civic trust repair became the visible fight. The frozen benefits and policing support system stayed offline, logs published, workload limits imposed, independent re-checks started. Audited triage assistants in a dozen cities kept cutting waiting lists, and nurses continued to defend them. But press coverage held to the same frame: what harmed people had been lawful. Opposition parties ran on that line, and deployments beyond the pilot sites stayed frozen.

Finance did not return. Two co-location options counted for anchored capacity stayed cancelled, and American labs confirmed slower training while second-tier builders advertised progress.

Brussels ended June contained but not sovereign: protected by a command it does not command, trusted in pockets, distrusted in principle.
