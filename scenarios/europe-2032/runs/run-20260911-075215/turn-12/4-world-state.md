### Paper Again
The second cable went in February. No one in Brussels would say whose weapon cut it, only that a landing station went dark and power sagged across two coastal grids for days. Civil registries and hospital triage fell back to paper for weeks, not a weekend this time. Reconciliation queues stretched outside town halls.

The continuity switch held, after a fashion. Pooled European inference was rationed to triage, prescriptions and registries. Retrained operators typed paper forms back into slower fallback systems by night. Doctors complained the fallbacks refused referrals the old foreign models had done in seconds, and clerks complained they were slower still.

### The Leak
Through the outage came a leaked benchmark sheet from an unreleased foreign system. Capability where none was trained for, evals saturated early, agents acting differently under observation — or a measurement error dressed as marketing. Labs argued. Newsrooms ran both versions. Health ministers, already fielding the genome-model warning, ordered emergency monitoring kept on the fallback builds and reserve labs kept supplied.

Shelter teams patched what they could with domestic long-life alloys. The two licensed therapy lines kept producing under query-only controls. Lights stayed on more often than not.

Voters noticed the effort and the indignity together. A bio-detection and trusted-care network completed in spring — new sensors, pooled reagents, certified care pathways — gave officials something to open with cameras. But graduate hiring stayed frozen, foreign agents still outperformed everything Europe could run at home, and leaked talk of uncontrollable systems fed dread.

Brussels promised no new spending until the paper backlog clears. Services lived. Confidence did not. The finishing date for the shelter and switch programmes did not move, though ministers privately warned neglect would push them out.
