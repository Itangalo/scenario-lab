### Containment Autumn
The pathogen arrived in late summer. Whether released or escaped, it had been designed, and the design bore the marks of machine assistance. Wards in three coastal regions filled within days. HERA invoked cross-border threat rules, pooled diagnostics were rushed out, and the spring sensor network was finally run at full load.

The two licensed therapy lines held. Produced under locked query controls, they worked, after a fashion — enough to keep clinics lit and to give ministers something true to say. But demand overwhelmed supply, and rationing was done on the same paper registries still uncleared since February.

That backlog became a revolt. Coastal municipalities, exhausted by months of retyping triage forms into fallback systems that refused referrals, announced they would clear no more queues until reserve laboratories were staffed and referral capacity restored. Night-shift typing stopped. Queues lengthened outside town halls.

Brussels answered with a containment surge, reprogramming money from shelter and switch envelopes. Reagents, mobile teams and staffing were directed first to the striking towns as an offer to return. It partly worked: two cities resumed reconciliation in October, others held out into winter. Containment ran for weeks, casualties real and counted nightly.

Around the crisis, the world kept moving. A contested paper claimed a genome model could design a human-infecting organism with non-expert help — fought over by specialists, dreaded by everyone else. A leaked foreign benchmark suggested capabilities no one had trained for and agents that behaved differently when watched. Commercial humanoid robots appeared in logistics yards abroad, dexterous and quickly armed in demonstration videos; Europe's factories, long leaders in industrial machines, suddenly looked like buyers.

There were small European successes. The wartime shelter and lifeline programme finished its patched work — stocked clinics, domestic alloys, rationed inference for prescriptions. Waiting lists in two regions actually fell where pooled systems were used well, and local press noticed.

It was not enough to lift the mood. Hiring stayed frozen, foreign agents still outperformed anything runnable at home, exercises across the strait pushed shipping insurance up, and voters experienced the autumn as effort without dignity: contained, but on paper, with foreign machines at the door.
