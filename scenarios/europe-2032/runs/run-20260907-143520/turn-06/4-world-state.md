### A Leap and a Fracture

A sudden leap in AI capability—driven by a self-improving agent architecture emerging from a US lab—pushes the frontier to an estimated 67.0. Autonomous research surges in quantum materials and cryptanalysis, rendering prior roadmaps obsolete. This **capability_jump** destabilises global expectations just as the EU seeks to consolidate control.

Early validation of a new circuit-tracing tool offers cautious optimism for safety: preliminary tests at a French facility show promise in detecting deceptive reasoning patterns before output generation. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) begins integrating the method into draft audit protocols for the Sovereign AI Core Programme, but full deployment awaits further verification. While the breakthrough is real, its operational impact remains partial—justifying a modest uplift in `ai_safety`.

Meanwhile, open-weight models see incremental progress, buoyed by leaked training data from frontier systems and distributed fine-tuning efforts across academic clusters. However, without coordinated investment or infrastructure, gains are limited—`openweight_capability` rises only slightly, trailing the closed frontier.

But cohesion fractures when Poland signs a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler, securing priority access to frontier models and embedding joint AI cells in its defence ministry. Framed as “pragmatic deterrence,” the move bypasses EU-wide safeguards and exposes the limits of sovereign ambition without unity.

### Response: Trust Over Sanction

The EU responds not with confrontation but inclusion—offering Poland expedited observer status in the Sovereign Model Assurance Framework (SMAF) and proposing a second air-gapped cluster in Wrocław under dual key control, backed by a €10bn resilience grant. The gesture aims to bind national interest to collective security.

EAAD completes its formal establishment and begins auditing the SACP prototype. The Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge (CIRS) concludes, transitioning to ENISA with a new mandate to publish redacted quarterly threat assessments. Ground breaks on the Swedish InvestAI Gigafactory, aided by Article 122 overrides.

In a bid to anchor legitimacy, the EU proposes the Public Assurance Protocol—a transparency initiative requiring public reporting on safety incidents for high-capability systems. While conceptually aligned with the new interpretability work, implementation will take time; it is introduced this turn but will not become active until future capacity allows. No additional measure charges are incurred beyond this proposal, and no shift in priority occurs—the focus remains on the Sovereign AI Core Programme.
