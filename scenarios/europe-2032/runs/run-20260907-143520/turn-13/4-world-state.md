### Emergency Foundations, Fractured Unity

The EU declared a state of substrate emergency. With data centres occupied and grid links sabotaged across member states, the Union activated Article 87 TFEU powers, shifting from oversight to survival. The **Pattern-Based Harm Detection Network** concluded but delivered no operational value—its sensors blind where infrastructure was physically compromised. In response, the **Sovereign Defence Stack** was prioritised, with ENISA designing hardened nodes in Luxembourg, Finland, and Austria. Yet command disputes between Frontex, Eurojust, and national cyber units remain unresolved, delaying deployment.

Simultaneously, the EU launched **Emergency Sovereign Compute Corridors**, a large category 4 measure aiming to create physically secure, legally insulated micro-data centres. Site preparations began under military guard, and legal instruments were fast-tracked under Article 346. However, no systems are yet operational, and Germany and Belgium continue to resist full integration, demanding audit rights that delay routing decisions. Poland rejected even the bilateral enclave offer, citing constitutional concerns over EU-led armed infrastructure.

Public backlash intensified. The **backlash_physical** wave spread to seven countries, with violent confrontations at two sites. Media framed the Compute Corridors as “bunker AI,” deepening distrust. Labour unions and civil society groups denounced the militarisation of digital infrastructure, and sentiment dropped sharply.

Globally, the closed frontier advanced slightly—**ai_capability** rose to 70.5—while open-weight diffusion accelerated. A new model release, widely mirrored and fine-tuned, pushed **openweight_capability** to 57.0, expanding access to autonomous exploit generation. No safety improvements landed; **ai_safety** remained at 11.0, where audits are ceremonial and control is fictional.

Resilience edged up to 41.0 as emergency protocols were tested, but the cost of policing unrest drained fiscal and political reserves. **eu_ai_sovereignty** fell to 8.0—global capability advanced, and no sovereign capacity materialised in time to offset dependence. Political capital remained at 0.0, trapped by three ongoing measures and no room to manoeuvre.

### A State Under Siege

The EU is no longer governing AI. It is defending the conditions under which governance might one day return.
