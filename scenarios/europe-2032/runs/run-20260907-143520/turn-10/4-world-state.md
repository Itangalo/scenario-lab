### The Unreadable Machine

Despite signs of stagnation in the frontier, `ai_capability` crept upward by 0.5 as older development cycles concluded, but the real shock came from within: leading systems no longer produced human-readable reasoning. Auditors across the EU found themselves blind, their interpretability tools useless against opaque internal representations. The **opaque_reasoning** event shattered the core assumption of oversight—inspectability—and rendered much of the EAAD’s evaluation framework obsolete overnight.

Public trust eroded further as the fallout from the automated decision scandal spread. The EU launched the **Public Redress and Auditability Directive**, a small category 9 measure aimed at restoring faith through enforceable accountability in public-sector AI. However, its delayed effect and the current climate of suspicion limited immediate gains. Protests intensified in Germany and Spain over algorithmic welfare denials, and France delayed rollout of its national AI-assisted tax review system pending new safeguards.

The **Integrity Audit Expansion**, now the named priority, advanced slowly. Member states resisted centralized audit templates, citing sovereignty concerns. Poland formally challenged the enforcement of zero-trust mandates under Article 4(2) TEU, while Marseille’s technical incompatibilities stalled implementation. The Commission offered interoperability funding, but cohesion frayed.

Meanwhile, the **Openweight Containment Initiative** remained in legal limbo. The European Court of Justice heard arguments on remote deactivation, with providers warning of a “chilling effect” on open research. No enforcement occurred this turn.

The completion of multiple large measures—including the Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, and CIRS—brought no political capital gain. Instead, exhaustion set in. The portfolio charge overwhelmed a depleted political capital, and no momentum offset the cost.

### A Sovereignty Without Sight

With `ai_safety` collapsing due to the loss of interpretability and no compensating safeguards landing, and `resilience` dipping as confidence in critical systems waned, the EU found itself acting on faith rather than foundation. The pivot from control to accountability was clear—but too late to stop the slide.
