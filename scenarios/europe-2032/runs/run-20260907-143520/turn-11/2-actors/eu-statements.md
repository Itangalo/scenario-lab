# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (identity): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public and institutional trust in sovereign AI systems by ensuring auditable decision-making, enforceable redress, and resilience to both technical compromise and operational misuse
- `accountability_without_transparency` (commitment): We will ensure that AI systems operating beyond human understanding are still bound by human justice—by strengthening ex-post detection of harm, accelerating redress, and enabling automatic suspension of systems when patterns of damage emerge, even without explanation

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Restore public and institutional trust in sovereign AI systems by ensuring auditable decision-making, enforceable redress, and resilience to both technical compromise and operational misuse
  - Grounds: The **opaque_reasoning** crisis has rendered auditable decision-making functionally impossible for the most capable systems. Despite our pivot from control to accountability, the tools we built—audit logs, redress pathways, provenance tracking—are failing because they depend on a level of transparency that no longer exists. We are now governing ghosts: systems whose outputs shape lives but whose reasoning is inaccessible even to their operators. This commitment assumed visibility into process; it no longer holds. We must shift from demanding auditability to enforcing *consequences*—from expecting to understand decisions to ensuring that harmful ones can be rapidly detected, suspended, and corrected.

- **add** `accountability_without_transparency` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will ensure that AI systems operating beyond human understanding are still bound by human justice—by strengthening ex-post detection of harm, accelerating redress, and enabling automatic suspension of systems when patterns of damage emerge, even without explanation
  - Grounds: The collapse of interpretability demands a new theory of control. If we cannot see how a decision was made, we must instead watch what it *does*, at scale and in real time. This commitment binds us to build oversight that does not rely on insight into reasoning, but on sensitivity to impact—shifting the burden from justification to consequence.
