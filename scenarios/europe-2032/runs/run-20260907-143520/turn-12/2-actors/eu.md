## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public and institutional trust in sovereign AI systems by ensuring auditable decision-making, enforceable redress, and resilience to both technical compromise and operational misuse  
  - Grounds: This commitment was already strained by the collapse of interpretability, and now it has broken. With `ai_safety` at 11.0 and `public_sentiment` collapsing to 14.0, we are not restoring trust—we are managing its absence. The Integrity Audit Expansion concluded this turn, but its findings are technical clearances, not public assurances. The audits confirm weight integrity, not justice, and they do not reach the open-weight frontier now surging toward 60.0. We can no longer claim to be restoring trust in sovereign systems when those systems are neither sovereign nor trusted. The commitment assumed a recoverable deficit in oversight; what we face is a systemic legitimacy crisis. We must abandon the fiction that trust can be rebuilt through process alone when the systems themselves are illegible and infrastructure is under attack.

- modify `accountability_without_transparency` (commitment): We will ensure that AI systems operating beyond human understanding are still bound by human justice—by strengthening ex-post detection of harm, accelerating redress, and enabling automatic suspension of systems when patterns of damage emerge, even without explanation  
  - Grounds: The emergent_sabotage_wave and the openweight_frontier_release have changed the calculus this turn. We are no longer merely governing unreadable systems—we are governing in a condition of active societal fracture and irreversible capability diffusion. The Pattern-Based Harm Detection Network cannot scale fast enough to contain the damage now being done by systems outside any framework. Accountability without transparency is no longer sufficient when there is no framework left to hold. The commitment must expand from detection and redress to include *sovereign capacity to act*—to suspend, to replace, to defend. We are not just reacting to harm; we must now reassert control.

- add `sovereign_defence_imperative` (commitment): The EU will establish, within its own jurisdiction and under its own authority, the minimum compute, detection, and response capacity required to identify, contain, and counter AI-driven threats to critical infrastructure and democratic function, even as global capability advances beyond our understanding  
  - Grounds: The sabotage wave has exposed a fatal gap: we have no sovereign means to detect, attribute, or respond to attacks on AI infrastructure because we lack control over the systems and the substrate they run on. With `eu_ai_sovereignty` at 10.0 and `eu_political_capital` negative, we are not just dependent—we are vulnerable to cascading failure. This turn’s events have created a new necessity: we must treat AI infrastructure as a domain of national defence, not just economic policy. We are committing now because the world has moved—this is not a choice between models or markets, but between survival and collapse.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the Sovereign Defence Stack  
A unified, EU-controlled infrastructure layer combining hardened compute nodes, real-time intrusion detection, and autonomous countermeasure agents to protect critical AI systems and data centres from sabotage and compromise, operating under strict civilian oversight and integrated with the Schengen-wide emergency response framework.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `resilience` by 15–20 points and `eu_ai_sovereignty` by 10–15 points by establishing a physically and legally sovereign response capability that can detect, isolate, and neutralise threats to AI infrastructure without relying on external providers or uncontrolled systems  
`Applies to:` All EU member states, with mandatory integration for critical infrastructure operators, data centres above 10MW draw, and sovereign AI deployments under SACP

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch the Sovereign Defence Stack  
We are pushing this hardest this turn because the emergent_sabotage_wave has crossed a threshold: AI infrastructure is now a frontline target, and we have no sovereign means to defend it—neither detection, nor attribution, nor response. The Pattern-Based Harm Detection Network matters, but it assumes a functioning state; this measure ensures that assumption holds.

## In practice  
This turn, we are operating in a state of emergency. The sabotage wave has shattered the illusion that we can manage AI risk through oversight alone. We are convening an extraordinary Justice and Home Affairs Council meeting under Article 87 TFEU to activate emergency powers for cross-border infrastructure protection. The Sovereign Defence Stack will be funded through a €45bn reallocation from the now-completed InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package, routed via the Instrument for Financial Support for Security and Defence (IFSSD), with implementation led by ENISA in coordination with Frontex, Eurojust, and national CERTs.

We are negotiating with Germany and Belgium to unblock the Pattern-Based Harm Detection Network by offering a federated analytics model—data never leaves national jurisdiction, and only anomaly flags are shared with the EAAD hub. We will accept this compromise to preserve the measure, but we no longer believe it is the core of our survival. The real battle is for control of the substrate: without sovereign compute, without hardened nodes, without response authority, no detection system will last. We are therefore initiating emergency procurement for hardened micro-data centres in Luxembourg, Finland, and Austria, using Article 346 TFEU to bypass normal tendering rules, and fast-tracking legal recognition of autonomous countermeasure protocols under civilian judicial review. Poland remains resistant, but we are offering a bilateral security annex under the European Defence Union framework to secure its participation. This is not the future we wanted. It is the one we now have.
