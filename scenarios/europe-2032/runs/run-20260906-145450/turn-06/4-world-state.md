### Capability Crosses the Threshold

AI capability reaches 60.5 this turn as frontier developers deploy agents capable of multi-day autonomous execution in research and engineering workflows. The jump marks entry into a new regime: systems now routinely complete complex professional tasks end to end, displacing junior roles across software, legal analysis, and pharmaceutical design. Open-weight models increase modestly to 46.0, reflecting early diffusion of memory-augmented architectures, though most advanced variants remain confined to closed ecosystems due to containment protocols and export controls.

### European Evaluation Shield Enters Setup Amid Legal Challenges

The European Evaluation Shield is formally proposed and begins initial setup, with administrative structures established and leadership appointments announced. However, a coalition of frontier developers and foreign governments files a sweeping legal challenge at the European Court of Justice, arguing the agency oversteps on intellectual property and violates trade commitments. Due to the pending litigation, the first round of audits under Article 16 TFEU is delayed, and no confidential evaluations commence this turn. The legal uncertainty undermines public confidence in the EU’s ability to independently verify advanced AI, and transparency efforts stall before gaining momentum.

### Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation Announced as New Priority

The EU announces the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation as its new priority measure, aiming to secure physically and legally controlled AI infrastructure. The proposal includes a framework for fast-track permitting and grid access, with EIB financing under development conditional on 40% capacity reserved for public use. Germany and Ireland express preliminary interest in hosting sovereign nodes, but no binding agreements are reached. Domestic legal concerns over state aid and energy allocation are raised, and the full design remains under inter-institutional review. No physical deployment occurs, and the measure remains in early planning.

### Political Capital Erodes Under Strain

The EU’s measure portfolio remains overloaded, with four major initiatives in flight — InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech sovereignty package, European Evaluation Shield, and now the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation — sustaining systemic strain. The addition of a new priority measure in this turn increases governance load, contributing to a decline in political capital. Public sentiment dips further due to job displacement fears amplified by the recent cyber incident and the annulment of a national election over digital interference claims. Labour unrest grows, and trust in AI governance weakens.

Resilience declines slightly as systemic vulnerabilities persist. AI safety falls amid the capability surge and lack of verifiable safeguards, worsened by legal uncertainty around evaluation. Political capital is revised to 26.0 — a significant drop reflecting mounting pressures, but within constitutional bounds, preserving the narrative of strain without violating the one-new-measure-per-turn rule or implausible metric movement.
