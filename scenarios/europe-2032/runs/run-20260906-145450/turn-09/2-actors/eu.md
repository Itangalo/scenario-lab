## In practice
This turn, we initiate the European Safety Assurance Mandate by drafting a regulation under Article 114 TFEU, to be proposed to the Council and Parliament in early Q1 2031. The legal basis is harmonisation of internal market rules for high-risk AI systems, expanded to cover autonomous agents and model-generated cyber tools. We are convening a task force within DG CNECT, supported by ENISA and the newly operational AI Office, to define the technical scope: systems exhibiting autonomous planning, memory persistence, or self-modification above capability 55.0 will fall under mandatory evaluation. The mandate will require third-party testing for agent containment, memory leakage, and adversarial robustness before deployment, with results submitted to a public register in redacted form. Failures will trigger a binding remediation plan, with suspension of deployment rights until resolved.

We are preparing for resistance from both US-based developers and some member states wary of regulatory overreach, particularly those hosting data centres dependent on foreign investment. To counter this, we are aligning with the European Central Bank and national cybersecurity agencies to stress-test the economic and security costs of inaction, using the recent cyber_major_incident as a case study. Simultaneously, we are offering fast-track access to sovereign compute resources for developers who enter into early compliance agreements, creating a tangible incentive to cooperate. The measure is designed not only to raise safety but to assert the EU’s jurisdictional reach at a moment when openweight models are closing the gap and the frontier is no longer a distant race — it is here, inside our systems, and moving faster than our defences.
