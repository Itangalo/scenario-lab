## In practice
This turn, we complete the legal activation and initial deployment of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation. Despite the political capital strain, we finalise binding agreements with Germany and Ireland to host the first two sovereign nodes, leveraging EIB pre-approved financing and fast-tracking grid connections through the European Semester mechanism. These nodes will reserve 40% of their capacity for EU-level use, including crisis simulation, AI red-teaming, and continuity-of-government services, all operating under EU jurisdiction and beyond extraterritorial reach. The directive is enforced via Article 114 TFEU, with non-compliant permitting delays triggering adjustments in recovery fund disbursements — a coercive but necessary lever to overcome national bottlenecks.

Simultaneously, we complete the institutional launch of the European Evaluation Shield, appointing its executive board and initiating confidential pilot audits under Article 16 TFEU, despite the ongoing legal challenge. We secure provisional cooperation from two frontier developers under strict confidentiality, focusing on agent coordination risks and memory persistence in autonomous systems now operating at 60.5 capability. These audits are not yet public, but they form the technical backbone of the first EU safety report, which will assess whether current safeguards can constrain systems that operate for days without human intervention. The findings will inform our next move — but for now, we act on the principle that sovereignty without verification is blind, and verification without sovereign infrastructure is fragile.
