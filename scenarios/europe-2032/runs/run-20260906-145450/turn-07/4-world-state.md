### Frontier Access Cut, Sovereign Nodes Stand Up

The EU’s emergency sovereign compute nodes in Germany and Ireland become operational just as access to the leading frontier AI model is abruptly suspended for EU institutions. Hospitals relying on diagnostic support, ministries running crisis simulations, and research consortia face immediate disruption. The cutoff, unexplained and non-appealable, is interpreted across member states as a strategic vulnerability made real.

In response, the newly activated sovereign infrastructure — built under the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation — begins initial deployment of red-teaming exercises and continuity-of-government systems, fulfilling its core mandate in limited scope. Work continues on establishing the 40% public-use commitment through recovery fund conditionality, but binding agreements with member states are not yet finalized. National procurement rules have not been adapted, and full implementation will require several more months of coordination. This marks the first time the EU operates high-end AI systems beyond extraterritorial reach, though current capacity remains partial and reliant on phased hardware delivery.

The European Evaluation Shield completes its institutional setup and announces pilot audits, but no frontier developer has yet agreed to participate. Legal uncertainty persists, with ongoing challenges to its authority delaying cooperation. The Commission opens technical dialogues with two developers, but binding access remains contingent on unresolved governance questions. Audit design focuses on autonomous agent persistence and memory leakage — critical concerns as systems now operate for days without intervention. No public findings are released, and internal assessments remain preliminary. While institutional foundations are laid, the Shield does not yet constitute an active measure with operational impact; its activation will depend on future developer engagement and legal clarity.
