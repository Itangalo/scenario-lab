By late 2030 EU resilience split into two speeds: certified hospitals, utilities and re-imaged municipal payments on the Shield registry and pre-execution check held through autumn phishing/copycat waves with mutual-aid support, while uncertified sites lingered on paper processes.

No domestic frontier capacity was built — blockaded compute sites stayed fenced, grid hearings suspended, contractors absent, US services unavailable, private funding thin.

Brussels turned outward, negotiating aligned export licences, joint rented-compute bargaining and shared evaluation slots with middle powers holding lithography, packaging, chemicals and cable landings; first joint rentals signed in November gave certified public buyers a fallback.

Council talks on gigafactory anchoring survived and street vetoes lost some mayors, but public mood was stabilisation without restoration: trust rose slightly from a low floor, hostility to data centres unchanged amid images of diverted ambulances and chained gates.
