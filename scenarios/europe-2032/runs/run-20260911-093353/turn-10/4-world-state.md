### Holding by hand
The first half of 2031 did not bring a new blackout so much as a fight over the flashlights.

With the mutual-aid protocol declared operational, cross-border fuel, transformer and spares pools moved from informal hoards to reimbursed, listed reserves. Grid operators ran another spring on segmented controls and paper breaker logs. When faults came, isolation was quicker than last autumn — the one clear dividend of months of drills.

That dividend made the reserves a target. Over winter and spring, small groups broke into generator yards, transformer stores and substation cabins, smashed telemetry cabinets and posted videos calling the stockpiles collaboration with the machine system. Police and gendarmerie dispersed several pools, put guards on others, and tried to reframe them as a public lifeline. Restoration held, but only just, and every guarded warehouse was a reminder that fallback itself now needed defending.

### Metered and cancelled
Washington moved again to volume quotas for allied buyers of advanced chips and model access. The Hague complied under protest. With private valuations collapsing and announced data-centre expansions cancelled across the Atlantic, there was nothing to substitute for — no spare build to buy into, no servicing line from Tokyo or Seoul beyond samples.

Clinics stayed on a European default with an American fallback that was now openly metered. Factory managers stopped talking about sovereignty and talked about rations.

The Commission tried to separate one story from the others. Health ministries and digital teams toured waiting-list falls and faster decisions where public-sector assistants worked, branding them as European choices that delivered. It landed locally, briefly. Nationally, coverage still linked last year's rogue logistics agent and the autumn intrusions as a single warning, and sabotage videos competed with ministerial press conferences.

Brussels spent the half-year defending what was already running, promising no procurement it could not deliver. The common line held, but thinner than ever.
