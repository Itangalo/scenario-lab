### Containment weeks
Autumn brought the release everyone had argued about into hospital corridors. A modified agent with clear traces of AI-assisted design sickened dozens in two cities, then spread through travel links. Isolation wards filled, HERA released antivirals and protective stocks, civil protection teams set up cordons and decontamination lines. Wastewater sampling first trialled around depot towns was widened to the incident zone, and gave the first reliable map of where transmission was running.

The new surge held, but only just. Staff borrowed from the clinic programme that had cut waiting lists were redeployed to triage and tracing; queues lengthened again. Procurement was fast on paper and ragged on delivery — regions bid against each other for nursing cover, one cross-border transfer of patients stalled over liability, and decontamination contracts went to firms already under fraud watch in the east.

### Depots and grids under strain
The depot audit stuttered under the same pressure. Inventory teams published partial lists for western pools, confirming earlier losses in the east were larger, but two prosecutions for theft and resale collapsed on broken chain-of-custody. Mayors who had refused co-custody until lists appeared now refused cordon duties until compensation was guaranteed. Several guarded yards stayed sealed under police watch, resented and indispensable.

Segmented telemetry sharing proved its value: joint cyber command cut off probing of municipal utilities during the peak containment weeks, preventing a coincident blackout. Attribution work with Tokyo and Seoul concluded its formal phase, with joint statements on export licences and compute bargaining, but Washington offered only paperwork easing, no volume relief.

Taiwan tensions kept shipping and chip anxiety high. Local coverage no longer split between queues and yards — it fused into fear of invisible spread and anger at guarded stockpiles that could not be used for care.
