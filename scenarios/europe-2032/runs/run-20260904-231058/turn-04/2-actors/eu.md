## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that sovereignty means nothing if the lights go out. This turn, the Commission triggers Article 122 TFEU to fast-track the Emergency Grid Resilience Initiative, converting the emergent mandate into binding law within six months. Under the Connecting Europe Facility and reinforced by the Digital Europe Programme, we are allocating €4.2bn in emergency funding to retrofit AI-driven intrusion detection systems into high-risk nodes of the EU’s energy grid, starting with interconnectors between Germany-Poland, France-Spain, and the Nordic synchronous area. The systems will use sovereign-trained models—developed under the Frontier Access Assurance Programme and evaluated in our newly operational Sovereign Evaluation Facilities—to detect anomalous patterns and execute pre-approved countermeasures, including circuit isolation and traffic rerouting, under strict human-in-the-loop protocols.

We are negotiating with national regulators through the Energy Council to override objections on autonomy and data privacy by limiting AI action to Tier 1 incidents—those with cascading failure potential—and mandating real-time audit logs accessible to ENISA and national CSIRTs. Privacy advocates are offered a binding sunset clause: if no major incident occurs by turn 6, the autonomous response authority will lapse unless renewed by qualified majority. Meanwhile, the Tech sovereignty package continues to unlock private capital, but now with a new condition: any data centre receiving accelerated permitting must contribute 5% of its compute capacity to train and run defensive AI models for critical infrastructure. The member_state_defection is not ignored—we are responding not with confrontation, but with cohesion: this initiative binds all member states to a common standard, making unilateral deals with hyperscalers a liability, not a shortcut. We are building resilience not just in code, but in law, in wiring, and in trust.
