### Grids and Divisions  
The Emergency Grid Resilience Initiative, already underway, advances into deployment phase across critical energy infrastructure under Article 122 TFEU. €4.2bn is being drawn from the Connecting Europe Facility, with disbursements tied to national implementation milestones and procurement timelines. AI-driven intrusion detection systems are being integrated in France, Germany, and Finland, where Sovereign Evaluation Facilities are now operational. These systems will support autonomous countermeasures under human oversight, though full delegation remains limited: AI may act only on Tier 1 threats, with real-time audit logs shared across member states. A sunset clause binds continued autonomy to verified incident occurrence, addressing privacy concerns while preserving responsiveness.

### Sovereignty Under Strain  
Despite coordination, a member state finalises a bilateral agreement with a U.S. hyperscaler for preferential access to next-gen AI infrastructure, weakening the EU’s unified posture. The move, justified domestically as ensuring continuity of supply, is interpreted externally as fragmentation. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive concludes this turn, reserving 30% of frontier compute for public use—but integration delays by U.S. providers reveal implementation gaps. The broader Tech sovereignty package attracts cautious investment, though supply-chain uncertainty persists, particularly around legacy semiconductor equipment.

### Capability Creeps Forward  
AI capability advances to 59.5, driven by tighter training loops and automated research scaling. Open-weight models rise to 49.0, reflecting broader diffusion of Kimi K3 derivatives and improved tooling that lowers barriers to semi-autonomous intrusion planning. While concerning, this capability remains below the frontier. The Sovereign Evaluation Facilities Acceleration completes, expanding audit capacity across the Union, though full interpretability of high-stakes models remains out of reach. Safety holds at 35.0: oversight is active, but not yet sufficient to close the growing capability gap.

### Public Trust Holds  
Visible public-sector applications in healthcare and education sustain positive sentiment, now at 48.0. The grid initiative is framed as preventive strength, not crisis response, reinforcing institutional credibility. Labour unrest continues in logistics and customer service sectors, where AI-driven restructuring accelerates, but no broad backlash has emerged.
