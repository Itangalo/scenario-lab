## New measure  
**Frontier Access Assurance Programme**  
Establish a permanent, legally anchored EU mechanism to evaluate, monitor, and condition access to frontier AI systems developed outside the bloc, leveraging the newly secured access terms.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to +15 to ai_safety by turn 5 through independent pre-deployment evaluation and continuous monitoring of frontier models; stabilises openweight_capability growth by enabling early intervention on dangerous capabilities  
`Applies to:` Non-EU frontier developers whose systems are deployed or accessible within the EU, under the condition of compliance with EU evaluation and audit rights

## Priority  
**Frontier Access Assurance Programme** — because securing access on our terms was a breakthrough, but access without sovereign evaluation power is dependence disguised as partnership, and at 54.0 capability with safety eroding, we cannot outsource our judgment to those who built the systems.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that we now have a foothold: eu_access_secured means we are no longer passive recipients of foreign AI power. But access is not control. This turn, the Commission launches the Frontier Access Assurance Programme under Article 16 of the AI Act and the newly invoked Digital Markets Act gatekeeper provisions, establishing a dedicated unit within the European AI Office with binding authority to audit model weights, inspect training data summaries, and mandate interpretability interfaces for any frontier system operating in the EU. This is not a request — it is a condition of market access at scale. The programme will be funded with €4.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and redirected portions of the paused InvestAI capital, and will stand up three secure evaluation facilities in France, Germany, and Finland by turn 4, staffed with vetted researchers and equipped with sovereign compute reserved under the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive.

We are also advancing the Tech sovereignty package by securing binding commitments from five additional member states to designate AI Acceleration Zones, bringing the total to eight. These zones will now include mandatory local evaluation nodes for any data centre over 200 petaFLOPS, ensuring that wherever compute lands, oversight can follow. The Council’s Competitiveness formation remains wary, but the adoption_success event — visible public gains in healthcare wait times and education outcomes from AI deployed under EU rules — is shifting the narrative. We are leveraging that goodwill to de-escalate legal confrontation on the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive, offering a phased implementation for existing operators and a fast-track compliance pathway in exchange for dropping legal challenges. This is not retreat — it is consolidation. We are building the architecture of autonomy, one legal base, one zone, one audit right at a time.
