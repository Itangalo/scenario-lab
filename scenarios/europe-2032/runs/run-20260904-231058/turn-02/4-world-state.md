### Breakthrough and Backlash  
The EU capitalises on its secured frontier access, launching the Frontier Access Assurance Programme as a bold extension of sovereignty. Binding audit rights and sovereign evaluation facilities are established in France, Germany, and Finland, marking the first time the bloc exercises independent technical oversight over foreign frontier models. The programme is framed not as protectionism but as a condition of responsible deployment, leveraging market access to enforce transparency.

Public-sector AI successes—shorter hospital wait times and adaptive learning tools in schools—gain media traction, boosting public confidence. The narrative shift helps soften opposition to the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive, with major US cloud providers pausing legal challenges in exchange for phased compliance. Eight member states now host AI Acceleration Zones, embedding local evaluation nodes into high-capacity data centres.

### Rising Tensions, Rising Capability  
Despite these gains, AI capability advances to 56.0, driven by automated research pipelines and tighter simulation loops in leading labs. Open-weight models absorb the leap, reaching 44.0, with Kimi K3 derivatives now capable of autonomous cyber reconnaissance. The gap between capability and safety persists: `ai_safety` rises only to 33.0, as new oversight mechanisms are still in setup and not yet applied to active systems.

Resilience dips slightly to 36.0, as no defensive measure has yet fully deployed. The stealthy grid intrusion remains unaddressed at scale, and emergency mandates for AI-driven monitoring stall in regulatory committees. Labour unrest grows in sectors exposed to AI displacement, though not yet at crisis levels.

Public sentiment climbs to 44.0, buoyed by visible public benefits and the perception of regained control. Political capital rises to 48.0, supported by the proposal bonus for a large, timely measure and sustained by improving sentiment.
