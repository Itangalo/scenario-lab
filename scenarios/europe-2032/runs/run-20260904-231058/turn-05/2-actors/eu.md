## New measure
**Sovereign Compute Buildout Acceleration**  
Fast-track the construction and legal anchoring of EU-controlled frontier data centres by streamlining permitting, guaranteeing grid access, and mandating sovereign compute contributions from all AI infrastructure receiving public support.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`

## In practice
We are acting on the fact that access is not control. This turn, the Commission launches the Sovereign Compute Buildout Acceleration under Article 114 TFEU, converting the urgency of the moment into binding law by turn 6. We are mobilising €32bn in blended funding — drawn from the Innovation Fund, the Recovery and Resilience Facility, and the European Defence Fund — to co-invest in EU-anchored data centres that meet three conditions: compute must be physically located in the Union, legal jurisdiction must be irrevocably bound to EU law, and at least 20% of capacity must be reserved for sovereign use in crisis, research, and public service. National governments are offered fast-track approval through a new Digital Infrastructure Council, which overrides local permitting bottlenecks for projects meeting the criteria.

At the same time, we are renegotiating the terms of the Tech sovereignty package: from now on, any developer receiving accelerated permitting or grid priority must contribute 10% of their operational compute to a Sovereign Reserve Pool, audited by ENISA and available for emergency deployment under Council authorisation. The Frontier Access Assurance Programme concludes this turn with a hard lesson: even structured access from allies comes with strings, and those strings can pull. We are not rejecting cooperation — the joint_threat_response event shows its value — but we are no longer building our sovereignty on trust. The Emergency Grid Resilience Initiative continues on track, with real-time telemetry now flowing into ENISA’s new Joint Cyber Situational Centre, but we know that resilient grids mean nothing if the AI defending them can be switched off by a foreign provider. That ends now.
