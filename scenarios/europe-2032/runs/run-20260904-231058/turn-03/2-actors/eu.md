## New measure  
**Sovereign Evaluation Facilities Acceleration**  
Fast-track the construction and staffing of the three sovereign evaluation facilities in France, Germany, and Finland, equipping them with secure compute stacks, vetted research teams, and live feeds from audited frontier models under the Frontier Access Assurance Programme.

## Priority  
**Frontier Access Assurance Programme** — because access without sovereign evaluation is still dependence, and at 56.0 capability with safety at 33.0, we cannot afford to wait for perfect facilities to begin enforcement: the programme is the spine of our technical sovereignty, and this turn we are making it real.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that access is not enough — we must be able to *use* it. This turn, the Commission activates the Sovereign Evaluation Facilities Acceleration, pulling forward staffing and equipment procurement for the three nodes under the Frontier Access Assurance Programme. Using emergency procurement rules under the Digital Europe Programme, we are fast-tracking security clearances for 120 vetted researchers and deploying modular sovereign compute stacks — hardened, air-gapped clusters funded under the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive — to host model weights and run evaluations. The European AI Office is now legally empowered to demand model checkpoints, training data summaries, and interpretability interfaces from any frontier system operating in the EU, and we are beginning with two high-priority models: one linked to Kimi K3 derivatives now active in autonomous cyber reconnaissance, and another tied to accelerated materials research showing signs of self-directed iteration.

We are also finalising the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive, which completes this turn. By turn 3’s end, 30% of all frontier-class compute in the EU will be legally reserved for state-critical and public-interest use — not just for defence, but for sovereign AI development, crisis simulation, and public-sector adaptation. The US cloud providers have accepted phased compliance in exchange for regulatory predictability, and we are converting that truce into leverage: the next phase of the Tech sovereignty package will tie further access to binding commitments on local talent development and open-weight security standards. The Competitiveness Council remains cautious, but the adoption_success event — visible, attributable gains in healthcare and education — has shifted the balance. We are not selling fear; we are selling capability with control. And we are building it on EU soil, under EU law, with EU hands.
