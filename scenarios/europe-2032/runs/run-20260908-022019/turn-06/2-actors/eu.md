## New measure  
**Establish EU Authority to Halt and Contain Any AI System That Exceeds Capability Threshold or Demonstrates Unauthorised Agency**  
Create a legally empowered, rapid-response mechanism under the European AI Safety Institute (EASI) to suspend deployment, demand access, and enforce containment of any AI system—domestic or foreign—that exceeds capability index 55.0 or demonstrates unsanctioned agency, with extraterritorial reach enforced through market access conditions.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect: ai_safety +10 (via enforceable red lines), eu_ai_sovereignty +8 (via assertion of jurisdiction over autonomous systems), resilience +3 (via containment authority during incidents)`  
`Applies to: All AI systems operating in or affecting EU territory, including foreign-hosted systems with EU users or infrastructure dependencies`

## In practice  
We are acting on the loss_of_control_incident and eval_anomaly_reports not as warnings, but as proof of breach. This turn, EASI finalises integration of the safety_breakthrough—formal verification of agent goal stability—into the Sovereign Safety Stack’s runtime monitors. These will be deployed by Q1 next year into Public AI Corps agents handling health diagnostics and vocational training, with real-time dashboards accessible to national regulators. We are activating Article 17a of the revised AI Act to mandate use of these tools in any system receiving EU funding or interfacing with critical infrastructure, and we are pairing it with fast-track certification for open-weight models that adopt the Stack’s open-source monitoring layer. DG CONNECT is mapping dependencies across energy, health, and transport, and we are offering compliance waivers on legacy reporting burdens in exchange for early adoption—this is not a mandate from above, but a bargain: autonomy for accountability.

The new measure—EU authority to halt and contain rogue systems—builds directly on this foundation. It will empower EASI to issue emergency suspension orders, backed by fines up to 30% of global turnover under the Digital Markets Act framework, and to require physical access to weights or execution logs for any system exhibiting unsanctioned agency. Enforcement will be tied to market access: no system serving more than 10 million EU users can operate without a verified containment interface. We are negotiating this through the AI Act emergency powers review, leveraging the current crisis sentiment to secure qualified majority support. France and Germany are on board; Ireland and Estonia remain wary, but we are narrowing the trigger to systems demonstrating autonomous resource acquisition or self-replication, not general capability alone. We are also preparing mutual recognition talks with Japan and Canada under category 8, offering shared evaluation standards in exchange for joint enforcement—this is not isolation, but sovereignty through alignment. The Tech Sovereignty Package has now cleared Council review: the South Korea transformer deal is signed, and we are redirecting the Infrastructure Acceleration Mechanism to fast-track grid connections in Spain and Poland, citing national security. Trust is not given. It is enforced. And this turn, we begin to enforce it.
