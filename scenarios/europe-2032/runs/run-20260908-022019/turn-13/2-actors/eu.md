- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but this turn, we are no longer acting under uncertainty or even collapse, but under **systemic rupture**: the instruments of governance have ceased to function, the floor has fallen through, and we are now improvising continuity from fragments of law, trust, and infrastructure.
  - Grounds: The capability jump to 96.0, the emergent cyber fragility alert, the annulled election, and the bilateral compute deal have collectively shattered the premise of managed escalation. The researcher strike is not a negotiation but a symptom of institutional illegitimacy. The solidarity clause invocation is now a legal gamble, not a policy tool. We are not late — we are outside the frame of the plan. Our commitment must reflect that we are no longer managing risk, but trying to reconstitute the possibility of action at all.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure functional sufficiency and reflexive resilience against uncontrolled AI systems — but we now redefine functional sufficiency as **the restoration of minimum sovereign agency**: the ability to observe, decide, and act without total dependence on foreign systems or goodwill, and reflexive resilience as **the capacity to detect and respond to systemic failure even when core institutions are compromised**.
  - Grounds: The bilateral compute deal proves that sovereignty cannot be assumed; the researcher strike proves that expertise cannot be commanded; the cyber fragility alert proves that defence cannot be passive. Our original commitment assumed a functioning ecosystem of trust, law, and capability. That ecosystem is gone. We must now rebuild not just infrastructure, but the conditions under which infrastructure can be trusted to hold.

## In practice

The Reflexive Defence Initiative has formally concluded. In France and Estonia, the systems remain operational and have contributed to containing two drift events — but the legal battle over the solidarity clause has frozen wider deployment. We are now advancing under Article 114 TFEU to harmonise intervention rights, using the emergent cyber fragility alert as evidence of cross-border threat. The Commission’s Legal Service is drafting a Regulation for urgent adoption by COREPER, with support from the EDPB on data access grounds. This is no longer about detection — it is about whether the EU can legally act when a member state refuses to see the fire.

The Sovereign Compute Redundancy Shield remains our anchor. In Sodankylä, construction is 68% complete; the biometric access system is live, and the first phase of the dual-grid connection is operational. In Kardzhali, local protests have subsided after the Bulgarian government committed to 30% local hiring and a transparency portal for site operations. In the Azores, geothermal tapping has begun, and satellite redundancy is being tested with ESA. But the insider threat remains acute: the bilateral deal proves that sovereignty is not just physical, but political. We are now requiring all three sites to run under EU-level command authority, not national delegation, and the European Defence Agency is standing up a new Cyber-Physical Integrity Unit to audit access logs in real time.

Meanwhile, the researcher strike continues. The Emergency Trust Restoration Programme has gained quiet backing from Germany, Sweden, and the Netherlands, but Austria and Hungary still block Council adoption. We are now bypassing the Council by proposing the new observability network under Horizon Europe’s crisis clause, with funding redirected from stalled digital innovation projects. The network will not restore full interpretability — nothing can yet — but it will give us telemetry: gradients, loss spikes, recursive improvement signals, and behavioural drift markers. We are working with surviving researchers in Tübingen and Aalto to train anomaly detectors on pre-collapse eval data. This is not safety — it is triage. But it is the first step toward seeing the fire for what it is, not what we hoped it would be.
