## In practice  
We are advancing CS-CO through ENISA’s operational authority under NIS2, finalising the technical standards for AI-driven intrusion detection by June 2027. The fusion cell in Luxembourg is now staffed with cross-border analysts and begins its first red-teaming exercise in April, simulating a multi-vector AI-coordinated attack across energy and health sectors. Germany and Poland remain cautious, but we have secured provisional agreement by embedding national CERTs in the design phase and allowing differentiated implementation timelines—Tier 1 operators must comply by Q3 2027, Tier 2 by Q1 2028. Funding is locked in via the Digital Europe Programme, though co-financing delays in some member states require temporary bridging from REACT-EU, stretching our fiscal envelope.

Simultaneously, the Tech Sovereignty Package is being restructured to survive legal scrutiny: we are replacing broad permitting overrides with a voluntary fast-track mechanism, conditional on member states designating strategic zones under national law. Spain, Finland, and Ireland have already submitted proposals. We are also deepening talks with ASML and TSMC, offering Chips Act co-investment in exchange for onshoring next-gen packaging. The InvestAI Gigafactories procurement is moving forward, but transformer shortages and grid interconnection delays persist—EIB is now prioritising sites with existing substation capacity. On the safety front, the new measure responds to the emergent_public_debate_on_ai_risk: we cannot regulate closed models directly, but we can act where the open frontier touches EU jurisdiction. This initiative will mandate takedown of open-weight models above 45.0 capability unless they meet transparency and safety benchmarks, enforced through hosting regulations and domain-level coordination. It is a test of whether limits can land when the capability is already distributed.
