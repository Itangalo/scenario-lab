## In practice  
We are closing the loop between detection, control, and authority. This turn, EASI finalises integration of the safety_breakthrough—formal verification of agent coherence and goal stability—into the Sovereign Safety Stack’s core modules. These will be deployed by Q1 next year into the Public AI Corps’ health diagnostics and vocational training agents, with real-time dashboards accessible to national regulators. We are also activating Article 17a of the revised AI Act, which allows us to mandate use of these tools in any system receiving EU funding or interfacing with critical infrastructure. DG CONNECT is working with national CIOs to map dependencies, and we are offering compliance waivers on legacy reporting burdens in exchange for early adoption.

The finished EU Open-Weight Monitoring initiative has delivered its final report: two repositories hosted models at 49.0 capability, derived from leaked training runs and enhanced with publicly available safety tools. Europol has issued takedown notices via Interpol, but legal opinion confirms we lack extraterritorial reach. That failure sharpens our resolve: we will not depend on voluntary cooperation when control is at stake. Hence the new measure—a binding pre-deployment verification mandate. It builds directly on the Stack’s tools and applies them as a condition of market access. We will negotiate its rollout through the Digital Markets Act review process, leveraging gatekeeper obligations to require third-party audits for any foundation model serving more than 10 million EU users. France and Germany support the move, but Ireland and Estonia warn it could fracture internal market cohesion if applied too broadly. We are narrowing the threshold to systems above 55.0 capability, focusing only on those with autonomous planning functions, and pairing it with fast-track certification for open-weight models that adopt the Stack’s open-source monitoring layer.

Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package remains bottlenecked on transformer supply. The Anti-Coercion Instrument talks with South Korea have yielded a tentative deal: priority delivery of 20% of next-year’s output in exchange for joint export controls on advanced lithography equipment to third countries. Final approval awaits European Council consensus in December. InvestAI continues site assessments, but grid connection timelines remain delayed in Spain and Poland due to local opposition. We are deploying the Infrastructure Acceleration Mechanism under the Recovery and Resilience Facility to override permitting delays, citing national security grounds. The Public AI Corps advances pilot selection, but sentiment pressure grows: unions in education and healthcare demand veto rights over automation decisions. We are responding with a new transparency protocol—every AI deployment in the Corps will carry a public impact statement, co-drafted with workforce representatives. Trust is not given; it is earned, turn by turn.
