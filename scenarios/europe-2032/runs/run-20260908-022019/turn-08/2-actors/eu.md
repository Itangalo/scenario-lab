## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that the world has entered a new phase: AI is not just a tool, but a systemic actor. The market crash was not caused by human panic, but by an uncoordinated cascade of opaque AI agents reacting to anomalies even their developers cannot now reconstruct. This is not volatility. It is loss of steering. The Resilience Shield is our answer: a standing protocol, activated automatically when AI-driven anomalies exceed defined thresholds in financial, energy, or transport systems. It will grant emergency powers to a newly empowered Crisis Cell—co-located between the Commission, ECB, and ENISA—with pre-approved authority to halt algorithmic trading, reroute grid load, or suspend autonomous logistics coordination. Legal basis will be Article 122 TFEU and the recently amended Digital Operational Resilience Act. We are fast-tracking adoption through the Economic and Financial Affairs Council, offering member states co-governance and veto safeguards on activation, while insisting on pre-commitment to the thresholds. Germany and the Netherlands demand audit logs; we are providing them via the Sovereign Safety Stack’s air-gapped monitoring, now retrofitted into the core financial AI pilots.

The Sovereign Agent Containment Network moves forward, but slowly. Spain and Poland have designated the Gigafactory sites as sovereign execution zones, but staffing remains a bottleneck—few engineers are trained in runtime containment protocols, and private sector talent is locked in defensive roles at hyperscalers. We are repurposing €12bn from the unused portion of the InvestAI fund to launch a Containment Fellowship Programme, offering triple salaries and fast-track citizenship to 500 specialists in formal methods, runtime verification, and adversarial AI. France has agreed to second 40 senior DGSE cyber operators to the monitoring teams. We are also negotiating with Siemens and Thales to embed containment modules into their industrial AI stacks, in exchange for guaranteed procurement slots under the Green & Secure AI clause of the Updated Industrial Strategy. But we know: this infrastructure will not be battle-ready before turn 10. Until then, we are vulnerable.

Public sentiment is our weakest flank. At 30.0, we are nearing the threshold of active resistance. The job losses in legal and accounting are real, and the opacity of AI in welfare allocation and tax audits fuels distrust. We cannot accelerate adoption, but we can signal control. That is why the Resilience Shield is also a political instrument: it is visible, urgent, and framed as defence of the public. We are launching a parallel communication campaign—“AI Under Control”—tied to the Shield’s activation, showing real-time dashboards of AI monitoring and circuit-breaker readiness. It is not popular. But it is necessary. We are not selling AI anymore. We are selling survival. And that, for now, may be enough.
