## In practice  
We are acting on the rarest of opportunities: a visible, attributable success in public-sector AI. The deployment in Flemish healthcare reduced surgical wait times by 40% in six months, and the French education pilot cut grading delays while improving student outcomes — both used EU-developed models under strict data governance. We are now consolidating these into a Public AI Corps under the Digital Europe Programme, hosted jointly by the European Commission’s DG DIGIT and the newly empowered European Health Agency. The Corps will identify, certify, and scale proven applications across health, education, and social administration, with a mandate to ensure interoperability, auditability, and citizen redress. Funding of €12bn is drawn from REACT-EU’s unused contingency and co-financed by member states at a 70:30 EU:MS rate, with early adopters gaining priority access to Compute Union’s fast-track zones.

At the same time, we are pushing CS-CO to completion in its final turn. The Luxembourg fusion cell has produced its first joint threat assessment, and we are using it to compel Germany and Poland to finalise integration by October, leveraging the upcoming Justice and Home Affairs Council meeting. A compromise text allows national CERTs to retain operational autonomy while feeding into a central AI-driven alert system — a minimal but sufficient standard to meet NIS2’s spirit. The Tech Sovereignty Package continues to lag, but Spain’s first fast-track zone is now operational, and we are redirecting EIB financing to prioritise grid-ready sites. The Open-Weight Initiative remains legally contested, but Europol’s Joint Cybercrime Action Taskforce has begun shadow-monitoring high-capability repositories, creating de facto pressure even without takedown powers. We are not waiting for perfect authority — we are using every lever of influence to make containment real, even if the law lags.
