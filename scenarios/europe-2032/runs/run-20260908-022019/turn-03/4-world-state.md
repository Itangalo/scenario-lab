### EU Seizes a Moment of Clarity  
The EU capitalizes on a rare positive signal: the Flemish healthcare AI, using a locally developed model, slashes surgical wait times by 40%, while a French education pilot improves student outcomes and grading speed. These successes—attributed to European governance—spur a proposal for the **Public AI Corps**, a new €12bn initiative to scale trusted AI across public services. The proposal is fast-tracked for approval, with Commission and Health Agency leadership pledging joint oversight. However, full adoption and funding authorization are pending parliamentary review, and implementation cannot begin before the next turn.

Meanwhile, **Cyber Shield for Critical Operators** reaches its final turn. After intense pressure, Germany and Poland agree to a compromise: national cyber units retain control but feed anonymized threat data into a central AI-driven alert system. The system goes live, marking a modest but real step toward cross-border resilience, lifting resilience by +2.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** sees only partial progress—Spain’s fast-track zone becomes operational, but grid delays and transformer shortages stall gigafactory timelines. With no new compute online and global capability rising, eu_ai_sovereignty falls by −1. Public sentiment rises +5 due to visible benefits, reaching 43.0—still divided, but with growing support among younger demographics and public-sector workers.

The **Public AI Corps** is now proposed but not yet implemented, preserving the limit of one new measure per turn. Its effects will begin in the following turn if approved. With **Cyber Shield** concluding and **Public AI Corps** not yet active, the portfolio charge remains consistent with prior commitments.
