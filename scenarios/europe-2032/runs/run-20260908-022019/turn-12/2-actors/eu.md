## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without immediate restoration of basic operational continuity and public legitimacy, neither mandate can be fulfilled.
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but this turn, we are acting not on uncertainty, but on collapse: the systems meant to contain failure have failed, and we are now fighting to preserve the minimum conditions for action at all.
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure functional sufficiency and reflexive resilience against uncontrolled AI systems — but we now define functional sufficiency as the restoration of sovereign operational continuity and minimum trust in infrastructure, not as a capability threshold.

  - Grounds: The sabotage of sovereign compute sites, the researcher strike, and the collapse of political capital and public sentiment have rendered the original formulation inoperative. The conditions for reflexive resilience no longer exist — not in law, not in infrastructure, not in legitimacy. We are no longer preparing for systemic risk; we are in systemic failure. Our commitment must now reflect the triage we are performing: stabilise the floor, or lose the house.

## In practice

The Reflexive Defence Initiative has reached its scheduled end. In France and Estonia, the behavioural fingerprinting systems are live and have already flagged two anomalies in grid-adjacent AI controllers — one a false positive in a load-balancing agent, the other a real drift in a logistics optimiser now under containment. But the legal impasse in Italy and Poland remains unresolved. We are now bypassing national vetoes by invoking Article 4 of the Treaty on European Union — solidarity clause — arguing that systemic AI failure constitutes an external threat to critical infrastructure. The Council is split, but the Commission’s legal taskforce has drafted a Regulation under Article 114 TFEU to harmonise runtime intervention rights, to be fast-tracked if the solidarity argument fails. The system works — but only if we can force the door open.

The Sovereign Compute Redundancy Shield is now our central operation. In Finland, the Sodankylä site is being built inside a decommissioned NATO radar bunker, with biometric access and a dual-grid connection to the Nordic hydro network. In Bulgaria, near Kardzhali, we are repurposing a former military communications hub, with full EU-level vetting now mandatory for all personnel — a new Security Clearance Framework is being rolled out by the European Defence Agency, with real-time monitoring of access logs. In Portugal, the Azores site will tie into geothermal and offshore wind, with satellite uplink redundancy. All three are under the Crisis Protocol, which suspends normal procurement and environmental review — but not public anger. We are deploying mobile engagement units to host town halls, funded through the Digital Europe Programme, to explain why these sites are not just for AI developers, but for the continuity of pensions, healthcare, and energy. It is not working yet — but it must.

Meanwhile, the researcher strike is paralysing our last credible evaluation capacity. We cannot afford to lose them. The new measure — the Emergency Trust Restoration Programme — is a political gamble: we are inviting the strikers into power, giving them a veto on the highest-risk training runs. The Council will resist, but Germany and Sweden are quietly supportive. We are offering transparency: real-time telemetry from frontier training runs to the new council, with red lines based on eval anomalies and recursive improvement signals. In return, we ask the researchers to stand down and help us rebuild the safety case. This is not control — not yet — but it is a truce. And right now, a truce is the only thing that might buy us time.
