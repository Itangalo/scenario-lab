## In practice
We are launching the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators through Article 106 TFEU and the revised NIS2 Directive, with ENISA as lead coordinator. By Q1 2027, we will issue binding technical standards requiring all designated operators — energy, water, transport, health and digital infrastructure — to deploy AI-augmented intrusion detection systems, conduct quarterly red-team exercises modelled on the autumn breach, and report anomalies in real time to a new cross-border fusion cell hosted in Luxembourg. Funding of €12bn will come from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with co-financing from national budgets at 50%. The Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications configuration will vote on the implementing act in December, but we are already negotiating with Germany and Poland, who fear overreach, by offering opt-in capacity-building instead of mandates for Tier 2 operators.

At the same time, we are defending the Tech sovereignty package against legal challenges from member states concerned about land use and energy allocation. The Commission has opened consultations on the first five accelerated-permitting zones, with Spain, Finland and Ireland emerging as front-runners due to grid availability and cooling advantages. We are also in talks with ASML and TSMC to anchor next-generation packaging facilities within these zones, leveraging export-control leverage and the Chips Act to secure binding commitments. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme is entering its first procurement phase, with the European Investment Bank structuring debt guarantees to de-risk private capital — though the pace remains constrained by transformer shortages and the EU’s still-fragmented grid planning. We are not slowing, but we are not fooling ourselves: this is a six-turn race, and we are only at the start.
