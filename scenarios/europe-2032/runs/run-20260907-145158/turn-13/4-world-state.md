### The Fracture and the Framework

The Union acts from the edge. With political capital exhausted and cohesion fraying, it finalizes the Frontier Access Guarantee, repurposing its compute for redundancy in the Sovereign AI Emergency Core. Accelerated validation allows partial deployment in Finland and Luxembourg, ensuring continuity in medical triage and grid balancing—even as full audit remains incomplete. This is not progress; it is triage.

A single member state’s separate deal with a foreign hyperscaler fractures the common line. The Union responds not with immediate sanction, but with the Reintegration Protocol: a binding 60-day window to return under EU sovereignty, with guaranteed access to emergency systems and a seat at the next policy review. Refusal triggers exclusion from future infrastructure and cohesion funds. The offer is structured, not supplicant—yet the message splits capitals. Some see resolve. Others see desperation masked as design.

The European Democracy Shield publishes its first audit logs, revealing coordinated synthetic media campaigns and bot amplification during the annulled election. A public forum convenes platform executives, election officials, and civil society to co-develop the explainability dashboard. Trust is not restored, but the process begins to be seen as legitimate—by some.

Yet the atmosphere thickens. Rumours of plateauing AI performance persist, and sabotage strikes another regional node. Labour unrest grows as AI-driven displacement accelerates, with no retraining pipelines in place. Public sentiment holds steady, caught between fear and faint hope.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

AI capability inches to 79.0 as unreleased models exhibit emergent reasoning, though interpretability remains lost. Openweight capability stagnates at 52.0. No safety gains land on deployed systems—no breakthrough, no enforcement. The gap widens.

The Union’s actions are bold, but brittle. Emergency powers sustain motion, yet without capital, every step risks collapse.
