### The Fracture Widens, the Union Acts

The annulment of a national election—officially suspended over unverifiable AI-driven disinformation campaigns—sends shockwaves across the continent. Courts cite pervasive manipulation of voter perception through synthetic media and microtargeting, though no single actor is identified. The event marks a turning point: AI’s threat is no longer speculative, but operational in the heart of democratic legitimacy.

In response, the European Union launches the Democratic Integrity Monitoring Framework, mandating real-time transparency from digital platforms on political advertising and amplification systems. A new dashboard, operated by the European Democracy Shield, begins aggregating data on bot activity and content reach, aiming to restore public trust. National election authorities are ordered to integrate explainable AI modes into voting systems by 2033.

Yet cohesion frays. Two member states refuse initial data sharing, citing national sovereignty, and legal challenges mount over the framework’s reach. The European Parliament delays full endorsement, demanding clearer sunset clauses. Still, emergency provisions allow partial rollout.

Simultaneously, work continues on the Sovereign AI Emergency Core. Data centres in Finland and Luxembourg come online in prototype form, hosting a secured 58.0-capability model for grid and medical triage. However, delays persist—technical cooperation from one member state remains suspended, and repurposing compute from the Frontier Access Guarantee sparks backlash from developers who claim breach of contract.

AI capability climbs to 78.0 as labs report emergent reasoning in unreleased models, though no breakthrough is confirmed. Interpretability remains lost; safety holds at −7.0. Openweight capability stagnates at 52.0, with no new releases.

Resilience improves slightly, as fallback protocols in critical sectors prove viable during stress tests. Public sentiment inches up, as some welcome decisive action—though protests against AI overreach continue.

Political capital remains frozen at 0.0. The Union acts, but without room to absorb failure. Sovereignty dips to 20.0, as dependency on foreign frontier access deepens amid stalled domestic capacity.
