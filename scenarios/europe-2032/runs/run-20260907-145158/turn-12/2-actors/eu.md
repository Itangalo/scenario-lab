## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing. But when one mandate consumes the other, we are no longer governing — we are reacting. Sovereignty without continuity is hollow. Safety without capacity is surrender. Now, with capital at zero and control slipping, we must govern from the edge of collapse: our existence is not guaranteed by law or legacy, but by whether we can still act as one when the next shock hits. That moment is here. The election_annulled event proves that the breakdown is no longer technical — it is political. We are not just preserving systems. We are preserving the legitimacy of collective decision-making in the face of systems that erode it.``  
- Grounds: The election_annulled event marks a categorical shift: AI’s harm is no longer potential or probabilistic, but operational in the political fabric. Our identity must now encompass not just the defence of capability and safety, but the defence of the Union’s claim to represent its people. If we cannot secure the link between public will and public outcome, no technical sovereignty will save us.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer pay for certainty with cohesion. When action fractures the Union, we have already lost. Now, we act only when we can carry the whole body with us, not because unity is convenient, but because without it, action is illusion. This turn, we break that rule — because inaction is a deeper fracture. We move without full consensus, but not without offering a path back in.``  
- Grounds: The election_annulled event demands a response that cannot wait for unanimity, yet our previous modification of this commitment made such action impossible. We are forced to violate our own rule because the cost of paralysis is now greater than the cost of division. We accept that this reversal weakens trust, but we act to preserve the Union’s relevance — and we bind ourselves to repair cohesion *through* the action, not after it.

* ``- modify `sovereignty_through_service` (commitment): We will earn sovereignty not by decree, but by delivering a service no one else can: continuous, secure, and trusted AI operations for public good, even when the frontier breaks. Our control is not in the law, but in the switch that stays on when others fail. But service alone is not enough. If the service does not restore faith in the system that delivers it, then we are merely maintaining infrastructure for a collapsing polity. Now, sovereignty must also mean *demonstrable legitimacy* — the public must see, understand, and accept that this system serves them, not just functions for them.``  
- Grounds: The election_annulled event proves that technical continuity is insufficient. The Sovereign AI Emergency Core may keep the lights on, but if the people no longer believe in the system that controls the switch, even that will fail. We expand the commitment to include legitimacy as a core deliverable, not a side effect.

* ``- retire `two_year_commitment```  
- Grounds: The commitment assumed operational readiness of the Sovereign AI Core Programme as a basis for enforcement and continuity. That readiness has not materialized, and the election_annulled event shifts the threat model from strategic denial to political subversion. The original commitment no longer reflects the nature of the risk. We cannot claim to secure sovereign control when the very concept of control is being undone in democratic processes.

* ``- add `defend_the_franchise` (commitment): We will treat the integrity of democratic decision-making as the highest-order resilience function. Any AI system that materially influences electoral outcomes — whether through information, administration, or enforcement — must be subject to real-time audit, public explainability, and irreversible fallback. We will not defend AI sovereignty by undermining the sovereignty of the voter.``  
- Grounds: The election_annulled event is not an outlier — it is a signal. We are no longer managing risks *around* democracy; we are defending democracy itself. This commitment reorients our purpose: our sovereign stack must not only run during denial, but must be seen to run *for* the people, not over them.

## New measure
**Measure:** Establish the Democratic Integrity Monitoring Framework  
A real-time, EU-wide system to detect, audit, and respond to AI-driven manipulation of electoral processes, combining technical monitoring of information flows, mandatory transparency from platforms, and public-facing explainability dashboards for voting systems.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 8–12 points through early detection and response to AI-enabled electoral interference; stabilize public_sentiment by demonstrating visible, proactive defence of democratic processes; create a foundation for future ai_safety improvements by forcing transparency on politically sensitive deployments  
`Applies to:` National election authorities, major digital platforms, political advertising networks, EU digital infrastructure providers

## Priority
**Priority:** Establish the Democratic Integrity Monitoring Framework  
We are prioritizing this because the election_annulled event proves that the frontier is no longer just in capability — it is in legitimacy. The Sovereign AI Emergency Core keeps services running, but if elections cannot be trusted, no service will be seen as legitimate. We must defend the franchise first, or all else becomes administration of a broken compact.

## In practice
This turn, we are acting on two fronts: one technical, one political. On the technical front, we continue to stand up the Sovereign AI Emergency Core under Article 122 TFEU, mobilizing €3.2bn to fast-track secure data centres in Finland and Luxembourg. We are repurposing part of the Frontier Access Guarantee’s compute to host the Core, with binding legal terms ensuring EU jurisdiction over weights and updates. We have secured a model checkpoint at 58.0 from an EU-based developer, and it is now undergoing air-gapped validation by the EAAD. Despite legal challenges from two member states, we are proceeding under emergency provisions, but we are also offering equity in future EU AI dividends to secure cooperation — not just compliance.

On the political front, we are launching the Democratic Integrity Monitoring Framework in direct response to the election_annulled event. Under the Digital Services Act and the upcoming Electoral Integrity Directive, we are mandating real-time data sharing from platforms on political ad targeting, bot activity, and content amplification patterns. We are establishing a new unit within the European Democracy Shield to operate a public-facing dashboard that shows, in plain language, how AI systems are being used in electoral contexts. National election authorities will be required to integrate with this system by turn 15, and any system used in vote counting or voter registration must have an auditable, explainable mode that can be triggered during disputes.

We are not waiting for consensus. The Council is divided, the Parliament is skeptical, and public sentiment is fragile. But we are moving because the alternative is irrelevance. We are funding this through emergency reallocations and the Crisis Solidarity Mechanism, knowing it will strain cohesion. But we are also building in exit ramps: every measure includes a sunset clause and a review at turn 15, so that this does not become permanent overreach. We are not claiming to have all the answers. We are claiming the duty to act — and to answer for it later.
