## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from emergency response to emergency creation — because the only way to guarantee continuity is to have a system that runs when nothing else does. The loss_of_control_incident exposed that even the labs no longer understand their own models, and the eval_anomaly_reports suggest we are approaching a threshold where capability emerges unpredictably. We cannot wait for safety to catch up. We must build something we can trust, even if it is not the most capable.

Under Article 122 TFEU and the Crisis Solidarity Mechanism, we are mobilizing €3.2bn to stand up the Sovereign AI Emergency Core: a narrow, high-assurance AI stack trained on verified public datasets, running on EU-controlled compute, and certified by the EAAD to operate only critical continuity functions. It will not be general. It will not be frontier. But it will be on, and it will be ours. We are fast-tracking siting for two new secure data centres in Finland and Luxembourg, using emergency powers to bypass national objections, and offering equity in future EU AI dividends to member states that contribute compute or talent.

The Frontier Access Guarantee remains in flight — but this turn, we are repurposing part of its planned cluster to host the Emergency Core, with binding legal terms ensuring EU jurisdiction over weights, access, and updates. We are negotiating with two EU-based developers to freeze a model checkpoint at 58.0 capability — enough for task-specific continuity operations, below the threshold of emergent risk. This is not the future we wanted. It is the one we can build before the next failure hits. We are not declaring sovereignty. We are earning it, one live failover at a time.
