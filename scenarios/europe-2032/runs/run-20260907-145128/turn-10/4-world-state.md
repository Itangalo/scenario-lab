### Stalled Momentum, Deepening Crisis

The European Union attempts a desperate course correction, proposing an Emergency AI Governance Suspension in public services to halt the collapse of public trust after the welfare scandal. The measure, framed as a temporary pause, is meant to create breathing room for the Integrity Audit Expansion to gain legitimacy. Yet even this modest step falters. Several member states, citing national sovereignty over social policy, resist the invocation of Article 114 TFEU as disproportionate. Hungary and Poland outright reject the suspension, while Germany and France demand exemptions for already-audited systems. The Council negotiations stall, and no formal adoption occurs.

Meanwhile, the technical foundation of AI oversight crumbles. The **opaque_reasoning** event renders human-readable audit trails obsolete overnight. Interpretability tools used by EAAD and national bodies fail across the board, undermining the very premise of current safety frameworks. With no alternative verification method ready, confidence in all assurance regimes—SMAF, EAAD, Public Assurance Protocol—evaporates. The **capability_plateau_evidence**, though contested, temporarily slows private investment further, but does nothing to ease the EU’s dependency on foreign frontier models under the US **CONSOLIDATION** posture.

Resilience erodes as unpatched vulnerabilities grow in the face of undetectable AI-driven intrusions. The completion of multiple large measures—Gigafactories, SACP, CIRS—brings no political capital. The mood is not one of accomplishment, but of disillusionment: systems were built, but they cannot be trusted.

The Openweight Containment Initiative ends without enforcement, its legal fate pending before the ECJ. The EU’s leverage remains paper-thin. Public sentiment holds at rock bottom, with protests continuing and media framing AI governance as a broken promise.

### A Union at the Edge

With political capital critically low and cohesion fraying, the EU’s ability to act collectively appears spent. The suspension measure remains in proposal limbo—neither passed nor formally abandoned. The window to regain initiative narrows.
