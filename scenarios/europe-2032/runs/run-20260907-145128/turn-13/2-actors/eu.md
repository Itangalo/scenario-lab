## In practice
This turn, we govern from a narrow ledge: legitimacy drained, capital nearly gone, and trust shattered. The MPACF remains in flight, but its diplomatic momentum is thin, and without domestic credibility, even our external leverage erodes. We cannot afford another turn of symbolic action while the ground burns. The strike wave has paralyzed municipal services in three member states; hospitals have been forced offline not by protest but by opportunistic ransomware that exploited AI-automated penetration tools. These were not state actors—just two-man crews using off-the-shelf exploit generators trained on open-weight models at capability level ~53.0. Yet they crippled systems built for yesterday’s threat model. This is the new normal: harm not from the frontier, but from the already-distributed.

So we act where failure is most visible and most unforgivable: protecting the lights, the water, the ambulances. Under the NIS2 Directive, we invoke emergency powers to designate 120 critical nodes—grid substations, water treatment plants, regional health data hubs—and mandate immediate patching, multi-factor authentication, and AI-augmented intrusion detection using pre-vetted tools from the ENISA rapid-response pool. Funding comes from unspent RRF allocations, redirected via Commission decision under Article 22 of the Regulation (EU) 2021/241, conditioned on compliance by March 2033. National governments resist—we know they will—but we tie disbursement of pending tranches to verified implementation. No negotiation, no delays.

At the same time, we keep the MPACF alive behind closed doors. Our envoys meet bilaterally with Seoul and Tokyo, offering side letters on talent mobility and semiconductor testing access to lock in resale controls. With Canada, we accept their parliamentary review as cover, but push for interim technical alignment through the OECD.AI working group. With Taiwan, we de-link access guarantees from export controls, offering instead joint research on verification tools—a face-saving path forward. It is not the pact we wanted, but it may become one. And if we survive the next six months, we will need it.
