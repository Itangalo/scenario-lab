The EU, facing collapsed internal AI governance and eroding public trust, launches the Middle-Power AI Coordination Forum (MPACF) to regain influence, convening Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Taiwan in Brussels. Ambitious goals for shared safety standards and export controls falter: Taiwan demands unmet access guarantees, Japan and South Korea avoid binding resale commitments, and Canada delays action pending review. The summit yields only a vague pledge to "explore cooperation," but the **middle_power_coalition** emerges, marginally improving EU access to compute and talent. This fragile external alignment gains symbolic value, especially as **member_state_defection** intensifies—Hungary signs a bilateral AI deal with a US hyperscaler, exposing EU fragmentation. Domestically, the **emergent_labour_strike_wave** continues, resilience declines, and the **Integrity Audit Expansion** is dismissed as meaningless without transparent AI reasoning. AI capability advances to 69.5, widening the control gap; open-weight development stagnates. Political capital dips further, saved only slightly by the perception that the EU has not yet become irrelevant. Sovereignty remains nominal, public trust frozen, and governance paralyzed.
