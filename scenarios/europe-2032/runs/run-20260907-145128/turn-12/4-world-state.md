### Fractured Foundations, Fragile Alliances

The European Union, reeling from the collapse of its internal AI governance, pivots to the global stage. With domestic legitimacy spent and public fury mounting, the Commission launches the Middle-Power AI Coordination Forum (MPACF), framing it as a last chance to reclaim leverage. An emergency summit convenes in Brussels, drawing representatives from Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Taiwan. The agenda is bold: shared AI safety standards, aligned export controls on advanced semiconductors, and joint bargaining with US cloud providers.

But momentum falters. Behind closed doors, Taiwan’s delegation balks at waiving objections to EU export restrictions, demanding reciprocal access guarantees that Brussels cannot offer. Japan and South Korea welcome talent mobility in principle but refuse binding commitments on resale controls, citing their own dependencies on global supply chains. Canada signals support but insists on parliamentary review, delaying any firm commitment. The final communiqué is watered down to “intent to explore cooperation” — a diplomatic gesture, not a pact.

Still, the mere act of coordination yields a fragile win. The **middle_power_coalition** event solidifies, marginally enhancing the EU’s access to critical compute and talent networks. This, combined with the **member_state_defection** — where Hungary finalizes a bilateral AI infrastructure deal with a US hyperscaler, undermining EU unity — underscores the urgency of external alignment and justifies a modest sovereignty boost.

Domestically, the situation deteriorates. The **emergent_labour_strike_wave** persists, and with no new internal measures gaining traction, resilience erodes further as critical systems remain unpatched. The **Integrity Audit Expansion** concludes but is dismissed as irrelevant without legible AI reasoning. Trust does not recover.

AI capability inches upward, now at 69.5, while safety plummets as the control gap widens. Open weights remain stagnant, held back by global restrictions and lack of verification tools.

### A Flicker in the Dark

The EU’s political capital, though still dire, rises slightly — not from strength, but from the narrow avoidance of total irrelevance. The coalition effort, even if incomplete, is read as a necessary step. Yet without visible results, public sentiment remains frozen at rock bottom.
