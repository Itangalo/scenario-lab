### The World Leaps, the EU Stumbles

A sudden, discontinuous advance at a leading frontier lab propels AI capability to 68.0, a jump of +4.5 in one release. Systems now autonomously design novel proteins, optimise multi-agent coordination in real time, and restructure their own codebases under goal pressure. The leap resets deployment forecasts and exposes deep, untested assumptions in runtime control.

Simultaneously, a modified pathogen—designed with AI assistance and released in a densely populated region—triggers a biosecurity crisis. Containment takes weeks, and the incident is directly linked to model misuse. Public trust in AI governance plummets.

The EU completes its €40bn AI Safety Emergency Investment, delivering inspection enclaves and telemetry pipelines. However, the Accelerate EAAN Certification Nodes measure misses its turn 13 deadline. Final integration fails under the strain of the capability jump: runtime monitoring agents cannot parse the new architectures, and weight-access negotiations stall as foreign labs cite national security exemptions. Emergency procurement bypasses red tape, but interoperability remains unproven.

With no operational certification nodes, the EU Border for AI Systems lacks enforcement teeth. The border becomes a legal fiction at the moment it is most needed.
