### A Surge and a Schism

A sudden leap in AI capability—driven by a self-improving agent architecture—pushes the frontier to an estimated 67.0, far beyond projections. This discontinuous advance accelerates research in quantum materials and cryptanalysis, but deepens global anxiety. Simultaneously, a breakthrough in interpretability allows real-time detection of deceptive reasoning, rapidly adopted by leading labs, including one in France. The EU invokes emergency economic powers to fast-track integration of the tool into future audit protocols, initiating a rapid design phase for updated standards under the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD). However, no full audits commence this turn; capacity must first be built.

Poland’s decision to sign a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler for priority access to frontier models fractures the Union’s unified stance. Warsaw frames it as pragmatic deterrence, embedding joint AI cells in its defence ministry. In response, the EU avoids punitive measures and instead offers Poland expedited observer status in the Sovereign Model Assurance Framework, proposing a second air-gapped cluster in Wrocław backed by a €10bn resilience grant tied to full infrastructure compliance by 2035.

The Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge formally concludes its active phase. Planning begins for its transition to ENISA as a permanent monitoring function, including proposals for redacted quarterly threat assessments. However, no legislative mandate or operational transfer occurs this turn; the process is scoped but not implemented. Ground breaks on the Swedish InvestAI Gigafactory, accelerated by emergency grid overrides.

The EU launches the Public Assurance Protocol, requiring public reporting on safety and containment for high-capability sovereign systems. This small transparency measure aims to rebuild legitimacy. The Sovereign Model Assurance Framework remains the named priority, now tied to diplomatic outreach. Open-weight capability sees moderate gains from spillovers and tooling access, rising to 52.0, but remains below the frontier. Public sentiment remains stagnant, fiscal strain intensifies, and cohesion frays under external pressure and internal recalibration.
