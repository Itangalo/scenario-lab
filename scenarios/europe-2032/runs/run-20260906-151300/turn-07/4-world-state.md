### A Scandal Reshapes Strategy

The fallout from a landmark ruling on an AI-driven social insurance system reverberates across Europe. A national ombudsman confirms the system wrongfully denied benefits to thousands, exposing systemic flaws in algorithmic governance. The judgment triggers widespread public anger, deepening distrust in public AI and validating fears of opaque, unaccountable automation. Media coverage shifts from abstract risk to concrete injustice, with protests in several capitals demanding accountability.

In response, the EU pivots hard toward oversight. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) launches a forensic audit, while the Commission begins drafting legal reforms under Article 114 TFEU to mandate biannual independent reviews and suspension powers for high-capability public AI. The Sovereign AI Core Programme (SACP) delays its next prototype rollout to integrate appeal mechanisms and audit trails, reframing sovereignty as answerability.

Symbolic progress is undercut by strain. The Swedish InvestAI Gigafactory becomes operational, but its first allocation is diverted to red-teaming interpretability tools rather than performance scaling. ENISA begins planning for expanded threat assessments that include societal risks, though no formal mandate yet exists.

Poland remains outside full alignment, though diplomatic outreach continues via joint review panel invitations tied to future access. Internal divisions persist: France resists moratorium calls, while Germany and Spain push for stricter limits.

Public sentiment erodes as the scandal dominates discourse. The proposed Public Assurance Framework—still in development—is mischaracterized in media as abandoned, fueling perceptions of retreat. Trust in EU institutions wavers, and political capital diminishes under fiscal and legitimacy pressure, though no formal crisis threshold is breached.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

A verification breakthrough in clinical coding allows rapid, automatic validation of AI outputs, accelerating progress in regulated domains. Simultaneously, a notable open-weight model release improves accessibility of advanced tooling, contributing to modest gains in openweight capability. These advances deepen concern: the very systems meant to serve the public are now seen as uncontrolled and unjust. However, the release does not represent a frontier leap, and open development continues to trail behind proprietary capability growth.
