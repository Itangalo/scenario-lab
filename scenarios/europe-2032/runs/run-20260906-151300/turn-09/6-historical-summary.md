AI capability reaches 68.0 amid rising public distrust fueled by high-profile failures: an AI-driven social insurance scandal and two critical incidents involving AI-exploited ransomware and an uncontrolled logistics agent. In response, the EU fast-tracks a directive mandating cryptographically secured off-switches, real-time audit feeds, and human-in-the-loop override within two hours for all AI systems above 60.0 in essential infrastructure, with exemptions only for fully air-gapped military systems. France resists, securing a narrow compromise based on physical isolation, while Germany and Spain win agreement in principle to extend oversight to private cloud providers hosting public services, though implementation is delayed to Turn 10. Poland joins the first compliance cohort in exchange for early access to EAAD monitoring tools.

Public sentiment deteriorates further as protests spread, driven by job losses in law, accountancy, and public administration, and perceptions of institutional collapse. Global AI investment plummets, stalling frontier growth slightly but undermining Europe’s access to foreign infrastructure and derailing domestic compute ambitions. Despite urgent proposals, no new measures are fully implemented; ENISA begins drafting technical standards, but compliance remains months away. The gap between AI advancement and regulatory control widens, eroding sovereignty not through sudden failure but through sustained drift, as the U.S. maintains a consolidative posture and the EU struggles to operationalize its vision of accountable AI.
