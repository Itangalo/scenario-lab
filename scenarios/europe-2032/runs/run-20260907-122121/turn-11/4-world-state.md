### Scandal and Stakes

The Dutch ombudsman’s report on the social insurance AI scandal lands with seismic force: thousands denied benefits due to an opaque, unappealable algorithm. Public fury erupts, with protests in Amsterdam and The Hague, and AI in public administration is cast as a tool of bureaucratic cruelty. The European Commission responds swiftly, suspending all high-impact administrative AI deployments pending review under the nascent Sovereign Evaluation Regime. Trust erodes further as media uncover similar systems in use across five other member states.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

Despite the political turbulence, work continues on the Sovereign Evaluation Regime. The Council’s Competitiveness configuration advances the legal basis under amended Article 12a, and €220 million is allocated to build red-teaming labs in Germany, Finland, and Belgium. ENISA begins drafting accreditation standards for third-party evaluators. However, U.S. frontier labs maintain their stance of non-cooperation, and legal teams at the Commission brace for a protracted WTO dispute.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Slips

AI capability advances to 71.5, driven by iterative self-improvement in closed training environments. Open-weight models benefit indirectly, reaching 58.25, as research spillovers from sovereign initiatives leak into open networks. Yet no safety upgrades land in deployed systems. The gap between capability and safety widens, and the automated decision scandal confirms systemic control failures. Resilience dips slightly as institutions divert focus to crisis management.

Public sentiment falls to 29.0, reflecting deepening anxiety. Political capital erodes sharply under the weight of the scandal and ongoing regime development, despite the suspension of risky systems. The EU acts, but legitimacy is lost faster than it can be rebuilt.
