AI capability rises to 71.5 by 2031, driven by closed-loop self-improvement, while open-weight models reach 58.25 through spillovers from sovereign initiatives. The Dutch social insurance AI scandal triggers a crisis of public trust, exposing opaque, high-impact algorithmic decisions that deny benefits to thousands. Protests erupt, and the European Commission suspends all high-impact administrative AI systems pending review under the Sovereign Evaluation Regime. Similar systems are uncovered in five additional member states, amplifying public backlash.

In response, the EU advances the Sovereign Evaluation Regime, securing legal footing under amended Article 12a and allocating €220 million for red-teaming labs in Germany, Finland, and Belgium. ENISA begins developing accreditation standards for third-party auditors. However, U.S. frontier labs refuse cooperation, and a WTO dispute looms. Despite regulatory momentum, no safety improvements are implemented in deployed systems, widening the capability-safety gap.

AI safety remains stagnant at 20.0, resilience dips to 57.0 due to institutional strain, and public sentiment declines to 29.0 amid growing anxiety over algorithmic accountability. Political capital erodes sharply as the EU struggles to manage the fallout, undermining legitimacy despite swift regulatory action. The scandal underscores systemic control failures, halting momentum on AI deployment in public services.
