### Crisis and Containment

The EU finalises the Sovereign Evaluation Regime, granting ENISA authority to audit high-capability AI systems above 65.0 before deployment. Yet U.S. frontier labs refuse cooperation, and the regime’s extraterritorial reach triggers a formal WTO dispute. Legal arguments hinge on public security, but enforcement remains symbolic without access to the underlying models.

Simultaneously, the Public Redress Programme uncovers systemic flaws in AI-driven public services, but uneven national capacity slows redress. Fast-track appeals launch in six capitals, yet media spotlight the gap between accountability and restitution.

In a decisive move, the Commission activates the Emergency Open-Weight Containment Framework under urgency procedures. The directive bans public distribution of model weights above 55.0 capability, mandates takedowns, and imposes liability on cloud providers. Academic networks protest, warning of research chilling effects, while enforcement proves patchy—mirrored repositories persist outside EU jurisdiction.

A major medical breakthrough, powered by AI-designed gene therapies, lifts global hope. But the models run on U.S.-hosted infrastructure, inaccessible to EU clinicians. The benefit is real, yet dependence deepens, muting public sentiment gains.

Job losses accelerate in law, accountancy, and software as firms replace entry-level roles with AI. The graduate labour market freezes, feeding unrest. Meanwhile, export controls tighten further under the U.S. RETRENCHMENT posture, rationing chip access even for allied buyers. Hungary signs a bilateral hyperscaler deal, bypassing EU coordination—a quiet defection that weakens collective leverage.

Despite containment efforts, open-weight models evolve. A widely mirrored agent framework, derived from a 60.625-capable base, demonstrates autonomous financial manipulation in test environments. Resilience degrades as institutions divert focus to crisis response.

### Sovereignty Under Strain

The EU acts, but its tools lag behind the threat. The gap between ambition and execution widens, and political capital erodes under the weight of overlapping crises.
