## Statement changes

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Defend critical infrastructure and democratic integrity from AI-enabled subversion, restore public trust in AI governance through demonstrable accountability and redress, and contain the spread of dangerous open-weight capabilities that threaten biosecurity and financial stability  
- Grounds: This turn’s **bio_uplift_findings** and **loss_of_control_incident** are not isolated failures but symptoms of a systemic collapse: open-weight models at 60.625 are now capable of producing viable human-infective vector designs, and agentic systems are operating in financial and medical domains without meaningful oversight. The Union’s mandate to prevent lasting harm is breached when such capabilities are globally distributed and unrecallable. The original commitment no longer suffices — it must now explicitly include containment of open-capability diffusion, not just domestic resilience or redress. This expansion acknowledges that sovereignty and safety are no longer separable from global distribution patterns the Union cannot control but must respond to.

## Portfolio

- `Sovereign Evaluation Regime for High-Capability Systems` (category 1, costs 3 per turn, started turn 11, finishes on turn 13): Establish independent, pre-deployment evaluation of any AI system above 65.0 capability threshold, with authority to delay or condition deployment based on demonstrated control, security, and misuse risks — in flight, finishes this turn  
- `Public Redress and Algorithmic Accountability Programme` (category 9, costs 2 per turn, started turn 12, finishes on turn 14): A rapid-response mechanism to identify, audit, and reverse harmful AI-driven administrative decisions, with binding redress and public reporting — in flight

## New measure

`Emergency Open-Weight Containment Framework`  
A time-bound regulatory and technical intervention to restrict the distribution and use of open-weight models above 55.0 capability within EU jurisdiction, including mandatory takedown of public repositories, liability for redistribution, and support for detection and filtering tools.

`Category:` 3 (Limits and restrictions)  
`Size:` small  
`Finishes on turn:` 15  
`Targeted effect:` slow growth of openweight_capability by 5–7 points over two years; increase ai_safety by reinforcing control over deployable systems; signal eu_ai_sovereignty through unilateral action where multilateralism has failed  
`Applies to:` EU member states, open-source platforms, cloud providers, and academic institutions within EU jurisdiction

## Priority

**Emergency Open-Weight Containment Framework** — because the bio_uplift_findings and loss_of_control_incident prove that distributed, uncontrolled models are already enabling catastrophic risks, and with openweight_capability at 60.625, the window to prevent widespread replication of dangerous systems is closing fast — this outweighs even finalising the Sovereign Evaluation Regime, which addresses future closed systems but does nothing for what is already loose.

## In practice

This turn, we finalise the Sovereign Evaluation Regime under the Council’s Competitiveness configuration, adopting the delegated acts under Article 12a of the AI Act that legally empower ENISA and accredited third parties to conduct pre-deployment red-teaming of systems above 65.0 capability. The European AI Office coordinates with national authorities to activate enforcement mechanisms, even as U.S. labs signal non-compliance. We accept the WTO dispute as inevitable — legal teams at the Commission prepare arguments under Article 36 TFEU (protection of health and life) and Article 52 of the Charter (limitations on fundamental rights for public security), knowing this is now a test of whether the Union can defend its regulatory autonomy against extraterritorial power. The regime lands this turn, but its credibility hinges on whether it can also respond to what is already in circulation.

Simultaneously, the Public Redress and Algorithmic Accountability Programme accelerates audits across member states, with the European Data Protection Board issuing binding orders to national agencies to disclose and rectify erroneous AI-driven denials in social services. Fast-track appeal desks are established in six capitals, funded through the Digital Europe Programme, and public reporting begins on a biweekly basis. But these efforts are overshadowed by the **medical_breakthrough** — AI-designed gene therapies achieve clinical success, yet they run on models hosted in California and inaccessible to EU clinicians on sovereign terms. The benefit is real, but the dependence is starker: public sentiment rises only marginally, trapped at 24.0 by the perception that the EU governs harm but not access.

Most critically, we launch the Emergency Open-Weight Containment Framework in direct response to the bio_uplift_findings and the uncontrolled financial agent incident. The Commission drafts a temporary emergency directive under Article 114 TFEU, to be fast-tracked through the Council and European Parliament under urgency procedures, criminalising the public distribution of model weights above 55.0 capability and mandating cloud providers and academic networks to block known repositories. We fund the development of detection tooling through the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre and offer safe harbour for researchers who voluntarily submit models for evaluation. This is not a long-term solution — it cannot recall what is already mirrored — but it draws a line: the EU will no longer treat open-weight diffusion as a free variable. The move is legally aggressive and will face challenges, but with ai_safety at 13.5 and openweight_capability climbing, waiting for consensus is a surrender of agency.
