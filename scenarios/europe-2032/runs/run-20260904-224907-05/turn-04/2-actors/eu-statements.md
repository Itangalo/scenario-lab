## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but this turn, we have been wrong in form, not in direction: the Compute Directive has cost us capital without securing capacity, and we now adjust instrument, not intent. However, the **ai_investment_collapse** and **campaign_backlash** have changed the terrain: we no longer face a frontier outpacing us, but one contracting — and a public that no longer fears what AI will do, but what it already has done. Our commitment now includes *anchoring sovereignty in restraint as much as in build-out*, accepting that legitimacy is not restored by speed alone.
  - Grounds: The sudden contraction in private AI investment — with valuations collapsing and build-out plans cancelled — means the global frontier is no longer accelerating beyond reach. For the first time in years, the pace of capability growth is slowing, not because of regulation, but because of capital discipline. This creates a rare window: the race is no longer purely about catching up, but about defining what kind of capability the EU wants, and under what conditions. At the same time, the **campaign_backlash** has moved moratoriums and worker protections into the mainstream, and the **automated_decision_scandal** has made unchecked deployment politically untenable. We were right to act early, but wrong to assume that sovereign compute alone would restore agency. Now, we must prove that our model is not just independent, but *different* — built under constraints that others ignore, and trusted because it is accountable. This recalibration does not abandon urgency, but redefines it: speed now serves legitimacy, not just capability.

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure independent AI capacity to act with sovereignty in an accelerating world — but this world is no longer accelerating in the way we feared. The **ai_investment_collapse** has paused the frontier’s advance, and the **emergent_regional_oversight_concerns** threaten to fracture our own governance. We now redefine sovereignty not only as *capacity we control*, but as *assurance we can enforce and trust we can earn*. The commitment stands, but its expression shifts: from pure build-out to *embedding enforceable safety and public accountability into the sovereign model as non-negotiable foundations*.
  - Grounds: The expiration of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive without delivering capacity, combined with the Gigafactory delays and the collapse of dependent investment plans, proves that sovereignty cannot be procured through legal fiat or foreign partnerships alone. At the same time, the **emergent_regional_oversight_concerns** — with member states demanding independent audit mechanisms — reveal a crisis of trust in centralised EU governance. If we cannot unify oversight, we will lose control. Therefore, we must bind our sovereign capacity to a new standard: not just compute on EU soil, but *safety that is auditable across borders, and decisions that are contestable by citizens*. This is not a retreat from capability, but a redefinition of what makes it usable. The commitment to independent capacity remains, but it will be hollow unless it is also the most trusted, most legible, and most accountable system in operation.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
