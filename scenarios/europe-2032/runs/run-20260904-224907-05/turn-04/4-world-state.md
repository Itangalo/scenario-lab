### A Pivotal Shift in Strategy

The EU pivots from acceleration to institutional legitimacy, launching the **Cross-Border AI Audit Corridor** as its new priority. Designed to unify fracturing oversight and restore public trust, the mechanism would grant binding audit powers over high-risk public AI systems and the sovereign model. However, resistance mounts: two member states reject supranational authority, citing sovereignty concerns, and private investors pull back further as the **ai_investment_collapse** deepens.

### Capability Growth Slows — But Safety Still Lags

Global AI capability rises only modestly to 63.5, as capital constraints limit training runs at frontier labs. The open frontier stalls at 47.0, with no major leaks. Yet no safety evaluations keep pace with deployment, and the gap between capability and control persists. The EU’s new audit initiative begins design work and earns partial credit, nudging **ai_safety** to 24.0, but full implementation remains distant.

### Sovereignty Erodes Amid Build Delays

No new sovereign compute comes online. Gigafactory delays continue, and the **ai_investment_collapse** voids planned private expansions. With **ai_capability** rising and no capacity secured, **eu_ai_sovereignty** falls to 19.0. The redirection of public funds helps, but it is too early to count.

### Political Capital Crumbles

The overloaded portfolio drains resources. The new large measure adds cost, and the priority surcharge applies. Public backlash intensifies as displaced workers and data centre moratoriums gain political traction. The automated decision scandal still shadows policy, and **public_sentiment** drops to 28.0. With trust eroding, **eu_political_capital** falls to 17.0, limiting future action.

### Resilience Dips

No resilience-building measure finishes, and rising AI capability without protective action pulls **resilience** down to 32.0. Critical sectors remain unevenly defended, and labour transition systems buckle under early displacement waves.
