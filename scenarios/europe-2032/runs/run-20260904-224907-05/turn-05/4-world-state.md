### A Shift in the Wind

The U.S. presidential election is decided: a wave of public backlash against unchecked AI deployment propels a retrenchment platform to victory. The incoming administration signals sweeping domestic restrictions, including data centre moratoriums, hiring bans in AI firms, and job guarantees funded by sector levies. American frontier progress slows abruptly, not from hardware shortages but political will. For the EU, the pressure eases — but so does the certainty of partnership.

### The EU’s Pivot to Legitimacy

With global momentum shifting, the EU doubles down on institutional credibility. The Cross-Border AI Audit Corridor advances to staffing, appointing a founding board with cross-sector legitimacy. Auditors from ELLIS, national supreme audit institutions, and civil society are named, and early targets are set: France’s welfare algorithm and Germany’s border risk model. To secure buy-in, the Commission links participation to fast-tracked Gigafactory grid upgrades — a quid pro quo that tempers resistance but fuels accusations of coercion.

Simultaneously, the Public Accountability Interface Mandate is launched, requiring real-time citizen-facing explanations for high-risk public AI decisions. Pilots begin in Dutch healthcare and Spanish unemployment systems, both reeling from recent scandals. The mandate is framed not as transparency for its own sake, but as a political tool: to make opacity politically costly.

### Cracks in the Foundation

Yet momentum falters. The backlash_physical intensifies, with sabotage of grid connections to planned data centres in Poland and Sweden. Policing costs mount, and investor confidence remains frozen. The Tech Sovereignty Package fails to attract private capital, its accelerated-permitting zones now seen as toxic.

The portfolio overhang drags on political capital. The new large measure adds strain, and the priority surcharge bites. Public sentiment dips further as displaced workers join protests, framing AI not as a future threat but a present injustice. Trust in public institutions erodes.

### Capability Creeps Forward

Global AI capability rises to 66.0, driven by non-American labs capitalizing on Washington’s retreat. Openweight capability inches to 48.0, with no major leak but steady refinement. Safety, however, declines: no evaluations land on deployed systems, and the gap between power and control widens. Resilience slips as protective measures stall. Sovereignty falls again — no new compute comes online, and export_control_escalation cuts access to advanced chips, even for allied buyers.
