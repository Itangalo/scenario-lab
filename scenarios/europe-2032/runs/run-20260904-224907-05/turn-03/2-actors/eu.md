## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: building unblockable capability and restoring public trust. The **Frontier Foundation Model Initiative** remains our core priority, now accelerated in response to the capability jump. EDIC has issued binding procurement orders for two exascale clusters, to be delivered by turn 5, with hardware sourced from EU-vetted suppliers under new chip-sovereignty rules. Training will begin as soon as infrastructure lands, using red-teamed datasets and a closed-loop evaluation framework managed by the AI Office. We are no longer waiting for permission or partnership — this model will be trained on EU soil, under EU law, with weights secured in sovereign vaults.

But the **automated_decision_scandal** has changed the political ground beneath us. A regional unemployment agency’s AI system, supported by a third-party model, denied benefits to thousands based on flawed risk scoring. A national ombudsman has ruled it a systemic violation of due process. Public sentiment is shifting from ambivalence to anger, and any expansion of AI in public services now faces fierce resistance. We cannot afford to build a sovereign model that is seen as another black box. That is why we are launching the **Sovereign Model Safety Envelope** — a binding technical and governance layer that ensures the model’s reasoning is auditable, its agents are monitored in real time, and its high-stakes decisions are flagged for human review. This is not optional compliance; it is foundational design. The AI Office will integrate this directly into the model’s training stack, working with ELLIS and EuroHPC JU to build interpretability from the ground up.

Resistance is mounting. The **emergent_data_center_resistance** movement has delayed two Gigafactory sites, with local governments citing energy strain and lack of local benefit. We respond by linking Gigafactory permits to binding community reinvestment agreements — 15% of local energy surplus must go to public services, and 10% of jobs to local hires. Some member states still resist mandatory participation in the Tech sovereignty package. We counter by conditioning access to the Sovereignty Talent Corridor on compliance. The US has not changed its export stance, but we are preparing contingency procurement through Japan and South Korea. Our capital is thin, but we are not retreating. We are consolidating around what works: sovereign capability, anchored in safety, and legitimised by accountability.
