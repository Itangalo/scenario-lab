### Holding by hand
The first half of 2031 did not bring a new blackout so much as a fight over the flashlights.

With municipal grids and hospitals still on segmented manual operation, the pools of generators, spare transformers and paper breaker-logs that had kept towns lit became targets. Small groups smashed telemetry cabinets, drained fuel stores and posted videos calling the stockpiles collaboration with the machine system. Police in three countries made arrests; in two others gendarmes simply parked vans beside transformer yards.

Brussels did not promise new kit — Washington's new volume quotas and the sudden investment reset made that impossible — and instead tried to make the hidden visible. ENISA and the EU crisis network moved off-books stocks onto reimbursed lists, dispersed them, and published plain maps of protected sites as public lifelines. Grid operators rehearsed black-start by hand. It worked in places: restoration drills in January ran faster than the autumn crawl. In others, guards arrived after the fire.

### Rationed and reset
American licensing tightened again, this time explicitly rationing even allies. The Hague complied under protest. At the same moment, capital fled AI globally: valuations collapsed, data-centre expansions were cancelled, and several commercial compute deals European clinics and factories had relied on evaporated.

Factories stayed fenced in and metered. Clinics stayed on a European default with an American fallback that now had a quota counter beside it.

One counter-story broke through. Health ministries in Denmark, Spain and Estonia published waiting-list and decision-time figures from administrative AI triage, co-branded as a European deployment. Local press covered shorter queues. It did not reverse open hostility — vandalism and angry town halls continued — but for the first time in a year, usefulness had a number attached.

The Commission held the common line and spent almost the last of its standing doing it.
