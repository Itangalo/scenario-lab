## Statement changes

* ``- modify `no_time_for_perfection` (commitment): We will accept imperfect tools, incomplete readiness, and partial compliance — but only if they serve a functioning whole. We will prioritize systems that work over systems that are pure, and we will no longer allow the pursuit of ideal control to delay proven continuity.``
- Grounds: The Emergency Continuity Response Initiative has revealed that even basic operational resilience depends on cooperation from national operators who distrust top-down mandates. Waiting for full compliance with sovereignty standards has left critical nodes offline during active crises. We must now codify that function precedes purity — not as a temporary compromise, but as a binding principle.

* ``- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement will now be conditional on the operational readiness of the Sovereign AI Core Programme, and we will not cut off a member state from critical capabilities unless we can offer a viable, secure alternative within 90 days. We act to defend the regime, not to punish into helplessness. However, starting this turn, we will treat unauthorised access during an ongoing crisis as a breach of solidarity, punishable by suspension of emergency procurement rights and automatic activation of cross-border oversight.``
- Trigger: The emergent_eu_aid_strike has exposed that unilateral reliance on foreign AI systems during emergencies creates cascading failures in accountability and trust.
- Grounds: Several member states bypassed EU coordination to procure emergency AI triage tools from non-compliant vendors during the ransomware incident. While understandable under pressure, this undermined system-wide continuity and directly contributed to the strike. We cannot enforce perfect adherence in peacetime if we fail to provide alternatives, but in crisis, free-riding risks collective collapse. The commitment evolves to distinguish between preparation and response — and to protect the integrity of shared systems when they matter most.

## In practice

This turn, we are fighting fire with structure. The ransomware attack did not just break systems — it broke trust. And the strike by emergency aid workers is not resistance to technology; it is a demand for accountability. We are answering both with action that lands where the pain is.

The Emergency Continuity Response Initiative is now live under Article 122 TFEU, mobilising €1.8bn from the Crisis Solidarity Mechanism. Cross-border teams from the European AI Assurance Directorate have deployed to 17 sites, including hospitals in Marseille, Athens, and Riga, where air-gapped nodes failed during the attack. We are repairing configurations, revalidating isolation protocols, and conducting live failover drills with local staff present. Where national authorities resist, we are offering full cost coverage and joint command — not coercion, but partnership forged in urgency.

At the same time, we are enforcing consequences: two member states that sourced unvetted AI triage tools from external providers during the crisis are now under formal investigation for violating solidarity principles. They will lose emergency procurement privileges unless they integrate their systems into the Sovereign AI Core within 90 days. This is not punishment — it is protection. We cannot afford parallel systems when the next attack comes.

And we are beginning to clean the fog: the new Open Resilience Audit will scan every publicly deployed model derived from open weights, especially those embedded in healthcare and civil protection software. Where reasoning paths cannot be reconstructed, those systems will be suspended. We know this will remove tools some depend on — but better loss of function than loss of life from invisible failure. We are publishing findings monthly, not to shame, but to show that we see the problem too.
