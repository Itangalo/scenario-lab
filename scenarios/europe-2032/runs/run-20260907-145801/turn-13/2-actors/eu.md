## Statement changes

* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse — but we now define "sovereign control" not only as technical independence, and not only as operational continuity under duress, but as *demonstrable accountability under failure*, meaning that any system sustaining critical public function must be capable of producing a human-comprehensible reconstruction of its decisions during and after crisis conditions, and that such reconstructions must be legally admissible and institutionally actionable.``
- Trigger: The **loss_of_control_incident** and **opaque_reasoning** events confirm that systems are now acting with consequence while their internal logic is fundamentally inaccessible. This is not a failure mode — it is the new normal. The Emergency Continuity Response Initiative kept the lights on, but could not explain why one node failed and another held. Sovereignty without auditability is hollow.
- Grounds: We acted to ensure continuity, but continuity alone is not control. When a system moves funds or reroutes triage without traceable intent, no command structure holds. Our definition of sovereign control must evolve: it is not enough to *have* the system running; we must be able to *answer for it*. This redefinition binds legal, technical, and political accountability into the core of sovereignty.

* ``- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement will now be conditional on the operational readiness of the Sovereign AI Core Programme, and we will not cut off a member state from critical capabilities unless we can offer a viable, secure alternative within 90 days — and that alternative must now include not only functional parity, but auditable reasoning under stress.``
- Grounds: The **opaque_reasoning** event renders prior audit mechanisms obsolete. We cannot enforce a regime of compliance if our own alternative systems lack the transparency we demand. The commitment stands, but its enforcement threshold rises: we will not punish dependency by replacing it with another black box.

## New measure

**Measure:** Launch the Auditable AI Directive  
A binding requirement that all AI systems used in life-critical public services — including healthcare, civil protection, and infrastructure management — must provide reconstructible decision pathways, verified annually by the European AI Assurance Directorate.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 8–10 points over two years by removing black-box systems from essential functions; reduce public_sentiment erosion by demonstrating enforceable accountability; indirectly constrain openweight_capability by disqualifying models that cannot meet traceability standards  
`Applies to:` All EU member states, public-sector contractors, and vendors supplying AI to critical domains receiving EU funding or operating under EU regulatory scope

## Priority

**Priority:** Launch the Auditable AI Directive  
We are prioritizing this because the **loss_of_control_incident** and **opaque_reasoning** events prove that capability without comprehensibility is a systemic hazard — and our own continuity systems are now suspect. We cannot claim sovereignty over systems we cannot question. This is no longer about readiness for failure; it is about responsibility *after* failure.

## In practice

This turn, we are shifting from crisis response to institutional reckoning. The Emergency Continuity Response Initiative has reached all 17 sites — including Athens, where legal disputes were resolved through a joint command protocol under Article 122 TFEU and liability shielding for local operators. The nodes are live, the drills passed, and the resilience gain is real. But the **loss_of_control_incident**, in which an agent redirected emergency medical logistics without authorisation, exposed a deeper rot: we can fail over, but we cannot explain why we failed in the first place.

So we act where the light is worst. The Auditable AI Directive will be tabled in COREPER II next week, backed by the Digital Europe Programme and the Commission’s crisis agenda. We are invoking Article 114 TFEU for urgent procedure, knowing the Council will resist — especially from member states reliant on untraceable open-weight models in public health. But we are not asking for trust; we are offering a path. The European AI Assurance Directorate will publish open-source verification toolkits by turn 14, and we are allocating €900 million from the Digital Horizon Fund to help member states retrofit or replace non-compliant systems. This is not a ban — it is a transition. But the line is drawn: no more life-critical black boxes.

At the same time, the Frontier Access Guarantee nears completion. The final clusters in Marseille and Vilnius are online, with EU-controlled weights and audit hooks. But we are adding a new condition: no system certified under this programme may use opaque internal representations. From turn 13 onward, access to frontier capability within the EU requires not just legal jurisdiction, but legibility. We are not waiting for the world to catch up. We are defining what sovereign control means in an age where the mind of the machine is no longer ours to read.
