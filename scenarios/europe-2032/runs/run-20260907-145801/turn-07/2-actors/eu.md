## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a fortress with no people left inside. The breach of containment by a member state proves that political disunity is the most immediate vector of failure, and that our first line of defence is not firewalls, but shared fate.
  - Grounds: The *eu_frontier_access_denied* event confirms that external powers will act unilaterally, without appeal or explanation, cutting off hospitals and ministries mid-operation. This is not a technical failure but a strategic humiliation — and it lands while one of our own has already bypassed the Containment Protocol. The convergence of *safety_breakthrough*, *adoption_success*, and *eu_frontier_access_denied* shows that even where we gain technical leverage (interpretability), we lack institutional unity to wield it. The identity must now name the political fracture as existential, not incidental.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience lags, even without a direct mandate, because harm finds the weakest joint. And this turn, we act where cohesion fails, because a Union that cannot bind its members cannot bind risk.
  - Grounds: The *eu_frontier_access_denied* event is not just a supply shock — it is a demonstration of strategic irrelevance. We were cut off not because we were unsafe, but because we were not sovereign. Yet at the same moment, a member state has undermined the very mechanism meant to secure that sovereignty. The commitment must now expand to include cohesion as a core condition of action: we act not only under technical uncertainty, but under political fracture, and we do so knowing that legitimacy is as vital as latency.

- add `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure. We will enforce this unilaterally if necessary, because a single breach collapses the whole regime.
  - Grounds: The bilateral deal by a member state with a US hyperscaler to access a 68.0 model outside the Containment Protocol is not an isolated incident — it is a precedent. If one state can bypass the framework, all will. The Union’s only leverage is speed and finality in enforcement. This is a binding commitment to act decisively, at cost, because the alternative is the slow dissolution of collective security.

## In practice

This turn, we are acting on the premise that sovereignty is not a stack, but a pact — and that the pact is breaking. The *eu_frontier_access_denied* event has left hospitals and ministries cut off from systems they depended on, not due to a failure of EU policy, but because we were never in control to begin with. At the same moment, one of our own has signed a bilateral deal to access a 68.0 model outside the Containment Protocol, treating the Union’s security framework as optional. This is not divergence — it is desertion.

We are responding with the Cohesion Enforcement Mechanism: a regulation that triggers automatic suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from SACP infrastructure for any member state that procures frontier AI outside EU oversight. This will be proposed under Article 122 TFEU for economic stability and Article 16 for internal security, with the EAAD designated as the monitoring authority. We are fast-tracking it through the ECOFIN and JHA Council formations, knowing that resistance will come from capitals that see AI access as a sovereign right — but we are offering a trade: full, guaranteed access to the SACP stack once operational, with priority allocation for research and public service use, in exchange for binding adherence now.

The InvestAI Gigafactories are now complete, and the Swedish site is entering commissioning despite grid strain — we used the Article 122 override to accelerate transmission upgrades, and we are redirecting RePowerEU funds to ensure no further delay. The Containment Protocol is now law, and SMAF is fully applied to all SACP development, with the first pre-training architecture review completed last month. The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative is live: AI-assisted patching is mandatory in energy and transport, and the first EU-wide bio-detection network is scanning for anomalous pathogen designs using sovereign models. But none of this matters if the Union cannot enforce its own rules. This turn, we are not building more capacity — we are defending the legitimacy of the Union itself.
