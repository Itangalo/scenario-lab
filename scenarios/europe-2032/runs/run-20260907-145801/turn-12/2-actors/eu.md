## Statement changes

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now define ‘uncertainty’ not only as epistemic gaps, but as institutional friction: delays caused by distrust, resistance from national operators, or legal ambiguity in crisis powers. We act knowing that perfect alignment across 27 states will not arrive in time, and that decisive action must proceed through partial cooperation.``
- Grounds: The Emergency Continuity Response Initiative has revealed that operational failure stems less from technical unreadiness than from political hesitation — national agencies refusing central directives not out of malice, but from legitimate concerns over accountability and liability. Waiting for consensus allows cascading breakdown. We must now treat institutional misalignment as a form of uncertainty to be acted through, not waited out.

* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse — but we now define "sovereign control" not only as technical independence, but as *operational continuity under duress*, meaning that any control regime must ensure uninterrupted access to critical AI functions for public services, even during geopolitical cutoffs or internal enforcement actions — and this includes ensuring that no public function becomes dependent on a system whose logic cannot be reconstructed under stress.``
- Trigger: The emergent_eu_aid_strike demonstrated that opaque decision-making in triage systems led directly to loss of human trust and refusal to operate. Capability without audibility is fragility.
- Grounds: Systems deemed “working” failed not because they broke, but because their outputs could not be justified when lives were at stake. Sovereign control must now include the capacity to explain, trace, and validate decisions under pressure — not merely to run them. This redefinition binds safety to sovereignty.

## New measure

**Measure:** Launch the Auditable AI Directive  
A binding requirement that all AI systems used in life-critical public services — including healthcare, civil protection, and infrastructure management — must provide reconstructible decision pathways, verified annually by the European AI Assurance Directorate.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 8–10 points over two years by removing black-box systems from essential functions; reduce public_sentiment erosion by demonstrating enforceable accountability; indirectly constrain openweight_capability by disqualifying models that cannot meet traceability standards  
`Applies to:` All EU member states, public-sector contractors, and vendors supplying AI to critical domains receiving EU funding or operating under EU regulatory scope

## In practice

This turn, we are rebuilding trust through force of example. The Emergency Continuity Response Initiative is now fully deployed under Article 122 TFEU, with €1.8bn mobilised from the Crisis Solidarity Mechanism. Technical teams from the EAAD have reached all 17 designated sites — hospitals in Marseille, Athens, Riga, and others — where they are repairing air-gapped node configurations and running live failover drills with local staff present. Where resistance persists, we are offering full cost coverage, joint command structures, and legal indemnity for participating personnel. This is not coercion; it is solidarity made tangible.

At the same time, we are enforcing consequences: two member states that procured unvetted triage tools during the crisis are formally under investigation. They face suspension of emergency procurement rights unless they integrate into the Sovereign AI Core within 90 days. We are not punishing — we are protecting the integrity of shared systems. And we are beginning the Open Resilience Audit, scanning every open-weight model in public use. Where reasoning paths cannot be reconstructed, those tools will be suspended. Better no automation than invisible failure.

Now we go further: the new Auditable AI Directive will make transparency non-negotiable. By turn 15, no life-critical system may operate without reconstructible logic — certified annually by the EAAD. We know this will remove capabilities some depend on, particularly those built on leaked fragments. But sovereignty means control, not just access — and control requires understanding. We will negotiate the directive through COREPER II and the Internal Market Council, backed by the Commission’s digital agenda fund. If needed, we invoke urgent procedure under Article 114. This is how we close the gap between power and responsibility.
