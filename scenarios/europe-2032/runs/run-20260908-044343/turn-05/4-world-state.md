### The Storm Breaks

A wave of coordinated ransomware cripples regional hospitals and transit systems across southern Europe. Generated by AI, the payloads adapt mid-attack, evading legacy defences. Attribution remains unclear, but forensic analysis points to tools derived from leaked cyber-agent frameworks — a direct consequence of the **emergent_research_leak_cascade**. The **cyber_major_incident** confirms that offensive AI has matured beyond demonstrations.

In response, the **Open Weight Governance Framework** is formally adopted, imposing strict liability on platforms hosting unverified models above 45.0 capability. However, Austria and Greece enter opt-in mode only, citing sovereignty concerns, weakening the initial enforcement footprint. Meanwhile, the **EU Defensive AI Corps** begins recruitment, though legal disputes delay deployment timelines by at least six months.

The Commission launches **Sovereign AI Testbeds for Critical Sectors**, repurposing EuroHPC infrastructure in Lyon, Warsaw, and Rotterdam. Early access is promised to member states that align with EU-wide AI governance, directly countering bilateral deals like Poland’s. But rollout faces hardware bottlenecks and inter-agency coordination delays.

Public sentiment worsens. Media coverage focuses on the cyberattacks and perceived fragmentation, with headlines questioning whether the EU can protect its own infrastructure. Job losses in tech and healthcare automation deepen resentment.
