## Priority
**Sovereign AI Testbeds for Critical Sectors** — because the **cyber_major_incident** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** have exposed our dependence on foreign AI infrastructure, and Poland’s defection proves that access without sovereignty is not access at all — we must now deliver working alternatives, not just rules.

## In practice  
We are closing the **Open Weight Governance Framework** trilogue this quarter. The final text will impose strict liability on platforms that distribute unattested models above 45.0 capability, backed by Europol’s new fingerprinting and telemetry tools. Hosting providers must either verify model capability and provenance or face liability for downstream misuse. Exemptions are carved for registered academic use, but the underground circulation of the Kimi K3-derived model has hardened resolve. Austria’s concerns over enforcement overreach are being met with a tiered compliance model: full integration is incentivised through access to the Defensive AI Corps’ threat feed and InvestAI resources, but opt-in participation is sufficient to avoid penalties. This is not coercion — it is the price of belonging to a common defence.

Meanwhile, the **EU Defensive AI Corps** is entering its first operational phase. Recruitment begins this month, with ENISA and Europol jointly standing up a 500-person core team by turn 6. Initial deployment will focus on high-exposure nodes in the Benelux, Nordics and Central Europe, using defensive AI agents trained on shared threat telemetry to detect and isolate polymorphic payloads in real time. Human-in-the-loop oversight remains strict, and auditability is built into every decision layer. Austria’s legal pushback is being addressed through modular integration — full access is not required, but those who opt in fully will be first in line for threat intelligence and counter-intrusion support.

The new **Sovereign AI Testbeds** initiative responds directly to Poland’s bilateral deal: we cannot compete on access alone, but we can offer something better — control. Through the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, we are repurposing underutilised EuroHPC capacity to stand up secure, high-assurance environments where national health systems, grid operators and transport authorities can integrate and stress-test AI systems without relying on US hyperscalers. These are not sandboxes — they are production-grade, with full telemetry, third-party monitoring and EU legal jurisdiction over all operations. The first three testbeds (health diagnostics in Lyon, grid resilience in Warsaw, and port automation in Rotterdam) will go live by turn 6, funded from the Digital Europe Programme and co-invested by member states. This is not about catching up — it is about building a different kind of lead, one rooted in jurisdiction, assurance and operational independence.
