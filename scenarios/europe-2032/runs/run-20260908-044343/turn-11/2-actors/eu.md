## In practice
The Sovereign Hardware Enforcement Initiative has formally concluded, marking the end of a six-turn sprint that consumed nearly all available political capital and left institutions strained. The physical layer of sovereign AI control now exists: open-specification interruption modules are being installed in critical infrastructure across three member states, with firmware signing restricted to EU authorities and full third-party auditability built into the design. This is not theoretical — it is hardware in the ground, capable of severing AI operations without foreign dependencies or provider cooperation. But its operational reach remains narrow, legally unharmonised, and politically fragile.

We are using this half-year to stabilise, not expand. ENISA leads a post-deployment review, assessing how the modules perform under real-world stress and whether their integration creates new vulnerabilities in tightly coupled systems. Legal services across the Commission are drafting a harmonisation proposal for cross-border activation protocols, to be ready when capital allows a return to legislative motion. Crucially, we are not forcing Germany’s hand on central authority; instead, BSI and ANSSI are deepening bilateral coordination through joint exercises simulating cascading failures in transnational energy grids. Poland’s semiconductor testing capacity is being folded into Eurotrust’s certification pipeline, strengthening eastern participation. But no new legal act will be tabled, no new funding line opened.

Public sentiment remains frozen at 20.0, anchored by perceptions of cost, delay, and invisibility — the modules do nothing until they are needed, and their value cannot be demonstrated without a crisis. Our transparency campaign reframes them as “trusted fail-safes,” borrowing legitimacy from fire extinguishers and circuit breakers: things you accept because you trust the system that maintains them, not because you understand them. This is not about adoption, nor diffusion — it is about preventing backlash. We are repairing cohesion, replenishing capital, and waiting for a signal: either an incident that demands action, or a breakthrough that restores momentum. For now, we hold.
