## In practice
We are treating the completion of the **Sovereign Assurance Stack** and **Emergency Runtime Interruption Protocol** as a necessary but insufficient milestone: they were built on the assumption that foreign secure enclaves would remain accessible and that runtime verification could precede enforcement. That assumption has collapsed. The **export_control_escalation** has cut off access to next-generation trusted execution environments, and the **openweight_frontier_release** has put systems within 8 points of the closed frontier into uncontrolled distribution — meaning that the most dangerous capabilities are already beyond reach of any pre-deployment control. We are now acting on the principle that sovereignty must be physical, open, and mandatory.

The **Sovereign Hardware Enforcement Initiative** is accelerating in Dresden and Grenoble, with Infineon and STMicroelectronics shifting production lines under a new Eurotrust certification regime that requires full open specifications, EU-only firmware signing, and third-party auditability. We are integrating this work with the **Eurotrust Certification and Integration Mandate**, which will legally require all AI systems in energy, transport, health and finance to use these modules by turn 12. This is not a recommendation: it will be enforced through the revised Critical Entities Resilience Directive, with non-compliant systems barred from public procurement and infrastructure access. Poland has formally joined the Eurotrust working group, bringing its semiconductor expertise and a renewed willingness to align on activation protocols — a shift we are reinforcing by offering Warsaw co-lead on the firmware verification track.

At the same time, we are confronting the reality that **eu_political_capital** is at 10.0 and **public_sentiment** remains hostile. We cannot launch broad new initiatives, but we can hardwire narrow ones into existing legal frameworks. That is why we are anchoring the mandate in CERD and ENISA’s audit authority, not creating new institutions. Germany’s legal objections to central activation will be met not with override, but with co-design: we are inviting Berlin to chair the activation threshold working group, on condition it accepts the binding nature of the hardware standard. This is sovereignty not as isolation, but as enforceable interdependence — the right to say *no* only matters if you have built the *yes* that follows.
