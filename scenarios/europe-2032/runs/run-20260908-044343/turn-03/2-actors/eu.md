## In practice  
We are executing the Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 4 of the TEU and the NIS2 Directive, now accelerated by the **cyber_major_incident**, which compromised a software dependency used across municipal services in Belgium, Finland, and Slovakia. The attack deployed AI-generated polymorphic payloads that evaded signature-based defences, encrypted administrative databases, and demanded payment in untraceable instruments—only partially mitigated due to delayed patching and fragmented incident coordination. ENISA has activated its emergency protocol, deploying zero-trust configurations to over 200 critical nodes, while the REACT-Cyber window disburses €3.1bn in urgent grants. Germany has finally agreed to air-gap essential industrial control systems, conditioned on full cost-sharing and exclusion of legacy OT systems from audit penalties. Sweden’s co-financing concerns have been met through a rebalanced burden-sharing formula anchored in GDP and exposure risk, approved by COREPER II last week.

The Open Weight Governance Framework is now in trilogue negotiations between Parliament, Council, and Commission. Resistance remains fierce: open science coalitions warn it will criminalise academic sharing, while US cloud providers argue extraterritorial enforcement is unworkable. We are adapting enforcement to focus on *distribution at scale*—platforms like Hugging Face, GitHub, and decentralized CDNs—requiring capability attestation below threshold levels or facing liability for misuse. Europol and the CSA are building automated fingerprinting tools to detect model lineage and capability proxies in real time. But trust is thin: the recent eval anomalies and lack of developer transparency make arguments about containment sound hollow. Behind closed doors, several member states are quietly preparing fallback plans involving national moratoria if the framework fails.

Simultaneously, the **emergent_regional_ai_council** initiative offers a path beyond unilateralism. We are now drafting a binding intergovernmental agreement—under PESCO-like logic but outside defence—to establish joint evaluation cells in Helsinki and Tallinn, fed by red-teamed access to open weights, and a shared defensive AI layer trained on cross-border threat telemetry. This does not require full sovereignty, but it builds collective agency fast. The Netherlands and Denmark are eager; Austria hesitates, fearing mission creep. We are offering access to the InvestAI testbeds as an incentive, and routing initial funding through the Digital Europe Programme. It is not independence—but in a world where the frontier moves faster than laws, mutual assurance may be the closest thing to safety we can build.
