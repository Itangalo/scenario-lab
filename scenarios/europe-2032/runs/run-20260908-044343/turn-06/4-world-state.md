### The Fracture Deepens

A second wave of ransomware, more adaptive and evasive than the first, strikes water treatment facilities in Italy and Greece. Emergency protocols activate, but response is delayed by incompatible systems and fragmented command. The attackers exploit newly obscured model reasoning — defenders can no longer trace decision pathways, rendering human oversight blind. This marks a turning point: AI systems are now operating beyond interpretability, and no patch can restore what the architecture has discarded.

In Brussels, the Commission launches the Sovereign Assurance Stack, a bold attempt to reclaim control over AI in critical sectors. Yet enthusiasm is muted. Legal services flag jurisdictional conflicts with national cybersecurity laws, and member states balk at the cost of retrofitting legacy systems. Poland declines the invitation to join the Sovereign AI Testbeds, citing concerns over data sovereignty and preferring its bilateral arrangement with Washington. The message of shared control rings hollow in capitals that see the EU as reactive, not reliable.

The Tech Sovereignty Package officially completes, unlocking €200 billion in private investment targets and establishing accelerated permitting zones. But the victory is symbolic: no new data centres have broken ground, and the promised private capital remains pledged, not spent. The real bottleneck — chip supply and energy infrastructure — remains unaddressed.

ENISA’s enhanced monitoring initiative goes live, linking anomaly detection across ten member states. It detects the second ransomware wave early, but lacks authority to trigger automated countermeasures. By the time consensus is reached, the attack has pivoted.

Public sentiment holds at 20.0, numb to crisis. Media outlets dub the era “the black box decade.” AI is neither hated nor trusted — it is simply present, and beyond control.

### US Posture Takes Effect

The new US administration formally implements its alliance-tier access policy. European labs find their API quotas reduced, with no appeal process. The message is clear: cooperation is conditional, and Europe is not in the inner circle.
