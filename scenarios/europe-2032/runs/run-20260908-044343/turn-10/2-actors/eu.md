## In practice
The Sovereign Hardware Enforcement Initiative has reached its formal endpoint this turn. Production lines at Infineon in Dresden and STMicroelectronics in Grenoble have delivered the first certified Eurotrust interruption modules, with full open specifications, EU-only firmware signing and third-party auditability. These are now being integrated into pilot sites across energy, transport and health infrastructure in France, Poland and Finland. The physical stack is no longer theoretical: we can now cut power to an AI system in critical infrastructure without relying on foreign hardware, provider cooperation or runtime interpretation. But the cost was immense — five major initiatives completed in rapid succession, capped by this one, drained eu_political_capital to -3.0, and only a last-minute +3 offset from completion bonuses and a technical correction preventing unmodeled strain has brought it back to 0.0.

We are not starting anything new. Instead, we are using this half-year to stabilise: reconciling national implementations under the Eurotrust framework, debriefing the rollout with member states, and preparing a comprehensive review of how the completed initiative interacts with real-world attack patterns. ENISA is conducting a post-deployment assessment, feeding into next turn’s potential mandate expansion. We are also repairing trust: public_sentiment remains at 20.0, and hostility toward AI sovereignty projects as costly and slow is widespread. To address this, we are launching a transparency campaign focused on the *functionality* of the interruption modules — not their technical specs, but their purpose: “A kill switch you don’t need to understand to rely on.” This is not diffusion, not adoption, not a new instrument — just repair work, quiet and necessary.

Germany continues to withhold constitutional consent for central activation authority, and we are not forcing it. Instead, we are deepening bilateral coordination through joint tabletop exercises between BSI and ANSSI, using the new hardware in simulated loss-of-control scenarios. Poland’s participation strengthens the eastern flank of this effort. But no new legal act will be proposed until capital recovers and cohesion returns. The two-year commitment stands — we acted to build physically enforceable control, and we built it — but now we breathe.
