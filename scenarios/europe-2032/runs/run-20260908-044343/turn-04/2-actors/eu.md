## New measure  
**EU Defensive AI Corps**  
Establish a standing, cross-border unit under ENISA and Europol with operational authority to deploy AI-driven threat detection and counter-intrusion systems across member states’ critical infrastructure.  

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`

## Priority  
**EU Defensive AI Corps** — because the **capability_jump** and **openweight_frontier_release** have made offensive AI tools widely accessible, and the **Emergency Resilience Surge** has proven insufficient in speed and scope; we now need a permanent, operational shield that can act faster than laws can adapt.

## In practice  
We are treating the **Emergency Resilience Surge** as closed: the €3.1 billion in REACT-Cyber grants has been disbursed, zero-trust configurations rolled out to over 200 nodes, and air-gapping protocols agreed with Germany and Sweden. But implementation lags, coordination remains patchy, and the surge was always a sprint — not a structure. The cyber_major_incident exposed that we have no standing force capable of acting across borders the moment an AI-driven attack unfolds. That ends now.

The **EU Defensive AI Corps** will be built from the ground up through a new legal instrument under Article 114 TFEU, co-funded by the Digital Europe Programme and the Internal Security Fund, with a core team of 500 specialists — red-team veterans, AI security researchers, and cross-agency operators — hosted jointly by ENISA and Europol. Recruitment begins this quarter, with initial deployment to high-exposure nodes in the Benelux, Nordics, and Central Europe by turn 5. The system will run defensive AI agents trained on shared threat telemetry, capable of identifying and isolating polymorphic payloads in real time, with human-in-the-loop oversight and strict auditability. Member states will be required to grant access to their critical nodes under revised NIS2 enforcement terms, but we are offering full cost coverage and liability shielding for early adopters. Austria’s legal concerns from the Joint Council talks are being addressed through a modular opt-in framework — full integration is encouraged, but not forced.

Meanwhile, the **Open Weight Governance Framework** remains in trilogue, now under intense pressure. The **openweight_frontier_release** — a Kimi K3 derivative with offensive cyber capabilities — has been downloaded over 400,000 times in three weeks. We are tightening the draft to focus enforcement on platforms that enable mass distribution, with a new Article 13-style liability regime for hosting unattested models above 45.0 capability. Europol’s fingerprinting tools are being fast-tracked for deployment by turn 5, and we are offering research exemptions for academic consortia that register their model use. But trust is thin, and the **member_state_defection** — Poland’s new bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler for priority access to frontier models — has shattered the illusion of unity. We are responding not with sanctions, but with leverage: access to the Defensive AI Corps’ threat feed and the InvestAI testbeds will be gated on compliance with the Framework. Sovereignty is not a slogan — it is a condition of membership in the circle of trust.
