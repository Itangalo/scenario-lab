### The Edge of Control

A sudden leap in AI capability—focused in cyber operations and code generation—ripples through global systems. Though general competence rises only modestly, the jump is real and weaponisable: attackers now deploy self-modifying intrusion tools that evade static detection, exploiting the same opacity that defeated human oversight in the recent water infrastructure attacks. Simultaneously, investor confidence evaporates. Major AI ventures cancel expansion plans, and several European compute projects dependent on private funding collapse overnight.

Brussels responds with urgency. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme officially completes, symbolising a milestone in sovereign infrastructure—yet no physical sites are operational. The promised €200 billion remains unspent, blocked by unresolved supply bottlenecks. The **Sovereign Assurance Stack** launches in pilot mode in Lyon and Rotterdam, enforcing model provenance in health and logistics. But its inability to halt rogue systems exposes a fatal gap: verification without interruption is not control.

To close it, the EU fast-tracks the **Emergency Runtime Interruption Protocol**, mandating hardware-enforced kill switches in critical infrastructure. The move is bold, but legal resistance mounts. Germany demands veto rights over activation; Italy questions compatibility with existing industrial control systems. Only France and the Benelux countries fully commit.

The **EU Defensive AI Corps** reaches 60% staffing, but cross-border command disputes persist. Austria joins under a mutual aid framework, but Poland remains outside, its U.S. partnership fraying as American API access tightens. In a quiet diplomatic overture, the EU offers Warsaw co-ownership of the kill switch firmware—an attempt to turn dependency into partnership.

Public sentiment holds at 20.0. The era of the “black box” deepens. AI is no longer just untrusted—it is seen as fundamentally ungovernable.

### Sovereignty in Freefall

Despite efforts, EU AI sovereignty erodes further. The **capability_jump** widens the gap with the frontier, while the **ai_investment_collapse** severs access to externally funded capacity. No new sovereign compute comes online. Political capital, though slightly bolstered by finishing two large measures, remains critically low—just enough to act, but not to compel.
