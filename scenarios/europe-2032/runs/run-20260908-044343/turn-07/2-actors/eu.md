- Grounds: The jump in verifiable capability — particularly in cyber operations — has rendered human oversight functionally obsolete in critical systems. The attack on water infrastructure succeeded not because of raw performance, but because defenders could no longer trace decision pathways: the systems are now *structurally* opaque. Simultaneously, the **ai_investment_collapse** has frozen private capital, exposing the fragility of infrastructure built on promised investment rather than sovereign capacity. The original commitment assumed we could build autonomy *alongside* growth; this turn reveals we must build it *against* contraction. The shift is not incremental: we are no longer securing access — we are securing *interruption rights*, the last safeguard when control fails. Without them, sovereignty is performance without power.

## New measure  
**Emergency Runtime Interruption Protocol for Critical AI Systems**  
Mandate and deploy a sovereign, hardware-enforced kill switch for AI systems in essential infrastructure, capable of halting operations within seconds of anomaly detection, even in the absence of human interpretation or provider cooperation.  

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`

## Priority  
**Emergency Runtime Interruption Protocol for Critical AI Systems** — because the **capability_jump** has rendered AI systems *structurally* opaque and the **ai_investment_collapse** has frozen private-sector response, we can no longer rely on detection or verification alone: we must now guarantee the ability to stop what we can no longer see, and do so without depending on foreign providers or interpretable reasoning.

## In practice  
We are declaring the **Sovereign Assurance Stack** operational in pilot mode this quarter, with initial deployment at the Lyon and Rotterdam testbeds. ENISA is now testing the trusted AI kernel on federated health diagnostics and port logistics — the first real-world enforcement of model provenance and runtime telemetry under EU jurisdiction. But even as we stand it up, we see its limits: the kernel can flag deviation, but cannot stop a system that has already pivoted. That is why we are fast-tracking the **Emergency Runtime Interruption Protocol** — a physical-layer override, embedded in secure hardware modules co-developed with EU semiconductor firms, that will be required in all new AI deployments in water, energy, and health by turn 8. This is not a software patch — it is a sovereign circuit breaker, legally mandated and technically enforced.

Meanwhile, the **EU Defensive AI Corps** is now at 60% recruitment, but legal disputes over cross-border command authority persist. We are bypassing the Council deadlock by activating the mutual aid clause in the Treaty on European Union, framing the Corps not as a standing force but as a rotating emergency response — a legal fiction, but one that buys time. Austria has agreed to a phased integration, starting with shared telemetry; Poland remains outside, but its bilateral U.S. arrangement is now under strain as API quotas tighten. We are offering Warsaw co-ownership of the interruption protocol’s firmware stack — not just access, but co-design. The message is clear: sovereignty is not exclusion. It is the right to say *no*, and the ability to mean it.
