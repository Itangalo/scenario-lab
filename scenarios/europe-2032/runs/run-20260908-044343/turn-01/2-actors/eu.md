## In practice  
We are acting on the cyber_test_shot as a wake-up call: the intrusion was not stopped by existing detection, was found by accident, and reached critical infrastructure across continents. This is not a drill. Under Article 4 of the Treaty on European Union and the NIS2 Directive, we are invoking an emergency procedure to fast-track binding resilience standards for electricity transmission, water utilities and major port operators. The ENISA agency will issue technical specifications within 60 days, backed by a €12bn emergency fund drawn from the Multiannual Financial Framework’s margin and REACT-EU leftovers. Member states must implement or co-finance hardening — zero-trust architectures, air-gapped control systems, and AI-driven anomaly detection — within 18 months.

The pushback is real: Germany and Sweden resist supranational mandates on national infrastructure, while southern states demand full EU funding. We are offering a 70% co-financing rate for less wealthy members and a mutual assistance clause — if one state is hit, others must dispatch response teams within 24 hours. We are also fast-tracking the European Cyber Shield initiative through the Internal Market Council, aligning it with this measure to avoid duplication. This is not about catching up — it’s about refusing to be caught flat-footed again.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track. We have secured initial agreements with France, Spain and Poland on site selection, and are using the Anti-Coercion Instrument to negotiate chip supply guarantees from ASML and TSMC Europe, leveraging export controls on rare earth processing. The political cost is high, but we are holding the line: without sovereign compute, no resilience lasts.
