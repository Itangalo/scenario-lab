## Priority  
**Emergency Activation Protocol Pilot (EAPP)** is my priority this turn because it directly addresses the core failure of the Sovereign Hardware Enforcement Initiative: capability without operability. With political capital at zero and public sentiment hostile, we cannot launch a broad sovereignty push—but we can run a bounded, high-signal experiment that proves the Union can act when it matters. This is not just technical rehearsal; it is political proof of concept.

## In practice  
We are not waiting for full legal harmonisation to begin restoring agency. Instead, we are launching the Emergency Activation Protocol Pilot under Article 12 of the Cyber Resilience Act, using existing crisis derogations to bypass the stalled cross-border activation framework. The pilot will run in three phases across six months: first, joint simulations in the Franco-German energy corridor; second, live firmware-level interruption drills in the Polish-Dutch rail logistics network; third, a no-notice test in the Mediterranean port coordination system where openweight agents have already shown emergent behaviour. ENISA will lead, with BSI, ANSSI, and NASK as operational partners. Each test will be witnessed by national parliaments’ digital committees and streamed—selectively—to the public through Eurovision’s public service channel.

Germany remains reluctant, but we have secured its participation by limiting the first phase to simulation and by co-hosting the exercise with the Bundeswehr’s Cyber Command. France has agreed to fast-track liability waivers for participating operators. Poland sees strategic value in eastern visibility and has offered its semiconductor testbed as a verification node. We are not requesting new funding—this runs on carryover from the Sovereign Hardware Enforcement Initiative—but we are burning goodwill: every day of live testing risks triggering the very instability we aim to contain. That is the point. We must show that sovereignty is not just hardware on a shelf, but a chain of command that holds. If we succeed, we rebuild capital. If we fail, we will have done so visibly, deliberately, and in a way that forces the debate we have long evaded.
