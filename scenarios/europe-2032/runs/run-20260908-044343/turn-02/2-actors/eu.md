## New measure  
**Open Weight Governance Framework**  
Establish binding EU-wide rules for the release and distribution of open-weight models above capability thresholds tied to current risk domains, with enforcement through digital gateways and hosting liability.

## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the **cyber_test_shot** has already demonstrated that detection failed and that essential systems are exposed; we must complete hardening before the next incident, and this surge is our only instrument moving at the necessary scale and urgency.

## In practice  
We are executing the Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 4 of the TEU and the NIS2 Directive, with ENISA now finalising technical specs for zero-trust architectures in energy grids and port logistics. The €12bn emergency fund is being disbursed through a new REACT-Cyber window, prioritising member states that commit to full air-gapping of industrial control systems. Germany’s resistance to supranational mandates is being met with bilateral assurances: we will not override national ownership of infrastructure, but we *will* require compliance with common minimums, backed by mutual aid obligations. Sweden’s concerns about cost are addressed through a revised co-financing model—wealthier members contribute more to the central pot, ensuring southern and eastern states aren’t left behind.

The InvestAI Gigafactories are advancing slowly. Site selection in France and Spain is complete, but grid connection timelines remain delayed by permitting bottlenecks—precisely what the Tech Sovereignty Package aims to fix. We are fast-tracking environmental assessments through the European Climate Law framework, treating compute infrastructure as strategic climate resilience. TSMC Europe and ASML are engaged via the Anti-Coercion Instrument: rare earth processing concessions are on the table in exchange for guaranteed lithography tool delivery. Still, `eu_ai_sovereignty` continues to fall relative to global capability, and public frustration grows.

Now, with the **capability_jump** in verifiable domains and the **eval_anomaly_reports** suggesting unexplained agent behaviours, we cannot assume that safety gaps will remain containable. The Open Weight Governance Framework responds to the widening risk: Kimi K3 showed that once a model is released, it cannot be recalled. We are drafting a regulation under the AI Act’s high-risk provisions to prohibit open releases above a threshold calibrated to current vulnerability in cyber and bio domains. Hosting platforms and CDNs operating in the EU would be liable for distribution unless they verify model capability below the line. This is enforceable at chokepoints—cloud APIs, app stores, peer-to-peer indexing services—and we are working with Europol and the CSA to build detection capacity. It will face fierce opposition from open science advocates and US-based foundations funding open labs, but we cannot let the frontier’s instability become everyone’s problem. We act under uncertainty, and this is its price.
