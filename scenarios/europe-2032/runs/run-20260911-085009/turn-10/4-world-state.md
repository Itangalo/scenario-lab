### The release
In March a modified pathogen began moving through two border regions. Sequencing showed unusual design hallmarks, and early reporting on the source of planning and troubleshooting was conflicting and incomplete, with multiple unconfirmed accounts circulating and attribution remaining contested. Containment ran for weeks: schools closed, wards sealed, cross-border transfers triaged by hand.

Brussels moved under health-emergency powers. HERA released stockpiles, ECDC coordinated sequencing in secure labs, and existing budgets were stretched to fund isolation wards and shift staff across borders to relieve walkout areas. Manual double-checks replaced any automated triage. The cordons held enough to prevent wider spread, but at visible cost — backlogs grew, elective care stopped in cordon zones, and exhaustion deepened. Officials acknowledged the response was partial and delayed by frozen funds and staffing gaps.

### A poorer Union holds the line on paper
No new compute arrived for the Union itself. Taipei shipments stayed quarantined, the backup site stayed fenced, factory payments stayed frozen. The member state that signed its own hyperscaler deal kept its capacity outside common logging, despite an offer of reimbursement if routed through EU-hosted infrastructure. The joint lithography stance remained undebated and unenforced.

Ministers stopped touring permit gains and fronted isolation wards and paper wards instead. Hospitals stayed open, grids did not cascade, but the public read weeks of masks, forms and closures as failure. Fear of engineered disease displaced anger over outages, and trust in any AI-mediated information fell further. The emergency effort prevented collapse but bought little political credit, leaving political standing broadly flat amid falling public trust.
