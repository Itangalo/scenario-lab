### Cash at last, leverage lost
The wage-bridge finally began to pay. After months of backlog, employment offices in France, Spain, Poland and elsewhere cleared first tranches of wage insurance, six to twelve months for former junior coders, analysts and support staff, paired with short vouchers for supervised roles in cyber-hygiene, care-tech and grid maintenance. Queues shortened slightly. Business federations still denounced the levy on automating employers as a tax on efficiency, and mayors said the money was months late, but for some households the transfer was the first tangible proof Brussels had noticed.

It was overshadowed by machines and ships.

In autumn Washington ordered a further cut to servicing and exports of Dutch lithography equipment — not only leading-edge tools but older machines used for ordinary chips, and quiet pressure to extend restraint to other customers. The Hague complied after a blunt exchange about American technology in the supply chain. Trade officials in Brussels filed a protest and asked for volume-licence waivers for European fabs; the answer was a wait. Editorial pages called it the moment Europe's one chokepoint was used, and not by Europe.

At the same time extended manoeuvres in the Taiwan Strait pushed shipping insurance up and delayed several electronics consignments. No blockade, no break, but procurement officers already told to wait for high-end accelerators added weeks to their forecasts.

Municipal rebuilds crawled forward. National response teams pushed cleaned images to replace the compromised update, while grid operators kept the homes-and-hospitals-first curtailment order in force. Power stayed expensive but on. Entry-level hiring did not return.

Around construction fences for new data centres and a few substations, night-time intrusions and graffiti multiplied — single-source reports, rumours of coordination, anger linking bills and lost first jobs to concrete being poured. Police treated them as vandalism; local press treated them as a mood.
The finishing date for the retraining scheme did not move: with funds only just flowing, no acceleration was credible, and Brussels did not claim one.
