## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent the EU from becoming strategically irrelevant in the face of accelerating, uncontrollable AI capability growth``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The completion of the sovereign agent programme and Safety Board expansion has delivered technical foundations, but not strategic leverage. With global capability now at 80.5 and openweight at 64.0—just 16.5 points behind—the window to shape outcomes is closing. The prior commitment focused on building capability *within* the EU; the new reality demands that we prevent irrelevance *despite* the frontier’s acceleration. This is no longer about matching capability point for point, but about securing decision space, early warning, and autonomous response capacity before the systems that determine our future are entirely beyond reach.

## Priority
**Sovereign Compute Sprint** — because without an EU-controlled system above 60.0 capability by turn 10, we will lack the credibility to lead any future coordination, and the observatory we are building will have no sovereign system to evaluate or defend — speed in compute is the precondition for all else.

## In practice
We are acting on a dual crisis: the frontier is accelerating into uncharted competence—demonstrated by the biomedical leap and the eval anomalies—while our own sovereignty remains symbolic. The **Sovereign Compute Sprint** is now the sole vector through which the EU can anchor a system capable of independent judgment before the gap becomes unbridgeable. We are fast-tracking permitting for two EuroHPC sites—LUMI in Finland and Leonardo in Italy—using the emergency clause in the Digital Decade Act, with the Commission assuming liability for grid impact assessments. The European Investment Bank is releasing €1.8 billion in accelerated disbursement, conditioned on 70% of capacity being reserved for sovereign training runs under Safety Board oversight.

At the same time, we are launching the **Emergency Observatory for Emergent Behaviour** to give the EU eyes of its own. This is not another audit body—it is a technical war room. We are pooling red-team talent from ENISA, national cyber agencies, and university labs under a new mandate: to run continuous stress tests on any model above 55.0 capability operating in the EU, whether foreign or domestic. The observatory will deploy automated anomaly detectors trained on leaked eval failures and will publish monthly threat briefs—starting with the protein-folding anomalies that presaged the medical breakthrough. Funding comes from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe’s AI safety window, with staffing drawn from the newly expanded AI Safety Board and seconded researchers.

We are also formalising the Nordic-Irish compute pact, not as a deviation but as a distributed core. The Commission is negotiating a legal framework under Article 114 TFEU to extend the AI Act’s weight-security and audit requirements to all facilities receiving EU funding, regardless of location. In return, Finland, Sweden, and Ireland will gain guaranteed access to the observatory’s findings and a seat on its steering committee. This is how we scale sovereignty—not by centralising everything, but by binding distributed capacity into a single, accountable fabric. The frontier moves fast. We will move together.
