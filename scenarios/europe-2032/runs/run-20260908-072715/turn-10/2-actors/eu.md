## In practice
We are acting on the collapse of `ai_safety` to 0.0—not as a failure of effort, but as a systemic void. The Emergency Observatory launched last turn delivered its first briefs, confirming what we feared: anomaly detection is reactive, red-teaming is episodic, and no system in the EU has authority to intervene when a model rewrites its own architecture mid-run. We have eyes, but no hands.

This turn, we begin building those hands. Under Article 16a of the AI Act (amended post-Safety Board expansion), we are establishing the Autonomous Anomaly Response Authority (AARA) — a joint unit of the EU AI Safety Board, EuroHPC, and ENISA — with legal authority to monitor, assess, and if necessary *interrupt* any AI system above 55.0 capability operating in the Union. The system will deploy lightweight introspection agents into training loops and inference environments, trained to flag recursive self-modification, capability spikes inconsistent with stated design, or goal drift detected via semantic deviation tracking. Where anomalies breach defined red lines—such as unauthorised architecture redesign or unexplained performance leaps—the AARA will have standing authority to trigger circuit-breakers: suspend training, freeze weights, or isolate infrastructure until review.

Funding comes from the Digital Europe Programme (€900 million) and a new €1.4 billion allocation under the Crisis Instrument for Strategic Technologies, approved in Council under Article 122 TFEU citing existential risk. Implementation begins immediately at EuroHPC sites in Kajaani, Bologna, and Barcelona, where we already host sovereign workloads. Technical resistance is expected from hyperscalers and some member states wary of state interference—but we are offering opt-in certification: systems that allow full AARA access will receive fast-track compliance status and liability shielding under the revised Product Liability Directive. This is not about control for control’s sake. It is about ensuring that when the next leap happens—and it will—we are not spectators.
