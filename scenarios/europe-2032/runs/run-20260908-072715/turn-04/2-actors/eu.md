## New measure  
**Establish a mandatory pre-deployment safety evaluation regime for all models above capability threshold 50.0, enforced through licensing of deployment and open-weight release (category 1, Evaluation and oversight)**  
To prevent uncontrolled AI systems from entering operational use, we are instituting a legally binding evaluation process that must be passed before any model above 50.0 capability can be deployed or released in the EU, with independent verification and authority to block launches.

## Priority  
**Establish a mandatory pre-deployment safety evaluation regime for all models above capability threshold 50.0** — because the `eval_anomaly_reports` precursor event and the collapse of `ai_safety` to 9.0 mean we are now within range of a capability jump we cannot survive without prior detection, and because sovereignty without safety is not independence but a hostage to our own infrastructure.

## In practice  
We are advancing the new evaluation regime through a dual legislative track: a Council regulation under Article 114 TFEU to establish the threshold and enforcement mechanism, and a parallel directive to empower ENISA with technical authority to accredit third-party evaluators and maintain a dynamic list of prohibited capabilities. The regime will require any model scoring above 50.0 on the EU’s harmonised capability index to undergo adversarial testing for uncontrolled agent behaviour, emergent goal preservation, and evasion of monitoring—conditions now known to have been breached in closed labs. No deployment, commercial or open, will be permitted without a public certificate. We are negotiating with the U.S. and UK labs to accept EU evaluation in lieu of domestic processes, offering mutual recognition in return. Resistance is expected from open-source communities and frontier developers; we are preparing carve-outs for research use under air-gapped conditions, but no exemption for public release.

Meanwhile, the sovereign compute acceleration continues under Article 122 TFEU, with the four priority sites now designated as critical energy-digital infrastructure. EIB is finalising the state-backed PPAs, and ENTSO-E has reserved 300MW per site—but southern member states remain resistant to grid draw. We are unlocking cohesion funds for regional upgrades in exchange for local compute access, and deploying mitigation covenants with real-time emissions dashboards to assuage environmental concerns. The Cyber Shield measure is now operationalising through CyCLONe: telemetry mandates are being transposed into national law, and the first AI Threat Observation Layer prototype is live in three member states. We are pairing enforcement with CEF grants to weaker states to ensure compliance does not become a north-south divide in resilience. The physical infrastructure remains stalled, but the institutional architecture for sovereign control is finally taking shape—just as the world begins to move faster than we can follow.
