## In practice  
We are treating InvestAI Gigafactories as a strategic infrastructure emergency. Under Article 122 TFEU, we are proposing a Council regulation to designate the four priority sites as critical energy-digital projects, triggering derogations from normal permitting timelines under the Environmental Impact Assessment Directive. The Commission is negotiating with ENTSO-E and national grid operators to reserve 300MW of dispatchable power for each site by 2028, backed by state-guaranteed power purchase agreements to de-risk private investment. Resistance is strongest in member states concerned about energy equity and local environmental impact; we are offering co-investment in regional grid modernisation and a 15% allocation of compute capacity for national public services as compensation. The legal base is Article 172 TFEU for cohesion funding and Article 114 for internal market harmonisation, with the Council’s Competitiveness formation as the lead negotiating body.  

The Tech sovereignty package is being reframed around enforceable obligations: any data centre receiving accelerated permitting must commit to EU legal jurisdiction, data residency, and a minimum 40% availability for public sector and research use. We are working with EIB to structure a blended finance vehicle that makes compliance cheaper than circumvention. Early resistance from hyperscalers is expected; we will meet it with the credible threat of withholding access to the very grid and land advantages we are offering.  

All of this rests on the political capital gained from framing compute not as a tech bet, but as energy sovereignty and industrial survival. The cyber_test_shot and eu_frontier_access_denied events are being used in communications to member states to stress that dependence is not just a risk to competitiveness — it is a live vulnerability. We are not waiting for consensus; we are building it through irreversible momentum.
