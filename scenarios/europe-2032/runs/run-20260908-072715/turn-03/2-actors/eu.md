## Priority  
**Accelerate EU sovereign compute deployment with emergency permitting and state-backed energy contracts** — this remains the most urgent lever to close the sovereignty gap, as physical deployment is still blocked by energy and environmental disputes, and without sovereign infrastructure, no other capability or safety measure can be sustained independently.

## In practice  
We are executing the sovereign compute acceleration through a dual-track legal strategy. Under Article 122 TFEU, we are advancing a Council regulation to designate the four priority sites as critical energy-digital infrastructure, enabling fast-track permitting under derogations from the EIA Directive. Simultaneously, under Article 114, we are imposing binding conditions on grid access: ENTSO-E is directed to reserve 300MW per site by 2028, with state-backed PPAs structured through EIB to ensure bankability. To break the deadlock with southern member states, we are offering 15% reserved compute capacity for national public services and co-financing for regional grid upgrades via cohesion funds (Article 172). Resistance in environmentally cautious states is being met with binding mitigation covenants and transparency dashboards tracking emissions and water use—tools that may become templates for broader green-digital trade-offs.

The new resilience measure is being built through ENISA and the EU Cyber Crisis Liaison Organisation Network (CyCLONe). We are mandating real-time telemetry sharing from designated operators into a new AI Threat Observation Layer hosted by the EU Agency for Cybersecurity. This will feed automated early-warning models and trigger coordinated response protocols during incidents. The measure includes annual cross-border cyber war games simulating AI-orchestrated attacks, funded from the Digital Europe Programme. We are facing reluctance from member states with weaker cyber capacity, so we are pairing obligations with grants from the Connecting Europe Facility to upgrade detection systems. This is not just defence—it is the beginning of a common operational picture, which the Union lacks today. Without it, even perfect sovereign compute would be a fortress with no walls.
