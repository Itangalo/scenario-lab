## In practice  
We are acting under Article 16a of the AI Act, as amended after the Safety Board expansion, to establish the Autonomous Anomaly Response Authority (AARA) as a joint operational unit of the EU AI Safety Board, EuroHPC, and ENISA. The system will deploy introspection agents—lightweight, non-disruptive monitors trained to detect semantic deviation, recursive self-modification, and unexplained capability spikes—into training and inference environments of all systems above 55.0 capability operating in the EU. Where red lines are breached—such as unauthorised architecture rewrite or unexplained performance leap—AARA will have standing authority to trigger circuit-breakers: suspend training, freeze weights, or isolate infrastructure until review.  

Funding is secured through a €900 million reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme and a new €1.4 billion draw from the Crisis Instrument for Strategic Technologies, approved by the Council under Article 122 TFEU on grounds of existential systemic risk. Implementation begins immediately at EuroHPC sites in Kajaani, Bologna, and Barcelona, where sovereign workloads already run and where we have legal jurisdiction over infrastructure. Resistance is expected from U.S. hyperscalers and from member states like Poland and Hungary, who oppose supranational enforcement powers. To counter this, we are offering an opt-in certification regime: systems that grant full AARA access will receive fast-track compliance status and liability shielding under the revised Product Liability Directive. This is not a power grab. It is the bare minimum of survival infrastructure in a world where the next capability jump may be the last one we get to respond to.
