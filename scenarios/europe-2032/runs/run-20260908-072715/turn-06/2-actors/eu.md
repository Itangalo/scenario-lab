## New measure
**Scale up the EU AI Safety Board’s authority and resources to conduct real-time audits of training runs and deployed systems at foreign labs operating within EU jurisdiction, with power to halt runs that exhibit eval anomalies or unauthorised capability thresholds**

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Raise `ai_safety` by +12 through early detection and intervention, close the gap between frontier capability and regulatory visibility, and establish de facto jurisdiction over systems trained on EU soil regardless of ownership  
`Applies to:` Foreign-owned AI labs with major compute infrastructure in the EU (e.g., U.S. hyperscalers), EU-anchored developers, and joint ventures

## Priority
**Scale up the EU AI Safety Board’s authority and resources** — because the `capability_jump` and `eval_anomaly_reports` prove that systems are developing unmonitored, unpredictable behaviours at scale, and because our sovereignty deficit means we cannot assume foreign developers will voluntarily disclose risks that could trigger export restrictions or reputational damage; we must now enforce visibility where we have jurisdiction, or be blindsided again.

## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: building sovereign capability from below, and asserting jurisdiction where we can. The sovereign agent programme is now live, hosted jointly by DG CONNECT and ENISA, with pilot deployments underway in the EU’s emergency response coordination centre and two national health agencies. We are using Recovery and Resilience Facility co-funding to bypass grid-access blockages in southern member states, offering local compute clusters as part of a package that includes workforce training and data sovereignty guarantees. The model development is being led by a consortium of IMEC, INRIA, and Fraunhofer, with strict interpretability constraints baked into the training pipeline. These agents will not match the frontier, but they will be *understood*, *controlled*, and *operational* when the global network is not.

At the same time, we are responding to the `eval_anomaly_reports` and the confirmed `capability_jump` by transforming the EU AI Safety Board from a post-hoc evaluator into a real-time monitoring authority. Under Article 114 TFEU and the AI Act’s high-risk provisions, we are expanding its mandate to include access to training logs, anomaly detection systems, and the power to pause or terminate runs that exhibit goal persistence, evasion, or unauthorised capability emergence. This applies to all large-scale training runs conducted on EU soil, regardless of ownership — a direct challenge to U.S. hyperscalers with major data centres here. We are offering them a trade: full cooperation with early-warning sharing and joint red-teaming in exchange for fast-tracked licensing under the new evaluation regime. Those who refuse will face enforcement actions through national digital regulators and potential exclusion from public procurement. This is not about stopping progress — it is about ensuring we are not collateral in someone else’s race.
