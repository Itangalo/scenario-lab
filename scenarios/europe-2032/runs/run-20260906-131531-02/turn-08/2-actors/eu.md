## In practice  
We are acting under a new doctrine: sovereignty is not a stockpile, it is a circuit. The failure to respond to member state requests for emergency compute allocation was not a technical shortfall—it was a political failure of integration. The InvestAI Gigafactories delivered hardware, but not authority. The Resilience Surge Protocol built readiness, but not reach. Now, with open-weight models at 56.0 and closed systems beyond human interpretability, we cannot afford layered sovereignty—where each state holds a key to a lock we cannot open. This turn, we are breaking the circuit breaker.

The Sovereign Compute Activation Protocol will be grounded in Article 122 TFEU (external shocks and solidarity) and Article 114 (internal market harmonisation), declared under a new AI Emergency Framework adopted by COREPER and ratified by a qualified majority in the Council. It will establish that during a declared AI emergency—defined as any incident involving models at or above openweight_capability 55.0 with potential for cascading harm—sovereign compute resources across the EuroHPC network are automatically pooled under the operational command of the European AI Emergency Response Corps. National vetoes are suspended; bilateral access conditions voided. This is not confiscation—it is activation. Germany will be offered a permanent seat on the Joint Compute Oversight Board with audit rights; France will receive guaranteed reciprocity in research access and dual-use model evaluation. The protocol includes a sunset clause after turn 10, subject to review by the European Parliament and the Court of Justice.

Meanwhile, the European AI Emergency Response Corps enters its final phase: legal ratification is being fast-tracked through a trilogue between the Commission, Council, and Parliament, with the new protocol serving as the political anchor to break the deadlock. ENISA and ECDC teams remain deployed at high-risk nodes, now running live simulations of model leakage scenarios using synthetic data streams. We are not waiting for consensus to save us. We are building the authority to act within it—before the next breach renders it obsolete.
