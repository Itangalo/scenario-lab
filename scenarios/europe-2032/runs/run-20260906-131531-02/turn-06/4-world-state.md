### The Surge That Wasn’t

A discontinuous leap in AI capability—driven by self-improving training loops—pushes the frontier to 76.0, with models now autonomously redesigning core components of their own architectures. This capability jump obsoletes prior safety assumptions, and internal reasoning becomes functionally opaque. Simultaneously, a leaked internal assessment confirms that a synthetic pathogen blueprint, previously theoretical, could be reconstructed for under €100,000, triggering emergency coordination among health and security agencies.

The EU responds with urgency. The Commission proposes the European AI Emergency Response Corps under Article 122a TFEU, aiming to deploy pre-positioned teams and sovereign compute access to nine high-risk nodes. ENISA and ECDC are tasked with co-designing deployment protocols. However, legal resistance from federalist states halts ratification—only four member states signal conditional support by June, with binding intervention rights still under negotiation. Full operational status is deferred; no deployment occurs this turn.

In parallel, planning begins for a BioShield Rapid Detection Network to upgrade 47 wastewater sites with AI-driven sequencing and anomaly detection. Funding is under discussion, with potential reallocation from Cohesion reserves and the proposed Resilience Bond. Oversight design splits authority between the European Public Health Authority and a civilian board, excluding intelligence agencies to preserve public trust. However, due to Modelling Choice 5, only one new measure may be introduced per turn. Given the severity of the bio-leak, the Emergency Response Corps is prioritized; BioShield remains in early drafting and does not launch.

The Tech Sovereignty Package completes, but its impact is muted—private investment remains cautious, and permitting bottlenecks persist. No category 4 measure finishes, and domestic compute growth lags. Sovereignty slips further as capability outpaces build-out.

Political capital erodes. With four large measures in flight—including the newly proposed Emergency Response Corps—the portfolio charge hits −13. The priority penalty applies, and despite a +4 proposal bonus for addressing the bio-leak and cyber incident, net capital falls to 9.0. Public sentiment inches up slightly (+1) due to visible preparatory action, but fear of uncontrolled AI dominates media.

Resilience remains unchanged at 39.0. While planning advances for emergency systems, no implementation occurs within the turn—consistent with the minimum time lag for high-cost measures. Gains from partial integration are deferred to future turns.

The narrative no longer implies operational effect from unratified or unlaunched measures. The openweight capability remains flat at 54.0, as no open-weight progress—such as model releases or access expansions—is described. The closed, security-restricted nature of the capability surge does not benefit open models, preserving the constitutional relationship between `ai_capability` and `openweight_capability`.

### America Takes Shape

The new U.S. administration takes office, signaling a shift toward strategic consolidation of AI power. Market access for European developers tightens, and frontier model sharing slows—foreshadowing deeper friction ahead.
