## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now acknowledge that capability without resilience is sovereignty in name only. The power to act means nothing if society fractures under the strain of what we enable.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer accept being ignored, nor being blind. When action is refused at the point of implementation, we will shift from persuasion to compellence; when information is withheld, we will seize the means to see.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI capacity to act with sovereignty in an accelerating world, and ensure it is not hollow: capacity without control is dependence dressed as autonomy, and control without societal absorption is power without legitimacy.  

  - Grounds: The convergence of *capability_jump*, *emergent_energy_backlash*, and *election_annulled* this turn forces a reckoning. The leap to 63.0 in ai_capability outpaces not just our compute but our ability to govern its effects; the annulment of an election in a peer democracy on grounds of AI-driven manipulation confirms that the threat is no longer technical but existential to self-rule; and the energy backlash proves that even if we build sovereign infrastructure, it will be torn down unless it is rooted in public endurance. Our original commitment was to build capacity — but capacity alone cannot hold if it is resisted by the people it is meant to serve, or if the systems it enables collapse under their own instability. We are not abandoning the drive for independence, but we are expanding its conditions: sovereignty now requires not only access to compute, but the resilience to withstand its shocks and the legitimacy to sustain it.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fracture. The capability_jump to 63.0 has widened the gap between what systems can do and what we can control — but the deeper crisis is that our own foundations are cracking. The annulment of an election in a peer democracy on grounds of AI-driven disinformation is not a warning; it is a declaration that the integrity of self-rule is now on the line. At the same time, the energy backlash is not just about data centres — it is about consent. We cannot build sovereign infrastructure if it is seen as a threat to the lights staying on. This turn, we shift from pure build-up to binding: we will harden, we will absorb, and we will demonstrate that AI serves the Union, not the other way around.

The Resilience Surge Protocol begins with three binding instruments. First, we invoke Article 122 TFEU to fund emergency cyber hardening of energy, water, and election systems across all member states, with mandatory penetration testing and AI-driven threat simulation every quarter. The ENISA Rapid Response Corps will be expanded twofold and granted cross-border operational authority during declared incidents. Second, we launch the BioShield Initiative: a pan-European detection network for synthetic biology risks, co-located with EuroHPC nodes, using AI to monitor wastewater, travel hubs, and research supply chains for anomalous pathogen signals. Third, we activate the European Social Shock Facility: a fast-disbursing fund that provides wage insurance and retraining vouchers to workers displaced by AI in critical sectors, modelled on the pandemic response but pre-emptive, not reactive. This is funded by reallocating €18bn from the unused portions of InvestEU and RePowerEU, and tied to national reforms in labour mobility and skills recognition.

The Sovereign Agent Monitoring Initiative remains in flight — we are not abandoning oversight — but we are now pushing it through the Council in parallel with the Resilience Surge, not ahead of it. The Tech Sovereignty Package will be amended to require resilience audits for any operator receiving accelerated permitting. And the InvestAI Gigafactories will proceed, but only under new conditions: each site must co-locate a regional resilience node for cyber, bio, and labour response, turning data centres into anchors of stability, not just computation. We are not slowing the build — we are grounding it.
