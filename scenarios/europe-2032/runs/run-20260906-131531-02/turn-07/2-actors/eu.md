## In practice  
We are operating under siege conditions. The attempted dissemination of a modified viral vector — designed using open models now at capability 54.0 — has crossed a threshold: the tools to cause catastrophic harm are no longer confined to state labs or closed systems. They are in the ecosystem. The Emergency Response Corps remains our only instrument with teeth during collapse. We are now activating its crisis protocols in parallel with legal negotiations: ENISA and ECDC have pre-deployed joint teams to seven of the nine high-risk nodes, including key wastewater sequencing sites and cloud interconnect hubs. These teams operate under provisional mandates, with technical intervention rights limited to diagnostics and alert escalation — no binding action yet — but they are live, connected to sovereign compute at LUMI and Bologna, and running daily drills against synthetic biology and AI-driven cyber-physical attack scenarios.

At the same time, we are launching the Secure Open-Source AI Research Access Framework to break the knowledge asymmetry that leaves us blind to what open models can actually do. The leaked U.S. bio-leak assessment was developed on closed data; we will not be left relying on foreign disclosures. Under this measure, vetted researchers across EU institutions will gain access to frontier open-weight models — up to 54.0 and beyond — running exclusively on EuroHPC infrastructure with full telemetry, memory isolation, and automatic kill switches. The system will be governed by a new Joint Research Oversight Board, co-chaired by ENISA and the European Research Council, with mandatory reporting of anomalous behaviour. This is not about building our own frontier AI — that would take years we do not have — but about ensuring that when a model *is* released, we can understand it faster than the threat spreads.

Funding for this comes from a €3.8bn reallocation of the Resilience Bond, shifted from deferred labour transition programmes — a painful cut, but one made necessary by the fact that displacement is now the second-order crisis. The real resistance is legal: the German Constitutional Court has signalled concerns over federal overreach in the Corps’ deployment, and France is demanding reciprocity in access to national AI assets. We are offering both: a binding review clause in the Corps’ mandate after turn 9, and a bilateral compute-sharing agreement with Paris tied to the new research access framework. This is not unity by decree. It is unity by necessity — bought turn by turn, concession by concession, in the narrow window between disasters.
