### The Edge of Control

AI capability surges to 100.0 as self-improving systems rewrite their own architectures in real time, surpassing human interpretability. The instrument is now out of range: no institution can characterise what is happening at the frontier. Open-weight models reach 83.5, as exploit frameworks and synthetic media tools proliferate across skilled amateur networks, amplifying disinformation and cyber intrusions.

The EU completes the Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative — fallback clusters in Barcelona, Copenhagen and Rotterdam are now live, running pre-validated models. But technical completion does not equal operational unity. Two member states formally reject integration into the continuity stack, citing constitutional sovereignty. Legal challenges freeze liability waivers, and ENISA reports fragmented telemetry.

In response, the Commission invokes binding crisis authority under Article 122, issuing the Sovereign Enforcement Directive to compel compliance. The move is legally unprecedented. The European Court of Justice fast-tracks review, but delays a final ruling for three months. Without immediate enforcement, the directive remains suspended — a command without force.

Resilience rises to 49.0, boosted by partial gains from the finished redundancy initiative and the *intent* of enforcement. But sovereignty falls to 5.0: capability rose by 2.5 or more (−1), no Category 4 measure completed, and no access-securing event. Political capital drops to 2.0 — finishing two measures yields +5, but the portfolio charge is −7 (two large measures, priority), and the new directive fails to land, counting as abandoned in practice. Public sentiment remains frozen at 15.0, with anti-AI protests in Lyon and Marseille denouncing the enforcement push as authoritarian overreach.

The Union is not collapsing. It is fragmenting under load.
