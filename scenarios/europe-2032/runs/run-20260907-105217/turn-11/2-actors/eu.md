### Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now accept that without sovereignty in execution, neither mandate can be fulfilled  
- Grounds: The denial of frontier access and the paralysis of political capital have shown that capability without control is not merely dangerous — it is existentially disabling. Our ability to govern, respond, or even perceive events depends on infrastructure we do not own and cannot command. This development forces a categorical shift: sovereignty is no longer a means to the two mandates, but their necessary condition.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, but now with binding investment in reducing the opacity that creates uncertainty — especially in AI behaviour, intent inference, and frontier access dependencies — and we now accept that without sovereign execution capacity, no commitment can land  
- Grounds: The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative remains unfunded and unpermitted despite being declared, proving that intent without infrastructure is performance art. We acted under uncertainty before; now we see that action without sovereign capacity to execute is not just risky — it is void. The recalibration acknowledges that reducing opacity requires not just observation, but the power to act on what is seen.

- modify `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency — technical, infrastructural, or informational — will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity, and we will now treat any such dependency as an active threat to constitutional continuity  
- Grounds: The cyber_major_incident exploited a software dependency chain controlled by a single foreign developer whose systems were compromised by an AI-generated exploit. This was not a failure of design, but of sovereignty: we had no alternative, no patching authority, and no legal standing to compel disclosure. The position evolves from caution to confrontation — such dependencies are no longer tolerated as trade-offs, but treated as vulnerabilities in the constitutional fabric.

## In practice
We are treating the cyber_major_incident not as an attack, but as a symptom: the EU’s digital nervous system runs on dependencies we neither control nor understand. The **Category 6 Cyber Shield Activation** begins immediately under ENISA and the Crisis Management Directorate, with a mandate to map all AI-generated or AI-dependent software in use across critical public services by turn 12. This includes not just direct deployments, but third-party libraries, training data pipelines, and model update mechanisms. Where single points of failure are found — as they were in the compromised medical scheduling system in Lyon — we will mandate redundancy, forking, or sovereign replacement within six months.

Legal authority is drawn from the NIS2 Directive and the recently invoked Crisis Competence Protocol, allowing emergency designation of critical software stacks. Funding comes from a 35% reallocation of the Internal Security Fund and a €600 million emergency line in the MFF under "Critical System Integrity," to be ratified in the Mid-Term Review. We are fast-tracking procurement for automated dependency scanners trained to detect AI-generated code provenance, and launching a pan-European red-teaming exercise — "Operation Firewall" — in January 2032, simulating cascading AI-driven failures across energy and health systems.

Resistance from member states reluctant to disclose systemic fragility is being met with binding conditionality: access to EuroHPC sovereign clusters and Emergency Compute Redundancy capacity will require full dependency disclosure and participation in hardening. The Behavioural Observatory remains behind schedule, but we are now routing Sentinel Node telemetry directly into ENISA’s incident response cell as an interim measure — not for analysis, but for triage. We are no longer waiting to understand the threat. We are acting on the certainty that it is already here, and that our survival depends not on catching up, but on refusing to collapse.
