### A floor built elsewhere
The first half of 2030 did not belong to Brussels. In Washington and Beijing, negotiators announced a limited risk pact — weights security, curbs on autonomous escalation, controls on a class of biological design tools — with verification described as thin but real. European capitals learned the outlines from wire copy and hastily convened briefings. For a Commission with almost no standing left to spend, the news landed as both relief and rebuke: the only brake on frontier speed was being built without it.

Money left the sector at the same time. After years of ever-larger rounds, valuations reset hard in the spring. Two build-outs that European planners had quietly counted on for interim compute were cancelled outright, not delayed, and contractors demobilised. Frontier labs spoke openly of training less. Progress did not stop, but it crawled.

Almost unnoticed beside that, a new open release matched the frontier within months. It was downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in its first week. Hospital IT staff already running fallback stacks folded it in where they could; so did countless others beyond any regulator's reach.

### Triage as doctrine
Brussels made a virtue of restraint. No new build was promised. DG CNECT, ENISA and health emergency teams kept cut-off hospitals and customs posts on degraded European-hosted and open systems, living off banked certificates, isolating suspect agent tenants, rebuilding ledgers by hand. It worked, barely. Clinicians still complained of mistranslations and slow scheduling, and councils quietly extended multi-year contracts for the inferior systems — the temporary becoming permanent.

The one new move was diplomatic. Using the Autonomy Pact network as credential, the external service sought observer status in the weights-security and bio-design workstreams, offering incident data and detection capacity as contribution. Americans and Chinese received the request politely and non-committally; alignment of certification began as paperwork.

By June, cohesion held but the mood darkened. Press coverage paired layoffs in AI start-ups with wards still on workarounds. Talk of being informed afterwards stung, and public patience thinned further.
