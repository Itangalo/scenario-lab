### Cut off
In August, access to the leading American system for European users simply stopped. No warning, no reason, no appeal channel. Hospital planning tools, ministry assistants and contractor workflows built on the foreign interface went dark in an afternoon. Helpdesks handed out freely shared models as replacements, weeks behind and rougher to run, while officials in Brussels called the move coercion and began filing it as evidence.

The timing could not have been worse. The automated assault that had begun in winter carried into summer: municipal portals encrypted, a poisoned code library forcing audits ward by ward and substation by substation. Engineers patched through nights with new self-updating defenses pushed out by the Union's cyber teams, working on existing budgets. Cascades were avoided, but clinics ran on fallback scripts and local grids on islanded improvisation.

### Holding without promising
The Commission made no new pledge. Ministers toured emergency wards and control rooms to speak plainly: cures and productivity now arrived through foreign gates or through models no one could recall, and there was no money to buy a way out this semester. Wage support cheques finally landed, but against darkened town halls they bought no goodwill.

By autumn, power held and water ran, and voters counted that as the minimum. Talk in capitals turned openly to whether dependence itself had been the policy failure, and who had let essential services be built on a switch in another country. Survival continued, credit did not.
