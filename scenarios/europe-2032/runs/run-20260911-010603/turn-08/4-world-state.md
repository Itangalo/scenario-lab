### Patching under fire
The spring brought another large automated assault. A ransomware sweep married to a poisoned software dependency moved through municipal IT, hospital administration and contractors in several states. Systems did not collapse, but queues, cancelled procedures and paper fallbacks returned. The tooling was plainly model-built. Attribution stalled again.

Defenders were not as blind as in autumn. The containment patch finished in winter — telemetry-linked backups, reserve licences, automated patching and swarm detection — was now the operating doctrine. ENISA's incident cell pushed patches first to the worst-hit cities, and payouts followed live feeds. Services degraded rather than stopped. For the first time officials could claim, credibly, that the gap had narrowed.

The economic backdrop shifted quietly. Law firms, accountants, newsrooms and consultancies reported steady productivity gains from AI assistants, strongest among juniors, without layoffs. Firms that had cut early rehired. The mood lifted fractionally, even as hospitals ran on fallback systems.

### The chokepoint spent by someone else
Washington ordered The Hague to cut servicing and exports of older chipmaking tools to China, invoking American technology in the supply chain. For ASML that was existential; for Brussels it was humiliation — its one real chokepoint spent without its consent, just as exercises in the Taiwan Strait lifted insurance rates and diplomats were expelled.

The Commission answered with a Servicing Shield: no Dutch cut beyond agreed dual-use lists without Council authorisation, a joint spares and servicing pool with Japanese and Korean suppliers, and linkage of recovery and gigafactory queue access to anchored supply. The Hague welcomed legal cover but did not refuse Washington outright. Servicing licences dribbled out under both regimes. American officials called alignment negotiated, then continued to demand it.

Continuity work stayed the priority. Public buyers remained on European-run models in the first powered gigafactory halls, queueing still political, grid priority still contested. The holdout member state with its outside hyperscaler deal stayed out, and threats over funds hardened the rift. Robots kept spreading in warehouses on foreign software and hardware, dividing warehouses between verifiable and non-verifiable tasks.
