Autumn 2030 saw a narrow leap in non-European code-writing models that accelerated intrusion kits — overnight recompiled exploits, repackaged poisoned libraries, fluent ransom notes. Municipalities and hospitals were hit harder with paper fallbacks returning, but winter-finished containment doctrine (isolated backups, spare licences, auto-patching, surge cell, telemetry-linked payouts/reinsurance) held; services degraded without cascade.

On chips, Washington pressed The Hague to cut servicing of older tools sold to China; Brussels held its Servicing Shield — no cut beyond lists without Council sign-off, joint spares pool with Japan/Korea activated, recovery/gigafactory queue access tied to anchoring. It half-worked: The Hague signed on but dribbled licences under U.S. pressure; servicing not strangled, but Taiwan-risk premiums rose and Dutch firms felt caught between allies. Inside the EU, public buyers stayed on European-run models in first powered halls with politicized queues/grid priority; outside-hyperscaler holdout stayed out as funding threats deepened rift; logistics robots spread on foreign stack with verifiable/non-verifiable task split.
