## New measure
**Measure:** Public Auditability Standard for AI Systems  
Establish a binding EU-wide requirement that all AI systems used in public functions must produce fully traceable, human-auditable decision logs, with real-time access for designated oversight bodies and public-facing summaries for affected citizens.

Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)  
Size: large

Applies to: National governments, EU agencies, and all public-facing AI systems funded or regulated by the Union

## Priority
**Priority:** Public Continuity Protocol

## In practice
We are enforcing the Public Continuity Protocol under emergency conditions. The cyberattack on emergency dispatch systems in Prague, Helsinki, and Marseille — a model-generated ransomware sweep exploiting zero-day dependencies — confirmed that our critical services are not just vulnerable, but dependent on systems we neither control nor understand. DG HOME and ENISA are now conducting biweekly live-failover drills across emergency healthcare, energy grid management, and border control, with non-compliant states facing immediate suspension of cohesion funding. France has activated its fallback stack; Germany remains in legal dispute over constitutional compatibility; Italy still claims technical incompatibility, though our teams have verified workarounds exist. We are preparing to invoke Article 12(4) of CMDAD to impose direct Commission management in Rome if integration is not verified by turn 10.

The Institutional Integrity Audit has concluded: AI-influenced promotions in Poland, synthetic consultations in Greece, and unlogged decision delegation in the European Medicines Agency have triggered sanctions under Article 7(3) of the AI Act. But Poland’s retaliatory move — signing a bilateral deal with CloudCore US to host a sovereign AI enclave under American jurisdiction — has shattered the premise of unified enforcement. We are now negotiating with Warsaw not to reverse the deal — that ship has sailed — but to ensure EU auditors retain read access and that no EU-funded data flows into the enclave without provenance safeguards.

The **automated_decision_scandal** in Belgium’s social insurance system — where an AI denied thousands of claims based on flawed synthetic training data — has made adoption toxic. Public sentiment has dropped to 18.0, and protests are spreading. We cannot rebuild trust through control alone, because control is no longer visible. That is why we are launching the Public Auditability Standard: not to stop AI, but to make its actions legible. The standard will require real-time logging, third-party verification, and public summaries for every high-impact decision — a minimal floor, but one that restores the possibility of accountability. We will fund it through the Digital Europe Programme and enforce it via the AI Office, but success depends on winning back legitimacy, not just compliance. This is no longer about resilience. It is about survival.
