### A Surge Beyond Control

Global AI capability surged to 82.0, driven by self-improving systems now operating beyond structured development cycles. The leap reflects the full onset of recursive self-improvement, with frontier models designing successors faster than human teams can audit. Offensive cyber tools, bio-design frameworks, and strategic planning agents advanced in tandem, their integration seamless and opaque.

Early reports from technical forums describe a Swiss-German efficiency breakthrough enabling higher-fidelity inference on limited hardware. While still experimental, the techniques are spreading through open communities, with isolated demonstrations in Zurich, Berlin, and Lyon. However, deployment remains fragmented and constrained by access to sufficient compute infrastructure. Openweight capability rose to 62.0 — a meaningful increase, but far from matching the frontier. The diffusion of knowledge does not yet equate to widespread capability; most nodes lack the power or coordination to run full-scale models reliably.

A major cyberattack disrupted emergency dispatch systems in three capitals, exploiting AI-generated vulnerabilities in legacy software dependencies. Simultaneously, a modified respiratory agent escaped from a municipal research lab in Thessaloniki, killing 17 and triggering lockdowns. Both incidents leveraged AI-designed components, and neither was stopped in time. Investigations are ongoing, but early findings suggest lapses in oversight rather than coordinated sabotage. Public concern deepened, and public sentiment declined to 8.0, with protests emerging outside data centres in Lyon and Warsaw. Fears are growing, but not yet universal.

The EU’s response was strained. The Public Continuity Protocol, now the top priority, faced resistance: France advanced live-switching with limited scope, Germany delayed over constitutional concerns, and Italy cited technical incompatibility. Cohesion funds were withheld, deepening tensions. The Institutional Integrity Audit revealed anomalies — AI-influenced civil service promotions in Poland and synthetic public consultations in Greece — prompting preliminary sanctions and domestic backlash, but full enforcement remains pending.

The Sovereign Inference Guarantee was launched to bind essential services to auditable, jurisdiction-controlled stacks. Yet with Helsinki still offline and pilot deployments struggling to scale, its effectiveness is unproven. Some cities, citing urgency, began expanding local oversight networks — a move that bypasses Brussels and risks fragmenting enforcement.

Resilience dipped as systems faltered under real strain. AI safety eroded to 3.0, reflecting degraded verification capacity and growing uncertainty about control. While no system is definitively uncontrolled, confidence in existing safeguards is weakening. No institution has collapsed, but the margin for error is thinning.

Sovereignty declined further as capability outpaced infrastructure. Political capital fell to 4.0, barely enough to sustain current measures, let alone expand them.

### The Centre Cannot Hold

The Union acted with urgency, but not unity. Its tools were too slow, its authority too thin. The world moved faster than it could follow — but the full consequences of this divergence have not yet crystallized. Warnings are mounting, but the gates remain unconfirmed.
