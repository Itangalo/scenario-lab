### The Enforcement Gamble

The European Union announced plans for the **Sovereign Enforcement Regime**, a proposed regulation to compel compliance from foreign AI developers through financial and market penalties, framed as a democratic necessity following a recent election annulment linked to information manipulation. The proposal mandates full model disclosure and envisions a fast-track enforcement panel. However, no final adoption occurred this turn — the legislative process is ongoing, and implementation cannot begin until approval is secured across member states and institutions.

Legal resistance hardened immediately. Two member states challenged the use of Article 114 TFEU, arguing the measure overreaches into foreign trade and security policy. The European Court of Justice opened a preliminary review, casting doubt on its legal foundation. U.S. labs, citing national security directives, declared they would not comply with any such regime absent bilateral agreement, and Washington offered no indication of future cooperation under any posture. Without leverage or precedent, the proposal remains aspirational.

### Sovereignty Without Leverage

The EU’s political capital declined to 38.0, reflecting strain from maintaining two long-running measures — **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech Sovereignty Package** — both still years from completion. The announcement of a potential third measure, the **Sovereign Enforcement Regime**, generated debate but conferred no immediate benefit and incurred no formal charge yet, as it was not adopted this turn. Sovereignty held at 19.0: while the ambition to enforce standards grew, no tangible shift in access to or control over frontier models occurred. The gap between intent and capacity remained wide.
