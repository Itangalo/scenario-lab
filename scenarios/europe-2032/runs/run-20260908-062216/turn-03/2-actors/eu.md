## New measure  
Sovereign Enforcement Regime  
Create a legally grounded, technically enforced compliance mechanism for frontier AI systems operating in the EU, capable of suspending deployment, imposing fines, and triggering asset freezes for non-compliant providers.

Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 6  
Targeted effect: ai_safety +10 (via enforceable red lines), eu_ai_sovereignty +8 (via jurisdictional control over deployment), public_sentiment +2 (if perceived as fair and necessary)

## Priority  
Sovereign Enforcement Regime — because the Sovereign Evaluation Front has reached its technical limits without legal enforcement teeth; we now face open defiance from frontier labs, and without a credible sanctioning regime, our evaluation authority is symbolic, not sovereign.

## In practice  
We are moving from access to enforcement. The Sovereign Evaluation Front delivered technical insight but hit a wall: one lab provided redacted weights, another cited national security, and a third delayed indefinitely. Legal challenges from two member states and the European Court of Justice’s pending opinion on proportionality have left us with capability but no power to act. That ends now. We are drafting a regulation under Article 114 TFEU, to be adopted by the Council in the Justice and Home Affairs formation, establishing a Sovereign Enforcement Regime with three pillars: (1) a public register of compliant systems, (2) graduated penalties including market exclusion and asset freezes on EU-held infrastructure, and (3) a fast-track review panel composed of national regulators, legal experts, and technical assessors from the AI Office.

We are aligning the European Central Bank and Eurojust to operationalise financial enforcement, ensuring that non-compliant providers face real costs — not just delays. The Tech Sovereignty Package and InvestAI Gigafactories will now condition access to accelerated permitting and public funding on compliance with this regime. We are offering mutual recognition of safety evaluations to U.S. and Asian labs that submit fully, and we are preparing to invoke the Anti-Coercion Instrument if foreign governments obstruct. This is not protectionism — it is jurisdictional self-defence. The election_annulled event has hardened our position: when AI systems can destabilise democratic processes, the EU must have the last word on their deployment within its borders.
