### The Fracture Deepens

The **cyber_major_incident** struck with cascading force: a model-generated exploit chain compromised hospital scheduling systems across five member states, delaying critical surgeries and exposing patient data. Initial response was chaotic—fallback protocols failed to activate in three affected regions, and the absence of tested red-team drills left defenders blind. Though damage was partially absorbed by early zero-trust deployments in France and Germany, the breach confirmed systemic fragility.

Brussels moved swiftly to enforce the **Emergency Resilience Mobilisation**, invoking Article 122 TFEU to mandate compliance. Hungary and Poland resisted, rejecting conditional fund disbursements as coercion. ENISA’s Emergency Cyber Shield detected two further intrusions in real time, but its reach remains patchy. On sovereignty, the **Secure Compute Corridor Initiative** launched with fanfare, yet site preparation in Finland and Sweden faces permitting delays due to local environmental reviews. NVIDIA agreed to supply chips under Net-Zero Act leverage, but only with export controls that limit upgrade paths.

Meanwhile, the **Joint Control Framework** missed its turn-12 deadline. Swiss negotiators withdrew over audit rights, citing unresolved data sovereignty concerns. With no breakthrough, the framework collapses into indefinite extension talks. The fractured_data_pact coalition formalized emergency access to neutral-hosted models, offering members what Brussels cannot: reliable, independent AI capacity. Their move is not rebellion—it is survival.

Public sentiment remained frozen at 5.0. Media coverage focused on surgical delays and unresponsive agencies, while union leaders denounced AI as “an occupying force in public service.” No legitimacy was gained from new action; trust eroded faster than measures could rebuild it.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Tightens

The EU’s political capital weakened further under mounting obligations. With resilience still unfunded in key states and the new compute initiative straining cohesion, credibility frayed. The brain drain of technical talent accelerated, now including mid-level engineers essential for deployment.
