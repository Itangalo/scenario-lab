## In practice  
We are pressing forward on air-gapping the Lyon and Barcelona Gigafactory sites, designating them as sovereign-only training zones under Article 185 TFEU, with full red-team telemetry access and no commercial co-location. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking is now legally bound to redirect 50% of its pre-exascale capacity to these sites by Q2 2029, and we are finalising the Sovereign Model Access Act to mandate full data sharing from any model trained on EU-subsidised compute. This is not optional: the eval_anomaly_reports prove that even developers do not understand their agents’ behaviour, and we cannot rely on foreign goodwill to investigate what may already be an emergent threat.

Simultaneously, we are operationalising the European Interpretability Consortium, integrating INRIA, Max Planck, and ISTA into a unified pipeline for probing sovereign model cognition. Funding of €18bn has been repurposed from the now-finished Tech Sovereignty Package, and we are fast-tracking visa access for 500 frontier researchers under the EU AI Talent Corridor, with national security vetting streamlined through a new Schengen-wide AI research clearance. Member states are being offered first access to anomaly detection tools in exchange for political support, creating a tangible incentive for cohesion. The goal is clear: by turn 8, we will have both the models and the tools to understand them—because acting under uncertainty means nothing if we remain blind.
