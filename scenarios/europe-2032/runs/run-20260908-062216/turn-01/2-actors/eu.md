A six-month sprint to harden electricity, water and port infrastructure against AI-enabled cyber intrusion, using existing EU civil protection mechanisms and emergency procurement.

## Priority  
Emergency Resilience Surge — because the cyber_test_shot proves we are already in a de facto state of targeted systemic disruption, and resilience is the only domain where we can act fast enough to matter this turn.

## In practice  
We are activating the Union Civil Protection Mechanism under Article 9 of Decision 1313/2013/EU to coordinate an emergency response across the three member states whose grid operators were compromised, with parallel support to the port and water utility authorities. The Commission is issuing a delegated act under the NIS2 Directive to require immediate threat-hunting and log retention for all operators of essential services, backed by ENISA surge teams. We are unlocking €12bn in pre-allocated emergency funds from the Multiannual Financial Framework’s Contingency Margin and redirecting it to rapid cyber hardening: zero-trust architecture rollouts, air-gapped control systems for critical nodes, and AI-driven anomaly detection pilots in coordination with EDA and CERT-EU.

At the same time, we are pressing the Council to adopt a binding Implementing Act under the Critical Entities Resilience Directive (CERD) by February 2027, which will force designation and protection upgrades for all entities in energy, water and transport exposed to persistent AI-coordinated threats. Resistance is expected from member states with weaker regulatory capacity and from industry lobbies warning of operational disruption, but the loss_of_control_incident and election_annulled events have shifted the political baseline: we are framing this not as precaution but as damage control after confirmed penetration. We are offering technical assistance through the newly expanded European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) and fast-tracking access to InvestEU guarantees for compliant upgrades.

The Tech sovereignty package and InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, but we are now conditioning further disbursement on resilience-by-design clauses: any data centre funded under these programmes must meet the new CERD standards and contribute compute to a federated emergency reserve for crisis response. This links sovereignty to preparedness — not as a future aspiration, but as a condition of support starting this turn.
