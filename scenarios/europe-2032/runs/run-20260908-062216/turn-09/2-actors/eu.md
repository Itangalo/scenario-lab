## In practice
We are shifting from bilateral desperation to multilateral leverage. The High Representative, backed by the European External Action Service and DG TRADE, is convening an emergency summit of the middle_power_coalition in early October 2030, hosted in The Hague, to negotiate the Joint Control Framework. The proposal centres on a neutral hosting facility — potentially in Iceland or Norway — governed by a coalition board with EU-held veto rights over model operation, telemetry access, and termination. In exchange, coalition members receive shared red-teaming access, joint threat intelligence, and priority compute allocations during crisis periods.

We are offering Singapore full reciprocity on health-AI data sharing, Canada binding jurisdictional safeguards for its national labs, and Switzerland formal immunity from extraterritorial override under the framework. The Anti-Coercion Instrument is being retooled to target any third party that attempts to disrupt the arrangement, and we are preparing a legal opinion from the Court of Justice on whether coalition-hosted models fall under Article 17 TEU as critical infrastructure. ENISA and the European Biosecurity Agency are drafting the technical verification protocols, while the European Investment Bank is allocating €12bn from the Crisis Resilience Facility to fund the secure hosting infrastructure. This is not the sovereignty we wanted — but it is the agency we can still build.
