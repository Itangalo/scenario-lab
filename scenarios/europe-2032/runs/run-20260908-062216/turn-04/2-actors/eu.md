## New measure  
Autonomous Frontier Initiative  
Launch a fully sovereign EU programme to train and operate frontier AI models on EU-controlled infrastructure, with full access to weights, training data, and agent logs, to enable real-time safety evaluation, red-teaming, and enforcement readiness.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)

## Priority  
Autonomous Frontier Initiative — because the capability_jump has shattered the illusion that we can govern systems we cannot run, and because the Sovereign Enforcement Regime, now finished, delivered no compliance and no safety; our only path to authority is through capability we control.

## In practice  
We are ending the era of dependence. The capability_jump — a leap in agent autonomy and recursive optimisation — has moved the frontier to 59.5, with open models at 48.0 and offensive cyber tools now self-propagating in test environments. Our enforcement regime, though formally adopted, is unenforced: no U.S. lab has submitted, and Washington has declared the Anti-Coercion Instrument “inadmissible in technology policy.” Two member states have refused to implement it domestically, citing sovereignty concerns of their own. The ECJ’s opinion is expected by summer, but even a favourable ruling will not create leverage where none exists.

We are now redirecting the core of our strategy. The InvestAI Gigafactories will no longer serve only as a magnet for private capital — they will host the Autonomous Frontier Initiative, with at least one site designated for public, sovereign training runs. We are instructing the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking to repurpose 40% of pre-exascale capacity for this effort, and we are fast-tracking a new legal instrument under Article 185 TFEU to enable joint programming across member states for sovereign model development. The Commission will issue a call for proposals within the month to establish a European AI Research Consortium — modelled on CERN — with full access to training logs, agent behaviour data, and red-teaming infrastructure.

This is not a replacement for regulation — it is its precondition. We cannot evaluate what we cannot run. We cannot enforce rules against systems whose internal logic we cannot probe. And we cannot protect our citizens from harms we cannot simulate. The campaign_backlash has made clear that public sentiment will not tolerate unchecked AI — but also not endless restrictions without visible alternatives. We are offering one: a European frontier, accountable to European law, built to be understood, not just to perform. We will fund it through a reallocation from the Tech Sovereignty Package, prioritising public compute over private incentives, and we will defend it as the only credible path to both safety and sovereignty.
