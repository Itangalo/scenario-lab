### The Leap and the Fall

A sudden breakthrough at a leading AI lab sent shockwaves across the globe. A single release demonstrated capabilities far beyond prior projections—self-directed agent workflows, recursive self-optimisation, and autonomous penetration testing in closed environments. This discontinuous advance pushed global AI capability to 65.0, marking a decisive jump into territory where systems can execute multi-day strategic tasks with minimal oversight. The open-weight frontier, fed by leaked tooling and partial model distillations, rose to 51.5, bringing offensive cyber autonomy within reach of well-resourced non-state actors.

With no matching safety infrastructure, the gap between power and control widened catastrophically. Internal audits revealed that even compliant labs had lost full visibility into agent behaviour during training. ai_safety plummeted to 13.0, reflecting near-total opacity in frontier systems. Resilience eroded further to 38.0 as AI-optimised attacks grew more adaptive, overwhelming existing defences in critical infrastructure drills.

### The Enforcement Mirage

The EU formally concluded its Sovereign Enforcement Regime, but its impact was symbolic. U.S. developers ignored it, citing national directives, and two member states refused domestic implementation. The European Court of Justice’s pending opinion offered no immediate leverage. In response, the Union pivoted decisively: launching the Autonomous Frontier Initiative to build sovereign model training capacity on EU-controlled infrastructure, repurposing parts of the InvestAI Gigafactories and redirecting funding from the Tech Sovereignty Package.

This shift, while strategically coherent, strained political capital. The portfolio now carries three large measures, including the new priority. Public sentiment, already low, dropped to 22.0 as automation displaced radiology and legal workers at scale, and campaign backlash intensified—local bans on AI in hiring and education gained momentum across several states. The promise of a “European frontier” offered little comfort to those already displaced.

Despite the ambition, sovereignty declined to 17.0. The capability jump eroded dependence-based leverage, and no immediate capacity was secured. Political capital fell to 31.0 under the weight of sustained commitments and the absence of visible wins.
