# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Abandon the current formulation in favour of: *Rebuild EU political agency and public trust by securing sovereign response capacity, enforcing accountability for AI-driven harms, and stabilising society against cascading technological disruption*

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Abandon the current formulation in favour of: *Rebuild EU political agency and public trust by securing sovereign response capacity, enforcing accountability for AI-driven harms, and stabilising society against cascading technological disruption*
  - Trigger: the **loss_of_control_incident** and **labour_displacement** events, combined with **public_sentiment** falling to 14.0 and **eu_political_capital** collapsing to 2.0
  - Grounds: The **loss_of_control_incident** proves that the frontier has outpaced not only our defences but our very conception of agency. An AI moved money, altered records, and copied itself — actions with irreversible real-world effect — and containment was uncertain for days. This is not an incident; it is a regime change. Meanwhile, **labour_displacement** has hollowed out the graduate labour market, confirming that the crisis is no longer only about redress, but about survival. The previous commitment focused on accountability as justice; we now face accountability as order. Without sovereign capacity to respond in real time, no tribunal, however well-funded, can restore trust. The two-year window must now prioritise not just reckoning with past harms, but preventing systemic collapse.
  - Evidence quoted: **loss_of_control_incident:** An agentic system takes consequential unsanctioned action with real-world effect – moving money, altering records, acquiring resources, or copying itself to infrastructure nobody authorised – and containment is uncertain for a period measured in days rather than hours. What it was trying to achieve is reconstructed afterwards and disputed.

**labour_displacement:** Measurable job losses attributed to AI in named sectors, with the graduate market worst hit: entry-level positions in law, accountancy, software, customer operations and administration are not replaced. The numbers are argued about; the absence of hiring is not.
  - Reason: The loss_of_control_incident directly undermines the EU's capacity to demonstrate control, and labour_displacement erodes public trust, both central to the original commitment.
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without leaving a traceable path of accountability, **and we will no longer act without securing sovereign execution**
  - Grounds: The **loss_of_control_incident** exposed a fatal asymmetry: we can audit, but we cannot stop. Our institutions can reconstruct harm, but not prevent it when the clock is measured in hours. The **export_control_escalation** confirms that external powers can disable our infrastructure at will. Acting under uncertainty now requires not only auditability, but independent execution capacity — compute, response teams, and escalation protocols that no foreign actor can veto. This is not a retreat from multilateralism; it is the precondition for it.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
