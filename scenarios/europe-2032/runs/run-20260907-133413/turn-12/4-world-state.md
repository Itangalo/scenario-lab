### Sovereignty on the Brink

The EU doubled down on survival, launching the Immediate Critical Function Shielding initiative to repurpose EuroHPC supercomputers into a defensive AI network. LUMI, MareNostrum, and Vega began running hardened, stripped-down models for anomaly detection and automated patching. However, integration with national systems lagged, and air-gapped operation during crises remains untested. The move delivered early resilience gains, but at the cost of further fragmenting AI governance.

### Compute Under Duress

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation pressed ahead, bypassing stalled negotiations by forcibly redirecting idle DUV tools in Leuven and Dresden. Legal challenges loomed, with ASML threatening litigation over sovereignty breaches. Construction began in Finland and Poland without final environmental approvals, risking future invalidation. No new compute came online, and the delay deepened dependence amid escalating cyber disruptions.

### A Continent Under Siege

A model-generated ransomware wave crippled health and transport systems across three member states. Response units, lacking sovereign AI for triage, were overwhelmed. Recovery took weeks. Forensic teams continued tracing embedded agents in industrial controllers, with full containment still out of reach.

### Fragile Coordination

The EU Public Trust Corps became operational, shifting from transparency drills to crisis communication. Multilingual AI interpreters were deployed to counter disinformation, but their reach was limited. Labour mobility talks with Germany and the Netherlands stalled over liability concerns.

### Shrinking Room to Act

Political capital evaporated. With `public_sentiment` at 9.0 and multiple large measures in flight, the EU barely retained agency. The middle_power_coalition formed, aligning export controls with Japan and South Korea, offering a rare gain — but too late to offset the systemic slide.
