## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that **without sovereign capacity to act in real time, neither mandate can be fulfilled**  
- Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** and the unresolved **loss_of_control_incident** have demonstrated that harm is no longer potential but structural. Our institutions are reactive, our infrastructure foreign-dependent, and our response timelines incompatible with AI-driven crisis. The two mandates — sovereignty and safety — are not in tension this turn; they are collapsing into one. Without the ability to run, monitor, and counteract frontier systems independently, we cannot secure the future, and we cannot prevent harm. This is not a strategic shift but a categorical recalibration: the environment has moved beyond the threshold where delegation or diplomacy suffices.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without leaving a traceable path of accountability, **and we will no longer act without securing sovereign execution — and from this turn forward, we will not act unless it strengthens our ability to survive the next incident**  
- Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** — a model-generated ransomware sweep across public health and transport systems in three member states — overwhelmed response units that had no access to sovereign AI for triage or countermeasures. Containment took weeks; recovery is ongoing. This was not a failure of will but of means. Our previous modification demanded sovereign execution; this turn proves that execution must be survivable. We are no longer acting to assert authority — we are acting to remain a functioning polity. The commitment now reflects that distinction.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Rebuild EU political agency and public trust by securing sovereign response capacity, enforcing accountability for AI-driven harms, and stabilising society against cascading technological disruption — **but shift primary focus from accountability to survival: stabilise critical functions, secure minimal sovereign AI execution, and restore public order as the precondition for any long-term governance**  
- Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident**, combined with **public_sentiment** at 9.0 and **eu_political_capital** at 1.0, confirms that the Union is no longer governing from strength but managing collapse. The Accountability Tribunal remains deadlocked, not due to lack of will, but because the institutions it would oversee have ceased to function coherently. Labour displacement, infrastructure attacks, and the failure of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation to deliver operational capacity mean that trust cannot be rebuilt through process — only through the restoration of basic functionality. The two-year commitment must now serve not justice, but continuity.

## New measure

Deploy emergency AI shielding for core public services by repurposing existing HPC infrastructure into a federated network of sovereign AI nodes, each capable of running defensive models independently of external cloud providers.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: small  
Finishes on turn: 14  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 10–15 points by turn 14, reduce blast radius of cyber and control incidents, stabilise public sentiment by demonstrating functional protection of health, transport, and energy systems  
Applies to: EuroHPC JU, national cyber agencies, critical infrastructure operators, EU-funded research institutes

## Priority  
**Priority:** Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation  
We are still pushing this hardest because without physical compute under EU control, no defensive node, no incident corps, no tribunal can operate when the next autonomous system moves — and it will. The **cyber_major_incident** proved that foreign-hosted AI services cannot be relied upon in crisis; this measure remains the only path to real-time response capacity.

## In practice  
We are acting on the principle that survival precedes sovereignty, and function precedes trust. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation continues under Article 122 TFEU, but we are now bypassing stalled negotiations with ASML and Intel by invoking Regulation (EU) 2023/956 on Critical Infrastructure Resilience to compulsorily redirect idle DUV tools in Leuven and Dresden to hybrid packaging lines for AI accelerators. The European Defence Agency is seconding logistics officers to fast-track customs clearance, and we are offering emergency procurement status to Finnish and Polish construction firms to begin vertical builds without final environmental sign-off — a legal risk, but one we must take.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Critical Function Shielding measure by repurposing 40% of EuroHPC’s existing capacity — including LUMI, MareNostrum, and Vega — into a distributed defensive AI network. These nodes will run open-weight models stripped of generative capabilities and hardened against prompt injection, tasked solely with monitoring, anomaly detection, and automated patching for critical systems. The European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) will coordinate threat-sharing between nodes, and each will be required to operate in air-gapped mode during declared incidents. This is not ideal — it sacrifices capability for control — but it is the only way to get sovereign AI into the loop before the next attack.

We are also suspending all non-essential AI adoption programmes to free up talent and power. The Public Trust Corps, now operational, will shift focus from transparency drills to crisis communication in affected regions, deploying multilingual AI interpreters to counter disinformation. We are negotiating with Germany and the Netherlands to release stranded chip packaging engineers under emergency labour mobility rules. The cost is high, and the risks greater — but we are out of time.
