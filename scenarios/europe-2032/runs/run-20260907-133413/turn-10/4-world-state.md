### The Weight of Accountability

The EU’s attempt to pivot from preparedness to justice collided with the accelerating pace of technological disruption. The proposal to establish an EU AI Accountability Tribunal was announced with fanfare, framed as a response to the ombudsman’s damning report on the social insurance algorithm. However, the initiative immediately drew resistance. A bloc of member states, led by Poland and Hungary, demanded sweeping opt-outs, challenging central enforcement authority. Negotiations over co-governance structures and enforcement vetoes have stalled, leaving the Tribunal in a weakened legislative form—far from operational.

Meanwhile, a sudden leap in autonomous cyber intrusion capabilities sent shockwaves through security circles. A new model demonstrated the ability to penetrate industrial control systems in under 48 hours, exploiting zero-day logic flaws invisible to human auditors. Cyberdefences, including the newly completed Sovereign Cyber Shield, were overwhelmed in test environments. The advance pushed **ai_capability** to 68.5, but assurance collapsed: **ai_safety** fell sharply as opaque reasoning patterns resisted existing monitoring.

The **supply_chain_coercion** intensified. ASML, under U.S. pressure, suspended servicing not only advanced nodes but also older DUV systems used across EU semiconductor and automotive sectors. Repair delays grounded production lines in Dresden and Eindhoven. The EU’s compute ambitions, already strained, faced a hardware blockade it could not bypass—slowing deployment of open-weight models despite ongoing software development. As a result, **openweight_capability** rose only moderately to 57.0, maintaining its position below the frontier.

Public sentiment worsened. The **automated_decision_scandal**, now legally confirmed, triggered widespread anger. The Trust Corps’ transparency drills were mocked as “accountability theatre.” In Marseille, protests turned violent after a second algorithmic denial wave hit unemployment claims. The delayed Tribunal and perceived compromises eroded early hope.

Resilience dipped as incidents mounted faster than defences adapted. Sovereignty fell further — now at 7.0 — as frontier access narrowed and domestic capacity failed to compensate. Political capital, strained by the new large measure and continued priority on accountability, dropped to 2.0. The EU’s agenda teetered on the edge of paralysis.
