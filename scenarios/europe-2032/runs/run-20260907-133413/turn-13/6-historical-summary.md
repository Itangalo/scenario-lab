The EU’s final attempt to deploy sovereign AI accelerators within six months failed despite invoking Article 122 TFEU, as ASML and Intel resisted equipment seizures, and protests disrupted logistics—no new compute capacity came online. Immediate Critical Function Shielding went live but remained ineffective due to partial integration and insufficient throughput, leaving critical systems exposed during a new wave of AI-driven cyberattacks. Sovereignty fractures intensified as one member state struck a bilateral deal with Washington, others rejected EU emergency decrees, and Finland halted its cluster project for constitutional review. Public trust collapsed: the Public Trust Corps’ messaging failed amid ongoing blackouts and infrastructure attacks, while local authorities in two countries blocked grid access to data centres. With political capital exhausted, all major initiatives—including Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation—ended without delivery, deepening the gap between threat and response. Only scattered adoption of open-source biosecurity tools provided minimal resilience, insufficient to offset systemic deterioration.
