## New measure  
**Measure:** Establish Emergency AI Accountability and Redress Mechanism  
Creates a temporary EU-level ombudsman and rapid compensation fund for victims of automated decision systems, paired with mandatory audits of all public-sector AI applications above 40.0 capability.

Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)

Finishes on turn: 5

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme (SCAP)  
This remains the decisive lever: without sovereign systems online by turn 4, every other measure — including accountability — depends on foreign goodwill, and the Union has already seen how quickly that vanishes.

## In practice  
We are executing SCAP as a binding emergency directive under Article 122 TFEU, with the Commission now empowered to override national permitting, energy allocation, and procurement objections for the three designated champions: CEA in France, Fraunhofer-Gesellschaft in Germany, and the Nordic AI Consortium in Sweden. The €50bn emergency envelope within InvestAI is being unlocked via EIB issuance, backed by a political guarantee from the Eurogroup, despite objections from the Court of Auditors. ENTSO-E has been formally instructed to reserve 8GW for SCAP sites by turn 3, with the Commission preparing to invoke Article 7 against any member state obstructing grid access. The Gigafactories in Brittany and northern Sweden are now legally designated as SCAP anchor zones, with accelerated permitting enforced through the revised Tech Sovereignty Package, which now ties 50% of its private capital incentives to co-location and EU-controlled software stacks.

The EAAD is being operationalised ahead of schedule: 20 senior technical staff from national cyber agencies and CERN have been seconded to form an interim evaluation cell under ENISA, with emergency access to model weights from all systems above 50.0 capability deployed in the EU. We are using €1.2bn in repurposed RRF digitalisation funds to cover its first-year operations, and a draft regulation under Article 114 TFEU will mandate real-time logging and interpretability tooling for high-capability systems, to be tabled at the December ECOFIN Council. The safety breakthrough in interpretability is being fast-tracked into this framework: we are requiring all systems above 50.0 to implement the new certification methods within six months of deployment, turning a private lab innovation into a public assurance standard.

In response to the automated decision scandal and the campaign backlash, we are launching a new Emergency AI Accountability and Redress Mechanism: a temporary ombudsman office hosted within the European Data Protection Supervisor, with authority to investigate systemic harms from public-sector AI, and a €500m rapid compensation fund for verified victims. This applies immediately to all AI systems used in social insurance, policing, and judicial support above 40.0 capability. Audits will be conducted by national data protection authorities with EAAD support, and findings will be published. This is not a substitute for regulation — it is a signal that the Union takes accountability seriously, and that sovereign AI must be legitimate to be sovereign.
