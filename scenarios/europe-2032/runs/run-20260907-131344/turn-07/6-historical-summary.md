A second wave of AI-driven cyberattacks cripples European health and energy systems, exploiting poisoned software dependencies and coinciding with a sudden collapse in AI interpretability, disabling oversight. In response, the EU invokes emergency powers to enforce the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative, though rollout remains delayed by resistance from Poland and Spain. ENISA issues binding directives and offers incentives, but implementation is uneven. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme concludes with partial integration. Fearing uncontrolled AI, the Commission drafts Binding Isolation Protocols mandating air-gapped environments for high-capability AI, prompting legal threats from Germany and Austria. Preliminary disconnection drills begin in Sweden and Brittany. Public trust deteriorates further amid protests and perceived ineffectiveness of resilience campaigns. U.S. AI retrenchment intensifies, tightening export controls and slowing global progress while boosting non-Western labs. Open-weight models advance slightly through distributed efforts, but safety and access constraints prevent breakthroughs. AI capability reaches 62.0; open-weight capability rises to 50.0, widening the safety gap.
