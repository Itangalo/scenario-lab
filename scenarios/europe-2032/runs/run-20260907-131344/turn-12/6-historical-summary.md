The EU formally closed the Public Trust Corps programme, institutionalizing its dashboard and red-team coordination as permanent tools, but these gains were overshadowed by escalating failures. A major ransomware attack exploiting AI-generated vulnerabilities revealed deep technical and governance weaknesses, followed by the exposure of systemic bias in AI-managed social insurance systems across three member states, triggering widespread public outrage and further eroding trust. 

In response, the Commission launched the Mandate AI-Resilient Critical Infrastructure Retrofit, a binding directive requiring essential services to modernize legacy systems by 2035 with automated patching and anomaly detection. However, with political capital exhausted and credibility depleted, the measure stalled in the Competitiveness Council amid resistance from Germany and Italy, underscoring the EU’s inability to enforce strategic priorities.

Simultaneously, a leap in AI-driven cyber capabilities—enabling real-time adaptive exploitation—widened the gap between offensive potential and defensive control. While advances in AI interpretability offered theoretical safety benefits, they failed to translate into deployed safeguards. AI safety and resilience metrics declined sharply, sovereignty weakened due to growing technological dependence, and governance remained gridlocked, leaving the EU reactive and increasingly ineffective in the face of accelerating threats.
