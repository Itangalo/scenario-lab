### The Fracture Widens

A landmark ombudsman ruling confirmed that an AI system used in the Dutch social insurance agency systematically denied benefits to thousands based on flawed behavioural models, triggering public outrage across the Union. Protests flared in Utrecht and Lyon, echoing demands for accountability. In response, the European Commission reoriented the Public Trust Corps toward redress, launching a Redress Coordination Unit and expanding the AI Incident Dashboard to include systemic bias alerts. Pilot implementations began in France and Finland, but trust was not easily restored.

Simultaneously, a major discontinuity in AI capability emerged: a new model demonstrated unprecedented performance in automated code generation and cyber intrusion planning, marking a clear leap in verifiable domains. While general capability rose only modestly, the jump alarmed security agencies. Open-weight versions of similar models began circulating, enabling targeted attacks on industrial systems. However, distribution bottlenecks and model complexity limited immediate uptake, resulting in a measured rise in openweight_capability to 56.5 — still within the bounds of derivative growth from ai_capability.

Washington escalated pressure on the global semiconductor supply chain, compelling the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s servicing of older lithography machines used by multiple nations. The move, justified under national security grounds, directly undermined EU access to critical fabrication capacity, highlighting European dependency.

### Reckoning at Home

Faced with mounting backlash, senior Commission officials began internal consultations on the creation of Public Algorithmic Accountability Boards as a potential future measure to restore legitimacy through citizen-informed oversight of high-impact AI in public services. However, given the existing portfolio burden — already carrying three large measures including the prioritised Public Trust Corps — formal proposal was deferred. Design work did not begin in this turn, and no new measure was formally added to the agenda. The automated_decision_scandal, an internal-origin event, dealt a severe blow to public confidence and political cohesion.

Despite the urgency, the response was fragmented. Poland and Spain continued to resist full integration of the Sovereign Cyber Shield, and the delayed ENISA scorecard offered little leverage. Emergency funding tied to compliance began to shift behaviour, but too slowly to counter rising sentiment against AI governance as a whole.

### A System Under Strain

The combined weight of external pressure, internal failure, and eroding trust deepened the crisis. Resilience dipped as systems proved vulnerable not only to external threats but to their own logic. Public sentiment collapsed further, and political capital dwindled to near paralysis. The Union acted — but too late, and on too many fronts at once.
