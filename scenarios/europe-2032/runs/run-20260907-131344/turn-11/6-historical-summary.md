A coordinated ransomware attack leveraging AI-generated exploits disabled healthcare systems in Belgium, Bavaria, and Emilia-Romagna, exposing critical gaps in EU cyber resilience and the limitations of voluntary coordination among member states. The Public Trust Corps activated emergency protocols, using the AI Incident Dashboard for real-time public reporting—the first such transparency during a live crisis—but lacked enforcement power, underscoring systemic fragmentation in EU incident response. 

Concurrently, a breakthrough in AI interpretability by DeepMind and Anthropic enabled prediction of emergent planning behaviors in advanced models, offering a foundational tool for proactive safety assurance. The EU’s Joint Research Centre fast-tracked integration of this framework into future oversight systems, marking a significant advance in technical governance capacity.

Despite these efforts, political and public trust eroded further as protests in Utrecht and Lyon expanded, now targeting AI use in migration and social services. The Commission proposed granting binding authority to citizen-led Public Algorithmic Accountability Boards under Article 114 TFEU, backed by technical support and funding incentives. However, opposition from Poland and Spain blocked consensus, leaving the Trust Corps as the sole operational mechanism—limited to monitoring and reporting without enforcement. Governance remained reactive, fragmented, and increasingly detached from societal expectations, deepening the EU’s crisis of legitimacy amid accelerating technological threats.
