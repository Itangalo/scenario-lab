### Holding by hand
The first half of 2031 did not bring a new blackout so much as a fight over the tools for surviving the last one. Small groups smashed telemetry cabinets, drained fuel stores and torched spares yards, leaving notes calling the stockpiles collaboration. Police reports linked a dozen sites across three countries. Where pools had stayed hidden, they burned. Where they had been brought onto municipal books and guarded, they mostly held.

Brussels made that the policy. The Commission, with ENISA and the EU crisis network, ordered the off-books pools registered, dispersed, reimbursed and put under police and gendarmerie watch. Grid operators published plain lists of protected fallback points — generators, transformers, manual breaker instructions — and stayed on segmented manual operation. The containment and mutual-aid protocol agreed the previous year was declared operational: common isolation playbooks, cross-border repair teams, shared fuel accounting.

It helped, partially. Restoration drills ran faster, and two sabotage attempts were stopped by guards. But guards cost money no one had, rural sites remained exposed, and operators complained the map of protected sites was also a map for the next attack.

### Quotas and cancelled cranes
Washington tightened again. Allied buyers were kept inside the system but put on strict volume quotas for advanced chips and model access, with servicing approvals case by case. In The Hague, the decision to comply once more made headlines as humiliation.

At the same time, the global AI building boom broke. Valuations fell sharply, crane sites went quiet, and several commercial compute deals European firms had counted on evaporated. No new procurement was possible even if money had existed. The Commission said openly it would not chase the reset.

One counter-story ran hard. Health ministries and city halls in several member states showed waiting lists falling and permit decisions arriving in days where European-tuned office systems had been deployed, branded explicitly as a European choice. Local press covered it warmly. National news buried it under sabotage and quotas.

The half-year ended with fallback holding but thinner, dependence harder, and the Commission still standing on the same line with almost nothing to spend.
