## In practice
We are closing the Sovereignty Escalation Protocol as planned, but its activation was too narrow and too late. The protocol triggered reciprocity clauses when access was denied — but it could not prevent the Austrian defection, nor could it stop the bio_incident from unfolding under our radar. That failure is now the anchor of our next move.

This turn, we are pivoting from access denial to incident response, using the diplomatic network we began building under the Middle-Power Coalition. The High Representative is convening an emergency session of the Multilateral AI Governance Forum in Helsinki, bringing together health ministers, intelligence leads, and AI safety directors from our partner states. The agenda is urgent: operationalise a joint early-warning system within six months, starting with shared bio-risk screening of model outputs and coordinated takedown of malicious deployments.

We are offering Finland and Sweden hosting rights for the permanent node, with data residency guarantees and joint oversight. Japan and South Korea are being offered priority access to EU interpretability research in exchange for real-time telemetry from their national monitoring systems. Canada will co-lead the legal working group to reconcile data-sharing frameworks across jurisdictions. The budget will draw from the unused portion of the €30bn surge fund, reprogrammed under Article 122 TFEU for crisis response. Resistance is expected from national security agencies and from the German interior ministry, which fears ceding sovereignty — but we are framing this not as surrender, but as scale: no one country can detect every threat, but together, we can see further. This is not just defence — it is the first real institution of collective AI sovereignty.
