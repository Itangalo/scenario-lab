The EU’s declaration that the *eu_frontier_access_denied* event marked a geopolitical rupture was met with mixed reactions. While hospitals and ministries scrambled to restore AI-dependent services, the Commission’s invocation of reciprocity clauses under the Digital Markets Act was stalled by legal challenges from Ireland and Luxembourg, who argued the measures overreached EU competence and threatened billions in cloud infrastructure investment. Emergency compute redirection began, but the sovereign inference grid remained under capacity, delaying transitions.

The new **EU Sovereignty Escalation Protocol** was formally adopted, but implementation lagged. Member-state resistance, particularly from economies tied to U.S. hyperscalers, weakened enforcement. Reciprocity obligations were not binding overnight, and no foreign provider had yet been suspended. The €30bn redirected to open-weight deployment boosted academic labs, but integration into public systems was slow.
