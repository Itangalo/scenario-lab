## In practice
We are treating *eu_frontier_access_denied* not as a technical outage but as a geopolitical act — one that bypassed diplomacy, ignored contracts, and disrupted hospitals and ministries alike. We are invoking Article 21 of the Digital Markets Act as legal basis to require any provider whose systems are embedded in EU critical functions to register under a new Reciprocity Obligation: if their home jurisdiction excludes EU actors, equivalent EU-based services will be excluded in return. The Commission is drafting implementing acts under the Digital Services Act and CLOUD Act equivalence frameworks to enable immediate suspension of data flows and API access upon verified denial.

At the same time, we are redirecting €30bn in unspent Digital Europe Programme funds to accelerate deployment of open-weight models at capability tier 50+ through the newly formalised EU Open Research Corridor. This includes pre-positioning inference clusters at GÉANT nodes and granting emergency compute vouchers to displaced public-sector users. National CIOs are being convened under the NIS2 directive to map dependencies on foreign-hosted AI and transition high-risk workloads within six months. We are offering Germany and France priority access to the new sovereign inference grid in exchange for dropping objections to mutual suspension clauses with U.S. providers. The pushback is fierce — particularly from Ireland and Luxembourg, whose economies rely on hyperscaler hosting — but we are framing this as a matter of continuity, not ideology: you cannot run a single point of failure at continental scale.
