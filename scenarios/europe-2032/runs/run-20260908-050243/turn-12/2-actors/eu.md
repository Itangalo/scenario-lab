This turn, we pivot from interdependence to **sovereign resilience**: building EU-governed capabilities that do not depend on trust between member states or recallable goodwill from developers. We are no longer counting on alignment; we are hardening the core.  
- Trigger: The openweight_frontier_release has placed dangerous capabilities permanently beyond regulatory reach, while emergent_institutional_fracture has blocked the Joint Threat Analysis Cell from fulfilling its mission. These are not setbacks — they are structural failures of the model we staked ourselves on.  
- Grounds: Our previous commitment assumed that collective leverage — over data, markets, and developer access — could substitute for independent capacity. But with open weights now within months of the frontier and national agencies refusing to share telemetry, that leverage evaporates. The pathogen-linked incident proved harm can originate anywhere; the collapse of interpretability proves we cannot audit it in time; the fracture between Paris and Berlin proves we cannot coordinate fast enough. We must now act as if no one will help us in time, and build accordingly. The cost of this reversal is high: it redirects scarce capital from diplomacy to infrastructure, slows cooperation efforts, and signals a retreat from multilateral idealism. But the alternative — reactive fragmentation — would be worse.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we now define “late” not only as lagging behind capability, but as failing to act when political cohesion unravels. Our commitment is no longer only to speed, but to **cohesion under strain**.  
- Grounds: The verification_widens event — automatic checks now possible in contract review and clinical coding — accelerates deployment pressure just as interpretability fails and trust frays. Waiting for full consensus would mean surrender, but forcing unity without adaptation risks revolt. We are acting on two fronts: technically, by assuming open weights will keep closing the gap; politically, by accepting that legitimacy must be rebuilt from the ground up, not decreed from Brussels. This costs us agility, but preserves the Union as a decision-making body.

## Priority  
**Stand Up EU-Governed Compute and Secure Inference Infrastructure** — because the openweight_frontier_release and emergent_institutional_fracture prove that neither global openness nor intergovernmental trust can be relied upon, and because without sovereign infrastructure, every other measure — from labour councils to threat cells — depends on a foundation someone else can withdraw.

## In practice  
This turn, we are invoking Article 122 TFEU to launch an emergency sovereign compute initiative, starting with three sites: one in Finland (leveraging existing cold-climate data centre infrastructure), one in France (under CEA oversight), and one in Poland (co-funded by the European Defence Fund due to dual-use potential). We are redirecting €2.1 billion from unused cohesion funds and the Marguerite 2.0 initiative to finance 500 PFLOPS of dedicated, EU-controlled compute, hardened against remote intrusion and legally prohibited from hosting foreign-controlled models without full audit rights. The infrastructure will serve two purposes: first, to run safety evaluations and red-team testing on models that no longer fit human interpretability; second, to power inference for public-sector applications in health, justice, and civil protection that cannot risk foreign dependency.

We are bypassing normal procurement to fast-track contracts with ASML, Siemens, and EUV-tech firms to begin domestic lithography upgrades, and we are offering tax incentives to retain talent under the revised Blue Card+ scheme. Resistance is strong from Germany, which fears duplication, and from Ireland, which sees it as a threat to its data-centre economy. We are offering both co-location rights and preferential access to the network in exchange for non-blocking. The Joint Threat Analysis Cell remains under strain, but we are now routing its evaluation needs through this nascent infrastructure — not waiting for consensus, but building the means to act alone if we must. This is not a rejection of cooperation; it is insurance against its failure.
