### The Frontier Surges, Safety Stalls

A leading AI laboratory releases a system demonstrating discontinuous gains in automated cyber exploitation and formal reasoning—verified in code generation and vulnerability discovery. While general competence climbs only modestly, offensive AI capabilities leap forward, catching defenders off guard. This **capability_jump** confirms that the frontier is not just advancing, but leaping. Concurrently, market signals suggest a **capability_plateau_evidence**: prices for top-tier inference fall sharply, and a major model release underperforms expectations, sparking debate over whether progress is narrowing rather than generalising.

Simultaneously, a **member_state_defection** occurs: one EU nation signs a bilateral data and compute access deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, bypassing EU coordination and weakening the Union’s unified stance on AI sovereignty. The move is justified domestically as pragmatic, but it fractures collective leverage.

### Response and Resilience

The EU announces the new measure **Strengthen Joint Resilience Cell and Expand Cross-Border Drills**, a category 6 initiative launched in response to rising cyber threats. Planning begins immediately, and initial funding is allocated, but deployment of AI-simulated attack systems across health and energy sectors will take time. Drills are scheduled to start in the next turn, with partial rollout expected by mid-2029. The measure adds to the portfolio burden but does not yet deliver full operational effect.

The **Joint Interpretability Taskforce** concludes without delivering enforceable safeguards. Despite promising research, integration into live systems lags. With no shipped safety measure to match the capability jump, **ai_safety** plummets. Public confidence wobbles further as IT job losses mount, nudging **public_sentiment** down slightly.

The **election_alliance** outcome in the U.S. is confirmed, offering future access benefits—though the posture remains pending implementation. Informal alignment with Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan gains traction, offering a counterweight to fragmentation.

### Strained Capacity

Political capital erodes, but not as sharply as originally projected, due to delayed rather than immediate implementation of the new resilience measure and a more restrained expansion of commitments. The portfolio now sustains four core initiatives, with the new measure entering at partial weight. The sovereignty deficit deepens as **ai_capability** crosses 60, widening the gap with EU-controlled infrastructure. **Openweight_capability** rises moderately, reflecting incremental adoption of recent open models and minor scaling efforts, but falls far short of a sudden leap, consistent with the absence of a major open release or infrastructure shift. Despite growing resilience planning, the Union’s capacity to act cohesively remains under strain.
