### Crisis and Command

The European Union declared an AI governance emergency under Article 222 TFEU, responding to a cascading loss of regulatory confidence and a major cyber intrusion enabled by AI-generated tooling. A sweeping moratorium was proposed on new AI deployments in critical infrastructure, with a fast-tracked reassessment framework to be finalized by mid-2033. The Commission issued a binding recommendation, backed by promises of sovereign compute access for compliant states, and convened a special European Council session for October.

But the unity it sought fractured on delivery. France delayed transposition, citing risks to its national health AI programs. Sweden and Finland raised alarms over economic disruption, demanding exemptions. Germany insisted the move overstepped EU competence, while Ireland warned of investor flight. The promised CERN-hosted Scientific Panel stalled—no members were appointed by year’s end.

The Joint Threat Analysis Cell remained offline, though intelligence feeds from bilateral pacts began informally shaping the safety reassessment. ENISA and the European Medicines Agency paused select integrations, but enforcement was patchy. Public messaging split: younger voters saw prudence; professionals in law and medicine decried paralysis. No visible benefit landed.

Meanwhile, offensive AI capabilities advanced. The cyber incident revealed self-modifying payloads that evaded static detection, and open-weight models capable of generating exploit code spread widely. With no new safeguards deployed, the gap between capability and control deepened.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

`ai_capability` rose by 1.5, driven by autonomous adaptation in cyber and coding tasks. `openweight_capability` jumped to 66.0, as frontier weights leaked and replication tools improved. But the EU’s emergency measures yielded no immediate safety gains—no new controls shipped, no interpretability restored. `ai_safety` remained frozen at 1.0.

Resilience held at 58.0, spared further erosion only because harm was absorbed locally. Sovereignty fell to 7.0: dependency deepened as capability surged and no sovereign compute came online. Political capital stayed at 9.0—trapped by portfolio costs and fragmentation. Public sentiment held, ambivalent and brittle.

The Union acted—but did not land.
