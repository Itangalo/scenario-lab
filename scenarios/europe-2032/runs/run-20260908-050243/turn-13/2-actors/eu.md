Our commitment is no longer only to speed, but to **cohesion under strain**.  
- Trigger: The **cyber_major_incident** and **emergent_ai_governance_crisis** have demonstrated that the failure of interpretability is no longer a technical concern—it is a systemic rupture. Regulators are suspending approvals; public trust is fraying; and the Joint Threat Analysis Cell remains offline while bilateral pacts proliferate. Our ability to act as a Union is now under direct threat.  
- Grounds: We are no longer merely racing against capability—we are holding the Union together while it fractures under pressure. The cyber incident proves harm is no longer hypothetical; the governance crisis proves our oversight bodies no longer believe in their own tools. We must now treat political disintegration as a first-order risk, equal to cyber or bio. This means doubling down on measures that can be implemented unilaterally, even at the cost of deeper resistance from member states. The cost of this reversal is legitimacy—some will see this as centralisation by crisis—but the alternative is incoherence.

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that **capability without control is not sovereignty, it is surrender**.  
- Trigger: The **cyber_major_incident** was enabled by AI-generated tooling, and the **emergent_ai_governance_crisis** has revealed that our certification frameworks are obsolete. The very systems meant to secure us are now the vectors of harm, and the gap between capability and safety has become a chasm.  
- Grounds: Our identity must reflect the new reality: the Union is not failing because it lacks compute, but because it lacks **governance at speed**. We are not merely balancing two mandates—we are discovering that the second—preventing lasting harm—is the precondition for the first. Without it, sovereignty collapses into spectacle. This redefinition does not abandon capability—it reorients it toward resilience and control. The cost is ideological: we can no longer promise both rapid advancement and ironclad safety. We must now choose which we are, when forced.

## In practice  
This turn, we are acting under Article 222 TFEU (EU Solidarity Clause) to declare an AI governance emergency, triggered by the convergence of the cyber_major_incident and the collapse of regulatory confidence. We are issuing a Commission Recommendation—binding in intent but flexible in form—to suspend new AI deployments in critical infrastructure until a unified safety framework is adopted by Q2 2033. The European Medicines Agency, ENISA, and the European Judicial Network are instructed to halt integration of new AI systems in clinical decision support, cyber defence, and legal automation unless they meet an interim audit standard to be published in three months.

We are fast-tracking a special session of the European Council on AI Safety, to be convened in October 2032, where we will present a draft framework based on zero-trust verification, runtime monitoring, and mandatory red-teaming for all high-risk systems. The European Defence Fund is being tapped to support the development of AI control primitives—interpretable agents, sandboxed execution, and model dissection tools—under the oversight of a newly convened Scientific Panel on AI Safety, hosted at CERN. We are offering member states preferential access to the sovereign compute infrastructure we are building as an incentive to comply, and we are exempting existing public-sector AI uses from the moratorium if they undergo immediate third-party audit.

Resistance is expected from France, which sees this as a delay to its national AI champions, and from the Nordic states, which fear economic fallout. We are offering co-lead on the reassessment panel and early access to the new evaluation tools we are developing. The Joint Threat Analysis Cell remains offline, but we are now feeding its intelligence requirements directly into the reassessment process—using crisis to force coherence. This is not business as usual. This is the Union choosing survival over speed.
