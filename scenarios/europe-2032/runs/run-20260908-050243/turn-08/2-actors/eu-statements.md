# Statements: The European Union (turn 8)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Build and wield independent AI leverage to ensure the EU can shape its own technological future, not merely adapt to it

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer mistake speed for agency when leverage lies elsewhere.
  - Grounds: The *openweight_frontier_release* event proves that capability diffusion is no longer a function of time or policy—it is now a matter of download speed. Our emergency redirection of compute and surge funding for open-weight deployment did not prevent disruption because the bottleneck was never infrastructure, but authority: we could not act coherently in the face of member-state fragmentation and external control. We remain committed to acting early, but this turn reveals that our early actions must be more precisely leveraged, not merely faster. The cost of this reversal is credibility with domestic reformers who expected decisive unilateral action, but it is necessary to align means with real power.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **add** `leverage_over_mimicry` — applied
  - Tier: position
  - Text: The EU’s path to sovereignty does not run through matching the frontier model for model, but through controlling access, shaping ecosystems, and binding the capabilities that matter into enforceable relationships.
  - Grounds: With openweight models now within 5–10 points of the frontier and permanently distributed, the idea that we can “catch up” via compute alone is obsolete. The denial of access was not reversed by building more data centres — it was ignored. Our leverage now lies in coordination, not duplication: in the chokepoints we hold collectively with other middle powers, in the rules we enforce on systems embedded in our critical functions, and in the speed with which we can *withhold* as well as adopt.
