### Emergency Resilience Ends, New Crisis Begins

The Emergency Resilience Surge concluded in June with technical success—no major infrastructure breach occurred during its six-month run, and automated patching reduced response times by over 70%. This modest improvement in system durability contributed to a slight rise in resilience and public confidence.

However, the broader context darkened. A global ai_investment_collapse saw venture funding dry up, with major AI labs scaling back training runs and scrapping planned data centres. While this briefly slowed the pace of capability growth, it also endangered EU compute partnerships tied to private Western capital.

### Crisis Response Meets Resistance

The EU launched the Autonomous Systems Emergency Restriction Order in response to rising alarm over uncontrolled agentic AI. Invoking emergency powers under the AI Act, the Commission imposed a moratorium on autonomous operation in healthcare, energy, and rail. The move was framed as a necessary pause to build verifiable safety controls.

Despite high-level coordination, pushback was immediate. France and Germany delayed Council adoption, demanding exemptions for their national champions. The Joint Assessment Cell was established but remains under-resourced. US developers with EU operations expressed compliance in public but continued deploying semi-autonomous systems through local subsidiaries under looser interpretations.

Still, the symbolic force of the action boosted public sentiment slightly, and preliminary safety improvements justify a narrow gain in ai_safety, as developers begin submitting containment protocols ahead of certification.

### Sovereignty Drifts, Capital Tightens

With ai_capability rising by 1.5, eu_ai_sovereignty declined by 1. No capacity-securing event occurred, and the investment collapse disrupted planned compute expansions.
