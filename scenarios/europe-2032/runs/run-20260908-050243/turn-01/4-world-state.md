### Emergency Response Under Pressure

The EU launched the Emergency Resilience Surge in response to the global cyber intrusion, mandating rapid deployment of AI-powered detection and automated patching across critical infrastructure. Leveraging the newly effective swarm-behaviour detection tools, the directive moved swiftly through a joint parliamentary procedure, narrowly passing in December despite resistance from smaller states concerned about sovereignty and cost. Full technical support and funding incentives secured compliance, and stress tests began in early January.

The measure’s visible momentum, coupled with the public success of AI in reducing hospital wait times in France and Finland, improved public sentiment. The narrative shifted from anxiety to cautious optimism, especially as no major disruptions occurred during rollout.

### Capability Creeps Forward

AI capability advanced steadily, reaching 54.0 as leading labs compressed release cycles further and integrated self-improvement loops. The safety_breakthrough—real-time prediction of emergent model behaviours—was rapidly adopted by major developers, boosting assurance on deployed systems. This, combined with reduced exploit surfaces from the Resilience Surge, lifted ai_safety to 39.0.

However, openweight_capability rose only modestly to 41.0, as the gap between closed and open frontiers narrowed slowly. Moonshot’s Kimi K3 remained the most capable open model, but no new releases pushed the boundary further.
