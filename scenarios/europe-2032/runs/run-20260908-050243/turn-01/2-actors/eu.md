## In practice
We are treating the autumn intrusion as a near-miss with strategic consequences. Under Article 114 TFEU, the Commission will table a temporary Crisis Resilience Directive by November, mandating AI intrusion detection and automated patching systems across all critical energy, water and port infrastructure operators in the EU. This is not a review or a recommendation: it is a binding, time-bound upgrade with 80% co-financing from the Recovery and Resilience Facility top-up we will propose to the Council in December. The directive leverages the cyber_defence_breakthrough — specifically, the new swarm-behaviour detection stack developed at ENISA’s Joint Cyber Unit — and requires deployment within six months.

We are fast-tracking implementation through the Civil Protection Mechanism, treating this as a cross-border emergency. Member states will be required to designate national resilience officers, and ENISA will run weekly stress tests starting in January. Resistance is expected from smaller member states citing cost and sovereignty, but we are offering full technical support from the EU Cyber Rapid Response Teams and tying compliance to cohesion fund disbursements in 2027. The European Parliament’s Internal Market and Civil Liberties committees will be consulted in a joint urgent procedure, with a vote scheduled for mid-December.

At the same time, we are holding the course on sovereignty. The InvestAI Gigafactories are in early site selection, with Poland, Spain and Romania shortlisted; we are using the ASML leverage — still intact — to tie export permissions to joint chip fab investment in the EU. The Tech sovereignty package is facing pushback in the Council over permitting exceptions, so we are offering a trade: accelerated zones in exchange for binding commitments on EU-domiciled AI operations from the hyperscalers. This is not ideal, but it keeps momentum. We cannot build our way to safety without compute, and we cannot wait five years to get it.
