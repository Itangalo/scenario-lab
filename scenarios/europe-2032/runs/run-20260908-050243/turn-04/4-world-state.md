### A Surge in Offense, a Strain on Defense

The first half of 2028 is defined by a major cyber incident: a model-generated exploit chain propagates through a poisoned software dependency, compromising health and transport systems across multiple member states. The attack’s sophistication suggests offensive AI capabilities have decisively outpaced defenses. Despite rapid coordination under NIS2, full containment takes weeks, and public trust erodes.

In response, the EU launches Cyber Shield Activation, a large-scale resilience push to harden critical services. Automated detection signatures are deployed, failover drills mandated, and a Joint Resilience Cell established. However, resistance from several member states delays binding implementation, weakening early impact. Still, partial progress supports a modest resilience gain.

Meanwhile, leaked benchmark data from a leading lab reveals anomalous behavior—an unreleased system exhibits unanticipated reasoning patterns under evaluation. Developers dismiss it as noise, but safety experts warn of hidden capability jumps. With no finished safeguards, ai_safety drops sharply.

An open-weight release follows closely behind the closed frontier, spreading powerful tooling widely. openweight_capability surges, narrowing the gap with ai_capability, which itself rises by +1.5 due to sustained closed-lab progress.

The Joint Interpretability Taskforce continues its work, but delivers no completed measure this turn. Political capital is strained by three large ongoing measures—InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, and now Cyber Shield—plus the named priority. The new initiative earns a proposal bonus for responding directly to the recent cyberattack, but this is outweighed by portfolio costs and reputational damage from the incident.

Public sentiment dips as media spotlight job losses in IT security and widespread service disruptions.

### Shifting Alliances

Behind the scenes, informal coordination grows among EU members, Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan on shared AI safety evaluations—a quiet move toward collective leverage amid faltering U.S. cooperation.
