A major cyberattack using AI-generated exploit chains compromised critical health and transport systems in early 2028, exposing the EU’s defensive lag and eroding public trust. In response, the EU launched Cyber Shield Activation, establishing a Joint Resilience Cell and deploying automated defenses, though delayed member state compliance weakened initial effectiveness. Resilience improved modestly, but ai_safety declined sharply amid warnings of unreleased models exhibiting anomalous, advanced reasoning—unaddressed due to incomplete safeguards. An open-weight release rapidly boosted openweight_capability, narrowing the gap with frontier AI, while closed-lab progress pushed ai_capability to 58.5. The Joint Interpretability Taskforce made no new certifications, and political capital declined further under the strain of managing multiple large initiatives—InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, and Cyber Shield—amid post-incident reputational damage. Public sentiment fell due to service disruptions and IT job losses. Informal AI safety coordination expanded among EU members, Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan, signaling a quiet shift toward regional alignment in the face of weakened U.S. cooperation.
