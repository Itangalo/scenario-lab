## In practice  
We are treating the safety_breakthrough not as a laboratory curiosity but as a pivot point: for the first time, a control technique has landed in deployed systems with measurable assurance gains, and the labs want it adopted. This is our opening. Under the Digital Europe Programme and in coordination with the AI Office, we are launching a Joint Interpretability Taskforce co-led by ENISA, the European Laboratory for Learning and Intelligent Systems (ELLIS), and a rotating chair from industry — selected from among those who have submitted verifiable containment protocols under the Emergency Restriction Order. Its mandate is clear: within two turns, produce an EU-wide standard for certifiable interpretability in high-risk AI systems, based on the breakthrough’s core method. This will feed directly into the AI Act’s conformity assessments and become a condition for market access.

We are allocating €1.2bn from the Digital Europe envelope to fund rapid prototyping and integration into existing evaluation pipelines at national level. The taskforce will have authority to fast-track third-party audits and to require model modifications for compliance — a power we are justifying through Article 46a of the AI Act, as amended by the 2025 Governance Package. To ensure uptake, we are linking participation to benefits: full adopters will receive reduced fees for certification and priority access to the InvestAI testbeds. Resistance will come from firms relying on opaque architectures, particularly in predictive policing and insurance underwriting, so we are preparing a phased rollout, starting with energy and healthcare. We are also using this moment to strengthen our own hand: a portion of the funding will go to sovereign research institutes to replicate and extend the method independently, ensuring the EU does not become dependent on foreign-held interpretability IP. This is not just about safety — it is about building sovereign technical muscle where it matters most.
