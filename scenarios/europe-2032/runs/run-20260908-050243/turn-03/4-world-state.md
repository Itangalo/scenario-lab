### A Narrow Window for Control

The Joint Interpretability Taskforce was launched with urgency, capitalising on rare alignment between regulators and developers following a major safety breakthrough. For the first time, a method for certifying model behaviour before deployment showed measurable gains in real systems, not just labs. The EU moved swiftly to institutionalise it, establishing a cross-border team led by ENISA and ELLIS, backed by €1.2bn in Digital Europe funds.

Early coordination proved difficult. Industry participation was uneven—firms in predictive policing and insurance resisted mandatory audits, citing trade secrecy. Only after threats to revoke testbed access did two major US-based developers agree to join. Still, the taskforce made progress: prototype evaluation modules were integrated into national AI offices by December, and the first draft of an EU interpretability standard was published.

Public sentiment edged upward as officials framed the initiative as a win for accountability. Visible public benefit from safer AI in healthcare monitoring contributed to a modest +1 lift.

However, broader structural pressures deepened. Global AI investment remained frozen, disrupting InvestAI Gigafactories' timelines despite continued funding. With ai_capability rising by +1.5—driven by closed-lab advances—the gap between frontier and sovereign capacity widened. eu_ai_sovereignty fell to 19.0, reflecting stalled domestic compute growth and no new secured access.

The Autonomous Systems Emergency Restriction Order formally concluded but left enforcement patchy. France and Germany granted limited exemptions, weakening precedent. No proposal bonus was earned for the new measure, as no qualifying negative event preceded it.

Political capital dipped to 37.0 under the weight of three large ongoing measures and the named priority. Though the taskforce is seen as strategically vital, its complexity added strain without immediate payoff.

### Signals of Shift

A growing coalition in the European Parliament now calls for binding agentic AI oversight, pushing beyond emergency powers toward permanent audit regimes—a shift from crisis response to institutional control.

Meanwhile, across the Atlantic, both presidential campaigns hardened their stance: technological dominance must be preserved at all costs. Any sharing, they argue, erodes America’s edge.
