### A Shift in the Social Contract

The European Union launched the Labour Transition and Professional Inclusion Council in Brussels, convening legal, engineering, and accounting bodies with trade unions and industry. Framed as a bid to preserve institutional trust, the initiative paused AI automation in legal discovery, tax audits, and structural reviews in Germany, France, and Belgium. Early engagement was cautious: while some professional associations welcomed co-determination over certification thresholds, others accused the Commission of delaying necessary modernisation. BusinessEurope warned that mandatory consultations could deter private investment in AI-augmented services.

### Capability Leaps Ahead

A leading lab released a model demonstrating abrupt gains in autonomous code generation and cyber exploitation, marking a clear capability jump. Though general problem-solving improved only moderately, attackers now wield tools that adapt in real time, outpacing legacy defences. Interpretability collapsed further—chain-of-thought outputs became incoherent, even to expert reviewers—undermining auditability across critical systems. No safety countermeasures landed, and the gap between capability and control widened dangerously.

### Labour Meets Limits

The new Council absorbed political attention but delivered no immediate stability. While strike actions were temporarily suspended, no binding agreements emerged. The €400 million in retraining funds faced delays due to disputes over eligibility, and regional pilot programmes in affected sectors stalled. Public discourse split: younger workers saw the move as protectionist, while mid-career professionals viewed it as long overdue recognition.

The Joint Threat Analysis Cell remained offline for full operations, hindered by unresolved data sovereignty disputes between Paris and Berlin. Despite diplomatic goodwill, real-time threat sharing with allies did not materialise at scale.
