### The jump and the machines
Winter ended with two shocks arriving together. An American lab demonstrated a new system that made last year's roadmaps look quaint — longer planning, stranger tool-use, benchmark scores passed around Brussels in leaked screenshots. Weeks later, commercial humanoids moved from pilot videos to order books. Chinese firms already building most of the world's units quoted delivery times; American control software set the terms.

For Europe it landed on the factory floor it still owned. German auto suppliers, Lombard logistics firms, Czech machine-tool makers received the same pitch: buy the platform now or lose the contract. Works councils asked what retraining meant when dexterity itself was for sale. Defence ministries circulated memos about logistics robots seen with weapons mounts, with no doctrine to answer them.

### Washington opens, Brussels bargains
In January the new American administration took office promising allied access on published tiers, joint testing and aligned export rules. The Commission sent negotiators to lock in researcher access and evaluation cooperation, insisting in every council that the deal would protect, not replace, the two narrowed gigafactory sites still holding grid priority while Taiwanese chip allocations stayed frozen.

Washington talked; delivery lagged. Tiers were published, pilot access granted to a handful of EU labs, joint evaluation procedures drafted but not yet run on the new system. Relief that cutoff was off the table mixed with suspicion that access meant dependence.

### Small towns, boil notices, robots
At home the Trust and Transition Pact became the face of Europe. Wage bridges paid out in three regions, staffing pacts put relief nurses and utility technicians into some municipalities, civil-protection teams drilled manual chlorination fallback after the autumn boil-water fiasco. Where crews arrived, mayors co-signed and waiting-list gains from triage assistants were replayed on public channels.

Where they did not, resentment hardened. Reinsurers kept small utilities at arm's length despite the public backstop. Two mayors refused to co-sign staffing deals, calling them cover for mandated procedures that had caused the failures. News of warehouse robots displacing night shifts in Liège and Łódź played beside those boil notices.

The older sovereignty drive and the bio-shield regime formally closed, freeing money but delivering mostly paperwork: permits, sites, monitoring networks. Capacity at home barely moved while the frontier leapt ahead.
