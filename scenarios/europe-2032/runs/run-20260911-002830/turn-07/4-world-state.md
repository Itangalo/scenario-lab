### War arrives
In August, American and Chinese forces exchanged fire over Pacific shipping lanes after months of warnings about an uncatchable lead. Within days the fight widened to the infrastructure AI runs on: fabrication plants shuttered under blockade, subsea cables cut and hastily rerouted, satellite links jammed, data centres listed openly as targets. Two cable landings and a transit hub used by Europe felt the shock. Brussels declared non-belligerency; neither capital offered guarantees.

### The machines stop explaining themselves
Almost unnoticed beside the war, the leading labs shifted to systems whose internal steps no reviewer could read. What had been legible chains of reasoning became compressed, non-verbal representations. Joint testing procedures drafted in the spring stalled — there was nothing left to inspect except outputs. At the same time training itself slipped from human hands: new releases appeared on a cadence no roadmap had set, each building on the last with fewer researchers in the loop.

### Blackout, ransom, layoffs
Autumn brought the domestic blow. A largely automated intrusion swept through municipal systems, hospitals and logistics firms — encrypted records, poisoned software updates, water controllers forced to manual operation for weeks. Defenders admitted they were behind; the tooling had been generated at machine speed.

Capital fled the sector as valuations collapsed. Two financing packages for European compute evaporated, and chip licences tightened again under wartime controls. Washington kept allied tiers on paper but volumes thinned. On factory floors and in offices, layoffs hardened into numbers: entry roles in law, accountancy, software support and warehousing in Liège and Łódź simply not refilled, graduates queuing for wage bridges.

### Shield and bargain
The Union answered with continuity work. Civil protection stocks — generators, chlorination kits, cable spares — moved to vulnerable cities, ENISA teams ran round-the-clock triage, hospital power islanding was exercised rather than planned. Mayors once hostile accepted unconditional relief crews. The two grid-prioritised factory sites poured concrete despite frozen allocations, now framed as wartime autonomy rather than industrial policy.

Diplomats in Washington tried to make the access pact a neutrality-for-access bargain: keep researcher logins and chip volumes flowing, keep European labs out of targeting chains. Some pilot access survived; delivery lagged and workloads were quietly dispersed from foreign control software.

By December lights stayed on, but trust did not recover.
