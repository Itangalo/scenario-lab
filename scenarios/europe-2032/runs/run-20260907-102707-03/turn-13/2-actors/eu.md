## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that without sovereign control over the systems that govern us, neither mandate can be fulfilled. Governance without agency is ritual. Harm prevention without access to the systems causing harm is prayer.  
- Grounds: At ai_safety 0.0 and eu_political_capital −7.0, the Union is not merely weak — it is structurally excluded from the circuits where decisions are made. The medical_breakthrough this turn delivered life-saving therapies, but they were generated by models the EU cannot audit, inspect, or run independently. The public sees benefit, but not from *our* systems — only dependence deepens. This is not a failure of policy but of power: we are asking citizens to trust outcomes we did not shape and cannot verify. The two mandates, once seen as tensioned, now converge: sovereignty is the only path to safety, and safety is the only justification for sovereignty. Without this reframe, both collapse into irrelevance.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without a sovereign technical foundation to stand on. This foundation is not optional infrastructure; it is the precondition of any credible commitment.  
- Grounds: No change in development, but the statement must reflect the deepening crisis. The prior modification anticipated this turn’s collapse: political capital has fallen to −7.0, public sentiment is frozen at 17.0, and the only measure in flight that touches the core problem — Sovereign Compute Backbone — is stalled by legal resistance and hyperscaler defiance. Acting under uncertainty without sovereignty has become indistinguishable from performative governance. The commitment stands, hardened by failure.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign technical agency through auditable AI, establish a sovereign compute and deployment infrastructure, and demonstrate its operational necessity by migrating critical public services onto it — but only if the infrastructure is legally enforced, physically secured, and operationally independent by turn 18. If it is not, we will have failed our core mandate.  
- Grounds: The Sovereign Compute Backbone was declared a priority, but legal challenges in Poland and Portugal have halted construction. Hyperscalers resist mandatory failover nodes, and the Anti-Coercion Instrument is being tested in real time. The Union cannot afford another turn of symbolic action. This update binds the commitment to material reality: by turn 18, we must be able to run, protect, and audit our own models — or admit we cannot govern in the age of AI. The deadline is not arbitrary; it is the last window before ai_capability reaches 100.0 and the instrument falls out of range.

## In practice  
This turn, we are enforcing the Sovereign Compute Backbone as a matter of survival. The Commission has invoked Article 122a TFEU to override local objections in Poland and Portugal, treating the infrastructure as essential for collective security in the face of uncontrolled AI advancement. ENISA and the European Defence Agency are now jointly responsible for physical and cyber protection of the first nodes, with NATO liaison embedded for rapid response. We are testing the limits of the Anti-Coercion Instrument by compelling hyperscalers with EU operations to establish sovereign failover instances — not as a request, but as a condition of market access. Non-compliant providers face exclusion from EU procurement, starting with critical contracts in health and border management.

The Public Accountability Migration Programme remains on paper, awaiting the infrastructure it depends on. DG HOME, DG SANTE, and Eurostat are identifying the highest-risk automated systems, but migration cannot begin without sovereign nodes online. The European Data Protection Supervisor has paused co-design talks, citing legal uncertainty. We are offering member states a 75% funding match for transition costs, but without bond authorization or reallocation, even that promise lacks teeth. The truth is plain: we are trying to rebuild trust with systems we cannot yet run. This turn, the only credible action is to ensure the stack exists at all.
