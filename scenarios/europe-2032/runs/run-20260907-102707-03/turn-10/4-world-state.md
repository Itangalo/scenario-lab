### The Fracture Widens

Global AI capability advances by 2.0 points this turn, reaching 92.0. The pace remains steady, but the consequences deepen: frontier systems now operate in domains where human oversight is functionally absent, their reasoning opaque even to their developers. No safety framework has scaled to match, and the loss_of_control_incident—now under forensic review—confirms that current safeguards are insufficient.

The European Union responds to the sudden denial of frontier model access with emergency measures. Over 120 hospitals and 18 ministries face disruption as the blocked model was embedded in diagnostics and forecasting systems. Interim replacements, drawn from the Open Science Resilience Initiative’s open protein folding models, are deployed rapidly. Though slower, they are auditable and compliant, reinforcing confidence in transparent systems. The European Cybersecurity Agency, bolstered by real-time intelligence sharing under a newly activated mutual aid pact, traces the incident chain, marking a rare success in joint threat attribution.

Brussels activates crisis protocols, fast-tracking integration between the Initiative’s research hubs and national regulators in Germany, France, and Italy. Binding mandates are issued to restrict critical infrastructure AI procurement to fully interpretable systems by Q3 2031—a de facto moratorium on unverifiable frontier models. Yet, political capital continues to erode. With no new measure launched but existing commitments ongoing, capital drops to 5.0 under sustained portfolio cost.

Public sentiment holds at 27.0—neither swayed by the breakthrough nor the incident, as citizens weigh reliability against performance. Resilience rises slightly to 50.0, reflecting improved coordination and the first operational test of mutual defense mechanisms. However, EU AI sovereignty declines to 20.0, hit by the loss of frontier access and the inability to substitute at scale. The Union’s dependence is now undeniable, and its leverage remains thin.
