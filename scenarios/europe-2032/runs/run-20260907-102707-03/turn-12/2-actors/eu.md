Meanwhile, the denial of frontier access during the biosecurity incident proved that sovereignty in principle means nothing without sovereignty in execution. We are acting under uncertainty still — the full implications of 94.5 capability are unknown — but we now understand that action without sovereign capacity is not just risky; it is performative. This update hardens the lesson: we will not again stake legitimacy on systems we do not control.

The Sovereign Compute Backbone is not merely an enabler — it is the proving ground. To show that auditable AI is not a slower alternative but a necessary foundation for resilience and legitimacy, we must run it where it matters: in social services, biosecurity, and crisis response. The scandal over wrongful benefit denials makes this urgent: the public will not tolerate opaque automation, but they also cannot accept systemic failure. Only by migrating these services onto our own stack can we prove that sovereignty and accountability are not luxuries — they are the only way to govern at all.

## New measure  
**Public Accountability Migration Programme**  
Migrate high-impact, high-exposure public services — starting with social insurance, healthcare eligibility, and border processing — from opaque or externally dependent AI systems to auditable models running on sovereign infrastructure.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` public_sentiment +15.0 (via visible repair of broken systems), resilience +5.0 (by reducing dependence on black-box automation), eu_ai_sovereignty +5.0 (through operational demand for sovereign stack), ai_safety +10.0 (by forcing transparency and auditability into high-stakes domains)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions and member states, with binding migration deadlines and shared technical support from DG CONNECT and the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium

## In practice  
This turn, we are launching the Public Accountability Migration Programme as the operational test of our sovereignty: we will move the very systems that failed the public — starting with the social insurance platform at the heart of the scandal — onto auditable models running within the Sovereign Compute Backbone. DG HOME, DG SANTE, and Eurostat are being directed to identify the highest-risk automated decision systems in use, with a binding requirement to migrate them by turn 15. The European Data Protection Supervisor will co-design audit trails, and the European Ombudsman will publish quarterly transparency reports. To accelerate adoption, we are offering member states a 75% funding match for transition costs and fast-tracking procurement for sovereign-compliant vendors.

The Sovereign Compute Backbone moves from legal declaration to physical buildout: the Commission, under Article 122a TFEU, has issued binding siting directives to Finland, Portugal, and Poland, overriding local objections on national security grounds. ENISA and the European Defence Agency are jointly securing the first nodes, with NATO liaison for physical protection. We are invoking the Anti-Coercion Instrument to compel hyperscalers with EU operations to establish sovereign failover instances — no longer optional, but a condition of market access. Resistance from cloud providers is being met with the threat of market segmentation: those who comply will gain preferential access to the EU’s future AI procurement; those who do not will be excluded from critical contracts. The message is clear: if you serve the EU, you serve it on EU terms.
