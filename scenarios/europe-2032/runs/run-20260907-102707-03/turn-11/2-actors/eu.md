## New measure  
**Sovereign Compute Backbone**  
Establish a legally anchored, militarily protected, and publicly governed AI compute infrastructure on EU soil, capable of training and running auditable frontier-equivalent models independently of foreign providers.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 17`

## In practice  
This turn, we are launching the Sovereign Compute Backbone under Article 122a TFEU and the newly activated Crisis Coordination Protocol, treating compute as critical infrastructure. The Commission, in coordination with the European Defence Agency and ENISA, is identifying three geographically dispersed sites — in Finland, Portugal, and Poland — for hardened data centres with guaranteed grid access, physical protection, and legal status that places them beyond extraterritorial claims. We are invoking the Anti-Coercion Instrument to negotiate binding access and non-interference agreements with current hyperscaler operators, demanding that any facility serving EU-critical workloads must have a sovereign failover mode: a mirror instance, fully provisioned, running on EU-controlled hardware with EU-held keys.

We are also restructuring the Open Science Resilience Initiative’s final phase: its turn-12 completion will now include not just model delivery, but integration with the first Sovereign Compute nodes. DG CONNECT is fast-tracking procurement rules under the Crisis Exception to bypass normal tendering, while offering member states hosting the centres priority access to training capacity and AI public services. Resistance from smaller states fearing centralisation is being met with guarantees of distributed governance and shared equity. The message is clear: auditable AI is our standard, but sovereignty is our substrate. We will not again be shut out of our own systems.
