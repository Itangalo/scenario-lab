# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign technical agency through auditable AI

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without a sovereign technical foundation to stand on.
  - Grounds: The **automated_decision_scandal** and the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event have converged to expose a fatal flaw: our capacity to act decisively is hollow without sovereign AI infrastructure. We acted under uncertainty last turn by doubling down on auditable AI, but we did so while still dependent on foreign systems for crisis response. Now, with public sentiment collapsing and political capital at 5.0, we see that commitment without capacity is not courage — it is performance. The scandal proves that even domestically deployed systems, when opaque or externally controlled, can become vectors of systemic harm. From now on, acting under uncertainty must mean building sovereign capacity *first*, not just reacting after the fact. This is not a retreat from boldness — it is a reordering of sequence. We will still move early, but only where we control the stack.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign technical agency through auditable AI, and establish a sovereign compute and deployment infrastructure to ensure it can operate at scale
  - Grounds: The Open Science Resilience Initiative has proven that auditable models *can* function in critical domains, but the denial of frontier access revealed that capability without sovereign delivery infrastructure is useless. We cannot mandate interpretable AI in hospitals and ministries if we cannot guarantee its continuous, independent operation. The current crisis — where 120 hospitals had to fall back on slower open models — is not a temporary setback; it is the baseline condition of dependence. To fulfill our commitment, we must now build the physical and legal infrastructure to run, update, and defend these systems without external permission. This expansion does not change our direction — it makes it real.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
