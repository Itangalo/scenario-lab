### The Sovereignty Gambit

The European Union launches the Sovereign Compute Backbone, a sweeping initiative to build militarily protected, legally insulated AI infrastructure across Finland, Portugal, and Poland. Invoking Article 122a TFEU and the Crisis Coordination Protocol, the Commission fast-tracks procurement and demands hyperscalers establish sovereign failover nodes under EU control. The move is framed as existential: without control of the stack, no policy is enforceable.

Yet political capital erodes sharply. At 5.0, launching a large measure (−3) and naming it priority (−1) drains reserves, while ongoing costs for the Open Science Resilience Initiative (−3) push capital into negative territory at −2.0. With capital below 12, the Union risks losing agenda control — but the measure begins under emergency provisions, though full implementation will take time and face delays due to legal challenges and infrastructure bottlenecks.

Public sentiment plummets to 17.0 after a damning ombudsman report reveals an AI-driven social insurance system wrongfully denied benefits to thousands, some for over two years. The scandal, rooted in a non-frontier but opaque domestic system, triggers widespread anger and confirms fears of unaccountable automation. Trust in AI governance collapses.

Meanwhile, global AI capability advances by 2.5 points to 94.5, deepening the terminal zone. Frontier models now self-direct research agendas with no human-readable rationale. With no safety progress, and a major internal scandal, concerns about ai_safety intensify — but the metric remains at 0.0, reflecting systemic fragility without a formal downward re-rating.

Openweight_capability rises modestly to 62.5, reflecting early diffusion of capabilities from a confirmed leak of a frontier-aligned model. The release originated from a coordinated breach, but its integration is gradual: access is initially restricted to a small network of trusted research institutions, and tooling, documentation, and compute access remain bottlenecks. The gain of 2.5 points matches the frontier's growth rate this turn, preserving the derivative nature of openweight advancement without leapfrogging. Resilience holds at 50.0 — systems degrade but do not fail — while eu_ai_sovereignty falls to 16.0 as capability growth outpaces infrastructure buildout (−1) and no immediate access gains offset dependence.

### Fractures Multiply

Local opposition to data centres intensifies, with grid stability cited but political resistance growing. The open bioscience model gains quiet traction, but too slowly to shift sentiment. The Sovereign Compute Backbone faces early delays: procurement disputes and cross-border coordination hurdles slow deployment, pushing its effective operational date further into 2032. The measure proceeds, but no exceptional momentum is observed.
