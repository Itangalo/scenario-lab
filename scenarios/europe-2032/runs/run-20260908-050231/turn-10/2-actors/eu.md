## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: finishing the last leg of our sovereignty build-out and pivoting decisively toward control. The completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories and Sovereign Inference Clusters marks the end of phase one — we now have physical infrastructure and legal anchoring for AI capacity that no external actor can switch off. But physical presence is not strategic autonomy if the intelligence running on it remains opaque, untested, and imported. We are converting residual equity stakes in Finland and Portugal into long-term state-backed industrial assets under Article 122 TFEU, with grid priority and tax stability guaranteed for 15 years. DG ENERGY and the EIB are restructuring debt to lock in viability, but the real test now is not compute — it’s cognition.

With `ai_capability` at 62.5 and openweight diffusion at 52.0, we are entering the zone where autonomous planning agents manage multi-day workflows without meaningful review. Yet `ai_safety` stands at 25.0 — below the level of verified pre-release testing, let alone post-deployment monitoring. The recent grid failure was not an outlier; it was a symptom of systems whose reasoning paths even their developers cannot fully reconstruct. At the same time, the emergent_regional_oversight_consortium and global audit movement have created unexpected momentum: civil society, researchers, and media are demanding verification frameworks, and some labs — particularly those with EU operations — are signalling conditional openness to inspection.

We are seizing this moment. Under Article 114 TFEU, we will table a regulation to establish the European Authority for AI Evaluation (EVAI), headquartered in The Hague with satellite labs in Toulouse and Warsaw. It will draw staff from ENISA, ECHA, and the Joint Research Centre, but with new powers: subpoena-like access to model internals during training, secure vaults for weight storage, and the right to run adversarial evaluations under controlled conditions. The first mandate will cover all models above 55.0 index used in energy, transport, finance, and health. We will offer mutual recognition to third countries with equivalent regimes — starting negotiations with Japan and Canada this quarter — but default to full inspection for non-cooperative jurisdictions. France and Germany support the principle; resistance will come from industry voices fearing IP exposure and from smaller member states wary of centralisation. Our counteroffer: transparency credits that reduce compliance burdens, and a sunset clause tying EVAI’s powers to demonstrated safety gains by turn 16. If we do not institutionalise understanding now, we will spend the next six turns reacting to cascades we did not see coming.
