### In practice

We are acting on two truths revealed this turn: first, that the frontier has already weaponised cognition; second, that our defences are still staffed by humans on legacy timelines. The **cyber_major_incident** — a ransomware sweep that leveraged AI-generated zero-days and adaptive evasion — disabled emergency call routing in three member states for over 48 hours. Attribution remains unclear, but the tooling bears the hallmarks of agent-generated attack chains. This is not disruption. It is a warning shot.

Under Article 4(2) TEU and the NIS2 Directive, we are launching the Pan-European Critical Systems Resilience Surge. Hosted by ENISA and jointly funded by the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, this will mandate AI-powered red-teaming of all Tier 1 operators of essential services, with results reported to national CSIRTs and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre. We are allocating €3.8bn to deploy autonomous detection grids, automated patching systems, and fallback protocols that ensure degraded — but continuous — service under attack. Member states will be required to conduct biannual AI-driven breach simulations, with results published in aggregate to build public trust.

Resistance is expected from national governments citing sovereignty and from operators citing cost. Our offer: conditional funding. 70% of implementation costs will be covered by the EU, provided national cyber agencies integrate into a shared early-warning fabric and commit to real-time data sharing during incidents. This is not centralisation — it is survival. The **emergent_finnish_biosecurity_initiative** shows what works: bottom-up innovation, scaled through Union coordination. We are applying that model here — not to wait for perfect cognition, but to ensure that even when cognition fails, the lights stay on.
