## New measure
### Emergency Resilience Surge
Launch immediate hardening of critical energy, water and port infrastructure against AI-enabled cyber intrusion, with binding standards and co-financed upgrades.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`

## Priority
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_test_shot proves that detection has failed, exposure is real, and the next intrusion may not be found by accident; this outranks forward-looking build-up this turn, even though both are urgent.

## In practice
We are treating the cyber_test_shot as a wake-up call: the intrusions were not stopped by existing defences, nor detected by automated monitoring, and they targeted systems whose failure could cascade across regions. This cannot wait. Under Article 12 of the Treaty on European Union and the NIS2 Directive’s emergency powers, we are convening an extraordinary COREPER meeting this month to fast-track a Council Implementing Decision mandating baseline AI-resistant configurations for all operators of essential services in energy, water, ports and electricity transmission. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will issue technical specifications within six weeks, drawing from NCSC-UK and NSA playbooks adapted to AI-driven lateral movement and zero-day exploitation at scale.

Funding will come from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused margins and the Digital Europe Programme, repurposed under conditionality: member states must adopt the new standards and conduct live-fire exercises within 18 months to remain eligible for digital modernisation funds. The Commission will offer matching grants covering 70% of upgrade costs for municipalities and regional utilities, paid from a reprofiled portion of the Just Transition Fund. Resistance is expected from smaller member states citing cost and sovereignty, but the recent breaches in Bulgaria and Finland give us leverage — we are framing compliance not as a transfer of control, but as mutualising defence in the face of a threat no single state can fully anticipate or contain.

At the same time, we are holding course on the two category-4 measures inherited from the start. InvestAI Gigafactories is now engaging national governments on site selection, prioritising locations near geothermal or hydroelectric baseload to meet the 80MW+ power demands sustainably. The Tech sovereignty package is advancing draft legislation for "AI Accelerated Zones" with automatic grid access and streamlined environmental assessments — though pushback from German Länder and French regional councils over land use requires quiet negotiation through ECOFIN and the Committee of the Regions. These remain our long-term spine, but this turn belongs to resilience: if we cannot keep the lights on, no amount of compute will save us.
