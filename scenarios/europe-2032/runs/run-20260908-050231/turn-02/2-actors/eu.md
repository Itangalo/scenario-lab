### Open-Source Threat Detection Commons

## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_test_shot revealed a failure not just of tools but of time: defences were too slow to detect and patch, and the new offensive tempo demands structural upgrades now, not just research or compute build-up, even as both remain essential.

## In practice  
We are executing the Emergency Resilience Surge with urgency. The Council Implementing Decision under NIS2 is now in force, mandating AI-resistant configurations for all operators of essential services in energy, water, and ports. ENISA has published its technical specifications, which include mandatory deployment of automated patching systems and behavioural anomaly detection capable of identifying swarm-style intrusions. We are leveraging the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme to co-finance upgrades, with 70% grants from the Just Transition Fund for regional utilities in Bulgaria, Finland, and other exposed states. Live-fire exercises will be conducted in Q2 2027 under ENISA coordination, with cross-border participation to stress-test mutual aid protocols. Resistance from smaller member states is being managed through bilateral assurances on data sovereignty and technical assistance from the EU Cyber Rapid Response Teams.

At the same time, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories initiative, with site selection nearing completion. The Commission has brokered agreements with Iceland and Portugal for geothermal and hydro-powered zones, while negotiations in France and Germany continue under ECOFIN mediation, focusing on fast-tracking environmental assessments without diluting standards. The Tech Sovereignty Package is moving toward trilogue, with the Parliament pushing for stronger clawback clauses if private investors fail to meet build-out timelines. These category-4 measures remain the backbone of our long-term sovereignty play, but they cannot address the immediate fragility exposed by the cyber_test_shot.

This turn, we are launching a new lever: the Open-Source Threat Detection Commons. With the cyber_defence_breakthrough proving that AI-driven detection can outpace AI-driven attack, we are institutionalising that edge. Hosted by ENISA and funded through Horizon Europe with €1.2bn over three years, the Commons will aggregate anonymised intrusion data from national CERTs and critical operators, enabling public researchers to train defensive models under strict data governance. Access will be tiered: open for low-risk datasets, vetted for high-fidelity logs. The goal is not just better tools, but a European public capability in AI security that does not depend on closed labs or foreign platforms. This is not a substitute for sovereign compute, but a force multiplier — and a signal that our sovereignty includes the right to understand and defend our own systems.
