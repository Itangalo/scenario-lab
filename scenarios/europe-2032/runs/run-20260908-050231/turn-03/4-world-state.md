### Resilience Surge Reaches Critical Mass

The Emergency Resilience Surge concluded its final implementation phase, with ENISA certifying compliance across all designated essential operators in energy, water, and ports. Binding AI-resistant configurations are now active, supported by automated patching and swarm-detection tools deployed through national CERTs. Co-financing from the Just Transition Fund accelerated adoption in Bulgaria, Finland, and Slovakia, though some Baltic states delayed full integration, opting for modular compliance. The cross-border alert system demonstrated interoperability in a live drill, marking a tangible upgrade in collective cyber defence.

### A New Coalition, A Visible Win

A coalition of middle powers—including South Korea, Canada, and Poland—formed a coordination framework for AI supply-chain leverage, aligning export controls and pooling evaluation capacity. This bolstered the EU’s bargaining position without ceding sovereignty. Simultaneously, the public celebrated the rollout of AI-assisted healthcare triage in France and digital education tools in Spain, both credited to EU-funded initiatives rather than foreign platforms—marking a rare moment of homegrown success.

### Foundations Laid, Delays Persist

The Open-Source Threat Detection Commons was formally established under ENISA, with €400 million in Horizon funding secured and governance protocols under review. However, no live models are yet trained on real data, and liability disputes with national authorities continue. Meanwhile, InvestAI Gigafactories advanced in Iceland and Portugal, while fast-tracked assessments in France and Germany shaved months off delays. The Tech Sovereignty Package remains in trilogue, with unresolved debate over clawback enforcement.
