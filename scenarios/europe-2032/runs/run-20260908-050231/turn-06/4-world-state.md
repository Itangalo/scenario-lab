### US Posture Hardens, EU Access Squeezed

The new US administration takes office under a clear doctrine: frontier AI is a sovereign asset, and access is now tiered. This shift materialises immediately as Washington pressures the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s servicing of older lithography tools—equipment vital to global chip supply, including in EU-aligned states. The move exposes Europe’s single point of leverage in the AI supply chain being used against it.

### Measures Close, But With Little Gain

Three major initiatives conclude this turn: the Tech Sovereignty Package, the Open-Source Threat Detection Commons, and the Secure Evaluation Mandate. None deliver meaningful outcomes. The sovereignty package yields only patchy permitting reforms, blocked by member states guarding local control. The detection commons remains dormant, still held hostage by Polish and Hungarian objections to data sharing. The evaluation mandate, though legally binding, has not been triggered—no model has yet crossed the 50.0 capability threshold under EU observation.

### Resilience Push Begins Amid Fragmentation

The EU begins drafting a **resilience-by-design mandate**, requiring red-teaming and hardened standards for AI in critical infrastructure. ENISA starts scoping the requirements under emergency powers, supported by the Regional Oversight Consortium. Germany and Finland advocate for strict enforcement, but France demands exemptions for defence systems. A compromise allows time-bound carve-outs under EDA review, delaying full implementation. As the mandate remains in development, only partial progress is made, with resilience seeing a modest increase reflecting early-stage planning and consultation.
