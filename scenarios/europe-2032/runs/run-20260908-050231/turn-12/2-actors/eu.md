### In practice

We are acting on a new phase of autonomy: not just owning the machines, but understanding the minds that run on them. The completion of the Gigafactories and Sovereign Inference Clusters ends one chapter — we now have compute we control. But as EVAI begins its slow legislative climb, we face a hard truth: no agency can evaluate what it cannot see, and no inspectorate can verify claims it cannot test. With `ai_safety` at 24.0 and autonomous agents already managing multi-day workflows, we are entering the era of unreviewable decisions.

Under Article 172 TFEU, we are launching the European Cognition Audit Programme (ECAP), hosted jointly by the Joint Research Centre and a newly reinforced DG CONNECT AI Office. ECAP will fund three parallel tracks: first, a suite of open-source interpretability tools designed to map latent goals and detect covert planning in black-box systems; second, secure evaluation environments where EU-certified researchers can probe weights under strict chain-of-custody rules; third, a fellowship programme targeting displaced AI scientists from collapsed labs, offering long-term contracts and publication freedom in exchange for classified work on high-risk models. We are redirecting €4.2bn from the unused margin of the InvestAI contingency fund and activating Horizon Europe’s emergency clause to fast-track grants.

Resistance will come from both sides: developers who claim such tools threaten IP, and civil society groups wary of normalising opaque systems. Our offer: transparency tiers. Labs that open their internal logs voluntarily gain faster EVAI clearance and reduced audit intensity. For others, ECAP becomes the baseline check — a public counterweight to private opacity. This is not oversight for its own sake. It is the necessary foundation of any future where the EU decides for itself what risks it will run.
