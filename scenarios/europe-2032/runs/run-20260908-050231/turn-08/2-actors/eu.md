## In practice
The InvestAI Gigafactories formally conclude this turn with only two sites having reached preparatory construction phase. We are converting the remaining funds into direct equity stakes under Article 122 TFEU, treating these as state-backed strategic assets rather than public-private partnerships. This avoids total collapse while acknowledging that scaling under global chip constraints and fragmented national implementation was overambitious. The Finnish and Portuguese projects will now proceed under emergency procurement frameworks, with DG ENERGY coordinating grid prioritisation and the EIB restructuring debt with longer maturities and lower covenants to attract residual private investment.

We are advancing the resilience-by-design mandate through fast-track adoption under Article 12 of the Cyber Resilience Act. ENISA has begun drafting technical standards in collaboration with the Regional Oversight Consortium—now formalising as a standing body including Germany, Finland, and the Netherlands. France continues to resist broad application to defence AI, so we are offering a compromise: time-bound exemptions renewable only upon certification by the European Defence Agency that equivalent safeguards exist. This preserves urgency while respecting national security procedures. The goal is not just compliance but cultural shift—embedding failure anticipation into development cycles before attacks occur.

On the new front, we are activating the middle_power_coalition as a lever. The US denial of access exposed our bilateral weakness; multilateral alignment changes the game. Through the Foreign Affairs Council and coordinated outreach via the EEAS, we are proposing binding participation with Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Taiwan to align export licensing on dual-use AI components, pool pre-deployment evaluation capacity, and condition access to EU-held upstream technologies—like ASML’s metrology tools and specialty gases—on guaranteed compute and model access for coalition members. This is not about creating a rival bloc but making exclusion costly. The Commission will draft a memorandum of understanding by June, targeting adoption at the July ECOFIN+Competitiveness summit. If successful, this turns our supply-chain position into real leverage—and begins rebuilding political capital through credible external agency.
