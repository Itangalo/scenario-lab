## In practice
The InvestAI Gigafactories reach their scheduled end this turn, but the outcome is not victory. Two sites—one in Finland, one in Portugal—have secured land and grid connection under emergency procurement, but neither has poured foundation. The European Investment Bank has restructured debt terms to attract co-investment, yet private capital remains hesitant without visible hardware delivery or credible access to chips. We are treating this not as closure but as collapse: the original vision of five gigafactories by 2036 is abandoned. Instead, we are converting the remaining funds into direct equity stakes in the two advanced projects, using Article 122 TFEU to justify state aid for strategic infrastructure. This avoids outright cancellation but acknowledges that scaling under current conditions was overambitious.

With the three expired measures now formally closed—each having failed to deliver—we clear dead weight from the portfolio. The Tech Sovereignty Package could not override national planning regimes; the Detection Commons was strangled by sovereignty concerns over data; the Evaluation Mandate proved irrelevant the moment the US cut access without consultation. These were built for a world where cooperation with the frontier labs was stable. That world is gone. Our new focus is control, not coordination.

We are now advancing the resilience-by-design mandate through fast-track adoption under Article 12 of the Cyber Resilience Act, with ENISA drafting technical standards in collaboration with the Regional Oversight Consortium. Germany and Finland support mandatory red-teaming; France continues to demand broad exemptions for defence AI. We are offering a compromise: time-bound carve-outs renewable only with EDA certification of equivalent safeguards, reviewed every six months. This preserves urgency while respecting national security procedures. The goal is not just compliance but cultural shift—embedding failure anticipation into development cycles before attacks occur.

But the true priority this turn is the sovereign inference clusters. With frontier access denied without warning—hospitals losing diagnostic support, agencies losing translation and document processing—we can no longer treat AI as a utility we rent. The clusters must come online before turn 8, even if initially underpowered. We are redirecting procurement authority to DG CONNECT to fast-track acquisition of available H100s through non-US channels, including secondary markets in Japan and South Korea. Simultaneously, we are instructing CEA and Fraunhofer to begin adapting open-weight models at 49.0 capability to run efficiently on heterogeneous domestic hardware. It will not match the frontier, but it must be sovereign, secure, and always on. This is no longer about competitiveness. It is about continuity of governance when others decide you don’t get to use what they built.
