### Resilience Proves Durable, but Strategic Gaps Widen

The Emergency Resilience Surge concluded with formal certification by ENISA, marking a milestone in EU cyber defence. Automated patching and cross-border alert systems now operate across 24 member states, with live drills confirming rapid response times. Even the Baltics, operating under modular compliance, are integrated into the detection swarm. This hardening absorbed recent intrusion attempts with minimal disruption, validating the investment.

### A Fragile Consensus on Open-Source Defence

The Open-Source Threat Detection Commons remains stalled. Despite €400 million in Horizon funding, liability disputes over data sharing persist. Germany and the Netherlands have committed anonymised intrusion data, but Poland and Hungary refuse, citing sovereignty. Proposed EU-funded, locally staffed anonymisation hubs failed to close the gap. Without live data, the platform cannot train, delaying operationalisation.

### Sovereignty Erodes Amid Accelerating Frontier

Global AI capability advanced to 57.5, driven by self-improvement loops. Open-weight models rose to 46.0, nearing the 52.0 threshold where autonomous agents become widespread. The EU’s new proposal for mandatory pre-release evaluation of high-capability models faced immediate resistance from frontier developers, who argue it overreaches and lacks reciprocity. Market access leverage is tested, but compliance is not assured.

### Political Capital Strained, Sentiment Shifts

Public sentiment dipped as election-related discourse turned critical of AI’s role in information manipulation, amplified by a recent high-profile annulment. The EU’s new measure, while symbolically strong, strained political capital. The portfolio remains heavy, and the priority on the Commons yielded no acceleration. Labour displacement concerns resurfaced, undermining enthusiasm.
