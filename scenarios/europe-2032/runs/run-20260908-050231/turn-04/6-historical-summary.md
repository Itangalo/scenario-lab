In 2027, the Emergency Resilience Surge concluded successfully, with ENISA certifying AI-resistant cybersecurity measures across 24 EU member states, including the Baltics under modular compliance. Automated patching and cross-border alert systems demonstrated resilience against intrusion attempts, solidifying EU cyber defences. However, the Open-Source Threat Detection Commons remained non-operational despite €400 million in funding, hindered by unresolved liability disputes and divergent national stances on data sharing, particularly from Poland and Hungary.

Global AI capability rose to 57.5, with open-weight models reaching 46.0, approaching the threshold for widespread autonomous agents. The EU proposed mandatory pre-release evaluations for high-capability AI models, but faced strong resistance from frontier developers over regulatory overreach and lack of reciprocity, testing the bloc’s market leverage. Meanwhile, InvestAI Gigafactories advanced in Iceland, Portugal, and parts of France and Germany, though the Tech Sovereignty Package remained delayed.

Public sentiment declined amid election-related concerns over AI-driven information manipulation and renewed fears of labour displacement, offsetting earlier gains from visible tech benefits. Political capital weakened as ambitious initiatives strained coordination, and progress on strategic AI sovereignty lagged behind accelerating global advancements.
