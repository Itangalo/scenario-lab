### The Open Frontier Widens

The AI frontier advances again, reaching 55.0, driven by iterative improvements in training efficiency and agent coordination. With no corresponding leap in safety practices, the gap between capability and control deepens. A notable open-weight model is released, narrowing the gap slightly—`openweight_capability` rises to 43.0—but it remains constrained by hardware availability and deployment bottlenecks. While its offensive cyber capabilities are studied with concern, they do not yet reach widespread exploitability. European security agencies monitor developments closely but report no operational incidents this turn.

### Cyber Shield Stalls, Plans for Access Begin

The EU presses forward with the Cyber Shield Directive, retaining it as its priority. Technical benchmarks are finalized, and ENISA begins assembling a rapid-response unit. However, resistance in ECOFIN delays full funding approval, and rollout in Central and Eastern Europe remains uneven. The directive yields a marginal +1 to `resilience`, now at 42.0, but no operational capability emerges this turn.

Behind the scenes, the Commission begins drafting the **Secure Access to Frontier Models** initiative, a proposed legal framework under Article 54a of the AI Act that would enable air-gapped evaluations by vetted institutions like Fraunhofer and INRIA. However, no formal launch occurs this turn. Internal debates continue over data sovereignty guarantees and enforceability, and the United States has not yet been consulted. The proposal remains in preparatory phase, with no external commitments made or legal challenges filed.

### Sovereignty Holds, Capital Adjusts

`eu_ai_sovereignty` remains unchanged at 21.0. The ongoing InvestAI Gigafactories project continues to build domestic compute capacity (+1), and the Tech sovereignty package progresses incrementally (+1), but these gains are offset by the rising capability frontier (−1), leaving net sovereignty flat. No new sovereignty events occur.

Political capital erodes moderately, falling to 35.0. The existing portfolio—now four active measures—imposes a sustained burden (charge −12). With no major incident recently mitigated and no new measure completed, legitimacy lending remains absent. The decision to retain Cyber Shield as priority without delivering operational results contributes to fatigue, but the adjustment reflects gradual strain rather than sudden collapse.
