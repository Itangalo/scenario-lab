### The U.S. Retreats, the EU Pauses — Momentum Without Execution

The 2028 U.S. election delivers a decisive shift: an administration defined by AI retrenchment takes power, ending years of aggressive frontier investment. Washington imposes data centre moratoriums, restricts AI in public institutions, and redirects funding toward job guarantees and sectoral transfers. American AI progress slows visibly, not from hardware shortages but from policy drift and internal conflict. For the EU, the immediate pressure eases — but so does the reliability of its most capable partner.

In response, the European Commission initiates high-level discussions on a potential **Sovereign AI Pilot Programme**, aimed at exploring end-to-end AI autonomy in strategic domains such as cyber defence. Preliminary talks include site assessments in Finland and France, but no formal proposal has been adopted, no budget allocated, and no legal basis established. The idea remains at the stage of strategic contemplation, with member states divided on scope, cost, and sovereignty guarantees. Germany and Poland express cautious interest, while France insists on binding commitments regarding data control and industrial participation before any planning advances.

The **Secure Access to Frontier Models** framework, proposed two turns ago, continues toward completion but is delayed by legal challenges and member state disputes over access criteria. It will not finish this turn. Its eventual impact remains uncertain, and no legitimacy gain is realized.
