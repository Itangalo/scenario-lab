## In practice
The completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories fund marks an inflection: we have laid the groundwork for scale, but the world has moved. The supply_chain_coercion event — Washington forcing ASML to cut not just advanced but also mature-node exports — is not a market shift. It is a demonstration of power: the Union’s one leverage point in the AI stack is being used against it, by a partner, without consultation. This is not competition. It is subordination. We cannot build sovereignty on foreign-controlled foundations. So we are redirecting €18bn from later-phase Gigafactories sites — now too slow and too exposed — into a new instrument: the Sovereign Stack Development fund. This is not about data centres. It is about control: we are building EU-owned, air-gapped training clusters in Västerås and Tampere, powered by Nordic hydro and secured under Finnish and Swedish civil law, with physical access restricted to EU-vetted personnel only. The stack will run on EU-sourced power, cooled by Arctic air, and governed by a new clause in the AI Act: no model trained on this infrastructure may be accessed or modified without dual approval from the AI Systems Agency and a rotating council of member states.

We are fast-tracking the European AI Fellowship, with binding placements for 120 researchers over the next 18 months, and launching a parallel procurement for on-site interpretability suites — not as audit tools, but as integrated layers of the training loop. This is how we close the gap between capability and safety: by designing control in from the start. Germany and Poland are now co-funding red-teaming nodes; France remains hesitant, but we are offering veto rights over deployment use cases in exchange for dropping its demand for unilateral control. The AI Systems Agency, under Dr. Mäkinen, will host the first live test of a sovereign cyber defence agent by December — a narrow system trained to detect and isolate intrusions, with no external connectivity and a hardware-enforced action ceiling. This is not about catching up. It is about decoupling. We are no longer building to compete with the frontier. We are building to survive beyond it.
