### The Frontier Surges, Visibility Faltens

The AI frontier advances to 56.5, driven by compounding gains in agent coordination and training efficiency. A notable open-weight model release—showing improved performance on red-teaming and automation benchmarks—begins to circulate within research and defensive cybersecurity communities. While it demonstrates capabilities approaching those of earlier frontier systems, it does not match current top-tier performance. This lifts `openweight_capability` to 44.5, reflecting incremental diffusion of recent advances, consistent with ongoing trends and the gradual spillover from frontier development.

Security agencies note early signs of adaptation for vulnerability discovery, but deployment remains limited. Developers attribute anomalous system behaviours to edge cases in deployment environments, not fundamental control failures. Nevertheless, the lack of enforceable safety standards in widely used open models fuels concern. With no safety measures yet operationalised at scale, `ai_safety` declines to 22.0, reflecting eroding institutional confidence.

### Cyber Shield Completed, But Gaps Remain

The Cyber Shield Directive formally enters implementation, marking a political milestone. ENISA activates a provisional rapid-response unit, deploying analysts to ten high-risk states. A €1.2bn funding envelope unlocks partial operational capacity, and red-teaming protocols are issued. This delivers a resilience boost to 45.0, though uneven readiness across member states limits the gain.

### Diplomatic Push, Domestic Pushback

The EU launches the European AI Evaluation Consortium (EUR-AIEC), a bold bid to institutionalise pre-deployment scrutiny with allies. Backed by recent middle-power coordination, Japan and Canada enter exploratory talks. However, the U.S. reacts coolly, viewing the air-gapped access framework as de facto extraterritorial regulation. Formal negotiations stall.

Meanwhile, public sentiment sours to 39.0. A wave of local campaigns opposes AI in hiring and education, framing it as opaque and unjust. The perception of uncontrolled model proliferation, combined with visible job displacement in tech support roles, fuels backlash.

Sovereignty holds at 21.0: domestic compute gains (+1) offset by frontier growth (−1). Political capital sees a modest rebound to 37.0, buoyed by the completed directive (+4) and the EUR-AIEC proposal (+3), despite ongoing portfolio costs (−12) and the priority penalty (−1).
