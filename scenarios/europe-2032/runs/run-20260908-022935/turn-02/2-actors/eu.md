## New measure
**Secure Access to Frontier Models for Public Evaluators**  
Establish a legal and technical framework enabling vetted public researchers and regulators to access powerful AI models under strict conditions, to conduct safety evaluations, interpretability research, and red-teaming.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`

## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure (Directive)** — because the cyber_test_shot revealed a blind spot in defences that, if left unaddressed, would collapse public and political confidence in both digital infrastructure and the broader AI adoption agenda, making any industrial strategy untenable.

## In practice
We are advancing negotiations on the Cyber Shield Directive in COREPER II under Article 114 TFEU, with the Commission’s Joint Research Centre finalising technical benchmarks for AI-driven detection efficacy and red-team frequency. ENISA’s expanded mandate and €1.2bn funding envelope — drawn from Digital Europe and Recovery and Resilience top-ups — are being used to stand up a rapid-response support unit for member states, particularly those in Central and Eastern Europe where implementation capacity lags. To counter resistance from finance ministries, we are offering 70% co-financing through cohesion funds, tied to adoption by Q2 2027, and pairing compliance with fast-track access to InvestEU guarantees for qualifying upgrades. The Council Presidency has agreed to fast-track discussion in the Competitiveness (Internal Market) formation, though ECOFIN remains a battleground.

Simultaneously, we are launching the framework for secure public access to frontier models. This is not open release, nor is it unfettered inspection: we are drafting a regulation under the AI Act’s Article 54a to create a vetted researcher access regime, governed by a new unit within ENISA and co-staffed by national cybersecurity agencies. Access will be granted only to EU-approved institutions conducting safety-critical research, under air-gapped conditions where necessary, with model weights stored in sovereign EU facilities. The first cohort of evaluators — drawn from Fraunhofer, INRIA, and a cross-border consortium led by the Dutch National Cyber Security Centre — will begin pilot assessments in Q3. This builds sovereign capacity to understand what we regulate, closing a critical gap: we cannot secure what we cannot see, and we cannot claim control over systems whose internals remain black boxes to public institutions.
