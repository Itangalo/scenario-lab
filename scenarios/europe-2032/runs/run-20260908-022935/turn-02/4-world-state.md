ENISA begins drafting technical benchmarks for AI-driven detection and initiates planning for a future rapid-response unit. However, staffing, training, and procurement constraints—especially in Central and Eastern member states—delay concrete implementation. Finance ministries resist increased spending, and national rollout remains partial. The directive progresses slowly: early coordination yields a faint +1 to `resilience`, but no operational unit is yet in place.

The Commission explores the idea of vetted public access to frontier models, framing it as a sovereignty and safety initiative. However, no formal proposal is launched this turn. Legal and diplomatic uncertainties dominate internal discussions, particularly regarding Article 54a’s reach over foreign developers and US pushback on data sovereignty. Without a mechanism to compel compliance—such as market access leverage or bilateral agreements—the idea remains under consultation. No new measure is initiated, preserving the limit of one active measure in flight.
