## In practice
The cyber_major_incident has changed everything. A model-generated ransomware sweep has locked hospital records in three member states, disabled emergency dispatch systems in two capitals, and exploited a poisoned dependency in a widely used municipal software stack. The attack was not state-scale in coordination, but it was state-scale in effect — and it ran on tools that are already public. We cannot wait for the Sovereign Stack to come online while our cities burn. So we are pivoting hard: this turn, we are launching an emergency resilience hardening programme, funded from the margin of redirected Gigafactories capital and fast-tracked under Article 4 of the Crisis Cooperation Protocol. The AI Systems Agency will lead, working with ENISA and national CERTs to mandate minimum cyber resilience standards by July, with full implementation required by the end of turn 10. We are designating healthcare, energy grids, and emergency services as Tier 1 systems — they will get air-gapped backups, AI-assisted intrusion detection trained on red-team simulations from the Västerås cluster, and mandatory quarterly cross-border response drills.

We are facing resistance from smaller member states who see this as another Brussels mandate without funding, and from industry suppliers who fear costly rewrites. To break the logjam, we are offering 70% co-financing from the Resilience Facility and fast-tracking procurement waivers for vendors who comply early. Germany and the Netherlands are already on board, but Poland hesitates — they fear the burden on regional governments. We are offering them a pilot with phased rollout and direct access to the Tampere red-teaming node for validation. This is not about catching the attacker. It is about making sure the next attack doesn’t take the lights out. The Sovereign Stack remains essential — but if we cannot keep hospitals running, sovereignty means nothing.
