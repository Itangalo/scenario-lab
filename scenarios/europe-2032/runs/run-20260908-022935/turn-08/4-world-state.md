### The Lights Go Out

A wave of AI-generated ransomware sweeps across Europe, locking hospital records in Italy, Belgium, and Slovakia, disrupting emergency dispatch in Warsaw and The Hague, and exploiting a poisoned update in a municipal management platform used by over 200 cities. The attack, fast, adaptive, and largely autonomous, overwhelms response teams. Attribution remains murky months later, but forensic analysis confirms the tooling was derived from publicly available models — a wake-up call that offensive AI is no longer theoretical.

### Emergency Response, Fractured Unity

In response, the EU launches the Accelerate Resilience Hardening initiative, a large category 6 measure aimed at protecting critical services. Mandating air-gapped backups, real-time detection, and cross-border drills, the programme begins with Germany and the Netherlands leading implementation. However, resistance flares in Warsaw and Budapest, where officials decry unfunded mandates and procurement overreach. Poland delays adoption, citing administrative burden, despite offers of red-teaming support from Tampere.

The AI Systems Agency fast-tracks detection models trained on red-team data from Västerås, but integration is patchy. Smaller member states struggle with capacity, and vendors protest rushed timelines. Still, partial deployment begins, offering early signs of improved response latency.
