The AI frontier advances by +1, widening the gap between emerging capabilities and existing defences. Open-weight models, including derivatives of Kimi K3, proliferate in underground ecosystems, enabling offensive cyber tools. A covert, cross-continental intrusion into critical infrastructure—undetectable by standard defences—exposes systemic vulnerabilities, particularly within EU energy grids. In response, the EU launches the Cyber Shield Directive, mandating AI-driven detection, red-teaming, and cross-border reporting for key sectors. ENISA receives €1.2bn in funding, with 70% co-financing offered to member states, though resistance emerges from finance ministries and Central/Eastern European states over costs and implementation. The large-scale measure, fast-tracked but not fully supported, strains resources and reduces capital. No proposal credit is earned as the measure follows near-miss detection rather than a realized attack. Sovereignty declines slightly as global AI outpaces domestic resilience, and safety drops due to unchecked capability growth. Resilience sees minimal gain from early Cyber Shield momentum. Public sentiment remains anxious but stable.
