## New measure  
**EU Talent Shield Initiative**  
Establish binding retention and recruitment contracts for frontier AI researchers in EU institutions, with fast-track residency, dedicated compute access, and salary parity with US counterparts.

## Priority  
**InvestAI Gigafactories** — This remains the foundational lever of sovereignty: without domestic compute at scale, no talent stays, no models run, and no independent evaluation or deployment is possible. The talent initiative is urgent, but compute is irreplaceable, and its lead times are longest.

## In practice  
We are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories programme through the newly empowered Competitiveness Council, using Article 118 TFEU as legal basis to fast-track cross-border infrastructure coordination. The Commission has designated four priority sites — in Finland, Spain, Poland, and France — each with pre-approved grid interconnection studies and emergency permitting authority under the Tech Sovereignty Package. We are negotiating with national governments to waive local zoning constraints in exchange for regional innovation spillover guarantees, including minimum staffing thresholds for domestic hires and open-access compute time for academic safety research. Resistance from environmental coalitions in two host regions is being addressed through binding environmental monitoring pacts and community reinvestment funds drawn from the €200bn envelope.

Simultaneously, the EU Talent Shield Initiative is being scoped with the European Research Council and EACEA to identify 500 critical positions across agentic systems, model alignment, and red-teaming. We are preparing a Council Regulation under the EU Blue Card framework to offer automatic residency, tax equalisation, and access to sovereign compute clusters for researchers who commit to five-year contracts with approved institutions. The key obstacle is fiscal: national governments fear brain drain from their own universities and are reluctant to cede control over research funding. We are offering a 70/30 co-financing model — EU covers salaries and compute, member states retain governance — and will tie participation to cohesion fund allocations in the next MFF review. This is not just about hiring; it is about anchoring capability where we can govern it.
