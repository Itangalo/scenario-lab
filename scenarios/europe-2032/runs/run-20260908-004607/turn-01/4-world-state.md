### Fractures Beneath the Build

The EU’s dual push for sovereign compute and talent retention advanced in principle but strained in practice. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme secured four priority sites, and fast-track permitting frameworks were adopted under the Tech Sovereignty Package. However, local resistance in northern Finland and southern Spain delayed environmental approvals, and the promise of open-access compute for safety research drew skepticism from scientists who questioned whether the EU could deliver on governance, not just infrastructure.

The EU Talent Shield Initiative met stiffer headwinds. While the Commission proposed binding contracts and fast-track residency, member states balked at ceding control over research appointments. Germany and the Netherlands raised concerns about sovereignty over national universities, and Italy demanded additional cohesion funds before signing on. The 70/30 co-financing model remained unadopted, and without a unified front, recruitment of frontier researchers continued to lag. The exodus to US and UK labs persisted, with two leading agentic systems teams departing from ETH Zurich and Sorbonne.

Meanwhile, the cyber_test_shot intrusion sent shockwaves through critical infrastructure circles. Though no major disruption occurred, the depth of access and lack of detection exposed systemic vulnerabilities. Resilience efforts, still in planning, offered no buffer. In response, the EU accelerated internal discussions on a cyber resilience measure, but no formal proposal emerged this turn.

On the global stage, the emergent_lab_security_reform was welcomed, but its extraterritorial reach sparked debate. The EU sought to leverage it into a mutual recognition framework, but US labs resisted third-party audits under EU jurisdiction. Public sentiment edged up slightly, buoyed by adoption_success in public services — a rare win — but the broader narrative remained one of dependency and drift.

Progress on open-weight models remained incremental, tied to the slow absorption of new compute capacity and limited access to frontier-scale training runs. While governance frameworks advanced, the technical capability to deploy sovereign open-weight models at scale has not yet caught up, keeping gains in `openweight_capability` modest. The political capital reservoir remained intact despite setbacks, as no major legislative loss or public reversal occurred — the current challenges are seen as implementation friction, not collapse.
