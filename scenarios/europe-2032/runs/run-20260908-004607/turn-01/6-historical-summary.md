The EU advanced its sovereign compute agenda by selecting four InvestAI Gigafactory sites and adopting fast-track permitting, though environmental delays in Finland and Spain and skepticism over governance weakened momentum. The Talent Shield Initiative stalled as member states resisted ceding control over research appointments, with Germany, the Netherlands, and Italy raising sovereignty and funding concerns, leaving the 70/30 co-financing model unadopted and failing to halt the brain drain to US and UK labs. A major cyber intrusion, cyber_test_shot, exposed critical infrastructure vulnerabilities, prompting accelerated internal talks on resilience but no formal action. Globally, the EU's lab security reform gained traction but faced US resistance over jurisdiction, limiting mutual recognition prospects. Public sentiment improved slightly due to AI adoption in public services, while progress on open-weight models remained slow, constrained by limited compute access. Despite setbacks, political capital held as challenges were framed as implementation issues rather than systemic failure.
