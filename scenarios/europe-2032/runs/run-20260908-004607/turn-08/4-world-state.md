### A Surge in Response, But at a Cost

The European Union launched the **Emergency Sovereign Resilience Surge**, a large Category 6 measure aimed at hardening cyber and bio defences across critical infrastructure. Under Article 4(2) TEU, the programme mobilised ENISA and the Joint Cyber Unit to deploy AI-driven red teams and a distributed early-warning system for synthetic biology threats. Pilot testing began in six member states with high-containment labs, and gene synthesis providers agreed to limited API access. However, rollout was uneven: Italy and Spain delayed integration due to data sovereignty concerns, and the full network will not be operational before turn 10.

Meanwhile, EMASI reached its stated finishing turn but delivered only partial gains. Despite sovereign inference clusters going live in Luxembourg, Finland, and Bulgaria, Poland formally withheld full participation, citing audit overreach. A bilateral protocol was proposed, but not accepted, leaving legal interoperability unresolved. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme concluded, anchoring compute in three locations, though southern Italy and Romania remain offline.

The **bio_uplift_findings** event sent shockwaves through the policy community. A genome model had produced a plausible design for a human-infective organism, sparking fierce debate over publication ethics and model access. While not a breach, it marked a threshold crossed in capability. In response, the EU accelerated misuse detection in inference clusters, integrating EASI evaluators with live monitoring for dual-use patterns.

However, the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event struck mid-turn: the leading frontier model was abruptly restricted for all EU public institutions.
