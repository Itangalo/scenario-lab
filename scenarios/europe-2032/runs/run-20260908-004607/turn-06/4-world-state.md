### A Crisis and a Pivot

The EU faced a perfect storm: a major cyber incident driven by AI-generated malware disrupted hospitals and energy operators, while frontier access was abruptly cut for key institutions. Public services faltered, and EASI, the new safety agency, stood idle—denied jurisdiction over the very models it was meant to evaluate.

In response, the Union activated emergency powers under NIS2, deploying defensive AI agents to 19 member states through ENISA and national cyber agencies. These agents, trained on synthetic threats at CERN and EMBL, provided immediate detection upgrades. Though partial, this effort absorbed some of the incident’s impact, marking a rare operational success amid broader strain.

Simultaneously, EMASI made progress toward political agreement. France dropped liability objections after securing indemnity coverage; Germany accepted governance influence in exchange for veto rights on shutdowns; Poland and Hungary withdrew legal challenges upon receiving regional hub status. However, full operationalization remains pending—sovereign inference clusters in Luxembourg, Finland, and Bulgaria are still in the provisioning phase, with irrevocable contracts now signed with non-US labs from Israel, Japan, and Singapore. The initiative is advancing, but not yet delivering at scale.

A coordination effort has begun with South Korea, Taiwan, and the Netherlands to align export controls on chipmaking tools, aiming to leverage model mirroring as a form of collective leverage. This dialogue is at an early stage and has not yet resulted in binding commitments.

Yet gains were narrow. The US administration officially adopted a **CONSOLIDATION** posture, rationing frontier access by strategic tier. This deepens Europe’s dependency and raises the cost of sovereignty-building. Open-weight models advanced, including one capable of generating pathogenic protein designs, exposing dual-use risks just as verification capacity lags.

Public sentiment dipped further, as job losses and the crisis fueled anxiety. Resilience improved modestly due to emergency shielding, but systemic fragility remains.
