The EU launched the EU Model Access and Sovereign Inference Initiative (EMASI) to secure sovereign access to frontier AI models after a sudden cutoff disrupted critical services, exposing Europe’s infrastructure dependency. Despite establishing physically hosted inference clusters in Luxembourg, Finland, and Bulgaria, EMASI faced resistance: Germany demanded veto control, France withheld co-leadership, Poland and Hungary challenged it legally, and US developers rejected mirroring mandates. EASI became operational and issued its first audits, but lacked enforcement power without model access. AI capability rose to 57.5 due to advances in automated verification, while safety lagged, widening the governance gap. Public sentiment soured amid disrupted healthcare and services, fueling backlash against AI and data centers. Political capital declined under the strain of multiple large initiatives, partially offset by EMASI's proposal bonus. Resilience remained stable through ongoing cyber directives, but sovereignty eroded further as regulatory ambitions outpaced implementation, revealing deep fractures in EU unity and capacity.
