## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): We will build sovereign capacity to act independently before the window closes, and we now treat access to operational frontier models as a core component of that sovereignty — without it, no evaluation, no defence, no independent policy is possible.

- Trigger: the development this turn we are reacting to: `eu_frontier_access_denied`  
- Grounds: The abrupt denial of access to the leading model has exposed a fatal fragility: our regulatory ambitions, including EASI, presuppose visibility into frontier systems that we no longer possess. Without direct, legally anchored access to capable models — not just audits, but working access under EU jurisdiction — our safety regime is inspecting shadows. This changes the nature of sovereignty from a long-term industrial project to an immediate operational necessity. The commitment stands, but its meaning has sharpened: sovereignty now includes the right and ability to run the most capable systems on infrastructure we control.

## New measure  
**EU Model Access and Sovereign Inference Initiative (EMASI)**  
Secure permanent, high-throughput access to frontier AI models for EU public institutions by establishing sovereign inference infrastructure — physically located in the EU, under irrevocable legal title, and governed by EU law.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `eu_ai_sovereignty` by +15–20 points; ensure uninterrupted access to models above capability level 55 for essential public services and EASI evaluators; reduce dependence on foreign providers and create leverage through reciprocity demands.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, EU institutions, frontier AI developers seeking market access, and cloud providers operating in the EU.

## Priority  
**EU Model Access and Sovereign Inference Initiative (EMASI)** — Because `eu_frontier_access_denied` has proven that market access and goodwill are not guarantees, and because EASI cannot enforce what it cannot inspect, we must now treat operational access to frontier models as non-negotiable infrastructure — as essential as energy or bandwidth — and build the legal and physical means to ensure it.

## In practice  
We are launching EMASI in direct response to the access denial incident, which disrupted hospitals, research institutes, and internal Commission operations. This is not merely a backup — it is a declaration that the EU will not outsource its cognitive sovereignty. We are repurposing €18bn from the unused portion of the InvestAI Gigafactories fund — redirected from greenfield construction to sovereign infrastructure deployment — to build three inference clusters in Luxembourg, Finland, and Bulgaria, each governed by a joint EU-member state entity with irrevocable access rights. These will host licensed copies of frontier models under EU legal control, with compute supplied by EU-anchored hyperscalers bound by service continuity clauses.

We are coupling this with a new market access rule: any frontier model above capability level 55 serving EU users must provide a mirrored instance on EU-controlled infrastructure, auditable and operable independently. This is not a data localisation demand — it is a continuity-of-service requirement, framed as critical infrastructure protection under Article 114 TFEU. We are offering reciprocity: EU-trained models will do the same in partner jurisdictions. But for now, access is a condition of operation. We are negotiating with France and Germany to co-lead the governance board, offering Poland and Hungary guaranteed access nodes in exchange for dropping legal challenges to EASI. Without this, every other measure we have built — safety, resilience, talent — rests on borrowed ground. We are no longer borrowing.
