## Priority
**EU Model Access and Sovereign Inference Initiative (EMASI)** — Because without enforceable access to operational frontier models, every other safety, resilience, and sovereignty measure we have built collapses into symbolic governance, and because the convergence of `election_consolidation` and `supply_chain_coercion` proves that dependence is now a lever held by others, not a temporary condition we can manage through goodwill.

## In practice
We are executing EMASI as a fait accompli in infrastructure and a negotiation in governance. The three inference clusters in Luxembourg, Finland, and Bulgaria are under construction using repurposed funds from InvestAI Gigafactories — €18bn redirected from greenfield sites to sovereign deployment. These will host licensed copies of frontier models on EU-controlled hardware, with compute supplied by EU-anchored hyperscalers bound by continuity-of-service clauses under Article 114 TFEU. Legal title rests with a newly established EU Public Trust for Cognitive Infrastructure, answerable to the Commission and Parliament.

We are pressing France and Germany to co-lead the governance board, offering France liability shielding through expanded EASI indemnity powers and offering Germany veto rights over shutdown triggers in exchange for dropping opposition. For Poland and Hungary, we are formalising their access nodes as regional hubs with local administrative control, conditioned on withdrawal of legal challenges to EASI’s authority. With US developers, we are presenting a choice: mirror your models on our infrastructure with auditable access, or lose market access above capability level 55. Reciprocity is offered in principle, but no partner currently meets the threshold.

Meanwhile, EASI begins its work despite crippled reach — issuing audit requests it cannot enforce, exposing the hollowness of regulation without access. The Tech Sovereignty Package advances permitting zones, but private investment stalls amid uncertainty. And the Cyber Resilience Directive rolls out unevenly, with only 12 of 27 member states meeting mid-term benchmarks. Without progress on EMASI, all these efforts risk becoming exercises in self-delusion — building defences against threats we cannot see, on foundations we do not control.
