### The Access Trap

The EU pressed forward with EMASI as both infrastructure project and geopolitical gambit. Construction advanced in Luxembourg, Finland, and Bulgaria, but the real battle was political — and it was being lost. France remained cautious on liability, Germany’s demand for veto power over model shutdowns stalled governance talks, and Poland and Hungary refused to withdraw legal challenges, arguing the EU was overstepping its mandate. Without full member-state buy-in, EMASI’s authority remained contested.

Meanwhile, US developers rebuffed mirroring mandates. With the election of a consolidation-aligned administration, frontier access was now explicitly rationed by strategic tier. American labs cited national security to deny replication, rendering EMASI’s infrastructure a shell without content. Market access leverage proved hollow: the threat to exclude models above capability 55 carried weight in theory, but enforcement required unity the EU did not have.

### Capability Leaps, Safety Crumbles

A new open-weight model, inspired by recent frontier breakthroughs and accelerated by automated training techniques, demonstrated unexpectedly strong performance in specialized domains. Early benchmarks suggest significant gains, particularly in law, engineering, and clinical coding, contributing to a rise in `openweight_capability`. However, full deployment remains limited by distribution bottlenecks and verification delays. The model’s release marks a clear step forward, but not an immediate leap to parity.

`ai_capability` reached 59.0, driven by compounding advances across the closed frontier, including optimized training runs and improved reasoning architectures. Yet safety efforts failed to keep pace. EASI issued audits, but without access to frontier systems, their scope was constrained. The gap between capability and safety widened, and with it, the risk of misuse.

Resilience dipped as cyber hardening efforts stalled in nine member states. The Cyber Resilience Directive’s uneven rollout left critical systems exposed. Public sentiment fell further, as job displacement in legal and medical support roles made headlines. The promise of AI as public good was fading.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Falters

`eu_ai_sovereignty` dropped to 17.0, hit by rising capability without offsetting gains and the `supply_chain_coercion` event, which severed access to critical compute infrastructure. Political capital eroded to 24.0 under the weight of five large measures and a failed priority pull. The EU’s agenda, ambitious but fractured, was outpacing its capacity to deliver.
