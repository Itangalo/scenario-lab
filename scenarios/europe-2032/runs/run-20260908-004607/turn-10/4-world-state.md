### Institutionalising Crisis Response

The EU moved swiftly to capitalise on the recent bio-incident and the rare momentum of a joint international threat response. The **Biological Threat Coordination Framework** was formally proposed as a large category 6 measure, aiming to convert emergency preparedness into permanent, binding cooperation. The initiative gained traction in Germany, Scandinavia, and among EU health agencies, and the Commission secured provisional support from Japan, Canada, and South Korea for cross-border data and sample sharing. However, Italy and Spain resisted full integration, citing national sovereignty and data governance concerns, while Poland dismissed the Eastern Node offer, calling it “a surveillance concession in disguise.”

Despite these frictions, the **Emergency Sovereign Resilience Surge** concluded successfully, delivering red-team insights, live monitoring integrations, and tested alert protocols. These elements now feed directly into the new framework, providing a technical foundation even as political alignment remains incomplete. The joint threat response mechanism, involving real-time biosurveillance coordination, added resilience value beyond what the EU could achieve alone.

### Stalled Sovereignty, Shrinking Capital

AI capability advanced slightly to 62.5, reflecting incremental progress in model efficiency and training stability, though no breakthroughs occurred. Open-weight models caught up modestly, reaching 54.0, as older frontier techniques leaked into the public domain. However, with no improvement in safeguards and no safety-focused measures landing, **ai_safety** remained stagnant at 10.0.

The **EU Model Access and Sovereign Inference Initiative (EMASI)** officially finished, delivering sovereign inference clusters in three member states. Yet without guaranteed model weights and with Poland abstaining, the sovereignty gain was partial. Combined with the ongoing US posture of **CONSOLIDATION**, which restricts frontier access, and no new compute build landing, **eu_ai_sovereignty** fell by 1 due to capability growth, settling at 16.0.

Political capital declined to 21.0. The portfolio charge was −7 (two large measures at −3 each, plus −1 for the priority). The new **Biological Threat Coordination Framework** earned a +2 proposal bonus for addressing the recent bio-incident and leveraging the joint response. No sentiment-driven boost applied, as public sentiment (32.0) did not exceed capital.

Public sentiment held steady, sustained by visible crisis management but weighed down by job displacement fears and media coverage of researcher recruitment into secretive defence projects.
