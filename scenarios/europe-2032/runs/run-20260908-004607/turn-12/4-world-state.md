### Compute Crisis, Capital Collapse

The European Union launched the Emergency Compute Sovereignty Initiative, a high-stakes bid to anchor 20,000 next-generation AI accelerators on EU soil within 18 months. Invoking Article 122 TFEU, the Commission committed €8.2 billion from repurposed recovery funds, targeting AMD, NVIDIA, and SiPearl for delivery by late 2023. Data centres in Finland, Sweden, and Luxembourg were designated as sovereign enclaves, immune to remote deactivation. Yet the move triggered immediate backlash.

Germany and the Netherlands welcomed the ambition, but Poland and Spain denounced it as economic coercion. Bavarian officials suspended construction of a key site amid public protests over energy allocation, demanding national veto power. Microsoft and Google offered conditional support, insisting on liability shields for AI misuse — a demand the Commission refused. Without agreement, procurement stalled, and only preliminary contracts were signed.

The Biological Threat Coordination Framework advanced technically, with six nations integrating detection systems, but Italy and Spain still withheld full data access. France, meanwhile, delayed participation in the Talent Compact, citing unresolved audit rights for dual-use research. The civil-military rift deepened, with defence officials accusing Brussels of “civilian overreach.”

AI capability edged up to 63.5, driven by efficiency gains, while open-weight models reached 55.5 as older systems leaked. No safety improvements landed. With no category 6 measure completed and capability rising, resilience held steady only due to prior investments.

Political capital plummeted. The new large measure cost −3, the existing two large measures −3 each, and the priority −1 — a total portfolio charge of −10. With capital already at 14.0, the EU could not absorb the strain. The proposal bonus for the Emergency Initiative was minimal — +2, given its scale and urgency — but this barely offset the drain. Public sentiment dipped to 29.0 as energy trade-offs and intergovernmental conflict dominated headlines.

Sovereignty fell to 14.0: no capacity was secured, and ai_capability’s rise cost −1. The Union’s leverage narrowed — not from lack of will, but from the gap between ambition and cohesion.
