## In practice
We are launching the Emergency Compute Sovereignty Initiative under Article 122 TFEU, invoking the economic and social cohesion clause to justify emergency grid access and accelerated permitting. The instrument commits €8.2 billion from the Repurposed Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme to pre-purchase 20,000 accelerators from AMD, NVIDIA, and EU-based SiPearl, with delivery staggered by Q4 2023. These chips will be physically installed in sovereign enclaves — dual-use data centres in Finland, Sweden, and Luxembourg — where EU law governs access, and remote deactivation clauses are voided by contract and national legislation. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking will operate the clusters, with priority access granted to EASI, Horizon Europe safety projects, and Talent Compact fellows.

To overcome energy constraints, we are negotiating with national regulators to reclassify these sites as critical infrastructure, ensuring power continuity even during grid stress. This will require trade-offs: one planned data centre in Bavaria faces local opposition due to projected load increases, and we are offering regional clean-energy investments in exchange for approval. Microsoft and Google have expressed conditional support, contingent on liability shields for AI misuse — which we are refusing. Instead, we offer guaranteed procurement volume for future generations if they accept audit rights and EU jurisdiction over incident response. The message is clear: you may bring compute to Europe, but if you want to stay, it must answer to Europe. This is not protectionism — it is the material basis of agency. Without it, every safety framework, every talent policy, every resilience plan is a performance for an audience that knows the real decisions happen elsewhere.
