## Priority  
**European AI Safety Institute (EASI)** — Because the emergent_lab_fracture exposed that voluntary safeguards are collapsing under capability pressure, and because verification_widens proves that frontier AI is now automating domains once thought safely human-supervised, we must act on the root failure: no authority exists that can compel transparency or enforce safety standards where it matters — inside the labs.

## In practice  
We are launching the European AI Safety Institute as a direct institutional response to the emergent_lab_fracture and the widening verification_widens crisis. Hosted under the Commission but operationally autonomous, EASI will be based in The Hague and governed by a tripartite board of technical experts, national security representatives, and civil society observers. Its first mandate is to establish binding pre-release evaluation protocols for all models above capability level 50, requiring labs to submit system cards, training data summaries, and agent-behaviour logs under penalty of market exclusion. We are redirecting €4.2bn from Horizon Europe and the Innovation Fund to capitalise EASI’s first phase, with staffing to begin immediately in Brussels and rotate into The Hague by turn 4.

Simultaneously, we are using EASI as a political vehicle to break the deadlock on sovereignty. To Germany and the Netherlands, we offer EASI as the alternative to ceding national research control: joint leadership, hosting rights, and first access to audit findings in exchange for full participation in the Talent Shield and Gigafactory programmes. To Poland and Hungary, we frame EASI as a counterweight to US-dominated labs — a European standard, not a federal imposition. We are also leveraging the emergent_lab_security_reform momentum to demand that any lab seeking to operate in the EU market submit to EASI’s authority, turning market access into leverage. This is not just technical capacity — it is the first real EU-wide enforcement node in the safety governance chain, and it will be judged not by its founding but by whether it can stop the next fracture before it happens.
