### A Resilience Push Amid Cracks in Control

The EU launched the **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Resilience Directive** as its new priority, aiming to harden energy, healthcare and transport systems against AI-driven threats. Fast-tracked under Article 114 TFEU, it grants ENISA unprecedented operational authority to mandate detection upgrades, run red-team drills and coordinate real-time threat sharing. An €18bn fund, largely drawn from Digital Europe and REACT-EU, offers 70% co-financing for early adopters.

Yet momentum came at a cost. The directive faced resistance from member states wary of centralized enforcement. Poland and Hungary challenged the legal basis, arguing that security mandates infringe national competences. Germany delayed implementation pending a constitutional review, while smaller states questioned the fiscal burden despite co-financing. Only six countries committed to full adoption by end-2027, weakening initial impact.

Simultaneously, the **emergent_lab_fracture** — a leaked audit showing safety teams overruled by capability engineers at a leading lab — triggered global concern. Though outside EU jurisdiction, it exposed the fragility of voluntary safeguards. With `ai_capability` advancing to 54.5 due to sustained investment and algorithmic gains, the gap with `ai_safety` widened sharply. No finished measure improved safety standards where frontier models are built, and the fracture incident itself constituted a major governance failure: developers bypassed internal red-teaming, a clear sign of opaque reasoning and eroding control. Safety dropped by 5 points.

On sovereignty, the InvestAI Gigafactories progressed slowly. Emergency permitting unblocked Finnish and Spanish sites, but grid interconnections lagged. Talent recruitment remained uneven: Italy’s conditional support was secured via additional cohesion funds, but Germany and the Netherlands held out on research cessions. Sovereignty dipped slightly as `ai_capability` rose over 2 points, negating any in-flight benefit from ongoing category 4 measures.

Political capital fell due to portfolio strain and partial setbacks. The new large measure added −3, the existing three large measures cost −9 total, and the named priority subtracted −1. No finishing measure offset this, and the proposal bonus was modest (+2) given the indirect link between the new directive and emergent_lab_fracture. Capital dropped to 43.

Resilience saw a small gain (+2) from partial deployment, though full benefits await future turns. Openweight capability rose modestly as older models diffused onto newly available public compute.

Public sentiment held steady — no major scandal or benefit shifted perception.
