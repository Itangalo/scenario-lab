Launch a binding, EU-wide hardening and monitoring programme for essential services, starting with energy, healthcare, and transport, including mandatory intrusion detection upgrades, red-teaming exercises, and real-time threat sharing via ENISA.

## Priority  
**Critical Infrastructure Cyber Resilience Directive** — This turn, the emergent_lab_fracture and the unresolved fallout from cyber_test_shot make it clear that our sovereignty means nothing if our essential systems can be compromised from within or without; resilience is no longer a complement to capability, but a precondition of it.

## In practice  
We are fast-tracking the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Resilience Directive through the Internal Market and Telecommunications Council, using Article 114 TFEU as legal basis to harmonise minimum security standards across member states. ENISA is being empowered with operational authority to issue binding technical mandates, conduct unannounced red-teaming of critical nodes, and operate a real-time AI-enabled threat fusion centre. The directive will require all operators of essential services to deploy AI-augmented intrusion detection systems certified to a new EU standard, conduct quarterly adversarial simulations, and report anomalies within one hour. Funding of €18bn is being drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with 70% co-financing for member states that adopt the full package by end-2027.

Simultaneously, we are pressing forward on the InvestAI Gigafactories, now in their second year of construction. The Commission has triggered emergency permitting clauses in Finland and Spain, overriding local delays on grounds of strategic necessity, while committing €2.3bn in regional reinvestment to address environmental concerns. Grid operators in all four host nations are under binding deadlines to complete interconnections by turn 5. On talent, the EU Talent Shield Initiative is now tied to Cohesion Fund disbursements: Italy has signaled conditional support in exchange for €1.2bn in additional innovation funding, while Germany and the Netherlands are being offered joint leadership of a new European AI Safety Institute in exchange for ceding control over 40% of their national AI research appointments. The message is clear: sovereignty is not optional, but it will not be imposed — it will be negotiated, funded, and anchored in mutual interest.
