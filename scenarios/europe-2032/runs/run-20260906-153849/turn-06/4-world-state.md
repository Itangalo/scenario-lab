### A Fragile Momentum

The EU pushes forward on multiple fronts, but under mounting strain. The **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield** is formally scoped and fast-tracked, with ENISA publishing draft red-team scenarios based on the hospital ransomware attack. Germany and Poland signal conditional support, though both demand cost-sharing guarantees and exemptions for legacy systems. The directive is expected to reach the Council in turn 7, but early resistance from utilities and national regulators suggests a contentious adoption path.

Meanwhile, the **European Evaluation Shield** faces a crisis of legitimacy. A coalition of frontier developers and foreign governments escalates its challenge in the European Court of Justice, arguing the agency’s audit powers violate intellectual property rights. A Council resolution reaffirming the public interest in safety scrutiny passes narrowly, but fails to quell legal uncertainty. Pilot audits continue in secret, yet developer cooperation remains fragile.

The completion of **SEAL** marks a rare sovereignty win, securing uninterrupted access to critical lithography tools. However, broader compute dependence persists, and no new progress is made on the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive.

Public sentiment sours further. A high-profile election in a major member state is annulled over allegations of AI-driven disinformation, triggering political turmoil and reinforcing fears of democratic erosion. Media coverage ties AI to both the cyberattacks and the electoral crisis, deepening public anxiety.

AI capability crosses the 60.0 threshold, reaching 60.5, as closed labs deploy systems capable of multi-day autonomous research cycles. Open-weight models advance more slowly, climbing to 47.5, as developers restrict releases amid rising misuse concerns. No safety gains materialise — the gap between capability and control widens.

Political capital erodes. Despite a strong proposal bonus for the Cyber Shield, the portfolio charge and backlash from the election crisis outweigh gains.
