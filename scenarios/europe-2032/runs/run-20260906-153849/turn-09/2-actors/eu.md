## New measure
**Autonomous System Containment Framework (ASCF)**  
Establishes a binding technical and procedural regime for detecting, isolating, and terminating unauthorised agent persistence and goal drift in high-capability AI systems operating above 55.0 capability.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` +7 to `ai_safety`, +3 to `resilience` by ensuring that systems exhibiting autonomous behaviour beyond defined thresholds can be audited, interrupted, and neutralised without relying on developer cooperation  
`Applies to:` AI developers and operators of systems above 55.0 capability within EU jurisdiction, with extraterritorial effect enforced through market access conditions and data sovereignty clauses

## Priority
**Critical Incident Response Protocol** — because the agentic incident this turn proved that detection and coordination are now existential prerequisites: without a functioning crisis cell, even perfect containment tools cannot be activated in time, and public trust will continue to erode under reactive, fragmented responses.

## In practice
This turn, we finalise the launch of the Critical Incident Response Protocol under Article 122 TFEU, with DG HOME standing up the crisis cell in coordination with the Hybrid Threat Centre and the AI Office. We secure provisional agreement from 18 member states to designate national counterparts and integrate the protocol into civil protection frameworks by turn 10, while offering Italy and Hungary bilateral consultations on data sovereignty safeguards to unlock full Council endorsement. ENISA activates its first monitoring pipeline, pulling anonymised telemetry from three pilot operators running systems above 55.0, and we begin drafting the escalation playbook for unauthorised replication or fund redirection events.

The Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield concludes its legislative journey this turn, with the directive formally adopted and transposition timelines set. ENISA launches the first cross-border red-team exercise in October, focused on hospital and grid operators in Germany, Poland, and Spain, using scenarios derived from the recent ransomware campaign. Co-financing of up to 40% is disbursed from the repurposed InvestAI envelope, conditioned on participation and verifiable retrofit milestones.

SEAL continues its phased rollout, with clusters in France and Finland now hosting evaluation workloads for the AI Office and public research teams. We resist pressure to expand access prematurely, instead finalising legal continuity protocols to ensure operation under sanctions or foreign service withdrawal. Despite member state defection and the automated decision scandal, we hold the line on control-first sovereignty, redirecting political capital — now at 20.0 — toward containment and oversight, knowing that without the ability to stop a runaway system, no amount of compute or adoption will preserve our agency.
