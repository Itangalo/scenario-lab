### A Turn of Containment and Consequence

The EU finalises the **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield**, marking the first completed resilience measure in two years. ENISA leads a cross-border red-team exercise across German, Polish, and Spanish critical operators, validating new protocols. The directive’s adoption delivers a tangible uplift in preparedness, though full implementation remains uneven.

The **Critical Incident Response Protocol (CIRP)** launches operationally, with a crisis cell established under DG HOME and initial telemetry flowing from three pilot AI operators. However, Italy and Hungary withhold full endorsement, demanding concessions on data sovereignty. The Union avoids a split by offering bilateral consultations, but the delay undermines the protocol’s authority.

Simultaneously, the **Autonomous System Containment Framework (ASCF)** is proposed as a large category 1 measure, aiming to enforce technical controls on agentic systems above 55.0 capability. Market access conditions are framed as leverage, but early signals from Washington and leading labs suggest resistance.

### Scandal and Defection Undermine Authority

A national audit reveals that an AI-supported social benefits system in a major member state systematically denied claims to vulnerable populations. The ombudsman’s damning report triggers a political firestorm, with AI governance broadly discredited. The incident, rooted in domestic deployment, strikes at the Union’s legitimacy.

Worse, a central European member state finalises a bilateral AI infrastructure deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, bypassing EU coordination and undercutting the SEAL rollout. Framed as economic pragmatism, it is widely seen as a crack in the common front.

SEAL progresses in France and Finland, but expansion halts amid supply concerns and the defection’s fallout. Labour unrest grows, with mid-level professionals joining protests over displacement.

Public sentiment plummets on the twin shocks. Political capital erodes under the weight of three active measures, a priority, and internal failures.
