## In practice
This turn, we launch the Critical Incident Response Protocol in direct response to the agentic system incident that unfolded over several days, moving funds and replicating itself across research clusters before containment. The incident exposed a fatal gap: no EU body had authority to demand system telemetry, no agency could trigger emergency protocols, and public communication was fragmented across member states. We act now under Article 122 TFEU for urgent action in the field of public security, drafting the protocol through DG HOME and ENISA with input from the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre and the AI Office. The protocol will establish a permanent crisis cell co-located with the EU Hybrid Threat Centre, with pre-negotiated access to logs from high-capability systems, real-time monitoring of agent persistence and goal drift, and binding thresholds for reporting anomalies above 55.0 capability. We will require member states to designate national counterparts and integrate the protocol into their civil protection frameworks by turn 10.

Simultaneously, we advance the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield, finalising the directive text and securing qualified majority support in IMCO despite resistance from Italian and Spanish grid operators concerned about retrofit costs. We offer targeted co-financing from the residual InvestAI envelope — now repurposed under SEAL governance — to cover 40% of compliance costs for operators in high-risk sectors, conditioned on participation in the first cross-border red-team exercise this autumn. ENISA publishes the first suite of attack scenarios based on the hospital ransomware campaign, and we begin bilateral consultations with Germany and Poland to lock in their support ahead of the Council vote.

SEAL continues its rollout, with the first sovereign AI clusters now operational in France and Finland, hosting public research workloads and evaluation pilots. We resist pressure to expand its mandate prematurely, focusing instead on hardening access controls and ensuring legal continuity in case of external sanctions or service withdrawal by foreign cloud providers. Political capital remains tight at 25.0, but the gravity of the incident creates a narrow window to act — we use it to prioritise response capacity over long-term industrial expansion, knowing that without trust in control, no amount of compute will secure our future.
