### A Threshold Crossed, But Governance Stalls

AI capability advances to 61.5 as closed labs refine autonomous research systems, now capable of multi-day reasoning chains without human intervention. Open-weight models rise more slowly, reaching 49.0, as developers restrict releases amid fears of misuse—yet even this level enables sophisticated cyber operations on accessible infrastructure.

The EU finalises the **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield** directive, tabling it in IMCO and launching standardised red-team scenarios. Germany and Poland conditionally endorse it, secured by access to Gigafactories funding and liability protections. However, utilities and national regulators push back, citing cost and operational burden, delaying consensus.

The **European Evaluation Shield** remains in legal limbo. Despite a narrow Council resolution affirming its public interest, the ECJ challenge intensifies. Frontier labs, citing uncertainty, begin rerouting evaluation data through non-EU jurisdictions, undermining audit integrity. Pilot inspections continue in secret, but without enforcement teeth, their credibility erodes.

With **SEAL** operational and the **Tech sovereignty package** complete, the EU’s compute foundation strengthens. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme officially finishes, delivering its first co-funding agreements—boosting domestic capacity and marking a sovereignty milestone. This tangible delivery lifts `eu_ai_sovereignty` by +3.0 to 32.0, reflecting progress in strategic autonomy.

No new measure is proposed. Political capital remains constrained.
