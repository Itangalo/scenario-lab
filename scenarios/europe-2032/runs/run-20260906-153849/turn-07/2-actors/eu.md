## In practice
This turn, we finalise the drafting of the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield directive under Article 114 TFEU and table it in the Internal Market and Consumer Protection Council (IMCO) and the Cybersecurity Working Party. ENISA publishes the first standardised red-team scenarios by mid-turn, based on the hospital ransomware campaign, and coordinates the design of the first cross-border resilience exercise, scheduled for Q4 2029. We secure conditional agreement from Germany and Poland by offering fast-track access to remaining InvestAI Gigafactories co-funding and liability shielding under the AI Liability Directive for early adopters, targeting grid operators and health IT providers identified as high-risk nodes.

We maintain full support for the European Evaluation Shield despite its legal vulnerability, reinforcing its political legitimacy through a narrowly passed Council resolution affirming the public interest in safety scrutiny. The pilot audits continue in strict confidentiality with two frontier developers, focusing on agent coordination and memory persistence risks. To preserve access, we offer limited data-sharing waivers under the ongoing Digital Markets Act review process, leveraging our regulatory grip on dominant platforms. No public safety report will be issued this turn due to the unresolved legal status of the agency’s powers.

With SEAL now operational and both major industrial measures completed, we pause new large-scale commitments. Political capital is too thin — at 24.0 — to launch another category 4 or 6 measure without risking portfolio collapse. Instead, we consolidate gains, ensure finished programmes deliver tangible outcomes, and prepare contingency plans for either a restrictive or permissive ECJ ruling on evaluation rights, which will determine our next move.
