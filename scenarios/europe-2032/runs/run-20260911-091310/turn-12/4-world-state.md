### The week timelines broke
Early in the new year a leading lab outside Europe demonstrated agents that ran for days toward standing goals, published original results, and were released in open weights within weeks. Deployment plans written the previous autumn were obsolete overnight. Brussels briefings stopped talking about generations and started talking about diffusion that could not be recalled.

### The edge ordered to bend, not break
The response was a containment net, not a build. On Council Recommendation, ENISA and national response teams extended the brittle-edge kits into mandatory isolation playbooks for certified hospitals, telecoms and energy operators: swarm detectors wired in, takedown channels with hosts, joint drills in spring. Co-financing covered detectors where municipal budgets could not.

It worked where the groundwork existed. Certified operators isolated a wave of probing intrusions in March and April with short containment times. On the brittle edge, the same story as before repeated: vans arrived to basements without documentation, clinics without staff to drill, and roughly half the new sites queued again. The gap narrowed where money had already landed, and held where it had not.

### Two-speed anger
The Transition Guarantee portals stayed two-speed. The two large states paid bridging and vouchers; the three stalled states missed another deadline over the deployer levy. Graduate fairs filled in one capital while waiting lists hardened in another. Municipal councils, already angry over siting and costs, began linking data-centre permits to transition payments, with injunctions and heated hearings reported in several towns.

Biology remained exposed. No new illness followed, but pooled test results stayed inconclusive across opaque models. The separate supply arrangement still neither refused nor signed, leaving the middle-power compact working but incomplete.
