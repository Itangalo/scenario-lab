### The Verification Shift

The discovery that domains like clinical coding, contract review, and structural engineering now admit automated verification sent shockwaves through the AI development community. With cheap, scalable checks suddenly available, performance in these fields surged, blurring the line between human-supervised and autonomous systems. `ai_capability` rose by 2.5, accelerating in newly verifiable tasks — a shift that rendered existing safety assumptions obsolete.

### EU Moves on Two Fronts

The European Union pressed forward with its **Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive**, now in decisive drafting. The Commission offered concessions: access to the InvestAI fund, liability protections, and an independent auction mechanism via the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium. Yet resistance hardened. Two northern member states formally challenged the use of Article 114 TFEU, arguing it overreached on single-market grounds, while US cloud providers threatened to delay expansion plans. The measure remained on track for turn 3, but its legal fragility grew.

Simultaneously, the **Critical Verification Access Framework** was proposed — a small, targeted oversight mechanism requiring real-time evaluator access in newly verifiable domains. Though low-cost and narrowly scoped, it faced immediate pushback from developers citing trade secrecy. Still, its precision and timing lent it credibility.

### Gaps Widen, Costs Mount

Despite these efforts, assurance failed to keep pace. The gap between capability and safety narrowed slightly due to the new framework’s partial early effect, but not enough to offset systemic drift. `ai_safety` rose by +2 from the in-flight measure (toward its eventual +2 to +4), though the underlying trend remained downward.

No resilience measure addressed the growing threat surface, and with `ai_capability` rising, `resilience` dipped by −1. Sovereignty fell again (−1) as the frontier outpaced EU infrastructure, with no capacity event to offset the loss.

Political capital eroded sharply. The portfolio cost of three large measures (−9) and a named priority (−1) drained resources. The new small measure added −2, for a total ongoing cost of −11. The proposal bonus was +3 — justified by the recent verification shift and the measure’s direct relevance — but insufficient to offset the strain. Capital fell to 32.0. Public sentiment held steady, but unease simmered beneath the surface.
