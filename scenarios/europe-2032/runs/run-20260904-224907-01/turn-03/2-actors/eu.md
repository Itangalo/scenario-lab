## New measure
**Sovereign Verification Nodes**  
Establish a network of EU-controlled verification environments in critical domains (clinical, legal, engineering) where real-time monitoring of frontier AI outputs can be conducted under strict data governance, using the Critical Verification Access Framework as legal basis.

Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)

## Priority  
**Sovereign Verification Nodes** — because the verification shift has created a narrow window to build public technical capacity where automatic checks are now possible, and without independent EU-run nodes, our oversight will remain reactive and dependent on goodwill, not sovereignty.

## In practice  
This turn, the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive crosses the finish line — formally adopted in the Competitiveness Council after a final round of concessions. The liability shield for EU-tasked operators is now law, and the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) has launched its first auction for reserved capacity, with three hyperscalers participating under revised terms. The legal challenges from two northern member states remain pending before the CJEU, but the instrument stands. This marks a foundational step: for the first time, the EU holds direct, enforceable access to a slice of frontier compute, not as a customer but as a condition of operation. The directive does not reverse our sovereignty deficit, but it establishes a precedent — intelligence infrastructure, like energy or telecoms, carries reciprocal obligations.

At the same time, we are operationalising the response to the verification shift. The Critical Verification Access Framework is now law, but access without capability is ceremonial. So we are launching Sovereign Verification Nodes: a distributed network of secure, high-assurance environments hosted on EU-reserved compute, where accredited evaluators can monitor live model outputs in clinical coding, contract analysis, and structural design. These nodes will be staffed by cross-border teams from national digital agencies and EU research institutes, governed by the AI Office under a strict data-protection protocol. This is not about building a parallel AI — it is about ensuring that when a model begins generating plausible but false clinical trial data or subtly unsafe engineering schematics, the EU sees it first, validates it independently, and acts before harm spreads. The nodes will integrate with the EDIC auction system, ensuring they run on compute we can trust and sustain.

We are not waiting for perfection. The nodes start small, focused on domains where verification is already algorithmic, and will scale as the frontier widens. This is sovereignty in motion: not just owning compute, but using it to run the checks no one else can compel.
