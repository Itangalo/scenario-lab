## New measure
**Sovereign AI Access Guarantee**  
Establish a legal and operational framework ensuring that essential EU institutions, critical public services, and designated research bodies retain guaranteed access to frontier AI systems — whether developed in-house, through allied partnerships, or via secured foreign access — under enforceable terms that prevent arbitrary withdrawal.

## Priority
**Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield** — because the loss-of-control incident revealed that our most vital systems are unprepared for AI-native threats, and with openweight capability now close enough to the frontier to enable distributed offensive use, we cannot afford to let resilience lag while sovereignty is rebuilt.

## In practice
This turn, we are acting on the dual shock of exclusion and exposure. The **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield** is now law under NIS3, co-decided in the Internal Market and Justice Councils. We have secured provisional agreement from Germany and France to host the first two sovereign red-teaming hubs, with Estonia leading the detection stack integration into the ENISA backbone. The €12bn European Cyber Shield Fund is operational, with 70% cost coverage for member states below the 70.0 resilience threshold — a critical incentive for smaller states resisting the burden. We are negotiating bilaterally with Poland and Romania to address concerns about implementation capacity, offering joint staffing pools and fast-track training through the Digital Europe Programme. Industry pushback from telecom and energy lobbies is being met with phased compliance deadlines and shared threat intelligence feeds to demonstrate value.

Meanwhile, the **Sovereign Verification Nodes** have reached completion. Pilots in Finland (clinical), the Netherlands (legal), and Italy (engineering) have demonstrated that EU-controlled verification environments can detect synthetic data anomalies 18 hours faster than commercial validators, validating our investment in public technical capacity. The AI Office is now integrating findings into the upcoming AI Act delegated acts, and we are preparing to extend the model to radiology and grid management in the next phase. Legal challenges to the Compute Directive remain, but the recent access denial has shifted public and political sentiment: sovereignty is no longer abstract.

With the **election_alliance** outcome, we have a window — not a solution. The US offer of structured access is real, but conditional on alignment in export controls and standards. We will engage, but on the principle that partnership must not erode the imperative to build our own capacity. That is why we are launching the **Sovereign AI Access Guarantee**, a new category 4 measure designed to turn dependency into a governed relationship. It will establish legally binding access rights for essential functions, backed by diversified supply pathways and fallback mechanisms, including priority routing through EDIC infrastructure. This is not about rejecting partnership — it is about ensuring that when the next cutoff comes, the EU does not again find itself locked out of its own future.
