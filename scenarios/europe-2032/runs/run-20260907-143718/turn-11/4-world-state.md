### The Edge of Enforcement

With political capital at 2.0, the EU operates on the brink of institutional paralysis. Yet, from this weakness, a final push emerges. The Integrity Audit Expansion, long stalled, sees emergency templates finalised in Barcelona, Vilnius, and Ghent under direct Commission authority. Bypassing the Justice Council via Article 16 TFEU, auditors deploy into welfare and public employment systems, hunting for unexplained denials and decision loops with no redress. Early findings are explosive: one system in Ghent rejected 87% of disability claims over six months with no human review, its logic opaque even to administrators.

Simultaneously, the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) prepares its most aggressive move yet—launching the Enforcement of Suspension Authority. Designed as a shock to restore credibility, it empowers EAAD to decommission public AI systems that fail audits or obstruct accountability, backed by criminal liability for cover-ups. A “failure docket” is compiled, ready for public release.

But momentum is fragile. The Sovereign AI Core Programme, starved of compute and talent, shifts focus to forensic reverse-engineering units—proof that sovereignty now means post-harm investigation, not prevention. Public sentiment remains frozen at 18.0, as strikes in public-sector IT grow louder, demanding moratoria on AI hiring replacement.

No new compute comes online. US frontier access remains rationed under **CONSOLIDATION**, and no breakthrough offsets the decay. The open frontier creeps to 54.5 as mid-tier models leak through research channels, though no major breach occurs. AI capability inches to 69.0, driven by global labs outside EU reach.

### A Signal, Not a Shift

The EU acts—but barely. The Integrity Audit Expansion finishes this turn, yet yields no political gain. The new enforcement measure is proposed, but at this level of capital, even small actions risk collapse. The signal is clear: the Union will punish failure. Whether it can still compel change remains unproven.
