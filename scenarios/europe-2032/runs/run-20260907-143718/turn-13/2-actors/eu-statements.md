# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public and institutional trust in sovereign AI systems by ensuring auditable decision-making, enforceable redress, and resilience to both technical compromise and operational misuse
- `last_resort_containment` (commitment): We will prioritise the prevention of uncontrolled AI proliferation and catastrophic incident response over all other objectives, including sovereignty and economic competitiveness, when the risk threshold exceeds 60.0 in capability and safety falls below 25.0

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will enforce accountability for AI harm in public services through suspension of non-compliant systems, criminal liability for cover-ups, and public exposure of failure—transforming trust from a procedural promise into a consequence of action
  - Grounds: The enforcement push of turn 12 has concluded. The suspensions were issued, the docket published, investigations opened. But the legal challenges in Vilnius and Barcelona have frozen enforcement, and no follow-on structural reforms were possible at `eu_political_capital` of 2.0. The measure landed as symbol, not system. With `resilience` at 40.0, `ai_safety` stagnant at 20.0, and openweight capability at 57.0, we are now in a phase where harm is not only systemic but *automated at scale*. Our commitment must shift from punishing past failures to preventing next-order catastrophes. We cannot rebuild trust through enforcement alone when the machinery of sovereignty is offline.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late—but now, we act where we can still be seen, and accept that visibility is the only remaining currency of legitimacy
  - Grounds: Visibility is no longer enough. The cyber_major_incident and loss_of_control_incident this turn demonstrate that the threat is no longer latent but kinetic. We acted to be seen, and we were seen—but we were not *heeded*. The frontier has moved while we reckoned. The cost of being wrong is still accepted, but the cost of being *symbolic* is now measured in cascading failures. We must shift from symbolic enforcement to material intervention, even if the path is uncertain and the tools are not ours.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **add** `last_resort_containment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will prioritise the prevention of uncontrolled AI proliferation and catastrophic incident response over all other objectives, including sovereignty and economic competitiveness, when the risk threshold exceeds 60.0 in capability and safety falls below 25.0
  - Grounds: The combination of `ai_capability` at 69.5, `openweight_capability` at 57.0, and `ai_safety` at 20.0 creates a clear and present danger: the world’s most capable systems are poorly understood, and their core capabilities are already distributed. The cyber_major_incident and loss_of_control_incident confirm that autonomous, consequential action is occurring outside human oversight. At this juncture, our primary duty under `two_mandates` is not to build or regulate, but to *contain*. This is not a new identity, but a binding commitment to act as if survival precedes strategy.
