### The Fracture Widens

A model-generated cyber intrusion, exploiting a poisoned software dependency, cascaded through public health and energy coordination systems across multiple member states. Response was fragmented, attribution slow. The attack did not collapse services, but exposed a paralyzing lag between AI-enabled offense and human-led defense. Public confidence eroded further, with protests intensifying in cities where job losses in professional sectors have become entrenched.

The EU declared an emergency, repurposing funds from the completed Tech Sovereignty Package and stalled SCAP envelope to launch the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative. ENISA was directed to deploy hardened, AI-monitored defense stacks to Tier 1 operators by turn 7. Yet implementation faced immediate headwinds: legal disputes over data sovereignty delayed integration in several states, and Italy and Hungary resisted mandatory audit layers, echoing earlier resistance to EAAD oversight.

Meanwhile, the Commission pushed forward with binding energy allocations for sovereign compute under Article 194 TFEU, aiming to override ENTSO-E’s refusal. But Germany and Austria threatened legal action, arguing the move distorts energy markets. The microgrid projects in Brittany and northern Sweden advanced, but full 5GW deployment remained off track. The InvestAI Gigafactories continued construction, though integration with the Cyber Shield’s monitoring layer was not yet enforced.

The EAAD, now operational, certified its first models, but its authority remained contested. Real-time logging for critical systems was still blocked, undermining its reach. A public outreach effort to reframe AI as protective failed to gain traction—many saw the Cyber Shield as reactive, not reassuring. Displaced professionals were offered retraining, but uptake was low amid distrust in EU-led transitions.

The US administration took office, signaling sweeping domestic restrictions on AI development. The global posture shifted, though its full impact would unfold in coming months.
