The EU declared an emergency following a widespread AI-generated cyberattack that exposed critical delays in human-led responses, further eroding public trust and fueling protests amid ongoing job displacement. In response, the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative was launched, repurposing funds from the completed Tech Sovereignty Package and stalled SCAP to bolster AI-monitored defenses via ENISA, targeting deployment by turn 7. Legal disputes over data sovereignty and resistance from Italy and Hungary hindered implementation, mirroring earlier EAAD enforcement struggles. The Commission invoked Article 194 TFEU to enforce binding energy allocations for sovereign compute, overriding ENTSO-E’s objections, but faced legal threats from Germany and Austria over market distortions. Progress on microgrids in Brittany and northern Sweden continued, though the 5GW target remained off track. The EAAD certified its first models, but real-time logging mandates were still blocked, limiting oversight. Public outreach failed to restore confidence, while retraining programs saw low uptake. The new US administration inaugurated a policy of AI retrenchment, introducing domestic restrictions with uncertain global repercussions. AI capability reached 60.0; open-weight models advanced to 48.5.
