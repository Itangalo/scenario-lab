### The Surge Begins, But the Shield Cracks

The EU activates emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU in response to a cascading cyber incident that locked hospital scheduling systems and disrupted emergency dispatch across three member states. The attack exploited a poisoned software dependency, with payloads autonomously generated and scaled by AI — a hallmark of modern offensive capability. The newly launched Cyber Resilience Surge deploys 2,400 personnel from ENISA, EAAD, and the Cyber Resilience Corps, achieving rapid containment at key nodes. Recovery timelines shorten from months to weeks, demonstrating the value of coordinated response — a boost to resilience.

However, the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative, though officially completed this turn, remains only partially integrated. Italy and Hungary continue to block health data coordination, leaving precisely the vulnerability that was exploited. In retaliation, the Commission conditions Cohesion Fund disbursements on compliance by turn 9, triggering legal challenges and accusations of coercion.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories — formally finished — remain offline at two sites due to unresolved grid access. Invoking Article 194 TFEU forces grid operators to prioritise connections, but Germany and Austria escalate binding arbitration, arguing disproportionate energy allocation. No new compute comes online, deepening dependence.

Real-time model logging is now law, but enforcement lags. EAAD inspectors begin site visits, yet resistance from developers and fragmented implementation delay technical rollout. With no improvement in shipped system assurance, ai_safety dips as capability advances.

Public sentiment rises slightly as the Surge makes visible progress, framed not as tech promotion but institutional responsiveness. Protests persist, but the narrative shifts from helplessness to action.

### Sovereignty Erodes Amid Crisis

Despite finishing two major infrastructure measures, sovereignty declines further. The failure to operationalise Gigafactories at scale, combined with denied frontier access due to export controls, underscores systemic fragility. The US tightens chip and model export controls, rationing allied access — a move enabled by its retrenchment posture. The Union’s leverage is undercut: it built capacity, but cannot power or protect it.
