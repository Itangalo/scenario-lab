The EU activated emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU following a major AI-driven cyberattack disrupting critical health and emergency systems, prompting the deployment of the Cyber Resilience Surge, which successfully contained the breach and shortened recovery times. Despite this, the Sovereign Cyber Shield remains partially ineffective due to ongoing non-compliance by Italy and Hungary on health data sharing—precisely the vulnerability exploited. In response, the Commission tied Cohesion Fund access to compliance, sparking legal disputes and accusations of coercion. The InvestAI Gigafactories, though completed, remain offline at two sites due to unresolved grid connection disputes, despite Article 194 TFEU enforcement, with Germany and Austria challenging energy prioritization. Real-time model logging is legally mandated, but implementation stalls amid developer resistance and fragmented rollout, failing to improve systemic safety. Public trust rises modestly as the Surge demonstrates institutional responsiveness, shifting narratives from crisis to action. However, sovereignty declines further as export controls and energy bottlenecks expose fragility, while US retrenchment and restricted access to frontier AI components deepen strategic dependence.
