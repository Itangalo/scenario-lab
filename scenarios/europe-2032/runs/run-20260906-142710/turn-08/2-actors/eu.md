## New measure
**Measure:** Launch EU-Wide Cyber Resilience Surge  
Deploy a temporary, high-capacity incident response unit composed of EAAD auditors, ENISA engineers, and Cyber Resilience Corps personnel to rapidly assess, contain, and recover from the ongoing major cyber incident, while establishing a permanent rapid-response capability modelled on US-CERT but with sovereign authority.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 10  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 10–15 points through demonstrated incident containment, reduce recovery time from months to weeks, and create institutional muscle memory for cascading AI-driven attacks  
Applies to: Member states, ENISA, EAAD, national CERTs, Cyber Resilience Corps, critical infrastructure operators

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch EU-Wide Cyber Resilience Surge  
This turn demands response at scale: the cyber_major_incident has exposed systemic fragility, and without a visible, unified recovery effort, public sentiment will collapse further and member-state cohesion will fracture. While the Sovereign Cyber Shield remains vital, it is preventive; this surge is corrective, and legitimacy now hinges on visible action in the wake of harm.

## In practice  
We are treating this turn as one of emergency consolidation. The cyber_major_incident—originating in a poisoned software dependency and propagated by autonomous model-generated payloads—has disrupted public services in three member states, locking hospital scheduling systems and delaying emergency dispatch. Attribution remains unclear, but the tooling was AI-synthesised and scaled beyond human coordination. We are activating the European Emergency Coordination Protocol under Article 122 TFEU, releasing €18bn in emergency infrastructure recovery bonds through the EIB, and mobilising the first wave of the Cyber Resilience Surge: 2,400 personnel drawn from the Corps, ENISA’s red teams, and EAAD’s audit pool, now deploying to affected nodes.

The Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative reaches its scheduled completion this turn, but its partial integration—still missing Italy and Hungary—left the health dependency chain exposed. We are conditioning Cohesion Fund disbursements on immediate compliance by turn 9, offering technical integration teams but no further delays. Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories remain offline at two sites due to unresolved grid access; we are invoking the Energy Union’s emergency powers under Article 194 TFEU to compel grid operators to prioritise connections, backed by arbitration panels including Germany and Austria. The Mandate for Real-Time Model Logging is now law, but implementation lags—we are deploying EAAD inspectors to Tier 1 operators starting this quarter, with enforcement tied to system certification. Public sentiment, at 21.0, is a powder keg; we are launching a visibility campaign around the Surge, not to sell technology, but to show that when systems fail, the Union still answers.
