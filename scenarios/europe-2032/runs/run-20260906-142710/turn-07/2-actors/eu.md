## New measure
**Measure:** Mandate Real-Time Model Logging for Critical Infrastructure  
Require all AI systems operating in or interacting with Tier 1 critical infrastructure to log all inputs, outputs, and internal state transitions in real time, with data stored in sovereign, tamper-resistant repositories accessible to EAAD and national CERTs.

Category: 2 (Transparency and reporting)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 9  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by 10–15 points by enabling post-incident forensics and live anomaly detection; lay the foundation for automated auditability across sovereign systems  
Applies to: Member states, critical infrastructure operators, EAAD, ENISA, national regulators

## In practice  
We are treating this turn as the hinge between infrastructure and integrity. The Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative is now live in pilot mode across 12 Tier 1 operators in France, Finland, and the Netherlands, with ENISA deploying standardised containers that enforce zero-trust verification of all model-generated code and automated patching of dependency chains. Integration with national CERTs is functional, but Italy and Hungary still refuse access to their health data coordination layers, citing sovereignty—despite the fact that the attack originated in a dependency chain they did not monitor. We are invoking Article 122 TFEU again to condition Cohesion Fund disbursements on full integration by turn 8, offering technical assistance but no further delays.

The InvestAI Gigafactories are complete on paper, but two sites lack grid connection due to local opposition and unresolved energy-sharing agreements. We are now conditioning final certification on integration with the Cyber Shield’s monitoring layer and real-time logging—sovereign compute must be auditable or it is not sovereign. The 5GW energy-backed compute target was missed, but we are repurposing the legal framework under Article 194 TFEU to apply to the Gigafactories directly, declaring them strategic energy users. Germany and Austria remain hostile, but we have opened binding arbitration under the Energy Union framework to avoid ECJ escalation.

Public sentiment is at 21.0—near revolt levels. The retraining pipeline for displaced professionals has been restructured into a Cyber Resilience Corps, fast-tracking 15,000 workers into monitoring and response roles funded by EIB emergency transition bonds. This is not just labour policy—it is legitimacy infrastructure. If the Union cannot be seen defending, it will not be seen leading. The new US retrenchment posture offers no immediate leverage, but we are preparing a coordination offer under category 8: a transatlantic audit corridor for shared critical infrastructure standards, to be proposed when our own systems are demonstrably secure. For now, we hold the line.
