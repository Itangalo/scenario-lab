## In practice  
We are treating this turn as one of enforcement reckoning. The Cyber Resilience Surge continues its work, with 2,400 personnel now embedded in affected health and energy nodes across three member states, containing the fallout from the poisoned dependency attack. But containment without accountability is temporary. The Mandate for Real-Time Model Logging reaches its final implementation phase this turn, yet audits show only 40% of Tier 1 operators have functional logging, and none in Italy or Hungary. We are now activating enforcement: EAAD will issue compliance notices this quarter, and by turn 10, any system without verified logging will lose its operational certification under the AI Act, blocking access to public procurement and Cohesion Fund-linked projects.  

We are pairing enforcement with support: technical integration teams from ENISA and EAAD are being dispatched to non-compliant member states, offering sovereign logging stacks and grid-powered data vaults to resolve infrastructure barriers. But we are no longer negotiating timelines. Germany and Austria’s grid disputes remain unresolved, but we are using emergency powers under Article 194 to prioritise connections for logging infrastructure, treating it as critical. Public sentiment is fragile — the automated_decision_scandal and emergent_labour_backlash have deepened distrust — so we are launching a transparency drive: anonymised logs from compliant systems will be made available to ombudsmen and auditors, not to sell AI, but to show it can be held to account. This is no longer about capability. It is about whether the Union still governs.
