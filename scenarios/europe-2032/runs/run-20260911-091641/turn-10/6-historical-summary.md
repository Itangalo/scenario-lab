January brought dual biosecurity shocks: a preprint for a viable human-infecting design allegedly reachable with chatbot help, and a frontier-class openly downloadable model mirrored hundreds of thousands of times before regulation. Brussels left building freezes and injunctions in place and pivoted to civil protection: pooled sequencing screens, referral-lab triage rules, emergency stockpiles and cyber hardening of hospitals and water utilities via health emergency authority. Rollout was uneven — big university hospitals got kits and visits by spring, smaller clinics/operators got checklists amid extra workload. The common access pact formally concluded with little change: separate supply deal kept, US only technical briefings, frozen sites silent. By June Europe felt marginally better defended against a nameable bio/cyber threat but no closer to building out of dependence; queues held where EU helpers worked, lengthened elsewhere.
