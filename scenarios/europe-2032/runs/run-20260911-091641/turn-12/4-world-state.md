### Triage holds, ground shifts
January to June 2032 was the semester Brussels learned to defend clinics it could no longer upgrade.

The Bio-Uplift shield remained the only active programme. Health emergency teams kept pooled sequencing and referral triage running through university hospitals, while cyber specialists pushed new automated patching and swarm-detection tools first into the same hospital, laboratory and water networks. Large centres reported fewer disruptive alerts. Smaller clinics described the same burden as before: logs, swabs, extra shifts, now with another software update.

That narrow hold was overtaken by news from outside.

Capital fled AI globally in the spring. Valuations reset, announced data-centre expansions were cancelled, and several private compute arrangements European planners had quietly counted on evaporated. In Brussels the collapse confirmed the decision not to table a building instrument — there was nothing to bargain with — but it also removed any near-term path back to expansion.

At the same time, graduate hiring froze. Law firms, accountants, software houses and customer operations stopped replacing entry-level posts, openly attributing the gap to automated systems. Ministers toured showcase regions where European triage helpers had cut waiting lists, hoping to show public benefit. Audiences asked about jobs instead.

Diplomats salvaged one counterweight. Staff-level coordination with other middle powers holding supply-chain pieces — aligned export licences, joint bargaining for compute, shared evaluation — moved from studies to a standing framework. No sovereignty was ceded, but for the first time Europe could withhold something others needed.

Rumours darkened the background: single-source police reports of makeshift biology setups using downloaded models, and whispers of sabotage around frozen factory sites and sequencing hubs. Neither hardened, but both kept local councils anxious.

By June, hospitals were patched, hiring was not, and Brussels was better networked abroad than at home.
