### A shield finished, a verdict lost
The second half of 2032 gave Brussels a rare completion and two shocks it did not control.

The hospital and water-network shield was declared fully operational in autumn. University hospitals kept pooled sequencing and triage running, and the automated patching tools pushed into labs and utilities cut disruptive alerts at large sites. Health officials toured the system as proof Europe could still deliver protection, and smaller clinics were told reporting demands would be eased while other storms passed.

The storm came from the welfare state. An ombudsman and then a court found that a benefits fraud-risk system used in several regions had systematically cut or flagged vulnerable households, with caseworkers clicking through machine-ranked queues in seconds and logs that were kept but never read. The Commission conceded the file fell squarely under the high-risk rules for social insurance and opened infringement screening, publishing oversight records. The admission saved procedure and lost politics: evening news ran queues of families, opposition parties called the law a paper promise, and mayors refused new automated tools outright.

From outside, Washington and Beijing announced a limited pact on securing model weights and restricting a class of biological design tools, with thin verification. Europe was not at the table. Diplomats sought observer access through the middle-power coordination framework and aligned export licences, presenting it as a floor that could make future safety work shared rather than solitary. It did not read that way at home.

A genuine bright spot barely registered. Researchers at a European institute, using AI to close the hard search step, reported a new solid-state electrolyte chemistry with sharply higher stability — specialists called it a landmark for batteries, industry saw a path to cheaper cells within two years. Ministers cited it as evidence rebuilding could one day pay. Audiences, facing frozen graduate hiring and the benefits scandal, shrugged.

By December, clinics were defended, trust was not, and the pact others had signed set the terms Europe would have to live with.
