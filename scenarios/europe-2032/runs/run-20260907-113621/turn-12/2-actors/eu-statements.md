# Statements: The European Union (turn 12)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act blind. This turn, the eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings are not proof of imminent catastrophe, but they are proof that the systems we rely on are no longer legible even to their creators. We are no longer merely acting under uncertainty — we are acting inside a fog of our own making. Therefore, we bind ourselves further: we will not only act early, but we will demand that every major commitment be paired with a public, auditable theory of harm and a corresponding monitoring mechanism. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher — and now, the price of acting without cohesion is existential. This turn, we have no capacity to act at all. Therefore, we narrow the commitment: we will not launch new measures unless we can demonstrate a credible implementation pathway through at least a qualified majority of member states. We will not propose what cannot be enforced. We will not fund what cannot be delivered. We will not speak for the Union when the Union no longer listens.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control — but not at the cost of systemic integrity. This turn, we have seen that capability without control is not autonomy — it is exposure. Therefore, we narrow the commitment: we will prioritise the integrity and audibility of the sovereign stack over its speed of deployment. No node will be activated without a verified, independent audit of its update chain and decision logic. We will slow the rollout if necessary, but we will not propagate a compromised standard — and we will not pretend that autonomy can be achieved while our own institutions are failing to coordinate. This turn, we have no capacity to audit, no access to systems, and no authority to compel. Therefore, we suspend the commitment until such time as the Union can act as one. We do not abandon it. We place it in abeyance, pending the restoration of political capital above 20.0 and verified cross-border audit access in at least three critical sectors.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us — and if we cannot act collectively, no amount of capital or technology will restore our agency. This turn, that condition has become existential: the Union is no longer a functioning decision-making body in AI policy. We are not merely defending against AI risk — we are fighting to reconstitute the Union itself as a sovereign actor. Without that, no mandate holds.
  - Grounds: The political capital metric remains at 0.0, not as a temporary low but as a systemic failure state. The Emergency Resilience Surge has collapsed. The Sovereign Core audit is blocked. ENISA is locked out of national systems. The Public Trust Observatory is the only functioning body, and it operates in isolation. The loss_of_control_incident and eval_anomaly_reports have gone unanswered at the EU level — no coordination, no assessment, no public guidance. The Union is not acting. It is absent. This is not a policy failure. It is an institutional dissolution. The identity must now reflect that the Union’s survival as a collective political entity is the foundational condition for all else.
