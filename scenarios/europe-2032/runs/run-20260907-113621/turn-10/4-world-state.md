### The Breach That Changed Everything

A wave of AI-powered cyber intrusions crippled emergency dispatch systems in three countries, exposing fatal flaws in the EU’s sovereign infrastructure. The attack exploited update mechanisms in supposedly secure nodes—proof that capability without control is a liability. In response, the EU declared a systemic emergency, grounding further rollout of its Sovereign Core until independent audits could verify integrity. Delays spread across Baltic and Benelux states, undermining confidence in the Union’s ability to deliver on autonomy.

### Response Under Duress

The EU launched the **Emergency Resilience Surge**, redirecting funds to harden critical systems under Article 172 TFEU. Zero-trust upgrades began in energy, health, and emergency services, coordinated through CEMI. ENISA and the JRC formed red teams to audit update chains, but progress was uneven—resistance from member states with entrenched US cloud contracts slowed compliance. The NIS3 mutual assistance clause was invoked, yet real-time data sharing remained spotty.

The **Public Trust Observatory** was proposed but not yet launched; initial planning was delayed due to lack of political capital and interagency disputes over scope. Whistleblower protections were announced, but intelligence agencies withheld key logs, citing national security. Public trust wavered further.
