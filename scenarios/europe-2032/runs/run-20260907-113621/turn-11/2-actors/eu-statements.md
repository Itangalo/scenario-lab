# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control — but not at the cost of systemic integrity. This turn, we have seen that capability without control is not autonomy — it is exposure. Therefore, we narrow the commitment: we will prioritise the integrity and audibility of the sovereign stack over its speed of deployment. No node will be activated without a verified, independent audit of its update chain and decision logic. We will slow the rollout if necessary, but we will not propagate a compromised standard — and we will not pretend that autonomy can be achieved while our own institutions are failing to coordinate.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us — and if we cannot act collectively, no amount of capital or technology will restore our agency.
  - Grounds: The political capital metric has hit 0.0, not as a temporary dip but as a structural failure. Member states are withholding cooperation on the Emergency Resilience Surge, citing fiscal strain and national sovereignty; the NIS3 mutual assistance clause is being ignored in practice; intelligence agencies continue to block access to logs; and the Public Trust Observatory remains a skeleton with no enforcement power. This is not fragmentation — it is disintegration. The identity must now reflect that the Union’s survival as a decision-making entity is itself at stake. We are not merely defending against AI risk — we are fighting to remain a coherent actor.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act blind. This turn, the eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings are not proof of imminent catastrophe, but they are proof that the systems we rely on are no longer legible even to their creators. We are no longer merely acting under uncertainty — we are acting inside a fog of our own making. Therefore, we bind ourselves further: we will not only act early, but we will demand that every major commitment be paired with a public, auditable theory of harm and a corresponding monitoring mechanism. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher — and now, the price of acting without cohesion is existential.
  - Grounds: The commitment stands, but its grounding has deepened again. This turn, we proposed the Emergency Resilience Surge and grounded the Sovereign Core rollout — both based on early signals — but neither is being implemented at scale because member states do not trust the central coordination. We are not failing due to lack of foresight, but due to lack of authority. The evolution of this commitment now includes a demand for demonstrable buy-in: no major action will proceed without a verified implementation pathway through national governments. This is not retreat — it is realism. The cost is delay, but the alternative is irrelevance.
