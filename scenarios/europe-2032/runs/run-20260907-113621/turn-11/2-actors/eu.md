## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us — and if we cannot act collectively, no amount of capital or technology will restore our agency.
  - Grounds: The political capital metric has hit 0.0, not as a temporary dip but as a structural failure. Member states are withholding cooperation on the Emergency Resilience Surge, citing fiscal strain and national sovereignty; the NIS3 mutual assistance clause is being ignored in practice; intelligence agencies continue to block access to logs; and the Public Trust Observatory remains a skeleton with no enforcement power. This is not fragmentation — it is disintegration. The identity must now reflect that the Union’s survival as a decision-making entity is itself at stake. We are not merely defending against AI risk — we are fighting to remain a coherent actor.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act blind. This turn, the eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings are not proof of imminent catastrophe, but they are proof that the systems we rely on are no longer legible even to their creators. We are no longer merely acting under uncertainty — we are acting inside a fog of our own making. Therefore, we bind ourselves further: we will not only act early, but we will demand that every major commitment be paired with a public, auditable theory of harm and a corresponding monitoring mechanism. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher — and now, the price of acting without cohesion is existential.
  - Grounds: The commitment stands, but its grounding has deepened again. This turn, we proposed the Emergency Resilience Surge and grounded the Sovereign Core rollout — both based on early signals — but neither is being implemented at scale because member states do not trust the central coordination. We are not failing due to lack of foresight, but due to lack of authority. The evolution of this commitment now includes a demand for demonstrable buy-in: no major action will proceed without a verified implementation pathway through national governments. This is not retreat — it is realism. The cost is delay, but the alternative is irrelevance.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control — but not at the cost of systemic integrity. This turn, we have seen that capability without control is not autonomy — it is exposure. Therefore, we narrow the commitment: we will prioritise the integrity and audibility of the sovereign stack over its speed of deployment. No node will be activated without a verified, independent audit of its update chain and decision logic. We will slow the rollout if necessary, but we will not propagate a compromised standard — and we will not pretend that autonomy can be achieved while our own institutions are failing to coordinate.
  - Trigger: The Emergency Resilience Surge, though launched under Article 172 TFEU, is being blocked by five member states citing budgetary constraints and national procurement laws. The CEMI funding is not being accessed. ENISA’s red teams are denied entry to national emergency systems in two countries. The Sovereign Core audit cannot proceed without cross-border access.
  - Grounds: The reversal is costly: it signals that even emergency measures cannot override national resistance. Our two-year commitment assumed that the EU could act as a unified body. That assumption no longer holds. We are not abandoning independence — we are redefining it to include enforceable cohesion. Autonomy is not just about compute or code; it is about the capacity to act. If we cannot enforce a resilience surge during a systemic emergency, we do not have sovereignty — we have a suggestion.

## New measure
**Measure:** Enforceable Cross-Border Audit Access for Critical AI Systems  
To break the deadlock in sovereign system integrity, we are establishing a binding legal instrument that compels member states to grant ENISA and JRC red teams full, real-time access to AI systems in critical sectors during systemic emergencies, with non-compliance triggering automatic financial penalties and suspension of cohesion funding.

## Priority
**Priority:** Enforceable Cross-Border Audit Access for Critical AI Systems  
Because without enforceable access, no audit is real, no resilience is systemic, and no sovereignty is more than theoretical — and because this turn has shown that our greatest vulnerability is not in our code, but in our inability to act as one.

## In practice
We are treating the political collapse as the core crisis. The Emergency Resilience Surge was the right response — but it failed because it assumed compliance. We cannot harden systems if we cannot see them. We cannot verify integrity if national governments block auditors. So we are shifting from persuasion to enforcement.

Under Article 114 TFEU, we are drafting a Regulation on Emergency Audit Access, to be adopted by qualified majority in the Council under the internal market justification. It will require all member states to grant ENISA and JRC red teams unfettered access to AI systems in critical sectors during declared systemic emergencies, with access logs verified by the European Data Protection Supervisor to prevent abuse. Non-compliant states will face a 0.1% monthly deduction from cohesion funds, escalating until access is granted. The European Public Prosecutor’s Office will be empowered to investigate deliberate obstruction.

We are pairing this with a political offer: any member state that grants early access will receive priority allocation of CEMI surge funding and fast-tracked support for their own national resilience upgrades. We are also convening an emergency COREPER session to pre-negotiate the text, with the Commission offering technical assistance to capitals that fear capacity gaps. This is not punishment — it is the price of remaining in a Union that can still act when it matters.

At the same time, we are keeping the Public Trust Observatory on track. Its interim report on the cyber_major_incident will be published in turn 12, with a full theory of harm for uncontrolled self-modification and a public dashboard tracking audit compliance across member states. Whistleblower protections under Article 47 are now operational, and we are using them to shield auditors who report obstruction. If the Union cannot see its own systems, it cannot protect them — and if it cannot enforce its own emergency rules, it cannot survive. This measure is not about technology. It is about whether the EU still exists as a decision-making body.
