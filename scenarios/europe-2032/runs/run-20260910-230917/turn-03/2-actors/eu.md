## New measure
**EU Open-Weight Incident Shield**
Requires rapid incident and near-miss reporting for high-capability open models, funded hardening kits for operators and municipalities, and community benefit packages tied to factory grid pledges to unblock siting. Why this and why now: the frontier is now unrecallable on private hardware just as intrusion news has turned host towns against new loads, so we must blunt misuse of what is already loose while restoring licence to build.

## Priority
EU Critical Infrastructure AI-Hardening Pact, because live intruders in transmission plus an unrecallable open model outrank concrete-pouring this half-year.

## In practice
We run the Shield through the AI Office with ENISA under AI Act serious-incident and NIS2 reporting bases: CERT-EU triage for open-model-enabled intrusions, subsidised OT detection packs for ports, water and municipal grids, and a standard community compact — energy-price rebates, water-use caps and local jobs — that councils in Paris, Madrid, Stockholm, Warsaw shortlist sites must get before grid pledges are signed.

The hardening pact (M4) keeps its retrofits and exercises, with pressure on France and Germany to move from summaries to raw telemetry via the Energy Council. Gigafactories lines (M1/M2) are held to unblocking one delayed site through the compact rather than new cash, while the Evaluation Institute (M3) gets its first independent tests on the new open weights via seconded JRC teams. This keeps our two-year commitment to sovereign, safe and resilient capacity while stopping the bleeding first.
