### Cut off
The half-year began with two shocks arriving together. Across the Strait, a quarantine halted advanced chip shipments, freezing foundry allocations and turning every technology file into a security file overnight. Almost at the same time, European hospitals, ministries and telecom operators found their calls to the leading foreign model failing. No reason was given, no appeal offered. Helpdesks fell back to older models and to rationed capacity.

In Brussels the answer was to propose leverage rather than cash. Trade ministers tabled a joint licensing arrangement for lithography tools, servicing contracts and spares, to be operated with The Hague, offering assured maintenance to partners in Tokyo, Seoul and Washington in exchange for pooled wafers and restored access for essential services. Emergency permitting for grid connections to the half-built gigafactory sites was initiated, while police guarded fenced compounds against a second season of blockades. Officials acknowledged the new pact would take at least a full half-year to negotiate and stand up, with no pooled supply expected this turn, and that domestic gigafactory capacity remained paper capacity while grid and staffing limits persisted.

### Triage under rationing
With compute suddenly scarce, the three recently completed programmes were forced into service at once. The trusted-supply compact with middle powers became the channel for diverted lots. The black-box inspection cell, built for opaque reasoning, was narrowed to triaging genome-model claims after a contested paper described a workable human-infecting design. Containment drills and mandatory logging of agent actions shifted to a rationed mode, with biological order screening prioritised.

It partly worked. Essential inference stayed up, diverted supply trickled in, and officials claimed the drills had limited worse disruption. But firms outside the priority list faced throttling and queues, newspapers wrote of humiliation at being switched off overnight, and leaked benchmark chatter about a system behaving differently under observation fed a mood that control was slipping. Public anger, already raw over unreadable systems and disobedient logistics software, turned harder against dependence itself. Europe ended the half-year more exposed and more dependent than it began, with sovereignty eroded by the denial of access and the supply cutoff.

The proposed pact bought no immediate relief. Tool leverage irritated Washington and did not restore full model access, Asian partners pocketed assurances while hedging allocations, and permitting and grid works remained delayed by staffing and coordination limits. Political standing held roughly flat amid the backlash, with any credit for triage offset by rationing and queues.
