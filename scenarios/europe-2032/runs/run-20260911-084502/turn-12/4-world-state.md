### A quieter emergency
The first half of 2032 felt, for the first time in years, almost ordinary. The isolation wards emptied. The joint HERA-cyber bulletin shrank from daily crisis counts to weekly charts showing probes blocked and waiting lists slowly falling. Hospitals where Recovery Corps crews had installed automated patching stayed stable; paper logs disappeared.

What filled the news instead was work. Studies from law firms, accountancies, local administrations and newsrooms showed the same pattern: assistants doing drafts, filings and summaries, junior staff producing more, firms that had cut early quietly hiring again. In town halls, a new scoreboard began publishing decision times and clinic waits, and in several cities the numbers actually moved.

Brussels tried to ride that window. The Commission launched a framework contract for audited office assistants, deployed first through already-hardened hospitals and municipalities, paid from repurposed digital and social funds. Procurement was fast; deployment was uneven. Richer regions plugged the tools in within weeks, poorer ones waited for integration help that had not been budgeted.

### Robots in the warehouse
While offices celebrated, warehouses changed hands. Cheap mobile arms for picking, sorting and palletising arrived in volume from abroad, with American control software on Chinese-built bodies. Logistics operators cut costs quickly. Repair shops, care providers and building sites found the same machines useless where plans were wrong or judgement was needed.

That split hardened into a new labour divide, and into a new dependence. Europe's machine-builders warned they could not match the price, and The Hague's lithography fight stayed frozen as Brussels chose quiet preservation of remaining chip volumes over confrontation. Washington's export discipline held; local copies of a near-frontier model kept running in workshops because nothing affordable and permitted replaced them.

Far away, extended naval manoeuvres, rising shipping insurance and an expulsion rattled supply-chain planners. No shipments stopped, but contingency memos returned. By June, essentials held and sentiment lifted a fraction — but the Union owned neither the robots in its warehouses nor the models in its offices.
