### The patchers arrive late
The attack did not announce itself as one attack. In February, booking systems in three health regions froze with identical ransom notes. In March, a widely used logging library pushed an update that opened a back door no scanner had flagged. Grid operators found the same machine-written probes in their logs. The tooling, analysts agreed, had been generated at scale. Attribution would take months.

Defenders were visibly behind for weeks. Hospitals reverted to the printed folders from the winter kits. Municipal clerks processed claims on paper again. Then the joint recovery teams began to show up — mixed crews from national response centres and contracted vendors, with pre-paid patch contracts and offline procedures rehearsed over the winter.

Where they arrived early, services degraded rather than stopped. Where they arrived late, mayors filmed empty counters. The daily technical notes continued, now covering both power and code. A single claims window for energy relief and recovery costs blunted, but did not kill, the charge that Brussels was relabelling old money.

### A better solver, fewer juniors
At the same time, laboratories demonstrated systems that solved sustained coding, mathematics and intrusion tasks far faster than their predecessors. Specialists argued about what it meant; operators noticed what it did. Intrusion attempts grew more automated within weeks.

By spring, graduate hiring data told the second story. Routine coding posts, standard analysis roles, first-draft writing and front-line support desks stopped hiring entry-level staff. Unions called it a lost generation of office work. Employers called it automation of the checkable. Forecasts of a broader wave were quietly pushed to next year.

Export licensing tightened again in April. European buyers kept volume access to advanced chips on paper, but quotas, delays and conditions made rationing real. Data-centre operators curtailed in winter for power were now throttled for supply. Open models weeks behind the frontier ran on local hardware in town halls and workshops — unrecallable, indispensable, and cited by both regulators and their critics.

By June, essentials held. Trust did not.
