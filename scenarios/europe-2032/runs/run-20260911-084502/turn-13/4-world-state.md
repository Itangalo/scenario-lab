### The autumn the lights flickered
The second half of 2032 broke the fragile ordinary. A largely automated extortion attack swept through municipal systems, hospitals and utilities at once — locked screens in town halls, appointment systems down, a tainted software component forcing engineers to hunt an unknown blast radius. Patch crews who had stabilised hospitals in spring were redeployed to utilities and communes. In richer cities service was restored in days; in poorer regions paper logs returned for weeks.

At the same time, substations and half-built computing sites were hit physically. Fences cut, transformers damaged, cranes idle under guard. Construction halted in four countries while soldiers and gendarmes stood watch and mobile repair kits were rushed south and east. The two shocks fed each other: digital restoration slowed where power was uncertain, and rumours blamed the machines themselves.

Brussels activated its emergency machinery. Joint restoration teams under civil protection, cyber specialists and police triage units were pushed out, paid from repurposed security funds. The office-assistant rollout was frozen where not already live to keep integrators on repair work. It stopped a cascade — grids held, wards stayed lit — but visibly late and unevenly.

Beyond Europe, Washington and Beijing announced a limited understanding on securing model weights, escalation risks and certain biological tools, with thin verification. Europe was briefed afterwards and asked for observer access through its evaluation institute. No shipments stopped in the Strait, but insurance rose again and contingency memos hardened.

Meanwhile a new freely downloadable model, close to the closed frontier, spread to hundreds of thousands of machines in days. Workshops welcomed it; security services noted the same tooling that had built the autumn attack was now unrecallable.

By December essentials functioned, but trust did not. Town-hall scoreboards that had shown faster decisions now showed outage hours. Blame settled on dependence: foreign robots in warehouses, foreign models in offices, and now foreign tools in the attack.
