### The jump no one ordered
Winter brought another demonstration from an American lab: planning and design work that had been pencilled for next year completed in an afternoon, with the methods published in outline and the weights held back. European licensing desks felt it at once as longer queues and higher prices for the same doses and scheduling tools.

Within weeks a distilled copy approximating the new level was circulating freely. Mirror sites, university clusters, small contractors — copies multiplied faster than takedowns. Security officials stopped talking about containment and started talking about patch speed.

### A treatment behind glass
Spring also brought genuine good news from oncology: tailored immune therapies, designed with frontier models, showing remissions in cancers with few options. A handful of European patients received them through compassionate channels and American partner hospitals. Families spoke of miracles on evening news.

For most, the miracle stayed on screen. Clinics in France, Spain and Poland could offer waiting lists, not courses, and health ministries were frank that supply depended on foreign allocation decisions made in wartime. Gratitude mixed with bitterness: the cure existed, but not here, not at scale.

### Living off the stack
Brussels proposed nothing new. With coffers empty and tempers raw, the Commission ordered the administration to run on what was already built. Grid crews under emergency powers guarded connection works and strung temporary reroutes around darkened cable landings. Hospital, power and telecom operators stayed islanded where possible. Sentinel wards kept passive watches for unusual infections while pharmacists split stockpiles dose by dose.

Bridge pay kept flowing to graduates without workplaces and warehouse crews without shipments, though several mayors said openly the money would not see another winter. Diplomats repeated the same offer to Washington and Beijing: keep European territory, landings and factory ground out of targeting, keep licensed flows moving, share test data. Replies were courteous and noncommittal.

Insurers quietly complicated repairs, with word spreading among port agents that commercial cover for cable ships near contested waters was being withheld without military escort. Jobs stalled for weeks at a time.

By June the lights stayed on and wards stayed open, degraded but functioning. Exhaustion showed. The public, seeing cures it could not touch and copies it could not control, treated advanced systems less as infrastructure than as weather from elsewhere — dangerous, distant, and decided by others.
