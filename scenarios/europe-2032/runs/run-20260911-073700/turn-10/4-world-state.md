### War over the frontier
In March the unthinkable became operational: American and Chinese forces exchanged strikes aimed at winning an AI lead deemed uncatchable if lost. No European capital was consulted. Within days, fabrication plants, subsea cables and satellite links became targets, and several landing points and transit hubs touching Europe went dark or were rerouted under military priority.

Brussels declared its territory, its cable landings and its two permitted gigafactory plots non-belligerent infrastructure, offering deconfliction and mutual test recognition to both sides in exchange for no targeting and continued supply of licensed therapies, scheduling software and models. The offer was acknowledged and then ignored where it mattered. Therapy queues lengthened as Californian allocation paused; stockpiled doses kept French, Spanish and Polish clinics running for weeks, then thinned.

### Fire at home, hiring freeze everywhere
The night fires spread from fenced robot pilots to grid-connection works for the gigafactories. Construction halted. The Energy Council took emergency control, posted guards and sent civil-protection repair teams, but no new siting promise could be made while cables were being cut.

At the same time a new open release matching the closed frontier spread to hundreds of thousands of machines in a week, beyond any recall. Entry-level hiring in law, accountancy, software and customer operations simply did not return. The graduate guarantee now paid bridge pay to young workers with no workplace to bridge to, and to warehouse crews idled by blockades and war disruption. Works councils held a few reopenings; mayors mostly did not.

The middle-power club met urgently, pooling what compute survived and pulling the wayward capital part-way back with a share of it. It kept Europe bargaining as one channel, but it could not keep the lights of the frontier on.
