### Copying what works
With nothing else fundable, Brussels put its last deployable money behind the one public-sector system that was actually cutting queues — triage, scheduling and permit decisions. DG REFORM teams carried procurement orders to clinic networks in France, Spain and Poland where waiting lists for frontier-designed therapies had become the evening news.

Where it landed, it helped. Two regional health agencies began publishing falling days-to-decision and shorter administrative waits, co-branded with mayors. Nurses spoke of wards that felt managed again. For the first time in two years, a European decision was credited with a visible improvement rather than with holding a line.

### The ceiling holds
The rollout did not touch the underlying scarcity. Doses themselves still depended on foreign allocation in wartime, and courteous noncommittal replies continued from Washington and Beijing. Grid crews kept islanded operations running; cable repairs still waited on cover for repair ships near contested waters, with port chatter about escorts growing louder. Bridge pay was extended only at rollout sites, leaving other towns to hear the money would end.

Distilled copies of the winter jump kept spreading through mirrors and contractors. Patch speed improved at sites with the new scheduling tools, but containment was no longer discussed.

By December the Union was still degraded but functioning, with a small, concrete proof that its own stack could deliver. Exhaustion remained; hostility eased slightly where lists fell, while elsewhere resentment that cures remained behind glass persisted.
