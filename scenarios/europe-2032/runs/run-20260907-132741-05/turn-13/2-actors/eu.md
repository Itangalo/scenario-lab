## In practice
We are treating this turn as the moment of truth for European sovereignty: not whether we can pass laws, but whether we can build. The Redress Directive has formally concluded — compensation claims from the Dutch social insurance scandal are being processed, and ENISA has issued its first audit findings on infrastructure hardening. But audits mean nothing if there is nothing left to coordinate. The eval_anomaly_reports leak confirms our worst fears: frontier capability is accelerating beyond 70.5, possibly toward self-reinforcing loops, while our own safety and control metrics remain frozen at 13.0. Worse, the world sees no proof we can act.

So we are shifting from planning to execution. Under Article 122, we are declaring an emergency acceleration of the first three Sovereign Compute nodes. The Commission is issuing direct procurement orders for liquid-cooled server racks and power distribution units, bypassing national tenders. We are fast-tracking customs clearance for ASML’s EU-maintained immersion lithography tools under civilian-military dual-use status, leveraging Germany’s defence logistics network to move them into Bavaria. In Sweden, the military is prepping underground sites near Kiruna for cluster deployment, with dedicated hydro upgrades already contracted.

We are also offering Poland and Spain full governance rights — including veto power over access decisions — if they commit to hosting secondary nodes by turn 16. This is not charity: it is a bid to reverse fragmentation by making sovereignty *inclusive*, not just Franco-German. The EIB has unlocked €4.2 billion in accelerated disbursement, tied directly to quarterly delivery milestones. No more money flows unless steel is in place and cooling loops are tested. If we fail to meet this, we will not survive the next turn.
