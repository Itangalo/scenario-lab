### The Weight of Steel

The EU declared an emergency push to ground sovereign compute, but the ground remains unbroken. Despite Article 122 powers and €4.2 billion in conditional funding, physical deployment of the first Sovereign Compute nodes stalled. French local authorities blocked construction in Brittany, citing environmental reviews exempt under national law. In Bavaria, ASML refused to release immersion tools, fearing U.S. sanctions. Only Sweden made progress, with military engineers beginning excavation near Kiruna.

Member states interpreted the acceleration as coercion, not coordination. Poland rejected governance offers, calling them "cosmetic inclusion." Spain demanded veto rights over software, not just hardware. France and Germany, despite hosting nodes, initiated parallel national compute projects, undermining EU unity. ENISA’s audits were ignored; Berlin cited "operational urgency" to bypass central directives.

Sovereignty fell to 2.0: despite the new measure, no capacity was secured, and ai_capability rose by 1.0, costing −1.
