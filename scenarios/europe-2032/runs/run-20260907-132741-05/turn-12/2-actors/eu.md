## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign Compute Corridor Initiative  
This is our dominant effort because the ASML servicing halt has exposed that our AI sovereignty is not just weak — it is nonexistent. With eu_ai_sovereignty at 5.0 and public trust at rock bottom, we are not just dependent — we are governable by others. The Emergency Directive addressed harm, but did not restore agency. Now we must act not as first responders, but as founders: of a physical and legal foundation for AI that no foreign power can switch off. Without this, every future measure — redress, safety, resilience — will be subject to veto from outside.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the hinge between collapse and reconstitution. The Emergency Redress and Infrastructure Protection Directive has now passed — narrowly, through a qualified majority in ECOFIN, leveraging cohesion fund conditionality and repurposing Frontex logistics for infrastructure protection. DG HOME is standing up the Redress Coordination Unit as a permanent body under Article 114, with authority to freeze deployments and issue compensation orders. France and Finland have re-engaged, accepting liability frameworks in exchange for enforcement autonomy at the national level. The first compensation claims from the Dutch social insurance scandal are being processed in pilot regions — not perfectly, but visibly.

But redress alone cannot restore agency. The ASML decision was a wake-up call: we do not own our compute, our talent, or our energy margins. The Sovereign Compute Corridor Initiative begins with a coalition of the willing — France, Germany, and the Nordic states — pooling sovereign funds and military logistics to build and protect distributed compute clusters across geographically resilient sites. We are invoking Article 122 for emergency industrial coordination, but this time with a fiscal anchor: €18 billion in repurposed recovery funds and EIB guarantees, matched by national contributions. The first sites — in Brittany, Bavaria, and northern Sweden — will host liquid-cooled clusters powered by dedicated grid upgrades, secured under national defence mandates. We are negotiating with ASML and IMEC to establish EU-controlled maintenance pools for mature-node tools, bypassing US jurisdiction through dual-use civilian-military status.

This is not a replacement for the frontier — we are not building GPT-7 here — but a floor beneath which no one can push us. The corridor will run open-weight models at 58.0 capability and serve as fallback for critical public services. It is sovereign not because it is closed, but because it is unrecallable. We are offering Poland and Spain entry into the governance council if they commit to hosting nodes — turning fragmentation into a new form of cohesion. If we succeed, by 2033 the EU will no longer beg for access. We will have a base from which to stand.
