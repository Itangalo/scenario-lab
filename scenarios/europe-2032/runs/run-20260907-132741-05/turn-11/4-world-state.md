### A Union Under Siege

The EU’s credibility is in freefall. The ombudsman’s ruling on the Dutch social insurance AI confirmed systemic harm, and protests have hardened into sabotage: data centres in Utrecht and Berlin faced blockades, one suffering a fire at its substation. The **Emergency Redress and Infrastructure Protection Directive** was proposed as a lifeline — a large category 6 measure aiming to bind redress and harden infrastructure — but with *eu_political_capital* at zero, it gained no traction. Member states deferred decision-making, citing legal uncertainty over the use of Article 122 for AI governance. The EIB refused to extend funding levers without a Council mandate, and Poland dismissed the directive as “crisis overreach.”

Meanwhile, the **Public Trust Corps**, though formally completed, failed to transition from pilot to operational force. France and Finland suspended their units pending clarity on liability and enforcement powers. The Redress Coordination Unit, meant to be the directive’s backbone, remained ad hoc and underfunded.

### Frontier Leaps, Defences Fray

A **verification_widens** breakthrough accelerated AI in legal and clinical domains, pushing **ai_capability** to 69.5. Automated contract review and diagnostic coding now scale reliably, widening the gap between closed and open models. **openweight_capability** rose to 58.0, fed by replication of older tooling, but still lags.

**labour_displacement** deepened, with law and accountancy firms cutting graduate intake by over 60%. This, combined with **backlash_physical**, further eroded public trust. Sentiment dropped to 5.0 as media framed AI as both a job-killer and a threat to democratic accountability.

Washington’s **supply_chain_coercion** tightened further, with ASML halting servicing for mature-node machines across multiple EU customers. This not only hampers industrial modernisation but strips the EU of leverage, accelerating **eu_ai_sovereignty**’s decline to 5.0.

An **emergent_eu_cyber_mobilization** emerged as France, Germany, and the Nordic states launched joint red-team drills outside ENISA’s framework, signalling growing distrust in central coordination.
