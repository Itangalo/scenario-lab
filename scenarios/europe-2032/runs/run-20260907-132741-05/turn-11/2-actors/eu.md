## Priority
**Priority:** Enact Emergency Redress and Infrastructure Protection Directive  
This is our dominant effort because the Union is now under dual assault: from within, by systems that have betrayed public trust, and from without, by actors who see our fragility — both physical and institutional. The Trust Corps has finished, but redress remains unstructured, and protests have turned to sabotage. We must now act as protector of both people and infrastructure, or lose both.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as one of survival. The Dutch ombudsman’s ruling did not just expose a flawed algorithm — it exposed a broken covenant. When people say “you judged us guilty,” they are not speaking metaphorically. In Utrecht, Lyon, and now Berlin, data centres are being targeted not as symbols, but as sites of perceived injustice. The **Emergency Redress and Infrastructure Protection Directive** is our response: a binding legal instrument that compels member states to establish fast-track compensation pathways for victims of AI-driven denials in social services, policing, and judicial support — and to protect critical AI infrastructure with minimum physical and cyber hardening standards.

We are building this on Article 114 TFEU for internal market harmonisation, but invoking Article 122 on emergency economic governance to justify speed. The directive will tie access to cohesion and recovery funds to compliance, as the EIB did with the Cyber Shield. We are working through ECOFIN and COREPER II to secure qualified majority support, bypassing the stalled LIBE route. DG HOME will lead implementation, with Frontex logistics repurposed for rapid deployment of protection teams to threatened sites. The Redress Coordination Unit — already piloted in France and Finland — will be codified into law, with powers to freeze deployments and issue compensation orders. This is not optional. If we do not institutionalise redress now, the Union will be torn apart by the very systems meant to serve it.
