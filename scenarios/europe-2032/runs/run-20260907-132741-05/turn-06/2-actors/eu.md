## New measure

Deploy AI-hardened, pre-automated defence stacks across all Tier 1 critical infrastructure operators, integrating swarm response, zero-trust verification, and real-time model monitoring to neutralise autonomous threats before escalation.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 20–25 points by turn 8, reduce incident impact from AI-generated attacks by at least 60%, and restore public confidence through demonstrable protection of essential services  
Applies to: Member states, ENISA, national CERTs, critical infrastructure operators, EAAD, EIB

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative  
This is no longer a preparedness measure but a triage response: the cyber_major_incident has exposed that our critical systems are blind and brittle, and without immediate, automated defence, even sovereign compute will run on networks that fail under coordinated attack. Resilience is now the bottleneck of sovereignty.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the point where preparedness becomes non-negotiable. The cyber_major_incident—targeting public health records and energy grid coordination through a poisoned software dependency—has confirmed that offensive AI capabilities now outpace human-led response. We are activating the Emergency Resilience Corridors immediately under Article 122 TFEU, directing ENISA to deploy standardised, AI-audited defence stacks to all Tier 1 operators by turn 7. These will include automated patching, swarm detection using models trained on adversarial behaviour, and zero-trust verification of all model-generated code in critical pipelines. Funding of €45bn is being reprofiled from the EIB’s stalled SCAP envelope and the now-completed Tech Sovereignty Package, with disbursement tied to integration milestones.

The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme, though officially finished, remains only partially implemented. We are now treating its legal and energy-binding components as urgent precursors to the Cyber Shield: without dedicated, isolated power, hardened systems will still fail during sustained attacks. The Commission is advancing a Council Regulation under Article 194 TFEU to override ENTSO-E’s refusal, declaring the 5GW allocation a matter of strategic infrastructure. Germany and Austria will be offered binding arbitration on market distortions, but the microgrid developments in Brittany and northern Sweden will proceed under direct Commission supervision. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, but we are now conditioning final disbursements on integration with the Cyber Shield’s monitoring layer—sovereign compute must be secure by design, not just by location.

Public sentiment, now at 24.0, is nearing a breaking point. We are launching a coordinated communication effort through the EAAD and national civil protection agencies, framing the Cyber Shield as the first visible shield against AI-driven chaos. Labour displacement continues, but we are repurposing parts of the failed SCAP outreach network to fast-track displaced professionals into cyber resilience roles—funded through a new EIB transition window. This is not just defence; it is re-legitimation. If we cannot show that the Union protects, no amount of capability will save us.
