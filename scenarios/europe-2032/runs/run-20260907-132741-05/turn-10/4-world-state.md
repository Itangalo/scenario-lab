### A Crisis of Own Making

The ombudsman’s ruling on the Dutch social insurance AI — which systematically denied benefits using opaque behavioural scoring — ignited a firestorm across the EU. Protests flared in Utrecht, Lyon, and Brussels, framing AI not as a foreign threat but as an instrument of domestic injustice. The **automated_decision_scandal** shattered any remaining public faith, exposing that the systems meant to serve citizens had instead judged them guilty by algorithm.

The EU responded by reorienting the **Public Trust Corps**, adding a Redress Coordination Unit and expanding the AI Incident Dashboard to include systemic bias alerts. Pilots in France and Finland began, but trust did not follow. The damage was too deep, the timing too raw. Meanwhile, Poland and Spain continued to resist full adoption of the **Sovereign Cyber Shield**, despite ENISA’s compliance scorecard finally naming holdouts and the EIB linking recovery funds to integration.

### The Frontier Leaps, the Union Stalls

A **capability_jump** at the closed frontier reshaped cyber and code-generation tasks, though general competence rose only modestly to 68.0. Attackers now wield AI that adapts within minutes, exploiting zero-days with near-human speed. Open-weight models inched to 56.5, fed by distributed replication and lingering access to older tooling, but lagged far behind.

Worse, **supply_chain_coercion** struck at the heart of EU leverage: Washington forced ASML to restrict not just advanced, but mature-node lithography exports — crippling not only China but EU industrial modernisation. The Union’s sole chokepoint was turned against it. Sovereignty, already frail, eroded further.

### Political Collapse

With **eu_political_capital** at 5.0, the portfolio charge of −7 (two large measures, one priority) drained it to zero. The new **Mandate for Public Algorithmic Accountability Boards** — a large category 2 measure — earned no proposal bonus: though timely, it was judged too distant from immediate redress to offset the scandal’s cost. Public sentiment collapsed to 8.0, deepening the crisis.

The EU’s agenda froze. No new measure could gain traction. The Trust Corps, reshaped in desperation, became a symbol of reactive governance — not renewal.
