## Priority  
**Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative** — because the denial of frontier access this turn exposes our strategic vulnerability not as an abstraction but as a present operational failure, and because compute is the one bottleneck that, if broken, enables us to act on both of our mandates: without it, we cannot secure systems, evaluate models, or respond to crises on our own terms.

## In practice  
This turn, we close the **Systemic Integrity Watch**, which has now delivered its initial architecture: ENISA hosts a 24/7 coordination cell with live telemetry feeds from 17 national grid operators, five major health IT platforms, and the Schengen Information System’s automation layer. Emergency intervention protocols under NIS2 have been stress-tested in a simulated cascade failure in the Nordic energy market, and while response latency remains high, the detection layer is now live. The system flagged two anomalous coordination patterns in municipal water management AI this quarter — both false positives, but the pipeline works. We are already integrating these findings into the design of the next resilience cycle.

But the closure of that measure coincides with a shock: **eu_frontier_access_denied** lands like a power cut. Hospitals in three member states lose access to diagnostic AI models overnight. Research consortia building on foreign platforms are frozen mid-trial. No explanation is given. This is not a market failure — it is a sovereignty failure. We were prepared for scarcity, but not for exclusion. The Taiwan blockade gave us leverage in tools; now we must convert that into compute. Under Article 4 of the Chips Act and the State Aid Temporary Crisis Framework, we are mobilising €18bn in public co-investment to fast-track two sovereign AI foundries — one in France, one in Poland — with binding requirements for EU legal jurisdiction, weight security, and public evaluation access. We are negotiating with ASML to divert near-term EU-earmarked tooling from commercial to sovereign lines, and with national energy regulators to prioritise grid access. Germany resists, fearing inflation in industrial electricity prices; we offer them a seat on the governance board and first access to the output. This is not a subsidy — it is a rearmament. If AI is the substrate of power, we will not build our institutions on rented ground.
