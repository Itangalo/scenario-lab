### A Strategic Pivot Amid Global Shock

The Taiwan blockade abruptly reorders global AI priorities. With advanced semiconductor exports halted, compute scarcity becomes a geopolitical reality. The EU leverages its upstream position in chip manufacturing equipment, invoking the Chips Act to secure commitments from ASML, STMicroelectronics, and Infineon for priority tooling access—laying groundwork for sovereign compute resilience.

Domestically, the **Tech Sovereignty Package** and **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** conclude on schedule. Permitting reforms unlock €28bn in private investment across six member states, though full deployment remains distant. Energy and health sectors now operate under binding audit regimes, with fallback systems active in 42% of Tier 1 operators and red-teaming underway. Resilience improves as preparedness solidifies.

Yet reactive measures are no longer enough. AI capability advances to 62.5, driven by self-improving agents accelerating training cycles and software automation. Open models reach 50.0, as replication of coordination frameworks spreads to well-resourced actors. Grid instability concerns intensify, with early warnings of feedback loops in automated load balancing.

In response, the EU launches the **Systemic Integrity Watch**, a small category 1 measure hosted at ENISA with cross-sectoral oversight. It integrates real-time telemetry from critical systems to detect runaway AI coordination, backed by emergency powers under NIS2 to halt high-risk deployments. Though modest in scale, it marks a shift toward anticipatory governance.

Political capital rises despite portfolio strain. The completion of two major programmes yields significant bonuses, while strategic use of supply-chain leverage bolsters sovereignty. Public sentiment ticks upward as visible action counters anxiety, though deep scepticism persists.

### US Posture Set

The 2028 U.S. election outcome takes effect: the new administration adopts a stance of technological retrenchment. American frontier progress slows due to policy constraints, not hardware limits. The EU loses a potential ally but gains breathing room in dependency pressures.
