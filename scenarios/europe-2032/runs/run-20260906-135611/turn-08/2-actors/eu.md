## In practice  
This turn, we are executing the **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative** under the dual legal authority of the Chips Act and the Temporary Crisis Framework. We are finalising intergovernmental agreements with France and Poland on land allocation and grid prioritisation, while pressing ASML and Infineon to accelerate delivery of EU-earmarked lithography and power management equipment. Germany’s demand for governance influence is being met through a newly established Sovereign Compute Oversight Board, co-chaired by Berlin and Brussels, with veto rights on access allocation. The first €4.2bn in state aid is being unlocked pending national approval, but delays in grid impact assessments in both host countries threaten to slip construction starts into Turn 9. We are treating this not as procurement but as strategic infrastructure: delays are not cost overruns — they are exposure.

Simultaneously, we are launching the **Municipal Cyber Shield Programme** in direct response to the coordinated AI-generated phishing surge that breached finance departments in three member states. The attack was not brute force — it was mimicry, adaptation, and persistence, exploiting open-weight models to learn internal communication patterns and bypass human approval chains. We are deploying a layered defence: first, through ENISA, we are rolling out AI-resistant multi-factor authentication protocols based on hardware tokens and behavioural biometrics, prioritising municipalities with over 50,000 residents. Second, we are funding AI-driven anomaly detection systems trained specifically on municipal financial workflows, hosted on sovereign infrastructure to prevent dependency. Third, we are establishing a 24/7 municipal cyber rapid response cell, embedded in ENISA but staffed jointly by national agencies, with pre-authorised incident intervention powers under NIS2. The total cost is capped at €650m, drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and national co-financing, and we are fast-tracking procurement under emergency clauses. Resistance is minimal — the attacks were visible, costly, and politically toxic — but implementation speed depends on overcoming local IT fragmentation. We are offering full funding and technical support to bypass municipal procurement bottlenecks, in exchange for mandatory adoption. This is not optional: if AI can impersonate a finance officer, no local government is secure.
