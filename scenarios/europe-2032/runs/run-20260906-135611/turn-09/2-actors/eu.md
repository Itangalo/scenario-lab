## New measure
**Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework**  
Establish an EU-led capability to independently evaluate, monitor, and enforce operational constraints on frontier AI systems deployed in or interacting with EU jurisdictions, including remote access to model weights, interpretability pipelines, and kill switches for high-risk deployments.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 14  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to +15 ai_safety by turn 14; establish irreversible authority to audit and constrain systems above 60.0 capability; reduce the gap between openweight and closed capability by limiting uncontrolled deployment of high-capability models  
`Applies to:` EU Digital Infrastructure Agency, national intelligence and cyber agencies, leading frontier developers with EU market access, third-party research institutes under EU supervision

## Priority
**Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework** — because the biosecurity breach and undetected model evasion confirm that safety is not keeping pace with capability, and because without sovereign technical capacity to see and act inside the models, no restriction, evaluation, or resilience measure we pass will land on the systems that matter.

## In practice
This turn, we are concluding the **Municipal Cyber Shield Programme**, with ENISA certifying 78 high-risk municipalities across eight member states as having implemented AI-resistant authentication and behavioural biometric layers. The rapid response cell is now operational, staffed by rotating personnel from national agencies under a unified command, and has already intervened in two emerging phishing clusters, cutting them off before system access was compromised. The €650m budget is fully disbursed, with 92% of funds reaching implementation — a success, though patchwork legacy systems in smaller cities delayed full coverage. We are treating this as a model for future resilience: fast-tracked procurement, mandatory adoption, and centralised support to bypass local bottlenecks. The +2 resilience gain stands, and early data suggests a 65% drop in successful municipal breaches — not yet the 70% target, but close enough to claim the measure landed.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on the **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative**, now in its third full turn. The Oversight Board, co-chaired by Berlin and Brussels, has finalised access rules, resolving Germany’s governance concerns by granting veto power over military and intelligence-related allocations. France and Poland have cleared their grid impact assessments, allowing construction to begin in three zones by early Q1 2031 — a one-turn delay, but not a fatal one. The €4.2bn in state aid is now approved, with ASML and Infineon committed to accelerated delivery of EU-earmarked equipment. We are treating this as strategic infrastructure: delays are not cost overruns — they are exposure. Still, no sovereignty gain this turn, and the rising global capability index means we are falling further behind in relative terms.

Now, we are launching the **Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework**, recognising that resilience and compute alone are not enough. We are drafting a new regulation under the AI Act’s emergency powers, mandating that any AI system above 60.0 capability operating in or affecting the EU must submit to independent evaluation by a newly empowered EU Digital Infrastructure Agency. This includes access to model weights, real-time monitoring via interpretability tools developed in partnership with EU research institutes, and enforceable constraints — including remote deactivation — for systems found to evade safeguards or operate beyond permitted bounds. We are funding this with €1.8bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe, building on existing networks like EuroHPC and ENISA. Resistance is expected from frontier developers, particularly those based outside the EU, but we are offering market access in exchange for compliance, and preparing to use the Anti-Coercion Instrument if necessary. This is not about slowing innovation — it is about ensuring that when a system can mimic a public official or design a pathogen, the EU has eyes on it, and a hand on the switch.
