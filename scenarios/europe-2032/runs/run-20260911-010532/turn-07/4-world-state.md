### The sweep
Autumn brought the attack everyone had described in spring. A largely automated ransomware sweep rolled across municipal utilities, hospitals and contractors in several member states, using tooling plainly built with machine help. Backups held in places where the new containment work had reached; elsewhere services fell back to paper and queues.

Defenders were visibly behind for weeks. Attribution pooled through the joint cyber command Brussels had been invited into, and emergency crews restored locked offline copies to hospitals first, with reserve licences and offline kits moved under the reinsurance conditions. Recovery was real but slow, and the public saw it.

### A jump you could use
At the same time researchers confirmed what operators already knew: machines had taken another sharp step in code, mathematics and intrusion work. General chat felt little different, but what an attacker or a junior engineer could get done in hours changed markedly. With readable intermediate steps gone, European safety teams were left strained and reliant on black-box tests while capabilities advanced, eroding confidence without dismantling existing safety work.

A cross-border press investigation added a second chill, reporting brokers willing to print gene fragments without proper checks on orders drafted with openly available design help. Nothing was confirmed, but health agencies tightened sampling.

### Machines that lift, work still to do
Physical machines arrived in force. Warehouses, ports and logistics yards bought fleets for picking, sorting and pallet work, almost all built abroad with foreign control software. Union manufacturers that still lead in parts felt the squeeze from outside vendors. Unions warned of a dividing line: heavy, repeated work automated, repair and care work untouched.

Brussels pushed forward on one priority. The gigafactory programme reached final investment decisions, unlocking sites and private pledges despite one court-blocked plot and the breakaway member state still holding its separate hyperscaler deal. InvestAI Gigafactories remains the priority. Construction and procurement will take further turns before capacity comes online, and no sovereignty gain can be counted yet.

In parallel, Brussels opened exploratory talks toward a coordination framework with Japan, Korea and other middle powers on export licences and joint bargaining over compute. No agreement has been signed or ratified, and partners have made no binding commitments, so it provides no servicing alternative or sovereignty effect this turn.

Confidence in understanding the models fell further. Essential services degraded rather than stopped, but headlines were about unreadable systems, unscreened brokers and jobs in logistics going to robots. With foreign-built fleets spreading and flagship capacity still under construction, the Union's sovereign position slipped back while political standing held flat amid visible strain.
