By late 2029 a largely automated AI-built ransomware sweep hit municipal utilities, hospitals and contractors across several member states. Where containment work had reached backups held; elsewhere services fell back to paper and queues. Defenders lagged for weeks; attribution pooled through the joint cyber command and offline copies were restored to hospitals first under reinsurance conditions. Recovery was slow and public.

At the same time capabilities took another sharp jump in code, maths and intrusion — little change in chat, large change in what an attacker/junior could do in hours. With readable reasoning gone, European safety teams relied on black-box tests and confidence in understanding models fell further.

A press investigation reported brokers willing to print gene fragments without proper checks on openly-designed orders; unconfirmed, but health agencies tightened sampling.

Foreign-built warehouse/port robot fleets with foreign control software spread fast, squeezing EU parts leaders and automating heavy repeated work while repair/care work remained manual.

The gigafactory programme reached final investment decisions, unlocking sites and private pledges despite one court-blocked plot and the breakaway member state's separate hyperscaler deal; no capacity online yet. Exploratory talks with Japan, Korea and other middle powers on export licences and joint compute bargaining began but with no agreement or commitments and no sovereignty effect. Essential services degraded not stopped; sovereign position slipped while political standing held flat.
