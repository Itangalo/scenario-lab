### Loose models, dark reasoning, one defector
The first half of 2029 broke the uneasy balance Brussels had held through December.

A discontinuous advance in verifiable work — code, maths, intrusion operations — spread within weeks. Almost simultaneously a leading lab released weights only months behind its closed system. Hundreds of thousands of downloads in days put multi-hour offensive tooling on private hardware permanently. Municipal IT chiefs who had survived November on backups now faced the same playbook with faster hands behind it.

At the same time the frontier went dark. The best systems stopped producing readable intermediate steps. Vendors presented it as an efficiency gain from non-verbal representations; European safety researchers read it as the loss of their main oversight tool. Chain-of-thought monitoring, already thin, stopped working at once. Black-box tests and slow activation inspection were all that remained.

Washington changed hands. The new administration, elected on moratoriums and job guarantees, turned inward and slowed federal frontier work for political reasons. European officials found less pressure to align, but also a distracted partner and slower joint reporting.

Then a member state broke ranks. A government signed its own compute and service arrangement with a foreign hyperscaler on terms that undercut the common EU line, defended at home as pragmatism. The Commission threatened to withhold recovery and resilience funds unless supply was anchored in Europe and pointed to Japanese and Korean servicing offers as an alternative. The deal stayed.

Brussels held two things. The tech sovereignty package closed, unlocking permitting zones and private data-centre pledges, though one gigafactory site remained in court and the defection drained its political value. The joint cyber and bio-surveillance integration also finished landing: seconded teams, shared telemetry, hospital sequencing feeds tied to funding.

The new containment patch — offline kits, locked-down backups, telemetry-conditional reinsurance for towns and hospitals — only started. Mediation kept the flagship compute build alive without new money, but staff shortages, permit fights and late mutual aid dragged. Assistants remained popular; headlines about unreadable models and unscreened DNA brokers did not. By June, essential services degraded rather than stopped, but confidence that anyone understood the models had fallen sharply.
