### The strait closes
Winter brought a quarantine around Taiwan. Advanced chip shipments stopped, servicing queues froze, and every data-centre plan in Europe became a question of who would keep the machines running. Washington tightened export paperwork again. Volume licences stayed open to allies who aligned, but the terms were political and the prices were not.

Brussels answered with what it had: lenses, mirrors, lasers and chemistry no fab can do without. Trade officials linked export-licence posture on lithography and optics to servicing guarantees from Tokyo and Seoul, using disbursement conditions on the gigafactory build-out rather than new money. The coordination framework sketched in 2029 hardened into joint bargaining over compute and licences.

It half-worked. Servicing channels stayed open and a joint evaluation pool was announced, but American officials treated the new coordination effort as useful and junior, offering less than asked. The breakaway member state's separate hyperscaler arrangement kept undercutting the common line, and construction sites for the gigafactories sat waiting on parts that now arrived late.

### Weights that cannot be recalled
At the same time a new openly released model landed within months of the closed frontier. Hundreds of thousands of downloads in days put near-frontier code, intrusion and design help onto private hardware for good.

The consequences arrived quickly. Investigative reporters exposed brokers routinely printing gene fragments without proper checks on orders drafted with openly available design tools. Health authorities ordered emergency sampling, licence suspensions and mandatory audits. Hospitals and municipal utilities hit in the autumn sweep received offline kits and reserve licences first, and the containment patch built last year held in places it had reached.

Defenders were still behind. The new release could do in hours what had taken teams days, readable reasoning was gone, and safety teams worked from black-box tests. Essential services degraded rather than collapsed, but queues, paper fall-backs and headlines about unscreened synthesis dominated the spring. Protection sold better than ambition, and cohesion held narrowly while the new shield remained months from delivery.
