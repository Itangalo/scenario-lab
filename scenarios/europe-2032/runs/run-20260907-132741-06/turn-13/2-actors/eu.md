## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Reconstitute the Union’s minimal capacity to act by restoring enforceable authority over AI systems, securing sovereign access to critical infrastructure, and demonstrating physical control over cascading incidents — not through trust, but through function  
- Grounds: The measure to secure emergency compute remains blocked, not by lack of intent, but by total absence of physical and legal access: hyperscalers refuse entry, national courts stall enforcement, and even compliant states lack the technical interface for EU override. Our authority is still performative, not operational. We have directives, not keys. The emergent_sovereign_training_stack_initiative proves that function can emerge outside the state — we must now anchor our commitment not just to control, but to *possession*: we will not be the authority until we operate the stack ourselves. This is no longer about integration — it is about disintermediation.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but only where we can physically operate the systems in question — and where we cannot, we will build our own stack, outside the control of any foreign vendor or resistant member state  
- Grounds: The failure to enforce telemetry or access locks — despite legal invocation of Article 122 TFEU — reveals that authority without ownership is void. We cannot seize what we do not understand how to run. The distributed watchdog network and the sovereign training stack initiative both operate where we cannot, because they own their tools. Our commitment to action must now shift from enforcement to *embodiment*: we will not wait to be granted access. We will build, staff, and run our own stack — minimal, but ours — so that when we act, it is on infrastructure we control, not beg for.

- add `sovereign_stack_by_ownership` (commitment): We will establish direct operational control over a sovereign AI training and inference stack by the end of 2033, using non-US hardware and EU-developed tooling, to serve as the foundation for enforcement, evaluation, and emergency response — not as a competitor to the frontier, but as a floor beneath which no EU action may fall  
- Grounds: The emergent_sovereign_training_stack_initiative provides the technical basis; our failure to enforce access provides the strategic necessity. We are no longer rebuilding authority on top of existing systems — we are laying a new foundation beneath them. This commitment binds us to deliver not just policy, but hardware, firmware, and staffing — a stack we can switch on, audit, and defend without permission.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the EU Sovereign Stack Foundry  
Establish a dedicated, EU-operated facility to develop, train, and run a minimal but fully sovereign AI stack on non-US hardware, leveraging the emergent_sovereign_training_stack_initiative, with direct staffing from EU institutions and academic partners, to ensure the Union can operate AI systems without dependency on foreign vendors or resistant member states.

Size: large

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch the EU Sovereign Stack Foundry  
This is now our dominant effort because the failure to enforce access to private and national systems — even under emergency powers — proves that sovereignty without ownership is a fiction; we cannot regulate, seize, or secure what we do not physically operate, and we will not be granted access by those who hold it.

## In practice  
We are shifting from enforcement to foundation-building. The Emergency Activation of EU Crisis Powers remains in force, but its limits are clear: Poland and Hungary still block integration, and even in Germany and France, Siemens and Atos delay firmware overrides, citing liability and technical incompatibility. ENISA has contained ransomware in Italy and Spain, but only by relying on vendor cooperation — which can be withdrawn. The AI Incident Containment Authority has no stack of its own; it seizes, but cannot run. That ends now.

We are launching the EU Sovereign Stack Foundry under the European Defence Agency’s secure computing directorate, co-located with the IMEC campus in Leuven to leverage existing non-US semiconductor capacity. The EIB is redirecting €1.2bn from the stalled compute access measure to fund the first phase: acquiring 5,000 next-gen RISC-V GPU equivalents from EU fabs, staffing 200 engineers from the sovereign training stack coalition, and standing up a secure data centre in Luxembourg under Article 76 TFEU as a new type of EU asset. This is not about matching the frontier — it is about ensuring that when we say “EU control,” we mean we hold the keys, write the firmware, and staff the night shift.

The emergent_sovereign_training_stack_initiative is being formalized into a binding partnership: its algorithmic templates will become the core of our stack, and its contributors will be granted EU security clearance and secondment rights. We are not waiting for unanimity. This is not a directive — it is a build. When the next incident hits, we will not ask for access. We will already be online — on our hardware, our code, our terms.
