### A Crisis of Legitimacy Deepens

The EU launched the AI Accountability Tribunal in a moment of profound institutional weakness. Despite urgent rhetoric, the proposal faltered in the Council, where a coalition of member states—led by Poland and Hungary—demanded sweeping opt-outs, arguing that national sovereignty over public administration must prevail. Negotiations collapsed when the Commission refused to cede investigative authority. No agreement was reached, and the Tribunal failed to launch. The €10bn Remediation Fund remained unfunded, and the European Investment Bank paused its conditional lending pending legal clarity.

Public sentiment worsened sharply after the ombudsman’s report on the social insurance algorithm became public. Media coverage framed the EU’s response as evasive, and protests in Marseille, Lyon, and Budapest grew. The Public Trust Corps’ transparency drills were increasingly dismissed as performative, with turnout dropping below 15%. “We don’t want promises,” read one banner, “we want reparations.”
