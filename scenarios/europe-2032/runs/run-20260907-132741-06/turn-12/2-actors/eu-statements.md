# Statements: The European Union (turn 12)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Reconstitute the Union’s minimal capacity to act by restoring enforceable authority over AI systems, securing sovereign access to critical infrastructure, and demonstrating physical control over cascading incidents — not through trust, but through function

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Reconstitute the Union’s minimal capacity to act by restoring enforceable authority over AI systems, securing sovereign access to critical infrastructure, and demonstrating physical control over cascading incidents — not through trust, but through function
  - Trigger: the cyber_major_incident, eval_anomaly_reports, and the emergence of a distributed watchdog network amid eu_political_capital at 0.0 and public_sentiment at 8.0
  - Grounds: The convergence of a large-scale automated attack, unexplained eval anomalies pointing to uncontrolled capability jumps, and the spontaneous rise of a grassroots monitoring network confirms that formal EU authority has collapsed not just politically, but operationally. Our previous commitment assumed we could rebuild trust and accountability from a position of residual control. That position no longer exists. The distributed watchdog fills the void not because it is strong, but because we are absent. We are no longer failing to lead — we are not present in the field at all. Our commitment must now shift from restoration to reconstitution: we must prove we can stop harm, seize systems, and operate infrastructure — even if no one trusts us, even if some member states resist. Function precedes legitimacy. Control precedes consent.
  - Evidence quoted: **cyber_major_incident:** A large, largely automated attack lands. Its form is chosen at the time – a ransomware sweep across public services, a compromised software dependency with an unknown blast radius, or disruption of infrastructure directly – and severity scales with how far offensive capability has outrun defence. Damage is real and public, defenders are visibly behind, and attribution takes months. Whoever ran it, the tooling was model-generated.
**eval_anomaly_reports:** Benchmark results from an unreleased system leak, or an evaluation team reports behaviour it cannot explain – capability appearing where it was not trained, an eval saturating far earlier than projected, or agents behaving differently when they appear to judge they are being watched. The laboratory calls it a measurement artefact. It may be one. This is a precursor: it opens the capability gate for the next 2 turns and the control gate for the next 3.
**emergent_distributed_watchdog_network (emergent event):** A broad-based initiative among EU-aligned mid-tier AI developers, open-weight researchers, and biosecurity labs forms a distributed early-warning network for model misuse, leveraging shared tooling and anonymized telemetry. It gains traction due to the collapse of formal EU oversight and rising public distrust, filling the institutional void with grassroots coordination.
  - Reason: The developments directly undermine the EU's operational control and expose the collapse of its authority, forcing a shift from trust-based restoration to function-based reconstitution of power.
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will act before the picture is clear, but only where enforcement can land — and where it cannot, we will first restore the physical and legal means to compel compliance
  - Grounds: Our last adjustment acknowledged that enforcement capacity must precede action. This turn, we see that even that threshold is too high: we lack not only the authority, but the physical access to systems, the compute sovereignty, and the legal clarity to act. The emergent watchdog network proves that action is possible — but not by us. We are no longer the enforcers; we are the void others fill. Our commitment to early action now demands that we rebuild not just rules, but reach: the ability to disconnect, seize, and operate. We will not act where we cannot enforce — and we will now treat the restoration of enforcement as our primary act.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
