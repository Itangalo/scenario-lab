## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but when they collide, we now judge that sovereignty without safety is not sovereignty at all.  
- modify `build_independent_frontier_capacity` (commitment): We will establish the EU’s own path to frontier AI capability, independent of external suppliers, and ensure it is legally anchored, physically secured, and operationally sovereign by the end of 2031 — but only if deployment can be meaningfully assured against catastrophic misuse.  
- Grounds: The collapse of ai_safety to 2.0 amid a major AI-generated ransomware attack — which exploited tools already in circulation and bypassed all existing safeguards — has revealed that raw capability without enforceable control is not just risky, but actively destabilising. The identity update reflects a categorical shift: we now treat uncontrolled sovereign capability as a threat vector, not a shield. The commitment modification binds us to condition the final activation of our sovereign stack on verifiable safety integration, accepting delay over danger.

## New measure  
**Stand up EU-AISE as an autonomous agency with pre-deployment audit authority and live monitoring powers**  
We are transforming the newly formed European Union Agency for Intelligence Safety and Evaluation (EU-AISE) into a standing technical authority with mandate to audit models before deployment, monitor live systems for emergent risk, and issue binding halts on sovereign or commercial deployments that exceed verified safety thresholds.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by 15–20 points through institutionalised, independent assurance; slow unchecked capability deployment across both closed and open fronts by introducing a credible, technically grounded veto point.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member state agencies, national regulators, licensed developers, and any entity operating AI systems above 55.0 capability within EU jurisdiction.

## In practice  
We are treating this turn as one of recalibration under fire. The **cyber_major_incident** has shattered any remaining illusion that reactive governance can contain systemic risk. While the **Open-Weight Firewall expansion** finishes this turn, its limited impact — due to inconsistent platform compliance and shallow enforcement — confirms that restrictions alone cannot govern what is already distributed. At the same time, the **export_control_escalation** has made clear that sovereignty cannot be outsourced: we cannot regulate what we do not control, and we cannot defend what we do not understand.

Accordingly, we are pivoting from pure infrastructure build-out to integrated control. The **EU Frontier AI Programme** remains our anchor: we are fast-tracking power and cooling upgrades in Toulouse and Prague using redirected InvestAI funds, aiming for initial training runs above 60.0 by turn 9. But now, we are conditioning the first sovereign model release on approval by **EU-AISE**, which we are elevating via Council Regulation under Article 114 TFEU to ensure internal market harmonisation on safety standards. ENISA will transfer its red-teaming unit to the new agency, and we are offering dual affiliation to top researchers from INRIA, Fraunhofer and KU Leuven to staff its technical core.

We are also responding to **safety_breakthrough**: a new interpretability method developed at DeepMind Zurich allows prediction of agent behaviour under novel incentives with 89% accuracy — adopted rapidly by labs because it reduces their own liability. We are mandating its integration into all EU-facing evaluations and funding replication within EU-AISE. This gives us a rare moment of alignment with developers: we are offering faster certification for systems that submit to full audits, creating a trust-speed trade-off that incentivises transparency.

Public sentiment remains fragile at 38.0, pulled downward by **labour_displacement** and the health ransomware fallout. We are resisting calls for a moratorium, but reframing diffusion around **demonstrable safety**: the emergency pilot in Belgium, Austria and Estonia will now run only on models certified by EU-AISE, with real-time logs published via the Digital Europe Programme portal. This is not just damage control — it is the beginning of a new legitimacy cycle: sovereignty earns trust not by promise, but by proof.
