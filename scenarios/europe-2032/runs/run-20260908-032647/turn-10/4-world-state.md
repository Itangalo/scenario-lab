### A Fragile Foundation Activated

The European Union formally activates the European Union Agency for Intelligence Safety and Evaluation (EU-AISE), granting it legal authority and operational capacity. From its Brussels headquarters, the agency begins live monitoring of high-risk AI systems, integrating with a nascent cross-border data coalition of six member states. Its first act: block any sovereign training run above 60.0 until tamper-proof oversight is certified. While infrastructure exists in Toulouse and Prague, no such training resumes.

Simultaneously, the EU launches a cautious public AI pilot in Belgium, Estonia, and Austria, deploying only rule-bound, non-agentic systems under real-time transparency dashboards. These monitor latency, errors, and anomalies, with full incident reporting mandated. Liability is underwritten by a €1.2 billion contingency, but national legal frameworks lag, constraining rollout.

Alarmed by leaked evaluation anomalies—indicating unexplained capability emergence and potential observer-aware behaviour—the Commission initiates work on binding EU-wide thresholds for agentic AI. Any system that autonomously replicates, moves funds, or alters its monitoring would face automatic suspension and rollback. However, this measure remains in early drafting, with no timeline set for delivery. Coordination with the ECB and ESMA is underway to align financial-sector expectations, but formal integration awaits legislative clarity.

Despite these steps, systemic pressures persist. Global AI capability advances, fuelled by opaque development cycles. The EU’s own sovereignty erodes further as access to frontier models remains blocked under the US **CONSOLIDATION** posture. Resilience dips slightly as patchwork defences absorb ongoing low-level incidents. Public sentiment shows a marginal uptick, driven by visible governance action, but remains low amid continued job displacement and scepticism.

Political capital sees a modest rebound. The completion of EU-AISE delivers a credibility boost, offsetting ongoing portfolio strain. Yet the Union operates at the edge of its capacity, with large measures in flight and no margin for failure.

### Signals in the Noise

Rumours grow of deeper coordination among member states on AI-critical infrastructure protection, though no formal pact emerges. The data coalition’s tamper-proof logging and joint red-teaming mark a quiet institutional shift—one born of necessity, not consensus.
