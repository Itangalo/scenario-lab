### Capability Creeps, Safety Erodes

AI capability advances to 59.5 as automated verification in law, engineering and clinical coding accelerates training loops. The frontier now reliably produces systems that execute multi-day professional workflows in several domains, with only patchy human oversight. This qualitative shift—codified in the **verification_widens** trend—fuels rapid capability growth, but assurance lags. No new safeguards land on shipped models, and the gap between what AI can do and what anyone can verify deepens. **ai_safety** declines to 16.0, reflecting opaque reasoning, poor monitoring, and the absence of independent pre-deployment review—yet the erosion remains gradual, consistent with sustained pressure rather than a sudden collapse.

### EU Moves to Secure Sovereign Compute Access

In response to growing dependency risks, the European Commission proposes emergency pre-positioning contracts to secure next-generation AI compute from EU-anchored providers. The initiative, framed under Article 122 TFEU, would offer EIB-backed guarantees to hyperscalers and chipmakers in exchange for binding commitments to prioritise EU access and resist foreign extraterritorial claims. Intel Ireland, OVHcloud, and the EuroHPC JU consortium are invited to early talks. However, the proposal triggers immediate debate: fiscal hawks in the Competitiveness Council question the budgetary implications, and no funding mechanism is yet approved. The measure is designated the EU’s priority for the coming period, but full adoption and financing remain pending. Implementation is delayed by at least one turn, with no guarantees activated this period.

### Measured Gains Amid Mounting Strain

The **Sovereign AI Testbed** and **Bio-Secure AI Monitoring Programme** both complete this turn. The testbed enables cross-border red-teaming, modestly improving preparedness; **resilience** rises to 44.0. The bio-monitoring programme integrates AI-driven genomic screening at ports and research hubs, earning a small sentiment boost as public health agencies take visible action. **public_sentiment** climbs to 45.0, aided by **adoption_success**—a rare win where EU-deployed AI cuts hospital wait times and is publicly credited.

Political capital erodes under sustained pressure. Five large or medium measures remain in flight, and the addition of a new priority risks overextension. While the new initiative is framed as a reprioritization rather than a net expansion—absorbing focus from the Tech Sovereignty Package—the portfolio charge remains high. The **campaign_backlash** further strains legitimacy, as grassroots resistance to AI infrastructure spreads. Though no measure fails outright, momentum stalls. **eu_ai_sovereignty** falls to 17.0 as global capability growth outpaces domestic build-out, and no new compute comes online.
