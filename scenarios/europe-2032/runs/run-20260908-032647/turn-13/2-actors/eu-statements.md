# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now recognise that inaction in the face of blocked paths is itself a decision, and one we will no longer treat as passive. In light of the **taiwan_blockade** and **capability_jump**, we further affirm that when strategic access is weaponised and capability advances discontinuously, we will treat leverage over supply and control over deployment as inseparable from survival. We act not because we see the full path, but because waiting for clarity is no longer an option.
- `build_independent_frontier_capacity` (commitment): We will secure the EU’s access to frontier AI capability through sovereign infrastructure, talent, and legal anchoring, but no longer expect to establish a full EU-led frontier training capability independent of external suppliers by the end of 2031. Given sustained denial of access to critical components and frontier models, we now explicitly condition our frontier access on enforceable legal and security arrangements with third-party providers, not on technical self-sufficiency. However, with the **taiwan_blockade** severing global compute supply, we now pivot from securing access to *mutualising scarcity*: the EU will treat its existing compute and supply-chain assets as strategic reserves, to be allocated only in exchange for full model access, red-teaming rights, and joint control over deployment in critical domains.
