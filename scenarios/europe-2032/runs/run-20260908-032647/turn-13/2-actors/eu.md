- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now recognise that inaction in the face of blocked paths is itself a decision, and one we will no longer treat as passive. In light of the **taiwan_blockade** and **capability_jump**, we further affirm that when strategic access is weaponised and capability advances discontinuously, we will treat leverage over supply and control over deployment as inseparable from survival. We act not because we see the full path, but because waiting for clarity is no longer an option.  
- Grounds: The **taiwan_blockade** has transformed semiconductor access into a hard security constraint, freezing all non-Chinese frontier development and exposing the EU’s total dependence on external compute chains. Simultaneously, the **capability_jump** demonstrates that attackers now possess verifiable, domain-specific superintelligence in cyber and code — tools that can exploit this very fragility. With `eu_ai_sovereignty` at 6.0 and `ai_safety` at 6.0, we are blind and bound at the worst possible moment. This revision embeds supply leverage and operational control into our definition of decisive action under uncertainty.  
- Trigger: taiwan_blockade and capability_jump

- modify `build_independent_frontier_capacity` (commitment): We will secure the EU’s access to frontier AI capability through sovereign infrastructure, talent, and legal anchoring, but no longer expect to establish a full EU-led frontier training capability independent of external suppliers by the end of 2031. Given sustained denial of access to critical components and frontier models, we now explicitly condition our frontier access on enforceable legal and security arrangements with third-party providers, not on technical self-sufficiency. However, with the **taiwan_blockade** severing global compute supply, we now pivot from securing access to *mutualising scarcity*: the EU will treat its existing compute and supply-chain assets as strategic reserves, to be allocated only in exchange for full model access, red-teaming rights, and joint control over deployment in critical domains.  
- Grounds: The **taiwan_blockade** has rendered all assumptions about scaling compute obsolete. There will be no new frontier training runs outside China for years. The EU’s value is no longer in its market size but in its upstream control — ASML, imec, STMicroelectronics — and its ability to deny access to others. We must now bargain from strength, not beg for inclusion. This shift turns our weakness in downstream capability into leverage upstream: no access to EU lithography, no path to next-gen chips. Our sovereignty now rests on coordinated denial, not just sovereign hosting.  
- Trigger: taiwan_blockade

This turn, we finalise the closure of the public AI pilot, publishing its full dataset and safety logs across all three test sites. The 38% latency reduction and zero breaches are real, but without a legal framework to scale, the results remain isolated. We are now redirecting the MFF resilience envelope to fund emergency hardening of energy and healthcare networks, deploying AI-driven intrusion detection systems co-developed with the **emergent_regional_defense_pact**, though their effectiveness is limited by the lack of shared telemetry standards and real-time kill switches.

The binding sovereign hosting regulation moves into trilogue negotiations under Article 114 TFEU, with the Commission proposing mandatory hosting, model access, and kill-switch integration for all systems above 65.0 capability. We are offering compliance fast-tracks under the Digital Markets Act in exchange for early adoption, but resistance from US hyperscalers is firm — they see the move as expropriation, not regulation. Behind the scenes, we are leveraging the **middle_power_coalition** to prepare a coordinated response: if they refuse hosting, we will refuse lithography. The Dutch and German governments are on board, but France hesitates, fearing retaliation in aerospace and cloud services.

Simultaneously, we activate emergency talks with Tokyo, Seoul, and Taipei, using the **middle_power_coalition** framework to align export controls on advanced chips and design tools. Our offer is simple: no unilateral deals with frontier labs unless all coalition members gain red-teaming access and veto rights over high-risk deployments. We are pooling remaining EU-accessible compute into a mutualised reserve, to be allocated only to providers who submit to EU-AISE monitoring and joint safety evaluation. This is not self-sufficiency — it is survival through shared scarcity. The Taiwan blockade has ended the era of abundance; our response must match it.
