We are operationalising the Bio-Secure AI Monitoring Programme with urgency. The ECDC is finalising detection protocols for AI-assisted genome design, drawing on the bio_uplift_findings to define signature anomalies in sequence patterns. Frontex is integrating these into screening at major data hubs and biological import points, while the EDA is adapting them for military bio-surveillance. We are mandating reporting from all platforms hosting genome-design models—like Bactobase and GeneForge—under Article 16 of the AI Act, with penalties for non-compliance. To overcome member state resistance, we are fast-tracking implementation through the Health Security Committee and offering co-funding from the EU4Health programme, tying compliance to priority access in future bio-research grants.

At the same time, the Sovereign AI Testbed is entering its red-team phase. ENISA, in coordination with national CERTs, is scheduling the first live exercises for Q4, targeting cross-border energy and port systems. German grid operators and the Port of Rotterdam have agreed to participate, with the EU covering 70% of costs through the Digital Europe Programme. Legal disputes in Poland and Hungary over Article 196 TFEU are being addressed through bilateral consultations, backed by the threat of withholding cohesion funds if critical infrastructure providers are excluded. The testbed uses EU-held models fine-tuned from open weights at 40.0, ensuring we simulate threats within our control.

Now, with the capability_jump confirming that offensive cyber and narrow engineering advances are accelerating outside verifiable safety, and with campaign_security_hawk hardening the US stance against any sharing, we cannot assume access to frontier models will endure. That is why we are acting now to cap openweight_capability: we are proposing a licensing regime that prohibits the public release of models above 50.0 unless they undergo safety review and demonstrate containment of dual-use risks. This applies to all developers serving the EU market, including foreign open-source actors. We will work with GitHub, Hugging Face, and EU-based hosting providers to enforce takedowns and reporting. The European AI Office will lead, supported by a new technical unit under DG CONNECT. This is not a ban on open research—it is a firewall at the point where openness becomes uncontainable risk.
