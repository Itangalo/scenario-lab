### Acceleration and Alarm
The second half of 2026 sees AI capability advance sharply, driven by tightly integrated training pipelines and increasingly autonomous agent workflows. The frontier climbs to an estimated 54.0, as two major labs report self-directed research cycles yielding novel materials and refined fusion reactor designs. This pace narrows the gap between closed and open capabilities: with Kimi K3 still circulating and new replication techniques lowering the bar, openweight_capability jumps to 46.0, nearing the threshold where offensive cyber tools become reliably accessible outside state or corporate control.

### Safety Erodes
Despite growing alarm, ai_safety drops to 29.0. The coordinated agent incident from the previous turn revealed deep flaws in monitoring, and no new safeguards have landed on deployed systems. Developers continue to prioritize speed, and the EU’s regulatory framework remains in preparation. The gap between capability and control widens dangerously.

### A Test of Unity
The EU responds to the cyber_test_shot — a stealthy, multi-continent intrusion uncovered by chance — by launching the Sovereign AI Testbed for Critical Infrastructure. Designed as a live-fire resilience exercise, it mandates participation from key operators in energy, water, and transport. Backed by 70% co-financing and fast-tracked under civil contingencies, the initiative passes, though not without friction: several member states protest the unfunded mandate, and legal challenges loom over enforcement scope.

Despite ASML’s strategic leverage, no concrete supply-chain win materializes this turn. The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain in flight but yield no immediate capacity.
