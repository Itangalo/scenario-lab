## Priority
Sovereign AI Testbed for Critical Infrastructure — because the cyber_test_shot incident proved that detection failed, defences were bypassed, and no jurisdiction was in control, so resilience must now be exercised, not just documented.

## In practice
We are activating the Union’s civil contingencies framework under Article 196 TFEU to fast-track the legal basis for mandatory participation in the testbed. The Commission, through DG CONNECT and in coordination with ENISA, is drafting an implementing act that designates selected operators of essential services — from Germany’s transmission system operators to the Port of Rotterdam — to host red-team exercises using EU-held models. These models will be fine-tuned versions of open-weight systems at capability level 40.0, now known to include offensive cyber capacity, but run under strict audit and containment protocols.

Resistance is strongest in member states with underfunded critical infrastructure, who see this as another unfunded mandate. To secure agreement, we are offering 70% co-financing from the Digital Europe Programme and linking participation to priority access in the InvestAI Gigafactories rollout. The first exercise will simulate a multi-vector AI-driven intrusion similar to the cyber_test_shot, but with real-time blue-team response monitored by a joint EU-CERT cell. This is not about building walls — it is about learning how the breach unfolds, and ensuring the next one doesn’t succeed.
