### The Cost of Control

The EU completed the InvestAI Gigafactories programme, bringing four exascale-capable sites online — but only two are operational. Delays in grid integration and staffing in Italy and Poland mean the full compute dividend is deferred. The EU Frontier AI Programme absorbs the momentum, with funding redirected to accelerate Toulouse and Prague, but training at capability 60+ remains two turns away. Some early open-weight model iterations benefit from partial infrastructure access, allowing a modest gain in openweight_capability, though far below frontier pace.

A sweeping ransomware attack, auto-generated by an advanced AI, cripples health systems across three member states. Response is fragmented, and recovery slow. Though the Sovereign AI Testbed’s detection modules are fast-tracked into national CERTs, the breach proves that reactive integration cannot close the gap. Resilience inches up (+1) from partial absorption, but the psychological toll is heavier.

Public sentiment drops sharply as citizens link the attack to uncontrolled AI. Trust erodes further when the Commission’s emergency pilot — meant to showcase sovereign AI safety — is delayed by legal disputes over data sovereignty. Automation fears, already simmering, now dominate discourse.

The US enacts tiered export controls: the EU is excluded from automatic access to next-gen chips. ASML confirms shipment delays. This *export_control_escalation* triggers a −2 sovereignty hit: the Union lacks substitute capacity. Sovereignty falls to 15.0 despite gigafactory completion, as global capability advances and dependence persists.

The EU announces an expansion of the Open-Weight Firewall Initiative to include real-time detection and automated takedowns. However, implementation will take time — platforms resist automated screening, and capability assessment remains inconsistent. The expansion is treated as a scope widening of the existing measure, not a new one, and therefore does not qualify for a new proposal bonus. With no safety protocols on shipped models and offensive tools proliferating, ai_safety erodes significantly, falling to 2.0. The drop reflects weakened enforcement and delayed pilots, but not a systemic dismantling of safety infrastructure.

### Flickers of Unity

Amid the strain, informal EU data-sharing talks show signs of deepening. Several member states begin synchronising cyber alerts, and joint red-teaming exercises are scheduled. While no formal pact has yet been ratified, the coordination marks a step toward greater cohesion. Discussions continue on mutual support for AI-critical infrastructure, but binding commitments, automated escalation, or coordinated sanctions remain potential future developments — not yet activated. The possibility of a future regional defense pact is noted, but its activation remains contingent on further alignment and external pressure, not assumed. No new event gates are logged at this time; the conditions for deeper integration remain emergent but unspecified.
