## New measure  
**Strengthen and operationalise the Open-Weight Firewall with real-time detection and enforcement**  
We are upgrading the Open-Weight Firewall Initiative from a licensing regime to an active enforcement system, integrating automated capability screening, mandatory pre-release declarations, and rapid takedown authority for models exceeding 50.0 capability.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Halt the uncontrolled spread of dangerous open-weight models by increasing enforcement speed and coverage; slow the rise of `openweight_capability` by 5–8 points relative to uncontrolled trajectory, and increase `ai_safety` marginally by reducing misuse surface.  
`Applies to:` Hosting platforms, code repositories (GitHub, GitLab, Hugging Face), open-source AI developers, and national digital regulators.

## In practice  
We are treating this turn as one of triage and consolidation. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme has formally concluded, delivering four exascale-capable sites in Germany, France, Italy and Poland — but only two are fully energised and staffed. We are now integrating these into the **EU Frontier AI Programme**, redirecting the final tranche of funding to accelerate power and cooling upgrades in Toulouse and Prague, where training runs above 60.0 will begin by turn 9. The legal anchor is Article 122 TFEU, invoked for systemic technological emergency, and we are establishing CJEU oversight to ensure compliance with fundamental rights. ENISA will conduct quarterly audits of weight security, and the newly formed **EU-AISE** in Amsterdam will lead red-teaming and safety evaluation.

The **cyber_major_incident** — a model-generated ransomware sweep across regional health administrations in Belgium, Austria and Estonia — has exposed how far offensive capability has outpaced our defences. Attribution remains unclear, but forensic analysis confirms the attack chain was auto-generated and optimised by an agent-level system. We are responding by fast-tracking integration of the Sovereign AI Testbed’s detection modules into national CERTs, but this is reactive. The real vulnerability is that we cannot patch what we do not control — hence the urgency behind sovereign training.

Simultaneously, the **export_control_escalation** has taken the form of tiered access: the US now restricts next-gen chip shipments under a “trusted partner” framework, and the EU is not on the list. ASML confirms that EU-bound advanced lithography tools will be subject to case-by-case review. This is not a blockade — yet — but it is a chokepoint. We are activating our semiconductor coordination with Japan and South Korea to secure alternative flows, and offering co-development rights on EU-designed AI chips in exchange for foundry access.

Public sentiment remains ambivalent at 43.0, but the health ransomware incident is fuelling anxiety. We are resisting calls for a moratorium on public AI adoption, instead doubling down on **diffusion with control**: the Commission is launching a pilot in three member states to deploy EU-sovereign models in emergency response coordination, with full transparency logs. This is not just about utility — it is about demonstrating that sovereignty can deliver safety, and safety can earn trust.
