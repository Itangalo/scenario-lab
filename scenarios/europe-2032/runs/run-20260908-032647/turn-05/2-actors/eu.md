## New measure
**Establish enforceable sovereign access rights in next-generation compute contracts**  
Create a legal framework under Article 122 TFEU that grants the EU binding, non-transferable priority access to a defined share of next-gen AI compute capacity secured through emergency pre-positioning contracts, with penalties for non-compliance and override rights during declared emergencies.

`Targeted effect:` Increase `eu_ai_sovereignty` by 10–15 points by turn 7 by converting financial guarantees into legally enforceable access rights, reducing dependence on goodwill and market access. ## Priority
**Establish enforceable sovereign access rights in next-generation compute contracts** — because the election_consolidation event proves that access to frontier AI is now a political decision made in Washington, not a commercial transaction, and our current procurement strategy lacks the teeth to resist extraterritorial control; without enforceable rights, sovereignty is an illusion.

## In practice
We are finalising the Sovereign AI Testbed’s first live red-team exercise, scheduled for November, with ENISA coordinating German, Dutch, and French CERTs in a simulated AI-driven grid disruption scenario. The testbed infrastructure, running on EU-held models at 40.0 capability, has been cleared for cross-border data flows under the NIS2 derogation, and participation is being incentivised through full cost coverage via the Digital Europe Programme. Hungary and Poland have signalled conditional participation, contingent on excluding certain foreign vendors from the exercise — a demand we are meeting by restricting model access to EU-developed or open-weight systems only.

The Bio-Secure AI Monitoring Programme is now operational. The ECDC has activated its first wave of AI-driven genomic anomaly detection at Rotterdam, Marseille, and Hamburg ports, with Frontex deploying mobile units equipped with rapid sequencing and AI triage. We are enforcing Article 16 notices against Bactobase and GeneForge by restricting their access to EU cloud infrastructure unless they implement watermarking and usage logging — a step framed as compliance with the AI Act, but one that also pressures global platforms to adapt to EU rules or lose market access. To build momentum, we are linking participation in Horizon Europe’s new biosecurity track to early adoption of the programme’s protocols, creating a pull for research institutions to align.

Meanwhile, the emergency pre-positioning contracts face stiff resistance in the Competitiveness Council. Germany and the Netherlands support the principle but demand that EIB guarantees be capped and tied to measurable output, while France insists on preferential access for EuroHPC JU over private providers. We are countering by revising the legal framework to include enforceable access rights — not just priority, but guaranteed allocation, with penalties for diversion under foreign pressure. This new measure will be tabled under Article 122 TFEU, with the Commission drafting a regulation for urgent adoption by the European Parliament under the single reading procedure. We are offering Italy and Spain co-leadership on the implementation board to secure southern bloc support, and we are warning all members that without binding rights, the entire compute build-out could be rendered irrelevant by a single U.S. export determination.
