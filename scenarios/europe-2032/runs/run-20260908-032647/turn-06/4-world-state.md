### The Frontier Slams Shut

In early 2029, the European Union was abruptly locked out of the most advanced AI model overnight. Hospitals relying on its diagnostics, ministries using its planning tools, and research labs dependent on its simulations were left stranded—no explanation given, no appeal possible. The cutoff, sudden and total, exposed the fragility of the Union’s digital foundations. Days later, a powerful open-weight model was released publicly, downloaded hundreds of thousands of times. Its capabilities, once confined to secure labs, were now permanently distributed.

The shock triggered a strategic reversal. The EU formally retired its two-year commitment to secure frontier access, acknowledging it had failed. Instead, it launched the **EU Frontier AI Programme**, a €35bn sovereign initiative to build an end-to-end AI stack under EU control by 2031. Three secure sites—Toulouse, Prague, Helsinki—were designated for training frontier models, with infrastructure hardened against foreign interference.

But the pivot came at a cost. Germany and the Netherlands resisted the state-led approach, warning of duplication and market distortion. France accepted only with conditions favouring defence applications. Public agencies welcomed the ambition, but the political strain was evident. The **Open-Weight Firewall Initiative** was strengthened, yet enforcement lagged as platforms struggled to assess model capability.

Resilience improved slightly, bolstered by the completion of two live cyber and bio-monitoring programmes. Yet rising automation in logistics and healthcare triggered fresh displacement fears, and sentiment dipped. With no new safety protocols on shipped models and capability advancing rapidly, **ai_safety** fell further into crisis.

The US administration took office under a posture of **CONSOLIDATION**, rationing frontier access by alliance tier—confirming the EU’s status as dependent, not partner.

### Sovereignty’s Narrow Path

The EU’s sovereignty metric held steady despite global advances, spared a deeper fall only by the completion of resilience measures. But without immediate capacity gains, the denial of access outweighed progress. Political capital eroded under the weight of seven ongoing initiatives, including the new priority. The portfolio charge was severe, and the moment of clarity brought no reprieve—only a harder road ahead.
