# Statements: The European Union (turn 6)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `build_independent_frontier_capacity` (commitment): We will establish the EU’s own path to frontier AI capability, independent of external suppliers, and ensure it is legally anchored, physically secured, and operationally sovereign by the end of 2031.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now recognise that without sovereign control over the means of AI development, both mandates collapse into irrelevance.
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event confirms that capability without sovereignty is an illusion: the Union was cut off from the leading model overnight, with no appeal, and critical functions in healthcare and governance were disrupted. This is not a failure of safety or resilience alone—it is a failure of agency. Our two foundational mandates cannot be pursued in parallel if one undermines the very possibility of the other. From now on, preventing harm must include securing the means to act—because without them, prevention is surrender.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late, but we will no longer act without a binding mechanism to secure enforceable access and a credible path to independent capability.
  - Grounds: The **openweight_frontier_release** has narrowed the gap between closed and open capability to within 5–10 points, meaning dangerous capabilities are now permanently distributed. Our previous strategy assumed we could regulate risk at the frontier while relying on goodwill for access. That assumption is broken. We must now act not only with urgency, but with enforceable rights and independent capacity—otherwise, our actions are performative.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **retire** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Trigger: the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event and the failure to activate enforceable access rights despite two years of effort
  - Grounds: The commitment to “secure sovereign access to frontier AI capability before it is allocated by others” has been overtaken by events. Access has already been denied, and no mechanism we built in time prevented it. The frontier is no longer a resource to be accessed—it is a weapon to be matched or endured. We failed to convert guarantees into rights, and now the moment has passed. We cannot claim continuity with a commitment that reality has invalidated.
  - Evidence quoted: **eu_frontier_access_denied:** The Union is cut off from the leading model at short notice, wholly or by nationality of user. No detailed reason is given, there is no appeal, and the immediate practical effect lands on hospitals, ministries and firms that had built on it.
  - Reason: The denial of access directly invalidates the possibility of securing sovereign access to frontier AI through external allocation, making the original commitment unattainable.
- **add** `build_independent_frontier_capacity` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will establish the EU’s own path to frontier AI capability, independent of external suppliers, and ensure it is legally anchored, physically secured, and operationally sovereign by the end of 2031.
  - Grounds: With access revoked unilaterally and open-weight models nearing frontier levels, the only way to reconcile our two mandates is to build what we cannot rely on others to provide. This is not a shift from cooperation to isolation—it is a shift from dependence to agency. The EU must run its own training runs, secure its own weights, and defend its own infrastructure, or it will not determine its own future.
