## Statement changes
* modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now recognise that without sovereign control over the means of AI development, both mandates collapse into irrelevance.
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event confirms that capability without sovereignty is an illusion: the Union was cut off from the leading model overnight, with no appeal, and critical functions in healthcare and governance were disrupted. This is not a failure of safety or resilience alone—it is a failure of agency. Our two foundational mandates cannot be pursued in parallel if one undermines the very possibility of the other. From now on, preventing harm must include securing the means to act—because without them, prevention is surrender.

* modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late, but we will no longer act without a binding mechanism to secure enforceable access and a credible path to independent capability.
- Grounds: The **openweight_frontier_release** has narrowed the gap between closed and open capability to within 5–10 points, meaning dangerous capabilities are now permanently distributed. Our previous strategy assumed we could regulate risk at the frontier while relying on goodwill for access. That assumption is broken. We must now act not only with urgency, but with enforceable rights and independent capacity—otherwise, our actions are performative.

* retire `two_year_commitment`
- Trigger: the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event and the failure to activate enforceable access rights despite two years of effort
- Grounds: The commitment to “secure sovereign access to frontier AI capability before it is allocated by others” has been overtaken by events. Access has already been denied, and no mechanism we built in time prevented it. The frontier is no longer a resource to be accessed—it is a weapon to be matched or endured. We failed to convert guarantees into rights, and now the moment has passed. We cannot claim continuity with a commitment that reality has invalidated.

* add `build_independent_frontier_capacity` (commitment): We will establish the EU’s own path to frontier AI capability, independent of external suppliers, and ensure it is legally anchored, physically secured, and operationally sovereign by the end of 2031.
- Grounds: With access revoked unilaterally and open-weight models nearing frontier levels, the only way to reconcile our two mandates is to build what we cannot rely on others to provide. This is not a shift from cooperation to isolation—it is a shift from dependence to agency. The EU must run its own training runs, secure its own weights, and defend its own infrastructure, or it will not determine its own future.

## In practice
We are declaring the end of dependence. The **Establish enforceable sovereign access rights** measure has formally concluded this turn, but its failure to activate contracts in time has confirmed what the **eu_frontier_access_denied** incident made plain: legal frameworks without independent capacity are paper shields. We are now acting on the conviction that the only enforceable right is one you can enforce yourself.

We are launching the **EU Frontier AI Programme** under Article 122 TFEU and the Digital Europe Programme, with a €35bn initial allocation from the Multiannual Financial Framework and EIB co-financing. The programme will integrate existing assets—EuroHPC JU’s exascale systems, IMEC’s chip research, and the Sovereign AI Testbed’s red-teaming infrastructure—into a unified sovereign stack. We are designating three secure sites—Toulouse, Prague, and Helsinki—as sovereign training zones, where models above 60.0 capability will be trained under EU jurisdiction, CJEU oversight, and ENISA monitoring. Access will be restricted to vetted researchers and public agencies, with model weights stored on air-gapped infrastructure.

We are meeting resistance from Germany and the Netherlands, who argue that this duplicates private investment and risks crowding out innovation. We are countering by offering them co-leadership on the evaluation and safety track, with a new public institute—**EU-AISE** (European Union AI Safety and Evaluation Institute)—to be hosted in Amsterdam. France supports the move but demands that 40% of training runs prioritise defence and civil protection applications—we are accepting this with a sunset clause to 2032. We are also negotiating with ASML and Infineon to secure priority access to next-gen lithography for sovereign sites, leveraging our earlier coordination with Japan and South Korea to frame this as a stability contribution, not a protectionist move.

The Open-Weight Firewall Initiative remains in force, but we are now treating it as a containment measure, not a control strategy. With openweight_capability at 53.0 and closing fast, we are accelerating the licensing regime and expanding cooperation with GitHub, GitLab, and Hugging Face to detect and delay releases above threshold. But we know the clock is against us: the frontier is open, and the only way to lead is to build.
