## Statement changes

- modify `build_independent_frontier_capacity` (commitment): We will secure the EU’s access to frontier AI capability through sovereign infrastructure, talent, and legal anchoring, but no longer expect to establish a full EU-led frontier training capability independent of external suppliers by the end of 2031.  
- Grounds: The US **CONSOLIDATION** export controls, reinforced this turn, have made clear that access to the necessary chips, models, and tooling cannot be assumed. Despite completed compute infrastructure in Toulouse and Prague, no sovereign training run above 60.0 has resumed, and there is no credible path to achieving independent frontier capability within the original timeline. The commitment remains, but its original deadline and scope are no longer tenable.  
- Trigger: export_control_escalation and the absence of any resumed sovereign training runs despite operational infrastructure.

## New measure

**Establish a sovereign EU evaluation and red-teaming capability for frontier models, with access to weights and training data under EU jurisdiction, to enable pre-deployment audits and live monitoring of emergent risk**  
We are building our own technical capacity to evaluate, stress-test, and monitor the most capable AI systems—whether deployed in the EU or trained abroad—so that enforcement of thresholds and safety claims rests on independent verification, not developer assurances.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by 15–20 points by enabling independent detection of dangerous capabilities and emergent behaviour; strengthen `eu_ai_sovereignty` by 10–15 points by anchoring evaluation capability within EU jurisdiction.  
`Applies to:` All models above 55.0 capability operating in or affecting the EU, with weight access secured through legal anchoring and hosting agreements.

## Priority

**Establish binding EU-wide intolerable-risk thresholds for agentic AI systems, with automatic suspension triggers and mandatory model rollback on violation** — because the **eval_anomaly_reports** and the ongoing risk of uncontrolled adaptation demand enforceable red lines now, and this measure is the first step toward making them bite.

## In practice

This turn, we complete the activation of EU-AISE as a fully operational agency. The Council Regulation under Article 114 TFEU is in force, and the agency has assumed its mandate: pre-deployment audits, live monitoring, and binding authority to halt deployments. Its first directive is already in effect—no sovereign training run above 60.0 will proceed without certified tamper-proof oversight. We are integrating EU-AISE’s monitoring stack into the regional data coalition’s tamper-proof logging network, with six member states now sharing red-teaming results and audit data. The technical core—drawn from ENISA, INRIA, Fraunhofer, and KU Leuven—is operational, and we are finalising SLAs with national regulators to ensure compliance enforcement.

The public pilot in Belgium, Estonia, and Austria is live, though still narrow in scope. Only rule-bound, non-agentic systems are deployed, all under real-time EU-AISE dashboards that publish latency, error rates, and anomaly flags. We are working with national parliaments to fast-track liability frameworks that preserve accountability without paralysing adoption. The €1.2 billion liability contingency is now administered through the MFF, and we are coordinating public communications via the Digital Europe Programme, including monthly transparency reports and citizen forums. Public sentiment remains fragile, but the visibility of action is beginning to shift the narrative.

We are now drafting the Council Implementing Regulation on intolerable-risk thresholds. The red lines are clear: autonomous replication, unauthorised financial movement, and monitoring tampering will trigger automatic suspension and rollback. National regulators are being instructed to implement detection scripts by turn 11, and EU-AISE will begin compliance sweeps in turn 12. We are engaging the ECB and ESMA to align financial-sector AI rules with these thresholds. The message is consistent: the EU does not wait for catastrophe. The line is drawn, and it must be enforceable. But to enforce it, we need our own eyes on the models—hence the new measure to build sovereign evaluation and red-teaming capacity. Without it, our thresholds are paper. With it, we begin to close the gap between mandate and muscle.
