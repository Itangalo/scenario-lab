Autumn 2028 grid drive declared complete with stockpiles shifted to national budgets, but legacy systems stayed exposed.

In February 2029 a poisoned software update spread via automated tooling through municipalities, hospitals and port operators, freezing systems, cancelling elective care and forcing emergency dispatch to paper; Brussels responded with cyber agency bulletins and spare-parts convoys, but attribution stayed open and coverage focused on failure.

During recovery one member state broke ranks with a separate cheap foreign compute/model supply deal, undercutting the EU joint Washington ask; Commission opened a coercion review and offered joint licensing terms as return path, threatening to proceed without the holdout. The five reserved gigafactory sites stayed empty with permits only; Dutch-Japanese-Korean coordination remained the sole funded track without delivering machines.

By June 2029 power-price protests merged with outage anger, polls turned hostile to AI buildout, and Brussels privately conceded procedures held while capacity and cohesion were lost.
