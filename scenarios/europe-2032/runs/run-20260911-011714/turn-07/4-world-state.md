### The second sweep
Autumn brought a second large automated attack, different in vector but familiar in effect. This time it moved through a shared software dependency into city administrations, clinics and logistics firms. Systems locked, appointments vanished, and recovery disks from the previous winter's stockpiles had to be pulled out again. Emergency teams triaged and isolated networks, publishing daily fixes without naming a culprit. Attribution stayed open for months.

The pictures again did the damage: handwritten charts in hospitals, closed counters at town halls. Polls already hostile turned harder. Talk of pausing new data-centre grid hookups until local votes spread from town to town, leaving permitted sites idle.

### Machines in the warehouse
While crews were still rebuilding servers, a quieter shift arrived in ports and warehouses. Commercial robots for picking, sorting and pallet work, built mostly abroad and running foreign control software, began to appear in tenders. Managers praised reliability on repetitive tasks; unions warned that the easy jobs were disappearing while care, repair and messy construction work remained untouched. Europe's own machine builders felt squeezed between cheap hardware from the east and software licences from the west.

A contested genome-study added unease. Researchers claimed a model had helped sketch a viable infectious design; others called the methods flawed and the publication reckless. Health authorities in Brussels raised sampling quietly under existing protocols, without announcing a new programme.

### A thin lifeline
The one advance was diplomatic. The supply-chain coordination table with Dutch, Japanese and Korean partners was signed into a standing framework for export-licence alignment and joint bargaining on compute. It brought no machines, but it gave Brussels its only funded leverage after the breakaway member state's separate cheap compute deal stayed in place. The coercion review stayed open, the joint licensing offer stayed on the table, and the five factory plots stayed empty.

By December exhaustion set the tone: defences held procedures together, cohesion frayed, and the public mood settled into open resentment.
