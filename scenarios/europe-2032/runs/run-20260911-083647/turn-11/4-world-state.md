### Holding on paper
Through autumn 2031 the Union ran on the one instrument it still had. Health emergency teams coordinated triage, wastewater testing and clinic rotas; civil-protection funds paid overtime; technical repair crews kept appointment and dispatch systems limping on offline kits and older European-hosted models from the cleared list. No new law was attempted, no factory money voted, no retaliation for the winter cutoff.

It worked after a fashion. Containment held, though respiratory clusters flared again in two districts in October before burning out. Hospitals did not close. Ports moved freight, slowly, with paper manifests re-typed at night. Mayors who had praised the crews in spring now spoke of exhaustion — staff quitting, volunteers not returning, sentinels blinking without anyone to read them.

### A farther storm
Attention in Brussels drifted east. Extended manoeuvres around the strait, rising shipping insurance, expelled diplomats — familiar, but harder to dismiss this time. The Commission quietly asked insurers and chip buyers what a longer disruption would mean, comparing notes with partners in Seoul, Tokyo and elsewhere. No stockpile was moved, no joint statement issued. With no money and no trust to spend, watching was all that could be afforded.

At home, a press investigation tracing design tutorials for the winter agent to a widely mirrored open model fed angry calls for takedowns of hosting providers. No order was given; lawyers argued over jurisdiction while providers left the files up.

By December general models had inched forward again, and machine-assisted exploit writing had become routine background noise for defenders. Understanding of the most capable systems slipped further. The Union ended the year degraded but not broken, with zero margin restored.
