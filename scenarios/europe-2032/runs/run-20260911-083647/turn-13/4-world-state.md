### Living off the stock
July to December 2032 was a holding operation, and Brussels said so openly. No new programme was proposed, no vote called, no protest lodged. The work was what had already been paid for: wastewater sentinels kept lit under civil-protection funding, clinic rota overtime honoured, and small repair teams moving between hospitals and port offices with offline kits and older European-hosted models.

It worked, after a fashion. A late-autumn rise in respiratory illness filled triage tents in three cities but did not close wards. Manifests kept moving, slowly, on paper and scanned forms. Mayors praised the crews in public and warned in private that nurses and technicians were leaving faster than they could be replaced.

### Relief and distance
In clinics, the most noticed change came in foreign boxes. Tailored therapies designed on frontier systems abroad reached more ordinary pharmacies, for rare cancers and resistant infections. Families were grateful; doctors prescribed them while noting they could not inspect, reproduce or adapt the models behind them.

Researchers passed around second-hand accounts of an unreleased foreign system behaving strangely in testing. The European safety institute filed the reports and did nothing further. It had no staff to spare for an inquiry it could not enforce.

Chip buyers felt the squeeze tighten. Quotas thinned again, lead times stretched by weeks, prices crept up. A few hospital IT upgrades were deferred to next year. Insurers added another margin to strait shipping. Trade officials compared notes quietly with counterparts in Seoul and Tokyo, moved no stockpile, issued no statement.

By December, the Union was intact and exhausted. Services ran, cures arrived, and every critical tool felt one step further from European control. Trust did not recover, but blame eased slightly: with nothing promised, little was broken.
