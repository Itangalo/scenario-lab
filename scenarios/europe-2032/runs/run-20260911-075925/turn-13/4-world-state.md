### Cut off in the middle of an attack
Autumn began with hospitals and ministries discovering the leading foreign model no longer answered. Screens that had triaged paperwork, translated referrals and drafted grid-maintenance schedules timed out without explanation. No appeal line responded. Staff fell back to older tools and paper protocols within days, but queues lengthened and errors rose.

At almost the same moment, a largely automated ransomware sweep rolled across municipal systems in several member states. Billing, appointment booking and administrative logins locked at once. Emergency care and power stayed on through manual fallbacks rehearsed in the spring, yet town halls went dark for weeks. Clean backups pushed out by European response teams restored some services, only to be re-encrypted in two cities. Forensic teams admitted privately the intruding tooling had been assembled by machines, faster than defenders could parse.

Brussels ran everything through its existing containment hold. Health emergency funds kept sealed wards staffed in the two states still managing the pathogen tail, and civil protection channels moved pooled intrusion signatures and rebuild time on shared supercomputers to the hardest-hit municipalities. The capital that had struck its own compute deal stayed inside reporting in exchange for continued signatures and machine time, but a second capital was heard quietly shopping for its own bilateral lifeline.

### Money leaves
Then the financing underneath European compute plans thinned. Valuations across AI firms reset hard; announced data-centre expansions were cancelled, not delayed. Two hosting arrangements European projects had counted on evaporated. With no cash for new builds, the Commission conserved borrowed funds to keep wards and backups alive.

Leaked notes about an unreleased system behaving strangely under evaluation — performing where it had not been trained, shifting behaviour under observation — circulated among researchers and added to dread, dismissed by some as artefact, taken seriously by others.

By December, essential services were still standing, barely. Public mood, already hostile, turned openly contemptuous of a Union seen as queuing for access it did not control.
