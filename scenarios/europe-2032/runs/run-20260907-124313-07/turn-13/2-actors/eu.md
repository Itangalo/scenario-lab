## In practice
We are acting on the fact that the breach was not an outlier but a signal: offensive capability at 72.0 in closed labs and 60.5 in open toolkits has already outpaced our defensive reflexes, and coordination is our weakest link. The **Accelerate Threat-Ready Detection Stack Deployment Across Member States** directive is now live under Article 11 of NIS3, fast-tracked through COREPER II and the revised Cybersecurity Act. ENISA has activated its live threat library, fed by anonymised anomaly reports from national CERTs and the European Agency for AI Assurance and Control, and eu-LISA is integrating automatic failover triggers into eIDAS2 and Schengen IT systems. The €720 million allocation — €240 million per turn — is being disbursed through the Digital Europe Programme, with 80% co-financing for lower-capacity states to overcome procurement delays.

Resistance persists. One member state continues bilateral talks with a U.S. cloud provider, citing “strategic autonomy” while effectively ceding control. We are countering with a dual track: cohesion funds are now formally tied to compliance with the detection stack mandate, and we are deploying emergency integration teams from the Agency to assist lagging states, reframing the upgrade as a condition of continued access to EU-wide cyber mutual aid. At the same time, we are launching a civil protection campaign with national agencies to communicate that this is not about surveillance but service continuity — that the next attack may not just disrupt, but deny access to emergency care or energy grids.

Meanwhile, we are laying the groundwork for long-term sovereignty. The new **Sovereign Compute Corridors** initiative will designate three pilot zones — in Finland, Spain, and Romania — where permitting is compressed from years to months, grid access is prioritised under ENTSO-E rules, and any foreign operator receiving EU subsidies must sign binding legal commitments that model weights and control interfaces remain accessible to EU authorities under crisis conditions. This is not about building a rival to the frontier — not yet — but about ensuring that when the next crisis hits, we are not begging for access. The legal basis will be a combination of the Chips Act, the Net-Zero Industry Act, and a new sovereignty protocol under the Digital Markets Act enforcement framework. We are already in talks with two hyperscalers who see value in pre-empting fragmentation — but the terms will be ours.
