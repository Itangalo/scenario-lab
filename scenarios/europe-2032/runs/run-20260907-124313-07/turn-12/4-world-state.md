### A Breach That Hardens Resolve

The adaptive cyber intrusion that crippled public services across five EU nations left deep scars — but also momentum. The **Secure Critical Infrastructure Against AI-Driven Cyber Threats** measure, a large category 6 initiative, concluded this turn, delivering detection upgrades and red-teaming protocols to pilot sectors. Though patchy in uptake, its partial deployment helped shorten containment time in follow-on incidents, earning a resilience boost.

In response, the EU launched **Accelerate Threat-Ready Detection Stack Deployment Across Member States**, a binding NIS3 directive mandating AI-resistant detection systems across all essential services. Fast-tracked with €720 million in funding and 80% co-financing for vulnerable states, the measure aims for universal rollout by turn 15. ENISA’s live threat library, fed by anomaly reports, began limited operation, and emergency deployment teams were dispatched to three lagging states.

Yet cohesion frays. One member state continues bilateral negotiations with a U.S. cloud provider, invoking “strategic autonomy” to justify bypassing EU coordination. Tensions flare in COREPER II, where smaller states warn against overreach. The push for unity risks deepening divides.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Erodes

Global AI capability rose to 72.0, driven by closed-loop training breakthroughs in a leading lab’s unreleased model. No safety mechanism was applied to deployed systems, and with no new assurance measure landing, ai_safety fell further to 6.0. Interpretability remains shallow; developers still resist data sharing with the European Agency for AI Assurance and Control.

Openweight capability climbed to 60.5, as adaptive exploit toolkits derived from leaked evasion logic spread through underground forums. While not yet frontier-grade, their modularity and self-tuning features suggest distributed offensive capacity is nearing the threshold of autonomous operation.

Public sentiment edged up slightly to 25.0, as visible recovery efforts and the promise of stronger safeguards tempered backlash — though protests at data centre sites persist.
