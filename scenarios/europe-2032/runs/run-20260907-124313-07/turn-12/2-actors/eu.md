## In practice
We are acting on the fact that the recent cyber intrusion — adaptive, persistent, and exploiting a foundational identity dependency — did not merely expose vulnerabilities but revealed a systemic lag in our collective response reflex. The initial **Secure Critical Infrastructure Against AI-Driven Cyber Threats** measure, while necessary, was designed as a pilot-scale mandate with opt-in momentum. That model failed: two member states delayed implementation, one pursued a bilateral cloud deal with a U.S. provider, and procurement bottlenecks stalled deployment in three others. The breach was not just in code — it was in cohesion.

This turn, we are replacing that approach with a binding directive under Article 11 of NIS3, fast-tracked through COREPER II and the Cybersecurity Act revision. The new **Accelerate Threat-Ready Detection Stack Deployment Across Member States** measure mandates adoption of ENISA-certified AI-resistant detection stacks — including behavioural baselining, synthetic red-teaming modules, and real-time anomaly correlation — by all operators of essential services in health, energy, and democratic infrastructure by turn 15. We are allocating an additional €720 million from the Digital Europe Programme (€240 million per turn) to cover full deployment costs, with 80% co-funding for lower-capacity states. ENISA will operate a live threat library, updated weekly from anonymised incident feeds and anomaly reports from the European Agency for AI Assurance and Control, while eu-LISA integrates automatic failover triggers into eIDAS2 nodes.

Resistance remains. The bilateral-minded member state argues for “sovereign cloud sovereignty” — a contradiction we cannot indulge. We are meeting it with a mix of pressure and support: cohesion funds are tied to compliance, and we are offering emergency deployment teams from the Agency to accelerate integration. At the same time, we are launching a targeted communication campaign with civil protection agencies to reframe the upgrade not as a cost, but as a guarantee of service continuity. The breach changed the clock. We are no longer building resilience for a future threat. We are rebuilding trust in real time.
