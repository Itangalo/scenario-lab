## In practice
We are acting on the fact that the last six months have delivered not one but two shocks: the annulment of a regional election due to synthetic media manipulation, and now a major AI-driven cyber incident — a supply-chain compromise of a widely used identity management dependency, which triggered cascading access failures across public service portals in five member states. The attack was not novel in vector, but in speed, scale, and evasion: it adapted in real time to patching attempts, mimicking legitimate traffic and delaying detonation until peak service hours. It was model-generated, and our defences were outmanoeuvred by an intelligence we did not legally recognise as such. This is not a failure of vigilance. It is a failure of preparedness.

This turn, we launch **Secure Critical Infrastructure Against AI-Driven Cyber Threats**, a large category 6 measure under the NIS3 framework and co-financed through the Digital Europe Programme (€480 million over three turns). The measure mandates that all operators of essential services in health, energy, and democratic infrastructure implement AI-resistant detection stacks by turn 13, including adversarial monitoring, behavioural anomaly baselining, and quarterly red-teaming with AI-generated attack profiles. ENISA will coordinate the development of standardised threat libraries drawn from declassified incidents and evaluation anomalies, while eu-LISA integrates failover protocols into the eIDAS2 backbone. We are convening the COREPER II committee next week to fast-track adoption, offering member states 70% co-funding and technical support through the newly operational regional nodes of the European Agency for AI Assurance and Control.

Resistance is expected — particularly from mid-sized municipalities and regional providers citing cost and complexity — but we are coupling this with emergency flexibility in cohesion funds and a temporary waiver on procurement rules for certified vendors. We are also launching a public communication campaign, coordinated with national civil protection agencies, to explain the necessity of these upgrades in plain terms. This is not about building walls. It is about ensuring that when the next autonomous attack comes — and it will — our systems degrade gracefully, respond coherently, and recover without strategic loss. The cyber_major_incident has reset the timeline. We are no longer preparing for a future threat. We are repairing a present breach.
