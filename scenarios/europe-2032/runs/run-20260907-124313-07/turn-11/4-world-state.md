### A Breach That Changes the Clock

A cascading cyber intrusion—originating in a compromised identity management library—paralyzes public service portals across five EU countries. The attack adapts in real time, evading detection and striking at peak hours. It is not merely fast; it learns. Defenders are outmaneuvered by an intelligence that behaves like a system, not a script. The EU declares a joint threat, but containment takes days, not hours.

In response, the EU launches **Secure Critical Infrastructure Against AI-Driven Cyber Threats**, a large category 6 measure under NIS3, backed by €480 million. It mandates AI-resistant detection, red-teaming with synthetic adversaries, and failover integration into eIDAS2. ENISA will lead threat library development, leveraging anomalies from recent evaluations. Fast-tracking begins in COREPER II, with 70% co-funding offered.

Yet resistance flares. Two member states warn of implementation burdens; a third initiates bilateral talks with a U.S. cloud provider, bypassing EU coordination. The move hints at fragmentation, though no formal defection occurs.

The **Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme** concludes, having deployed neuromorphic edge systems in 78% of targeted nodes. Localized inference is now operational in grid controls and health hubs, reducing cloud dependency. The success lifts sovereignty, but only modestly.

Meanwhile, a frontier lab’s unreleased model shows unexplained eval saturation—capability emerging where none was trained. The lab dismisses it as noise. Days later, a new open-weight model, derived from leaked evasion logic, spreads across dark web forums. It is not state-grade, but it is adaptive. Early analysis suggests it could elevate open-weight capability, but widespread deployment and refinement will take time. The diffusion is monitored, but not yet dominant—raising concern without triggering a leap.

Public sentiment dips further. A protest blocks construction at a data centre in Bavaria; police intervention turns tense. The image circulates widely: AI as occupier, not tool.

### Sovereignty Gains, Safety Slips

The agency for AI assurance becomes operational, but its reach is tested immediately. Developers resist data sharing, citing competitive risk. No breakthrough in shipped-system control emerges. With capability rising and no safety measure landing, assurance erodes.

Informal discussions among four member states on aligning AI threat response protocols are noted, but no formal pact is established. The talks are preliminary and do not constitute a new EU-level initiative. They may inform future coordination, but for now, they remain exploratory—background motion, not policy.
