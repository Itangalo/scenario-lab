Autumn crisis gave way to fragile recovery then a split over cures: after spring stabilization, backlogs cleared and medics withdrew, tailored therapies for untreatable conditions reached ordinary clinics with early queues and ministerial tours.

The cures depended on foreign models and compute outside Brussels-negotiated contracts. A large member state formalised its own hyperscaler supply deal for doses as pragmatism; others called it an auction of the common line. The Commission's joint procurement pact offering pooled negotiation, second-source clauses and first-delivery slots if the separate deal was folded in stayed unsigned through December.

Money left the sector amid the split: valuations reset hard, announced data-centre expansions cancelled, financing structures evaporated, and promised machine labour underdelivered — warehouse robots worked only where floors were rebuilt around them.

Biology defences held: detection network and mutual-aid stocks finished rollout, routine routing and referral labs cleared cases without panic, and no second wave came, claimed as quiet success.

Earlier fractures persisted: the Washington-Beijing weights/bio-tools pact with thin verification, Brussels sidelined; March agentic incident contained; interpretability advances in EU checklists; illicit clusters of leaked near-frontier model recirculating despite seizures; two AI factory sites still frozen; trust recovered only slightly as patients asked why Europe could administer cures it could not supply.
