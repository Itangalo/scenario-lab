## New measure
`Digital Markets Act Compute and Energy Audit Framework (category 2, transparency and reporting)`  
Establish a mandatory disclosure regime under the Digital Markets Act requiring dominant AI providers to report their EU energy consumption and verified idle compute capacity, with remote auditing enabled through standardized telemetry interfaces.

- `Category: 2 (Transparency and reporting)`
- `Size: small`
- `Finishes on turn: 14`
- `Targeted effect:` Increases visibility into private-sector compute footprint; supports future leverage over infrastructure. Aims to raise *eu_ai_sovereignty* by +3–5 if fully implemented, contingent on enforcement follow-through.
- `Applies to:` Dominant digital platforms operating in the EU, particularly those providing large-scale AI inference or training services.

## Priority
**Digital Markets Act Compute and Energy Audit Framework** — because with sovereign compute stalled and safety enforcement blocked, establishing baseline visibility into concentrated infrastructure is the only actionable lever left to prevent total opacity in a domain that shapes our strategic autonomy.

## In practice
This turn, the Commission finalizes the draft implementing act for Article 5 of the Digital Markets Act, expanding its scope to include energy use and idle compute capacity as metrics of market fairness. DG COMP, in coordination with ENISA and ACER, defines technical standards for remote verification of telemetry data from provider-owned clusters, ensuring that reported figures are not self-certified but auditable via secure API access. The framework will be proposed to the Digital Markets Committee under comitology, with adoption expected by mid-2032 if no member state triggers referral to Council.

We frame this strictly as a competition measure: hoarding of computational infrastructure without transparency distorts access for smaller innovators and public actors alike. By avoiding security or safety language, we sidestep immediate resistance from member states wary of centralizing control. Still, pushback emerges from two net-importer states concerned about retaliation from hyperscalers, and from DG GROW, which warns of investment chilling effects. To secure qualified majority support, we offer a phased rollout—only aggregated, anonymized data will be public initially, with granular disclosures reserved for EU agencies until turn 14. No penalties attach yet; compliance is incentivized through faster market authorization for cooperating firms in upcoming AI Act conformity assessments. This is not control—but it is sight, and for now, sight must precede power.
