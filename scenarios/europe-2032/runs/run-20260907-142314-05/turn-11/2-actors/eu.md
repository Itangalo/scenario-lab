## In practice
This turn marks the formal completion of the Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration. ENISA finalises the “Trusted Resilience Node” certification under NIS2, granting Lyon, Barcelona, Helsinki, Bologna, Ljubljana, and Tallinn full operational status. These cities now exchange threat models, coordinate incident response, and maintain shared access to audited AI tools for crisis simulation and infrastructure management—using existing disaster recovery envelopes and Structural Funds, not new appropriations. The system operates without central command: decisions are made locally, but standards are EU-backed, and interoperability is verified quarterly through joint exercises coordinated by the European Emergency Response Coordination Centre.

The Commission uses this quiet success to reframe its leverage strategy. With state-led compute mobilisation blocked and evaluation authority undermined, we pivot from control to visibility. DG COMP, supported by ACER and ENISA, advances the Digital Markets Act review, framing energy and spare compute audits as a competition fairness issue. We draft Article 5 guidance stating that dominant AI providers must disclose their EU energy footprint and allow remote verification of idle capacity—framed not as security mandate but as transparency necessary to prevent anti-competitive hoarding of critical digital infrastructure. No enforcement yet; no penalties. But the groundwork is laid: if private compute will not be ours, it must at least be legible, and potentially reachable when thresholds shift. This is not sovereignty as ownership, but sovereignty as reach—and for now, it is all we can afford.
