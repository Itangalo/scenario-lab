### A Union Straining to See

Global AI capability advances again, reaching **63.0**, as frontier labs refine agent coordination and training pipelines. The pace remains steady within the terminal zone, but each increment widens the gap between what exists and what the EU can monitor, let alone govern. Openweight capability rises modestly to **54.5**, supported by incremental diffusion of fine-tuned variants and broader availability of distributed tooling. While open models are gaining autonomy in narrow tasks, they have not yet reached the threshold for self-directed vulnerability exploitation—though the trend is clear and concerning.

The European Union, reeling from deepening institutional fractures, announces the **European Evaluation Shield Activation and Data-Sharing Incentive Scheme**—a large, ambitious measure aimed at restoring the Union’s capacity to evaluate AI risks. Framed as a reciprocity play, it offers member states access to red-teaming resources, sovereign evaluations, and cyber upgrades in exchange for joining a secure, Luxembourg-based Trusted Evaluation Node. Binding data protections under a new tier of the Cyber Resilience Act are introduced to assuage sovereignty concerns.

However, the proposal does not constitute implementation. Two member states immediately signal resistance, citing constitutional limits on data sharing with central bodies. The legal base under Article 114 TFEU faces early challenges from national courts, and DG JUST warns of protracted disputes. No state joins the Node this turn, and no operational capacity is established. The initiative remains in formation, with comitology procedures only beginning.

Meanwhile, the ongoing **Digital Markets Act Compute and Energy Audit Framework** continues to stall in comitology, still unadopted. Investor anxiety grows as enforcement looms, and net-importer states dig in. No progress is made on resilience or safety infrastructure.

Political capital, already critical, erodes further. With a new measure now proposed while the prior one remains unresolved, the portfolio charge applies fully: −2 for the ongoing framework, −1 for maintaining a priority, totaling −3. The new initiative yields no immediate benefit, and no proposal bonus applies: while institutional fracture is real, it was not a sudden event, and the response is seen as overdue rather than timely. Public sentiment holds at **36.0**, marked by quiet resentment and media focus on bureaucratic infighting.

Sovereignty declines to **29.0**, as capability growth outpaces any tangible gain. The promise of future evaluation does not offset the ongoing erosion of shared capacity. The EU enters 2032’s final months blind to the systems it seeks to govern—watching the frontier move, but unable to see it clearly.
