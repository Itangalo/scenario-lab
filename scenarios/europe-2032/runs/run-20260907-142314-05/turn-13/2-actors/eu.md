## Priority
**European Evaluation Shield Activation and Data-Sharing Incentive Scheme** — because the emergent institutional fracture makes clear that transparency without evaluation capacity is blind, and sovereignty without safety insight is hollow — this measure restores the Union’s ability to know what it must govern.

## In practice
We act on the fracture: two more states have opted out of data sharing with the Evaluation Shield, citing sovereignty concerns, and the shield remains a skeleton without teeth. We cannot wait for consensus to emerge; we must create the conditions for trust by changing the incentives. Under the legal base of Article 114 TFEU and using funds from the Digital Europe Programme and the Horizon Europe AI Safety Cluster, we launch a binding offer: any member state that joins the Evaluation Shield data-sharing framework by turn 15 will receive priority access to EU-funded red-teaming resources, sovereign model evaluations, and cyber resilience upgrades through ENISA. We pair this with a new tier of data classification under the Cyber Resilience Act, ensuring that shared evaluation data is processed only in EU-certified secure enclaves, with audit trails and penalties for misuse. The European Commission, through DG CONNECT and in coordination with the European Defence Agency, will stand up a Trusted Evaluation Node in Luxembourg by turn 15, staffed by vetted personnel from across the Union, to host and process shared data. Resistance from federal-minded states is met not with coercion but with reciprocity — we offer not just obligation but capability in return. This is not a bypass; it is a bridge. And if the US administration shifts posture next turn, we must have eyes to see it with.
