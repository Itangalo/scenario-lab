* `- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Maintain independent access to AI capabilities sufficient for strategic autonomy and crisis response`

The two-year commitment now reads: *Ensure that all critical public systems running AI above 50.0 capability are verifiably resilient, with independent oversight and failover capacity, by the end of 2034.*

## Priority  
Sovereign Oversight and Verification Initiative  
Because the Emergency Resilience Mobilisation cannot succeed without trustworthy systems to protect — and because the Union is now exposed not just to external attack but to internal collapse from unaccountable AI — we must shift from patching to verifying. This is not about more power, but about making the power we have actually governable.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that we no longer know what our own systems are doing. The social insurance scandal was not a breach — it was a design flaw hidden by opacity. We invoked Article 122 to act, but we acted on symptoms. Now we must act on cause.

The Commission will draft a Sovereign AI Oversight Regulation within six weeks, establishing a new mandate for ENISA: not just emergency audits, but continuous, independent verification of all AI systems above 50.0 capability in public service. This includes mandatory model transparency — not just weights, but reasoning traces — and real-time monitoring through a new Public AI Observatory hosted by the European Commission’s AI Office. We are allocating €2.1 billion from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility to fund interpretability research and deployment of monitoring tooling, with a requirement that all public AI contracts after January 2023 include third-party verification clauses.

Germany and France support this, but Spain and Italy resist not just on cost, but on sovereignty — they fear EU overreach into national administration. We are offering them co-ownership of the verification framework through a new Inter-Ministerial AI Safety Board, with rotating presidency and shared staffing. The European Parliament’s Legal Affairs Committee demands judicial review rights; we are conceding to binding appeal mechanisms for citizens affected by AI decisions. This is not a technical fix — it is an act of institutional repair. We are no longer building resilience to AI. We are rebuilding trust in the state.
