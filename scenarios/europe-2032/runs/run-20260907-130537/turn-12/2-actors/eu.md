## In practice  
We are acting under Article 122 TFEU, invoking exceptional circumstances threatening the functioning of the Union’s essential services, to launch the Emergency Resilience Mobilisation. The Commission will draft a temporary Crisis Resilience Directive within four weeks, mandating AI-incident response plans, red-teaming of all AI-supported public systems above 50.0 capability, and failover protocols for health and social services. ENISA will be empowered to conduct unannounced audits and enforce patching within 72 hours. The EIB will unlock €12 billion in emergency financing, matched by a 50% top-up from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, conditioned on national implementation within six months.

Germany, France, and Poland have signalled support, but Spain and Italy resist binding timelines, citing fiscal strain. We are offering them a 70% co-financing rate and deployment of EU cyber rapid-response teams from the newly activated MPAC pool. The European Parliament’s Civil Liberties Committee demands stronger oversight; we are conceding to a temporary Resilience Oversight Panel with cross-party and civil society representation, reporting directly to the European Council every six weeks. This is not business as usual — we are treating fragility as the strategic threat it is, and we are spending our remaining political capital to buy time, not prestige. The Core is ours. Now we must make the systems that depend on it unbreakable.
