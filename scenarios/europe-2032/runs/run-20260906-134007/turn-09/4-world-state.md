### Crisis and Containment

AI capability advances to 81.0, driven by unrelenting progress in model scaling and training efficiency. Despite the EU’s efforts, the frontier pulls further ahead, widening the operational blind spot. Open-weight models now reach 65.5, enabling distributed actors to run sophisticated cyber and bio-design workflows — a threshold crossed with little fanfare but profound consequence.

The EU attempts a dual move: closing the Sovereign Verification Regime by executive reinterpretation under Article 172 TFEU and launching the Emergency AI Oversight and De-escalation Protocol. The former succeeds in form but not in substance — the ECJ challenge remains, and the reclassification to research security allows only proxy red-teaming, yielding limited insight. The verification gap persists, and with ai_safety at just 4.0, the Union remains blind to embedded risks.

The new Protocol, though small in scope, triggers fierce resistance. Several member states reject opt-in funding, citing sovereignty concerns, and civil society groups denounce it as a surveillance enabler. Only four sign on initially, undermining its legitimacy. Still, the mere proposal slightly stabilises institutional morale, marginally slowing the erosion of safety.

### Sovereignty Under Strain

The InvestAI Gigafactories and Hardened Cyber Shield finish deployment, but their impact is muted. Cyber defences prove reactive, not predictive, and the ransomware scars remain. The EU Frontier Intelligence Initiative progresses slowly, hampered by talent drain and data-access delays. Sovereignty, already weakened by dependency, drops to 19.0 as capability surges past 80 — a threshold that deepens structural exposure.

Political capital collapses to 5.0. The portfolio remains overloaded, with four large measures in flight. The priority penalty applies, and the new small measure adds cost. No finishing bonus arrives — the Sovereign Verification Regime’s forced closure is seen as legally dubious, not a victory. The backlash_physical intensifies, with data centre blockades spreading to two new sites. Labour displacement continues, and public sentiment dips to 22.0.
