### The Weight of Crisis

The first half of 2030 is defined by shock and reaction. A sophisticated ransomware campaign, powered by AI-generated exploits, cripples municipal services in three member states. Recovery is slow, and public trust erodes further. Simultaneously, a controversial study confirms that accessible AI models can assist non-experts in designing viable human pathogens — a finding that ignites fierce debate in scientific and security circles.

The **Hardened Cyber Shield** completes deployment, enabling automated patch generation and accelerating recovery — a partial validation of prior investment. Yet the **cyber_major_incident** reveals that defensive automation lags behind offensive agility. In response, emergency NIS3 powers are invoked to *propose* a mandate for AI-assisted patching within 72 hours of disclosure. The proposal triggers immediate legal and operational consultations across member states; full implementation will require at least one full turn to operationalise, as technical standards, monitoring mechanisms, and compliance pathways are still under development.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme finishes, delivering sovereign compute capacity. This allows preparations for the **EU Frontier Intelligence Initiative** to begin in earnest, with planning underway for its first training run focused on pathogen detection and cyber-physical defence. Binding data-sharing agreements are being negotiated under Horizon Europe, though EMBL expresses concern about talent retention amid growing disparities with U.S. research infrastructure.

A new **Biothreat Detection and Response Network** is announced, with initial planning and inter-institutional coordination now underway. Its scope is ambitious, and early discussions with member states surface concerns about centralised bio-surveillance. Integration with border systems is under discussion but not yet initiated; staffing, governance frameworks, and technical architecture remain to be finalised. The network is expected to take multiple turns to reach operational status.

The **Sovereign Verification Regime** remains frozen by an ongoing ECJ challenge, forcing regulators to rely on proxy red-teaming with limited fidelity.

Public sentiment declines as job losses in legal, accounting and administrative sectors mount. The **labour_displacement** wave fuels resentment, and no visible public benefit offsets the disruption. The narrative of AI as a tool for resilience rings hollow to those locked out of the job market.

Despite completing two major measures, political capital dips further. The portfolio remains overloaded, and cohesion frays under pressure. The shift toward focused AI development is underway, but its impact on public confidence remains uncertain.

### Flickers from Below

Amid top-down strain, a network of major cities quietly expands its local AI oversight framework, sharing incident reports and transparency protocols. While ignored by Brussels, it gains credibility as national agencies falter.
