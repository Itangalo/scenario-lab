Our previous commitment assumed that sovereign general capability was the only path to security — but with `ai_safety` at 15.0 and `resilience` at 48.0, we cannot afford to wait for that. ## In practice  
We are reorienting the **EU Frontier Intelligence Initiative** around narrow, high-impact domains: pathogen detection and cyber-physical defence. The first training run, now scheduled for Q2 2030, will focus on a model that ingests genomic data, predicts virulence, and generates neutralising antibody candidates — all under EU-controlled compute at the newly operational Gigafactories. We are tasking EMBL and the ECDC with co-leading this effort, with binding data-sharing agreements enforced through Horizon Europe’s legal base. The Council will vote under Article 172 TFEU to classify this as a mission-critical research priority, unlocking fast-tracked funding and overriding national vetoes on data access.

The **Sovereign Verification Regime** remains in legal limbo, but we are no longer waiting for the ECJ. We are instructing EAAD+ to begin red-teaming foreign models using proxy architectures trained on declassified data — a stopgap, but one that maintains operational readiness. The **Institutional Integrity Audit** is now in its final phase, with preliminary findings showing AI-assisted decision-making in 40% of EMA drug approvals — all based on foreign models. We are treating this not as a scandal, but as a roadmap: every dependency identified will be matched with a sovereign alternative by 2031.

The **cyber_major_incident** has landed — a ransomware sweep across municipal services in three member states, enabled by AI-generated zero-days. ENISA confirms the attack tools were model-designed; recovery is underway, but confidence is shaken. In response, we are activating emergency provisions under NIS3 to mandate AI-assisted patch generation and deployment within 72 hours of vulnerability disclosure. This is not a new measure — it is the enforcement of what we already built. But it is not enough. That is why we are launching the **Biothreat Detection and Response Network** — because the next incident may not lock our data, but our bodies. We are not waiting for the recipe to be published. We are building the antidote first.
