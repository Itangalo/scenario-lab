AI capability reaches 67.0 amid escalating crises. A major AI-powered ransomware attack disrupts municipal services across three member states, exposing persistent cyber vulnerabilities despite the completed Hardened Cyber Shield, which enables automated patching but lags behind offensive AI agility. In response, emergency NIS3 powers are invoked to propose a 72-hour AI-assisted patching mandate, now under consultation but not yet operational.

The biosecurity landscape deteriorates as a confirmed study demonstrates that accessible AI models can enable non-experts to design viable pathogens, triggering alarm and accelerating the launch of the Biothreat Detection and Response Network. Initial planning focuses on pathogen detection and cyber-physical defence, though operational deployment will take multiple turns due to unresolved governance, staffing, and surveillance concerns.

The InvestAI Gigafactories programme concludes, delivering sovereign compute capacity and enabling the EU Frontier Intelligence Initiative to begin preparations for its first training run. Data-sharing agreements are being negotiated under Horizon Europe, but talent retention remains a challenge. The Sovereign Verification Regime remains paralyzed by an ECJ challenge, forcing reliance on low-fidelity red-teaming.

Public sentiment declines sharply due to widespread labour displacement in legal, administrative, and accounting sectors, with no visible benefits from AI initiatives to counter economic disruption. Political capital erodes further amid governance overload and fragmented Council cohesion. A grassroots network of cities expands local AI oversight independently, gaining credibility as national and EU-level institutions struggle to respond effectively.
