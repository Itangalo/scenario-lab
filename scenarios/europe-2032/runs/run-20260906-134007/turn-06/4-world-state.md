### The Verification Gambit

The EU proposes the Sovereign Verification Regime, demanding checkpoint access and telemetry from all high-capability AI systems operating in Europe. The move is framed as a minimal act of sovereignty — the right to inspect what it cannot control. However, implementation is delayed by legal and diplomatic hurdles. The U.S. developer behind the medical breakthrough refuses initial cooperation, citing national security. Several EU hospitals using the model in oncology pause treatments, sparking public outcry. The Commission invokes emergency health powers, but full access remains blocked pending review, leaving the regime in a state of contested rollout.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** formally concludes, but its legacy is mixed. Two new data centres open under accelerated permitting, yet private investment has dried up. The pivot to public co-ownership of Gigafactories is approved under urgency, but markets react skeptically — the move seen as reactive, not visionary.

The **Hardened Cyber Shield** is fully operational as NIS3, with 82% compliance. However, legacy vulnerabilities persist, especially in regional health systems relying on proxy defences. No major breach occurs, but confidence in systemic resilience remains fragile.

The **Institutional Integrity Audit** uncovers irregular data flows in two national ID systems, triggering emergency reviews. The Public Trust Observatory launches, publishing real-time audit statuses — a step toward transparency, but not yet trust.

Public sentiment shifts cautiously upward due to the **medical breakthrough**, though the EU’s inability to access the underlying model tempers enthusiasm. Protests continue, now split between those demanding stricter control and those calling for unfettered access to life-saving AI.

### Sovereignty Undercut

The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event lands hard. The cutoff from the leading model exposes strategic fragility. Despite compute growth, the EU cannot run or verify the most capable systems. Sovereignty erodes further.

Political capital declines under the weight of an overstretched agenda. With five active measures already in flight — including the newly proposed verification regime — governance capacity is strained. The drop reflects mounting friction and coordination costs, but not a complete collapse, as emergency legitimacy efforts absorb some pressure. The portfolio remains overloaded, but not to the point of systemic failure.
