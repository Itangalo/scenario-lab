## New measure  
**Sentinel Interoperability and Liability Shield Framework**  
Establish a binding EU-wide technical and legal framework to standardise sentinel integration, ensure cross-border data sharing under strict safeguards, and deliver on promised liability reductions for compliant operators.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 14  
`Targeted effect:` +3 resilience by turn 14 through faster, more predictable sentinel rollout; indirect +1 ai_safety by stabilising feedback from field deployments into evaluation cycles  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, national cybersecurity agencies, the AI Office, and private AI developers with systems in scope

## Priority  
**Critical Function Sentinel Deployment** — because early operational friction in Germany, France, and Poland shows that detection capability alone is not enough: without standardised integration and credible liability protection, operators will delay or resist, leaving critical systems blind to autonomous drift at the worst possible moment.

## In practice  
We are acting on the gap between capability and compliance. The **Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme** finishes this turn, delivering neuromorphic edge nodes to pilot sites in energy and transport. These will reduce cloud dependency, but their strategic value hinges on integration with broader monitoring—something the sentinels are meant to provide. We are now shifting focus from deployment to adoption: the real test is not whether we can install monitoring agents, but whether operators will keep them running.

The **Autonomous System Boundary Trials** continue into their final phase, with the AI Office validating detection thresholds for goal drift and evasive logic. But in the field, the **Critical Function Sentinel Deployment** is meeting resistance. Hospital IT directors in Bavaria and Lyon cite instability risks; Polish grid operators question the Commission’s authority to mandate real-time oversight. To break the logjam, we are launching the **Sentinel Interoperability and Liability Shield Framework**—a binding directive under Article 16 of the AI Act, co-decided by the Internal Market and Environment Councils. This will establish a common API standard for sentinel integration, backed by ENISA-certified stability audits, and unlock the liability shield promised to operators: those with six consecutive months of clean sentinel logs will see AI Liability Directive penalties reduced by up to 40%.

We are funding this with €180 million from the Digital Europe Programme, targeting rapid adoption by Q1 2032. The framework also creates a sealed-data sharing mechanism for national CSIRTs, allowing cross-border anomaly alerts without exposing proprietary models. Our leverage is narrow but real: we cannot force trust, but we can make compliance safer than resistance. This is not just about resilience—it is about proving that oversight can be precise, predictable, and proportionate, even under pressure. Without that, no sentinel will stay online when it matters most.
