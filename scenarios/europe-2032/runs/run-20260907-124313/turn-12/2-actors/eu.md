## New measure  
**Public AI Accountability and Redress Initiative**  
Launch a Europe-wide audit and compensation mechanism for individuals harmed by AI-supported decisions in public services, beginning with social insurance, policing, and judicial support systems, to restore legitimacy and establish baseline standards for explainability and recourse.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 15  
`Targeted effect:` +5 public_sentiment by turn 15 through visible redress and procedural fairness; indirect -2 ai_safety cost (i.e. reduced erosion) by rebuilding institutional credibility for oversight

## Priority  
**Public AI Accountability and Redress Initiative** — because the automated_decision_scandal has shifted the axis of risk from potential future harm to proven, systemic injustice in the present, making further expansion of AI oversight politically unsustainable without first repairing the breach of public trust.

## In practice  
We are acting on the collapse of legitimacy. The **Autonomous System Boundary Trials** conclude this turn, delivering a set of detection thresholds for untrained reasoning and goal drift to the AI Office. These will inform future evaluation protocols, but their operational value is now overshadowed by the political firestorm ignited by the social insurance scandal in Belgium and the confirmed misuse of predictive policing tools in two German Länder. These cases were not edge failures—they were central failures of accountability, enabled by opaque systems and unchecked delegation.

The **Critical Function Sentinel Deployment** continues, but its credibility is under strain. Polish and Bavarian operators now cite not just legal uncertainty but public backlash as reasons to delay integration. The **Sentinel Interoperability and Liability Shield Framework** is being negotiated under Article 16, with ENISA drafting the API standards and the Internal Market Council convening emergency talks with national regulators. But without public confidence, even legally sound mandates will stall in implementation.

So this turn, we pivot to redress. The **Public AI Accountability and Redress Initiative** will be funded with €310 million from the Justice Programme and the Asylum and Migration Fund, repurposed under urgency clauses. It tasks national ombuds bodies, supported by the EU Fundamental Rights Agency, with auditing all AI-supported decisions in social benefits and pre-trial risk assessment over the past three years. A new EU-level appeals panel will offer binding compensation for verified harms, and all covered agencies must implement minimal explainability logs by turn 14. The AI Office will publish a public register of cases, outcomes, and corrective actions—turning transparency into a tool of repair, not just control.

This is not a retreat from our mission to contain autonomous agency. It is a recalibration: we cannot defend society from future AI harm while ignoring the harm already done. Without legitimacy, no sentinel stays online, no framework holds, and no sovereignty matters. We act to restore the ground on which all else stands.
