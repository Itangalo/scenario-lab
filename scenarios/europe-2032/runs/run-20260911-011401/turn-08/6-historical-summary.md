By late 2028 EU dependence was exposed when US tiered-access controls throttled allied calls, hitting clinics, ministries and logistics already weakened by January's machine-written intrusions.

January brought a largely automated ransomware sweep across throttled public services — Lombardy clinics to paper triage, Tallinn ministry offline, Rotterdam port stalled — with attribution lagging.

Brussels' emergency repair cell became the standing response backbone via ENISA/Joint Cyber Unit, pooling clean backups, hardware and CERT staff; where crews arrived services degraded rather than stopped. A certified lab monitoring technique predicting behavior was attached to restoration, cutting false alarms and speeding deployment, without replacing lost capacity.

Without funds, Brussels reactivated the Netherlands-Japan-Korea table for aligned export licences and pooled procurement, keeping limited hardware quotas flowing. EU-run models cutting waits were offered to reintegrate the breakaway capital; it refused. Gigafactory permits for five sites stayed legally alive but unbuilt amid financing gaps, blockades and arson against fences/grid; police held perimeters, no forced restart ordered.

Public mood split: credit for shorter queues and measured office productivity gains without layoffs (firms rehiring), blame for renewed dependence, fraud, outages and empty fenced fields. By mid-2029 the Union had held together where it was dark, but built little new.
