## In practice
We are accelerating the InvestAI Gigafactories through binding agreements with France, Sweden and Spain to finalise site designations by the end of Q2, backed by a €15bn disbursement from the European Investment Bank under Article 122 TFEU. The Commission is invoking the Net-Zero Industry Act to override permitting delays in Brittany and northern Sweden, conditioned on local reinvestment in grid modernisation and green hydrogen infrastructure. ENTSO-E has reserved 15GW of planned grid capacity, contingent on national energy plans being submitted by June — a deadline we are enforcing through the Energy Union governance process. The goal is shovels in the ground by turn 3, not paper studies.

The Tech sovereignty package advances through the Digital Markets Council, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable commitments on EU-based operational control and energy efficiency. The private capital mobilisation is being structured through the InvestEU framework, with tranches tied to ASML supply chain resilience, Blue Card+ talent recruitment, and long-term PPAs with offshore wind. Legal work on the Article 114 TFEU directive to harmonise permitting timelines continues, aiming to cut approvals from 48 to 12 months.

On EAAD, we are pivoting to an interim model: rather than wait for the levy and full autonomy, we are seconding staff from national cybersecurity agencies and nuclear safety regulators to begin red-teaming high-risk models under ENISA’s mandate. We are also fast-tracking the regulation granting access to model weights, anticipating the CJEU challenge, and preparing a political counteroffensive through the European Council. The openweight release this turn makes this urgent — hence the new OWMI measure, which tasks ENISA and Europol with building a distributed monitoring network to detect dangerous capabilities in open models, using adversarial benchmarking and dark web scanning. This is not a substitute for sovereignty, but a circuit breaker while we build it.
