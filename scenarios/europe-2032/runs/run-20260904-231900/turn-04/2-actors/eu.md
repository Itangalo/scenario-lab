## In practice
We are pressing forward on InvestAI Gigafactories with binding disbursement conditions: €15bn in EIB funding is now tied to site finalisation in France, Sweden, and Spain by end-Q2, backed by Article 122 TFEU emergency provisions. We are invoking the Net-Zero Industry Act to override permitting bottlenecks in Brittany and northern Sweden, but only in exchange for co-investment in grid modernisation and green hydrogen hubs — a trade that secures local political buy-in and aligns with the Green Deal Industrial Plan. ENTSO-E’s 15GW grid reservation remains active, but we are now conditioning further support on the submission of national energy plans by June, enforced through the Energy Union governance framework. The European Council has granted political cover after closed briefings on the strategic cost of delay, but cohesion is fraying — hence the urgency.

On the Tech Sovereignty Package, we are advancing the accelerated-permitting directive through the Digital Markets Council, trading faster approvals for enforceable commitments on EU-based operational control, energy efficiency (PUE <1.2), and 70% renewable PPAs. The InvestEU structure is being finalised to unlock private capital tranches, tied to milestones in ASML supply chain access, Blue Card+ recruitment of 15,000 AI engineers by 2030, and long-term offshore wind contracts. Legal drafting under Article 114 TFEU continues to harmonise permitting timelines from 48 to 12 months — now framed as a strategic imperative, not just an economic one.

EAAD’s interim model is operational: 120 staff seconded from national cybersecurity and nuclear safety agencies are conducting red-teaming under ENISA’s mandate, focusing on model leakage, agent persistence, and jailbreak resilience. We are fast-tracking the regulation that grants legal authority to delay releases, anticipating CJEU challenges, and preparing a political counteroffensive in the Council to link market access to safety compliance. Meanwhile, OWMI has launched — ENISA and Europol are building a distributed monitoring network using adversarial benchmarking, dark web crawlers, and model fingerprinting to detect dangerous capabilities in open models. This is not sovereignty, but a circuit breaker while we build it.

Now, with the adoption_success event proving that European-led AI use delivers public benefit, we are leveraging that credibility to open new negotiations: the new measure proposes binding reciprocity agreements with the U.S., UK, and Japan. These will guarantee EU access to frontier models in exchange for market access and technical cooperation — but only if developers comply with EAAD evaluation standards and accept EU jurisdiction over deployed systems. This is not dependence repackaged; it is leverage turned into law. We begin talks through the Trade and Technology Council, with DG TRADE and the High Representative leading, backed by the threat of market restriction under the Digital Markets Act if reciprocity is refused. This measure costs political capital, but it is necessary: sovereignty is not just what we build, but what we can compel.
