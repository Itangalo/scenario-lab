## New measure
**Sovereign AI Core: Deploy EU-Controlled Frontier-Comparable Systems for Critical Functions**  
We will field a suite of high-capability AI systems—trained and operated under EU legal and physical jurisdiction—for use in crisis response, infrastructure resilience, and regulatory oversight, ensuring the Union can act even when external access is denied.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 10–15 points by turn 8; create a fallback capability for evaluation, cyber defence, and emergency planning independent of foreign providers; begin closing the gap between openweight capability (50.0) and sovereign utility.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national crisis agencies, ENISA, EAAD, and selected critical infrastructure operators.

## Priority
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
This remains the backbone of our sovereign capacity: without domestic compute at scale, no evaluation, no control, and no fallback when access is rationed — as election_consolidation now confirms will happen. The Gigafactories are not just economic infrastructure; they are the physical foundation of political agency in an AI-defined world. Every delay risks locking the EU into permanent client status.

## In practice
We are doubling down on InvestAI Gigafactories, leveraging emergency provisions under Article 122 TFEU to fast-track site finalisation in France, Sweden, and Spain. €15bn in EIB funding is now conditional on binding commitments by Q2: land secured, grid connections reserved, and environmental mitigation plans co-signed with regional authorities. To overcome local resistance, we are pairing each site with a green hydrogen hub and grid modernisation package under the Net-Zero Industry Act — turning energy security into political currency. ENTSO-E’s 15GW reservation is now tied to national energy transition roadmaps due by June, enforced through the Energy Union framework. The Council has granted temporary derogations from standard permitting, but only for projects that meet PUE <1.2 and 70% renewable PPAs — conditions that align sovereignty with sustainability.

The Tech Sovereignty Package advances through the Digital Markets Council, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable operational control: any data centre benefiting from fast-track approval must host a sovereign AI node for EAAD and civil protection use. Legal drafting under Article 114 TFEU continues to harmonise permitting timelines from 48 to 12 months, now explicitly framed as a strategic resilience imperative. The InvestEU mechanism is being calibrated to release tranches upon milestones in ASML supply chain access and Blue Card+ recruitment of 15,000 AI engineers by 2030 — linking talent and hardware to infrastructure.

EAAD’s interim team, drawn from nuclear and cyber safety agencies, is conducting red-team evaluations on agent persistence and model leakage, operating under ENISA’s mandate pending full legal authority. We are fast-tracking the regulation that allows EAAD to delay unsafe deployments, anticipating legal challenges from industry and preparing a Council-level political defence linking market access to compliance. Meanwhile, OWMI has formally launched: ENISA and Europol are deploying dark web crawlers and model fingerprinting tools to detect dangerous open-weight variants, providing early warning even as full resilience lags. The breach of audit protocols is under formal investigation, but we are already rebuilding trust through transparency — publishing redacted findings and reinforcing weight security with hardware-enforced air gaps in evaluation environments.

With the new measure, we are initiating the Sovereign AI Core: a programme to deploy EU-operated, frontier-comparable systems for critical functions. This is not about matching the frontier in raw capability — yet — but about ensuring that when the next crisis hits, the EU has AI it can run, inspect, and trust. We are tasking DG Connect and the European Defence Agency to co-design the architecture, drawing on lessons from EAAD and OWMI. The first nodes will support cyber incident response, biothreat detection, and labour transition planning — high-leverage domains where dependence on foreign models would compromise autonomy. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with compute drawn from the earliest operational Gigafactory clusters. This is the next step: from building capacity, to wielding control.
