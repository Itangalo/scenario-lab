## In practice
We are enforcing movement on the InvestAI Gigafactories by activating emergency provisions under Article 122 TFEU, releasing €15bn in EIB funding tied to binding site finalisation in France, Sweden and Spain by Q2. The Commission is invoking the Net-Zero Industry Act to override permitting delays in Brittany and northern Sweden, conditioned on co-investment in grid modernisation and green hydrogen hubs to secure local buy-in. ENTSO-E’s 15GW grid reservation stands, but we are now linking disbursement to the submission of national energy plans by June — a deadline we are enforcing through the Energy Union governance process, with political cover secured in the European Council after closed briefings on the sovereignty risks of delay.

The Tech Sovereignty Package advances through the Digital Markets Council, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable commitments on EU-based operational control, energy efficiency above 1.2 PUE, and 70% renewable PPAs. The private capital mobilisation is being structured under InvestEU, with tranches unlocked by milestones in ASML supply chain access, Blue Card+ recruitment of 15,000 AI engineers by 2030, and long-term offshore wind contracts. Legal drafting on the Article 114 TFEU directive to harmonise permitting timelines continues, aiming to cut approval cycles from 48 to 12 months — a target now framed as strategic, not economic.

On EAAD, we are operationalising an interim model: seconding 120 staff from national cybersecurity agencies and nuclear safety regulators to begin red-teaming under ENISA’s mandate, focusing on model leakage, agent persistence and jailbreak resilience. We are fast-tracking the regulation granting access to model weights, anticipating CJEU challenges, and preparing a political counteroffensive in the European Council to frame safety as a condition of market access. Simultaneously, the newly launched OWMI tasks ENISA and Europol with building a distributed monitoring network — using adversarial benchmarking, dark web crawlers and model fingerprinting — to detect dangerous capabilities in open models. This is not sovereignty, but a circuit breaker while we build it.
