### A release no one can recall
Autumn brought a powerful openly downloadable model, only months behind the closed frontier. It spread to hundreds of thousands of machines in days. For grid operators and hospitals, the timing was grim: the same code that could help draft maintenance plans could also probe control systems, and once downloaded it could not be taken back.

In two countries, municipal water and power utilities had already stopped connecting new automated controls. After last winter's rogue software episode, their insurers called the upgrades uninsurable. Rollouts froze through December.

### A backstop without building
Brussels answered with what it could afford. A temporary indemnity pool was stood up under emergency solidarity powers to cover towns that resumed upgrades, paired with funded kits for manual override, paper fallback and islanding drills. Transmission operators shared breaker logs from last year's hardening work; civil protection channels moved the kits.

It partly worked. A handful of cities restarted stalled projects, and blackout drills ran through the autumn. But lawyers argued over who paid if something failed, payouts were capped at six months, and many councils waited. No new computing was bought — continuity funds were simply relabelled.

### Leverage, but no chips
The trade offer built around European lithography servicing stayed on the table. Envoys shuttled with pooled licensing terms, asking for guaranteed chips, spare capacity and maintenance help in return.

Washington and Beijing, meanwhile, announced a limited understanding on securing model weights, escalation risks and certain biological design tools, with thin verification. Europe asked for observer access to shared testing rather than a full seat. It was thanked and kept waiting. American suppliers praised the European proposal in public while offering only expensive spot capacity in private. Equipment makers in Eindhoven and Jena kept lobbying against joint licensing.

By December the lights stayed on and clinics stayed staffed, but hiring freezes and postponed upgrades persisted. The bargain with health workers held, morale frayed, and the chips had still not arrived.
