## Priority
**Priority:** Establish the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD)  
This remains our highest priority because the collapse in private investment creates a narrowing window to institutionalise public authority over safety before the next wave of capability resurgence — and because the interpretability breakthrough now embedded in leading labs offers a technical foundation for real-time monitoring that may not survive the next training cycle.

## In practice
We are finalising the EAAD regulation under Article 114 TFEU, anchoring it in product safety law to bypass the political gridlock around fiscal instruments. The legal team is aligning the directive with the Machinery Regulation and Radio Equipment Directive to ensure CJEU admissibility, while the technical secretariat integrates the new interpretability standard into the red-teaming protocol. By June, we will have standing agreements with BSI and CERN to conduct live adversarial evaluations of agent persistence and goal drift, and nuclear safety secondments from ASN, BfS, and STUK are being embedded into the core audit team. This is not oversight as certification; it is as enforcement — a model cannot go live in the EU unless a team trained to fail it has tried and failed.

The InvestAI Gigafactories are transitioning from planning to procurement: ENTSO-E has confirmed grid access for the French and Spanish sites, and local energy-sharing models in Sweden and Brittany have been formalised into co-ownership structures that tie green hydrogen output to municipal energy credits, reducing resistance and accelerating permitting. Tenders for construction will be issued by June, with first power expected in turn 6. The European Investment Bank has confirmed disbursement readiness, and we are monitoring supply chain risks closely given the investment collapse — but for now, the pipeline holds.

The Tech Sovereignty Package advances in the Digital Services Council, where we are conditioning accelerated permitting on binding commitments for EU-based operational control and local energy sourcing. This is not about speed alone; it is about ensuring that no data centre built under fast-track rules can be remotely disabled by a foreign entity. We are negotiating with hyperscalers directly, making clear that access to EU markets and permits depends on architecture that respects EU sovereignty.

The European Workforce Transition Accelerator (EWTA) concludes its first phase this turn: €45bn from cohesion and Recovery and Resilience funds has been allocated, with wage insurance now covering 70% of lost income during retraining in participating sectors. Sectoral councils in legal tech, AI-augmented administration, and cyber-physical maintenance are operational in nine member states, with full rollout expected by turn 5. While backlash persists, early uptake suggests the circuit breaker is holding — and we are preparing a public communications campaign for turn 5 to reframe AI as a managed transition, not an uncontrolled disruption.
