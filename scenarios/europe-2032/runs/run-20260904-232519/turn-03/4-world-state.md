### Frontier Momentum Persists Amid Doubt
Despite signs of a plateau—underwhelming releases and falling prices at the high end—AI capability advances by 1.5 points to 57.0. Behind closed doors, training efficiency and agent orchestration continue to scale, suggesting the slowdown is cyclical, not structural. The interpretability breakthrough from last turn is now embedded in leading labs’ pipelines, marginally improving confidence in system behaviour. Yet no new safety protocols have landed on deployed models outside the EU’s influence, limiting broader gains.

### EU Doubles Down on Assurance
The European Union pushes forward on institutionalising AI safety, advancing the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) through a legal framework based on product safety under Article 114 TFEU. Red-teaming protocols with BSI and CERN are operational, and nuclear safety agency secondments signal seriousness. However, legal uncertainty lingers as the CJEU has yet to rule on related precedents, delaying full authority.

### Compute Buildout Stays on Track
InvestAI Gigafactories progress to procurement, with grid access secured in France and Spain, and community energy-sharing models pacifying local opposition in Sweden and Brittany. Tenders are on schedule for mid-year award. The Tech Sovereignty Package inches forward, linking fast-tracking to EU control and energy sourcing—aimed at ending foreign override risks.

### Workforce Response Activated Amid Rising Tension
The European Workforce Transition Accelerator (EWTA) launches with €45bn in cohesion funds, funding retraining in legal tech and cyber-physical maintenance. Wage insurance and sectoral councils are introduced, but rollout is patchy. Public sentiment dips as campaign-style backlash against AI intensifies—data centre moratoriums and worker protections gain mainstream traction, framing AI as a threat rather than a tool.

### Political Capital Erodes
With four large measures in flight and no major completion, political capital erodes under sustained portfolio strain. The priority on EAAD brings no acceleration, and no finishing benefit lands this turn. Sovereignty slips as global compute growth outpaces EU buildout, despite planning gains.
