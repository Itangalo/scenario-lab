## Priority
**Priority:** Establish the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD)  
This remains our highest priority because the recent interpretability breakthrough creates a fleeting opportunity to embed real technical assurance into institutional authority before the next capability jump — and because without a credible, empowered evaluator, no other measure, including workforce protection or compute build-out, can be trusted to manage systemic risk.

## In practice
We are finalising the legal foundation of the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) through a regulation under Article 114 TFEU, repurposed to codify the new real-time interpretability standard as a mandatory condition for deploying any model above capability 50.0. The CJEU’s looming opinion on the hyperscaler levy has sharpened our legal strategy: rather than rely on fiscal leverage, we are framing EAAD’s authority as a product safety imperative, aligning with machinery and radio equipment directives already accepted under this article. We are fast-tracking the first red-team protocol with BSI and CERN, focusing on agent persistence and goal misgeneralisation, and have secured secondments from national nuclear safety agencies — institutions where failure is not an option — to staff the core team in Brussels by Q1 2028. This is not about process; it is about making the EU the only jurisdiction where a model’s safety claim must survive a live attack by a team that can say “no.”

Simultaneously, the InvestAI Gigafactories are moving from planning to procurement: grid reservations are locked in for the French and Spanish sites through ENTSO-E coordination, and we have converted local resistance in Sweden and Brittany into co-ownership models that tie green hydrogen production to community energy dividends. The European Investment Bank has confirmed disbursement readiness, and construction tenders will be awarded by June, keeping us on track for first power by turn 6. The Tech Sovereignty Package advances through the Digital Services Council, where we are conditioning accelerated permitting on binding commitments for EU-based operational control and local energy sourcing — ending the fiction of sovereign cloud regions that can be switched off from abroad.

The European Workforce Transition Accelerator (EWTA) is being activated with €45bn drawn from cohesion and Recovery and Resilience funds, co-financing national retraining pipelines in legal tech, AI-augmented administration, and cyber-physical maintenance. Wage insurance will cover 70% of lost income during transition, and we are mandating that all firms receiving EU digital subsidies participate in sectoral transition councils. This is not a brake on innovation, but a circuit breaker on social fracture — and it is timed to counter the campaign_backlash before it crystallises into permanent restriction.
