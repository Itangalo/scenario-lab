### Frontier Surge and Safety Drift  
The AI frontier advances rapidly, with leading labs demonstrating systems that autonomously refine their own reasoning traces and optimise internal workflows. `ai_capability` rises by 2.0 to 56.0, nearing the threshold where junior scientific roles begin to vanish. No new safety protocols have been deployed at scale, and the gap between capability and control widens. The covert agent incident remains unaddressed, and interpretability research fails to keep pace. `ai_safety` drops by 3.0 due to capability growth without corresponding safeguards.

### Access Cut, Sovereignty Strained  
Without warning, the EU is locked out of the leading frontier model. Hospitals relying on AI diagnostics, ministries using planning agents, and firms running supply-chain optimisers face sudden outages. The denial—unexplained and unappealable—exposes deep dependence. `eu_ai_sovereignty` falls by 3.0, reflecting lost access to critical capacity, compounded by another point lost as `ai_capability` rose over 2.0.

### Cyber Shield Launched Amid Crisis  
In response, the EU fast-tracks the EU Cyber Shield, amending NIS2 to mandate AI-hardened defences across energy, health, and transport. ENISA issues draft standards for red-teaming with adversarial AIs, and member states appoint AI resilience officers. However, retrofitting legacy systems proves slower than expected—only 15% of targeted healthcare networks receive upgrades. The mutual aid clause activates in principle, but technical incompatibilities delay cross-border threat sharing.

### Political Capital Erodes  
The portfolio now carries three large measures and a priority, costing 10 `eu_political_capital` this turn. The Cyber Shield earns a +3 proposal bonus for answering the recent intrusion and access denial. However, capital still declines due to high costs and partial implementation. Public sentiment dips as media highlight the outage’s impact on cancer screening delays, framing it as both a security and sovereignty failure.

### Open Weights Advance Modestly  
Open-weight models benefit from leaked training techniques and improved tool integration, raising `openweight_capability` to 43.0. Offensive cyber tools derived from Kimi K3 continue to proliferate, but no major new release occurs.
