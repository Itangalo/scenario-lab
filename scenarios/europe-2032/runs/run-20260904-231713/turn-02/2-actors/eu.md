## New measure  
**Measure:** EU Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure  
A binding directive requiring AI-hardened intrusion detection, automatic segmentation, and real-time threat sharing across all operators of essential services, with mandatory red-teaming using AI-optimised attack simulations.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 4  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 10–12 points; reduce systemic vulnerability to AI-optimised cyber intrusions by enforcing detection, response, and mutual aid protocols  
Applies to: All EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators in energy, health, transport, and digital infrastructure

## Priority  
**Priority:** EU Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure  
This turn, the breach of grid operators and the denial of frontier access have converged into a single reality: sovereignty without resilience is a hollow shell, and our two-year commitment cannot survive cascading failures in essential services — so we act now on the most immediate vector of harm.

## In practice  
We are activating the EU Cyber Shield through the NIS2 Directive framework, amending it via comitology to impose AI-specific hardening requirements on all essential service operators. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will issue binding technical standards by turn 3 for AI-resistant detection systems, including anomaly monitoring trained on synthetic attack patterns generated by red-team AIs. Member states must designate national AI resilience officers and integrate automated breach containment protocols by turn 4. Funding of €18bn will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with 40% allocated to retrofitting legacy systems in healthcare and municipal grids. We are fast-tracking mutual aid clauses so that a detected AI attack in one member state automatically triggers defensive updates across all others within 24 hours.

At the same time, we are re-focusing the stalled EAAD effort on immediate operational viability: instead of waiting for full autonomy, we are embedding its first evaluators within ENISA and national cyber agencies under secondment agreements, with access to model logs from hyperscalers conditioned on continued market access. The legal challenge to the 0.5% levy is being reframed as a matter of reciprocity: non-EU providers must either submit to EAAD evaluation or face exclusion from public procurement and critical infrastructure contracts. On the industrial front, we are leveraging the Tech Sovereignty Package to accelerate permitting in Sweden and Spain, where local opposition is weakest, while offering Brittany a binding cap on freshwater use and a €2bn green hydrogen co-investment to unlock the northern site. The Gigafactories fund is now conditioned on 60% EU-based operational control and on-site interpretability tooling for any AI systems managing grid or cooling infrastructure — tying compute sovereignty directly to safety and resilience.
