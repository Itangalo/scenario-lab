## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity to ensure strategic autonomy and risk resilience``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The previous commitment to secure foundational AI capacity under EU control was necessary but insufficient. With the frontier now at 60.0 and openweight capability at 48.0, and with the Union abruptly cut off from foreign frontier models, foundational access is not enough — we must now complete the stack, operationalise it across critical domains, and ensure it can scale under crisis conditions. The new commitment reflects that sovereignty is not a capability level but the ability to act independently when it matters most.

``modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.``
- Grounds: The denial of frontier access — sudden, unexplained, and with immediate operational consequences — confirms that waiting for full clarity is a path to dependency. We acted on incomplete evidence in establishing EAAD and the Sovereign AI Stack, and we were right to do so. The cost of hesitation is now visible in hospitals and ministries left without tools. This commitment holds, but its meaning has sharpened: uncertainty is not an excuse for delay when the risk is strategic exclusion.

## In practice
We are treating the completion of EAAD and the Cyber Shield as inflection points, not endpoints. EAAD is now fully staffed and legally empowered, and we are transitioning its focus from model evaluation to system assurance: it will audit not just pre-release models but live deployments in critical infrastructure, with real-time authority to demand changes. The Cyber Shield’s red-teaming mandate is being institutionalised as the EU Threat Simulation Facility under ENISA, with live attack simulations now running quarterly across energy, transport, and health networks. We are also responding to the bio_uplift_findings by expanding EAAD’s remit to include bio-risk evaluation protocols for models above 50.0 capability, developed in coordination with the ECDC and EMA.

The Rapid Open-Weight Containment Protocol has formally completed, but implementation remains partial. Germany and Poland still resist mandatory takedowns, so we are pivoting to a cooperative model: offering national CERTs joint access to the EU’s detection sandbox and behavioural analysis tools in exchange for voluntary compliance. We are also working with GitHub EU, GitLab, and open research hubs to embed capability watermarking and provenance tracking at upload — a softer path to containment that respects open science while slowing dangerous diffusion.

Most urgently, we are launching the European Foundation Model Deployment Accelerator to close the gap between capability and utility. The Sovereign AI Stack’s first model will reach 52.0 capability by turn 7, but if it sits unused, it is no shield. We are allocating €4.8bn from the Digital Europe Programme to co-fund integration into radiology diagnostics, grid load forecasting, and emergency dispatch coordination. Each deployment includes a safety co-pilot trained to flag anomalies and a human-in-the-loop protocol. We are fast-tracking regulatory sandboxes in six member states and offering training grants to public-sector AI stewards. This is not just about efficiency — it is about proving that the EU can deliver AI that serves, protects, and earns trust.
