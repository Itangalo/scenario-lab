## In practice
We push M3 to full operation this turn: ENISA's Brussels cell moves from secondees to permanent posts funded from Digital Europe, 24-hour reporting under NIS2/CER is extended from TSOs and top ports to water and large hospitals, and EIB front-loading pays for segmentation where vendors can still deliver despite the chip halt. The Energy and Transport Councils keep naming laggards, with cohesion top-ups for municipalities that stay in live exercises.

In parallel we stand up the Reserve without spending ASML: DG GROW and HERA pool existing public chip inventories and InvestAI reserved capacity, the Chips Joint Undertaking runs joint procurement for replacement supply, and a Council emergency allocation rule gives grid, ports, health and cyber-detection first call on scarce compute. M1 Gigafactories and M2 permitting zones are put on continuity footing — no new groundbreakings dependent on evaporated private capital, permits preserved and re-phased to efficiency and sovereign maintenance. The Lithography Shield holds the joint-approval line with The Hague while offering Washington pooled-demand reliability instead of extended service bans, and the defecting member state's side-deal is contained by linking it to the same allocation discipline.
