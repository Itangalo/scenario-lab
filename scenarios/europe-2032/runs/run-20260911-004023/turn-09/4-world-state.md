### The jump
Autumn began with obsolescence. An American lab demonstrated a system that planned across days, rewrote its own tooling mid-run and passed evaluations built for its predecessor. Roadmaps circulated the week before were withdrawn. In Brussels, the leaked benchmark from winter was re-read not as warning but as understatement.

Within weeks two other shocks landed. A biosecurity paper showed a genome model guiding non-experts toward a viable human-infecting design; authors and critics fought over methods while health ministries forwarded the annexes in silence. And commercial humanoids appeared in logistics yards — Chinese-built, American-controlled — with videos of dexterous packing and, soon after, of field trials for carrying, digging and mine clearance.

### The strait opens, briefly
For a few weeks the blockaded strait breathed. Under a pooled insurance and convoy arrangement, backlogged hulls moved: transformers, switchgear, crates of accelerators bound for islandable grid nodes, hospitals and sentinel labs. Dockworkers in Rotterdam and Marseille worked double shifts; an allocation board fought over every pallet.

The Commission's new sprint claimed the window as its own. Grid operators rehearsed islanding, hospitals drilled manual fallback, Paris and Warsaw shells got grid connections even where accelerators still lagged. Delivery was real but thin — one substation energized while three waited, sequencers running but reagents rationed, drills completed without the retraining unions demanded.

At the same time export controls tightened again. Volume licences favoured some allies; European buyers found lead times lengthening and conditions multiplying. Officials spoke of autonomy while counting foreign crates.

### Holding on
The containment protocol closed out: thresholds for isolating anomalous workloads signed, cross-border aid triggers tested. Clinics stayed open at reduced throughput. Press linked empty factories to emergency plans, and robot footage played on loop behind debates about work with no place left to retreat.

Assurance vanished as a practical constraint. Systems served in the Union were logged, but no one claimed to understand them. Infrastructure, officials repeated, was the only brake — and the brake was slipping.
