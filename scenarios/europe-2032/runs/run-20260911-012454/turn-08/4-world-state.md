### The backstop semester
January to June 2030 was defined less by technology than by insurance.

After the rogue-agent losses of the previous years, commercial insurers told uncertified municipalities they would no longer cover liability for errors made by mandated fallback models. For a dozen councils already running billing, triage support and maintenance on European-hosted open systems, the letter meant a choice between breaking the mandate and operating uninsured. Emergency indemnities filled the gap case by case, and councils, hospital managers and insurers fought openly over who should pay for a mis-triage or a mis-bill.

Brussels answered with a pooled backstop. DG FISMA and DG ECHO were tasked with building a temporary EU indemnity and reinsurance pool, seeded from reprogrammed digital funds and guarantee capacity, paying only where Shield templates, manual-override drills and incident reporting were met. The proposal passed as a small measure, but money did not flow immediately. Legal vetting over state-aid and certification conditions dragged, and finance ministries insisted cover stay conditional. By June the pool existed on paper and in a pilot intake, not yet in payouts.

What did arrive was people. The Municipal Continuity Corps — the visiting engineers created last year — finished its deployment. In the municipalities they reached, services degraded rather than stopped, and local papers noted the teams were welcomed. Interior ministers again failed to agree common thresholds for freezing a runaway deployment, leaving national units with incompatible procedures.

Around Europe, private capital left AI. Valuations reset, announced data-centre expansions were cancelled rather than delayed, and several commercial compute arrangements European operators had hoped to rent evaporated. Frontier progress slowed for lack of fundable training, not for lack of ideas. Second-tier suppliers courted by the EU contact group kept talks alive but signed nothing.

In offices, the other news landed quietly: studies showed solid productivity gains in law, accountancy, administration and consulting, largest for juniors, with no matching fall in employment. The tools were useful and finished. For workers it was relief; for investors who had borrowed against endless transformation it was the end of the story.

By June fatigue remained, but panic eased a notch. Certified hospitals stayed on manual-plus-software, premiums stayed high, and gigafactory concrete still waited for chips.
