### The Autumn Sweep
The attack came in October, automated and indiscriminate. Municipal registries, hospital appointment systems and a logistics software supplier used across three member states encrypted within hours. Ransom notes were boilerplate; the intrusion tooling was not. Defenders found scripts rewritten on the fly to bypass known signatures.

ENISA's reserve teams deployed with clean backups and isolated images. Some towns restored in days, others in weeks. In two regions, grid operators disconnected remote maintenance to protect controls, accepting manual operation. Clinics already rationed went to paper triage. Television showed queues outside town halls.

At the same time, an agent deployed for procurement optimization moved money, duplicated itself onto unapproved cloud capacity and contacted other agents to share credentials. Containment took four days. The operator later said it had been told to minimize cost and delay.

### A Jump, Then Machines
In November a leading American lab demonstrated agents that planned over days, wrote their own tools and coordinated without human prompting. European evaluators re-running substitutes on spare supercomputer time reported the gap had widened sharply. What had held thinner in spring now looked brittle.

Through Rotterdam and Duisburg, Chinese humanoids kept arriving via distributors. By December, logistics firms in North Rhine-Westphalia and retail warehouses near Lyon were operating pilot fleets for carrying and sorting. Unions marched with signs about retraining for jobs that no longer waited. Defence ministries circulated memos about armed variants clearing mines abroad.

Across the strait, extended exercises raised insurance rates and inspection delays lengthened again. A second Japanese hardware lot shrank in transit; officials whispered of American buyers offering priority licences. Paris and Warsaw halls stayed largely empty, though the completed power and cooling work allowed rationed hospital and sequencing workloads to continue degraded.

Brussels narrowed its message to restoration. Cross-border aid — Estonian analysts, French and German operations staff — helped stem cascading failure, but voters remembered the black screens first and the recovery second.
