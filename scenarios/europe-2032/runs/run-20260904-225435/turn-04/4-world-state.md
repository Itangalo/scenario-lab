### Europe Acts Under Siege

The Taiwan blockade abruptly severed access to advanced semiconductor exports, transforming AI policy into a national security imperative. In response, the EU invoked Article 356 TFEU to propose the **Sovereign Semiconductor Conversion Initiative**, aiming to compel firms like ASML and Soitec to redirect a share of next-node output toward sovereign AI compute. Legal authority was asserted, but negotiations remain tense and unresolved: industry warns of retaliation, Washington has not clarified its stance, and no funding mechanism has been established. Without compensation provisions or a clear budget, the proposal faces immediate legal and economic hurdles. Implementation is blocked pending resolution of these issues, and no physical conversion has begun. As a result, the initiative remains in early negotiation, with no measurable impact on compute stockpiles or open-weight control this turn.

The **Secure Open-Source Evaluation Access Framework**, introduced in Turn 3, was completed this turn. Certification of air-gapped evaluation sites at CEA, BSI, and the EU AI Office is now finalized. The Code of Responsible Evaluation is enforceable law, though academic exclusion fuels criticism. This strengthens EU oversight at a critical moment.

The **European AI Incident Response Corps** expanded telemetry links to seven member states and began live-fire simulations. It remains operational and ongoing, but full capacity is distant. The completion of the Secure Open-Source Evaluation Access Framework clears one major item from the portfolio; however, the Incident Response Corps continues as an active large measure.

Political capital eroded further under the weight of four large measures. The completion of a category 6 measure — the Secure Open-Source Evaluation Access Framework — brought a +3 bonus justified by emergent cyber threat evidence, but this was offset by ongoing charges. The attempted launch of a new large measure — though justified by crisis — strained cohesion and was not operationalized due to capacity, legal, and funding constraints. Public sentiment held steady at 42.0, reflecting ambivalence: dependence grows, trust does not.
