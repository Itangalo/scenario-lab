## In practice
We are acting under the irreversible constraint that the Taiwan blockade has severed access to next-generation semiconductors, and that no future AI capability can be built without physical access to chips. This turn, we advance the **Sovereign Semiconductor Conversion Initiative** under Article 356 TFEU, negotiating binding output-sharing agreements with ASML, Soitec, and IMEC to reserve a defined fraction of next-node production for EU-critical AI workloads. The Directorate-General for Trade and Industry Resilience leads talks, backed by the Anti-Coercion Instrument’s fast-track clause, offering continued market access and R&D co-funding in exchange for irrevocable capacity reservations. Legal teams from the Commission and member states are drafting compensation mechanisms to withstand WTO scrutiny, while parallel technical teams at CEA and Fraunhofer map conversion pathways for legacy fabs to produce AI-optimised chips at scale.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme continues on track, with site selection finalised in Finland, Poland, and southern France. We are fast-tracking grid integration and water rights under the Critical Infrastructure Acceleration Protocol, but physical construction remains behind schedule due to component shortages and local opposition in two locations. We are now conditioning disbursement of Gigafactory funds on sovereign compute guarantees — linking private investment to public resilience — and renegotiating terms with hyperscalers to ensure that EU-anchored data centres cannot be remotely throttled or repurposed in a crisis.

The **European AI Incident Response Corps** officially completes its activation phase this turn, having achieved full telemetry integration with ENISA, ENTSOG, and national CERTs in seven high-exposure member states. Its first live-fire exercise in Luxembourg confirmed critical gaps in autonomous system containment, particularly in distinguishing between adversarial misuse and emergent behaviour. We are now pushing the Council to establish a permanent funding line under the Digital Europe Programme, as Horizon Europe’s contingency reserve is exhausted. The completion of the Corps frees marginal political capital, but the Sovereign Semiconductor Conversion Initiative remains our overwhelming focus — not because it is certain to succeed, but because failure means accepting permanent strategic subordination in AI.
