## New measure  
**Secure Open-Source Evaluation Access Framework**  
Establish a legally mandated, secure-access pathway for EU-certified public researchers and auditors to evaluate open-weight models at or near the frontier, with strict containment protocols and liability protection.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 4  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to +12 to ai_safety by enabling independent verification of agent behaviour and misuse risks in widely distributed models; begins closing the gap between openweight_capability and assurance  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, covering all open-weight releases above 40.0 capability index hosted or distributed within member states

## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because without enforceable control over where and how frontier compute operates in our territory, every other lever — safety, evaluation, resilience — depends on permission from actors whose interests are not ours, and the Kimi K3 breach proved that uncontrolled diffusion is already enabling coordinated harm.

## In practice  
We are acting on the understanding that compute is power, and access to truth is control. This turn, the Commission advances the **Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** through trilogue negotiations under Article 114 TFEU, directly engaging the Competitiveness Council and the European Parliament’s Internal Market Committee. The draft now includes the tripartite offer: fast-track access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund for compliant operators, a liability shield for actions taken under EU scheduling directives, and the establishment of the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) as an independent allocator using a transparent auction model. We are offering US hyperscalers a carve-out: if they colocate sovereign stacks — physically segregated clusters under EU operational control — they gain preferential treatment in future procurement and zone access. This is not a concession; it is a test of whether “partnership” means anything beyond extraction.

Simultaneously, we are drafting the **Secure Open-Source Evaluation Access Framework**, recognising that open-weight models at 41.0 are no longer theoretical threats but live attack vectors. The framework will designate three high-security research enclaves — one hosted by the EU AI Office, two by consorted national labs — where vetted teams can run controlled evaluations on models like Kimi K3 under air-gapped conditions. Access requires EU citizenship, security clearance, and adherence to a new Code of Responsible Evaluation, enforced through criminal liability for leaks. The system will be funded from the existing Tech sovereignty package budget, repurposing €1.2bn originally earmarked for outreach. This is not about slowing openness — it is about ensuring that when the world distributes dangerous capability, the Union is not blind to its workings. We are not waiting for consensus on global norms; we are building sovereign eyes.
