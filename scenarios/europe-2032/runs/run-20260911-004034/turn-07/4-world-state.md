### The wave
It began as a paper. A genome model, outside Europe, produced a workable design for an organism able to infect humans. Virologists argued over methods, accused the authors of alarmism and of printing a recipe at once. The argument never left specialist channels.

Weeks later people began dying. A modified agent, with clear traces of model assistance in its design, escaped or was released — the investigations never settled which in 2029. Hospitals in three member states reported clusters of severe pneumonia that did not test for anything they knew. Sequencing, pooled on strained supercomputing time, came back with the worst answer: it was designed.

By autumn the continent was in pandemic mode.

### Triage Europe
Brussels declared a health emergency. Reporting from labs and hospitals became mandatory, joint buying of masks, antivirals and tests restarted, and temporary limits on movement and gatherings returned in border regions. Work was channelled through the existing EU Supply Leverage and Continuity Compact: the continuity cell that had rationed chips now rationed chemicals for diagnostics, remaining compute for hospitals and grid operators, with empty factory shells mothballed and data-centre sites repurposed for medical logistics.

No new large regime takes full effect this turn. A broader Bio-Emergency Containment and Continuity Regime was tabled as the single new proposal for this turn, but as a high-cost measure it cannot complete in one turn. Staffing, money and clean-room capacity are missing, so the narrative is delay and partial blockage: the first gigafactory shells outside Paris and Warsaw received power and clean-room fit, though with no machines to fill them they became storage and sequencing annexes. The evaluation unit's power to pause risky launches was extended in principle to a hard stop on further biology-capable releases, but enforcement remains partial while legal powers and inspectors are stood up.

It was not enough to stop cascade. Clinics overflowed, energy and water utilities ran on skeleton crews, care robots from third-party distributors were seized for disinfection runs. Voters who had resented rationing for cancer care now queued for triage tents. Trust in institutions briefly rallied around civil-protection staff, then sank as deaths rose and every other front — chips, models, access to American systems — froze. Political capital is spent, not gained, in triage.

By December, Europe was holding but hollowed: services running, degraded, and nothing else moving.
