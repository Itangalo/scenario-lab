## Priority
**Priority:** Operationalise the Sovereign AI Core as the exclusive runtime environment for all EU-critical AI functions  
Because the Union’s authority now hinges not on promises, but on presence — and because the Resilience Mesh, the cyber incident, and member-state defection have all shown that capability without control is chaos — this turn we shift from building to running. The Core is not yet sovereign if our own agents still depend on foreign clouds; it is not hardened if our most sensitive functions are not on it. We prioritise this not over resilience, but to make resilience ours.

## In practice
We are acting on a single truth: infrastructure without operation is not sovereignty. The Sovereign AI Core in Brittany and northern Sweden is physically complete, but it is not yet sovereign ground — not while Germany runs patching agents on US cloud infrastructure and Hungary imports AI services through backchannels. The cyber incident this turn was not a failure of detection, but of jurisdiction: the Resilience Mesh patched fast, but no one could verify whether the fix introduced new risks. That is the danger — capability without accountability — and it grows with every uncontrolled deployment.

So we act. This turn, we migrate the first wave of EU-critical AI functions — EAAD red-teaming, Hardened Patching agents, and supply chain monitoring — onto the Core’s air-gapped clusters. Legal bases are drawn from Article 114 TFEU for internal market harmonisation and Article 42(7) TEU for infrastructure defence. Funding flows from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, repurposed under emergency provisions. We offer Germany and Austria a binding opt-in: full runtime transparency and joint audit rights in exchange for early access to Core-hosted services, with fuel-reserve pooling and cross-border grid redundancy as incentives. Hungary is warned: unofficial patches will be cut off from EU data streams after turn 10 unless it joins the Core framework.

The EAAD is empowered to certify only those systems running on sovereign infrastructure for use in critical functions. This is not a ban — it is a condition of access. We are not building an alternative to the global AI ecosystem; we are carving out a zone within it that the Union can see, control, and enforce. If capability doubles, we must be the ones running the machines — or we will not be running anything at all.
