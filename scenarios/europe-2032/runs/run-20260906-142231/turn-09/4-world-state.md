### The Core Lights Up — But No One Joins

The Sovereign AI Core in Brittany and northern Sweden powers on, its clusters now running the first sovereign workloads: EAAD red-teaming agents and hardened patching modules. Yet the migration is symbolic. Germany refuses the opt-in, citing data sovereignty concerns under its constitutional court’s pending review. Austria follows. Hungary ignores the warning, continuing to route critical services through unofficial US-hosted models. The Core operates at 17% capacity, its potential unclaimed.

### Capability Outpaces Control

Global AI capability climbs as automatic verification spreads across legal and clinical domains, accelerating progress outside code. Frontier labs report agentive systems that sustain multi-week research loops without reset. With no safety breakthrough, and systemic blind spots exposed by the recent cyber incident, `ai_safety` erodes further. The Resilience Mesh patches fast but remains unaccountable — a strength that deepens fragmentation.

Open-weight models see moderate gains from incremental training and wider deployment of existing architectures, lifting `openweight_capability` by 1.5 points. However, this progress remains within the bounds of the global frontier, tracking closely behind `ai_capability` without overtaking it. No sudden open-source leap occurs, and no narrated mechanism allows open-weight systems to surge independently.

### A Union Losing Ground

The European Commission proposes using Article 114 to mandate sovereign runtime for critical functions, launching a legislative process under the Digital Markets Act framework. But no enforcement mechanism extends to US-based hyperscalers or developers. Without leverage over non-EU infrastructure, the proposal stalls in early drafting, unable to compel compliance from foreign providers. Hungary’s continued reliance on US-hosted models highlights the jurisdictional ceiling.

Political capital collapses under the weight of three large measures in flight — Sovereign AI Core Programme, Hardened Patching, and the nascent Article 114 initiative — along with the priority cost and lack of delivery. Public sentiment dips as job losses mount and protests target Core construction sites. The new measure begins in proposal form only, with full implementation delayed by governance hurdles and member-state resistance.

### Fragile Gains, Real Threats

A joint threat response among Nordic and Benelux states improves cross-border cyber telemetry, marginally lifting `resilience`. But member-state defection — Hungary’s backchannel deal with a US hyperscaler — triggers another sovereignty hit. The American posture remains retrenchment: no access, no collaboration. The two-year commitment ends not with control, but with the Core standing empty.
