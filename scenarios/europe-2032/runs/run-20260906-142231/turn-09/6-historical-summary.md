The Sovereign AI Core in Brittany and northern Sweden becomes operational, running limited red-teaming and patching workloads, but remains underutilized at 17% capacity due to Germany and Austria withholding participation over constitutional concerns, while Hungary continues relying on unofficial US-hosted models. Global AI capability advances as agentive systems achieve sustained autonomous research, outpacing stagnant safety measures; open-weight models gain 1.5 points in capability but trail behind the frontier. The EU proposes mandating sovereign runtime via Article 114 under the Digital Markets Act, but lacks enforcement power over non-EU providers, stalling progress. Resilience improves marginally through Nordic and Benelux cooperation, yet fragmentation deepens as Hungary strikes a backchannel deal with a US hyperscaler. Public unrest grows amid job losses and protests targeting Core sites, while political capital erodes under unmet expectations and failed initiatives. The two-year effort concludes with the Core symbolically online but largely abandoned, underscoring Europe’s struggle for technological sovereignty amid internal disunity and external retrenchment.
