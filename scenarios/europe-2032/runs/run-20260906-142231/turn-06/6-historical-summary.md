The EU launches the Sovereign AI Core Programme in response to a major cyber intrusion in public health systems, aiming to establish a secure, state-controlled AI infrastructure, but faces deepening fragmentation as Germany, Austria, Italy, and Hungary reject key measures on sovereignty and audit grounds. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty initiative and broader tech sovereignty efforts collapse with no physical deployment, leaving the promised 5GW of low-carbon power unbuilt and €80bn in redirected funds ineffective amid legal gridlock and vanishing private investment. A poisoned software library exposes critical vulnerabilities, revealing that even advanced detection systems failed due to reliance on commercial providers, grounding ENISA’s response and prompting EAAD to expand into runtime monitoring—though enforcement remains hollow without real-time logging across member states. Public trust erodes further as hospital disruptions, job losses, and misinformation fuel widespread protests, while EU communication efforts are dismissed as propaganda. Political momentum stalls under legal disputes and institutional inertia, underscoring a crisis of leadership and cohesion. AI capability reaches 60.0 globally, with open-weight models at 48.5, as the incoming US anti-AI administration prepares to take office, signaling potential global shifts.
