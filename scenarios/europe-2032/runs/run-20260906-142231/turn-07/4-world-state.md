### A Fractured Response

The EU declares the Sovereign AI Core Programme operational in law and intent, launching construction in Brittany and northern Sweden under emergency provisions. France, Spain, Finland, and the Netherlands join a Joint Operational Board, ceding partial control over energy and security to anchor the first hardened nodes. Yet Germany and Austria reject integration, calling the move a constitutional overreach, while Italy and Hungary refuse audit compliance—prompting Brussels to enforce “no audit, no access” for high-capability systems in critical sectors. The policy hardens resistance rather than securing unity.

### Access Denied, Coalition Forged

At the same moment, the Union is abruptly cut off from the world’s most advanced AI model. Hospitals and ministries relying on it face immediate disruption. The justification is vague, the appeal nonexistent. The blow confirms the urgency of sovereignty—but also exposes how little ground has actually been secured. In response, early diplomatic feelers are extended to Japan, Canada, Norway, and South Korea to explore mutual access frameworks based on shared semiconductor IP and rare earth processing leverage. No formal coalition is established this turn; talks remain preliminary, and US scrutiny over potential Taiwan inclusion halts momentum. Coordination remains fragile, with no binding agreements signed.

### Institutional Paralysis Deepens

Multiple member states deploy incompatible AI monitoring systems, fragmenting threat response and eroding trust. The Commission’s mediation fails amid legal disputes over data jurisdiction. Meanwhile, a decentralized “Resilience Mesh” of open-source developers and municipal IT teams begins patching critical software using open-weight models—bypassing EU channels entirely. The initiative is effective but uncontrolled, highlighting the gap between official strategy and grassroots action.

### Stalled Momentum

The InvestAI Gigafactories programme officially concludes with no new sites completed—delays in permitting and energy grid access doom physical rollout. While symbolic value was hoped for, no measurable political capital is gained due to the absence of tangible delivery or crisis mitigation. Public sentiment remains low, as job losses and system failures dominate headlines. The narrative of elite detachment solidifies.
