The EU declares the Sovereign AI Core Programme operational, initiating construction in Brittany and northern Sweden under emergency powers, with France, Spain, Finland, and the Netherlands forming a Joint Operational Board and ceding partial sovereignty over energy and security. Germany and Austria reject integration on constitutional grounds, while Italy and Hungary refuse audit compliance, triggering Brussels’ “no audit, no access” policy for high-capability AI in critical sectors, further deepening fragmentation. The Union is abruptly cut off from the world’s most advanced AI model, disrupting hospitals and ministries, and confirming the need for sovereignty—but also exposing its absence. Preliminary diplomatic talks begin with Japan, Canada, Norway, and South Korea to form a mutual AI access coalition leveraging semiconductor IP and rare earth processing, though US concerns over Taiwan involvement stall progress and no binding agreements emerge. Member states deploy incompatible AI monitoring systems, paralyzing coordinated threat response, while a decentralized “Resilience Mesh” of open-source developers and local IT teams fills the gap using open-weight models—effective but outside official control. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme ends without completing any sites due to permitting and energy grid delays, failing to deliver tangible results or boost public confidence. With no physical infrastructure built, trust continues to erode amid job losses and systemic failures, cementing perceptions of elite detachment and institutional paralysis.
