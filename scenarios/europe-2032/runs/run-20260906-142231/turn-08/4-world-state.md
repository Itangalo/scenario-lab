### The Core Under Strain

Construction of the Sovereign AI Core in Brittany and northern Sweden continues, but sabotage of a key substation by an anti-AI collective delays grid integration by weeks. Military engineering units are deployed under Article 42(7) to secure the sites, marking the first invocation of mutual defence for infrastructure protection. Despite this, Germany and Austria reject runtime access offers, citing constitutional limits, while Hungary mocks the Union’s dependence even as it benefits from unofficial patches.

### A Fractured Response to Crisis

A major cyber incident—driven by AI-generated exploitation of a critical software dependency—disrupts hospital scheduling and regional power grids across three member states. The Resilience Mesh, a decentralized network of open-source developers, patches vulnerabilities faster than any central authority. The EU responds by launching the Hardened Patching and Runtime Monitoring initiative, deploying EU-controlled AI agents on air-gapped clusters to detect and patch threats in real time. Trusted Mesh teams are integrated under EU liability protection, but tensions flare over accountability and control.

### Political Capital Erodes Further

The EU reclassifies its strategic identity around *act_under_uncertainty* and adopts the position *no_ground_without_core*, declaring the Core Programme non-negotiable. Yet the InvestAI Gigafactories programme ends with no physical sites completed, eroding credibility. Labour displacement accelerates, with entry-level jobs in law, accountancy, and software vanishing. Public sentiment sinks further as protests turn physical—data centre occupations and grid sabotage underscore growing backlash.

### Sovereignty Without Support

Despite the urgency, sovereignty declines: the US posture remains *RETRENCHMENT*, slowing global AI progress but offering no access to Europe. *ai_capability* rises to 64.5, widening the gap with safety, which plummets after the cyber incident exposed systemic blind spots. Resilience sees a marginal gain from partial deployment of patching agents, but fragmentation and sabotage limit gains. Political capital drops further as the portfolio cost bites and no measure delivers visible success.
