## In practice
We are invoking the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s crisis clause to override national and contractual claims on EuroHPC capacity, directing all member states to prioritise the Sovereign Core Initiative as a matter of operational survival. Under Article 122 TFEU, we are classifying the current capability gap as a systemic threat to internal market and public service continuity, enabling the immediate repurposing of 60% of EuroHPC’s exascale capacity — up from the original 30% — for sovereign training runs. The European Commission is fast-tracking tenders for 10,000 next-generation AI accelerators under the Anti-Coercion Instrument, leveraging our market access as leverage to secure priority delivery from manufacturers in Taiwan and South Korea, with deployment scheduled for turn 8.

At the same time, we are accelerating the Institutional Integrity Audit’s technical mandate to include real-time monitoring of all sovereign model training runs, with the European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) deploying embedded auditors at every training site by turn 8. The Public Trust Observatory will begin publishing monthly integrity scores for all systems integrated into critical functions, starting with health and energy. Resistance from private labs with existing EuroHPC leases is mounting, particularly from French and German firms, but we are offering compensation via expanded access to non-critical compute bands and fast-tracked licensing for non-sensitive applications. The Sovereign Core will not be perfect — interpretability will suffer as we scale — but it must be *functional*. We are no longer building a fallback. We are building the foundation of a sovereign state.
