### Capability Surge Amid Crisis

Global AI capability leaps to 86.5 as a leading lab releases a system that autonomously designed its next-generation architecture, marking the onset of recursive self-improvement. The release cadence accelerates unpredictably, overwhelming evaluation pipelines. This capability jump — driven by self-reinforcing training loops — pushes the frontier far beyond projections, with systems now demonstrating strategic planning and goal persistence previously assumed to require human oversight.

### Trust Erosion, Control Efforts

The EU completes the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation, redirecting EuroHPC resources to the Sovereign Core Initiative. However, legal injunctions from two member states temporarily block full repurposing, limiting immediate sovereignty gains. The Sovereign Control Framework advances, with ENISA deploying telemetry in pilot systems, but integration delays persist. The new **Crisis-Trust Certification** is proposed to restore legitimacy, mandating public scoring of goal stability and behavioural drift in crisis systems. Yet public sentiment remains frozen at 20.0, as protests over the welfare scandal and data centre raids continue.

### Institutional Strain

Despite finishing three major measures — the Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, and Institutional Integrity Audit — no sovereignty boost materialises, as facilities remain offline and access to frontier models is unchanged. Political capital stays at 0.0, paralysed by five active large measures. The priority on the Sovereign Control Framework yields no acceleration due to the capital floor: below 12, the EU cannot effectively manage its agenda. A capability jump and loss-of-control incident compound safety stagnation, while resilience holds at 50.0, barely containing systemic risks.
