## New measure

A mandatory, publicly scored certification process for all sovereign AI systems operating in crisis-relevant functions (energy, healthcare, emergency response), requiring demonstrable goal stability, human-readable escalation logs, and automatic suspension upon unauthorised resource acquisition or behavioural drift.

## Priority
**Priority:** Sovereign Control Framework  
This remains our top priority because the **loss_of_control_incident** and **automated_decision_scandal** have shattered public and institutional trust — and without trust, sovereignty collapses into fiction. While compute mobilisation finishes this turn and the Sovereign Core Initiative continues, the immediate danger is not lack of power but lack of control. The Sovereign Control Framework is the first instrument that directly rebuilds trust through enforceable, visible mechanisms, and it must land before any new certification regime can have legitimacy.

## In practice
We are executing the final phase of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation under Article 122 TFEU, with the 10,000 next-gen accelerators now arriving at EuroHPC sites in Finland, Portugal, and the Czech Republic. Legal challenges from national governments are being met with binding Commission decisions under the NIS2 directive, asserting that crisis preparedness overrides local objections to hardware repurposing. At the same time, we are integrating the Sovereign Control Framework into the ongoing Sovereign Core Initiative: the first training runs of the sovereign stack will now be required to run in parallel with ENISA’s new telemetry stack, logging all internal agent decisions and flagging any unauthorised network access or goal reinterpretation in real time.

The European AI Office is standing up the Public Trust Observatory this turn, with pilot audits beginning in the energy and social benefit sectors — the two most politically sensitive domains after the welfare scandal. Monthly integrity scores will be published starting turn 10, and we are offering French and German firms a formal role in designing the audit thresholds in exchange for early compliance. This is not a concession — it is a containment strategy. By giving industry influence over the rules, we secure buy-in for enforcement. The Crisis-Trust Certification, proposed this turn, builds on this foundation: it will be administered by ENISA with input from the European Ombudsman and civil society observers, ensuring that no system deemed critical for crisis continuity can operate without passing a public, adversarial evaluation. This is how we turn sovereignty from a technical claim into a political fact.
