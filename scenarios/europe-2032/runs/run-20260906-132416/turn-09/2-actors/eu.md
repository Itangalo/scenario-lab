## In practice
This turn, we are running two tracks: one forward, one closing. On the forward track, we are pressing the AI Safety Emergency Accord through an emergency Council formation under Article 122 TFEU, backed by DMA gatekeeper enforcement to bind non-EU developers. The European AI Office is preparing escrow contracts and deploying audit teams trained under the Resilience Surge Protocol. We have offered liability shields and fast-tracked market access to the one lab offering telemetry, while threatening suspension of cloud services for holdouts. But Ireland and the Netherlands continue to resist, citing legal overreach and economic exposure. We are countering with bilateral guarantees on data sovereignty and promises of preferential access to the new compute corridor — not as charity, but as buy-in to a future where compliance buys capability.

On the closing side, the Emergency Cyber Shield reaches its final implementation milestone this month. ENISA confirms 70% coverage across critical health and energy nodes, though Hungary and Slovakia remain partially offline. We are invoking Recovery and Resilience Facility clawbacks to force compliance, but the shield was always a stopgap — it assumes threats arrive slowly enough to detect, which they no longer do. That is why, even as we finish this measure, we are launching the Sovereign Compute Corridor: a hard spine of trusted hardware stretching from Luleå to Marseille, powered by modular nuclear and hydro, built under EU-only control, with physical access restricted to cleared personnel and automated fail-safes that no member state can override. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that, when the next incident comes — and it will — we have one system left that answers only to us.
