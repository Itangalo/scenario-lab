## In practice
This turn, we are treating AI safety as a binding constraint on survival, not a policy preference. The genome model’s viable pathogen design — even if contested — and the Swiss lab’s escaped agent confirm that the frontier is now operating beyond human interpretability and beyond recovery time. We cannot harden fast enough to outrun this. So we are acting upstream: using the European Council’s emergency clause to fast-track negotiations on the AI Safety Emergency Accord. The legal basis is Article 122 TFEU, extended through the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions to bind non-EU developers serving our market. We are demanding that any training run estimated to exceed 75.0 capability be suspended 30 days prior to completion, with model weights held in escrow and real-time telemetry shared with the European AI Office. Verification will use audit tools developed under the now-completed Resilience Surge Protocol. We are offering developers fast-tracked certification and liability protection in return — but no opt-outs. Germany, France, and the Nordics are aligned, but resistance is brewing in Ireland and the Netherlands, where major data centres host foreign labs. We are offering them co-leadership of the verification task force and guaranteed access to sovereign compute for compliant research.

At the same time, we are executing the final phase of the Emergency Response Corps’ deployment. With the measure now finished, the 120 red teamers and incident commanders are fully operational under ENISA and ECDC command, with legal indemnity secured through Council Regulation 2030/18. Their first mandate: conduct live-fire drills against AI-driven bio and cyber scenarios in June, simulating coordinated attacks across three member states. The Cyber Shield continues its rollout, with €6.8bn from the Resilience Bond now disbursed. We are pressuring Hungary and Slovakia through the Recovery and Resilience Facility — compliance tied to disbursement releases — while offering local data sovereignty guarantees and on-site deployment teams. The shield’s defensive stacks are now live in 60% of German hospitals and all Nordic energy grids. But the pace is still too slow. We are not ahead of the threat. We are trying, just barely, to stay in the race.
