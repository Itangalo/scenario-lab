### The Surge That Wasn’t

The EU doubles down on crisis response. The European AI Emergency Response Corps begins recruitment and is embedded across ENISA and the ECDC, but legal resistance stalls deployment: three member states refuse to ratify the liability shielding protocol, citing constitutional concerns over extraterritorial intervention powers. Without full cross-border authorisation, the corps remains a skeleton force, its operational mandate hobbled.

Simultaneously, the Commission launches the Mandatory Pre-Deployment Biosecurity Audit Regime, demanding adversarial testing for all high-capability models sold in the EU. While framed as a sovereignty play through standards, pushback is immediate. U.S. labs denounce the regime as a de facto market blockade, and Meta suspends planned EU model releases pending legal review. No external developer fully complies in this turn; alignment talks stall over data access and intellectual property.

### Frontier Leaps Ahead

A major capability jump occurs: a leading lab releases a system that autonomously designs and simulates novel viral vectors, compressing months of research into hours. This pushes `ai_capability` to 76.0. The leap is opaque — internal decision pathways are no longer interpretable — and no safety evaluation lands on the new systems. With the audit regime unimplemented and the jump unmitigated, `ai_safety` plummets by 7 points to 7.0.

The leaked biosecurity assessment confirming low-cost pathogen recreation circulates in closed policy channels, intensifying alarm but not public awareness. Resilience holds at 39.0 — the Surge Protocol funds are allocated, but legal fragmentation prevents scaling. Openweight capability rises to 58.0, reflecting lagged diffusion of prior frontier models.

### Political Capital Frozen

The portfolio charge remains crushing: four large measures in flight cost −12, the priority −1. A +1 proposal bonus is granted for the bio audit regime, responding directly to the emergent leak, but this is offset by ongoing strain. Political capital remains at 11.0 — too low to gain legitimacy from public sentiment, and too thin to force member-state alignment. Sovereignty falls to 14.0 as capability growth outpaces domestic build-out and no category 4 measure completes.
