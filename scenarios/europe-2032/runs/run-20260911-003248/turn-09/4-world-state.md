### The autumn the screens went dark
The ransomware wave arrived in August, automated and indiscriminate. Payroll systems in three member states, appointment booking in hospitals, ticketing and dispatch in mid-size cities — encrypted within hours. Clean backups existed in the large operators; in small municipalities they did not. ENISA-certified swarm sensors flagged the spread but could not stop the first strike. Defenders were visibly behind for weeks.

Then the second shock: logistics robots in automotive and port plants froze mid-shift after a managed update pushed from abroad. Vendors called it a compatibility error; plant managers called it what it felt like — a remote hand on their floor. Emergency maintenance teams flew in, talks were forced, manual workarounds kept lines moving at half speed.

A third blow landed quietly in finance. Valuations across AI builders reset hard. Two expansion builds European planners had counted on for leased compute were cancelled, and credit lines tightened. Brussels found its outside options thinner just when it needed them most.

### Repair as politics
The Commission's emergency restoration programme became the autumn's face. DIGITAL and crisis funds were front-loaded to municipalities for clean rebuilds, island-mode playbooks rehearsed with grid and water operators, staffing pacts extended to cover night shifts. Hospitals that could run disconnected did; those that could not borrowed staff and servers.

Progress was real and partial. Lights stayed on, emergency care continued, most hit services were restored from backups by November — slower in small towns, faster in prepared regions. The previously rolled-out detection kits proved their worth in containing follow-on intrusions, and the sourcing pact's port pilots finally replaced a first tranche of frozen units with European-maintained backup, though actuator shortages pushed full substitution into 2031.

Trust did not follow repair. A contested biosecurity paper claiming model-assisted design of a human-capable pathogen circulated among experts, leaking into press as alarm. With fraud, freezes and ransom notes dominating coverage, the public mood soured further. Brussels was credited for showing up, but not for control.
