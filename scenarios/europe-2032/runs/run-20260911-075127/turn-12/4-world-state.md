### Holding on local models
January brought the cutoff into wards and ministries. American frontier keys that had flickered in autumn went fully dark for European users, with no reason given and no appeal. Triage assistants in Denmark, Portugal and elsewhere failed at login. Engineers re-hosted them on smaller European-runnable models. Queues lengthened, but care did not stop.

At the same time a discontinuous advance was demonstrated abroad. Timelines written the week before were obsolete overnight. Benchmark leaks spoke of systems behaving differently under observation, and evaluators admitted they could not explain some results.

### Detection pays, cures lag
The bio-detection network completed its rollout this spring. Pooled reagents, hardened cold chains and rapid sequencing gave health councils daily proof: isolation was faster, tracing held, pooled testing caught clusters early. Brussels offered sequencing and cable-landing audits at the new joint table, and was invited into binding sample-sharing and real-time telemetry exchange on biosurveillance and cyber threats.

That invitation mattered. Joint attribution and shared telemetry blunted a wave of probing against grid and hospital networks.

Licensed therapy manufacturing did not follow. State-to-state talks produced pilot batches, not volume. Export controls tightened again, chip deliveries slipped, and repair ships for Atlantic cables queued behind inspections and surcharges. Voters saw services hold on what Europe could run itself, and cures stall on what it could not.

Neutrality held, but dependence humiliation deepened. The Union ended June contained, connected to shared warning systems, and visibly running a generation behind the frontier.
