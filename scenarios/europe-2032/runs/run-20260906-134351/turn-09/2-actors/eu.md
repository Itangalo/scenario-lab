## New measure
**Measure:** Fallback Compute Assurance Programme  
Launch a €38bn emergency initiative to map, secure, and integrate shadow compute alliances into a legally recognised, auditable fallback network under EU oversight, transforming informal capacity into sovereign redundancy.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 13  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–20 points; stabilise resilience by formalising access to 15–20 exaflops of currently unaccounted compute; reduce dependency on foreign hyperscalers by creating a binding legal and operational framework for crisis access  
Applies to: Mid-sized member states with informal compute alliances (Slovakia, Finland, Denmark, Ireland), EU-approved foreign data centres in Japan, South Korea, and Canada, and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU)

## Priority
**Priority:** Emergency Continuity Stack  
We are pushing this measure hardest because the cyber crisis has proven that continuity is sovereignty — and without a functioning fallback stack, every audit, every verification, every protest is academic. The ransomware wave is not slowing, and when the next cascade hits, we will have nothing to fall back on unless this stack is operational.

## In practice  
We are forcing the final integration of the **Emergency Continuity Stack** through the Civil Protection Mechanism, overriding Italian objections with a Council decision under Article 122 TFEU. The air-gapped nodes are now running at 57.5 capability on frozen openweight models, hosted on repurposed German and French servers, with ENISA conducting red-teaming this quarter. Full redundancy remains delayed, but we are deploying regionally: the first three nodes go live in Lyon, Berlin, and Warsaw by Q4. HERA is coordinating medical triage logic, while the Commission’s Crisis Cell manages logistics routing. We are accepting risk — the models are static, unlearning, and narrow — but they are *ours*, and they are *on*.

The **Sovereign Verification Regime** enters its enforcement phase this turn. EAAD+ has issued binding audit notices; one U.S. developer complied, two refused checkpoint handover. We are invoking Article 17 to suspend their models in oncology, energy, and border systems. Legal challenges are incoming, but we are pre-emptively filing with the CJEU for an advisory opinion to solidify our authority. We are also fast-tracking the recruitment of 1,200 red-team engineers through Horizon Europe, targeting Eastern European technical universities and offering dual postings with national cyber agencies. The Slovak bilateral deal is now frozen under EU scrutiny — no member state can outsource its sovereignty.

We are launching the **Fallback Compute Assurance Programme** in direct response to the emergent black market in sovereign compute. This is not a punishment, but a recognition: the mid-sized states acted from necessity, not disloyalty. We are convening them under EuroHPC JU to map existing foreign access deals, classify them by security tier, and bind them to EU audit and activation protocols. Japan, South Korea, and Canada are being approached through the Digital Partnerships Council for mutual recognition of fallback access. This is not ideal — it externalises risk — but it is real. We are turning shadow capacity into sovereign insurance, before it becomes a backdoor for coercion.
