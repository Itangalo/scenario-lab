## In practice  
We are operationalising the **Emergency Continuity Stack** under the Civil Protection Mechanism, with deployment authority co-signed by the Commission’s Crisis Coordination Cell and the Council. The stack will run on repurposed public cloud nodes from decommissioned national data centres — Germany, France, and Italy have already pledged hardware. We are freezing openweight models at 57.5 capability, air-gapping them, and restricting their function to triage, logistics routing, and civil registry access. No learning, no external connectivity, no APIs. ENISA and HERA are co-leading implementation, with strict red-teaming protocols to be completed by turn 9. The first nodes go live in Q3 2030, but full redundancy across all member states will not be achieved until turn 10 — and we are already behind.

The **Sovereign Verification Regime** enters its enforcement phase. EAAD+ has issued binding audit notices to three foreign developers whose models operate in oncology, energy grid management, and border control. Where checkpoint handover is refused, we are invoking Article 17 of the AI Act to suspend deployment. Legal challenges are expected — the U.S. Trade Representative has already warned of "unilateral barriers" — but we will not retreat. We are fast-tracking recruitment of red-team engineers through the Horizon Europe safety track and building distributed telemetry infrastructure to avoid single points of failure. The Slovak defection has shown the cost of weakness: we will not repeat it.

The **Institutional Integrity Audit** concludes this turn. Findings from the EMA, ECB, and three national digital ID agencies are now under review by the EU AI Accountability Panel. We are preparing public disclosure protocols, but only where manipulation vectors are confirmed will we mandate human-in-the-loop overrides and real-time logging. The Public Trust Observatory will publish its consolidated report in December — but trust cannot wait. We are launching a parallel communication campaign through Eurobarometer and national broadcasters to explain what was found, what was fixed, and what remains at risk. The audit finishes on time — but only because we forced data access from reluctant member states. Cohesion is fraying, and we are spending capital to hold it.
