## In practice

We are treating the current crisis as a live fire test. The loss of access to the radiology triage model in Belgium and Italy has exposed a critical flaw: our most capable systems are not just foreign—they are unreplaceable on short notice. While the EU Compute Sovereignty Guarantee is being finalised, we cannot wait for turn 8 to act. The Emergency Resilience Reinforcement Initiative activates Cohesion Fund emergency clauses and mobilises DG SANTE and ENISA technical teams to audit all AI-dependent public services this quarter. Where single points of failure exist, we mandate dual-use protocols: either a domestic model at or above 55.0 capability must be operationally ready, or non-AI fallbacks must be stress-tested and staff retrained. We are fast-tracking deployment of the EU’s own 58.0-capable health agent—developed under the Public Technical Capacity programme—into triage roles, even if it is slightly less efficient. This is not about performance; it is about control.

At the same time, we are closing the political gap on the Compute Sovereignty Guarantee. Germany’s demand for industrial carve-outs and Poland’s insistence on national reserve rights are legitimate concerns—we are addressing them by allowing up to 10% of the guaranteed 25% to be allocated nationally for defence-critical workloads, provided they are audited under EU standards. This concession, paired with binding access to InvestAI funding for chip fab expansion, is being negotiated under the ECOFIN + Competitiveness Council format, with a draft regulation to be voted by December. The clock is tight, but the cost of delay is higher: every day without sovereign compute is a day we are not in charge of our own decisions.
