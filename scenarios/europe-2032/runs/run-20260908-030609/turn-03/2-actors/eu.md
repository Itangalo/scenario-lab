## Priority  
**Sovereign Audit Access Directive** — The automated_decision_scandal this turn proves that post-deployment regulation is too late, and the joint_threat_response gives us leverage to demand transparency from foreign providers under the guise of collective security; we must convert that seat at the table into sovereign control.

## In practice  
We are drafting the Sovereign Audit Access Directive under Article 114 TFEU, using the internal market as legal basis to compel access for ENISA, the EU AI Office, and designated national authorities. The directive will require that any model above 48.0 capability—measured by our own evaluation framework—deployed in the EU must provide auditors with secure, read-only access to weights, training data provenance, and internal telemetry, including agent behaviour logs. This is not a request: it is a condition of market access. We are aligning this with the joint_threat_response framework, framing audit access as a prerequisite for participation in the new EU-led cyber intelligence pool, thereby turning security cooperation into a compliance lever.

Resistance will be fierce from U.S. hyperscalers and even from some EU developers who cite trade secrets and operational risk. To overcome this, we are offering a tiered access model: basic compliance requires only redacted telemetry and hashing-based verification of training data; full access unlocks fast-tracked AI Act certification and preferential procurement status. We are also offering to host the audit infrastructure on sovereign EU cloud backends, with zero-knowledge encryption and strict access controls, to mitigate IP concerns. The automated_decision_scandal has shattered public trust; we cannot afford another incident that outruns our understanding. This directive closes the gap between detection and prevention. We are not banning powerful AI—we are ensuring that no powerful AI operates in the dark.
