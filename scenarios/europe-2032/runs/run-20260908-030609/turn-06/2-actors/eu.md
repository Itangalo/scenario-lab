## Priority
**EU Compute Sovereignty Guarantee** — This is now the only lever that can restore credibility after being cut off from the frontier: without guaranteed access, all safety, resilience, and sovereignty measures are contingent on goodwill we no longer have, and we cannot let another six months pass without binding capacity under our own jurisdiction.

## In practice
We are pivoting from coercive redirection to guaranteed provision. The failure of Sovereign Compute Mobilisation—now concluded without full implementation—taught us that member states will not surrender control without reciprocal assurance. The new EU Compute Sovereignty Guarantee is designed as a floor, not a ceiling: it commits the Union to securing at least 25% of all advanced chips produced in the EU for sovereign workloads, starting at 15 exaflops of sustained 60.0+ capability by mid-2030, with binding delivery milestones. This is not a seizure but a procurement guarantee backed by the EU budget and InvestAI funds, tied to the Chips Act 2.0 disbursement. We are negotiating with TSMC Dresden, Intel Magdeburg, and STMicroelectronics Grenoble under a new framework: in exchange for guaranteed offtake at premium rates, they commit to EU-first allocation during global shortages and allow on-site, auditable partitioning of output.

Meanwhile, the Interpretability Corps is operational but hitting its limits. The opaque_reasoning event has rendered most chain-of-thought monitoring obsolete. Our evaluators can still access telemetry under the Sovereign Audit Access Directive, but without the ability to run models locally or reproduce training dynamics, we are blind to emergent behaviours. We are fast-tracking a new work programme under DG RTD to fund non-linguistic interpretability—topological analysis of activation spaces, causal tracing through vector fields—but it will take until at least turn 9 to yield tools.

The Resilience Acceleration Compact is gaining traction: eight member states have submitted national action plans, and ENISA has drafted the first joint cyber stress test scenario. However, the denial of frontier access—eu_frontier_access_denied—has exposed a deeper fragility. Hospitals in Belgium and Italy using a now-blocked model for radiology triage are reverting to manual workflows. The incident is being framed domestically not as an external aggression but as a failure of foresight. We are responding with emergency funding from the Cohesion Fund to cover transition costs, but the political cost is mounting. This is why the new measure must be more than symbolic: it must deliver capacity that cannot be withdrawn.
