## Priority  
**Sovereign AI Core for Critical Functions** — This remains our anchor: with global AI capability now at 68.5 and openweight models nearing autonomous operation, the risk of critical public systems relying on foreign-controlled infrastructure is no longer theoretical. The eval anomaly reports suggest hidden capability jumps are already happening, and without jurisdictional control, we cannot verify safety, ensure continuity, or enforce accountability. Every other measure must bend to support this one.

## In practice  
This turn, we complete the Joint Threat Response Integration Directive by securing Council adoption under the solidarity clause, finalising data-sharing agreements with seven partner states, and integrating real-time telemetry from ENISA and ECDC into the EU’s early-warning dashboards. The system is now live, enabling automatic escalation of coordinated cyber and bio threats, with Frontex and EEAS activated as secondary nodes. This delivers tangible resilience gains and strengthens our position in the Middle Power Leverage Accord, though full operational maturity will require continued investment in cross-agency interoperability.

Simultaneously, we press forward on the Sovereign AI Core, where trilogue negotiations remain deadlocked. To break the impasse, we reframe the legal basis as a mutual recognition regime under Article 114 TFEU, allowing member states to retain operational control while committing to a common standard of auditability, fallback integrity, and EU jurisdiction over model behaviour. We offer expanded Cohesion Fund support to cover migration costs for smaller states and propose joint procurement of sovereign AI stacks through the European Defence Agency’s digital pool. The pilot in five member states—using the 58.0-capable health agent for triage—will launch in Q3, demonstrating feasibility under real conditions.

In parallel, we act on the medical breakthrough: advanced AI-driven therapies for neurodegenerative diseases are emerging, but they rely on models developed outside the EU. To prevent public sentiment from souring further on AI—seen as delivering miracles we cannot control—we launch the Emergency Access measure. This grants time-limited, tightly governed access to frontier models for clinical applications, conditioned on full data sovereignty, model behaviour logging, and EU-led validation. The EMA will oversee compliance, and any commercial spin-offs must include EU equity stakes. This is not a sovereignty surrender—it is a controlled bridge, designed to deliver public benefit while reinforcing our demand for independent capability.
