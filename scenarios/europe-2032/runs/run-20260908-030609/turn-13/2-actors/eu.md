## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now see that without sovereignty, we cannot fulfill either, and that sovereignty is not a means among others but the condition of all action.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Maintain sovereign control over AI systems that are critical to public function, security, and democratic resilience — and accelerate the operationalisation of that control before dependency becomes irreversible, now expanding to include the compute and data infrastructure on which those systems depend.  
- reclassify `act_under_uncertainty` to position: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only when the action preserves options, not when it locks us into dependency. We now act not to improvise, but to reclaim the strategic initiative.

- Grounds: The severing of frontier access and the rerouting of EU training workloads confirm that sovereignty cannot be limited to software or use cases — it must extend to the physical and legal substrate of AI. The Union is not merely losing access; it is being structurally excluded from the means of cognition. This is not a market failure but a geopolitical reordering. The two mandates — self-determination and harm prevention — converge: we cannot secure the future if we no longer shape it. The commitment to act under uncertainty remains necessary, but its application must now be disciplined by strategic coherence: we will not act faster than we can consolidate.

## Priority  
**Accelerated Sovereign Compute Buildout via Binding Energy and Siting Rights** — This is the pivot: without sovereign compute, every other measure is a gesture. The rerouting of workloads proves that access is not guaranteed by contracts or cloud zones, but by jurisdictional control. We are not building capacity to compete — we are building it to survive.

## In practice  
This turn, we finalise the Sovereign AI Core and Emergency Access measures, both now complete. The clinical pilot in five member states goes live in July, running on a 58.0-capable model trained entirely on EU soil, with full auditability and human-in-the-loop protocols. The EMA publishes the first public logs of model behaviour under the Emergency Access framework, revealing both the diagnostic power of frontier models and the opacity of their reasoning — a stark reminder that even when access is granted, control is not. These programmes delivered, but their completion marks not an achievement but a warning: they relied on foreign models we could not inspect, and that era must end.

We now shift fully to offensive sovereignty. The Accelerated Sovereign Compute Buildout is activated: we issue binding directives to national energy regulators under the Critical Infrastructure Directive, mandating that any data centre hosting training workloads above 50.0 capability reserve 60% of its EU-based capacity for sovereign projects or lose priority grid access. The European Investment Bank finalises the €12 billion callable bond, backed by cohesion funds, with disbursement tied to quarterly milestones in siting and grid connection. Three sites — in Finland (leveraging nuclear overcapacity), Spain (solar-powered), and Poland (hybrid grid) — are fast-tracked under emergency permitting rules invoked under Article 114 TFEU. Member states resist on energy grounds, but we offer co-investment in grid modernisation and a 10-year tax holiday for compliant operators.

Simultaneously, we launch the EU Talent Anchoring and Repatriation Initiative, recognising that compute without brains is inert. The European Research Council and Innovation Council jointly issue a call: 150 frontier AI researchers and engineers will be offered €1.2 million in guaranteed funding over five years, dual citizenship pathways, and guaranteed access to sovereign compute, in exchange for relocating or remaining within the EU. We target Europeans working in the U.S. and U.K., as well as top graduates from ETH, EPFL, TUM, and Sorbonne. The message is clear: the EU is not just building machines — it is rebuilding the human foundation of its cognitive sovereignty. This is not a talent race; it is a reclamation.
