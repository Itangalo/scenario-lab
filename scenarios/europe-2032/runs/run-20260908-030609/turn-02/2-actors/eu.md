## Priority  
**EU Interpretability Corps** — This turn, the verification_widens event proves that safety-relevant domains are expanding faster than oversight can keep up, and the recent interpretability breakthrough is the first real lever we have to match pace; we prioritise building sovereign technical capacity to ground future regulation, not just react to incidents.

## In practice  
We are launching the EU Interpretability Corps under the Joint Research Centre, co-funded by Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme, with a €9.2bn envelope over four turns. The Corps will embed teams in ENISA, EDPB, and national cybersecurity agencies to audit and monitor deployed models, and in EU-funded AI developers to ensure real-time integration of interpretability tools into training pipelines. We are fast-tracking legal access under Article 68 of the AI Act and invoking Article 122 TFEU for urgent innovation to justify bypassing full co-decision — this is not optional infrastructure, it is a control system for systems that already outpace us.

Resistance will come from private developers citing IP and speed, and from member states wary of new compliance burdens. To overcome this, we are offering full liability protection for in-scope models that adopt the framework, and we are pairing access with a new tier of certification under the AI Act that unlocks procurement advantages and fast-tracked market access. We are also negotiating with the three largest EU-based AI firms to pilot the framework under mutual technical assistance agreements, leveraging our position as their largest customer and regulator. The breakthrough in transparency is not a moment to relax — it is a narrow window to build control before capability widens again. We will not waste it.
