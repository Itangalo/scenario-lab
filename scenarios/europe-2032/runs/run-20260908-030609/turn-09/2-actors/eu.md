## Priority
**Sovereign AI Core for Critical Functions** — This is the logical successor to the Compute Sovereignty Guarantee: without controlling not just the compute but the systems that run on it, we have only half the sovereignty we need. The openweight_frontier_release proves that capability is now globally distributed and irreversible—our response must be to anchor the *use* of that capability in our own jurisdiction, not just the hardware.

## In practice
We are treating the completion of the EU Compute Sovereignty Guarantee as a necessary but insufficient step. The fact that open-weight models now reach 52.5—and are downloaded irreversibly into private hands—means that containment through access control is dead. Our focus must shift from securing compute to securing *function*: ensuring that the AI systems upon which hospitals, grids, and emergency services depend are not only available but under our jurisdiction, our audit regime, and our operational control. This turn, we begin drafting the Sovereign AI Core regulation under Article 114 TFEU, with DG CONNECT leading and ENISA providing technical architecture. The core will initially cover triage, grid balancing, and emergency dispatch, requiring that any AI used in these functions must be trained on EU-anchored compute, use open-weight or EU-closed models subject to pre-deployment evaluation, and operate within a monitored, air-gapped environment where updates are logged and verified.

Germany and France have already signaled support, but resistance is emerging from smaller states worried about cost and duplication. To address this, we are offering full Cohesion Fund coverage for the first two years of migration and embedding the Core into the existing Resilience Acceleration Compact framework. We are also fast-tracking certification for the EU’s own 58.0-capable health agent, which will become the default model for triage under the Core. This is not about banning foreign models—it is about making sovereign systems the *only* option for critical functions. The Middle Power Leverage Accord gives us fallback access, but our people must know that when the system fails, the person in charge is accountable to *them*, not to a foreign board or a revoked API key. That is the line we are drawing now.
