## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor the operation of AI systems essential to public function, security, and democratic resilience within EU jurisdiction, ensuring enforceable oversight, operational continuity, and accountability under Union law  
- Grounds: The openweight capability rise to 54.0 confirms that containment through access control is no longer viable; sovereignty must now be defined not by ownership of hardware or models, but by control over *operation*. The two-year commitment must evolve from securing compute to securing function—ensuring that critical AI systems are not only hosted in the EU but are legally, technically, and institutionally answerable to it.

## Priority
**Sovereign AI Core for Critical Functions** — This remains the anchor of our strategic effort: without jurisdictional control over the AI systems that run critical functions, no amount of shared threat intelligence or external cooperation can guarantee our autonomy. The Taiwan tension rise underscores that geopolitical shocks will test not just our readiness but our independence—this measure ensures that when crisis hits, the systems keeping our lights on and our hospitals running answer to EU authorities, not foreign APIs.

## In practice
This turn, we advance the Sovereign AI Core by finalising its technical architecture with ENISA and launching trilogue negotiations under Article 114 TFEU, with DG CONNECT steering and strong backing from the French and German delegations in COREPER. The key hurdle is legal: we are framing the Core as a mutual recognition mechanism for AI systems in critical infrastructure, allowing member states to retain operational control while binding them to a common standard of sovereignty, auditability, and fallback integrity. To overcome resistance from smaller states, we are expanding Cohesion Fund coverage to include full migration support and offering joint procurement of sovereign AI stacks through the European Defence Agency’s digital infrastructure pool. The first pilot—EU-certified AI triage in public hospitals—will launch in Q3 in five member states, using the 58.0-capable health agent developed under the Tech Sovereignty Package.

Simultaneously, we activate the Joint Threat Response Integration Directive by designating ENISA and the ECDC as EU nodes in the new coalition framework, signing data-sharing memoranda with seven partner states and integrating their telemetry into the EU’s early-warning dashboards. This is not a standalone capability build but a binding alignment: we are committing to automatic incident escalation and joint response activation, which requires Council approval under the solidarity clause. To secure that, we are linking the directive to the ongoing resilience review, showing how shared threat visibility reduces domestic risk without requiring new domestic infrastructure. The move strengthens our hand in the Middle Power Leverage Accord and builds trust with partners at a moment of rising tension in the Taiwan Strait—where the same systems used for logistics optimisation could soon be repurposed for coercion.
