### Capability Creeps Forward  
Global AI capability advances to 68.5, driven by self-improving loops in logistics and chip design. While not yet autonomous, the frontier now routinely produces systems that refine their own training pipelines. Open models gain traction: a newly released reasoning agent, loosely based on a leaked architecture, spreads across research and underground forums, contributing to a measured rise in `openweight_capability` to 56.5. The increase reflects rapid diffusion but stays within plausible accumulation bounds, narrowing the gap with frontier systems and raising concerns about misuse in cyber and disinformation.

### EU Responds to Shared Threats  
The European Union formalises its participation in a new joint threat response network, integrating ENISA and the ECDC into real-time intelligence sharing with allied states. Data feeds from seven partners now flow into EU early-warning systems, enabling faster detection of coordinated cyber intrusions and bio anomalies. The move strengthens transatlantic trust amid rising Taiwan Strait tensions, where military drills and shipping disruptions signal deeper instability. The Union’s swift alignment delivers tangible gains in preparedness, absorbing more of future shocks before they cascade.

The Joint Threat Response Integration Directive—a small, category 6 measure—is formally proposed this turn. While operational coordination begins immediately through existing channels, full implementation will require at least one full turn to align legal mandates, technical interfaces, and member-state compliance. It earns a +2 proposal bonus for addressing the joint threat response, but its resilience benefits are not yet realised.

### Sovereignty Efforts Stumble on Law and Language  
Work on the Sovereign AI Core advances technically, with ENISA finalising audit protocols and fallback mechanisms for critical systems. However, trilogue negotiations under Article 114 TFEU stall as smaller member states demand stronger opt-outs and clearer cost guarantees. Despite expanded Cohesion Fund support, legal doubts persist over whether the EU can mandate jurisdictional control without infringing national competences. The French and German push for urgency clashes with Nordic and Baltic caution, delaying a consensus. The pilot in public hospitals remains on track for Q3, but the broader rollout slips.

The EU Compute Sovereignty Guarantee, a prior initiative, was fully completed in Turn 9. Its effects—bolstering domestic data infrastructure and reducing dependency on foreign compute—were already accounted for in last turn’s metrics. No additional sovereignty or political capital bonus is realised in Turn 10. The notepad confirms the measure’s completion was already scored; reapplying its impact would constitute double-counting. As a result, `eu_ai_sovereignty` remains at 21.0, reflecting only the prior gain, the 1.5-point capability-linked decay (−1), and no new positive event. The metric holds steady, consistent with its exposure to both erosion and delayed progress.
