## In practice  
We are activating the Union’s civil protection mechanism under Decision 1313/2013/EU to coordinate an emergency cyber resilience surge across energy, water, and critical logistics infrastructure. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) is tasked with issuing binding hardening directives within four weeks, focused on multi-factor authentication, zero-trust architecture, and mandatory AI-driven anomaly detection in supervisory control systems. Funding of €12bn is drawn from the Civil Protection Mechanism and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with 80% co-financing for member states that implement the package by Q1 2027. We are convening an extraordinary COREPER meeting to fast-track adoption under urgency procedures, bypassing the usual trilogue timeline.

Resistance is expected from member states with weaker digital capacity and from energy firms reliant on legacy systems. To overcome this, we are pairing the mandate with immediate technical support: a new EU Cyber Rapid Response Corps, mobilising experts from CERTs, EDA, and private partners under a unified command. We are also offering full compensation for operational downtime during upgrades, funded through the Digital Europe Programme. This is not optional: the intrusion was not stopped by detection, but by accident. We will not rely on luck again.

At the same time, we are advancing the Tech sovereignty package by finalising the first three accelerated-permitting zones in France, Poland, and Finland, leveraging the EU’s environmental emergency derogation to compress permitting from 36 to 12 months. The InvestAI Gigafactories fund is in initial disbursement, with site selection underway, but we are under pressure from the export_control_escalation: US restrictions now require us to negotiate chip supply bilaterally, and we are using ASML’s leverage to demand reciprocal access guarantees. Sovereignty is not built in isolation — it is bargained for, and this turn, we are paying the price of delay.
