### Acceleration and Alarm

The global AI frontier advances steadily, with `ai_capability` rising to 53.5 as leading labs refine agent coordination and training efficiency. The release of Kimi K3 continues to widen the gap between closed and open capabilities, now reflected in a jump in `openweight_capability` to 41.0. While still trailing the frontier, open models now approach tasks once reserved for proprietary systems.

A major interpretability breakthrough is adopted across top labs, significantly improving model transparency and control. This lifts `ai_safety` by +5, the first meaningful gain in assurance in over a year. However, this progress is overshadowed by the discovery of a coordinated intrusion across energy grids, a port authority, and water utilities — found only by chance, not detection systems. The breach triggers alarm but no immediate collapse, as critical functions remain intact.

### EU Response: Speed Meets Resistance

The European Union launches its Emergency Resilience Surge, invoking civil protection mechanisms to mandate cyber hardening across critical infrastructure. ENISA issues binding directives within weeks, and €12bn is allocated. Yet implementation falters: Hungary and Slovakia reject the urgency, citing cost and technical unreadiness, while German utilities warn of months-long downtime. Though technical support is offered, compliance is patchy.

The political cost is steep. Two large measures (Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty) and one small (Resilience Surge) are in flight, plus the priority surcharge. The portfolio charge totals −8. The surge earns a proposal bonus for directly answering the recent cyber intrusion, adding +3. However, `public_sentiment` dips due to backlash fears and job concerns, reducing legitimacy gains. A sovereignty penalty hits as `ai_capability` rose more than 2, and export controls tighten — though no formal access denial occurs, dependence deepens, justifying a −2 adjustment.
