We are invoking Article 4 of the Treaty on European Union — the solidarity clause — to reframe advanced compute as critical infrastructure under existential threat. Under this authority, the Commission is issuing binding directives to all EU member states and semiconductor facilities on Union territory: no shipment of chips at or below 5nm node may leave EU jurisdiction without Council approval, and all such chips produced in the EU over the next 18 months are pre-allocated to the InvestAI Gigafactories and the EU Interpretability Corps. We are not asking; we are reallocating. This is not export control — it is wartime economic mobilisation under the guise of internal market integrity.

Resistance will come from industry, the U.S. government, and even from member states reliant on export revenue. To contain it, we are offering compensation: a 30% premium on chip purchases made under the directive, paid from the European Defence Fund’s newly expanded technology resilience envelope. We are also fast-tracking antitrust waivers for joint procurement by EU hyperscalers and research institutions, so that pooling demand becomes the only viable path forward. Crucially, we are tying access to the Sovereign Audit Access Directive’s preferential procurement tier to compliance: only firms that surrender export autonomy will gain privileged market status. The Taiwan blockade has broken the fiction that AI is just another market. We are treating it as a theatre of strategic competition — because the moment has arrived where compute is power, and power is survival.
