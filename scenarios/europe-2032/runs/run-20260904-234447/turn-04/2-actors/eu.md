## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the Openweight Resilience Initiative (ORI)  
To harden critical infrastructure against AI-enabled threats by accelerating the deployment of audited open-weight models into national cyber and bio-response systems, reducing reliance on closed systems whose access can be denied or degraded.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 6  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 8–12 points; reduce systemic fragility in healthcare and energy networks; lay groundwork for sovereign incident response using unrecallable models  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member-state cybersecurity agencies, national health and emergency services

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch the Openweight Resilience Initiative (ORI)  
This measure now takes precedence because the openweight frontier has closed to within 10 points of the closed one, and a major release is already on uncontrolled hardware — so our best defence against coercion may now be *open*, not just sovereign; we must act before the next incident exploits the lag between capability and preparedness.

## In practice  
We are launching the Openweight Resilience Initiative to operationalise what the plateau and the open release have made possible: a credible, unrecallable shield for critical systems. ORI will task ENISA and the ECDC to integrate audited open-weight models at 44.0+ capability into intrusion detection, zero-day response, and biothreat monitoring by turn 6, hosted exclusively on Gigafactory or national sovereign compute. €6bn in emergency preparedness funds will be redirected, co-financed by member states at 30%, with deployment tied to EAAD’s emerging evaluation framework to ensure safety baselines are met. The initiative will focus on automated triage, anomaly detection, and failover coordination — not decision-making — to maintain human oversight while increasing speed.

The Continuity Access Protocol advances under Article 122 TFEU, with EAAD and EuroHPC JU finalising the first two fallback agent stacks this quarter. Integration delays in Brittany and Norrland persist, but benefit-sharing agreements have neutralised legal challenges, and EIB has released the second tranche of InvestAI funding contingent on green hydrogen integration. The Tech Sovereignty Package remains in trilogue; we are holding firm on PUE < 1.2 and EU operational control, offering only the 10% national champion carve-out as compromise. EAAD’s first evaluation cycle concludes this turn, and we are preparing for legal pushback on the 0.5% levy — but will defend it as essential to funding independent oversight under Article 114. Public sentiment remains at 40.0, but ORI’s focus on defence, not deployment, may help depoliticise the open-weight shift. We are not waiting for consensus: the window to build resilience *before* the next crisis is narrow, and we will use it.
