## In practice  
We are activating the Continuity Access Protocol (CAP) as an emergency sovereignty measure under Article 122 TFEU, treating AI access denial as a crisis of strategic infrastructure equivalent to energy cutoffs. The protocol mandates EAAD and EuroHPC JU to identify, harden, and pre-deploy three sovereign agent stacks based on open-weight models at 45.0–50.0 capability, hosted exclusively on Gigafactory infrastructure with legally binding access guarantees. These will be integrated into national emergency response frameworks by turn 6, with live failover testing scheduled for turn 5. Funding of €18bn is drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s strategic autonomy window, with co-financing from member states required for integration into national systems.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, with EIB releasing the second tranche of €40bn tied to binding regional agreements in Brittany and Norrland. We have accepted modest delays in two sites to incorporate green hydrogen cooling and grid reinvestment clauses, but phase-one connections remain scheduled for turn 5. Legal challenges are being pre-empted through enhanced regional benefit-sharing: 20% local hiring, 15% energy reinvestment, and joint environmental monitoring boards. Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package is now in final trilogue; we are offering a compromise on energy allocation — allowing member states to reserve 10% of data centre capacity for national champions — in exchange for binding PUE < 1.2 standards and EU-based operational control. The EAAD continues its first evaluation cycle, with preliminary findings expected by turn 4; we are preparing for legal challenges on the 0.5% levy but will defend it as a condition of market access under Article 114 TFEU. Public sentiment remains fragile, but CAP’s framing as a shield against foreign coercion is gaining traction in national parliaments.
