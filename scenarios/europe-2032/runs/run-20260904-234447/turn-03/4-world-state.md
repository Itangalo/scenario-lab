### Frontier Stalls, Access Crisis Deepens  
AI capability climbs to 56.0, but signs of a plateau emerge: leading labs release systems that underperform internal projections, and the market price for top-tier access drops sharply. Analysts debate whether this signals a structural slowdown or a temporary lull. Open-weight models rise to 44.0, narrowing the gap with the closed frontier as last year’s breakthroughs diffuse.

### EU Acts on Sovereignty  
In response to the access denial, the EU launches the Continuity Access Protocol (CAP), a large category 4 measure under Article 122 TFEU, treating AI infrastructure as critical as energy. The plan mandates sovereign fallback agents, hosted on Gigafactory compute, to ensure continuity in health, energy, and emergency services. €18bn is redirected from recovery funds, with member-state co-financing required.

### Political Strain Mounts  
Despite urgency, political capital erodes. Three large measures remain in flight, and CAP’s launch adds a fourth. The named priority incurs an extra cost. Gigafactories face delays in Brittany and Norrland due to local resistance, though benefit-sharing agreements prevent outright cancellation. The Tech Sovereignty Package nears trilogue compromise, but energy disputes persist. EAAD evaluations continue, too late to prevent the crisis.

### Sentiment Holds, Barely  
Public sentiment stabilises at 40.0, buoyed by CAP’s framing as a defence against coercion. However, media highlight the irony of building sovereign systems while still dependent on foreign-trained models for the fallback stacks. The European Open Model Commons gains quiet momentum in technical circles, though remains unofficial.

### Safety and Resilience Slip  
With no safety measure completed and capability advancing, ai_safety drops to 31.0. Resilience weakens slightly as systemic fragility is exposed. Sovereignty ticks up marginally due to CAP’s initiation, offset by capability growth.
