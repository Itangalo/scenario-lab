AI capability reaches 56.0 but shows signs of plateauing, with leading models underperforming and market prices for top-tier access declining. Open-weight models advance to 44.0, closing the gap with closed frontier systems. The EU declares an AI infrastructure emergency under Article 122 TFEU, launching the €18bn Continuity Access Protocol (CAP) to establish sovereign fallback agents in critical sectors, funded by redirected recovery funds and member-state co-financing. CAP leverages Gigafactory compute, though deployment faces delays in Brittany and Norrland due to local opposition, mitigated by benefit-sharing agreements. The Tech Sovereignty Package nears trilogue agreement but remains bogged down by energy disputes, while the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) continues safety evaluations too late to prevent the crisis. Public sentiment stabilizes at 40.0 due to CAP’s framing as a defense against external coercion, though skepticism persists over reliance on foreign-trained models for fallback systems. The European Open Model Commons gains traction in technical communities. AI safety declines to 31.0 amid advancing capabilities and lagging safeguards, while resilience weakens and sovereignty sees a marginal net increase due to CAP’s launch. Political capacity remains strained by four concurrent major initiatives, limiting agility in the face of deepening systemic fragility.
