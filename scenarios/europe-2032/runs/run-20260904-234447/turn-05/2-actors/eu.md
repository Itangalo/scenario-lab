## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Openweight Resilience Initiative (ORI)  
This measure takes precedence because the openweight frontier has reached 45.5 and is spreading uncontrollably — so our best chance to harden critical systems lies in deploying defensive models before the next incident exploits the gap between capability and control.

## In practice
We are launching ORI under Article 175 TFEU on civil protection, tasking ENISA and the ECDC with integrating audited open-weight models into intrusion detection, zero-day triage, and biothreat anomaly monitoring by turn 6. Deployment will run exclusively on sovereign compute — Gigafactory nodes or national infrastructure — with access governed by the EAAD framework once available. €6bn in emergency preparedness funds are being redirected, co-financed at 30% by member states, with an emphasis on automated coordination and failover, not autonomous decision-making. The initiative is framed defensively to maintain public support amid ambivalent sentiment.

The EU Continuity Access Protocol advances under Article 122 TFEU, with EuroHPC JU and EAAD finalising the first two fallback agent stacks this quarter. Integration delays in Brittany and Norrland persist due to local grid constraints, but benefit-sharing agreements have neutralised legal challenges, and EIB has released the second tranche of InvestAI funding contingent on green hydrogen integration. The Tech Sovereignty Package remains in trilogue; we are holding firm on PUE < 1.2 and EU operational control, offering only the 10% national champion carve-out as compromise. EAAD’s first evaluation cycle concludes this turn, and we are preparing for legal pushback on the 0.5% levy — but will defend it as essential to funding independent oversight under Article 114. Public sentiment remains at 41.0, but ORI’s focus on defence, not deployment, may help depoliticise the open-weight shift. We are not waiting for consensus: the window to build resilience *before* the next crisis is narrow, and we will use it.
