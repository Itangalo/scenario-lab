## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the EU Continuity Access Protocol (CAP)  
To guarantee uninterrupted access to frontier AI systems for essential public services, even when external providers withdraw access, by establishing sovereign fallback systems and pre-positioned open-weight agents.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 6  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 10–15 points; reduce disruption risk from external access denial; anchor resilience in sovereign capability  
Applies to: EU institutions, member-state critical services (health, energy, emergency response), and designated industrial operators

## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
This measure remains the cornerstone of our two-year commitment: without physical, legally anchored compute on EU soil, no fallback system, no assurance regime, and no continuity of access can be trusted — and we are still years behind in a race where compound delays become permanent exclusion.

## In practice  
We are accelerating the InvestAI Gigafactories through direct intervention in national permitting processes, invoking the Net-Zero Industry Act and Article 122 TFEU to override local delays in Brittany and Norrland. The European Investment Bank is releasing €40bn in tranche one, conditioned on binding agreements with regional governments: 20% of operational jobs reserved for locals, 15% of energy output reinvested in regional grids, and co-location of green hydrogen plants to offset water cooling demands. ENTSO-E has confirmed 15GW of reserved grid capacity, with phase-one connections scheduled for turn 5. We are also fast-tracking talent visas under Blue Card+ for 5,000 engineers, with relocation incentives tied to the Gigafactory sites.

The Tech sovereignty package is now in trilogue between the Commission, Parliament, and Council. We are trading accelerated permitting — cutting approval timelines from 48 to 12 months via a new EU-wide digital permitting hub — for enforceable commitments on energy efficiency (PUE < 1.2) and EU-based operational control. The private capital mobilisation is on track, with €62bn already pledged from consortiums led by Siemens, Orange, and Enel. A directive under Article 114 TFEU will harmonise standards by turn 6, but we are facing resistance from member states concerned about national sovereignty over energy allocation.

The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) is now operational in interim form under ENISA, with 70 staff hired, including red-team leads from national cyber agencies and nuclear safety inspectors seconded for rigour. The first evaluation cycle has begun on two foreign models above 52.0 capability, using model access secured through conditional market access. The 0.5% levy on non-EU hyperscalers is being collected provisionally, though legal challenges are expected at the CJEU. Meanwhile, the newly proposed EU Continuity Access Protocol (CAP) will deploy sovereign fallback systems by turn 6: we are tasking EAAD and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) to identify and harden three open-weight agent stacks at 45.0–50.0 capability, pre-deployed on Gigafactory infrastructure, ready to activate if access to external models is severed again. This is not about matching the frontier — it is about ensuring the lights stay on.
