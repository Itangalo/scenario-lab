### Rationing held, work vanished, systems broke
The winter pact worked as printed. When the freeze returned, hospitals islanded on schedule, water and ports held, and the suburbs that had gone dark without warning in November stayed lit. Municipal crews credited the spares, paper folders and islanding kits now sitting in depots. Mayors still called the relief money relabelled, and data-centre operators went to court over being curtailed first.

It was overshadowed within weeks.

A largely automated intrusion swept through public services and mid-size suppliers via a poisoned software update. Clinic bookings, municipal payroll and parts of rail ticketing fell back to paper for days. Defenders admitted the payloads were machine-built and adapted faster than signatures. Attribution slipped away into months of audits. The dependency playbook from the security agency helped contain the blast radius, but evening news showed queues and handwritten prescriptions.

At the same time hiring data hardened. Routine coding, standard analysis, first-draft documentation and tier-one support stopped hiring juniors. Firms confirmed the separations were automation-driven. Universities reported graduates stranded; unions marched in three capitals. The wider economy kept growing, which made the narrow collapse more bitter.

Then Washington tightened chip and model licences again, and this time European buyers were rationed alongside everyone else. Volume licences shrank, delivery dates slipped, officials spoke of pragmatic talks while technicians quietly leaned harder on openly available weights that ran on hardware already in basements.

Brussels answered with a wage-bridge and fast retraining scheme for the displaced tier-one workers, paid in part by a levy on automating employers. Employment ministers promised cheques within months through existing social funds and job centres, with courses in grid, care and cyber-hygiene roles. Business lobbies called it a tax on efficiency; unions called it late and small. Payouts had not started by June, and the message shift from rationing endured to jobs protected did not land.

By summer power was stable, services were patched, and consent was thinner than ever.
