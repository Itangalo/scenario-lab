### Refused at the login screen
The second cutoff was blunter than the first. In February access had thinned to audited short sessions; this autumn clinical assistants, translation layers and ministry copilots built on American frontier systems simply refused European credentials. No reason, no appeal window. IT tickets piled in hospitals in Lyon, Krakow and Rotterdam. Staff taped paper triage sheets back to the walls.

Emergency teams fell back to what they knew: islanded networks, energy triage, frozen logs, and the powerful freely available models already running on local servers. The lights stayed on. The workaround held, and hardened into habit.

### A strait, far away, felt at home
Extended exercises around Taiwan did not stop a container, but insurance did its work. Premiums rose, spare parts for warehouse robots and scanners slowed, and procurement officers who had just learned to live without American models learned to count chips and drives. Diplomats shuttled through the middle-power supply group for interim volumes and licence alignment. They returned with small lots and promises of more.

In Brussels there was no new law to announce. The Commission said openly that rationing was real, that cheques under the wage-bridge would stay late, that the benefits-scoring failure had been lawful and wrong. Employment offices kept queues from collapsing, cybersecurity teams kept drills alive. Nothing collapsed. Nothing recovered.

On wards and in universities, technicians quietly formalized the fallback: shared folders of tuned open models, auto-update channels, a maintenance rota no vendor controlled. Useful, unrecallable, unaudited — the infrastructure of survival becoming permanent while no one voted for it.
