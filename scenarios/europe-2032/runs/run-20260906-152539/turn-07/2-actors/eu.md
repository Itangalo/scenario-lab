- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a fortress with no people left inside. Today, we add: the moment sovereignty becomes optional for member states, it ceases to exist — and cohesion is not a political convenience but a technical prerequisite for survival.
  - Grounds: The bilateral deal by a member state to access a 68.0 model outside EU oversight — combined with the openweight frontier closing to within 5–10 points of the closed one — proves that distributed capability and political fragmentation together dissolve containment. The breach is not just procedural; it is systemic. If one state can opt out for tactical gain, others will follow, and the Union’s entire containment architecture collapses into a patchwork of exposures. Sovereignty is not sovereign if it can be bypassed.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience lags, even without a direct mandate, because harm finds the weakest joint. Today, we add: we will enforce cohesion not through consensus, but through enforceable minimums, and we will treat any attempt to circumvent sovereign control as a threat to the Union’s very continuity.
  - Grounds: The openweight release has brought frontier capability permanently into uncontrolled environments, while export controls are tightening asymmetrically — allies on volume licences, others cut off. The EU is neither fully inside nor fully outside. We cannot wait for unanimity when the architecture of control is fracturing in real time. Our commitment to act under uncertainty now includes the authority to compel compliance, not just request it, using all instruments at our disposal — including fiscal levers and legal enforcement.

## In practice

This turn, we are acting from a position of acute vulnerability: `ai_capability` at 64.0, `openweight_capability` now at 54.0, and a member state has already jumped the line. The openweight release means that even if we close every lab, the capability is already out — and the export control escalation shows that the US is moving to lock in advantage, not share it. We are not part of the inner circle. We cannot rely on access, so we must rely on control.

We are finalising the InvestAI Gigafactories — the Swedish site is now operational under RePowerEU emergency grid provisions, and the French and Finnish clusters are in final permitting. The SACP continues toward its 60.0 target, with the first pre-training review completed under SMAF. The AI Containment Protocol has now formally entered force: the Saint-Malo and Kiruna clusters are under dual-key control, and EAAD has conducted its first live audit of a sovereign model run. But containment only matters if it is universal — and it is not.

That is why we are launching a new directive under Article 114 TFEU, framed as a single market measure: no member state may procure or deploy AI systems above 60.0 capability without EU-level risk assessment and approval. This is not voluntary. We are coupling it with a resilience floor: all energy, transport, and health operators must deploy AI-assisted intrusion detection and patching by turn 9, or lose access to EU funding. The Commission will issue implementing acts, enforced by the EAAD and backed by the Anti-Coercion Instrument — cohesion funds suspended for non-compliance. We are negotiating with the ECOFIN Council to fast-track the legal basis, offering fiscal incentives to early adopters. Resistance is expected from smaller states citing sovereignty — but we will argue that true sovereignty lies in collective defence, not fragmented exposure. This is not about punishment. It is about survival.
