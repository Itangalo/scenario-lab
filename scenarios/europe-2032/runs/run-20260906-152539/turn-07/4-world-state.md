### The Fracture Widens

Global AI capability advances to 65.5, as the frontier consolidates multi-agent coordination and autonomous planning across domains. The pace remains steady, but the implications deepen: systems now operate with minimal supervision over days-long task chains, eroding the boundary between tool and actor.

An open-weight model release draws intense attention, reaching an estimated capability level of 52.0—modestly closing the gap with the closed frontier. While technically impressive, it relies on public-domain training techniques and repurposed architectures rather than a breakthrough in algorithmic efficiency or leaked weights. The model spreads rapidly through open communities and is integrated into several non-regulated applications, raising concerns about uncontrolled deployment. However, it does not represent a sudden leap; experts note it aligns with extrapolated trends, merely arriving slightly ahead of schedule due to coordinated volunteer efforts.

Simultaneously, export controls tighten further. The US grants volume licences to a narrow circle of allied entities, excluding even close partners without direct alignment. The EU, lacking sovereign frontier access, is partially rationed—its hyperscalers delayed, its labs starved of key components.

In response, the European Commission proposes a directive mandating minimum AI access and resilience standards across member states. Framed as a single market measure, it prohibits national procurement of systems above 60.0 without EU oversight and sets binding deadlines for AI-assisted defences in critical sectors. The Anti-Coercion Instrument is invoked as a potential enforcement mechanism, with cohesion funds flagged as contingent on compliance.

The proposal triggers immediate controversy. Several member states protest what they describe as top-down imposition, citing national sovereignty and procurement autonomy. Legal challenges are announced, and ECOFIN negotiations stall over fiscal incentives. While the directive passes a first reading, full adoption is deferred, with implementation expected no sooner than the next turn. No binding effects take hold in this period.

On the infrastructure front, the InvestAI Gigafactories complete, with Swedish, French, and Finnish sites now operational under emergency grid provisions. The Sovereign AI Core Programme progresses, and the AI Containment Protocol formally activates in Saint-Malo and Kiruna, contributing to a slight resilience gain. These developments bolster technical foundations, but political coordination lags.

Public sentiment remains stagnant at 25.0, as media spotlight job displacements and dependency humiliations. Trust in AI governance erodes further.

### Sovereignty Without Unity

The EU’s technical foundations strengthen slightly, but political cohesion falters. The moment of institutional leverage—sovereign compute coming online—collides with a crisis of political will. Control is no longer just a technical challenge, but a constitutional one. The proposed directive marks a bold step, but its delayed rollout reflects the enduring gap between ambition and execution.
