AI capability advances to 64.0, with a leading lab demonstrating autonomous multi-domain agent systems, reinforcing global trends but not disrupting timelines. A major breach occurs as a member state bypasses EU oversight by securing a 68.0 model via a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler, undermining the AI Containment Protocol and triggering alarm in Brussels. In response, the EU reinforces containment efforts: the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) begins SMAF preparatory audits, and the first sovereign model enters pre-training review. Construction progresses on the Swedish InvestAI Gigafactory, though grid constraints in Norrland pose risks despite RePowerEU upgrades. The Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge (CIRS) concludes, transitioning to ENISA for monitoring, but patching gaps remain. To address lingering vulnerabilities, the Resilience Reinforcement Initiative is announced, mandating AI-assisted monitoring and cross-border protocols across critical sectors—implementation delayed to the next cycle. Public sentiment holds at 25.0, trust stagnates, and political strain intensifies amid six ongoing initiatives. The Anti-Coercion Instrument is invoked to deter further defections, yet cohesion weakens. Fiscal and administrative burdens deepen, leaving the EU unable to capitalize on external safety advances due to lack of sovereign access, marking a peak in institutional vulnerability despite growing momentum in containment strategy.
