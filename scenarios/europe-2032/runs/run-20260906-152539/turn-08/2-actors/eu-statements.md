These developments have categorically shifted the environment: we are no longer managing a transition, but responding to a crisis of legitimacy. - **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience lags, even without a direct mandate, because harm finds the weakest joint. Today, we add: we will enforce cohesion not through consensus, but through enforceable minimums, and will treat any attempt to circumvent sovereign control as a threat to the Union’s very continuity. **Now, we further commit to act even when capital is low and sentiment is hostile, because waiting for permission in a crisis is how mandates are lost.**
  - Grounds: With `eu_political_capital` at 14.0 and `public_sentiment` at 25.0, the political system is near paralysis. Yet the **cyber_major_incident** and **emergent_ai_terror_cell** demand action now, not when conditions improve. Our reversal is not in direction, but in tolerance for risk: we once acted under uncertainty to avoid being late; now, we act under duress to avoid being irrelevant. The cost of this reversal is high — legal challenges, fiscal strain, member-state defections — but the cost of inaction is the end of the Union as a decision-making entity. Markets, laboratories, and now non-state actors move faster than unanimity allows. We bind ourselves to act, even when the ground is against us.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse — **but now, we explicitly include within that control the capacity to respond to distributed, low-sophistication, high-impact threats emerging from open-weight systems, and to do so without relying on external actors for detection, response, or legitimacy.**
  - Grounds: The **openweight_frontier_release** has brought 52.0 capability into uncontrolled environments, and the **emergent_ai_terror_cell** proves it is being weaponized at the fringe. This is not a frontier-lab risk — it is a societal one. Our original commitment assumed catastrophic misuse would come from high-end systems; now, it is clear it may come from widely available tools in the hands of decentralized actors. The shift does not abandon our goal — it expands it to match reality. We must now build sovereign control not just over what is built, but over how the Union absorbs and responds to what is already loose.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
