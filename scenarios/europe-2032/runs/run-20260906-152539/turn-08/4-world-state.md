### Crisis Response in Real Time

The EU activates emergency protocols as a wave of AI-generated ransomware cripples municipal services across three member states. Blamed on a decentralized network exploiting newly released open-weight models, the attacks expose critical gaps in cyber resilience. In response, the European Commission repurposes the newly completed Sovereign AI Core Programme and InvestAI Gigafactories to support an AI-assisted civil defence effort under Article 222 TFEU.

ENISA assumes coordination responsibility, deploying SACP models for intrusion detection and automated patching, with initial integration into national CSIRTs beginning. The existing Resilience Reinforcement Initiative infrastructure is leveraged to avoid introducing new measures, and no new permanent node is established. Plans for a centralized EU Civil Defence Node in Luxembourg are announced, but legal challenges from two member states over data sovereignty delay staffing and cross-border operational authority. As a result, full integration remains months away, with only preliminary technical links established by the end of the turn.

Emergency funding is drawn from RePowerEU and pandemic-era mechanisms, bypassing stalled ECOFIN negotiations, enabling partial deployment of monitoring tools in energy and transport sectors. Patch deployment improves from days to under 12 hours in pilot regions, though uneven adoption limits system-wide impact.

### Fragmented Unity, Measured Gains

Global AI capability advances to 66.5, as frontier labs refine autonomous agent coordination. A limited open-weight release, aided by leaked training pipelines and distributed fine-tuning, raises openweight capability to 53.5—reflecting incremental diffusion of frontier advances, but within plausible accumulation rates. This "frontier bleed" increases risks from non-state actors but does not represent a sudden leap.

AI safety declines further, as rapid capability growth outpaces oversight. No new safeguards land on deployed systems, and the open release operates entirely outside regulatory reach.

Sovereignty decreases slightly: despite emergency action, the EU remains dependent on foreign models and chips. Political capital erodes under the strain of unilateral action and unresolved governance disputes. Resilience improves moderately, with early gains in detection and response, but full operational capacity is deferred. Public sentiment ticks up slightly, as visible efforts counter narratives of helplessness, though expectations outpace delivery.
