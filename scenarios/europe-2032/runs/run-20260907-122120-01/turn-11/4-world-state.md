### Capability Creeps Forward

AI capability advances steadily, reaching 70.5 as self-improving agents refine reasoning pipelines and broaden task autonomy. The open frontier sees accelerated diffusion: a new release, building on architectures first observed in earlier open leaks and incremental improvements tracked over recent turns, spreads rapidly. More efficient fine-tuning methods and wider access to mid-tier compute clusters allow capable actors to adapt and deploy powerful models with minimal overhead. Open-weight capability rises to 57.0—reflecting sustained momentum rather than a sudden leap—narrowing the gap with frontier systems and amplifying concerns about misuse in cyber and information operations.

A wave of job losses sweeps through entry-level professions. Law firms, accounting offices, and software houses no longer hire juniors, relying instead on AI systems that require minimal supervision. The absence is felt most by young graduates, sparking protests in several capitals and intensifying pressure on social systems.

Worse, the EU is abruptly cut off from the leading AI model. Hospitals using it for diagnostic support, ministries running policy simulations, and industrial labs fine-tuning robotics are left stranded. No explanation is given, and appeals go unanswered. The outage exposes deep dependency—and fragility.

### Response Under Strain

The EU pushes forward with the **Sovereign Evaluation Regime**, establishing red-teaming labs and mandating interpretability hooks for high-capability models. France secures runtime telemetry requirements; Poland joins under a SME exemption deal. But the access denial undermines credibility: how can the bloc enforce external rules while lacking sovereign capacity?

Plans for a broader response to labour displacement are debated, with Germany and Spain advocating for an EU-wide transition fund. However, disagreement over funding mechanisms and conditionality delays formal adoption. A proposal for the **EU-Wide AI Displacement Transition Fund** is advanced to draft stage, with wage insurance and retraining frameworks under discussion, but full launch is postponed pending consensus. The delay frustrates affected workers, though early commitments from national governments help stabilise sentiment slightly.

Public discourse fractures: some hail the EU’s focus on evaluation and control as necessary and timely; others condemn it as too little, too late, pointing to the cutoff and job losses as proof of strategic failure.
