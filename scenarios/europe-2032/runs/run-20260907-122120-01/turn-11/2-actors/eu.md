## In practice
This turn, we advance the Sovereign Evaluation Regime despite legal resistance and political fragmentation. The European AI Office begins staffing its new High-Capability Systems Unit, drawing secondments from national cybersecurity agencies, DG CONNECT, and ENISA-accredited auditors. We activate €240 million from the Digital Europe Programme to fund red-teaming labs in Germany, Finland, and Belgium, with procurement launched under urgent-rules for adversarial simulation platforms. Crucially, we invoke Article 12a of the AI Act to require all models above 65.0 capability seeking single-market access to undergo evaluation before deployment — a line no lab can bypass without forfeiting the EU. France pushes for real-time monitoring baked into model weights; we compromise by mandating runtime interpretability hooks and secure telemetry, to be validated during audits. Poland agrees to join if SME developers are exempted below a usage threshold — a concession we accept, knowing the regime’s credibility hinges on enforcement against the powerful, not the many.

At the same time, we respond to labour_displacement with urgency. The graduate job market has collapsed in law, accountancy, and software — not due to recession, but because entry-level tasks are being fully automated and firms are hiring neither juniors nor trainers. Public anger simmers beneath the surface, feeding into the 32.0 public_sentiment. We cannot wait for structural reforms. So we launch the EU-Wide AI Displacement Transition Fund under the Social Climate Fund’s governance, with co-financing from the Just Transition Mechanism. It will provide 60% wage insurance for displaced workers under 35 for up to 18 months, paired with fast-track digital upskilling in AI-augmented professions: hybrid legal analysts, cyber-physical system operators, bio-informaticians. Member states must opt in, but we tie access to cohesion funds — making refusal costly. Germany and Spain signal early interest; Hungary remains silent, still tethered to its U.S. hyperscaler partner. This is not charity. It is strategic absorption: if we do not manage this transition, the backlash will paralyse every future move we make.
