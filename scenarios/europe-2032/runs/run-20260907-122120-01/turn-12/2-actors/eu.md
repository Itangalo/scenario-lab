## Priority

**Sovereign Evaluation Regime for High-Capability Systems** — because the cyber_major_incident and the cutoff of foreign AI access prove that unverified systems already operate in critical domains, and without sovereign evaluation, we cannot distinguish between capability and control: our own rules are meaningless if we depend on others to enforce them.

## In practice

This turn, we advance the Sovereign Evaluation Regime despite legal resistance and political fragmentation. The European AI Office begins staffing its new High-Capability Systems Unit, drawing secondments from national cybersecurity agencies, DG CONNECT, and ENISA-accredited auditors. We activate €240 million from the Digital Europe Programme to fund red-teaming labs in Germany, Finland, and Belgium, with procurement launched under urgent-rules for adversarial simulation platforms. Crucially, we invoke Article 12a of the AI Act to require all models above 65.0 capability seeking single-market access to undergo evaluation before deployment — a line no lab can bypass without forfeiting the EU. France pushes for real-time monitoring baked into model weights; we compromise by mandating runtime interpretability hooks and secure telemetry, to be validated during audits. Poland agrees to join if SME developers are exempted below a usage threshold — a concession we accept, knowing the regime’s credibility hinges on enforcement against the powerful, not the many.

At the same time, we respond to the cyber_major_incident and the AI cutoff with strategic acceleration. The attack — a model-generated compromise of a widely used software dependency — disrupted public health systems in three member states and exposed the fragility of just-in-time digital infrastructure. More alarming is the silence from the provider that cut us off: no appeal, no notice, no fallback. We cannot build evaluation regimes on borrowed intelligence. So we launch the EU Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme under the Chips Act’s emergency provisions and the Digital Europe Programme’s innovation window. We designate €3.2 billion in blended funding — 70% from the EU, 30% matched by member states — to deploy three exascale-class clusters by turn 18, hosted in geographically dispersed, energy-secure locations (Finland, France, and southern Spain). Access will be tiered: full for EU institutions and red-teaming labs, audited for strategic industries, and strictly logged. This is not about matching the frontier — not yet — but about ensuring that no public service, no evaluation body, no critical function depends on a foreign stack that can vanish overnight. Germany and the Netherlands back the plan; Hungary resists, fearing U.S. retaliation. We make clear: sovereignty is not optional. If they will not build it, they will not control it — and when the next cutoff comes, they will be on their own.
