### Holding the line
Brussels chose to start nothing in the second half of 2031, and for once restraint read as realism. With capital markets in full retreat from AI, no new funded instrument could have cleared Council or a courtroom.

The semester was dominated by the grid attacks. Coordinated sabotage of interconnectors feeding the two frozen factory sites cut power locally for days. Police treated it as anti-data-centre action; grid operators switched to redundancy feeds and negotiated guarded repair windows with the freeze councils. The tape stayed up, but the dispute was no longer about water — it was about energy security. Vigils hardened, mayors demanded protection money for substations, and the Commission's refusal to challenge the injunctions while investigations ran frustrated both builders and opponents.

The investment collapse did the rest of the de-escalation. Valuations reset, announced build-outs were cancelled, and several commercial arrangements European compute had relied on evaporated. Washington offered sympathy and no restoration date for frontier access. The access pact held on paper, banked as exhortation.

Meanwhile the two shocks from spring deepened. The contested genome-model paper hardened into a full methods war, with health agencies issuing monitoring guidance that clinics largely ignored. The new open download, now on hundreds of thousands of machines, became standard kit in municipal IT and university labs despite do-not-install memos. Hospitals kept the continuity stack alive on maintenance funds; showcase sites still showed shorter waits, elsewhere queues grew.

One bright note arrived from ordinary offices: measured studies showed solid productivity gains in law, accountancy, administration and journalism, concentrated among juniors, with no matching job losses. Employers banked it; lenders who had bet on endless transition did not.

By December cohesion held, but nothing unfroze, nothing was funded, and the bio question would not go away.
