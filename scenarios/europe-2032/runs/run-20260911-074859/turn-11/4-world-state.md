### Shelter Holds, Work Vanishes
The second half of 2031 did not bring invasion, but it brought the ledger of war and automation together.

Atlantic landing stations stayed guarded, registries stayed dispersed, and the non-belligerency line held. Engineers patched around the cut cables with rerouted capacity; hospitals kept triage assistants alive on pooled reagents and, when the lights flickered, on paper. The tailored therapies first administered in Copenhagen and Lisbon in spring expanded to a few hundred more patients by autumn, negotiated as licensed batches made on European-run lines. Families wept on regional news. Editorials added the same footnote: the models that designed the doses still ran elsewhere.

That footnote stung because hiring did not return. Law firms, accountants, software houses and customer operations confirmed what graduates already knew: entry posts were simply not reopened, sorting machines and drafting agents having taken their place. The Transition Shield paid out its final top-ups — steady in the north, restarted late in the south after fuel and air-defence diversions — but payments read as compensation, not a bridge.

Brussels named the shelter mission its priority and executed it competently. Southern arrears were cleared by October; reagent pooling averted ward closures. Yet with no fiscal room for new builds, dependence deepened visibly. Talk of a neutral corridor for medical and registry traffic stayed at the level of corridor rumour: a diplomat's aside, an insurer's memo, nothing ships could sail by.

By December, Europe was sheltered, treated, and increasingly jobless — grateful for healing it could not provide on its own.
