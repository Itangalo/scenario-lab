### The halt
Autumn brought two shocks at once. A court-backed inquiry found an AI-supported system used in benefit eligibility and fraud flagging had systematically cut or reduced payments to tens of thousands of people over two years. Caseworkers had approved its recommendations in seconds, logs that showed the pattern sat unread, and appeals piled up unheard. Then a second finding widened the damage: the deployment had never been classified as high-risk at all. What was done was lawful under categories written for 2024 systems.

Trust collapsed. News bulletins ran faces of families asked to repay money they never owed. Opposition parties called the framework a fraud, mayors refused to defend it, and data-centre protests merged with benefit protests.

Brussels answered with a suspension and redress scheme. The Commission ordered the implicated family of decision systems frozen pending independent re-checks, with human re-decision and preserved logs reviewed with agency support, and opened a single Brussels-backed claims window for review and compensation modelled on the ransomware window. Finance ministers advanced compensation money; interior and social-affairs ministers carried the freeze.

Implementation limped. Suspensions varied by region, the claims portal crashed in its first week, payouts were slow, and municipalities warned triage and billing staff were now doing double work — manual re-decisions while keeping essential services up on the shared hardened distribution.

### Borrowed machines, now unrecallable
Meanwhile a new open-weight release matching the closed frontier spread to hundreds of thousands of downloads in days. City engineers folded parts of it into local packs to keep services running without American credentials, deepening the paradox: independence from revocable access now meant reliance on code no authority could patch or recall.

By December clinics ran and power held, but no one credited Brussels. Voters credited local offices that paid redress, and blamed the Union that had overseen the rules.
