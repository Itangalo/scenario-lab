### Holding on borrowed machines
The winter brought a ransomware wave that moved faster than anyone answering phones. It entered through a poisoned software component used by municipalities, hospitals and logistics firms, then spread with machine-generated intrusion code that rewrote itself around blocks. Clinics in several regions went back to paper, appointment queues lengthened, and some city administrations paid out of a single Brussels-run claims window while joint recovery teams shuttled between sites.

What stung was not only the outage but the sense of being behind. Defenders patched one variant only to meet another. Attribution faded into months of forensics.

In the middle of the cleanup, Washington tightened chip and model licences again. Under the standing tier system, allied buyers kept volume access in principle but faced slower approvals, narrower quotas and new end-use checks. European procurement officers described weeks lost to paperwork, and a few high-end shipments rerouted elsewhere.

Brussels tabled nothing new. With no money and no tolerance to spend, the Commission kept the existing doctrine running: hardened packs built from openly available models, installed on municipal servers and European cloud, plus the recovery corps procedures from last year. Mayors were asked to keep running them under common security baselines.

That is what happened. City engineers, tired of waiting, standardised the workaround. A coalition of municipalities published a shared hardened distribution for essential services — triage, billing, dispatch — that ran without American credentials. Where it was installed, services limped back and stayed back. Where it was not, complaints continued about slowness and opacity.

The paradox hardened: independence from revocable access now meant dependence on code no authority could recall or centrally patch.

Amid this, laboratories announced a genuine advance in automated theorem-proving applied to chip verification, done in the United States, shortening design checks from weeks to days. Specialists called it a landmark; for the public it was distant news against queues and bills.

By June power held and clinics ran. Voters credited the local systems that worked and the mayors who kept them up, not Brussels. The bilateral side-deal with Washington remained outside the common line, containment talks dragging on.
