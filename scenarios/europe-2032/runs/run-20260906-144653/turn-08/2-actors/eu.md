## In practice
We finalise the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield directive this turn, adopting it under Article 114 TFEU and binding it to enforcement mechanisms in the NIS3 framework. The Cybersecurity Working Party, under ENISA leadership, confirms consensus among member states after Germany and Poland accept conditional offers: fast-tracked access to InvestAI Gigafactories funding and liability protections under the AI Liability Directive, in exchange for immediate compliance by energy and health grid operators. ENISA publishes standardised red-team scenarios by mid-turn, based on forensic analysis of recent hospital ransomware attacks, enabling cross-border testing of intrusion detection and failover systems ahead of the first joint exercise in late turn 8.

We defend the European Evaluation Shield’s mandate in the face of intensifying legal resistance, leveraging political backing from the Council resolution to maintain pilot audits under strict confidentiality. Two frontier developers participate, exchanging limited data-sharing waivers under the Digital Markets Act review for full access to model internals, preserving evaluation integrity. The first public safety report—assessing whether current safeguards can constrain systems near 60.0 capability—remains finalised but withheld pending ECJ guidance, though internal summaries circulate among national security agencies.

With the ai_investment_collapse eliminating several private compute partnerships the EU had counted on, and the capability_plateau_evidence suggesting a structural shift rather than a temporary stall, we treat this turn as a hinge: the frontier is no longer reliably pulling us forward, and the open frontier is closing in. Our focus shifts decisively from anticipation to consolidation—delivering what we have built, hardening what we depend on, and preparing for a world where sovereign capacity is not a complement to the global frontier, but the only thing standing between the Union and systemic disruption.
