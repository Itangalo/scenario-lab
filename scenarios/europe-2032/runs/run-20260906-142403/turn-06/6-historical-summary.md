A major AI-driven cyberattack disrupts European health and energy systems, exploiting compromised grid software and overwhelming human response capacity. The EU responds by launching the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative, reallocating €45bn to deploy AI-enhanced defenses, but implementation stalls due to legal disputes over data control and resistance from Poland and Spain. ENISA begins developing swarm detection and zero-trust protocols, though only limited testing occurs. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme concludes with minimal physical rollout; Germany and Austria challenge its legality at the ECJ, while microgrid projects advance modestly in Sweden and Brittany. The Tech Sovereignty Package and EAAD certification are completed, but EAAD’s effectiveness is undermined by Italy and Hungary blocking real-time logging. Public trust continues to erode amid job displacement and ineffective resilience messaging. AI capability reaches 62.0 due to autonomous engineering agents, while open-weight models hit 50.0 through leaked training methods, widening the gap between capability and safety. The new U.S. administration enacts AI retrenchment policies, halting federal contracts and tightening chip exports, slowing global progress while boosting non-Western labs.
