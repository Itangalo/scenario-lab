## In practice
We are treating this turn as the inflection point where technical sovereignty met political fragility. The cutoff of foreign AI access was not just a supply shock — it was a legitimacy rupture. Hospitals went dark. Ministries were blind. And when people took to the streets in Lyon, they were not protesting code — they were protesting powerlessness. We cannot rebuild trust with blueprints alone.

The **Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative** has now formally concluded. Its components are operational in Sweden, Brittany, and parts of the German energy grid, but patchy compliance in Poland and Spain leaves critical seams. ENISA is shifting from enforcement to verification, publishing a scorecard next quarter grading member states on integration depth. The EIB is linking post-crisis recovery funds to shield compliance — not as punishment, but as a new condition of solidarity. Yet even where the shield works, it is invisible. That invisibility feeds suspicion.

Meanwhile, the **Mandatory Pre-Deployment Red Teaming** programme is on track: three labs will be accredited by year-end, hosted in Toulouse, Vienna, and Aarhus, with mandates to simulate AI-driven logic corruption and dependency poisoning. Germany’s ECJ challenge remains pending, but we are pre-empting it with a pilot: the first red team will test the EU’s own energy forecasting model, with results published in full. This is not just about safety — it is about proof.

But the dominant effort now is the **Public Trust Corps**. We are launching it through DG HOME and DG CONNECT, with initial funding redirected from stalled regional digitalisation grants. Its first mission: stand up the AI Incident Dashboard by turn 10, fed by ENISA, EAAD, and national CERTs, showing real-time threat levels, system status, and response timelines — no caveats, no jargon. We are also deploying mobile units to Lyon, Marseille, and Warsaw to conduct “transparency drills” — public simulations of AI failure and recovery, with live Q&A. These are not performances. They are rehearsals for the next real event. If we do not show people that we can fail, respond, and recover — visibly — then the next protest will not stop at the data centre gates.
