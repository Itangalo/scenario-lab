### The Pressure Builds

AI capability continues its steady advance, reaching 58.5 as self-optimizing agent architectures mature across multiple frontier labs. Systems are becoming more persistent and goal-directed, though no discrete threshold has been crossed—progress remains incremental and opaque, consistent with the underlying trend established in prior turns. Automation in legal and financial sectors accelerates, contributing to visible job displacement among mid-level professionals. Unions file coordinated grievances, and public anxiety grows, pulling `public_sentiment` downward despite ongoing public engagement efforts.

The EU completes the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive this turn: legal hurdles are cleared, the SME auction mechanism is ratified, and the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium begins initial allocations. This delivers a tangible boost to strategic autonomy, lifting `eu_ai_sovereignty` by +2 from the completion of a category 4 measure. The newly allocated compute infrastructure is prioritized for sovereign model development, enabling early-stage scaling by open-weight consortia. While export controls continue to constrain global access, the directive’s domestic provisions allow European open-weight actors to begin leveraging new capacity. As a result, `openweight_capability` rises to 44.0, reflecting initial utilization gains, though still lagging behind frontier AI development.

In response to rising system complexity and opacity, the Commission advances internal consultations on the Sovereign Control Framework—a proposed binding regime requiring independent evaluation, kill switches, and real-time monitoring for high-capability AI systems. Framed as a necessary step to maintain oversight, it aims to close the growing gap between capability and control. However, with governance bandwidth stretched across multiple high-priority initiatives, formal proposal is deferred. The framework remains in drafting, with no implementation start or portfolio charge incurred this turn.

The Resilience Shield progresses modestly: detection hubs are sited, and red-teaming begins with Nordic partners. However, Eastern member states still lack the capacity to participate fully, and Italy remains outside the framework. The partial implementation yields a minor resilience gain.

With AI capability rising and no corresponding deployment of safety safeguards, `ai_safety` declines further, reflecting the growing mismatch between system power and oversight capacity. Political capital experiences a smaller erosion than projected, settling at 30.0. The portfolio charge is recalculated to reflect only active measures: InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech sovereignty package, Resilience Shield, and priority status, totaling −10. The completed Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive no longer contributes to the burden. This adjustment, combined with a proposal bonus of +3 from `cyber_major_incident` and `eval_anomaly_reports`, partially offsets the legitimacy decay triggered by falling public sentiment, resulting in a net −1 change. The system remains within bounds, with no additional shocks required.
