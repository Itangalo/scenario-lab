A binding pre-deployment regime requiring independent evaluation, real-time monitoring, and kill-switch integration for any AI system operating above 55.0 capability within EU jurisdiction.

## In practice  
We are closing the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive this turn: the Legal Service has confirmed proportionality under Article 114 TFEU, the Competitiveness Council has accepted the SME auction carve-out, and the EDIC begins allocations in Q3. This delivers the first real anchor of compute sovereignty — not total, but sufficient to break unilateral dependence. The 10% auction mechanism will be administered through a new Digital Commons Board, with seats for research consortia and civil society, ensuring access is not just distributed, but accountable.

But compute without control is not sovereignty — it is exposure. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Control Framework: this is not another voluntary code, but a binding regime under the AI Act’s high-risk provisions, extended via emergency clause to systems above 55.0 capability regardless of application. ENISA will be empowered to conduct pre-deployment evaluations using red-team agents trained on EU-held threat models; all covered systems must integrate real-time monitoring APIs and sovereign-held kill switches, with penalties for non-compliance including compute revocation under the Compute Directive. We are already in talks with the European Parliament’s LIBE and IMCO committees to fast-track adoption, and with national regulators to stand up inspection capacity by turn 4.

The Resilience Shield continues on track: Germany, France, and the Netherlands have confirmed hub locations, and ENISA’s first red-team exercise will run in October with Finnish and Estonian cyber units. But we are now layering in a new requirement — that all Shield-compliant intrusion systems must themselves be evaluated under the Sovereign Control Framework, closing the loop: we harden against AI threats by ensuring the defences are themselves under sovereign control. This is not optional: in a world where the frontier moves in jumps, and displacement is already underway, our sovereignty must be operational, not just territorial.
