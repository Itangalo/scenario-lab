## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because despite rising resistance, this remains the fulcrum of sovereignty: without binding control over compute on our soil, every resilience gain, every safety protocol, depends on the goodwill of those who own the machines, and the eval anomaly confirms we are already operating beyond full understanding.

## In practice  
We are acting on two truths this turn: first, that capability is outpacing control, and second, that sovereignty without resilience is hollow. The **Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** is now in its final drafting phase under Article 114 TFEU, with the Commission’s Legal Service confirming its compatibility with single-market principles through the proportionality doctrine. We are engaging the Competitiveness Council through a revised offer: in addition to fast-track access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund and liability shielding, we are now proposing that 10% of the reserved 30% capacity be auctioned to European SMEs and research consortia, ensuring the directive does not merely shift control from US hyperscalers to Brussels, but distributes strategic access widely. The European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) is being staffed with technical governors from six member states and will begin its first allocation cycle in Q3, ahead of the directive’s formal adoption.

At the same time, we are launching the **Resilience Shield**, a direct response to the cross-continental intrusion and the eval anomaly. This is not a study or a recommendation: it is a binding mandate under NIS2, requiring all operators of essential services to deploy AI-powered intrusion detection systems trained on EU-held threat models, with red-teaming conducted quarterly by the newly expanded ENISA Cyber Rapid Response Teams. The Shield will be co-financed at 50% by the Digital Europe Programme, with an initial €12bn allocation over two years, drawn from reprogrammed cohesion funds. We are already in talks with Germany, France and the Netherlands to host the first three regional monitoring hubs, and with Finnish and Estonian cyber defence units to lead the first exercise cycle this summer. This is not optional resilience — it is the minimum operational standard for a Union that refuses to be a target.
