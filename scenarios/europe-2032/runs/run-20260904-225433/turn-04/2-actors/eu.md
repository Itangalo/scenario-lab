## In practice  
We are activating the teeth of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive: the right to revoke compute access is now being operationalized through the Digital Commons Board and ENISA, with legal backing from the Commission’s Internal Market and Security services. This is not symbolic. We are drafting binding technical standards for remote attestation and sovereign kill switches on all infrastructure receiving EU-subsidized power or grid priority, to be enforced by national digital regulators by turn 5. The Netherlands and Germany have agreed to pilot the mechanism with two of the Gigafactory sites, linking power allocation to compliance with EU command protocols. When a provider denies access — as just happened — we do not beg; we retask. That is the meaning of enforcement.

At the same time, the Sovereign Control Framework is entering its critical phase: we have secured provisional agreement from the LIBE Committee to invoke the AI Act’s emergency clause for systems above 55.0, and ENISA is standing up a red-team unit in Dublin with €1.2bn from the Resilience Shield budget. They will begin live evaluations of three foreign models by Q3, using threat models derived from the emergent_lab_consulting_leak. We are also requiring that all Resilience Shield defences be evaluated under the Framework — closing the loop between protection and control. France and Italy are resisting, citing national sovereignty, but we are offering joint command seats in the red-team hub in exchange for compliance.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is now tied to enforcement: we are amending the accelerated-permitting zones to require that any private data centre receiving fast-tracked approval must integrate sovereign monitoring APIs and accept conditional compute revocation. This is not optional. The Gigafactories continue construction, but we are redirecting €30bn of the fund to harden the energy grid around the sites — a move demanded by the Resilience Shield’s red-teaming results. We are not building capacity to lose it at the first crisis. The world is testing us. We are answering.
