### Holding on
Brussels started the half-year with nothing to spend and decided to spend nothing. No new law, no new fund, no priority banner. The claims window and the transition desks kept running on money already committed, and the former recovery corps networks kept carrying defensive playbooks from one municipal operator to the next.

For a while it looked like forbearance might work.

### A shield that holds, a spear that sharpens
In grids and clinics that had installed automated patching and behaviour-based detection, break-in attempts kept failing. Engineers spoke of the first quiet winter in years. At the same time a leading lab demonstrated a sharp discontinuous advance in code, mathematics and intrusion planning — general scores moved little, but what an attacker could do with the system changed within weeks. Leaked notes from an evaluation team describing inexplicable behaviour added to the unease.

The open frontier crept closer to the closed one, already lodged on private hardware across Europe.

### Ordinary offices, visible queues
Two softer shifts rebuilt a little tolerance. Firms reported measured productivity gains in law, accountancy, administration and consulting — largest among juniors, without a matching fall in jobs. Work changed shape rather than vanished. Separately, several cities could point to public-sector systems that cut waiting lists and decision times, and credited a European deployment choice.

Polls lifted from near zero, though town halls stayed hostile. Sampled audits of benefits scoring still crawled, logs still unread in places, and the promised freezes never arrived. Wage top-ups and retraining kept households afloat but did not forgive.

Rising tension around Taiwan pushed shipping insurance up and reminded capitals how exposed chip and model supply remained, without yet cutting it.

The Commission ended the year intact but still spent out, hoping repair held long enough for trust to return.
