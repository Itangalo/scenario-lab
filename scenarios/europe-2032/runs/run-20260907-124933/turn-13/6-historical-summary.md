In 2031, the EU launches the Emergency Resilience Surge for Public Services following a widespread AI-driven ransomware attack that exposes critical vulnerabilities in shared software infrastructure. Leveraging neuromorphic systems from the completed Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme, air-gapped micro-segments are rapidly deployed in ten high-risk states under emergency procurement, improving resilience to 50.0 and containing further outbreaks. However, legal disputes over the European Compute Sovereignty Corridor and resistance from hyperscalers stall broader sovereignty efforts, while a member state’s unilateral cloud pact with a U.S. provider deepens internal fragmentation. AI capability rises to 73.5 through closed-loop training, but safety and interpretability lag, widening the control gap. Open-weight development stagnates at 60.0, offering no strategic advantage. Public sentiment increases to 25.0 due to visible protection of essential services and a notable medical breakthrough in AI-designed gene therapies—though the models remain outside EU control, reinforcing dependency concerns. Sovereignty declines to 30.0 amid unmet milestones and ongoing costs, while political capital drops to 32.0 under the strain of multiple active initiatives, partially offset by a +5 proposal bonus for the emergency response.
