### The Surge Begins

The EU launches the Emergency Resilience Surge for Public Services in direct response to a devastating AI-generated ransomware attack that crippled regional health registries and municipal tax platforms across five member states. The assault, fully automated and adaptive, exploited known vulnerabilities in shared software dependencies, evading legacy detection systems. With the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative proving too narrow in scope, the Union acts under emergency provisions, mobilizing ENISA and national cyber teams to deploy air-gapped micro-segments using neuromorphic stacks from the recently completed Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme. Fast-tracked procurement bypasses standard tenders, and ten high-risk states accept 85% co-financing for rapid hardening.

Despite urgency, progress is uneven. Legal disputes over the European Compute Sovereignty Corridor persist, and hyperscalers resist model jurisdiction, stalling broader sovereignty gains. A member state signs a unilateral cloud pact with a U.S. provider, weakening the Union’s negotiating front and signaling internal fracture.

### Capabilities and Consequences

AI capability advances to 73.5, driven by closed-loop training innovations, but no corresponding safety improvements emerge. Interpretability remains poor, and control mechanisms are outpaced. The openweight frontier stagnates at 60.0—no major release occurs, and distributed models show no leap in offensive utility.

Resilience climbs modestly to 50.0 as early micro-segment deployments reduce attack surface in targeted systems. The surge is credited with containing secondary outbreaks, though full effects will take time.

Public sentiment rises slightly to 25.0. The visible protection of frontline services generates cautious approval, but grassroots data revolts grow louder, with citizen networks interoperating across borders, rejecting centralized control. A medical breakthrough in AI-designed gene therapies boosts optimism—yet the models behind it are inaccessible to EU institutions, framing the advance as another episode of dependency.
