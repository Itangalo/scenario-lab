## In practice
We are acting on two levels: defending the present and reclaiming the future. The **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative** is our immediate line of defence. Under Article 114 TFEU, the Commission is issuing binding directives within 90 days, backed by €1.1 billion from the Connecting Europe Facility and the Internal Security Fund. ENISA will operationalise the Resilience Monitoring Board in The Hague, with authority to verify compliance and trigger funding suspensions. We are dispatching technical liaison teams to capitals to assist national regulators — especially in states resisting on cost grounds — offering co-financing up to 70% for the first two years and mutual aid guarantees modelled on the Civil Protection Mechanism. This is not optional: when a financial agent can move millions unchecked, or an election collapses from synthetic manipulation, we are no longer managing risk — we are managing collapse.

At the same time, we are launching the **European Compute Sovereignty Corridor**, a large-scale industrial response to the sovereignty crisis exposed by the access denial and member-state defection. This will consolidate three existing high-energy zones — in Finland, France, and Poland — into a legally and physically integrated compute corridor, with priority grid access, fast-tracked permitting for data centres, and binding legal commitments from hyperscalers that any frontier model trained here remains under EU jurisdiction and audit. We will leverage the Chips Act II funding, the Innovation Fund, and national co-investment to secure 20 exaFLOPs of sovereign AI training capacity by turn 17. Crucially, this includes on-site lithography support and secure firmware chains, so that the stack — from silicon to software — is EU-controlled. We are already in talks with ASML, Intel Europe, and several national energy ministries to lock in supply and grid stability.

The **European Agency for AI Assurance and Control** continues its build-out, now entering its second phase: recruitment of 300 technical staff, deployment of monitoring nodes in five member states, and negotiation with developers for early access to model checkpoints. But its authority means nothing if the models it must evaluate are beyond our reach. That is why sovereignty is no longer a secondary concern — it is the foundation of safety, control, and legitimacy. We are not building capacity to catch up. We are building it so that, for the first time, the EU can say: *this intelligence, this infrastructure, this future — it answers to us.*
