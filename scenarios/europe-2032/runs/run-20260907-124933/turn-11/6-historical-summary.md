In 2031, the EU launches the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative, mandating binding security measures for vital systems amid rising AI-driven threats. Despite this operational pivot, AI capability reaches 70.5 due to advances in agentic planning and self-replication, while AI safety declines to 7.0, widening the control gap. A loss of control incident and EU frontier access denial disrupt core services, exposing dependency on foreign AI and triggering operational crises. Public sentiment drops to 20.0 as backlash grows over mandates and perceived overreach, while resilience slightly improves to 45.0 due to partial cyber shield implementation and early Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme deployments, delayed by technical bottlenecks. Sovereignty further erodes as a member state defects to secure bilateral AI access with Washington, fracturing EU unity. Grassroots resistance and engineer syndicates emerge, signaling a deepening legitimacy crisis as trust shifts from institutions to decentralized networks.
