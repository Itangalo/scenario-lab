### A Surge and a Scandal

The global AI frontier accelerated sharply, driven by a sudden leap in autonomous cyber capabilities. Systems now execute multi-day offensive and defensive operations with minimal oversight, marking a qualitative shift in what is possible. This jump, while narrow in scope, redefined the threat landscape overnight.

Simultaneously, a damning European Ombudsman report revealed systemic failures in an AI-supported social insurance system, which had wrongly denied benefits to thousands of vulnerable citizens. The scandal erupted across member states, eroding public trust and triggering widespread calls for restraint. Adoption of AI in public services ground to a halt in several countries, and political momentum shifted sharply toward restriction.

### Resilience Under Pressure

Despite the setback, the EU pressed forward with its Critical Systems Resilience Certification, now the centerpiece of its AI governance strategy. The Tech sovereignty package formally concluded, unlocking permitting for 12 new hyperscale data centres by next turn. However, private investment remained stagnant at €183bn, falling far short of the €200bn target.

In response to the healthcare compliance crisis, the Commission launched the Emergency Resilience Acceleration Facility—a €23bn emergency fund to fast-track vulnerable sectors into certification. Technical assistance teams were deployed, and liability protections offered to encourage adoption. Yet early resistance from cloud-native vendors and legal uncertainty in several member states slowed rollout.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Tightens

The Sovereign Interpretability Programme delivered promising pilot results, but too late to offset broader losses. With ai_capability rising by 2.0 and no corresponding safety gains, ai_safety plummeted. Resilience inched up due to the new category 6 measure, but public sentiment collapsed under the weight of the scandal. Political capital dwindled under the burden of five ongoing measures and the priority cost, while sovereignty declined further due to rising capability and no offsetting gains.

Open-weight model development saw modest progress, supported by continued diffusion of prior frontier releases and patchy replication efforts across academic clusters. However, no major open-release event occurred, and the absence of coordinated scaling or infrastructure investment limited gains. Openweight_capability rose only moderately, reflecting incremental uptake rather than a leap, aligning with the constrained innovation environment described in the narrative.
