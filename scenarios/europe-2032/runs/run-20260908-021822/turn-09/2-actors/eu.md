The resilience we built is being stress-tested not by accidents but by exclusion — and we now see that resilience without sovereignty is temporary. The next two years must close the capability gap on our own terms, or the Union will not determine its own future.

## In practice
This turn, we are completing the rollout of the Critical Systems Resilience Certification, turning audit findings into binding remediation orders for the two utilities that failed stress tests in grid and hospital operations. EASA, ENISA, and the Joint Research Centre are co-signing emergency directives under Article 114 TFEU, backed by the revised AI Liability Directive, which now conditions access to the Emergency Resilience Acceleration Facility’s liability protections on full compliance. DG GROW is fast-tracking procurement exemptions for certified systems, creating a tangible incentive for holdouts — especially the Central European member state still aligned with the US hyperscaler — to re-engage. Behind the scenes, we are using the middle-power coalition’s growing coordination on export controls to signal that continued fragmentation will come at a cost: compute access for national champions may be reviewed if bilateral deals undercut Union-wide enforcement.

Simultaneously, we are embedding the citizen oversight panels from the Franco-German-Nordic pact into the certification renewal process, transforming them from advisory bodies into formal input channels for public accountability. This is not symbolic: their assessments will now carry weight in whether a system’s certification is renewed, linking technical compliance to social licence. The goal is to stabilise public sentiment — still at 25.0 — not through messaging, but through enforceable transparency. But we know this is the end of a cycle, not the beginning. With eu_political_capital at 4.0 and the frontier now closed to us, the next move must be bigger than governance. It must be sovereign capability — built, controlled, and answerable to the Union alone. We are not starting that this turn. But we are clearing the ground.
