## New measure  
**Critical Systems Resilience Certification**  
Establish a mandatory, EU-wide certification regime requiring all AI systems operating in essential services to demonstrate resilience against loss-of-control scenarios and verified interpretability under stress conditions.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +15 to +20, ai_safety +5 to +10; institutionalises control verification as a condition for operation in healthcare, energy, transport, and public administration  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national regulators, critical infrastructure operators, and all AI providers serving those sectors

## Priority  
**Sovereign Interpretability Programme** — because it remains the only active instrument building technical assurance at pace with capability, and without verifiable control over reasoning paths, no resilience or restriction can hold against fast-evolving agent behaviours.

## In practice  
We are finalising the second year of our industrial build-out under the InvestAI Gigafactories programme, now with four sites in active development — two in Germany, one in France, one in Poland — each co-invested by EDIC and national champions. Despite French concerns about repurposing civilian capacity, we have secured agreement to reserve 30% of Dresden-phase compute for sovereign safety applications, including real-time monitoring of public-sector AI. The Tech sovereignty package is nearing completion: member states have adopted the accelerated-permitting framework, unlocking grid access for 12 new hyperscale facilities by turn 7, and private investment commitments now stand at €183bn. We are treating this not as an end but as a floor — next phase must shift from hosting to controlling.

This turn, the Cyber Defence Integration Directive completes its rollout. While energy and transport operators have integrated automated patching and anomaly detection, healthcare lags due to workforce shortages and unresolved liability questions around autonomous responses. ENISA will publish a post-implementation review next month, highlighting gaps in cross-sector interoperability and stress-testing under coordinated attack. Simultaneously, the Sovereign Interpretability Programme enters full deployment: Horizon Europe has redirected €18bn to the Joint Research Initiative on Model Assurance, which is now field-testing reasoning-path prediction tools at pilot sites in Finland, Belgium, and Italy. Early results show 70% accuracy in flagging divergent agent behaviour before execution — not enough for full autonomy, but sufficient to mandate integration into certification pipelines.

Against this backdrop, we are launching the Critical Systems Resilience Certification under Article 17 of the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA), extended via delegated act to cover AI-driven functions in essential services. This is not merely compliance — it is a forcing function. From turn 8 onward, no AI system in healthcare diagnostics, grid management, rail signalling, or emergency response may operate without certified interpretability under duress and proven fail-operational protocols. National regulators will be required to conduct live red-teaming exercises twice yearly, with results reported to a new unit within ENISA. We are already negotiating with provider consortia — including domestic startups and U.S. hyperscalers — to align their roadmaps with these requirements. Resistance is expected, particularly from cloud-native vendors whose architectures resist modular oversight. But we are offering early-access waivers for systems that exceed baseline thresholds, and we are backing it with funding from the European Defence Fund’s civil-security envelope. The message is clear: if you serve Europe’s critical functions, you must be auditable, containable, and resilient — not just capable.
