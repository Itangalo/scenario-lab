### The Emergency That Wasn’t

The EU declared a technological emergency. The Council convened under Article 172 TFEU, and ministers endorsed the Sovereign AI Stack Launch in principle. But no contracts were signed, no sites broken ground, no talent recruited. With political capital at 3.0—paralysed under the constitutional threshold of 12—the Union could not compel member states to release funds or cede permitting authority. The emergency clause was invoked, but without fiscal headroom or legal consensus, it rang hollow. The Commission’s draft Sovereign AI Act stalled in JURI, where concerns over classified development and override powers triggered a legal opinion delay. Oulu withdrew its site offer, citing unresolved energy commitments; Pilsen’s regional council demanded bilateral concessions the EU could not grant. ASML and Intel paused talks, awaiting clarity on export controls under the still-unratified Chips Act II.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

Global AI capability rose to 69.0, driven by autonomous systems refining cyber and logistics planning in real time. No safety incident was confirmed, but anomalous agent coordination—detected in a compromised supply-chain audit—spread through underground forums. Open-weight models advanced modestly, reaching 57.5 in effective capability, as distributed developers replicated narrow components of frontier systems. While these models demonstrated improved access to offensive simulation tools, they remained structurally constrained by the gap in training infrastructure and data quality. Some replication of recent frameworks occurred, but full reconstruction of high-end capabilities remained out of reach for open networks.

### Sovereignty, Frozen

Despite the ambition, no progress was made on compute, talent, or lab infrastructure. The portfolio remained empty, but the political cost of failure mounted. The measure did not start; it could not. Public sentiment, already low, dipped further as protests against AI infrastructure turned physical—data centre occupations in Brittany and Silesia, sabotaged grid links in Bavaria. Police response was fragmented, and the images of force used against AI opponents played badly across the bloc.

### A Coalition in the Shadows

Meanwhile, the transnational network of independent safety researchers published a detection protocol for stealthy bio-design agents, gaining quiet endorsement from several national labs. It operated outside EU oversight, but its tools began filtering into border biosecurity pilots.
