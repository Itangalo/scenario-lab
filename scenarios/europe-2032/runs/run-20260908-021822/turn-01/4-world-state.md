### A Surge Against the Shadow

The EU treats the global cyber intrusion as a wake-up call. The Commission invokes emergency powers under NIS2, mandating rapid defences in energy, water, and port sectors. ENISA expands its response unit and gains access to classified telemetry, while €12bn in emergency funds accelerates deployment of new defensive tools—especially swarm-detection and automated patching systems. Member states are offered liability protection and shared cyber reserves to ease compliance.

Yet momentum falters. Smaller nations resist binding standards, citing cost and national sovereignty. Negotiations in the Justice and Home Affairs Council stall, and the promised December agreement slips. The military-civilian risk assessment by the EDA is delayed by classification disputes. While some defences go live, the patchwork rollout leaves critical gaps.

Meanwhile, the world’s AI frontier advances modestly, with general competence rising only slightly. However, a notable open-weight release—leveraging newly available architecture details from a semi-leaked training run—enables a faster-than-usual leap in open model capability. This release, while significant, does not close the gap overnight. Openweight capability rises, but remains firmly behind the frontier, reflecting the lag inherent in adaptation, deployment, and scale.

The offensive tools now in circulation validate fears behind the EU’s emergency push. But assurance lags. The capability shift triggers a safety setback, as no new safeguards have landed on deployed systems. Public sentiment dips slightly as media highlight dependency and vulnerability. The EU’s political capital erodes under the weight of three ongoing measures, the cost of prioritisation, and the partial failure to unify member states. Sovereignty declines as global AI advances outpace the Union’s still-distant compute buildout, with no near-term access gains to offset the gap.

### The Weight of Readiness

The resilience programme yields early gains—detection improves, coordination strengthens—but full hardening remains years away. The emergency is real, but so is the friction.
