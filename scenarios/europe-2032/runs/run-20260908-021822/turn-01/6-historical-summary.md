The EU responds to a major cyber intrusion by invoking NIS2 emergency powers, deploying €12bn in funds to strengthen defences in critical sectors and expanding ENISA’s capabilities. Efforts are hampered by member state resistance over sovereignty and cost, stalling key agreements and delaying military-civilian risk assessments. Defences advance unevenly, leaving critical gaps. Globally, open-weight AI models see a notable capability jump due to a semi-leaked architecture, but safety safeguards fail to keep pace. Public confidence wanes as vulnerability concerns grow, and the EU’s political capital erodes amid fragmented unity and lagging compute infrastructure. Early resilience gains are offset by long-term challenges, with full system hardening still years away.
