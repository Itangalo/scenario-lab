## New measure  
**Emergency Resilience Surge**  
Launch a six-month emergency programme to harden critical infrastructure against AI-enabled cyber intrusion, using newly revealed attack patterns from the cyber_test_shot.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`

## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_test_shot revealed that current detection is blind to AI-coordinated intrusions already in the wild, and no amount of sovereignty or evaluation matters if essential services collapse before we can respond.

## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_test_shot as a live-fire warning. This turn, the Commission invokes Article 12 of the NIS2 Directive to mandate immediate defensive upgrades across designated essential entities in energy, water, and port logistics — those hit or nearly hit by the intrusion. The ENISA Rapid Response Unit is being scaled up by 300 personnel and given direct access to classified intrusion telemetry from the affected operators, under a new legal basis approved by the Justice and Home Affairs Council. We are fast-tracking deployment of the new defensive tooling revealed in the cyber_defence_breakthrough, particularly swarm-behaviour detection and automated patching systems, through a €12bn emergency allocation from the Civil Protection Mechanism, co-financed by member states on a 70/30 basis.

At the same time, we are pressing the European Council to adopt binding minimum hardening standards by December, ahead of the formal finish of this measure in turn 3. Resistance is strongest in smaller member states citing cost and sovereignty, so we are pairing the mandate with guaranteed access to EU-level cyber reserves and liability shielding for operators that comply. The EDA is being asked to assess military-civilian crossover risks, particularly in port authorities with dual-use infrastructure. This is not about catching up — it’s about ensuring that when the next probe comes, it doesn’t find the door open.
