## In practice  
This turn, we are cutting through the legal and fiscal logjam by treating sovereign compute as emergency infrastructure under Article 172 TFEU and the Crisis and Investment Instrument. We are bypassing the stalled Sovereign AI Act and instead activating binding directives to Grenoble, Pilsen, and Oulu to begin immediate site hardening, with classified grid access and physical security protocols managed jointly by the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking and Frontex for perimeter integrity. €4.1 billion in repurposed Digital Europe funds will be released in tranches tied to milestones: 30% on site lockdown, 40% on power and cooling installation, 30% on system integration.  

We are engaging the Council in an emergency ECOFIN+COMPET formation to override the Court of Auditors’ objection, framing this not as a reallocation but as a crisis continuity imperative under Article 122. Host states are offered 75% co-financing and automatic energy deficit compensation, while non-hosts are guaranteed access to 20% of total capacity for essential functions. ENISA and the Public AI Safety Unit are being redirected from protocol development to integration: their task is no longer to build a monitoring agent from scratch, but to deploy a lightweight, auditable version of the shadow network’s detection tool on the new nodes by turn 13, focused exclusively on identifying unauthorised agent activity in energy, health, and transport systems. Civil liberties concerns are addressed through EDPB co-certification of the monitoring layer and a sunset clause tied to the restoration of external AI access. This is not the future we wanted. It is the one we can still have.
