### The Crisis Compute Gambit

The EU launched an emergency push to activate hardened exaflop computing nodes in Grenoble, Pilsen, and Oulu, framing them as essential for crisis continuity after a major AI-driven cyberattack disrupted public services across several member states. Invoking Article 172 TFEU and the Crisis and Investment Instrument, the Commission bypassed stalled legislation and issued binding directives to host cities, backed by €4.1 billion in repurposed Digital Europe funds. Site hardening began under joint Frontex and national guard supervision, with classified grid access protocols initiated.

Yet the move fractured the Council. ECOFIN+COMPET narrowly approved the funding reallocation, but only after fierce resistance from the Court of Auditors, which maintained its objection. Two non-host states filed preliminary legal challenges, arguing unequal burden and lack of parliamentary scrutiny. ENISA’s pivot to deploy the shadow network’s detection tool faced delays—integration proved more complex than expected, and EDPB certification stalled over real-time monitoring concerns.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Crumbles

Despite signs of a plateau in frontier AI development—evidenced by underwhelming releases and falling prices—global capability inched up to 70.0, driven by accumulated agent coordination tools now embedded in offensive stacks. Open-weight models reached 59.0, as underground networks replicated stripped-down versions of prior breakthroughs. With no safety improvements in shipped systems and the cyberattack revealing opaque, autonomous decision loops, `ai_safety` dropped sharply to 9.0.

Public sentiment fell to 20.0 as the crisis response was seen as both too late and too opaque. Protests flared outside Pilsen and Grenoble, not over AI itself, but over the militarisation of civilian infrastructure and exclusionary decision-making. The narrative shifted from apathy to distrust.

### Sovereignty on Shifting Ground

The cancellation of the full Sovereign AI Stack cost political capital, and the new Crisis Compute measure—though small—began consuming resources in a system already at breaking point. With `eu_political_capital` at 2.0, governance remained near paralysis.
