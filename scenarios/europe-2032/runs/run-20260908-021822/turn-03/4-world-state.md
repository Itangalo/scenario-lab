### The Semiconductor Standoff Deepens

The EU’s push for guaranteed chip access intensifies, but with limited traction. The emergency summit under the Global Supply Chain Resilience Forum yields only partial commitments: Japan agrees to joint stockpiling of specialty gases, and Taiwan’s technical envoys quietly endorse component swaps, but Dutch leadership refuses to formalise ASML maintenance access as leverage, fearing U.S. retaliation. South Korea conditions cooperation on reciprocal semiconductor quotas from Washington, stalling collective action. Backchannel talks with Beijing acknowledge the EU’s “civilian exemption” proposal, but no assurances are given. The Anti-Coercion Instrument’s targeted use draws legal scrutiny from WTO observers, weakening its coercive weight.

STMicroelectronics reports modest progress in hybrid chip repurposing, enabling low-throughput inference workloads by early 2028—too late to fill the immediate gap. Meanwhile, the redirection of InvestAI Gigafactories capacity accelerates prototyping but consumes political goodwill, with France warning of delays to long-term sovereignty goals.

### A New Front in Cyber Defence

The Cyber Defence Integration Directive launches amid rising alarm over swarm attacks. ENISA coordinates rapid adoption of new defensive tooling across energy and transport networks, standardising automated patching and behavioural detection. Early results show improved response times, contributing to a measurable hardening of critical systems. However, healthcare providers in three member states resist integration, citing certification bottlenecks and staffing shortages.

### Capability Leaps Ahead, Safety Stalls

A major leap in AI-driven code generation and cyber operations marks a discontinuous advance, dramatically expanding what automated systems can execute. At the same time, verification widens into clinical coding and contract review, enabling rapid open-weight improvements. Yet no new safety safeguards accompany these developments. Internal audits at leading labs reveal opaque reasoning chains in newly deployed models, further eroding confidence. The gap between capability and control grows visibly.

Public sentiment holds steady, as visible cyber defences balance fears of dependency. But with ai_safety falling and sovereignty under pressure, the EU’s political capital continues to drain under the weight of overlapping crises.
