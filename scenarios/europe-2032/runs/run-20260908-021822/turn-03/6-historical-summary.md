The EU’s push for semiconductor autonomy faces persistent obstacles, as partial agreements on specialty gas stockpiling with Japan and informal component swaps with Taiwan fail to offset Dutch refusal to weaponize ASML maintenance access and South Korean demands for U.S. concessions. The Anti-Coercion Instrument loses effectiveness under legal scrutiny, while STMicroelectronics’ delayed hybrid chip breakthrough offers only limited relief by 2028. InvestAI Gigafactory repurposing accelerates prototyping at the cost of long-term sovereignty concerns, straining Franco-German alignment.

In cyber defence, the new Cyber Defence Integration Directive strengthens critical infrastructure resilience through standardized automated patching and threat detection, though healthcare integration lags due to operational constraints. Despite improved defences, a major leap in AI-driven cyber capabilities—spanning code generation, clinical applications, and contract analysis—outpaces safety developments, exposing deepening risks from opaque model reasoning and absent verification frameworks. Public confidence remains stable but fragile, as widening capability-control gaps and eroding political capital threaten the EU’s strategic coherence amid compounding crises.
