The Taiwan blockade disrupts global semiconductor supply chains, forcing the EU to leverage control over upstream components like ASML’s maintenance and specialty gases to secure AI chip access. Emergency coordination through the Global Supply Chain Resilience Forum yields limited results: Japan commits to shared stockpiling, but Dutch and South Korean hesitancy weakens collective action. U.S. and China reject EU mediation, while the narrow use of the Anti-Coercion Instrument fails to extract concessions. Technical setbacks delay hybrid chip repurposing by STMicroelectronics and Infineon, postponing interim compute solutions.

Internal divisions deepen as France and Germany back strategic autonomy, while Eastern members question the cost of defying U.S. interests. Public support wanes amid economic pressures and a leaked ENISA report exposing vulnerabilities in automated patching systems. Globally, AI capabilities advance rapidly through closed-loop self-improvement and a semi-leaked architecture that accelerates open-weight models, though safety measures lag. The EU’s restrictions limit its access to breakthroughs, even as a growing data transparency movement demands accountability from AI labs—offering oversight, but no operational resilience.
