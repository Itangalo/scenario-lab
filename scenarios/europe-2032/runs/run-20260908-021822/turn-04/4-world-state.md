### A Fragile Step Forward

The EU seizes on a rare breakthrough in AI interpretability, launching the Sovereign Interpretability Programme to embed new verification tools across critical infrastructure. The initiative, fast-tracked through Horizon Europe, aims to give EU agencies independent oversight of model reasoning—reducing reliance on developer assurances. Early adoption by ENISA and national safety offices begins, though full integration remains distant.

Simultaneously, the Secure Semiconductor Access Initiative concludes. Japan and Taiwan formalise component swaps and gas stockpiling, while the Netherlands agrees to share non-military ASML maintenance logs via a neutral EU clearinghouse. South Korea commits to bilateral talks on quotas, conditional on U.S. reciprocity, and quiet understandings emerge with Beijing and Washington on civilian exemptions. These gains are narrow but stabilise access for essential systems.

### Capability Pressures Mount

Global AI capability advances further, nearing the 60 threshold, driven by improved code synthesis and autonomous cyber operations. Open-weight models benefit indirectly, gaining ground through replication of newly disclosed techniques. Yet safety lags—until now. The recent interpretability advance lifts confidence, and the EU’s rapid mobilisation delivers an early boost to assurance, though deployment is partial and untested at scale.

Cyber defences harden incrementally. The Cyber Defence Integration Directive progresses in energy and transport, but healthcare adoption remains patchy due to certification delays and workforce gaps. No major incident occurs, but probing attacks intensify, testing response protocols.

### Political Strain Deepens

The EU’s political capital erodes under the weight of overlapping commitments. The new Sovereign Interpretability Programme, while timely, stretches already thin resources. Public sentiment edges up slightly on visible action, but labour concerns and dependency anxieties persist. France voices growing unease over the Gigafactory repurposing, warning of long-term sovereignty trade-offs. The emerging civil society campaign gains traction in national parliaments, framing AI displacement as a democratic issue.
