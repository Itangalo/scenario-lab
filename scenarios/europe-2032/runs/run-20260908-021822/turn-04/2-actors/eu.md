**Sovereign Interpretability Programme**  
Launch a publicly funded, pan-European initiative to adopt and scale the recent safety breakthrough in model interpretability across all EU-deployed frontier systems, starting with critical infrastructure and public services.

## Priority  
**Sovereign Interpretability Programme** — because the safety_breakthrough this turn is a rare window to close the control gap from below, and without sovereign technical capacity to verify and enforce it, the EU will remain dependent on developer goodwill in a world where capability is accelerating past oversight.

## In practice  
We are treating the interpretability breakthrough not as a technical footnote, but as a strategic inflection point. This turn, the Commission activates the Horizon Europe emergency clause to redirect €18bn toward a Sovereign Interpretability Programme, co-located with national AI agencies and hosted under a new Joint Research Initiative on Model Assurance. The goal is singular: within 12 months, deploy tools that can predict and certify the reasoning paths of any AI system operating in EU critical functions. We are tasking ENISA and the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) to integrate these tools into certification pipelines for public procurement and infrastructure access, making them a de facto requirement for operation in sensitive domains.

At the same time, we are completing the final phase of the Secure Semiconductor Access Initiative. The emergency summit under the Global Supply Chain Resilience Forum has yielded a fragile but functional agreement: Japan and Taiwan will formalise component swaps and gas stockpiling, the Netherlands will allow non-military maintenance access logs to be shared via a neutral EU clearinghouse, and South Korea will engage bilaterally on quota swaps if the U.S. reciprocates. We are also finalising backchannel understandings with Beijing and Washington on mutual civilian exemptions—narrow, but enough to insulate essential health and energy AI systems from targeted disruption. These are not victories, but they are footholds. The redirection of 15% of InvestAI Gigafactory capacity to hybrid chip testing continues, now in coordination with STMicroelectronics’ new Dresden line, though we acknowledge the tension this creates with long-term sovereignty goals. France remains sceptical, but we are offering guaranteed first access to sovereign interpretability tools for national defence AI as a bridge. The message is clear: we are not choosing between security and independence. We are building both, because we cannot afford to lose either.
