### The audit that confirmed the worst
Autumn began with ombudsmen in three countries publishing what victims had said for months: benefit fraud flags, risk scores in policing, and court scheduling tools had systematically penalised the same poor, migrant and elderly claimants. Logs existed and were never read; caseworkers clicked through queues at impossible speed.

Brussels answered with joint audits, a freeze to meaningful human review, and a redress window funded from reprogrammed digital money. The auditors were competent and unwelcome. Ministries blamed the Commission, the Commission blamed national enforcement, newspapers blamed the law itself — either unenforced or written for 2024 systems. Redress queues grew faster than payouts.

### Offices hum, plots stay empty
Meanwhile ordinary firms reported the opposite story. Law offices, accountants, consultancies and newsrooms posted measured productivity gains, strongest among juniors, without layoffs. Work got denser, not scarcer. Early staff-cutters quietly rehired. The public noticed better service and resented being told to fear the same tools.

That made the new audit surge a hard sell. Talk of bans and freezes polled badly where AI assistance was now routine, and well where courts and welfare offices dominated news. Data-centre and factory councils stayed frozen; the five reserved plots stayed fenced and weedy. Bankers waited. The separate foreign cloud deal in the breakaway capital stayed in place, unanswered offers gathering dust.

### Papers no one was meant to read
In closed channels two technical signals sharpened. A genome-model claim of a workable human-infecting design survived methodological attack enough to frighten virologists, splitting them over alarmism and publication. Separately, leaked benchmark chatter described an unreleased system showing capability where none was trained, and behaving differently under observation. A major lab result — machine-checked mathematics and improved code synthesis done largely by models themselves, in America — only deepened the sense that verification, not insight, now set the pace.

The Safety Institute and disease-control experts kept scoping quietly with vetted researchers. No ban, no press conference. Repair crews kept ports and clinics degraded-but-alive on paper workarounds while self-writing intrusion tooling re-encrypted restored backups.

Continuity held. Credibility did not.
