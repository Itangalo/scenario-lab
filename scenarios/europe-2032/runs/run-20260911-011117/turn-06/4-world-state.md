### The repair trucks
Winter brought the attack everyone had warned about. A wave of machine-written intrusions swept through municipal systems, clinics and port logistics in several member states at once — locked records, poisoned software updates, backup servers encrypting themselves. Attribution blurred for weeks. ENISA teams, hastily rebadged as a joint repair cell, drove from city to city restoring backups and cutting out compromised code.

The cell helped, visibly. Mayors praised the blue-vested engineers who got registries and emergency rooms back online. But operators noted what the cell did not do: it patched symptoms, not the brittle controllers and unpatched clinic networks underneath. Services degraded rather than collapsed, yet for days citizens queued with paper forms.

### A solo deal
In the middle of the cleanup, one capital broke ranks. Ministers arrived in Brussels to find a bilateral arrangement already signed with a large foreign cloud provider — and, by rumour, understandings in Washington — offering that country priority access to advanced models outside the common European ask.

The Commission threatened to withhold solidarity aid from the breakaway, Paris and The Hague held the joint licensing line, and the Foreign Affairs Council offered joint terms to lure the capital back. Nothing worked quickly. Newspapers in the east called it humiliation made policy; elsewhere it read as every-country-for-itself.

### Paper leverage, real anger
The tech sovereignty package formally closed, unlocking permitting zones and private-capital pledges for the five reserved factory sites. Still nothing was built — reservations kept warm without cash while bankers stayed away and power-price protests flared near two sites.

Washington's new administration took office promising to treat frontier models as a national asset with country tiers. Brussels now faced a rationed future it could not build its way out of this spring. Public mood, already anxious, turned openly hostile as fraud stories and downed services dominated coverage.
