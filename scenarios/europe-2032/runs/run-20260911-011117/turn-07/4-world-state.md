### The second wave
The repair trucks had barely returned to base when the second wave hit. This time it was not clinics alone but a poisoned software component buried in logistics and municipal IT — backups encrypting, port cranes pausing, registries freezing. Emergency rooms went back to paper triage. The blue-vested joint repair teams were cheered where they arrived, but they were visibly outpaced: automated intrusion tooling regenerated faster than backups could be restored.

Attribution dissolved into competing claims. What officials would say, grimly, was that the tooling was machine-written and markedly better than in winter.

### Holding the line
With no money and no votes to spare, Brussels doubled down on what it had. The joint repair cell was kept in the worst-hit cities and ports under emergency crisis powers, funded by shifted programme money. Continuity held — hospitals stayed open, registries degraded rather than stopped — but underlying controllers and clinic networks remained unpatched. Mayors praised the engineers while quietly telling capitals the help treated symptoms. The continued operation consumed scarce goodwill without rebuilding it, leaving political capital still floored.

Safety oversight thinned under the same strain. Audit teams and red-teamers were seconded to incident response, scheduled reviews were deferred, and patch verification slipped. No safety board was disbanded and no audit regime repealed, but coverage eroded modestly while attention was elsewhere.

The solo deal festered. The breakaway capital kept its separate cloud arrangement and hinted at privileged access abroad. The Foreign Affairs Council offered joint terms for a return to the common position and warned that solidarity aid could be withheld; Paris and The Hague held a common licensing front. The capital did not return. Eastern papers called it abandonment; western briefings called it blackmail.

### Concrete on paper only
The sovereignty package closed this autumn on paper. Permits and reserved plots for five factory sites were now legal fact, and a compact with middle powers on supply chains was signed. Foundations are not yet poured and no new capacity is online — bankers stayed away, power-price anger flared near two sites where protesters blocked access roads and grid works for days, and local councils froze some works. With no capital secured and physical works stalled, construction is delayed and no sovereignty gain materializes yet; the continued fragmentation weighs slightly on the common position.

Washington's tiered-access doctrine hardened in the background, rationing the most capable models by country tier. A discontinuous advance in coding and intrusion tooling, demonstrated abroad, made the rationing bite harder. Fraud and outages led evening news on several nights, and distrust of automated systems ticked further down without a wholesale collapse in trust.
