### The Crackdown Begins

The EU launched the **Sovereign Model Registry and Deployment Freeze**, invoking emergency powers to halt all new frontier AI deployments pending forensic verification. Market access is now conditional on registration with ENISA, proof of weight provenance, and clean development logs. The move, framed as a *defensive pause*, triggered immediate legal challenges from U.S. developers and backlash from member states wary of unilateralism. The European Court of Justice fast-tracked a preliminary hearing, but enforcement remains patchy.

The **Mandatory Audit and Redress Framework** officially completed, establishing binding audit rights for public-sector AI. However, its impact was muted: audits remain suspended pending the Court’s final ruling on constitutional admissibility, and no redress mechanisms were activated.

The **Emergency Fallback Compute Assurance Expansion** made limited progress, with the EU securing non-binding crisis-use agreements for existing EU-located clusters from NVIDIA and AMD. No new hardware was delivered, and technical integration remains in early stages. The **Sovereign Evaluation Enclave Initiative** received redirected funding, accelerating forensic protocol development by BSI, ANSSI, and the Dutch NCSC, though no operational enclave is yet live.

### Fractures and Fears

Public sentiment held steady at 25.0. While some welcomed the freeze as overdue, others saw it as symbolic posturing. Physical protests at data centres intensified, straining local police and deepening public unease. The informal cyber defence coalition held its first joint threat assessment, detecting increased probing of critical infrastructure — now attributed to autonomous offensive agents.

Despite bold moves, EU political capital rose only slightly, weighed down by multiple concurrent initiatives. Sovereignty dipped further as dependency on foreign compute and model access remained unresolved. The American posture of **ALLIANCE** softened resistance but did not eliminate jurisdictional friction.
