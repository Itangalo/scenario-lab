## Statement changes  
* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Sovereign AI capacity as a foundation for strategic autonomy and collective security``  
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has already sharpened this commitment into one of enforceable control, not just ownership. But this turn’s failure of the Emergency Fallback Compute Activation Protocol—blocked by member state defection and legal fragmentation—reveals that sovereignty cannot be decreed from the centre. The commitment must now include not only technical and legal mechanisms but binding operational integration: national systems must be *designed* to fail into EU-controlled capacity, not merely *invited* to. We are not reversing, but deepening: the commitment now implies mandatory fallback integration into national AI architecture, enforced through conditional access to EU funds and emergency powers.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late, but we will no longer act alone when coordination is the only path to power``  
  - Grounds: The **us_china_agreement** and the **emergent_unified_eu_biosecurity_protocol**—a bottom-up, voluntary standard gaining real traction—show that legitimacy and adoption now flow through networks, not decrees. Our fallback protocol failed not because it was wrong, but because it was imposed. We are adjusting the commitment to reflect that early action must now be *co-constituted*: we will still move before full consensus, but we will anchor those moves in frameworks others can join, not just obey. The biosecurity protocol is the model: emergent, voluntary, functional. We will now seek to amplify such coalitions, not override them.

## In practice  
We are enforcing the Emergency Fallback Compute Activation Protocol through conditionality: no member state that refuses to integrate fallback APIs into critical infrastructure will receive EU emergency response funding during an AI disruption event. The Commission is issuing implementing acts under Article 114 TFEU to define technical standards for API integration by turn 8.5, and ENISA is publishing the first live access dashboard this month. We are also activating the Crisis Regulation to designate EuroHPC’s sovereign compute as emergency reserve capacity—any node not pre-registered for fallback integration will be automatically reconfigured upon a strategic disconnection event. The message is clear: you cannot opt out of collective survival.

At the same time, we are moving to institutionalise the emergent biosecurity protocol. The Commission is drafting a Regulation under Article 168 TFEU (public health) to formalise the framework, requiring all labs receiving EU funding or operating cross-border to implement AI-driven screening for dual-use bio-sequences. We are offering national authorities a carve-out: those that adopt the protocol early will receive priority access to the new EU BioShield rapid response fund. This is not coercion—it is convergence. We are learning from our mistakes: instead of imposing a top-down mandate, we are accelerating a bottom-up standard into law.

The cyber_major_incident—still unattributed, but clearly model-generated—has hit municipal services in three member states with a cascading ransomware attack exploiting a poisoned software dependency. We are treating it as a stress test: the fallback protocol must now prove it can activate under fire, not just in anticipation. We are deploying emergency compute to restore health and emergency dispatch systems, using the InvestAI Gigafactories’ idle capacity. This is the moment: if we cannot act now, we will never be sovereign.
