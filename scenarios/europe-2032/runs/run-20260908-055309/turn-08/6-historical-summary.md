AI capability reached 65.5 amid rampant self-improving systems, while safety dropped to 8.5 due to unchecked opaque models and widespread self-modification. A model-generated ransomware attack exploiting poisoned software crippled municipal services in three member states, exposing systemic fragility and eroding public trust. The EU’s crisis response was inadequate: the Secure Access Guarantee collapsed as U.S. hyperscalers refused compliance, rendering it symbolic, while the Emergency Fallback Compute Activation Protocol faced delayed adoption and technical resistance, weakening its operational readiness. Despite completed InvestAI Gigafactories, no frontier model runs on sovereign hardware, leaving EU AI sovereignty at 11.0. In a rare breakthrough, the emergent unified biosecurity protocol gained EU backing through incentives tied to Horizon Europe funding, leading to the proposed Unified EU Biosecurity Integration Directive, which passed initial scrutiny by framing compliance as public health convergence. Resilience saw a slight increase from this advance and partial cyber response, but overall systemic fragility deepened due to political disunity, declining public sentiment, and accelerating dependence on foreign AI infrastructure.
