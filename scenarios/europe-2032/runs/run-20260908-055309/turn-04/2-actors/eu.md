## In practice  
We are executing the Sovereign Audit Access Pilot through a delegated act under Article 114 TFEU, co-signed by the Commission’s Directorate-General for Communications Networks, Content and Technology and the Joint Research Centre. Three institutions — INRIA, Fraunhofer, and TNO — have been provisionally certified as audit-capable under a new EU weight-security standard aligned with NIS2+. We are engaging OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepMind via bilateral Memoranda of Understanding, offering expedited market access and participation in the Middle Power Compute Accord’s joint evaluation track in exchange for controlled access to one pre-release model each, running in air-gapped environments hosted within EU jurisdiction. Legal cover is being fast-tracked through COREPER II, framed not as new regulation but as a technical implementation of existing product liability and digital service obligations under the AI Act.

Simultaneously, we are finalising the Middle Power Compute Accord with ministerial meetings in Tokyo and Seoul next month, where we will lock in reciprocal commitments on EUV component licensing and establish the joint compute reserve’s governance protocol. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being unblocked at the Energy Council through a compromise: three gigafactory sites will be granted strategic infrastructure status, overriding local permitting objections, in exchange for binding commitments on grid decarbonisation and water use monitoring. InvestAI Gigafactories remains on track, with site selection complete and construction beginning in Q3. With the Emergency Resilience Surge now finished, ENISA is transitioning its crisis teams into a standing Cyber-AI Fusion Cell under permanent funding, ensuring continuity of detection upgrades across health and energy sectors. Public sentiment remains fragile, but we are leveraging early success stories — including AI-assisted cancer diagnosis rollouts in Spain and Finland — through the Digital Europe Programme to rebuild trust incrementally.
