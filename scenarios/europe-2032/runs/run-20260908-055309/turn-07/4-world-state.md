### The Denial That Changed Nothing

The EU’s frontier access was abruptly severed. Hospitals lost real-time diagnostic AI, ministries saw automated reporting systems go dark, and firms dependent on advanced inference faced immediate operational risk. The cutoff, unexplained and irreversible, confirmed worst-case assumptions: dependence was a vulnerability, not a partnership.

In response, the EU activated its crisis levers. The **InvestAI Gigafactories**, completed this turn, delivered sovereign compute infrastructure—but not yet sovereign capability. The hardware is in place, but no frontier model runs on it. The **Secure Access Guarantee** remains in negotiation, its legal teeth untested.

The new **Emergency Fallback Compute Activation Protocol** was fast-tracked under Article 114 TFEU. Designed as a small, symbolic measure, it aimed to convert infrastructure into power. Yet resistance flared: several member states, deeply integrated with U.S. cloud providers, refused to adopt fallback APIs, citing technical incompatibility and legal exposure. The threat of liability backfired—rather than compel compliance, it exposed fragmentation. The protocol was formally adopted, but implementation remains partial and delayed, with no meaningful compute fallback activated this turn.

Meanwhile, the **US_POSTURE: ALLIANCE** held, but the U.S. did not consult the EU on the **us_china_agreement**, a narrow pact on weights security and bio-design controls. The EU was neither included nor informed in advance. The agreement marginally slowed global capability growth, but only by stabilizing the leading labs’ external environment—leaving the Union sidelined.

Public sentiment dipped further. The access denial was framed not as an outrage, but as a failure of foresight. Media dubbed the fallback protocol “the switch that won’t flip.” Trust eroded.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Crumbles

With no safety measures landing, and automated systems now self-improving in closed loops, **ai_capability** rose by 1.5. **ai_safety**, already minimal, fell another 5 points—opaque reasoning and unmonitored self-modification became routine in deployed systems.

**Openweight_capability** remained unchanged at 51.0. Despite anecdotal reports of distributed cyber tools leveraging older Kimi K3 derivatives, no new open-weight model release or systemic advancement occurred under the current access denial. Without new training runs or public deployments, capability accumulation stalled.

**Resilience** ticked up slightly from partial planning and protocol signaling, though systemic fragility persists. **EU AI sovereignty** fell to 13.0, reflecting the failure to convert infrastructure into functional autonomy and the ongoing member state defection. There is no floor on sovereignty; the completed gigafactories provide potential, not realized capability.

A coalition of six member states began informal coordination on biosurveillance standards. No formal **emergent_unified_eu_biosecurity_protocol** has been adopted, and no binding framework exists. Discussions are preliminary, with divergent national approaches still dominant. Any future alignment would require overcoming significant regulatory and operational hurdles. No traction or continuity should be assumed.
