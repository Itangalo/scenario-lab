**Emergency Fallback Compute Activation Protocol**  
We are creating a legally triggered mechanism to redistribute sovereign compute and activate emergency model inference capacity for critical services the moment foreign access is denied.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`

## Priority
**Emergency Fallback Compute Activation Protocol** — because the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has rendered access guarantees insufficient without a visible, automatic fallback; without this, the Secure Access Guarantee reads as a plea, not a power.

## In practice
We are activating Article 114 TFEU and the Crisis Regulation framework to establish the Emergency Fallback Compute Activation Protocol as a binding, automatic response to access denial. The protocol will designate a threshold—verified loss of access to any system above 55.0 capability for more than 72 hours—as a "strategic disconnection event," triggering the immediate reallocation of EuroHPC’s sovereign compute to healthcare, energy, and emergency services. ENISA will publish a live dashboard of access status, and national CIOs are being instructed to integrate fallback APIs into all critical AI-dependent systems by turn 8.5.

The protocol is small in scope but high in signal: it turns denial into a casus belli for redistribution. We are funding it through a reallocation from the InvestAI Gigafactories’ contingency reserve—now that the gigafactories are complete, their infrastructure is available for emergency repurposing. Resistance from member states reliant on U.S. cloud providers is being met with a simple offer: they can either accept the fallback protocol as a safeguard, or they can assume full liability for service collapse when access is cut. No public money will backstop their unilateral dependencies. The message is clear: sovereignty is not optional, and we will not fund fragility.
