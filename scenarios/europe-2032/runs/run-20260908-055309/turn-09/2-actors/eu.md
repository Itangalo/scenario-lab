## New measure
**EU-Wide Mandatory Fallback Integration Certification**  
We are establishing a legally binding certification standard requiring all critical national AI systems to be architected for automatic failover to EU-controlled compute and models upon disconnection from foreign providers, with non-compliant systems barred from public procurement and emergency funding.

## Priority
**Unified EU Biosecurity Integration Directive** — because the **openweight_frontier_release** and falling ai_safety make bio-risk the most immediate vector of uncontrolled harm, and because this turn’s **automated_decision_scandal** has made new top-down mandates politically toxic, we must deliver a visible success in preparedness that is framed as health protection, not control. The biosecurity directive is our only measure gaining public legitimacy—its passage now locks in a model of convergence we can replicate elsewhere.

## In practice
We are treating the completion of the Emergency Fallback Compute Activation Protocol as both a milestone and a warning: it exists in law, but not in practice. The cyber_major_incident exposed that integration is patchy, APIs are untested, and EuroHPC nodes remain disconnected from real-time crisis response. We are now shifting from activation to enforcement. Under Article 114 TFEU, the Commission is adopting implementing acts that define technical standards for fallback integration, and we are instructing ENISA to publish a public dashboard rating member states’ readiness. Crucially, we are amending the Crisis Regulation to withhold emergency funding from any state whose critical systems lack certified fallback architecture by turn 12. This is not optional: survival is now a condition of solidarity.

At the same time, we are fast-tracking the Unified EU Biosecurity Integration Directive through the Public Health Committee, anchoring it in Article 168 TFEU to avoid the digital sovereignty battlefield. We are offering early adopters not just Horizon Europe access, but priority drawdown from the new EU BioShield fund—making compliance a competitive advantage. Labs in Germany, Belgium, and Finland are already piloting the AI screening layer; we are expanding that network through joint procurement of standardised tools. This is how we build power now: not by decree, but by proving value, then mandating it.

The automated_decision_scandal—revealing systemic bias in an AI-supported social benefits system in one member state—has frozen public appetite for AI adoption. We are not ignoring it: we are containing it. The Commission is launching a rapid review under the AI Act’s conformity assessment powers, but we are framing it narrowly as a governance failure, not a technology one. Our message is clear: the problem is not AI, but unaccountable AI. That distinction is our lifeline—and it only holds if the biosecurity directive passes as health policy, not tech control. If we lose this, we lose the horizon.
