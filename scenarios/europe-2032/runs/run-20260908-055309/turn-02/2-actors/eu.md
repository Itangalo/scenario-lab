## Priority
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because a major incident is already unfolding, and while sovereignty through evaluation remains essential, we cannot afford to be strong in oversight but weak in survival; this surge uses unexpected momentum from civil society adoption of auditing tools to lock in real defensive gains within months, not years.

## In practice
We are activating an Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 122 TFEU, treating the current wave of AI-generated cyberattacks as a cross-border crisis requiring immediate action. The European Commission, in coordination with ENISA and the newly formed Crisis Response Cell for AI-Enabled Threats, is issuing binding guidance to member states to implement protective measures across energy, health, and public administration within 90 days. Funding of €8.2bn is being redirected from unused cohesion funds and the Digital Europe Programme, with disbursement tied to verified deployment of intrusion detection upgrades, software supply chain audits, and integration of the emergent open auditing framework into operational monitoring.

This effort builds directly on the unexpected traction of the university-developed auditing framework, which we are now formalizing as a baseline standard for public-sector AI assurance. We are fast-tracking its incorporation into NIS2 enforcement protocols and offering technical support teams from the EU Cyber Rapid Response Framework to assist national operators. Resistance from lower-capacity member states is being met with targeted aid packages and shared SOC (Security Operations Centre) capacity. At the same time, we are maintaining pressure on the Sovereign Evaluation Regime negotiations in the Council, where legal concerns persist — we are now proposing a phased implementation, starting with voluntary weight-sharing incentives for cloud providers in exchange for market access advantages, to build precedent before full compulsion takes hold. The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track, though permitting delays in two host states require mediation at the Energy Council level next month.
