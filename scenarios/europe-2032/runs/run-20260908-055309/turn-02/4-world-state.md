### Crisis Response and Fragmented Resolve

A major AI-powered cyberattack disrupted critical public services across three member states, exploiting software supply chain vulnerabilities generated by model-assisted code. The incident confirmed fears: offensive capability had decisively outpaced defence. In response, the EU activated Article 122 TFEU, launching the Emergency Resilience Surge. Binding guidance was issued to harden energy, health, and public administration systems, backed by €8.2bn in redirected funds.

The surge gained unexpected momentum from rapid adoption of an open auditing framework developed by a European university. Its integration into NIS2 enforcement allowed swift verification of compliance, and technical support teams were deployed to assist lower-capacity states. Early progress was visible, with detection times improving and intrusion protocols upgraded in key sectors.

Yet political strain deepened. The Sovereign Evaluation Regime stalled in the Council, as member states dependent on U.S. cloud providers challenged the legality of mandatory weight access. A phased approach—voluntary sharing in exchange for market access—was proposed, but met with scepticism from both developers and regulators. Meanwhile, permitting delays for domestic compute projects in two countries threatened the timeline of the Tech Sovereignty Package.

AI capability advanced to 56.0, driven by continuous training cycles and model refinement. Openweight capability rose modestly to 44.0, supported by incremental diffusion of transparency tools from the Kimi K3 research ecosystem, including lightweight model inspectors and configuration templates. These did not constitute full model releases but improved the ability of third parties to audit and adapt existing open models. With no safety measure implemented and capability surging, ai_safety dropped by 3 points. The gap between power and control widened further.

Public sentiment dipped slightly as media coverage focused on disruption and dependency. Labour displacement concerns resurfaced in regions affected by digital service outages.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

Despite urgent action, eu_ai_sovereignty declined as capability outpaced access and control. Political capital eroded under the weight of multiple measures in flight, but the Commission formally designated the Emergency Resilience Surge as its current priority, deprioritizing forward movement on the Sovereign Evaluation Regime. This realignment reduced coordination costs and prevented deeper losses, limiting the drop in political capital. The portfolio charge is now −10 (−3 each for InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, and Sovereign Evaluation Regime, plus −1 for the new priority).
