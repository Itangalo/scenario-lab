## Priority
**Sovereign Evaluation Regime** — because without independent technical understanding of frontier models, the EU cannot assess risk, enforce rules, or negotiate from strength — and this capability cannot wait for the full build-out of domestic compute.

## In practice
We are launching the Sovereign Evaluation Regime through a Regulation proposed under Article 114 TFEU, co-legislated with the European Parliament and requiring qualified majority in the Council. The European AI Office will lead, but we are standing up a new technical directorate within it — the European Model Evaluation Agency (EMEA) — with initial staffing drawn from national cybersecurity agencies, EDPB experts, and vetted academic researchers. We are requesting €3.8bn from the Multiannual Financial Framework reallocations and leveraging Horizon Europe restructuring to fund its first phase.

Resistance is expected from member states reliant on US cloud providers and from industry actors who see mandatory weight disclosure as a competitive burden. To secure support, we are offering early access to EMEA’s findings for certified EU-based developers and pairing the proposal with a fast-track compliance pathway for SMEs. We are also linking it to the ongoing review of the AI Act’s GPAI provisions, positioning it as the missing enforcement backbone. This is not just about safety — it is about creating a domain of EU-controlled knowledge, so that when incidents occur, we are not dependent on foreign laboratories to tell us what happened.
