### A Framework for Trust, Amid Ongoing Drift

The EU completed the **Unified EU Biosecurity Integration Directive**, transforming voluntary protocols into binding obligations for AI-driven screening of synthetic biology risks across member states. With Horizon Europe funding and infrastructure access now tied to compliance, the measure strengthened preparedness at key labs and biofoundries. Fast detection and shared response templates led to a coordinated rehearsal of a simulated dual-use agent leak, earning a resilience boost as systems proved interoperable under stress.

Simultaneously, the Commission launched the **Mandatory Audit and Redress Framework for Public-Sector AI Systems**, a large category 2 measure aimed squarely at the fallout from the social benefits scandal. Framed as a rule-of-law imperative under fundamental rights law, it mandates independent audits after high-impact AI decisions and triggers automatic redress when systemic bias is confirmed. The "TrustMark" certification, linked to future procurement eligibility, added leverage over vendors. Initial buy-in was mixed: several member states welcomed the clarity and access to the €1.2 billion Public Trust in AI Fund, while others resisted, citing administrative sovereignty. The measure passed, but with delays in ratification procedures expected.

Despite its ambition, political capital tightened further. The biosecurity directive’s completion delivered a +4 boost, but the new audit framework imposed a −3 recurring cost and a −1 priority penalty. With public sentiment still low, no sentiment-based capital top-up occurred. Sovereignty declined slightly, as AI capability growth outpaced the EU’s limited compute build-out, and no access-related events offset the −1 decay.

Public sentiment edged up as the audit framework was framed not as regulatory overreach but as institutional repair. Media coverage highlighted the ombudsman’s findings and the new redress mechanisms, offering a narrative of accountability. Still, trust remains fragile, and the broader perception of AI as a source of administrative harm persists.

### Faint Signals, No Shifts

Informal coordination among European and like-minded nations continued, with quiet talks on compute sharing and joint evaluations. Yet no formal coalition emerged, and U.S. posture remained **ALLIANCE**, with structured access unchanged.
