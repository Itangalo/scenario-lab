## New measure
**Mandatory Audit and Redress Framework for Public-Sector AI Systems**  
We are establishing a legally binding process requiring all AI systems used in public administration to undergo independent, retroactive impact audits following any major decisional outcome (e.g., denial of benefits, tax penalties, immigration rulings), with findings published and corrective redress mechanisms automatically triggered when systemic bias is confirmed.

## Priority
**Mandatory Audit and Redress Framework for Public-Sector AI Systems** — because the automated_decision_scandal has shattered public trust and made further top-down AI mandates politically impossible, we must act decisively on accountability *now*, not later, to prevent a total backlash that would derail both sovereignty and resilience efforts; this measure reframes AI governance as justice, not control, and offers a path to rebuild legitimacy from the wreckage.

## In practice
We are treating the social benefits scandal not as an outlier, but as the first visible fracture in a system-wide failure of oversight. The Commission is invoking Article 42 of the AI Act to launch emergency conformity assessments in all member states using AI for social welfare, but we are going further: under the new framework, every public AI decision that materially harms an individual will now trigger a mandatory audit by an independent authority—either national data protection bodies or a newly reinforced European Data Protection Board, depending on cross-border implications. These audits will assess not just technical performance, but systemic bias, feedback loops, and downstream impacts. Where harm is found, redress will be automatic: backdated benefits, legal standing for class actions, and public disclosure of root causes.

We are anchoring this in Article 19 TFEU (protection of rights) and the Charter of Fundamental Rights, not in digital policy, to avoid the toxic framing of “Brussels vs. innovation.” We are offering member states a carrot: early compliance unlocks access to a new €1.2 billion **Public Trust in AI Fund**, co-financed by the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, to upgrade legacy systems and train auditors. For vendors, we are introducing a "TrustMark" certification—only AI systems that have passed a pilot audit cycle can carry it, and only TrustMark systems will be eligible for EU public procurement after turn 15. This is how we restore credibility: not by promising better tech, but by proving we can enforce better outcomes. Without this, no directive, no compute grid, no fallback protocol will survive the next scandal. We are acting not because the moment is convenient, but because it is late.
