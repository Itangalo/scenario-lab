## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyberattack is still unfolding, detection-response gaps remain acute, and public trust is fraying; this is the only measure delivering tangible defensive gains within the next six months, and its momentum from civil society adoption of auditing tools must be locked in before the window closes.

## In practice  
We are executing the Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 122 TFEU with binding guidance now in force across energy, health, and public administration sectors. ENISA, supported by the Crisis Response Cell for AI-Enabled Threats, is deploying technical teams to 14 member states to verify implementation of software supply chain audits and intrusion detection upgrades. The open auditing framework developed by TU Delft has been fast-tracked into NIS2 compliance protocols, and we are offering shared SOC capacity and €8.2bn in redirected cohesion and Digital Europe funds to ensure rapid uptake, especially in lower-capacity states. Resistance from member states reliant on U.S. cloud infrastructure is being met with conditional offers: full technical cooperation in exchange for early access to the Middle Power Compute Accord’s benefits.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the Middle Power Compute Accord through a series of ministerial dialogues under the Digital Partnerships framework. The Commission is negotiating joint export-licence alignment on EUV-related components and establishing a shared compute reservation pool for critical evaluations. This leverages our combined grip on lithography materials, packaging, and inspection tools to create a counterweight to unilateral dominance. The initiative directly supports our two-year commitment by securing compute access on terms we help set, not accept. Meanwhile, permitting delays in the Tech Sovereignty Package are being addressed at the Energy Council, where we are pushing for designated AI-critical infrastructure status to override local bottlenecks. The Sovereign Evaluation Regime remains stalled, but we are using the eval_anomaly_reports precursor to build quiet consensus on the need for weight access—framing it not as sovereignty for its own sake, but as a necessary condition for detecting hidden capabilities.
