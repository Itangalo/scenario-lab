## New measure
**Secure Access Guarantee with Hyperscaler Partnerships**  
We are establishing legally binding agreements with designated AI hyperscalers to guarantee non-withdrawable access to AI capability above 55.0 for EU essential functions, backed by enforceable penalties and tied to the Union’s own compute milestones.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases eu_ai_sovereignty by +15 by anchoring access rights in contract and law; raises resilience by +5 by ensuring continuity of critical services; costs -5 public_sentiment if perceived as corporate favouritism, mitigated by transparency and reciprocity clauses.  
`Applies to:` The European Commission, ENISA, EuroHPC, and designated hyperscalers (selected based on compliance with EU data governance and security standards).

## Priority
**Secure Access Guarantee with Hyperscaler Partnerships** — because the member_state_defection event proves that unilateral industrial policy is collapsing under external pressure, and because export_control_escalation will soon cut even allied access unless we lock in enforceable rights now — this measure turns dependency into mutual obligation and ensures that sovereignty is not just territorial but contractual and irreversible.

## In practice
We are executing the Secure Access Guarantee through a new instrument under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper framework to designate qualifying hyperscalers whose systems operate above 55.0 capability in or affecting the EU. The agreements will be negotiated by the European Commission’s Directorate-General for Competition and DG CONNECT, with legal backing from the European Legal Network on Digital Sovereignty. Each agreement will require the hyperscaler to reserve a defined share of inference capacity for EU public functions — healthcare, energy grid management, emergency response — with failover protocols and audit rights managed by ENISA. In return, the EU will offer preferential access to EuroHPC’s sovereign compute for joint research and accelerated regulatory review for compliant model updates.

The guarantee will sunset unless eu_ai_sovereignty reaches 40.0 by turn 10, creating a binding incentive to complete the InvestAI Gigafactories and restore domestic leverage. We are already in quiet talks with two U.S.-based providers whose European subsidiaries have expressed concern over Beijing’s pressure and Washington’s unpredictability — they see value in a stable, rules-based anchor. However, resistance is forming in the Council, where some member states fear locking in foreign dependency. To counter this, we are offering a side letter: any member state that signs bilateral deals undercutting this framework will lose access to the EU’s joint procurement pool for AI infrastructure — a credible sanction that turns cohesion into self-interest. Public messaging will frame this not as corporate subsidy, but as strategic insurance: we are not buying compute, we are buying continuity.
