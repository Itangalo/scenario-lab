### A Widening Frontier, a Narrowing Margin  
The frontier in AI capability advanced steadily, reaching 64.0 as automated systems demonstrated reliable performance in contract review and clinical coding—domains previously thought to require human judgment. The emergence of cheap, automated verification in these fields accelerated progress, reinforcing the trend toward broader, faster capability growth. With no safety measures implemented on deployed systems, and the gap between capability and control widening, `ai_safety` dropped further, now at 15.0—deep in the zone of minimal assurance.

### Open Replication Gains Ground  
Despite hardware shortages from the Taiwan blockade, openweight capability rose to 51.0 as distributed actors refined fine-tuned Kimi K3 variants on consumer-grade clusters. These models, while not frontier-level, proved effective in narrow cyber operations, raising concerns about uncontrolled proliferation.

### EU Doubles Down on Access, Not Just Infrastructure  
The European Union renewed its strategic commitment to AI sovereignty, pivoting from pure compute investment to enforceable access rights. The new **Secure Access Guarantee with Hyperscaler Partnerships**, a large category 4 measure, was formally proposed. It aims to lock in non-withdrawable AI capacity for essential services through legally binding contracts, backed by sanctions on defector states.

However, the initiative faced immediate headwinds. A member state, citing national interest, announced a separate deal with a U.S. hyperscaler—undermining the EU’s unified position and triggering the **member_state_defection** event. This, combined with tightening **export_control_escalation** that now rations even allied access, exposed the fragility of external dependencies.

The measure’s ambition strained political capital. With four initiatives now in flight—including the ongoing gigafactories project—and a named priority, the portfolio charge was heavy. Public skepticism grew, particularly around perceived corporate favouritism, pulling `public_sentiment` down. The promise of strategic insurance rang hollow to some, while others saw it as necessary pragmatism.
