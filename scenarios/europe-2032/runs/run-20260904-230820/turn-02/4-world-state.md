### The Storm Breaks  
A wave of AI-powered ransomware cripples regional hospitals, transit hubs, and water authorities across three member states. Generated by open-weight models now capable of chaining zero-day exploits, the attack bypasses legacy defences with eerie precision. Initial containment takes days; full recovery, weeks. Public anger flares. The incident confirms what the stealthy grid intrusion hinted at: offensive AI is no longer theoretical. With no finished resilience measure in place, `resilience` erodes further.

### EU Responds — At a Cost  
The Commission launches the Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative, a large-scale mandate for AI-driven defences across essential services. Funding is secured via reallocation, and the European AI Defence Lab begins assembling. However, pushback is immediate: mid-sized operators protest the compliance burden, and legal challenges loom over the use of autonomous response under NIS2. The initiative yields early detection pilots but no systemic upgrade yet — a +2 partial benefit to `resilience`.

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive advances in trilogue, but US cloud providers file pre-emptive legal objections. No compute is yet reserved. The Tech Sovereignty Package gains three new AI Acceleration Zones, but private investment remains sluggish. Sovereignty holds steady — no gain, but no loss.

### Political Capital Erodes  
Two large measures now drain `eu_political_capital` at −3 each, and the named priority adds −1. The proposal bonus for the Hardening Initiative is +4 — a large response to a major, fresh incident — but this is outweighed by the costs. Sentiment drops sharply as job losses in public IT and visible disruption feed anxiety. No legitimacy lending occurs.

`ai_capability` rises +2.0, driven by automated research loops. Safety falls further — opaque reasoning and the lab incident remain unresolved — and openweight models absorb the leap, rising to 44.0.
