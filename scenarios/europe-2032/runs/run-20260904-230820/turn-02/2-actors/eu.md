## New measure  
**Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative**  
Launch a binding programme to retrofit cyber defences across EU energy, water, and transport networks using AI-driven anomaly detection and autonomous response systems under human oversight.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to +15 resilience by turn 5; reduce blast radius of future cyber incidents by at least 60%; begin closing the gap between offensive capability in open models and defensive readiness  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, with co-funding and compliance enforced through the NIS2 framework and the European Cyber Resilience Act

## Priority  
**Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative** — because the cyber_major_incident proves that offensive AI tools are already in circulation, and with resilience at 37.0 and openweight capability rising, we cannot wait for the Sovereign Compute Directive to land before defending what we already have.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that the attack this turn was not a warning — it was the first shot in a new operational reality. The cyber_major_incident exploited AI-generated tooling to chain vulnerabilities across public services, and while it was contained, it revealed that our defences are static, siloed, and blind to novel patterns. This is no longer about patching — it is about rearchitecting. This turn, the Commission will propose the Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative under Article 12 of the Cyber Resilience Act, mandating that all operators of essential services deploy AI-powered monitoring systems capable of detecting and responding to zero-day exploits in real time. These systems will be developed through a new public consortium — the European AI Defence Lab — drawing on talent from ENISA, national CERTs, and vetted private researchers.

Funding will come from a reallocation of €18bn from the Digital Europe Programme and NextGenerationEU, with 70% going directly to operators for deployment and 30% to the Lab for R&D and validation. Compliance will be tied to NIS2 enforcement: non-compliant operators will face fines up to 2% of turnover and exclusion from public contracts. We know this will meet resistance — especially from mid-sized utilities with thin margins — so we are pairing the mandate with a transition shield: full cost coverage for the first 18 months, and a fast-track access lane to the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive’s reserved capacity for those who certify early.

At the same time, we are pressing forward with the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive, finalising the draft under Article 114 TFEU and entering trilogue negotiations with the Council and Parliament. The Competitiveness Council remains uneasy, but we are holding firm: the liability shield and public utility model are non-negotiable. We are also deepening the Tech Sovereignty Package by securing binding commitments from five additional member states to designate AI Acceleration Zones, with Greece, Poland, and Finland moving forward with pilot sites. These zones will not just attract investment — they will become the proving grounds for sovereign AI in action.
