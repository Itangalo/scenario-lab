### The Capability March Continues  
AI capability advances to 58.0 as automated research systems accelerate breakthroughs in materials science and cryptanalysis. The closed frontier pulls ahead, and open-weight models absorb much of the leap—now at 46.0—enabling more sophisticated, adaptive cyberattacks. Defensive AI struggles to keep pace, and with no safety breakthroughs, opaque reasoning persists. `ai_safety` drops to 27.0 as developers prioritise performance over interpretability.

### A Workforce in Crisis  
The EU’s response to the ransomware wave intensifies, but a critical bottleneck emerges: a severe shortage of skilled cyber defenders. Despite the launch of the Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative, many member states lack personnel to implement AI-driven defences. Autonomous systems are increasingly deployed out of necessity, raising concerns about accountability and failure modes.

In response, the European Cyber Resilience Corps is launched, mobilising €4.2bn in social and education funds to train 15,000 AI-literate cyber defenders by 2030. The programme gains early support from Germany and France, but recruitment timelines remain tight, and private-sector competition for talent shows no sign of easing. The initiative yields a small resilience boost (+1) as pilot cohorts begin training.

### Sovereignty Under Strain  
The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive clears trilogue but faces legal challenges from US cloud providers contesting the 30% reservation mandate. While the directive passes, its enforcement is uncertain, and no new sovereign compute comes online this turn. With `ai_capability` rising by 2.0, `eu_ai_sovereignty` drops to 21.0 due to growing dependence on foreign frontier capacity.

The Tech Sovereignty Package progresses slowly—three AI Acceleration Zones are designated—but private investment remains below target, reflecting broader market hesitancy.

### Political Capital Erodes  
Three measures now drain political capital: two large (−3 each) and one small (−2), plus the −1 priority cost. The proposal bonus for the Cyber Resilience Corps is +2—modest, given its scale and the severity of the labour crisis. Total capital falls to 39.0.
