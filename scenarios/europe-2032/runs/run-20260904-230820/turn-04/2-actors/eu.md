## New measure  
**Sovereign AI Testbed and Assurance Facility**  
Establish a publicly governed, technically independent facility to evaluate, stress-test, and secure AI systems deployed in EU critical functions, with full access to model weights and training data under strict legal safeguards.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to +15 in ai_safety by turn 7; begin closing the gap between private capability and public understanding; create a foundation for future sovereign model development and certification  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member-state critical agencies, and any private provider of AI systems used in public or essential services within the EU

## In practice  
The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive has now formally passed and is legally in force, though its implementation remains contested. The 30% reservation mandate stands, but the legal challenges from US cloud providers have delayed physical deployment. We are shifting focus from negotiation to enforcement: the Commission is preparing a binding interpretation under the Digital Markets Act’s ancillary powers to clarify that compute access for public interest qualifies as a fairness obligation, and we are working with ENISA to define what “state-critical use” means in practice—starting with AI-driven grid management, emergency response coordination, and cryptographic infrastructure. The Competitiveness Council remains uneasy, but the cyber_major_incident has hardened support in Germany, Spain, and the Nordic states. We are offering joint EU-US research carve-outs, but not at the cost of the directive’s core function: compute we can rely on, when we need it.

Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package continues to underperform on private investment. The three AI Acceleration Zones—now designated in Greece, Poland, and Finland—are ready for deployment, but developers are waiting for clarity on energy access and liability. We are preparing a targeted incentive: priority access to sovereign compute for firms that co-locate with EU-hardened grids and commit to open-weight security audits. This will not close the gap alone, but it begins to align market incentives with strategic resilience.

The Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative is now technically feasible in design but still starved for people. The European Cyber Resilience Corps is on track—pilot cohorts have begun in Lyon, Berlin, and Warsaw—and we are fast-tracking integration with national CERTs. But the talent pipeline will not relieve frontline pressure until turn 5. In the interim, we are issuing interim guidance through ENISA to limit autonomous response to tiered, bounded actions with mandatory human re-authentication every 72 hours. This is not ideal, but it prevents drift into uncontrolled autonomy while we rebuild human capacity.

Most urgently, we are launching the Sovereign AI Testbed and Assurance Facility because we can no longer outsource understanding. With ai_safety at 27.0, we are flying blind. This facility—hosted jointly by the EU AI Office, a consortium of national labs, and selected academic partners—will have legal authority to inspect model weights, run red-team evaluations, and mandate fixes before deployment in public functions. It will not develop frontier models, but it will understand them. We are funding it through the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe, with an initial €1.8bn commitment spread to turn 7. The legal basis will be Article 12 of the AI Act, expanded via delegated act. France and Germany support it; some member states fear duplication, so we are positioning it as a *public complement* to private evaluation, not a replacement. This is not just about safety—it is about reclaiming the ability to know what we are running. Without that, we are not sovereign. We are just tenants.
