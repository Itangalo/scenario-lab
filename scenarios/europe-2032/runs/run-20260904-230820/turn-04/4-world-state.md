### The Threshold Crossed  
AI capability reaches 60.0 as closed labs deploy systems capable of completing multi-day professional projects autonomously, including full software audits and synthetic vulnerability generation. The milestone reflects sustained progress rather than a sudden leap, with no implication of self-acceleration or systemic inflection within this turn.

### A Crisis in the Shadows  
A major cyber incident strikes: a ransomware sweep paralyzes emergency dispatch systems in three member states. The attack exploits AI-generated zero-days in a widely used public safety communications platform. Response is delayed by inconsistent patching and a lack of skilled defenders. Attribution remains unclear, but forensic analysis points to model-generated tooling. Public anger mounts, and trust in digital infrastructure wavers.

### Open Models, Slow Diffusion  
Openweight capability rises to 49.0, reflecting gradual incorporation of recent advances into publicly available models. While some adversarial tools derived from frontier systems appear in the wild, their deployment is fragmented and delayed by release cycles, access barriers, and integration lags. The narrative of open models "absorbing much of this leap" is revised: their impact grows, but at a measured pace consistent with prior diffusion trends.

### Sovereignty, Still Out of Reach  
The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive is now law, but no new capacity has been secured. Legal challenges stall deployment, and foreign providers continue to limit access. With AI capability rising by 2.0, EU AI sovereignty drops to 20.0, reflecting deepening dependence. The Tech Sovereignty Package fails to attract expected investment, as firms cite energy costs and regulatory uncertainty.

### Political Capital Under Pressure  
The EU announces plans for the Sovereign AI Testbed and Assurance Facility, a category 5 measure intended to rebuild technical understanding and oversight capacity. However, due to constitutional limits, it cannot enter the portfolio this turn—the existing four measures (Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive, Tech Sovereignty Package, Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative, and European Cyber Resilience Corps) remain active, with the first as the named priority. No proposal bonus is applied, and the measure remains in pre-implementation. The cyber incident and ongoing delays erode confidence. Public sentiment falls to 30.0, and political capital declines to 37.0, reflecting sustained portfolio costs and legitimacy concerns—within bounds, as sovereignty’s direct impact on political capital remains limited by institutional resilience.

### A Talent Gap That Won’t Close  
The European Cyber Resilience Corps begins training, but too slowly to prevent overreliance on autonomous defences. Interim safeguards are issued, but human oversight remains fragmented. Resilience dips to 39.0 as systemic vulnerabilities persist.
