### The cut-off
The message arrived as error screens. In hospitals, ministries and logistics firms, the leading American model stopped answering — quotas suspended, contracts under review, no appeal channel. Staff who had built triage summaries and procurement tools on top of it scrambled to re-route work.

Brussels published a priority queue for pooled capacity hosted with Japanese, Korean, Canadian and Gulf partners, with reimbursement through civil protection funds. Some hospital workloads moved within days. Many others waited: authentication mismatches, test data that did not transfer, opaque-model evaluations that had to be redone. The capital that had kept its own American line refused to join the queue, calling pooling a downgrade.

### The lithography squeeze
At the same time Washington pressed the Dutch equipment maker to extend cuts beyond leading-edge machines to older tools and servicing. The company, caught between American technology controls and European pressure, slowed compliance but did not refuse it. Trade officials opened a joint retaliation file and promised legal cover, but engineers kept cancelling service visits. Editorials called it Europe's only chokepoint being spent by someone else.

### Streets and desks
As casualty counts from the spring pathogen wave closed out openly, attention shifted to empty job-guarantee desks. Coordinated marches of graduates filled squares in several capitals, then moved to data-centre fences. Labour ministers refilled counselling shifts with capped funds and police were ordered to de-escalate. It worked unevenly: one night of batons outside a fenced site dominated coverage for a week, and local papers ran portraits of graduates queuing for appointments that lasted minutes.

Shielding kits repackaged for small clinics and town utilities helped contain the tail of the outbreak, and mayors acknowledged stocks had arrived. But field teams left, phone lines filled again, and the sense stuck that Europe had absorbed the blow without answering the dependence behind it.
