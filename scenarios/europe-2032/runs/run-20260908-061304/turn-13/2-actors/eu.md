## New measure
**Secure Critical Compute Corridors (SC3)**  
Launch an emergency infrastructure programme to fast-track sovereign access to high-end AI compute through strategic partnerships with trusted foreign hyperscalers, conditional on legal anchoring, data sovereignty, and failover guarantees under EU jurisdiction.

## Priority  
**Secure Critical Compute Corridors (SC3)** — because the Taiwan blockade has exposed a fatal vulnerability: we can evaluate models all day, but without sovereign access to the compute that runs them, SEAP becomes a ritual, not a shield — and this turn, with political capital just high enough to risk a fight and before openweight models breach 60.0, is the only window we may get to bolt real capacity to EU jurisdiction before the next capability leap.

## In practice  
This turn, we are converting the completion of SEAP into leverage. DG CNECT, backed by the Commission’s legal service, is entering negotiations with three US hyperscalers — two with existing EU data centres and one with expansion plans — offering preferential access to EU public-sector AI procurement in exchange for legally binding commitments: compute infrastructure must remain operational under EU jurisdiction during crises, model weights must be secured to a state-actor standard on EU soil, and failover protocols must be exercised annually with ENISA. We are using the semiconductor crisis as justification: no more “cloud on trust.” The Council Presidency has circulated a draft Council Recommendation under Article 114 TFEU to frame this as a single market imperative, pre-empting fragmentation.  

Resistance is expected from member states reliant on foreign cloud infrastructure and from legal scholars questioning extraterritorial enforceability. We are offering Hungary and Poland targeted retraining grants and priority access to new compute nodes in exchange for abstaining in the Competitiveness Council. The European Defence Agency is quietly included in the technical working group, not because we are militarizing AI, but because only defence-grade continuity planning has survived recent shocks. This is not a full sovereignty build — we cannot yet fund our own frontier labs or fabs — but it is the first time we are treating compute as critical infrastructure, not convenience. If we lock in just one transatlantic corridor with real teeth, we begin closing the gap between evaluation and control.
