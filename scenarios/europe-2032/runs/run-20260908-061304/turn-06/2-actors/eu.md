## In practice
This turn, we operationalise the Sovereign Inference Corridor, now formally completed, by activating its first three nodes in Finland, Portugal, and the Czech Republic. These will run hardened openweights at capability 44.0+, isolated from public networks, with biometric access, continuous firmware verification, and real-time anomaly detection powered by the newly adopted safety breakthrough in runtime interpretability. The agreement with Italy and Spain, secured through co-governance seats and €3.5bn in fast-tracked resilience co-funding, has unlocked full Corridor coverage — but integration remains fragile. We are deploying ENISA teams to each site to conduct initial audits and establish secure update pipelines, using repurposed InvestAI capital and EIB emergency lending to close funding gaps.

The safety_breakthrough — a method for certifying model behaviour in deployment through lightweight formal verification — has changed the game. It is being rapidly adopted by leading labs not out of compliance, but because it reduces their own operational risk. We are mandating its integration into all systems under the Hardened Openweight Deployment Programme, and requiring red-teaming units to attempt to bypass the certification as part of their exercises. This is not just about security: it is about creating a feedback loop between control assurance and operational resilience, so that what we run is not only sovereign, but *knowably* safe.

The Tech Sovereignty Package has now delivered its final phase: the EIB has issued full 80% loan guarantees for data centre builds in Sweden and Romania, and two new accelerated-permitting zones are online, cutting approval times from 36 to 12 months. But political capital remains thin — at 24.0 — and public sentiment is still low. The new EU-Wide Red-Teaming and Resilience Drills will test not only systems, but trust: by making failures visible before they become crises, we aim to rebuild credibility from the ground up. These drills will be coordinated through the Competitiveness Council and the Cybersecurity Act framework, with mandatory participation from all member states’ critical operators. The first cycle will simulate a coordinated compromise of fallback models during a denial-of-service crisis — the very scenario the Corridor was built to prevent. We are not waiting for the next incident. We are rehearsing for it.
