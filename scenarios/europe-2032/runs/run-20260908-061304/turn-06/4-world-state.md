### A Fragile Leap Forward

The EU marks a technical milestone: the Sovereign Inference Corridor is operational, with nodes active in Finland, Portugal, and the Czech Republic. Air-gapped clusters now run hardened openweights, secured with biometric access and real-time anomaly detection. Italy and Spain, brought in through governance concessions and fast-tracked funding, complete the network’s geographic reach. Yet integration remains brittle—ENISA audits reveal configuration drift and inconsistent patch cycles, exposing gaps between policy and practice.

A major interpretability breakthrough reshapes the safety landscape. A new method for certifying model behaviour in runtime is adopted rapidly—not by coercion, but because leading labs see it as reducing their own operational risk. The EU mandates its use in the Hardened Openweight Deployment Programme, and red-teaming units are instructed to attempt to bypass the verification, turning assurance into a live test.

Buoyed by this progress, the EU launches the EU-Wide Red-Teaming and Resilience Drills, a large category 6 measure. It mandates semi-annual live-fire simulations across critical infrastructure, targeting model compromise and denial-of-inference attacks. Coordination is tight on paper, but early pushback emerges from member states wary of exposing vulnerabilities publicly.

The US administration takes office, and the posture is clear: alliance. Access to frontier AI will be structured, not blocked. Brussels breathes easier—dependence remains, but it is now governed.

Still, momentum comes at a cost. Political capital, already thin, buckles under the weight of multiple large ongoing measures. The new drills add strain, and no relief arrives from public sentiment, which remains flat at 32.0, anchored by job displacement fears and private-sector retrenchment.

### Sovereignty, Tested

The Corridor’s completion boosts EU AI sovereignty, but rising capability and lingering dependence cap gains. The safety leap, while real, does not yet reach beyond the open frontier.
