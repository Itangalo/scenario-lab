## In practice  
This turn, we are consolidating the Sovereign Evaluation Authority Pilot into permanent oversight infrastructure. The European Commission, through DG CNECT and the JRC, is finalizing the integration of red-team results from ENISA’s adversarial simulations into a unified scoring framework for model risk, now being applied to live submissions from providers seeking access to energy grid control systems and national health data platforms. Legal teams are preparing a joint defence of SEAP’s mandate under Article 16 TFEU, backed by preliminary evidence from stress tests showing that unassessed models fail under simulated cyber-physical strain at three times the rate of evaluated ones. We are offering early-access member states—France, Germany, and the Netherlands—priority inclusion in a new fast-track certification lane for domestic AI ventures, conditioning it on full adherence to SEAP protocols.

At the same time, the Taiwan blockade has crystallized a strategic emergency: capability without access is powerless. With no sovereign compute base capable of running even today’s frontier models, let alone tomorrow’s, our evaluation capacity risks becoming a ceremonial gatekeeper rather than a shield. The Competitiveness Council’s emergency session is mapping the rupture—not just chips, but packaging, cooling, power, and talent pipelines—toward a unified diagnosis. But without fiscal headroom and with Hungary and Poland resisting any centralised industrial directive framed as solidarity, we cannot yet launch a response. Instead, we are using SEAP’s completion as leverage: offering conditional access to evaluation data and future certification benefits in exchange for member-state buy-in on upcoming sovereignty measures. This turn is not for action, but for alignment—forcing the reality of dependence into the centre of the next move.
