### A Strategic Pause Amid Supply Shock

The European Union completes the rollout of the Sovereign Evaluation Authority Pilot (SEAP), marking a milestone in its ambition to independently assess high-risk AI systems. Testing frameworks are operational, red-team exercises conducted, and early evaluations underway for critical infrastructure access. While legal challenges persist, the invocation of Article 16 TFEU lends temporary legitimacy, and cooperative national regulators begin integrating findings. The measure lands as designed—delivering moderate gains in oversight capacity.

However, the global landscape shifts violently. A blockade halts advanced semiconductor exports from Taiwan, freezing the supply of cutting-edge AI chips. Overnight, compute becomes a strategic bottleneck. The EU’s reliance on foreign frontier models and manufacturing infrastructure is exposed as an existential vulnerability. Though SEAP can now evaluate systems, the physical means to run or develop sovereign alternatives remain scarce.

With political capital critically low and no new funding available, the Commission refrains from launching new initiatives. Instead, it convenes emergency talks under the Competitiveness Council to map dependencies and prioritize responses. Yet without financial headroom or unified member-state backing—particularly from Poland and Hungary—the discussions yield analysis, not action.

The pause prevents further erosion of focus, but sovereignty declines sharply. Despite SEAP’s completion, the semiconductor crisis triggers a −3 event penalty under metric rule 5: the Union’s access to essential AI capacity is effectively severed. This loss outweighs any momentum from completed measures. Meanwhile, `ai_capability` advances by 0.5, contributing another −1 to sovereignty due to capability pressure.

Political capital sees a modest rebound. The completion of a large category 5 measure provides +4, offsetting ongoing portfolio costs. With only SEAP in flight (now finishing) and no new measures proposed, the portfolio charge is minimal. However, public sentiment remains stagnant—no visible benefits emerge this turn, and anxiety over technological dependency grows.

Open-weight models continue their slow climb, reaching 54.5, as distributed developers refine accessible architectures in the absence of new frontier releases.
