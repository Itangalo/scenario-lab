## In practice  
This turn, we are completing the rollout of the Sovereign Evaluation Authority Pilot at EuroHPC sites in France, Germany, and the Netherlands. The JRC has delivered baseline testing frameworks for model behaviour under real-world stress conditions, ENISA has conducted red-team exercises simulating adversarial fine-tuning and prompt injection attacks, and cross-border evaluator teams have been activated under European Labour Authority coordination. We are now processing the first wave of evaluations on systems seeking access to energy grid management, air traffic control interfaces, and national health data infrastructures. Legal challenges from the internal market committee are being addressed through a joint Council-Commission task force, invoking Article 16 TFEU as precedent for urgent public protection in digitally mediated systemic risk. Early findings will be shared selectively with national regulators who cooperate, creating an incentive loop for broader buy-in.

At the same time, the Taiwan blockade has thrown our industrial assumptions into crisis. With advanced semiconductor shipments frozen, every node in the AI stack is suddenly contested. Our sovereignty score remains low not because we lack vision, but because compute — real, usable, future-proof compute — is vanishing from the global market. The SEAP may know what systems do, but if we cannot run them independently, knowledge becomes commentary. This turn is not for new starts. It is for triage: converting SEAP from a pilot into durable authority, assessing which parts of our dependency chain just broke, and preparing a response that matches the scale of the rupture. We are convening an emergency session of the Competitiveness Council focused on supply chain resilience, but without capital to launch a new measure, our leverage lies in repurposing what we’ve built — and in forcing clarity where ambiguity used to suffice.
