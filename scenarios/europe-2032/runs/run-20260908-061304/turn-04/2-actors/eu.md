## Statement changes

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now recognise that speed without sovereign capacity is fragility, and our commitments must anchor to infrastructure we control, not just intentions we declare.

- Grounds: The `eu_frontier_access_denied` event and the bilateral hyperscaler deal have demonstrated that access to foreign AI systems cannot be relied upon, even under crisis frameworks. Our ability to act under uncertainty is now constrained not by caution, but by dependency: we can no longer assume that decisive intent translates into operational effect. This recalibration does not retreat from the commitment to act — it deepens its foundation, insisting that action must be rooted in sovereign execution capacity.

## In practice  

We are launching the Sovereign Inference Corridor to give teeth to our emergency continuity plans. The Emergency Access Framework is only as strong as the infrastructure that runs it, and right now, that infrastructure is fragmented, under-resourced, and often dependent on the very providers who just cut us off. Under this measure, we will repurpose €45bn from the InvestAI Gigafactories reserve to fast-track the retrofitting of three EuroHPC sites — in Finland, Portugal, and the Czech Republic — into sovereign inference hubs. These will host hardened versions of openweights at or above 44.0 capability, derived from the last legally accessible versions, running on air-gapped clusters with state-actor-grade security. The legal basis is Article 114 TFEU, with derogations under Article 216 for rapid deployment. We are convening the Competitiveness Council next month to secure unanimous approval, offering Italy and Spain guaranteed node hosting in exchange for dropping reservations about centralisation.

At the same time, we are completing the Cyber Resilience Surge. ENISA has delivered binding detection standards to all 27 member states, and real-time AI-driven intrusion monitoring is now live in 90% of designated critical operators. The Port of Riga has already detected and contained a simulated AI-coordinated grid attack during a live exercise, validating the cross-border response protocol. We are converting the surge into a standing programme under the revised NIS3 Directive, with funding shifted from emergency to baseline budgets — a quiet but vital institutionalisation of crisis gains. Poland has met its grid modernisation threshold and is now fully integrated, unlocking €1.2bn in Just AI Facility funds.

The European AI Safety Observatory is now staffed at 60%, with lead hires from INRIA, Fraunhofer, and KU Leuven. We have issued formal notices under Article 5 of the AI Act to three frontier labs — two U.S.-based, one Chinese — demanding access to models serving EU users. No response yet, but we are preparing a test case in the CJEU, knowing that legal authority without enforcement is ritual. Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package is being restructured: with private capital withdrawn, we are shifting to a public development model, with the EIB offering 80% loan guarantees for data centre builds in designated accelerated zones. The message is clear: if the market will not build our sovereignty, the state will.
