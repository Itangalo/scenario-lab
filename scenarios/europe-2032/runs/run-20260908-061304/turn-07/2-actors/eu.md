## In practice
This turn, we are completing the rollout of hardened openweights across all five Sovereign Inference Corridor nodes, now fully geographically operational with Finland, Portugal, Czechia, Italy, and Spain connected. ENISA teams are embedded on-site to enforce configuration standardization and patch-cycle discipline, using emergency EIB financing to close gaps left by delayed national contributions. The new runtime certification method is being integrated into every deployment, enabling continuous behavioural verification — not just pre-deployment checks — and we are tasking red-teaming units under ENISA’s coordination to attempt to bypass these controls in real time. This turns assurance into an active discipline: if the verification can be spoofed, we need to know before crisis hits.

The EU-Wide Red-Teaming and Resilience Drills are now legally mandated under the revised Cybersecurity Act, with participation tied to Cohesion Fund disbursements to ensure compliance. The first live-fire exercise will simulate a cascading failure in which fallback models are poisoned during a denial-of-inference attack on public health systems. Scenarios are co-developed with national CERTs and EuroHPC operators, and results will feed directly into the Hardened Openweight Programme’s update cycle. Resistance from two member states — citing sovereignty concerns — is being addressed through bilateral technical support packages and observer roles in the Competitiveness Council. We are not trading security for unity; we are making unity a condition of security.
