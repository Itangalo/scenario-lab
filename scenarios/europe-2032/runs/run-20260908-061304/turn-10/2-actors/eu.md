## In practice  
This turn, we are launching the Sovereign Evaluation Authority Pilot (SEAP), a time-bound but fully resourced effort to stand up, by turn 13, a functioning evaluation capability with legal authority to inspect, test, and conditionally block AI systems before deployment in high-risk domains. The legal base is Article 16 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU for urgent public protection, paired with delegated powers under the revised AI Act. The JRC will lead technical development, ENISA will handle operational security and red-teaming, and the European Labour Authority will second staff to manage cross-border coordination of evaluator deployment. We are drawing on the €1.2 billion in dormant InvestAI contingency funds, reprogramming them under the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s strategic autonomy clause.

We are negotiating with Germany, France, and the Netherlands to co-host the first evaluation nodes, each tied to existing EuroHPC sites to reuse hardened infrastructure. Resistance is expected from member states with large AI import dependencies, who fear market disruption, and from the European Parliament’s internal market committee, which sees this as overreach. To counter, we are offering early access to SEAP’s findings for national regulators and a sunset clause at turn 15 unless converted into standing law. This is not a research project: it is a regulatory weapon under construction, designed to force transparency where voluntary cooperation has failed. The cyber_major_incident this turn—likely enabled by model-generated exploit chains—only confirms that we are already operating in a world where capability outpaces accountability, and that the EU’s survival as a decision-making body depends on its ability to *know* what it is up against.
