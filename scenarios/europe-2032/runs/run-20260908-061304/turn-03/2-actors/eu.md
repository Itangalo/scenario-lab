## Statement changes

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Maintain sovereign AI capability and prevent catastrophic risk under uncertainty, while adapting to abrupt shifts in global access and investment that undermine prior assumptions of steady build-up.

- Grounds: The twin shocks of `ai_investment_collapse` and `eu_frontier_access_denied` fundamentally alter the strategic calculus: the assumption that sovereign capacity could be built gradually through aligned private investment is no longer tenable. The Union must now act to secure minimal operational continuity and crisis response capacity under conditions of scarcity and exclusion, not just long-term competitiveness. This does not abandon the original commitment but reframes it around survival of capability under disruption.

## New measure
**Emergency Access & Continuity Framework**

We are establishing a legal and technical fallback mechanism to ensure essential public services retain minimal AI functionality even when cut off from external frontier models.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` prevents collapse of critical digital services during access denial; increases resilience by 8–12 points by turn 5 by hardening continuity protocols; slows decay of eu_ai_sovereignty under exclusion  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member state ministries, healthcare providers, and critical infrastructure operators reliant on foreign AI systems

## Priority  
**Emergency Access & Continuity Framework** — because `eu_frontier_access_denied` has already disrupted hospitals and ministries, and without immediate action to guarantee fallback operations, the Union risks institutional paralysis that would invalidate all other measures, regardless of their ambition.

## In practice  

We are activating Article 216 TFEU to establish an Emergency Access & Continuity Framework, treating the sudden loss of frontier model access as a material threat to the functioning of the Union. This is not about matching the cutting edge—it is about ensuring that hospitals can triage, courts can schedule, and energy grids can forecast demand when external APIs go dark. The framework mandates that all essential public services using foreign AI systems must deploy locally hosted, reduced-capability fallback models within 18 months. These will be derived from last-accessed openweights at or above 40.0 capability, hardened against misuse, and maintained on sovereign infrastructure funded through repurposed InvestAI reserves. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking will coordinate deployment across EuroHPC nodes, with DG CONNECT fast-tracking procurement for edge-compatible inference stacks.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on the Cyber Resilience Surge, now entering its final year. ENISA teams are embedded in real-time at Germany’s transmission system operator, a French water utility, and the Port of Riga, co-developing AI-driven intrusion detection systems trained on synthetic attack patterns generated by the newly operational (but still pre-evaluation) European AI Safety Observatory. Emergency procurement waivers under DORA are being used to bypass national tender delays, allowing contracts with EU-based firms like NormShield and Darktrace EU to proceed in weeks rather than months. Poland remains conditionally compliant, with full participation tied to completing its grid modernisation milestones under the Just AI Facility—this leverage is proving decisive where persuasion failed.

The Tech Sovereignty Package and InvestAI Gigafactories remain in flight but are being recalibrated in light of the `ai_investment_collapse`. Several private partners have withdrawn, forcing us to restructure the funding mechanism toward greater public anchoring. We are convening an extraordinary ECOFIN Council to discuss converting committed private capital into callable guarantees, preserving site development timelines despite market withdrawal. Meanwhile, the Safety Observatory begins staffing this quarter, though its authority remains untested—no lab has yet submitted weights. We are preparing legal notices under Article 5 of the AI Act to assert jurisdiction over any model serving EU users, knowing enforcement will require a test case we may not win. But we must try: sovereignty without reach is ritual, not power.
