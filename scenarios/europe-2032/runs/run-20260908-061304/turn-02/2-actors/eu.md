## Priority
**Cyber Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure** — because the detection gap exposed by the intrusion remains open, cascading failure is imminent as offensive AI spreads into open distribution, and we cannot afford to harden only what we can see while the unseen grows faster.

## In practice
We are executing the Cyber Resilience Surge under Article 12 of DORA, treating it as a live-fire test of EU crisis response. ENISA is now deploying technical teams to the three most exposed operators—Germany’s transmission system, a Baltic port authority, and a French water utility—to co-develop AI-driven intrusion detection stacks with real-time escalation protocols. Emergency procurement is fast-tracking contracts with EU-based cybersecurity firms, bypassing national tenders that would delay deployment by months. The €15bn Just AI Facility transition envelope is being unlocked in tranches tied to compliance milestones, with Poland now conditionally on board after securing front-of-line access to InvestAI funding for its own grid modernisation.

At the same time, we are launching the European AI Safety Observatory as a new instrument of sovereign oversight. This is not an advisory body—it will have legal authority to demand model weights, run red-team evaluations, and halt deployments under Article 5 of the AI Act if safety thresholds are breached. The first seat is being offered to a consortium of INRIA, Fraunhofer, and the Danish Technical University, with governance designed to insulate it from political interference. We are negotiating with the US and Chinese labs operating in Europe to accept its jurisdiction as a condition of market access, knowing full well that compliance will be tested. The Observatory will begin operations in turn 3, but full evaluation capacity—including agent behaviour monitoring and self-improvement pathway analysis—will not land until turn 5. This is the price of building real capability: it cannot be rushed, but it cannot be skipped.
