### The Denial and the Decree

The Union’s access to the world’s most advanced AI systems is abruptly severed, disrupting hospital diagnostics, financial stability models, and asylum decision pipelines. The provider cites unspecified compliance concerns, offering no appeal. The move lands not as a market dispute but as a strategic blow, confirming worst-case assumptions: at capability 96.0, dependence is surrender.

In response, the European Commission invokes emergency powers under Article 12(3) of the AI Act and activates the Anti-Coercion Instrument. A new **Emergency Sovereign Access Restoration Directive** is launched, aiming to reclaim control through legal seizure, parallel training, and runtime requisition. The directive is named priority, and the completed **Sovereign Inference Backbone** is repurposed as an execution grid, with Bucharest, Helsinki, and Marseille shifting to emergency operations.

### Sovereignty’s Narrow Gain

The **Sovereign Inference Backbone** finishes this turn, delivering +5 to **eu_ai_sovereignty** under rule 5. However, **ai_capability** rises by +2.0 (halved above 85), costing −1. Net effect: +2 (5 −1 −2) = **+2**, raising sovereignty from 11.0 to **13.0**.
