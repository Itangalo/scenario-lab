### Cut off
In late July, hospital IT staff in Lyon, Rotterdam and Warsaw found their calls to the leading American model returning refusals. No notice, no appeal. Administrators confirmed within days: European users tied to clinical and ministerial contracts were suspended. Wards that had built triage prompts and sequencing analysis on that system fell back overnight to European substitutes run on EuroHPC.

The continuity board, made permanent the year before, ordered rationing. Scarce accelerator time went to cancer therapy lines and grid control. Queues lengthened. Health ministers toured the same wards with a new line — access denied, care continues — but local coverage counted waiting days.

### Designs you cannot read
Two other shocks landed while clinics re-platformed. A genome modelling paper circulated a viable design for a human-infecting organism, with methods contested and authors accused of both hype and recklessness. Brussels moved synthesis screening and mandatory incident reporting through health and dual-use channels, and funded rapid detection in sequencing hubs.

At the same time, frontier developers acknowledged their best systems no longer reasoned in readable words. Oversight that depended on inspecting step-by-step traces stopped working. The EU evaluation institute issued written safe-use limits for supervised substitutes, which calmed operators and angered parliamentarians asking why assurance now meant paperwork.

### Shield first, chips later
The Commission did not order new gigafactory builds. Concrete shells in Paris and Warsaw stayed re-phased. Instead money and attention went to the new continuity and bio shield: reallocation to keep degraded care running, screening rules, and conditioning chemicals and maintenance offers to Seoul and Tokyo on bio-screening alignment.

A promised breakthrough in tailored therapies reached ordinary clinics in Milan and Lyon in autumn, but on throttled, substitute compute. Gratitude was muted; many patients experienced it as proof of what full access could have delivered. By December rationing held without collapse, detection posts were staffed, and nothing new had been built.
