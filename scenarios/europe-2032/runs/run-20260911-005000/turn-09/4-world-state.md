### A ward holds, a door closes in Washington
Autumn brought two kinds of news to European hospitals. In Milan and Lyon, oncologists began using a new tailored therapy protocol for resistant blood cancers, built on protein-design work published from a Zurich-Basel collaboration. Remissions that had been anecdotal became routine enough to be counted. Families wept on camera. The same week, administrators had to explain that the American systems behind much of the planning remained available only through rationed European substitutes, with slower turnaround and strict human sign-off.

That contradiction defined the semester. The care continuity system built over the past two years did its job: the emergency allocation board kept scarce high-performance computing time flowing to cancer lines, triage and grid control, screening of gene-synthesis orders held, and detection teams in sequencing hubs stayed staffed. No ward collapsed.

But the ground shifted across the Atlantic. Washington placed its leading AI laboratories under direct federal control — cleared officers inside training operations, weights treated as defence articles, foreign customers now chosen by government decision rather than commercial contract. Brussels instruments designed to pressure companies suddenly pointed at nothing. Commission lawyers admitted privately that market access no longer bought assurance.

The response was to open a different channel. The External Action Service, mandated by health and energy ministers, began seeking a government-to-government memorandum for guaranteed access for essential services, escrowed fallback models, and mutual briefing before major releases, offering cooperation on chemicals, maintenance and screening enforcement in return. Early talks were polite and non-committal; American counterparts listened, promised nothing, and cited national security review.

Meanwhile economists stopped citing productivity studies that showed work changing shape without jobs disappearing. Hospital rostering, logistics planning and back-office coding in several member states were now visibly shedding junior posts, and the older reports read as descriptions of a labour market already gone. Public mood lifted slightly on the medical news, then settled back into anxiety about dependence.
