### Holding the line
January to June 2030 was a semester of rationing made routine. The permanent continuity board under HERA kept EuroHPC substitute time allocated to cancer therapy lines, triage support and grid control. Hospital managers in Lyon, Rotterdam and Warsaw learned to schedule around queues; waiting days stopped growing but did not fall.

The Evaluation Institute's supervised-use limits became the daily operating rule. With frontier reasoning no longer readable, ward staff worked from checklists and human sign-off rather than traces. Operators reported fewer surprises. Parliamentary hearings called it paperwork in place of assurance, and neither side moved.

### Shield work, no new concrete
No new gigafactory order went out. The shells outside Paris and Warsaw stayed mothballed and re-phased, a decision ministers defended as avoiding a second failed build promise. Money stayed with the continuity and bio shield: synthesis screening enforced through dual-use channels, mandatory incident reporting, and funded detection posts in sequencing hubs staffed with ECDC and ENISA teams.

The supply compact concluded this spring gave Brussels steadier chemicals and maintenance channels, and helped hold screening alignment with Seoul and Tokyo. Officials presented it as proof that care continued despite denied access. Health ministers repeated that line on ward visits while local press counted queues.

Tailored therapies reached more clinics in Milan and Lyon, but on throttled compute with slower turnaround. Patients thanked staff and blamed Brussels and Washington in the same sentence. By June the system held without collapse, detection stayed staffed, and nothing new had been built.
