## In practice
We are activating the final phase of the European AI Emergency Response Corps. With the legal framework under Article 122a TFEU now ratified by nine member states, we are finalising deployment protocols and launching the first live-fire exercise in April 2030 — a simulated AI-driven ransomware cascade across health and energy systems under degraded communications. ENISA and ECDC are integrating red-team pipelines and pre-positioning response teams in Brussels, Helsinki, and Warsaw. The Council’s working group has secured liability shielding agreements from half the bloc, and we are pressing the stragglers through the ECOFIN formation, offering co-funding of national cyber reserves in exchange for opt-in. This is not preparedness as policy paper — it is muscle memory being built now, because when the next incident hits, there will be no time to negotiate.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Emergency Labour Transition Compact in direct response to the visible hollowing out of graduate labour markets. The Commission is drafting a regulation under the European Social Fund+ with binding targets: member states must offer 18 months of wage insurance at 70% of previous income for displaced workers under 30, paired with fast-tracked digital upskilling in AI-augmented roles. We are offering to cover 85% of costs for the first two years, but conditioning disbursement on national action plans submitted by Q3. Resistance is expected from fiscally strained states like Italy and Hungary, but we are leveraging cohesion funds and the new middle-power coalition’s political momentum to isolate obstruction. This is not a long-term fix — it is triage, to prevent social fracture while we rebuild the ladder upward.

The Joint Compute Access Pact remains in negotiation, but we are not launching it this turn. With political capital at 14.0 and two large measures already in flight, we are pacing our commitments. Instead, we are using bilateral channels — especially with Japan and Canada — to lock in export control alignment ahead of the formal proposal, ensuring that when we do table it in December, it passes not as ambition, but as inevitability.
