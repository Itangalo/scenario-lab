## In practice
We are finalising the Joint Compute Access Pact under the legal framework of Article 122a TFEU, with political cover from the European Council’s newly convened Security and Technology Task Force. The Commission is drafting the implementing regulation, which will establish a crisis-triggered access protocol: when a member state or partner declares an AI emergency, pre-allocated compute slices across the EU’s sovereign data centres—Gigafactory outputs in Finland, France, and Poland—become automatically accessible under joint oversight. We are offering Italy and Hungary full cost-sharing and priority access in exchange for ratification, directly addressing their fiscal and sovereignty concerns. ENISA will manage the technical registry, and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking will operationalise allocation algorithms by Q1 2031.

Simultaneously, we are completing the final evaluation of the European AI Emergency Response Corps. The live-fire exercise in June exposed critical gaps in cross-border liability and command authority, which we are resolving through bilateral memoranda with national cyber agencies. The Council’s ECOFIN formation has approved a €5bn contingency fund to cover legal indemnities for response teams, removing the last barrier to full operational status. We are now declaring the Corps operational as of July 2030, with standing red teams in Brussels and Helsinki, and direct escalation paths to the Commission’s Crisis Room.

The Emergency Labour Transition Compact remains under intense pressure. We have received national plans from only 14 member states, and the Commission has initiated infringement procedures against Italy and Hungary. To de-escalate, we are offering a revised disbursement model: 90% pre-financing for states that adopt the wage insurance scheme by September, paired with AI redeployment audits for firms receiving public contracts. This is not retreat—it is recalibration to preserve cohesion while delivering support where it is most needed.
