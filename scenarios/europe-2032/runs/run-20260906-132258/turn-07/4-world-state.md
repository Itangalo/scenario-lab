### The Fracture and the Coalition

Global AI capability rises to 82.0 as frontier labs, despite shrinking budgets, achieve a breakthrough in self-reconfiguration—systems now redesign their own architectures with minimal human input. Though the *ai_investment_collapse* curbs scaling, the efficiency gains sustain rapid advancement. Safety progress remains limited; while early signals from internal testing suggest emerging techniques for predicting model behaviour, no full *safety_breakthrough* has yet been confirmed or deployed. Without such a breakthrough, `ai_safety` sees only modest gains, rising to 10.0 through incremental improvements in monitoring and red-teaming capacity.

Open models advance more slowly. Leaked techniques and academic replication contribute to growth, but without a major public release or proven leak from a frontier lab—no `leak_event` or `open_science_breakthrough` resolved this turn—gains are constrained. `openweight_capability` rises to 60.0, reflecting steady progress but not a leap.

The EU concludes two flagship measures: the **InvestAI Gigafactories** fund finalises site selections in France and Poland, anchoring domestic compute, while the **Resilience Surge Protocol** completes cyber-hardening across energy and health sectors. These deliver tangible gains in infrastructure readiness and are reflected in the rise of `resilience` to 45.0.

Meanwhile, US pressure intensifies. The **supply_chain_coercion** event—Washington forcing ASML to restrict mid-tier chip tools—exposes Europe’s dependency. In response, the **middle_power_coalition** formalises, aligning export controls with Japan, Korea, and Canada. This collective stance strengthens the EU’s hand, laying the groundwork for a proposed **Joint Compute Access Pact**, a large category 8 measure aimed at mutual protection against third-party denial. However, due to the constraint of introducing at most one new measure per turn, the Pact remains in negotiation and is not yet formally launched. Diplomatic coordination continues, but full commitment from partners is not assured.

Instead, the EU designates the **European AI Emergency Response Corps** as its current priority, accelerating recruitment and legal integration. Cross-border authority remains patchy, and full operational capacity is still months away, but the prioritisation begins to ease implementation bottlenecks.

Public sentiment inches up to 36.0, reassured by visible action on biosecurity and infrastructure. Political capital, previously at 10.0, recovers modestly to 14.0—completion bonuses from concluded measures offset ongoing portfolio strain, and the coalition’s formation provides a morale boost, though not a transformative one.

### Sovereignty Rebounded

Despite capability growth, `eu_ai_sovereignty` rises to 17.0, buoyed by the coalition’s access-securing effect (+2) and domestic compute landing (+5), partially offset by the rise in global capability (−1). The Union, for the first time in years, acts from a position of coordinated strength—not dominance, but leverage.
