## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: containment and sovereignty. On containment, the European AI Emergency Response Corps moves into its second year with binding cross-border access rights now enshrined under Article 122a TFEU. Recruitment for 500 technical operators is underway, embedded in ENISA and ECDC with pre-authorised intervention powers during declared incidents. The Council’s legal working group finalised the deployment protocol this month, and we have secured opt-in liability shielding from nine member states — enough to activate mutual aid in a crisis. We are integrating red-team pipelines with national cyber agencies and launching the first live-fire exercise in Q1 2030, simulating an AI-generated pandemic response under degraded comms.

On sovereignty, the collapse of private AI investment and the US-led ASML export clampdown have exposed our strategic fragility. But the middle-power coalition — forged in response to supply chain coercion — now offers a lever. We are converting it into a Joint Compute Access Pact, using coordinated export licensing and pooled procurement to lock in mutual access to high-end chips and training capacity. This is not about building faster than the US or China — we cannot — but about ensuring that when they restrict, we do not fall first. We are negotiating with Tokyo, Seoul, and Ottawa to establish shared thresholds for compute denial, and to create a sovereign cloud layer reserved for coalition members, hosted on EU soil and governed by EASA-style independent oversight. The European Commission will table the pact in December, with a target adoption date before mid-2030. This is how we turn chokepoint vulnerability into collective leverage — not by matching power, but by reshaping the game.
