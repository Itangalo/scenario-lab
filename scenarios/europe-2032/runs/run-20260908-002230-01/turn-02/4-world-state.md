### The Summer of Fire and Fixes

A wave of AI-powered ransomware crippled emergency dispatch systems in three EU countries, exploiting zero-day vulnerabilities generated by frontier models. The attack, lasting over 36 hours, exposed deep fragilities in public services and triggered a political scramble. In response, the EU fast-tracked its **Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure**, upgrading co-financing to 90% for critical operators and deploying ENISA rapid-response teams. Early red-teaming revealed additional weaknesses, and full implementation remains months away, with effects only beginning to register.

Simultaneously, France faced fallout from an automated decision scandal: an unvetted open-weight model used in social insurance falsely flagged thousands for fraud, denying benefits. Public trust eroded overnight, with protests demanding accountability. This backlash undercut enthusiasm for open models despite a promising defensive breakthrough — a new swarm-detection system developed jointly by German and Finnish researchers began rolling out, offering real hope against AI-driven attacks.

In response to the crisis, the European Commission drafted the **Secure Open-Source Evaluation Framework (SOEF)**, aiming to mandate third-party audits of high-capability open models. However, legal and technical consultations delayed formal adoption, and the framework did not enter implementation this turn. Widespread developer skepticism and unresolved questions over extraterritorial reach slowed momentum, leaving SOEF in the proposal phase.

Diplomatically, the **middle_power_coalition** yielded results: coordinated pressure on export licences unlocked delayed chip equipment for Finland and Spain, advancing the **InvestAI Gigafactories**. However, U.S. controls remain a bottleneck, and no sovereign compute capacity came online this turn.

Despite these efforts, resilience improved only modestly, as institutions strained under concurrent crises. Political capital dipped due to the cost of multiple large measures and the domestic fallout from the scandal. Public sentiment turned negative, dragged down by visible harm and bureaucratic responses. Open-weight capability saw only incremental gains, limited by the ongoing scrutiny and lack of major new model releases.
