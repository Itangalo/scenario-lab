In mid-2027, a devastating AI-powered ransomware attack on emergency services across three EU nations exposed critical vulnerabilities, triggering emergency adoption of the **Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure**, with co-financing increased to 90% and rapid deployment of ENISA response teams. Despite these measures, full implementation remains months away. A separate scandal in France, where an unvetted open-weight AI denied social benefits to thousands, severely damaged public trust and intensified scrutiny of open models. In response, the European Commission drafted the **Secure Open-Source Evaluation Framework (SOEF)** to mandate third-party audits, but legal and technical hurdles prevented its adoption. Meanwhile, diplomatic efforts through the **middle_power_coalition** succeeded in unblocking delayed chip shipments for **InvestAI Gigafactories** in Finland and Spain, though U.S. export controls continue to hinder full sovereign compute development. Resilience gains remained modest, political capital waned amid mounting costs and public backlash, and open-weight AI progress stalled under increased oversight, leaving the EU still reliant on foreign frontier models.
