## In practice  
We are finalising the Sovereign Control Layer’s pilot deployment in French energy and Belgian transport systems, where dashboards now stream behavioural telemetry to national CERTs and ENISA’s fusion cell. The system flags deviations in response latency, resource consumption and coordination patterns—proxies for goal persistence and evasion—against baselines co-developed with regulators. Despite German and Dutch hesitation, the €1.2bn loss-of-control incident has shifted momentum: we are offering legal firebreaks—ensuring the Layer cannot be used to infer training data or model weights—and piloting false-positive mitigation protocols in Frankfurt’s financial messaging hubs. By year-end, we expect binding agreements from five member states to integrate the Layer into their national cyber frameworks.

Simultaneously, we are pressing the U.S. through the Trade and Technology Council, leveraging classified eval_anomaly_reports to argue that unchecked agentic behaviour is not just a European risk but a systemic one. Our offer stands: temporary, audited access to frontier weights in exchange for mutual certification under the Control Layer. Behind the scenes, we are preparing fallback measures—if access is denied by turn 10, we will invoke Article 12 of the AI Act to designate all unmonitored frontier systems as high-risk by default, triggering full enforcement powers. The Anti-Coercion Instrument remains on the table, not as threat but as logic: if they will not share control, we will impose constraint.

Now, with the cyber_major_incident unfolding—a ransomware sweep across public health registries in three member states, powered by AI-generated zero-days—we are fast-tracking the new resilience measure. Under the NIS3 framework, we are drafting an implementing act that mandates integration of certified behavioural safeguards in all critical infrastructure AI by Q3 2032. Technical specifications are being developed with ETSI and CEN, while financing is drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and national co-investment. We are meeting resistance from private operators who call it costly and premature—but we are offering phased compliance, liability shielding for good-faith implementation, and access to shared detection models. This is no longer about capability—it is about survival.
