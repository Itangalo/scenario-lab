## In practice  
We are activating Article 114 TFEU as the legal base for a new Cyber Shield Directive, to be negotiated in the Internal Market and Consumer Protection Council formation with co-legislation in the European Parliament. The directive will mandate real-time anomaly detection systems using AI-driven monitoring, require all critical operators to participate in a quarterly cross-border red-teaming exercise coordinated by ENISA, and establish a rapid-response reserve of cyber experts funded through the Digital Europe Programme and the recently expanded Civil Protection Mechanism. Member states resisting due to cost — particularly in Central and Eastern Europe — will be offered 85% co-financing from a repurposed portion of the InvestEU budget, conditioned on compliance by Q4 2027.

At the same time, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by fast-tracking environmental assessments for the first two sites — in Finland and Spain — using the newly created Joint Undertaking for Strategic Computing, which pools resources from the Commission, EIB, and national champions like Atos and Siemens. We are negotiating with energy regulators in host countries to guarantee grid priority and long-term power purchase agreements, while leveraging our upstream dominance in lithography through coordinated export licensing with ASML to secure commitments from NVIDIA and AMD to locate next-gen chip assembly in EU zones. Resistance from industry over permitting delays is being met with a trade: accelerated approval in exchange for legal anchoring of control and data sovereignty clauses in all public-private partnerships.

The Tech sovereignty package is being reframed not as a market incentive but as a security imperative. We are convening a summit of national energy ministers and telecom regulators in December to align on grid expansion timelines, and using the adoption_success in public services to build momentum for broader procurement rules that favour EU-controlled AI stacks in health and education. Public sentiment remains ambivalent, but we are amplifying the visible wins — shorter waiting lists, faster permits — to insulate the broader industrial push from backlash. This turn, we act on infrastructure, because if we cannot defend what we have, no sovereignty we build will stand.
