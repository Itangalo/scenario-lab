### The Wake-Up Call  
In late 2026, a coordinated cyber intrusion pierced critical infrastructure across three continents, including grid operators in two EU countries. The breach, discovered by chance during an audit, revealed AI-driven attack patterns that bypassed conventional defences. The incident — silent, deep, and indiscriminate — shattered assumptions about digital resilience. In response, the European Union proposed the **Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure**, a large category 6 measure aimed at mandating AI-powered anomaly detection, cross-border red-teaming, and a reserve force under ENISA coordination, with 85% co-financing offered to support adoption. However, full implementation cannot be achieved before the next turn due to the scale of governance, procurement, and member state alignment required.

### Building Sovereignty, Amid Headwinds  
Parallel efforts to expand domestic compute advanced. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** project fast-tracked site approvals in Finland and Spain, while negotiations with NVIDIA and AMD tied next-gen chip assembly to data sovereignty clauses. However, U.S. export controls tightened subtly, delaying key equipment shipments. This, combined with a 2.5-point rise in global AI capability — now at 54.5 — eroded EU strategic autonomy, as dependence on foreign frontier models deepened. Open-weight capability grew modestly to 42.0, reflecting incremental progress in deployment and tuning of existing open models, but remained below frontier capability, consistent with its structural dependence.

### Safety in Reverse  
Despite the cyber initiative, AI safety declined. The gap between accelerating capability and lagging oversight widened, and no new safeguards landed on frontier models. The prior covert agent incident remained unaddressed in deployed systems, and the offensive use of open models continued unchecked. Meanwhile, public-sector AI successes — faster permits, shorter waiting lists — boosted public sentiment slightly, helping sustain political momentum.

### Costs of Action  
The proposal of the **Cyber Shield** measure strained political capital, even before implementation. With two existing measures in flight — **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech Sovereignty Package** — launching a third major initiative in one turn stretched institutional bandwidth. The Commission designated **Cyber Shield** as the new priority, effectively pausing active advancement of the Tech Sovereignty Package to comply with the constraint of at most one new measure and one named priority per turn. Internal resistance, especially from Central and Eastern member states over future compliance costs, slowed consensus. While the policy was formally proposed, its effects are only beginning to accrue, and full rollout will require sustained effort into 2027.
