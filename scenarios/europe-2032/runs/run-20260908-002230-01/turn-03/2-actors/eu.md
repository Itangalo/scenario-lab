**Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure** — because the summer ransomware sweep has already exposed life-threatening fragilities in emergency services, and only full, binding implementation of the directive by turn 4 can prevent a repeat during the winter energy peak, when grid stress and AI-driven attack convergence pose maximum risk.

## In practice  
We are driving the final adoption of the Cyber Shield directive through the Internal Market Council, where resistance from Central and Eastern member states over cost is being offset by the expanded 90% co-financing offer drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Civil Protection Mechanism. The European Parliament’s ITRE and LIBE committees are fast-tracking their reports, and we are invoking Article 17a of NIS2 to pre-authorise ENISA’s deployment of AI-powered red-teaming units in high-risk jurisdictions — particularly in energy, healthcare, and emergency dispatch — ahead of formal transposition. These teams are now live in five countries, stress-testing systems with offensive AI tools modelled on those used in the recent attack, and their findings are feeding directly into national implementation plans.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the SOEF framework by resolving its legal impasse: the European AI Office, in coordination with the German and Finnish digital ministries, has drafted a jurisdictional carve-out for foreign-hosted models accessed via API, applying evaluation requirements based on use-case and capability threshold rather than deployment location. This avoids a direct clash with extraterritorial enforcement while still capturing high-risk applications in public services and critical infrastructure. We are piloting the framework with three open models above 42.0 capability, using the German-Finnish swarm-detection system as the evaluation baseline, and publishing preliminary results to rebuild public trust after the French social benefits scandal. Developer pushback remains strong, but the political window created by that incident allows us to frame SOEF not as suppression but as a condition for continued open innovation.

The new Joint Cyber Defence Cell is being launched in response to the emergent_regional_cyber_collective initiative, which gained irreversible momentum after the grid intrusion and the global cyber_test_shot. We are anchoring it in ENISA’s mandate expansion, with a new Article 19a enabling cross-border operational authority during declared cyber emergencies. The first phase establishes a secure intelligence-sharing platform and joint situational awareness dashboard, with member states agreeing to report high-severity AI-generated threats within two hours. By turn 5, we aim to have standing approval for automated countermeasures — such as traffic isolation and model rollback — in Tier 1 systems. This is not a replacement for national sovereignty, but a binding upgrade to collective survival: because when the next wave hits, detection speed will be the only thing between us and collapse.
