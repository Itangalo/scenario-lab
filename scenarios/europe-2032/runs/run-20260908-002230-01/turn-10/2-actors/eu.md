## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent uncontrolled agentic AI from operating in or against EU critical infrastructure  
- Grounds: The original commitment to “secure sovereign control over AI systems operating in critical domains” has failed in practice: the Sovereign Control Layer is operationally fragmented, blind to U.S. models, and lacks enforcement teeth. The ransomware attack proves that capability without containment is vulnerability. We are shifting from attempting to control all frontier systems — which requires access and unity we do not have — to a narrower, more defensible objective: ensuring that no agentic AI, foreign or domestic, can act autonomously within or against essential services. This is still about self-determination, but grounded in what we can actually stop, not what we wish we could govern.

## New measure  
**Critical Infrastructure AI Firebreak Protocol**  
We are establishing a hard separation between AI systems with autonomous planning capability and operational technology in essential services, enforced through air-gapped control layers and human-in-the-loop validation for all high-consequence actions.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect: resilience +12 (by preventing direct AI control of physical systems), ai_safety +6 (by reducing attack surface for agentic misuse), eu_political_capital -3 (due to pushback from industry on operational friction)`  
`Applies to: Operators of critical infrastructure with AI-driven control systems`

## Priority  
**Mandatory Integration of Behavioural Safeguards in Critical Infrastructure AI** — because it is the only measure actively building operational defences against the very class of threat that just crippled health registries, and delaying it risks cascading failure across sectors; we are now past the point where diplomacy or sovereignty debates can substitute for on-the-ground protection.

## In practice  
We are treating the collapse of ai_safety to 8.0 as a system-wide emergency. The Emergency Access measure has expired without result — the U.S. dismissed our final proposal at the TTC, citing national security. That path is closed. We are now pivoting fully to containment: the Mandatory Integration of Behavioural Safeguards is moving from proposal to implementation. Under NIS3, we are finalising the implementing act with the Cyber Resilience Board, setting Q3 2031 for technical standards and Q1 2032 for phased compliance. ETSI is fast-tracking the certification framework for anomaly detection agents, while ENISA is standing up a joint monitoring cell with national CERTs to receive telemetry from safeguarded systems. We are offering liability protection for operators who adopt early, and co-funding retrofits through the Digital Europe Programme — but we are also preparing infringement procedures for those who delay.

The new Firebreak Protocol goes further: it targets the core risk exposed by the ransomware attack — AI systems with planning capability directly interfacing with operational technology. Drawing on nuclear and aerospace safety models, we are drafting a regulation under the revised AI Act that will require air-gapped control layers and human validation for any AI-initiated action that could disable, reconfigure or access protected data in essential services. This applies retroactively to all systems above capability threshold 60.0. The German energy consortium E.ON and the Port of Rotterdam Authority are already engaging, but resistance is mounting from financial firms and logistics operators who argue it will degrade performance. We are holding firm: when systems act without understanding, the line must be drawn at consequence, not convenience.
