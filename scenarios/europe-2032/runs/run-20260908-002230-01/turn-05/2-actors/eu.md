This new formulation reflects that the objective is not merely to possess AI, but to possess it under conditions we can trust, sustain, and defend.

## New measure
**Sovereign Evaluation and Control Environment**  
Establish a fully EU-governed, physically isolated evaluation and monitoring infrastructure for high-capability AI systems, including sovereign access to frontier models under strict auditing conditions, to enable real-time behavioural analysis, anomaly detection, and safety enforcement.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`

## Priority
**Sovereign Evaluation and Control Environment** — because the behavioural anomalies now evident in frontier models, combined with the EU’s total lack of access to inspect or test them, create a blind spot that no directive or industrial programme can close; we must build sovereign technical capacity to see and understand what we depend on, or we will be governing in darkness.

## In practice
We are launching the Sovereign Evaluation and Control Environment from the European AI Office in coordination with EuroHPC and the EDA, using secure compute nodes hosted within the InvestAI Gigafactories’ sovereign zones. The legal basis will be Article 114 TFEU, extended by a Council decision under the emergency innovation clause, with funding drawn from a reallocation of 15% of the Tech Sovereignty Package’s private investment stream — not as a new subsidy, but as a condition of access to accelerated permitting. This environment will host two core functions: first, a closed, air-gapped evaluation suite where EU-certified auditors can probe high-capability models — both sovereign and foreign — for emergent behaviours, alignment drift, and security vulnerabilities; second, a live monitoring layer integrated with the JCDC’s threat feeds to detect AI-enabled anomalies in real time across critical infrastructure.

We are treating the completion of SOEF and JCDC not as endpoints but as foundations: SOEF’s evaluation protocols will be embedded into the new control environment as the baseline for open-weight scrutiny, while JCDC’s intelligence-sharing architecture will feed into the anomaly detection layer. The German and Polish governments remain resistant to data-sharing protocols, citing national sovereignty — we are offering them co-location rights and veto power over audit scope in exchange for participation. Meanwhile, we are engaging with US labs under the new bilateral science and technology framework, proposing a mutual access arrangement: EU models evaluated in Washington, US frontier models audited here, under reciprocal safeguards. If they refuse, we will proceed unilaterally, using mirrored open-weight models and red-teamed proxies to build our own understanding — because we cannot afford to wait for permission to see what could break us.
