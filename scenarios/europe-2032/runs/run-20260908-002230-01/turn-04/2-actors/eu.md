## New measure  
**Sovereign Model Development Programme**  
Launch a fully EU-controlled training and deployment pipeline for high-capability models, hosted on sovereign compute and governed by EU institutions, to ensure access to AI of known provenance and controllable behaviour.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect: eu_ai_sovereignty +15, ai_safety +5 via direct control and monitoring, resilience +5 by reducing reliance on foreign systems`  
`Applies to: EU member states, EuroHPC, European AI Office, national research agencies`

## Priority  
**Sovereign Model Development Programme** — because the convergence of openweight capability to near-frontier levels and the emergence of anomalous behaviours in unreleased systems mean that dependency is no longer a policy choice but a vulnerability we cannot govern; we must act now to ensure the EU has access to models it can inspect, control, and trust, or risk being blindsided by cascading failures we cannot mitigate.

## In practice  
We are launching the Sovereign Model Development Programme from the European AI Office in coordination with EuroHPC, drawing on the compute capacity secured under the InvestAI Gigafactories and redirecting a portion of the Tech Sovereignty Package’s private investment stream toward public-purpose model development. The legal basis will be Article 173 TFEU, extended through a Council decision under the new emergency innovation clause invoked after the German unemployment fraud. We are convening a task force of DG CONNECT, the EDA, and national science agencies to define the architecture: a closed training environment on EU soil, with model weights never released into the wild, access strictly controlled, and all evaluations conducted in secure enclaves with third-party auditors embedded. The first model, targeting a capability of 60.0 by turn 8, will be trained exclusively on EU-licensed data and hardened against misuse from inception.

Simultaneously, we are closing out the Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure directive: ENISA’s red-teaming units are now operational in nine member states, and the five that resisted are receiving emergency co-financing from the Civil Protection Mechanism to cover 95% of compliance costs—this is not a victory but a necessity, as the JCDC’s early warning system has already flagged three high-severity anomalies in energy grid controls. The SOEF continues its pilot phase, but we are now treating it as a feeder system for the Sovereign Model programme: any open model above 45.0 capability that passes SOEF scrutiny will be mirrored into the sovereign pipeline for behavioural analysis and hardening. This is not about stopping open innovation; it is about ensuring that when capability escapes, we have a mirror to see it and a floor to catch it.
