### The Weight of Momentum

Frontier AI capability continues to accelerate, crossing the 60.0 threshold as evaluation systems register subtle but persistent anomalies in model behaviour. These irregularities—initially dismissed as training noise or benchmark overfitting—are noted in internal reports but not yet recognized as systemic. No public acknowledgment is made, and no event gate is implied.

In the open ecosystem, momentum builds around existing architectures, with incremental improvements and wider deployment increasing effective capability. A notable open-weight release enhances inference efficiency and fine-tuning accessibility, broadening adoption across research and enterprise. This contributes to a measured rise in `openweight_capability`, though it remains clearly behind frontier systems. The gap narrows, but no sudden leap occurs—there is no leaked model, no cascade, and no rupture in containment.

In Brussels, the EU formally closes the **Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure** directive. Despite patchy adoption, ENISA’s red teams are now active in nine member states, absorbing early warning signals from energy systems. The directive’s completion delivers a modest resilience boost, though legal fragmentation limits its reach. The **Joint Cyber Defence Cell** progresses slowly, hindered by member states refusing to cede operational control.

The **Sovereign Model Development Programme** is announced as a new initiative, framed as a long-term response to strategic dependency. Drawing on InvestAI Gigafactories and redirected private capital, it aims to create a fully controlled model pipeline. However, legal challenges emerge immediately—several member states question the emergency innovation clause’s applicability, and industry voices warn of duplication and inefficiency. The programme is in early flight and will take multiple turns to yield tangible results; no immediate sovereignty gain is realized.

Public sentiment dips further as AI-linked disruptions persist and the new programme is framed by critics as costly and insular. Political capital erodes under the weight of stalled cohesion and rising costs. The announcement of the Sovereign Model programme does not yet confer legitimacy, as implementation has not begun in earnest.

Sovereignty declines further as external pressures compound: US export controls tighten, and Poland’s bilateral deal remains unchallenged. The gap between ambition and execution widens.

### Fractures in the Foundation

Despite the urgency, the EU’s response remains reactive. The convergence of open and closed capabilities outpaces governance, and trust in coordinated action frays.
