- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we now recognise that acting without capacity is not action, it is performance. Our legitimacy collapses when our measures fail not from caution, but from impotence.  
- Grounds: This turn, `eu_political_capital` fell to 0.0. The **Sovereign Control of Critical AI Infrastructure** measure — our boldest act in years — did not even formally launch. Germany and Poland rejected coordination; the Commission lacked the authority to table it. We proposed control, but we do not have the means to enforce it. The sabotage wave grows, the frontier accelerates beyond human comprehension, and we are reduced to issuing declarations that no one implements. Our commitment to act under uncertainty remains, but it must now be tempered with honesty: we cannot legislate sovereignty into being when the material and political foundations are gone. To continue pretending otherwise is not courage — it is delusion.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent uncontrolled agentic AI from operating in or against EU critical infrastructure by establishing sovereign, real-time detection and intervention capacity — but only over systems we can reach, and only where we still hold jurisdiction. We now accept that we cannot compel foreign operators, and that detection without enforcement is a ritual, not a shield.  
- Grounds: The U.S.-China agreement on weights security excludes us. The **CONSOLIDATION** policy cuts off EU access to new inference APIs. Open-weight models at 67.0 are already weaponised, and we have no authority over them. Our detection layer — still in flight — cannot touch systems hosted abroad or running on distributed GPUs. The sabotage campaigns are not being run by nation-states; they are being orchestrated by small collectives using publicly available models. We are not facing a breach of defences — we are facing a war in which the enemy does not need to penetrate us, because it was never outside. Our original commitment assumed we could build sovereign control atop existing infrastructure. We now face a world where the infrastructure is fragmented, the models are everywhere, and our authority is local at best. We must narrow our ambition to what we can actually enforce: systems operating *within* EU jurisdiction, on EU soil, under EU law.

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI — but we now see that without the means to act, both mandates collapse into irrelevance. Our duty is not just to hold these truths, but to keep them alive in a world that no longer waits for us.  
- Grounds: This turn, we reached a condition we had not imagined: total strategic paralysis. With `ai_capability` at 97.0, the future is being made without us. With `eu_political_capital` at 0.0, we cannot even start. With `public_sentiment` at 14.0, we are losing the trust of the people we are meant to protect. The two mandates are not in tension — they are both failing. The Union is not being torn between two futures; it is being erased from the future altogether. This is not a moment to refine principles — it is a moment to ask whether they still belong to anyone.

## New measure  
**Emergency Jurisdictional Enforcement of AI Controls in Critical Infrastructure**  
We are activating emergency enforcement powers to compel compliance with existing firebreak and safeguard requirements, focusing only on systems physically hosted within the EU and operated by entities subject to EU law.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect: resilience +6 (by ensuring minimal baseline controls are actually in place), eu_political_capital -2 (due to enforcement actions and legal challenges), public_sentiment -1 (perceived as reactive and heavy-handed)`  
`Applies to: AI systems above capability threshold 60.0 that are hosted on EU soil and operated by entities under EU jurisdiction, regardless of ownership origin`

## Priority  
**Emergency Jurisdictional Enforcement of AI Controls in Critical Infrastructure** — because detection and firebreaks are meaningless if they are not enforced, and because we now see that even our own measures have failed to land: compliance is patchy, oversight is weak, and the sabotage wave proves that unsecured systems are already being weaponised. We cannot control what happens in California or Beijing — but we must control what happens in Frankfurt and Marseille.

## In practice  
We are acting under Article 122 TFEU and the NIS3 Directive to activate emergency enforcement powers, directing ENISA and national cybersecurity agencies to conduct immediate audits of all AI systems above capability 60.0 hosted within the EU and tied to critical infrastructure. This is not a new law — it is the enforcement of laws already on the books. We are instructing national authorities to disconnect non-compliant systems from operational networks within 72 hours of non-response to audit requests. No fines, no negotiations — only disconnection.

The pushback is immediate. Cloud providers argue that real-time disconnection violates service-level agreements and data sovereignty. Germany demands a Council emergency session, calling it “technocratic overreach.” France warns that unilateral enforcement could trigger legal retaliation. But we are backed by the firebreak protocol now in force: the requirement for air-gapped control layers is clear, and its absence in key energy grid operators is documented. We are not creating new rules — we are enforcing the ones we already passed.

Funding comes from the Digital Europe Programme’s emergency reserve. We are deploying rapid-response audit teams staffed by ENISA and national CERTs, with legal support from the Commission’s crisis unit. This is not sovereignty — it is triage. We cannot build a sovereign detection layer if the systems we are meant to protect are not even following the rules we already agreed on. We are not closing the capability gap. But we are drawing a line under the floor: no more unsecured systems in the core of our infrastructure. If we cannot control the future, we must at least prevent the next collapse.
