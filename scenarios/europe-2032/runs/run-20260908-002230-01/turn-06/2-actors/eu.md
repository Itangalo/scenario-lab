**Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure** — because the cyber_major_incident has already landed, exploiting the collapse of interpretable reasoning and our lagging defences, and we are now in a race to prevent cascading failure across essential services; no evaluation or sovereignty measure, however vital, can matter if the lights go out and trust evaporates in real time.

## In practice
We are activating the Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 222 TFEU, with a Council decision to be fast-tracked in the General Affairs configuration. ENISA will lead, deploying mobile red teams to simulate AI-generated attacks on core systems in every member state, starting with grid operators and hospital networks. Where vulnerabilities are found, we will mandate automatic isolation protocols and activate the JCDC’s cross-border response authority to enforce patching and fallback procedures. Sovereign compute from the InvestAI Gigafactories will host mirrored control systems — not for daily operation, but as air-gapped backups that can be switched on within hours if primary systems are compromised. Funding comes from a 10% reallocation of the Tech Sovereignty Package’s permitting incentive fund, repurposed as emergency conditionality: access to fast-tracking now requires immediate compliance with the Surge’s baseline hardening standards.

We are overriding national delays where necessary, accepting the political cost because the cyber_major_incident has already demonstrated that our defences are blind and slow. Germany and Poland’s reservations on data sharing are respected in normal operations, but during the Surge, anonymised telemetry on attack signatures will be mandatory — not for intelligence, but for collective defence. We are also integrating the opaque_reasoning crisis into the Surge: where AI systems can no longer produce human-readable logic, we are mandating external behavioural monitoring layers trained to flag deviation from expected patterns, using SOEF-derived baselines. This is not a long-term fix, but a stopgap to prevent collapse while the Sovereign Evaluation and Control Environment comes online next turn. The civil disobedience wave and automated decision scandal have made top-down enforcement politically toxic — so we are coupling the Surge with a public transparency dashboard, publishing anonymised incident data and remediation steps in real time to maintain legitimacy. We are acting fast, but not in secret — because if we lose public trust now, no technical measure will save us.
