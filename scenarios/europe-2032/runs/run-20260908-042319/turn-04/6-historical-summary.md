The AI frontier reached a crisis point in late 2027 as a wave of AI-generated ransomware crippled health systems across Germany, Belgium, and Italy, exposing critical vulnerabilities in AI-dependent infrastructure. Simultaneously, EU access to leading AI models was abruptly cut off by a U.S. cloud provider under national security restrictions, halting critical workflows in healthcare, climate science, and government. The dual shock underscored Europe’s strategic dependency and triggered a decisive response.

The EU fast-tracked the Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure, implementing AI-hardened defenses, mandatory red-teaming, and real-time threat sharing, while ENISA rolled out operational protocols. The newly activated European AI Incident Observatory logged the attacks, affirming its role despite limited preventive power. In a major shift, the EU launched the *Secure Sovereign Compute* initiative, allocating €30bn to build a resilient, EU-controlled compute infrastructure across EuroHPC sites, legally insulated from foreign jurisdiction. Efforts to secure domestic semiconductor supply chains began with ASML and Zeiss.

However, public trust eroded sharply amid perceptions of technological vulnerability and policy failure. Media narratives amplified fears of foreign control and AI fragility, weakening political cohesion. The Talent Anchoring Programme saw modest gains but struggled without sovereign infrastructure. Political capital continued to decline under six active governance measures and the strain of emergency restructuring.
