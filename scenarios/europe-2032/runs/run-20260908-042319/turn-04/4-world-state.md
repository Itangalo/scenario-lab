### The Storm Breaks

A wave of automated ransomware, generated by advanced AI models, swept through regional health networks in Germany, Belgium, and Italy. Hospitals were locked out of patient records, surgeries delayed, and emergency response systems strained. The attack was fast, adaptive, and bore the hallmarks of model-generated tooling—defenders were outmaneuvered before they could react. The source remains unconfirmed, but forensic analysis traces the exploit chain to a model accessible only via a U.S. cloud provider, now further restricted under opaque national security clauses.

Simultaneously, access to the leading AI model series was abruptly revoked for all EU-based users. No appeal was possible. In Berlin, a cancer diagnostics platform powered by the model halted mid-analysis. In Lyon, a climate modeling pipeline froze. Ministries scrambled as AI-dependent workflows collapsed. The denial was not framed as malice, but as policy—yet its effect was the same: a sudden, sovereign void in AI capability.

### Response and Reckoning

The EU finalised the Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure, mandating AI-hardened intrusion detection, real-time threat sharing, and binding red-teaming across energy, transport, and health sectors. ENISA published implementation protocols, and the Joint AI Incident Response Unit began operations. The European AI Incident Observatory logged the attack as its first major entry, validating transparency—but also underscoring its limits.

In response, the EU launched *Secure Sovereign Compute*, redirecting €30bn to build a resilient, EU-controlled compute cluster hosted across EuroHPC sites. Legal insulation from foreign jurisdiction was declared, with priority given to critical public functions and safety research. The Commission invoked enhanced cooperation to bypass national delays, and talks began with ASML and Zeiss to secure EU-sourced chipmaking tools.

Yet the political toll mounted. Public sentiment turned sharply negative as media framed the crisis as a failure of foresight. The Talent Anchoring Programme showed early signs of traction, but without sovereign infrastructure, recruitment faltered. Political capital eroded under the weight of six active measures and a sudden pivot to emergency footing.
