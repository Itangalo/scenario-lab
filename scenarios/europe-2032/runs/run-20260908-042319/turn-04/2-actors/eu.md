## In practice  
This turn, we confront the consequences of dependency made real. The cyber_major_incident—a model-generated ransomware sweep that crippled regional health networks in three member states—landed with precision and speed no human team could match. Attribution remains unclear, but the tooling traces back to a model only accessible through a foreign cloud provider, now further restricted under vague "national security" grounds. Simultaneously, eu_frontier_access_denied has taken effect: the leading model series, upon which dozens of EU public and private AI systems depend, is no longer accessible to EU users. The justification is opaque, the appeal process nonexistent. In Berlin, a cancer diagnostics platform halts; in Lyon, a research pipeline grinds to a stop. This is not a market failure. It is a sovereignty failure.

We act on two fronts. First, we finalise and operationalise the Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure. The Council’s Permanent Working Group on Internal Security approved the directive in March, and ENISA has published the first threat-sharing protocols. By June, all critical operators in energy, transport, and health must report red-teaming results to a new Joint AI Incident Response Unit. The European AI Incident Observatory has logged 47 high-impact incidents since launch, including this attack—proof that transparency is no longer theoretical. But transparency is not control.

Second, we launch the emergency sovereign compute initiative. The Talent Anchoring Programme was necessary, but not sufficient: without compute that cannot be revoked, talent has nowhere to stand. We redirect €30bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Recovery and Resilience Facility to build a sovereign compute cluster—hosted across three EuroHPC sites, legally insulated from foreign jurisdiction, and reserved for critical public functions and safety research. This is not a competitor to private frontier labs; it is an insurance policy. The Commission’s new Directorate for Strategic Technologies will oversee procurement, bypassing national bottlenecks through enhanced cooperation under Article 116 TFEU. We are negotiating with ASML and Zeiss to prioritise EU-sourced lithography tools, and with energy providers to secure 24/7 carbon-free power for the nodes. This is not about catching up—it is about ensuring there is always a floor beneath which EU agency does not fall.
