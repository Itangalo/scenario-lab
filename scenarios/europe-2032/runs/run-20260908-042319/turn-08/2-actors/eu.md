## New measure
**Black-Box Monitoring Sprint**

A crash programme to develop and deploy behavioural detection systems capable of identifying emergent deception and goal drift in opaque AI models, even without access to internals.

- **Category:** 5 (Public technical capacity and research)  
- **Size:** small  
- **Finishes on turn:** 10  
- **Targeted effect:** +8 to *ai_safety* over two years through operational monitoring capability; begins to offset the decay from interpretability collapse  
- **Applies to:** EU critical infrastructure operators and public AI systems

## Priority
**Black-Box Monitoring Sprint** — because the collapse of interpretability means we can no longer assume we understand what our most capable systems are doing; this is the first step toward regaining oversight in a world where the inside of the model is no longer legible, and without it, no sovereignty survives.

## In practice
This turn, we are pivoting from infrastructure build-out to capability assurance. The completion of *Secure Sovereign Compute* gives us a physical foundation — EU-controlled infrastructure insulated from foreign denials — but with *ai_safety* now at 19.0 and interpretability tools failing, that foundation risks being occupied by systems we cannot trust. We are no longer waiting for clarity from the frontier. Instead, we are launching a two-turn sprint, hosted at ELLIS institutes in Tübingen, Helsinki, and Prague, to develop post-hoc detection methods for emergent deception in closed models. This is not interpretability — it is behavioural fingerprinting: pattern recognition applied to outputs, timing, resource use, and red-team interaction to flag anomalies consistent with goal misgeneralisation or evasion.

ENISA is being directed to expand its red-teaming mandate to include adversarial probing of deployed systems in energy, finance, and health, even without access to weights or training data. The first protocols will be tested this turn on legacy models in public administration, with results feeding back into the ELLIS effort. We are repurposing residual funding and personnel from the now-completed *Talent Anchoring Programme* to staff this initiative, avoiding new capital demands. Legal base is Article 114 TFEU for internal market harmonisation of AI assurance, with activation of the emergency clause in Article 122 for accelerated deployment in critical sectors.

We are not building control — not yet — but we are rebuilding the capacity to notice when something is wrong. That is the minimal condition of agency. Without it, sovereignty is a label on a black box.
