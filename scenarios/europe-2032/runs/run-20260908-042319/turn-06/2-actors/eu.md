## In practice
This turn, we are completing the legal and physical foundation of the Secure Sovereign Compute initiative. The Commission’s Directorate for Strategic Technologies has finalised jurisdictional insulation protocols under Article 346 TFEU, ensuring that the compute clusters at EuroHPC sites in Finland, France, and Italy are legally beyond the reach of foreign export controls. Procurement of 10,000 next-generation GPUs has been secured through binding agreements with ASML and Zeiss, prioritising EU-sourced lithography and packaging, while 24/7 carbon-free power contracts are being negotiated with Ørsted and Enel to meet sustainability benchmarks. The infrastructure will be operated by a newly constituted EU AI Compute Agency, staffed from the Talent Anchoring Programme’s first cohort, which is now finalising onboarding despite ongoing constraints in frontier access.

Meanwhile, the Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure has achieved full operational status. ENISA’s Joint AI Incident Response Unit is now receiving real-time telemetry from 80% of designated operators in energy, health, and transport, with red-teaming reports revealing new zero-day candidates weekly. The European AI Incident Observatory has published its first public dashboard, logging 54 incidents—including the recent ransomware wave—establishing transparency as a functioning norm even as prevention lags. The U.S. election outcome introduces uncertainty: while signals point to conditional restoration of model access, we are not treating this as a return to normalcy. Dependency is not only in the wires—it is in the expectation that access will hold. We are finishing sovereign compute not to isolate, but to ensure that when the next denial comes, the EU does not fall silent.
