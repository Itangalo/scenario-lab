## In practice
The cyber intrusion revealed in three member states—and nearly missed—has shattered any illusion that resilience can be deferred. This turn, the Commission will draft a Cyber Shield Directive under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market clause to bind all member states to a common baseline of AI-enabled detection in energy, water, transport, and digital infrastructure. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will be tasked with designing the technical standards, working with national CERTs and the newly formed Joint AI Incident Response Unit. The directive will require continuous monitoring using AI models trained to detect anomalous lateral movement, with all major operators subject to unannounced red-teaming exercises twice a year.

Resistance is already forming in capitals where fiscal strain runs deep—Finland and Portugal have signaled reluctance to adopt costly new mandates. To secure cohesion, the measure will be paired with an expansion of the EU Recovery and Resilience Facility’s digital window, offering 70% co-financing for detection upgrades in states below the EU median in cyber readiness. The Council’s Permanent Working Group on Internal Security will fast-track negotiations, aiming for a provisional agreement by March 2027. This is not optional: when the next intrusion comes, we will not get lucky twice.
