### The Weight of Crisis

The EU’s critical infrastructure reeled from a sophisticated, AI-powered ransomware campaign that disabled hospital systems across three major member states. Defenders were overwhelmed by the speed and adaptability of the attack, which exploited zero-day vulnerabilities generated and deployed autonomously. The European AI Incident Observatory confirmed the incident’s severity, marking it as the most damaging to date. Public trust eroded further as headlines framed the crisis as a failure of both technology and governance.

In response, the Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure went fully live, mandating hardened detection, red-teaming, and real-time threat sharing. ENISA activated its Joint AI Incident Response Unit, and initial compliance reached 80% in high-risk sectors. Yet the damage had already landed—resilience absorbed some impact, but not the political fallout.

Simultaneously, access to leading AI models was abruptly severed for EU institutions and research bodies, halting diagnostics, climate modeling, and public AI pilots. The denial, justified under foreign national security grounds, exposed the Union’s strategic fragility. In a decisive move, the EU launched the Secure Sovereign Compute initiative, redirecting €30bn to build jurisdictionally insulated infrastructure across EuroHPC sites. Legal protocols under Article 346 TFEU were fast-tracked, and early supply deals with ASML and Zeiss signaled a push for hardware autonomy.

The U.S. election concluded with a decisive victory for the candidate favoring transatlantic technological alignment. Early signals suggest structured access to frontier models may return for vetted EU entities, tied to shared standards and export controls. This shift offers immediate relief but risks weakening long-term investment in sovereign capacity.

Public sentiment declined sharply, driven by visible dependency, job disruptions in AI-dependent sectors, and a growing narrative of technological humiliation. The Talent Anchoring Programme made modest progress, but recruitment remained hobbled by uncertain compute access.
