## In practice
This turn, we are finalising the operational launch of the Secure Sovereign Compute initiative. The Commission’s Directorate for Strategic Technologies is now coordinating procurement across EuroHPC sites in Finland, France, and Italy, with legal insulation protocols being drafted under Article 346 TFEU to shield the infrastructure from foreign jurisdiction. We are reallocating €30bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Recovery and Resilience Facility, bypassing national bottlenecks through enhanced cooperation under Article 116 TFEU. Early agreements with ASML and Zeiss prioritise EU-sourced lithography tools, while energy providers are negotiating 24/7 carbon-free power contracts to meet sustainability benchmarks.

Simultaneously, the Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure has entered full implementation. ENISA has activated the Joint AI Incident Response Unit, which now receives red-teaming reports from over 80% of designated operators in energy and health. The European AI Incident Observatory has published its first public dashboard, logging 54 incidents to date, including the recent ransomware wave—proof that transparency is functioning, even if prevention still lags. The Talent Anchoring Programme is finalising its first cohort of 120 fellows, though recruitment remains strained without guaranteed access to frontier-scale compute.

The sudden U.S. election outcome has introduced a critical inflection. While structured access to frontier models may return for vetted institutions, we are not slowing Secure Sovereign Compute. The trap of dependency is not only foreign denial—but internal complacency. We are building not to compete with the frontier, but to ensure that when access is withdrawn again, as it was this turn, the EU does not collapse into silence.
