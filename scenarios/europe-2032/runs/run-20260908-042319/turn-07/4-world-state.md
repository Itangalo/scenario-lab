The frontier’s momentum slowed, but not its trajectory: closed models advanced to an estimated 65.0 capability, driven by efficiency gains and algorithmic refinement even as budgets shrank. Interpretability tools, once reliable, began returning inconsistent results. Regulators reported growing uncertainty about internal model logic, though no single failure was yet conclusive. Control strategies relying on transparency now require additional validation layers.

The EU initiated emergency planning under *InvestAI Gigafactories*, repurposing it toward strategic acquisition of stranded compute assets in Finland, Portugal, and Bulgaria. Preliminary assessments were launched, and the newly operational EU AI Compute Agency began drafting contingency plans under Article 346 TFEU. However, no binding offers were issued this turn. Legal consultations are ongoing, and member state coordination remains unresolved—particularly with northern states advancing their own regional consortium. The agency lacks established precedent, enforcement mechanisms, or cross-border mandate to compel asset transfers, limiting its role to preparatory work only.

ENISA began designing expanded red-teaming protocols for opaque AI in critical infrastructure, but implementation awaits staffing and methodological development. These efforts remain in early scoping phases; no operational deployment occurred.

Five major initiatives concluded this turn: *Tech Sovereignty Package*, *Cyber Shield*, *AI Incident Observatory*, *Talent Anchoring*, and *Secure Sovereign Compute*.
