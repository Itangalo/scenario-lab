## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure** — because the intrusion across three continents proved AI-driven threats are already evading human-led defences, and without hardened critical infrastructure, no amount of talent or compute will preserve European agency when systems fail under load.

## In practice
The cyber intrusion that slipped through human monitoring for weeks has not been forgotten. This turn, we are finalising the draft of the Cyber Shield Directive under Article 114 TFEU, with ENISA leading the technical design in close coordination with national CERTs and the Joint AI Incident Response Unit. The directive will mandate AI-powered intrusion detection systems trained to flag lateral movement and zero-day exploits, real-time threat sharing across operators, and unannounced red-teaming twice a year. The Council’s Permanent Working Group on Internal Security is fast-tracking negotiations, aiming for provisional agreement by March 2027. To secure buy-in from fiscally strained members, we are pairing the directive with expanded co-financing through the Recovery and Resilience Facility—70% coverage for detection upgrades in states below the EU median in cyber readiness.

At the same time, we are launching the European AI Incident Observatory, a central registry under the authority of the European AI Office, to collect and publish verified reports of serious incidents involving AI systems—especially those affecting critical infrastructure, democratic processes, or public safety. This fulfils a growing demand for transparency after the annulled election and the recent breach, and it begins to close the loop between detection and learning. Reporting will be mandatory for all operators of high-impact AI systems under the AI Act, with anonymised data made available to researchers and the public. The observatory will go live in turn 3, with full reporting rules in force by turn 4, aligning with the Cyber Shield’s implementation. This is not symbolic: we are building the feedback infrastructure that turns incidents into systemic improvement.
