## Priority  
**Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure** — because the narrowing gap between open and closed capability means that even a single breach in energy, transport or health could cascade into strategic paralysis, and without binding, AI-hardened defences now, no amount of talent or compute will matter when the lights go out.

## In practice  
This turn, we finalise negotiations on the Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure, breaking the deadlock with Hungary and Slovakia by offering a revised legal basis under Article 4(2) TEU — national security remains under member state control, but the directive now frames cyber resilience as a cross-border operational necessity, not a sovereignty transfer. ENISA, in coordination with the Joint AI Incident Response Unit, has completed the technical annexes: all critical operators must deploy AI-driven intrusion detection systems trained on EU-generated threat data, conduct unannounced red-teaming twice a year, and feed anonymised attack signatures into a shared threat pool. Co-financing remains at 70% for states below the median in cyber readiness, drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme. The Council’s Permanent Working Group on Internal Security will vote in November; we expect adoption by December, with full implementation by Q2 2028.

Meanwhile, the European AI Incident Observatory goes live as planned — a milestone in transparency. The European AI Office opens the registry, with mandatory reporting now enforceable under the AI Act for all high-impact systems. The first entries include the disinformation campaign that triggered the annulled election and a near-miss in automated grid management in the Baltics. Data is anonymised and published quarterly; researchers can apply for vetted access. This closes a critical loop: incidents now feed back into preparedness, and the public sees action, not just promises.

But the quiet bleed of talent cannot wait. With mid-tier labs folding and frontier access concentrated in the US and UK, we launch the EU Talent Anchoring Programme: a €12bn initiative over three years, combining €75,000 fellowships for top AI researchers, dedicated compute allocations on EuroHPC systems for safety-critical work, fast-track Schengen visas for non-EU experts, and matching grants for universities that create AI safety chairs. The programme will be administered by a new Talent Directorate within the European Commission, with oversight from a council of national science academies. This is not just recruitment — it is a signal that Europe is not a spectator in the race, and that its values can anchor the next generation of builders.
