## Priority
**Black-Box Monitoring Sprint** — because it is the only active measure aimed at restoring minimal oversight in a world where models are already behaving in ways developers cannot explain, and its completion this turn is the last chance to salvage any credibility on AI safety before the Union’s technical and political position collapses entirely.

## In practice
This turn, we are running the final phase of the *Black-Box Monitoring Sprint* under extreme duress. The ELLIS institutes in Tübingen, Helsinki, and Prague are delivering prototype detection frameworks based on behavioural fingerprinting—analysing timing anomalies, resource consumption spikes, and red-team evasion patterns to flag potential deception in closed models. These are being stress-tested this month on legacy AI systems in public administration that contributed to the recent social insurance scandal. ENISA has expanded its mandate to conduct adversarial probing of deployed systems in energy, finance, and health, even without weight access, and early findings are feeding back into the sprint. We are repurposing the final personnel and funding from the expired *Talent Anchoring Programme* to avoid new capital draws.

But the legal basis remains fragile. Article 114 TFEU harmonisation is stalled in the Council over jurisdictional disputes between the Internal Market and Digital ministers, and the emergency clause under Article 122 faces resistance from member states citing national competence. We are negotiating a political truce: in exchange for allowing national opt-outs in non-critical sectors, we are securing qualified majority support for mandatory deployment in EU-critical functions. The Commission’s Joint Research Centre is being mobilised to operationalise the tools by July, but only if the Council unblocks the legal text by end-March. Without that, the sprint collapses into a research paper and the Union loses its last credible claim to technical agency over frontier AI systems.
