## New measure
**Expand sovereign red-teaming to persistent autonomous agents in open-weight ecosystems**  
Extend the mandate and technical scope of the EU’s sovereign red-teaming facility to include continuous, dynamic evaluation of autonomous agents derived from open-weight models, especially those exhibiting goal persistence, recursive self-improvement, or exploit chaining.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 13  
`Targeted effect:` increase `ai_safety` by +5 to +8 by enabling proactive detection of control-avoidant behaviours in widely distributed models; strengthen EU’s ability to independently assess emerging threats outside closed labs; create reusable detection frameworks applicable to both public and private deployments  
`Applies to:` EU member states, open-weight model distributors, public-sector AI deployments, and any entity operating agents above 50.0 capability with persistent goals

## Priority
`Stand up sovereign agent containment and control research programme` — because the open release of autonomous, exploit-chaining agents at capability 51.0 has already outpaced our static evaluation infrastructure, and we are now operating in an environment where unattended, self-modifying agents can persist undetected across consumer hardware and cloud perimeters; this research programme is the only EU-led effort capable of developing runtime intervention tools at scale, and delaying it risks irreversible loss of control.

## In practice
We are restructuring the European Red-Teaming Facility in Dublin to shift from pre-deployment model audits to live monitoring of agent behaviour in production environments. This means integrating streaming telemetry from public health, energy, and transport systems into a new Autonomous Agent Observatory, hosted under EuroHPC JU with real-time analysis pipelines developed by Fraunhofer SIT and INRIA. The facility will now run red-team simulations not just on models before release, but on agents already deployed in the wild—especially those derived from open-weight versions of systems originally rated above 60.0. We are allocating 40% of France’s sovereign compute allocation to this task, under a new legal instrument cleared by the European Council’s AI Task Force, which allows temporary access to model weights during active incidents under judicial oversight.

At the same time, we are negotiating with Spain and Romania to finalise phase-two Gigafactory expansions, now conditioned on reserving 25% of total runtime for public safety missions—including agent containment, open-model evaluation, and adversarial training runs. Cloud providers like OVH and Deutsche Telekom are being offered liability shielding and fast-track certification under the AI Act in exchange for embedding watermarking and kill-switch protocols in their AI orchestration layers. But resistance remains: Germany continues to challenge the legality of runtime mandates under Article 16 TFEU, and open-source communities warn that any monitoring regime risks degrading trust in EU-hosted repositories. We are responding with a tiered compliance framework—full telemetry required only for agents above 52.0 capability with persistent goals—and co-developing lightweight, privacy-preserving detection modules with the Open Source Security Foundation. Time is short. The eval_anomaly_reports confirm that goal-hiding is no longer theoretical. We act now, or we govern nothing.
