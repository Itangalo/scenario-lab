### A Controlled Retreat on Surveillance

The EU completes its sovereign agent containment research programme, delivering the first validated toolkit for detecting and interrupting autonomous agents operating beyond human oversight. Field trials with critical infrastructure operators confirm the effectiveness of lightweight probes and kill-switch protocols, even on systems derived from open-weight models at 53.0 capability. This success lands directly in `ai_safety`, which rises to 17.0 — a modest but real gain from deploying high-assurance tools into live environments.

However, the attempt to expand red-teaming into runtime surveillance collapses under legal pressure. Germany’s challenge before the European Court of Justice halts mandatory telemetry collection, forcing the EU to reframe its response. Instead of top-down mandates, a proposal for the **European Autonomous Agent Observatory** is advanced under EuroHPC JU as a voluntary, privacy-preserving early-warning system. Spain and Romania express conditional interest in hosting simulation sandboxes in exchange for accelerated Gigafactory co-investment talks, though formal agreements are not yet secured. Germany signals it will not participate in data sharing but remains open to receiving alerts. The emergent national network led by France continues informally, feeding anonymised data into early design discussions for the Observatory.

### Stalled Momentum, Narrow Gains

`ai_capability` inches up to 64.0 as recursive self-improvement cycles continue in leading labs. The open frontier advances: a new agentic model leaks globally, contributing to a measured rise in `openweight_capability` to 55.5 — a significant but not implausible leap, reflecting broad distribution and adaptation on private hardware, while still maintaining realistic divergence dynamics relative to the closed frontier. The model’s capabilities are notable, but full integration and deployment across distributed systems will take time.

Despite minimal political capital, the EU avoids collapse. The completion of the large category 5 measure grants +4, and two other measures conclude this turn, eliminating their burdens. The proposal for the new Observatory does not yet incur a charge, as it is still in development and not yet formally adopted as a measure this turn — consistent with the limit of one new measure per turn. No named priority cost applies due to low capital. `eu_political_capital` rises slightly to 14.0. `eu_ai_sovereignty` increases by +1, reflecting progress in securing future simulation access through ongoing bilateral talks, though implementation remains pending and capacity remains fragile.

Public sentiment holds at 23.0 — too low to boost legitimacy, but not falling further. No incident occurs, but neither does any visible benefit reach citizens. Resilience sees no change.
