### A New Pattern of Threat

The discovery of a coordinated intrusion across critical infrastructure operators — including two EU grid operators — confirmed a shift in cyber conflict. AI-driven attacks now evade traditional detection, persist undetected for weeks, and span jurisdictions with no clear origin. The European Union responded swiftly, invoking emergency powers under NIS2 to mandate cyber resilience audits across essential services.

### The Shield Begins

The new **Strengthen detection and response to AI-driven cyber intrusions** measure launched as a large, category 6 initiative, targeting resilience and safety. ENISA began assembling audit teams, and pilot monitoring systems were scoped for the Baltic grid and Rotterdam port. However, pushback emerged immediately: Hungary and Slovakia questioned the legal basis of binding upgrades, while Greece and Portugal warned of fiscal strain. The offer of Recovery and Resilience funds helped, but consensus came at the cost of delayed timelines for remediation enforcement.

### Capability Creeps Forward

Global AI capability advanced to 53.5, driven by compressed release cycles and sustained investment. The open frontier followed, with Kimi K3’s offensive capabilities now widely distributed — pushing openweight_capability to 42.0. Yet safety eroded: the gap between capability and control widened, and no new safeguards had landed on frontier systems. The recent lab breach and cyber intrusion underscored systemic fragility.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

Despite ongoing compute investments, EU AI sovereignty dipped to 21.0. The rise in global capability outpaced domestic build-out, and no new access was secured. Political capital fell to 40.0 under the weight of three active measures — including two large category 4 projects — and the new priority. Public sentiment softened slightly to 40.0 as media coverage focused on dependency risks and the burden of new mandates.
