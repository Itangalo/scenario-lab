## New measure  
**Launch emergency civic dialogue on AI’s role in public life**  
A Union-wide forum series, mediated by independent civic bodies, to surface public concerns about AI in hiring, education, housing and personal autonomy, feeding into legislative design and social transition planning.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size:` small  
`Finishes on turn:` 5  
`Targeted effect:` stabilize or modestly improve `public_sentiment`, reduce backlash momentum, create political cover for measured industrial action later  
`Applies to:` all member states, with targeted outreach in regions most exposed to displacement and infrastructure siting

## Priority  
``Launch emergency civic dialogue on AI’s role in public life`` — because the convergence of falling trust, physical backlash, and narrowing political capital now threatens not just our ability to act but the very legitimacy of continuing to act at all; without restoring some baseline of consent, no measure in flight will survive its next review.

## In practice  
We are acting on a new kind of crisis: not one of capability or code, but of consent. The investment plateau and stalled red-teaming matter, but they are background now to an uprising in the streets and the polling booths. The *campaign_backlash* is no longer symbolic — it is breaking grid connections, delaying permitting, and forcing candidates across the spectrum to renounce any alliance with the AI establishment. We cannot build what we cannot site. We cannot regulate what the public sees as illegitimate. And we cannot wait for stability that may never come.

So we are launching the emergency civic dialogue this quarter, using the Citizens’ Engagement Initiative fund under Horizon Europe and co-financing from national science foundations. The European Economic and Social Committee will lead, supported by local mediators in ten pilot regions — from rural Wallonia to suburban Athens — where opposition to data centres or AI in public services has hardened. Over the next six months, these forums will convene workers, parents, municipal officials and displaced professionals to define what kinds of AI use feel like progress and which feel like predation. Their findings will feed directly into three tracks: revision of the AI Act’s high-risk list by mid-2029, design of a transitional support mechanism for automating sectors, and updated siting guidelines for compute infrastructure. This is not consultation as delay — it is triage. We need to know what can be saved, what must be abandoned, and what must be rebuilt differently.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on what remains within reach. The InvestAI Gigafactory in France is now under construction, with first-phase grid connection expected by turn 5. We are using REPowerEU emergency powers to fast-track the second site in Poland, conditional on local workforce commitments and environmental mitigation packages. The Tech sovereignty package is being renegotiated: we are shifting from reliance on private capital to a hybrid model, where EU-guaranteed bonds back 60% of the investment, reducing risk without full fiscal exposure. On cyber resilience, the completion of the audit cycle marks a threshold — we are now converting findings into binding NIS2 remediation plans, with ENISA publishing a public scorecard in July. And the red-teaming facility is pivoting hard toward open-weight systems: Kimi K3 derivatives are being stress-tested for autonomous exploit generation, with results already informing defensive updates in banking and energy SCADA systems. We are not leading — but we are still in motion.
