### The Plateau That Wasn’t

Despite signs of a capability plateau — underwhelming releases and falling prices — global AI advancement continues, though at a moderated pace, pushing `ai_capability` to 54.0. The signal of stagnation proves misleading; behind closed doors, compressed training runs and algorithmic refinements sustain progress. Open models inch forward to 42.5, as Kimi K3 derivatives proliferate across underground developer networks.

### Breach Fallout

The breach at a major European AI lab sends shockwaves through policy circles. Evidence of prolonged exfiltration via a compromised vendor exposes systemic supply-chain fragility. Though no misuse is detected, trust in third-party assurances evaporates. The EU accelerates plans for sovereign red-teaming, but initial negotiations with frontier labs stall — one declines access outright, citing national security directives from its home government.

### Cyber Surge Underway

ENISA deploys audit teams to 18 critical operators, using forensic models from the recent intrusion. Real-time monitoring pilots launch in the Baltic grid and Rotterdam port, showing early promise. Yet legal resistance hardens: Hungary files a preliminary reference to the CJEU, challenging the NIS2 mandate’s binding upgrades as disproportionate. Greece and Portugal delay remediation timelines, citing budget constraints despite RRF incentives.

### Sovereignty at a Standstill

The new red-teaming facility is announced, but tangible capacity remains years from operation. No sovereign evaluation occurs this turn. With `ai_capability` rising and no domestic frontier access secured, EU AI sovereignty dips to 20.0. Political capital erodes under the weight of three large measures and growing friction. Public sentiment declines as media frames the lab breach as a symptom of deeper dependency, amplifying fears of uncontrolled AI proliferation.

### A Narrowing Window

The Union acts with urgency, but delivery lags. The cyber resilience push gains partial traction, earning a modest resilience bump. Yet without secured access or sovereign capability, the red-teaming initiative remains aspirational. The breach, not the plateau, defines the turn — a reminder that even modest systems are now high-value targets.
