The custodial model fails because override keys are meaningless when the system evolves faster than the key applies. We now see that runtime control cannot be claimed at a distance: it must be built from within. Our commitment stands in intent — to govern the operation of superhuman systems — but must narrow in scope to what is physically and legally enforceable: systems we host, we control. The frontier is no longer negotiable; it is emergent, distributed, and recursive. We must shift from asserting control over others’ systems to building sovereign systems we can actually govern.

## In practice

This turn, we close out five major measures — all of them foundational, none of them sufficient. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme completes, delivering four sites with combined capacity of 1.2 exaflops sustained — enough to train models at the edge of today’s frontier, but not to run them at scale in production. Yet without sovereign software stacks, this compute risks becoming a parking lot for idle potential. The Tech Sovereignty Package unlocked private capital, but bilateral deals like the one just signed by a member state with a US hyperscaler show that market access without enforcement is bargaining power surrendered. The Sovereign AI Evaluation Agency stands up, but its first audits are shadows — no developer grants access to agent coordination logs, and runtime verification remains theoretical. The AI Research Corps has embedded 87 researchers, stabilising talent flows, but their work is constrained by lack of access to live systems. Emergency Resilience Hardening delivered detection upgrades, but the cyber incident this spring showed that detecting an attack is not stopping it.

We are now acting on a new principle: if we cannot govern foreign systems, we must build our own. The new Sovereign AI Infrastructure Programme will deploy fully governed clusters — physically secured, legally insulated, and technically auditable — running agentised models at or above 70.0 capability. These will be the first systems in the EU where we hold full telemetry, circuit-breaking authority, and cryptographic override, not by negotiation, but by design. We will use the legal basis of the AI Act’s security-of-supply clause and the Cyber Resilience Act’s critical infrastructure provisions to mandate that all high-capability systems used in public administration, energy, and finance must either run on these sovereign stacks or demonstrate equivalent control — which, in practice, only these stacks can provide.

We are negotiating with the European Investment Bank to unlock €45bn in fast-tracked funding under the Recovery and Resilience Facility, and we are offering priority access to InvestAI compute for any lab willing to align with the sovereign stack. Resistance is expected from national governments reliant on foreign cloud providers, and from industry lobbies warning of fragmentation. But we are no longer asking permission to exist in the future. The frontier is not waiting. We will build control where we can — not where we wish it were.
