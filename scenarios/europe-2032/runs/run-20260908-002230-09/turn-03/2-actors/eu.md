## Priority  
**Emergency resilience hardening for critical infrastructure** — because the cyber major incident has already landed, public services are exposed, and without functioning defences, no evaluation, sovereignty, or talent strategy matters: resilience is the floor beneath everything else.

## In practice  
This turn, we are finalising the Emergency Resilience Hardening measure through a binding Council Implementing Act under Article 122 TFEU, leveraging the solidarity framing to secure unanimous adoption. ENISA is now deploying the first wave of AI-augmented intrusion detection systems to 18 critical energy operators, with full rollout scheduled by March. The mutualised risk pool is capitalised at €4.2bn, with Germany, France, and the Netherlands covering 60%, and liability shielding under NIS2 is being operationalised through a delegated act to enter force by February — this removes the legal hesitation that slowed adoption in Q4.  

On the sovereignty front, the ASML bottleneck forces acceleration of the DUV pilot lines: we are redirecting €18bn from the InvestAI reserve to fast-track the Wrocław and Valencia sites, accepting lower yield but gaining sovereign access to 45.5-level capability by turn 5. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being revised to tie permitting advantages to binding commitments on data localisation and energy sourcing, turning economic incentive into strategic leverage — this is now under trilogue negotiation with the Parliament’s ITRE and ECON committees. Meanwhile, the sovereign evaluation agency is being scoped with CERN-like governance: a Council decision will invite member states to contribute scientists and funding, while enforcement will be anchored in the Digital Markets Act and AI Act frameworks.  

The new measure, the European AI Research Corps, responds directly to the emergent centrifuge: we are launching a €6.5bn instrument under Horizon Europe+, with 70% dedicated to five-year research fellowships, 20% to sovereign compute slices reserved for public research, and 10% to legal shielding against foreign data access requests. The first cohort — 42 researchers, including 11 from former US labs — will be hosted across INRIA, Max Planck, and ISTA by June 2028. This is not a talent competition; it is the foundation of an independent EU technical epistemic community.
