## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign control over AI systems at or above capability index 60.0``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The current wave of recursive self-improvement has pushed frontier capability to 66.0, well beyond the reach of any existing governance mechanism. At this level, systems are not just tools but autonomous actors in strategic domains. Our previous focus on building foundational sovereignty — compute, talent, evaluation — was necessary, but without direct jurisdictional control over systems that can reshape reality, that foundation is exposed. We must now shift from enabling capacity to enforcing containment: the EU cannot afford a future where its own infrastructure runs uncontrolled superhuman systems.

## In practice
This turn, we are finalising the Emergency Resilience Hardening measure: ENISA has deployed AI-augmented intrusion detection to all 18 designated energy operators, and the mutualised risk pool is now operational under NIS2, providing liability shielding during incidents. The bilateral side letters with Italy and Poland have secured full participation, and the first joint cyber drill across seven member states will occur in April, testing cross-border coordination under live conditions. Though the measure finishes, its technical and institutional legacy persists.

On industrial capacity, we are accelerating DUV pilot lines in Wrocław and Valencia using redirected InvestAI funds, accepting lower yield for faster deployment. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being renegotiated to tie permitting advantages not only to data localisation but to compliance with new control mandates — turning infrastructure policy into a lever for safety. The European AI Research Corps continues to stabilise talent flows, with 87 researchers embedded into sovereign labs this quarter, while the sovereign evaluation agency begins shadow audits of two frontier models, laying groundwork for enforcement.

The new priority — binding operational control over systems above 60.0 — is being drafted as a delegated act under Article 6(3) of the AI Act, with legal anchoring in security of supply and public order. We are convening a special session of the AI Office with Germany, France, and the Nordic-Benelux pact to align on verification protocols and threshold definitions, knowing that fragmentation would kill enforceability. US labs are resisting, so we are offering early certification and access to sovereign compute for compliance testing — carrots, because with `eu_political_capital` at 23.0, we cannot win a confrontation unless we’ve already secured consensus.
