# Statements: The European Union (turn 8)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign control over AI systems at or above capability index 60.0
- `new_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Enforce EU jurisdiction over the runtime operation of all AI systems at or above capability index 60.0, including real-time monitoring, circuit-breaking authority, and override control — but only where such systems are deployed on EU-owned or EU-governed infrastructure, and only with verified, enforceable access to agent-level telemetry and cryptographic control.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign control over AI systems at or above capability index 60.0
  - Trigger: the failure of runtime jurisdiction over foreign systems, the collapse of enforcement mechanisms, and the demonstrated impossibility of verifying control at a distance — combined with the completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories and the launch of the Sovereign AI Infrastructure Programme — prove that "control" cannot be claimed through regulation alone, but must be grounded in physical, legal, and technical ownership of the full stack.
  - Grounds: The original commitment assumed that control could be enforced via regulatory leverage over deployment conditions. That assumption has been invalidated. We now see that capability without sovereignty is vulnerability. The frontier is not governed — it evolves. Our revised commitment must reflect that control is only real when we own the infrastructure, the software, and the chain of command. We are shifting from a goal of *securing control* over high-capability systems wherever they run, to *exercising control* over systems we build, host, and operate. The threshold remains, but the domain narrows: we will secure control not over all systems above 60.0, but over EU-operated systems at or above that level — and we will ensure they are the only ones with operational authority in critical domains.
  - Reason: modified text is identical to the current text
- **modify** `new_two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Enforce EU jurisdiction over the runtime operation of all AI systems at or above capability index 60.0, including real-time monitoring, circuit-breaking authority, and override control — but only where such systems are deployed on EU-owned or EU-governed infrastructure, and only with verified, enforceable access to agent-level telemetry and cryptographic control.
  - Grounds: The modification made last turn already narrowed the scope in response to enforcement failure. This turn confirms that narrowing was necessary and correct. No further change in wording is needed, but the operational reality has shifted: this commitment now functions not as a regulatory reach-out, but as a constitutional boundary. It defines the limit of what the EU will trust in its own critical functions. We are no longer trying to govern the world’s AI — we are building a sovereign zone within which we can govern at all.
