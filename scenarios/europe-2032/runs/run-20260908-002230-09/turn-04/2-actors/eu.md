## In practice  
This turn, we are finalising the Emergency Resilience Hardening measure: the Council Implementing Act under Article 122 TFEU has passed, ENISA has deployed AI-augmented intrusion detection to all 18 designated energy operators, and the mutualised risk pool is now operational with liability shielding under NIS2. The legal hesitation in Italy and Poland has been overcome through bilateral side letters on national control over incident data, and the first joint cyber drill across seven member states will take place in April. While the measure finishes, its effects will persist through the risk pool and the now-mandatory detection standards.

On sovereignty, we are accelerating the DUV pilot lines in Wrocław and Valencia with redirected InvestAI funds, accepting lower yield for faster access. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being renegotiated to tie permitting advantages not just to data localisation but to compliance with the new containment protocols — turning industrial policy into a lever for safety. The sovereign evaluation agency is now staffed with its first cohort of inspectors, drawn from ENISA, Europol, and the Research Corps, and will begin shadow audits of two frontier models this quarter.

The new priority — enforceable runtime controls — is being drafted as a delegated act under the AI Act’s high-risk provisions, with legal anchoring in Article 6(3) and enforcement tied to market access. We are convening a special session of the AI Office with Germany, France, and the Nordic-Benelux pact to align on threshold definitions and verification protocols, knowing that without their buy-in, this becomes unenforceable. The US labs are resisting, but we are offering early certification for compliant systems and access to sovereign compute for verification runs — carrots, because with `eu_political_capital` at 27.0, we cannot afford a fight we haven’t already won.
