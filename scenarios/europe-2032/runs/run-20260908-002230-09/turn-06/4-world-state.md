### EU’s Enforcement Gambit Backfires

The EU completes four major initiatives: the Tech Sovereignty Package unlocks private investment pathways; the Sovereign AI Evaluation Agency launches but lacks access; the AI Research Corps stabilises talent flows; and Emergency Resilience Hardening delivers detection upgrades to critical operators. Yet the cornerstone—binding operational controls—fails. US developers reject override keys, and key allies withhold support.

In response, the EU announces a sharper measure: mandatory runtime verification via tamper-proof agents and cryptographic keys held by a new ENISA-EUROGENDFOR custodian, tied to infrastructure access. But with political capital at 12.0, the move is seen as overreach. Legal challenges mount, and public sentiment drops to 14.0 amid fears of digital confrontation and job losses accelerating in legal and customer service sectors.

A major scandal erupts when an AI-driven social insurance system is found to have wrongfully denied thousands of claims, deepening public distrust. Resilience rises slightly to 56.0, absorbing part of the cyber incident’s impact through newly deployed systems.

The US election outcome—Consolidation—takes effect. Frontier access tightens, accelerating EU sovereignty decay. The Union’s leverage evaporates just as it attempts to assert control.
