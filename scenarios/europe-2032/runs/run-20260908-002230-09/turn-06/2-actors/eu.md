## In practice

This turn, we are closing out four major measures: the Tech Sovereignty Package, the sovereign evaluation agency, the European AI Research Corps, and Emergency Resilience Hardening. The latter has delivered AI-augmented intrusion detection to all 18 critical energy operators, and the mutualised risk pool under NIS2 is now live, with seven member states completing the first joint cyber drill in April. The sovereign evaluation agency, though newly empowered, lacks teeth — its shadow audits of two frontier models were conducted without access to training logs or agent coordination data, and both developers restricted visibility to static model weights. The Research Corps has embedded 87 researchers into EU-hosted labs, stabilising talent flows but not yet producing sovereign capability at the frontier.

The binding operational controls measure, intended as our anchor this turn, has failed to gain traction. US labs have refused to surrender override keys, citing national security and intellectual property; the Nordic-Benelux group has withheld alignment, demanding independent verification of both capability thresholds and control mechanisms. We offered early certification and access to InvestAI compute, but the offer lacks leverage — with eu_political_capital at 12.0 and no enforcement mechanism, it reads as desperation.

Now we pivot: the new measure under Category 1 establishes a sovereign custodian — a joint ENISA-EUROGENDFOR unit — to hold cryptographic override keys and conduct runtime verification via tamper-proof agents installed directly into foreign AI systems. We will tie this to infrastructure licensing: no access to EU cloud networks, power grids, or telecom backbones without verified agent deployment. Legal anchoring will come through Article 17 of the Cyber Resilience Act and the AI Act’s security-of-supply clause. We are preparing for a confrontation, knowing the US administration takes office next turn — but if we do not act now, there will be no sovereign line left to hold.
