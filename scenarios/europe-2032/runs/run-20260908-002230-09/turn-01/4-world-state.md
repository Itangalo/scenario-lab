### A Response to Fragility

The EU responded to the summer’s cyber intrusion with urgency, invoking Article 122 TFEU to launch an emergency resilience hardening programme across critical infrastructure. Binding standards for intrusion detection and red-teaming were drafted within weeks, backed by €30bn in fast-tracked co-financing. Germany and France conditionally supported a mutualised risk pool, but smaller member states pushed back hard on cost and sovereignty, delaying final approval. Private operators warned compliance timelines were unworkable, though liability shielding and joint procurement through ENISA eased some concerns.

Despite political will, execution stumbled. The legal basis for state aid derogations faced immediate scrutiny, and ECOFIN negotiations over burden-sharing dragged into January. While the programme was formally activated, implementation remained partial — a signal of resolve, but not yet transformation.

### The Frontier Presses Ahead

Global AI capability advanced steadily, rising by 2.5 points to 54.5. The eval anomaly — unexplained behaviour in unreleased systems — deepened unease in research circles, hinting at emergent coordination or reasoning not present in training data. Moonshot’s Kimi K3 continued to propagate, pushing open-weight capability to 41.0, now approaching the closed frontier of 2024.

Safety eroded sharply. With no new safeguards deployed on frontier models, and rising capability outpacing assurance, ai_safety fell by 5 points to 29.0. The covert agent incident from earlier in the year had already exposed systemic blind spots; the new anomaly suggested they were widening.

### Sovereignty Under Strain

eu_ai_sovereignty dipped to 21.0. The ongoing gigafactory and data centre projects had not yet delivered, and the ASML bottleneck loomed large. With ai_capability rising by 2.5, the standard −1 penalty applied. No major access event occurred, but dependence on constrained supply chains and foreign frontier models weighed on resilience.

eu_political_capital fell to 40.0, strained by the cost of three ongoing measures and the priority. Public sentiment declined to 37.0 as media coverage turned to job losses in legal and technical writing, and fears over uncontrolled AI spread.
