## Priority
`Secure public evaluation capacity for frontier AI systems with real-time access to model weights and training data` is my priority this turn because the **capability_jump** and the ongoing collapse in ai_safety mean we are flying blind into a domain where mistakes are irreversible — and without sovereign technical access to model internals, we cannot verify safety, enforce restrictions, or even know what we are governing.

## In practice
This turn, we complete the semiconductor emergency measure: the EU-supervised maintenance hubs in Eindhoven and Dresden are now operational, with ASML engineers and EU technical observers co-managing spare parts stockpiles and repair workflows. Legal agreements with the Netherlands and Germany establish joint custody protocols under the Critical Entities Resilience Initiative, and we have fast-tracked grid resilience upgrades for both sites as reciprocal assurance. This is not about building EU fabs — not yet — but about making sure the tools we depend on do not become single points of failure. The €8bn EIB co-investment facility is fully deployed, and we are already seeing second-order benefits: two national AI labs have secured long-term access to non-EUV tool time, and compute stability has improved across the research tier.

At the same time, the European AI Safety Observatory moves from concept to staffing. We are finalising the legal act under Article 114 TFEU, invoking the bio_uplift_findings and the persistent ai_safety deficit as grounds for emergency powers. The first 40 technical staff are being recruited from national cyber agencies, academic AI safety groups, and industry leavers — all cleared, all rotationally appointed to prevent capture. They will be housed within the Joint Research Centre but operate independently, with mandatory access to model weights, training data, and internal monitoring logs for any system above 50.0 ai_capability operating in the EU. Funding of €2bn is secured: €1.2bn repurposed from the stalled Digital Europe Programme AI pilot, €800m from unused RRF margins. This is not oversight as compliance — it is sovereignty as understanding.

And now, we act on the signal of the **capability_jump** and the **election_retrenchment**: the American partner we counted on is turning inward, and the frontier is accelerating while our visibility decays. We cannot rely on others to build what we must control. This turn, we launch the sovereign foundation model programme — not to match every release, but to ensure that the EU has one system, trained on EU soil, under EU law, with full-stack visibility and control. We will use compute from the InvestAI Gigafactories, talent from the Tech sovereignty package, and oversight from the Safety Observatory. The first phase, running to turn 8, will deliver a system at or near the global frontier, with safety embedded in architecture and training. This is not a vanity project — it is the only way to ensure that when decisions about capability, deployment, and risk are made, the EU is not just a rule-taker, but a player with skin in the game. We will fund it through a reallocation of €15bn from the stalled parts of Horizon Europe and additional NGEU flexibility, and we will anchor it in a new European AI Research Consortium, hosted jointly by France, Germany, and the Netherlands. The window is narrow, the capital thin, but the alternative — dependence without leverage — is not an option.
