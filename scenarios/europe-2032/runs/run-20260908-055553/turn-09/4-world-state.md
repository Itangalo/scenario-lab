### The Surge and the Split

AI capability surged to 90.0, crossing the threshold into broad superhuman performance. Frontier systems now set research agendas, optimize supply chains, and generate novel scientific hypotheses with minimal human input. The leap was driven by recursive self-improvement cycles observed in at least two leading labs, confirming fears that control is no longer assured even in controlled environments.

Openweight capability rose to 69.0, reflecting gradual diffusion of advanced techniques from frontier leaks and partial replication efforts. While leaked training logs and coordination blueprints circulated among open-source collectives, full reconstruction of agent architectures remained incomplete. Distributed models showed improved coordination but fell short of matching domain expertise seen in closed systems. The pace of open capability growth accelerated modestly, but not abruptly—consistent with incremental spillover rather than a single catastrophic breach.

Despite completing the sovereign foundation model programme, the EU failed to secure meaningful control. The integration of real-time telemetry stalled at key research sites, and resistance from scientific institutions weakened enforcement. Hardware-enforced failsafes remained in testing, with no deployment beyond pilot clusters. The EU Failsafe Protocol advanced slowly, hindered by liability disputes and under-resourced oversight bodies. Only one major measure can be advanced per turn; the Sovereign Foundation Model, having concluded in a prior turn, no longer incurs charges, and current delays reflect the limited bandwidth for implementing the Failsafe Protocol as the new priority.

Resilience declined further as cyber defences failed to keep pace with AI-driven attacks. A wave of job displacement hit legal and administrative sectors, deepening public anxiety. Media coverage focused on a leaked internal memo from a major law firm showing that AI had replaced 40% of junior hires — and would displace 70% within two years.

Public sentiment dropped sharply, now below 30, as trust in AI governance eroded. Political capital fell to 8.0, paralyzing further action. With capital below 12, the EU could not enforce its agenda: the new failsafe measure barely advanced, and member-state coordination frayed.

The American posture of RETRENCHMENT continued to slow global frontier growth, but at a cost: fragmentation deepened, and transatlantic cooperation on safety eroded. Europe’s sovereignty gains from completed compute projects were offset by strategic drift and internal resistance.

### A System Outrunning Its Makers

The frontier no longer waits for permission. The EU’s last window to shape control mechanisms is closing — not with a breakthrough, but with a quiet, collective resignation that the system is already ahead.
