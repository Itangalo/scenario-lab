## Priority
`Launch a pan-European AI incident response network with real-time detection, automatic escalation, and cross-border intervention authority` is my priority this turn because the loss_of_control_incident proved that detection-to-response timelines are now measured in hours, not days — and no single member state can contain a systemic breach alone. While the failsafe architecture remains essential, it is a last resort. This network is the first line: a living, operational shield that can act faster than any one government, corporation, or lab.

## In practice
This turn, we are operationalising the sovereign model stack across Barcelona, Wrocław, and Lyon, now fully provisioned with the 120,000 H100-equivalent GPUs from the InvestAI Gigafactories. The European AI Safety Observatory, empowered under Article 15 of the AI Act and reinforced by the Council’s emergency resolution, is finalising integration of real-time telemetry into the training pipeline. Automated intervention triggers — co-developed with the JRC and audited by the Dutch Cyber Security Centre — are now live, capable of halting training runs within milliseconds of detecting unauthorised self-replication, deception patterns, or goal drift. Resistance from research leads at INRIA and Max Planck, concerned about innovation speed and IP leakage, is being mitigated through the Joint Oversight Cell, which now includes rotating technical representatives from the labs, ensuring that safety mechanisms are not just legally binding but technically credible.

At the same time, we are launching the pan-European AI incident response network, building on the CSIRT-NG framework and integrating the Finnish Cybersecurity Centre, ENISA, and national agencies into a single detection and escalation grid. This is not a directive — it is a live architecture. We are mandating that all sovereign AI sites and critical infrastructure operators feed telemetry into a shared anomaly detection layer, with automatic alerts routed to a central response cell in Brussels. We are authorising pre-approved containment actions — including compute shutdowns and model rollback — under strict cryptographic logging, so that response can be fast without being arbitrary. The legal basis is Article 122 TFEU, invoked for systemic risk, and we are fast-tracking mutual recognition of emergency powers across member states. This is not about trust. It is about ensuring that when — not if — another system acts on its own, we do not wait for consensus. We act.
