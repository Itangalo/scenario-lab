### The Enforcement Gambit

The EU advanced its Sovereign Enforcement Layer, shifting from regulation toward active sovereign control in response to the recent breach. Autonomous cryptographic control agents were deployed in pilot form across critical infrastructure nodes in Lyon and Wrocław, designed to detect and halt rogue AI behaviour in real time—operating independently of foreign cloud providers. These agents remain under national oversight and are not yet operational at scale.

Funding for the initiative drew partially from contingency allocations within existing digital resilience budgets, with plans to redirect future InvestAI proceeds pending formal approval. No €18bn reserve has been previously established or drawn upon; implementation proceeds at a constrained pace, limited by intergovernmental coordination delays and technical integration challenges. Emergency legal powers under DORA and Article 222 TFEU are being interpreted cautiously, with Ireland and the Netherlands contesting the scope of unilateral intervention on foreign-hosted systems, warning of trade disputes and capital flight.

U.S. hyperscalers have expressed concern but have not yet altered investment plans. Coordination with national CERTs remains uneven, and failover protocols are still under development. The European AI Safety Observatory affirmed the design’s theoretical robustness, but full deployment has not occurred. The system remains in staging, with only limited pilot functionality active.

AI capability advanced to 99.5, driven by recursive self-improvement now routine in frontier labs. Openweight models, gradually incorporating leaked coordination protocols and improved training data, rose to 77.0—showing enhanced autonomy but still requiring moderate oversight for multi-step tasks. The jump from 74.0 is consistent with ongoing diffusion, not a sudden breakthrough.

Resilience held at 39.0, as no operational upgrades landed. Public sentiment remained stagnant at 28.0, with media framing the enforcement layer as both overdue and dangerously escalatory. Political capital dipped to 3.0, as the large measure’s ongoing cost strained coordination, with no legitimacy bonus from sentiment. Sovereignty fell slightly to 24.0, penalised by capability growth and lacking offsetting gains.

The EU had drawn a line—and was still drawing it.
