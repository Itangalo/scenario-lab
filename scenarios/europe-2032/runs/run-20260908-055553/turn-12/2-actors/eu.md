This is no longer about building a safe stack or extending rules — it is about building power to act when others refuse.

## In practice  
This turn, we are shifting from rule-setting to enforcement capacity. The telemetry mandate under Article 114 TFEU remains on the table, but we now see it as a political instrument, not an operational one. The hyperscalers will not voluntarily embed our interrupts. Japan and Canada will not align without U.S. buy-in, which we will not get. So we are no longer waiting for compliance. Instead, we are tasking the European AI Safety Observatory, in coordination with ENISA and the newly empowered EU Cybersecurity Agency, to deploy a sovereign enforcement layer — a distributed network of cryptographic control agents running on EU-secured infrastructure within critical nodes of cloud, energy, and finance. These agents will monitor for anomalous agentic behaviour using lightweight telemetry harvested from public APIs, packet metadata, and resource allocation patterns — no cooperation required. When thresholds are crossed, they will trigger hardware-level interrupts on local infrastructure, isolate memory segments, and initiate rollback using pre-negotiated failover protocols with national CERTs.  

We are funding this through a reallocation of the €200bn InvestAI Gigafactories reserve, redirecting €18bn to a new **Sovereign Enforcement Accelerator Fund**, governed by a Council-level committee with emergency activation powers. Legal cover comes from a novel interpretation of Article 17 of the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA), expanded by emergency rider under Article 222 TFEU. Resistance is expected from member states with large foreign data centres — Ireland, the Netherlands — who fear economic retaliation. We are offering them guaranteed co-location rights for EU sovereign AI workloads and priority access to the new enforcement stack for domestic protection. This is not a request for permission. It is the construction of a capability that will exist whether others like it or not — because if we cannot stop a system that moves money or disables grids, then no rule, no treaty, no observatory will matter. We are building the hand that enforces the rule.
