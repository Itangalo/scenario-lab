## Priority  
``Strengthen cyber resilience of critical infrastructure against AI-generated threats`` is my priority this turn because the **cyber_major_incident** has exposed systemic vulnerability at a moment when offensive AI capabilities are now open and distributed — and because resilience is the one lever fully within our control when sovereignty and safety lag.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that the frontier has already leaked: Kimi K3’s release means offensive cyber tools are now downloadable, and the **cyber_test_shot** and **cyber_major_incident** prove we are behind. This turn, the Commission will table a Directive under Article 114 TFEU requiring all operators of essential services — energy, water, transport, health, and digital infrastructure — to implement AI-driven red-teaming and anomaly detection systems within 18 months. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will be tasked with developing the technical standards by Q1 2027, and Member States must designate national authorities to conduct audits and enforce compliance by turn 4.

We are funding this through a €12bn reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with co-financing required from national budgets to ensure ownership. Resistance is expected from smaller member states citing cost and from industry actors who argue that compliance timelines are unrealistic. We are offering fast-track access to InvestAI Gigafactory compute capacity for firms that comply early, and a temporary 75% EU co-financing rate for Member States below the resilience median. This is not just about patching systems — it is about building muscle: detection, response, and institutional continuity under sustained AI-augmented attack.

At the same time, we are advancing the Tech Sovereignty Package by convening energy ministers and national regulators to fast-track grid access for AI data centres in designated zones, and we are negotiating with ASML and EUV suppliers to secure priority maintenance and parts delivery for EU-anchored compute. The Gigafactories fund is in early due diligence on five sites, with the first contract notices to be published by December. We are not waiting for permission — but we are also not pretending that compute alone is enough. If we cannot defend what we build, sovereignty is an illusion.
