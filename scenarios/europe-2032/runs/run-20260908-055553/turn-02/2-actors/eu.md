## Priority  
``Strengthen cyber resilience of critical infrastructure against AI-generated threats`` is my priority this turn because the **cyber_major_incident** has already struck, and the window to rebuild trust and institutional muscle before the next attack is closing — this is the one lever we fully control when both safety and sovereignty are under external pressure.

## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: defending what we have, and securing the foundations of what we must become. On resilience, the Commission has tabled the Cyber Resilience Directive under Article 114 TFEU, mandating AI-powered red-teaming and anomaly detection for all operators of essential services. ENISA is finalising technical standards by June, and we are deploying fast-track access to InvestAI compute as an incentive for early compliance. To overcome resistance from smaller member states, we are offering a temporary 75% EU co-financing rate for those below the resilience median, funded by reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility. This is not just compliance — it is muscle memory for a world where attacks are continuous and AI-augmented.

At the same time, the ASML supply chain crisis demands immediate sovereignty action. The U.S. extraterritorial reach over non-EUV systems threatens the very compute infrastructure we are building. This turn, we are invoking the Critical Entities Resilience Initiative and the recently updated Foreign Interference Regulation to justify emergency intervention. The Commission is launching a €8bn co-investment facility with the EIB to secure spare parts stockpiles, on-site maintenance teams, and alternative lithography pathways for EU-anchored data centres. We are negotiating directly with ASML, Dutch authorities, and EU member states hosting compute sites to establish a “sovereign maintenance corridor” — legally protected, EU-supervised servicing chains that bypass third-country jurisdiction. This is not about blocking U.S. policy — it is about ensuring that our strategic autonomy does not depend on someone else’s export license. The Gigafactories due diligence proceeds, but we now treat compute infrastructure as critical as energy or defence: if it can be switched off from outside, it does not belong to us.
