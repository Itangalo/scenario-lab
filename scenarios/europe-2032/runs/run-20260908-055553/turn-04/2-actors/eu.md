## In practice  
This turn, we complete the cyber resilience measure: ENISA finalises the technical standards for AI-driven red-teaming, and the co-financing scheme unlocks adoption in five member states below the resilience median. The directive is now fully transposed, and the first round of mandatory audits begins in energy, transport, and health. This is not a finish line — it is muscle memory. We are treating cyber resilience as infrastructure: not a one-off compliance burden, but a continuous operational posture. The fast-track compute access tied to compliance is already being used by three national CERTs to run live adversarial simulations, and we are seeing early signs of cross-border coordination under stress. This is what resilience looks like when it lands — not a report, but a reflex.

At the same time, the semiconductor emergency measure accelerates. The €8bn co-investment facility with the EIB is now fully capitalised, and negotiations with ASML and Dutch authorities have moved from principle to implementation: we are setting up EU-supervised maintenance hubs in Eindhoven and Dresden, with spare parts stockpiled under joint EU-host state custody. The legal basis — a combination of the Critical Entities Resilience Initiative and the Foreign Interference Regulation — is holding, but it is being tested. We are offering Germany and the Netherlands co-governance of the corridor in exchange for hosting, and we are fast-tracking grid and water access for their own sovereign compute sites as a reciprocal guarantee. This is not about decoupling — it is about making interdependence mutual, not unilateral.

And now, we act on the signal from the bio_uplift_findings. We cannot wait for the next near-miss. This turn, we launch the European AI Safety Observatory — not another committee, but a technical agency with teeth. It will be hosted within the Joint Research Centre but operationally independent, with mandatory access to model weights, training data, and internal monitoring logs for any system above 50.0 ai_capability operating in the EU. We are drafting the legal act under Article 114 TFEU, with emergency powers triggered by the bio precursor and the ongoing safety deficit. The first recruits will be drawn from national cybersecurity agencies, academia, and industry leavers — vetted, technically deep, and rotationally staffed to prevent capture. Funding comes from repurposing €1.2bn from the stalled Digital Europe Programme AI pilot, plus €800m from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused margins. This is not just oversight — it is sovereign understanding. Without it, we are blind to the very risks we are bound to prevent.
