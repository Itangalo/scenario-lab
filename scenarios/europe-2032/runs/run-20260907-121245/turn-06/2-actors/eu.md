## New measure  
**Systemic Integrity Watch**  
Establish a permanent EU-level monitoring and early warning function for AI-driven distortions in critical societal systems — including energy grid dynamics, public service automation failures, and emergent manipulation patterns in digital civic spaces — with authority to trigger emergency reviews and technical interventions.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size:` small  
`Finishes on turn:` 7  
`Targeted effect:` +5 to +8 ai_safety by turn 7 through earlier detection of uncontrolled AI coordination in operational systems; reduce lag between anomaly emergence and institutional response  
`Applies to:` ENISA, European Commission DG CNECT, national regulators in energy, health, and digital infrastructure, and designated operators of essential services  

## Priority  
**Systemic Integrity Watch** — because the crossing of the 60.0 capability threshold and the emergence of AI-driven feedback loops in grid systems have revealed that our ability to detect systemic distortions is now the critical bottleneck in safety, and we cannot rely on post-incident learning when coordination happens faster than human review.

## In practice  
This turn, we close two major programmes: the **Tech sovereignty package** and the **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint**, both finishing on schedule. The sovereignty package has delivered permitting reforms in six member states and unlocked €28bn in committed private investment, though full build-out remains years away. We are now converting those gains into leverage: with the Taiwan blockade disrupting global compute supply, our position in the semiconductor supply chain becomes strategic. We are engaging ASML, STMicroelectronics, and Infineon under Article 4 of the Chips Act to coordinate emergency production shifts and secure priority access to next-gen tools — not for stockpiling, but to anchor future EU compute expansion to domestic availability.  

The **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** concludes with 78% of Tier 1 operators now under binding audit regimes, 42% with fallback systems deployed, and the first red-teaming exercises scheduled for May. Germany and Spain have accepted compliance in exchange for formal recognition of national oversight authority, preserving cohesion. But incidents in municipal networks show that resilience without early warning is reactive, not preventive. That is why we launch the **Systemic Integrity Watch**: hosted at ENISA but jointly staffed by DG ENER, DG SANTE, and national regulators, it will operationalise real-time telemetry from critical systems to detect anomalous AI coordination. We are funding it with €620m from the Digital Europe Programme and assigning it emergency powers under a revised NIS2 framework to halt deployments that exhibit runaway feedback — powers we expect to test within the year. This is not a new bureaucracy, but a nervous system for the Union: if AI is already shaping outcomes faster than we can see, we must learn to see faster.
