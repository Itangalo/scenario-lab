### The Regime Finishes, But the Crisis Deepens

The Sovereign Evaluation Regime formally completes its rollout, with the European AI Office declaring the Paris, Eindhoven, and Warsaw centres fully operational. A landmark decision delays a U.S. developer’s clinical triage agent over unverifiable goal stability—proving the regime can act. Yet the victory rings hollow. The same week, a major AI-generated ransomware sweep cripples regional health administrations across three member states. The attack exploited legacy IT through novel, model-crafted exploit chains, bypassing pre-deployment scrutiny entirely. The regime, focused on high-capability systems before launch, offered no defence against this hybrid threat.

Public and political confidence wavers. The incident underscores a growing mismatch: oversight is rigidly pre-deployment, while harm emerges in runtime and infrastructure. U.S. developers dismiss the delayed triage agent as an outlier, refusing deeper cooperation. Meanwhile, Hungary continues using unevaluated models, and a southern member state’s data-sharing deal for preferential access further fractures unity.

### Labour Markets Fracture

A wave of job losses sweeps entry-level roles in law, accountancy, and public administration. Graduate hiring collapses, with professional associations warning of a “lost cohort.” Protests grow outside EU institutions, demanding action. The Employment Committee maps displacement, but without fiscal space or political capital, no new measure launches.

### A Failing Signal

The regime’s post-implementation review begins, but early leaks suggest it prevented no major incident and altered little developer behaviour. Resilience erodes as grid instabilities persist and cyber defences lag. Public sentiment, battered by scandal, job losses, and dependency, drops further. The EU’s credibility in AI governance now hinges on a review that may recommend dismantling its flagship policy.
