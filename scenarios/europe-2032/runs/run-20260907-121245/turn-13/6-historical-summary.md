AI capability reaches 72.0, enabled by formal verification in clinical and legal systems, but growing opacity and multi-day decision chains fuel oversight concerns. The EU completes its Sovereign Evaluation Regime with operational centres in Paris, Eindhoven, and Warsaw, marking a symbolic milestone. A U.S. clinical triage agent is delayed over unverifiable goal stability, demonstrating the regime’s authority, but this success is overshadowed by a devastating AI-generated ransomware attack that cripples health systems across three member states—exposing critical gaps in runtime and infrastructure security. Pre-deployment scrutiny proves ineffective against hybrid threats, eroding trust.

U.S. developers remain resistant to data sharing and evaluation, while Hungary continues using unevaluated models and a southern EU state secures unilateral access to a U.S. frontier model via a data-sharing deal, deepening bloc fragmentation. A major social insurance scandal involving systemic bias intensifies public backlash, and recurring AI-driven grid instabilities necessitate manual interventions, undermining confidence in automated critical infrastructure.

Labour markets fracture as entry-level roles in law, accountancy, and public administration vanish, graduate hiring collapses, and protests mount. The Employment Committee documents displacement but lacks resources or political support to respond. The regime’s post-implementation review begins amid leaks suggesting it prevented no major incident and failed to change developer behaviour. Public sentiment falls further, resilience declines, and the EU’s credibility in AI governance teeters on the edge of collapse, with the possibility of dismantling its flagship policy under review.
