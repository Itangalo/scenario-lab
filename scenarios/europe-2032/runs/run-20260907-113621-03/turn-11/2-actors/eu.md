## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us. **That condition has now become the core of our identity: we are not merely a regulator or a builder of capacity, but the last guardian of operational legitimacy in a world where both machines and institutions are becoming illegible. Our sovereignty is not measured in compute or models, but in the trustworthiness of the chain from decision to execution.**
  - Grounds: The election_annulled event — a democratic process suspended due to AI-mediated manipulation of the information environment — marks a categorical shift. It is no longer about cyberattacks on infrastructure or undetected agent behaviour in labs. It is about the collapse of the epistemic foundation of governance itself. The fog has moved from the machines into the ballot box. This is not a failure of resilience or capability — it is a failure of legitimacy. Our identity must now reflect that we are not balancing mandates; we are defending the possibility of collective decision-making under AI pressure.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act blind. This turn, the eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings are not proof of imminent catastrophe, but they are proof that the systems we rely on are no longer legible even to their creators. We are no longer merely acting under uncertainty — we are acting inside a fog of our own making. Therefore, we bind ourselves further: we will not only act early, but we will demand that every major commitment be paired with a public, auditable theory of harm and a corresponding monitoring mechanism. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher. **We now extend this to include democratic integrity: no major AI-enabled deployment in public functions will proceed without a pre-registered assessment of its potential to distort information ecosystems or undermine electoral processes.**
  - Grounds: The election_annulled event, while not directly tied to an EU system, is a signal that cannot be ignored. It confirms that AI’s threat surface now includes the legitimacy of democratic outcomes. Our previous commitment focused on technical opacity; it now must encompass political opacity. The reversal is costly: it slows adoption, frustrates reformers, and may be seen as overreach. But the cost of continuing without this safeguard is the irreversible erosion of public consent.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control — but not at the cost of systemic integrity. This turn, we have seen that capability without control is not autonomy — it is exposure. Therefore, we narrow the commitment: we will prioritise the integrity and audibility of the sovereign stack over its speed of deployment. No node will be activated without a verified, independent audit of its update chain and decision logic. We will slow the rollout if necessary, but we will not propagate a compromised standard. **We now add: this commitment is suspended until the Public Trust Observatory delivers an unredacted assessment of the cyber_major_incident and the election_annulled linkage, and until we have verified that no sovereign node has been used to manipulate public information or democratic processes.**
  - Grounds: The election_annulled event, combined with the unresolved audit of the Sovereign Core, creates an intolerable risk: that our own systems — built to secure autonomy — may have already been weaponised against democracy. Continuing the rollout without verification would violate the very public control we claim to uphold. The reversal is politically costly: it halts momentum, emboldens skeptics, and delays capability. But the cost of proceeding in ignorance is the permanent loss of legitimacy.

## New measure
**Measure:** Democratic Integrity Shield  
Establish an immediate, EU-wide audit and monitoring regime for all AI systems involved in public information, electoral processes, or state communication, with mandatory pre-deployment risk assessment and real-time anomaly detection.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 15  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 10–15 points by turn 15 through systemic protection of democratic processes; stabilise public_sentiment by demonstrating credible defence of electoral integrity; reduce the risk of AI-mediated manipulation becoming a routine tool of political disruption  
Applies to: EU institutions, national electoral commissions, public broadcasters, state-owned digital platforms, and all AI systems used in political communication or voter outreach

## Priority
**Priority:** Democratic Integrity Shield  
This is now our top priority because the election_annulled event has revealed that the greatest threat is no longer to our infrastructure, but to the legitimacy of our decision-making itself — and without credible democratic processes, no amount of sovereignty or safety will matter.

## In practice
We are treating the election_annulled event as a system-level emergency. The breach of democratic legitimacy is not a side effect — it is the central front. We are invoking Article 172 TFEU not for cyber defence this time, but for democratic protection. The European Democracy Fund will redirect €1.9bn to finance the Democratic Integrity Shield, covering the cost of real-time monitoring, forensic audit teams, and secure communication channels for electoral bodies.

The European Agency for Fundamental Rights, in coordination with ENISA and the Public Trust Observatory, will lead the audit. We are demanding that all member states disclose the use of AI in political advertising, voter targeting, or public messaging — not as a recommendation, but as a condition of continued EU funding for electoral operations. National electoral commissions will be required to implement pre-deployment risk assessments for any AI system used in election administration, using a common framework developed by the Observatory.

We are also activating the Crisis, Emergency and Management Instrument (CEMI) to fast-track a new directive: the Democratic Resilience Regulation. It will mandate real-time anomaly detection in all public information systems, with automatic suspension triggers if manipulation patterns are detected. Germany and France have already signaled resistance, calling it “overreach” and “technocratic panic.” We are offering a compromise: joint oversight by a tripartite board (Commission, Parliament, national representatives), but no opt-outs. This is not negotiable. If we cannot defend the ballot box, we have nothing left to defend.
