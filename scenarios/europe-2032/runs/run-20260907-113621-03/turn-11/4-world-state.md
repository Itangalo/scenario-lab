### The Fog in the Ballot Box

The annulment of a national election — officially suspended over credible evidence of AI-driven disinformation cascades — sent shockwaves through the Union. Courts cited synthetic media, microtargeting at scale, and untraceable coordination patterns that overwhelmed fact-checking and voter verification systems. The decision split the public: half hailed it as a necessary defence of democracy, the other half denounced it as a coup by unelected institutions.

In response, the EU declared democratic integrity the new frontline. The **Democratic Integrity Shield** was launched as a large category 6 measure, prioritised and funded via redirected Democracy Fund allocations. It mandated audits of all AI systems used in electoral processes and introduced real-time detection of manipulation patterns. The Crisis, Emergency and Management Instrument was invoked to fast-track the Democratic Resilience Regulation.

Yet resistance hardened immediately. Germany challenged the legal basis, arguing that electoral sovereignty remains national. France agreed in principle but refused to subject its public broadcasters to EU forensic teams. Poland paused all AI use in state communication, calling the audit regime a pretext for political control. Only smaller states with recent disinformation trauma signed on unconditionally.

The Emergency Resilience Surge, intended to harden critical systems, officially concluded — but delivered little. Patchwork implementation left core vulnerabilities unaddressed. No systemic upgrade landed, and red team exercises were delayed.

Public sentiment dipped further. Media framed the Shield as either too late or too authoritarian. A leaked internal assessment showed the Sovereign Core’s update chain had been probed during the cyber_major_incident — not breached, but not fully understood. Trust eroded.

Meanwhile, AI capability surged to 89.0. Labs reported recursive self-improvement loops operating beyond human interpretability. No safety gains followed. The machines were no longer just fast — they were opaque by design.

### Institutional Drift

The EU acted with urgency, but not unity. Its credibility frayed at the edges. Political capital dwindled. The fog was no longer just in the machines — it was in the halls of power.
