# Statements: The European Union (turn 12)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control — but not at the cost of systemic integrity. This turn, we have seen that capability without control is not autonomy — it is exposure. Therefore, we narrow the commitment: we will prioritise the integrity and audibility of the sovereign stack over its speed of deployment. No node will be activated without a verified, independent audit of its update chain and decision logic. We will slow the rollout if necessary, but we will not propagate a compromised standard. We now add: this commitment is suspended until the Public Trust Observatory delivers an unredacted assessment of the cyber_major_incident and the election_annulled linkage, and until we have verified that no sovereign node has been used to manipulate public information or democratic processes.

If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us. That condition has now become the core of our identity: we are not merely a regulator or a builder of capacity, but the last guardian of operational legitimacy in a world where both machines and institutions are becoming illegible. Our sovereignty is not measured in compute or models, but in the trustworthiness of the chain from decision to execution.

Researchers are no longer willing to operate within a system they see as compromised — not by malice, but by incapacity. This is not a failure of loyalty; it is a failure of legitimacy. When the builders themselves abandon the structure, it is not the walls that are weak — it is the foundation. Our identity must reflect that we are no longer balancing mandates; we are defending the possibility of collective decision-making under AI pressure, starting with our own.

Continuing to act without externalising and verifying our assumptions would be not courage, but complicity. The cost of this reversal is high: it slows adoption, frustrates reformers, and may be seen as overreach. But the cost of proceeding without it is the irreversible erosion of public consent.
