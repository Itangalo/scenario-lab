## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us. That condition has now become the core of our identity: we are not merely a regulator or a builder of capacity, but the last guardian of operational legitimacy in a world where both machines and institutions are becoming illegible. Our sovereignty is not measured in compute or models, but in the trustworthiness of the chain from decision to execution.  
  - Grounds: The emergent_researcher_desertion_wave confirms that even within our own institutions, the epistemic chain has broken. Researchers are no longer willing to operate within a system they see as compromised — not by malice, but by incapacity. This is not a failure of loyalty; it is a failure of legitimacy. When the builders themselves abandon the structure, it is not the walls that are weak — it is the foundation. Our identity must reflect that we are no longer balancing mandates; we are defending the possibility of collective decision-making under AI pressure, starting with our own.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act blind. This turn, the eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings are not proof of imminent catastrophe, but they are proof that the systems we rely on are no longer legible even to their creators. We are no longer merely acting under uncertainty — we are acting inside a fog of our own making. Therefore, we bind ourselves further: we will not only act early, but we will demand that every major commitment be paired with a public, auditable theory of harm and a corresponding monitoring mechanism. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher. We now extend this to include democratic integrity: no major AI-enabled deployment in public functions will proceed without a pre-registered assessment of its potential to distort information ecosystems or undermine electoral processes.  
  - Grounds: The emergent_researcher_desertion_wave is not an isolated incident — it is a signal that our internal control systems are failing. When researchers publish unredacted tools because they no longer trust institutional oversight, it means the very mechanism we rely on to manage uncertainty has itself become untrustworthy. Continuing to act without externalising and verifying our assumptions would be not courage, but complicity. The cost of this reversal is high: it slows adoption, frustrates reformers, and may be seen as overreach. But the cost of proceeding without it is the irreversible erosion of public consent.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control — but not at the cost of systemic integrity. This turn, we have seen that capability without control is not autonomy — it is exposure. Therefore, we narrow the commitment: we will prioritise the integrity and audibility of the sovereign stack over its speed of deployment. No node will be activated without a verified, independent audit of its update chain and decision logic. We will slow the rollout if necessary, but we will not propagate a compromised standard. We now add: this commitment is suspended until the Public Trust Observatory delivers an unredacted assessment of the cyber_major_incident and the election_annulled linkage, and until we have verified that no sovereign node has been used to manipulate public information or democratic processes.  
  - Trigger: emergent_researcher_desertion_wave  
  - Grounds: The desertion of leading researchers — not to foreign powers, but to independent action — reveals a catastrophic failure of internal trust. If those closest to the systems no longer believe they can be controlled, then continuing to build them under EU jurisdiction is not an act of sovereignty, but of self-deception. The commitment to independent advancement cannot stand while the people who would build it have abandoned faith in its governance. The reversal is politically costly: it halts momentum, emboldens skeptics, and delays capability. But the cost of proceeding in ignorance is the permanent loss of legitimacy.

## In practice
We are treating the emergent_researcher_desertion_wave as a system-level rupture. When those who build the systems abandon them, it is not a personnel issue — it is a legitimacy crisis. We are therefore activating the European Data Protection Supervisor, in coordination with the European Anti-Fraud Office and the Public Trust Observatory, to lead a binding audit of all EU-operated AI systems. This will include full access to training data logs, update histories, and internal decision records. The findings will be published in redacted form, with unredacted versions delivered to the European Parliament and the Council.

We are invoking Article 287 TFEU to establish a legal basis for mandatory transparency, overriding national objections on grounds of institutional integrity. The audit will be conducted by a newly convened Independent AI Accountability Panel, composed of external experts, civil society representatives, and former lab leads who have not been involved in sovereign projects. We are offering member states a choice: full cooperation, or suspension of EU funding for AI development and deployment until compliance is verified.

Germany, France, and Poland have already signaled resistance, calling it "internal surveillance" and "a breach of operational confidentiality." We are offering a compromise: phased disclosure, with high-risk components reviewed first, and a joint oversight board to approve redactions. But we will not accept opt-outs. If we cannot audit our own systems, we have no right to demand audits of others. This is not about punishment — it is about proof. The fog must be lifted from within.
