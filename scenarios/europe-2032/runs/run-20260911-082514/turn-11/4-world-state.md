### The table set elsewhere
Washington and Beijing announced a limited risk pact in autumn — weights security, escalation guardrails, curbs on a class of biological design tools — with thin verification attached. Brussels learned the outlines after the text was settled. The offer to plug in pooled telemetry and Shield logs earned polite interest and a technical working slot, not a seat.

At home that landed as another humiliation. Ministers had sold the liaison as a route back to relevance and to chip quotas. What returned was observer paperwork.

### Licences tighten
At the same time export controls tightened again, and this time the rationing reached allies. Volume licences for advanced chips were cut, review queues lengthened, and Dutch, Japanese and Korean counterparts told Brussels quota relief would wait for binding controls and for the new superpower verification to bed in.

The co-location tranche kept triage pilots alive, but procurement officers quietly stretched replacement cycles and hospitals delayed orders. Talk of catching up from second-tier builders grew louder.

### What still held
The joint cyber command link held. Autumn probes against municipalities and clinics were again isolated in hours, and auditors again credited shared alarms. Nurses praised audited triage sites that cut waiting lists, and liability cover held for those sites alone.

But the wider freeze stayed. Benefits and policing support systems remained capped, finance stayed out, and coverage dwelt on lawful harm. Repair bought forgiveness in cooperating towns, not momentum.

Brussels ended the year inside the watch floor, outside the accord room, and shorter on chips — protected, uninvited, and visibly dependent.
