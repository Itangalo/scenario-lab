### Containment semester
Autumn began with sirens, not summits. A modified pathogen — assembled with help from openly available design tools — sickened dozens in two member states before referral labs flagged it. Containment ran for weeks: wards sealed, sequencing pooled through the health emergency authority, stockpiled countermeasures released. Methodologists no longer debated preprints. Casualties were counted on evening news.

As hospitals triaged, a largely automated ransomware sweep rolled across municipal services, clinics and a water utility's control network. Backups failed in places; queues lengthened. Emergency cyber teams isolated systems and restored core care, catching some intrusions faster where hardening had landed, but defenders were visibly behind the tooling. Attribution remained open.

Then, at short notice, European hospitals, ministries and firms found the leading American model unreachable — accounts suspended by user nationality, no reason given, no appeal. Triage helpers, coding assistants and procurement tools built on it stalled. Showcase sites fell back to older helpers and paper protocols. The capital with its separate supply contract kept limited access, straining the pact against undercutting to breaking point.

Brussels ran the semester as civil protection. Health and interior ministers gave legal cover for detection, lab triage and network isolation; test kits moved through working triage points. The factory water-power compacts stayed in draft, diggers taped off. No new build was started.

Amid the damage, two quieter facts landed. Warehouses and a few hospitals took delivery of boxy carrier robots for fixed transport and pharmacy runs — useful inside controlled corridors, useless outside them. And a European-American materials result, AI-designed catalysts cutting a chemical step's cost, drew specialist praise but no public notice.

By December, containment held and services limped on. The public had learned three things at once: dangerous knowledge could not be recalled, defences bent but did not break, and Europe's most-used intelligence could be switched off from abroad.
