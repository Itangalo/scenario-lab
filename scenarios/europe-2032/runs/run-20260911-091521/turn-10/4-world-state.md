### The recipe that would not go away
In February a preprint claimed a genome model had helped produce a viable design for a human-infecting organism. Methodologists attacked the paper within days, and editors argued over whether even publishing the critique spread too much. The fight stayed inside journals and closed briefings, but health ministries read it as categorically different from previous warnings.

Weeks later a frontier-class open release landed. Hundreds of thousands of downloads in the first week put that capability onto private machines for good. European labs confirmed the weights ran the biosecurity-relevant tricks as advertised. Recall was no longer a concept.

### Screening without building
Brussels did not fight the building freezes. The Commission launched a civil-protection shield instead: pooled sequencing screening, referral-lab triage rules, stockpiled response through the health emergency authority and disease centre, and emergency hardening of hospital and water-system controls by the cybersecurity agency using the continuity teams.

Hospitals liked the money. Regions with working triage sites became distribution points for test kits. Elsewhere nurses asked why screening was arriving while locum funding had ended and wards were still thinly staffed.

The water-and-power compacts for the frozen factory sites stayed in draft. Lawyers shuttled between two capitals and the courts; diggers stayed taped off. Washington repeated sympathy. The capital with its separate supply arrangement kept buying outside the common line, and the pact against undercutting held only because no one tested it.

By June, screening coverage had widened and a few intrusion attempts on hospital networks were caught faster. But routine care still queued, the two concrete shells still stood empty under vigil candles, and the public learned two facts at once: the dangerous knowledge was now unrecallable, and Europe's own capacity to replace what it had lost was still stopped concrete.
