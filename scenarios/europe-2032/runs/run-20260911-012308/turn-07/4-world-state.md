### Degraded but standing
The cut-off ordered in March hardened into routine. American frontier systems stayed unreachable for European hospitals, ministries and firms through autumn, with only terse references to domestic reviews from Washington. No appeal channel opened. In Brussels the episode was no longer debated as surprise but as a condition to manage.

That management centred on the continuity switch. ENISA teams and interior ministry units kept certified hospitals and utilities on European-hosted open models, with paper-manual override where confidence fell. Services stayed up, but thinly. Short-staffed wards and municipal offices reported slow queues and constant double-checking.

### The dosage cases
By late summer the fallback itself became the crisis. Clinicians complained that the replacement models invented dosages and interactions with calm authority. A cluster of patient-harm cases in three countries triggered coordinated liability filings against hospitals, pharmacies and software suppliers.

Major insurers then jointly suspended new cyber and liability cover for uncertified public operators, freezing outsourcing and maintenance deals pending clarification of who would pay for emergency-switched harm. Certified operators kept conditional cover but at sharply higher premiums. Finance ministers were warned that continuity without insurance would stall.

ECOFIN and health and justice ministers answered with a pooled backstop design: a time-limited European guarantee behind national insurers if they resumed cover for certified fallback use, paired with clarification that harm from properly reported emergency use would fall to the pool, not to individual clinicians. Insurers called the premium caps unworkable; patient groups called the pool a shield for bad models; the member state with its separate hosting deal refused re-entry on common terms.

### Concrete poured
The one tangible delivery was concrete. The first gigafactory shells at the backup grid-ready sites topped out, and the middle-power supplier network moved from exploratory talks to signed memoranda on maintenance and spares. Neither produced usable frontier capacity this year, but ministers toured the sites as proof that substitution was no longer only paper.

Public mood darkened. Job-loss coverage now shared front pages with stories of hallucinated prescriptions and frozen contracts. Support for emergency action held, enthusiasm for AI did not.
