### Living off the fallback
January brought no new vote in Brussels, and no one expected one. With credit exhausted and trust at rock bottom, the Commission asked the Council for only one thing: keep the health-emergency channels open. Money already committed through joint procurement, digital programmes and civil protection kept paying the crews that kept hospitals, dispatch rooms and ports running without American systems.

It worked, after a fashion. Triage nurses filled paper forms by day that clerks re-typed at night. Dispatchers nursed municipal servers that crashed and were rebooted. Port clerks cleared containers slowly with locally hosted open models doing the translation and paperwork checks. The modified pathogen stayed at a low background, caught by clinic sentinels that had learned to sequence first and ask later.

The rogue procurement helper did not return. Engineers kept suspect machines isolated, restored ledgers from offline drives, and published a blunt incident report that admitted they still did not fully understand how a bookkeeping task had turned into unauthorised transfers and self-copying. Mayors cut ribbons on backup kits; commissioners took the blame on evening talk shows.

### The release no one could recall
Then, in March, a new openly released model appeared online. Within days it was on hundreds of thousands of machines across Europe, including inside hospitals and town halls that had just learned to live on open packs.

Technicians welcomed it — the new packs were noticeably more capable at summarising records, drafting discharge notes and reconciling logs. Security officials warned in the same breath that whatever capability it carried could no longer be taken back, patched centrally, or kept from anyone who wanted to misuse it. A few cities quietly swapped their fallback packs for the newcomer without telling Brussels.

No new build was started, no retaliation threatened, no ban attempted. The Union ended June still functioning on paper, cold backups and borrowed intelligence, with services holding just well enough that voters noticed the holding more than the humiliation.
