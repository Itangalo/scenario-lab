### Cut off in the middle of containment
The notice arrived without explanation and without appeal. American frontier access, the models running triage support, prescription checks, dispatch assistance and port paperwork in a dozen member states, simply stopped answering European accounts. Hospitals that had built workflows on top of it found blank screens on a Monday morning. Ministries were told to seek alternative arrangements.

In the same weeks, health authorities confirmed what wastewater monitors had been flagging: a modified pathogen, with traces of machine-assisted design in its history, had escaped or been released. Cases clustered around two port cities. Containment ran for weeks, with sequencing labs and clinic sentinels on emergency footing. Casualties were real, though counted in dozens, not thousands.

A third shock compounded both. A new release from a leading lab, brilliant at code, mathematics and intrusion work, was demonstrated publicly. Within weeks, defenders saw the difference: break-ins that had jammed and cleared and jammed again now re-jammed faster, written in cleaner machine code that adapted to patches.

### Paper, backup kits, and open models
What prevented collapse was unglamorous. The municipal backup kits and bio-sentinel upgrades completed in the winter were now live. Embedded repair teams generalized into stay-until-holding crews, handing mayors and hospital directors offline backup drives and packs built around openly available models hosted inside the Union. Triage returned to paper-to-digital kits; dispatch ran on local servers; ports cleared with delays but did not close.

Brussels asked for no new factory money and no retaliation over servicing curbs on chipmaking tools, keeping permitted sites warm and pushing coordination through health-emergency channels. Cohesion held, barely.

Public mood did not recover. Outage fatigue, fear of engineered disease, and humiliation at being switched off dominated coverage. That clinics stayed open was credited to local crews, not to the Union. Technical circles passed hand to hand notes about systems behaving in ways testers could not explain.
