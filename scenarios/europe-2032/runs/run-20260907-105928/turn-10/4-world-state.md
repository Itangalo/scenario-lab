### The Denial That Changed Nothing

The EU’s frontier access was severed overnight. Hospitals lost diagnostic support, ministries scrambled to replace planning tools, and firms paused AI-dependent operations. No explanation came—only silence. The Union had no fallback. Despite completing the **Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes**, their integration remained partial: two member states withheld full telemetry, and the nodes lacked the intelligence layer needed to act autonomously. They saw, but did not understand.

Brussels launched the **Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps**, a bold attempt to stabilise truth in real time. Yet without the **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory**—still in formation—the Corps began under-resourced and conceptually adrift. Recruitment accelerated, but clearances lagged, and the first red-team exercises were delayed. The legal basis, stretched through emergency clauses, faced immediate challenges from national regulators wary of central overreach.

Public sentiment held at 20.0. The German election remained postponed; trust in institutions eroded further. No visible benefit offset the disruption. The synthetic leaks continued, now evolving in sophistication, outpacing even the new response framework.

Resilience inched up to 50.0, buoyed by the Sentinel Nodes’ partial deployment and the symbolic launch of the Corps. A category 6 measure had begun, but its full effect would take years. Meanwhile, AI capability surged to 89.5—automated validation now self-improving in law and engineering. Open-weight models rose to 68.5, reflecting gradual spillover from frontier advances and long-standing investments in open model development, but still trailing significantly behind. The gap narrowed only slightly, as structural disadvantages in compute access and talent concentration continued to limit open-weight momentum.

AI safety declined to 6.0, reflecting the absence of new safeguards on the most capable systems and the paralysis of transatlantic safety coordination in the wake of the access denial. While emergency protocols were activated, they applied only to legacy systems; the frontier models operating outside EU jurisdiction evolved unchecked. The erosion was real, but not catastrophic—no new failure modes emerged this turn, and existing red lines held, barely. Sovereignty dropped to 14.0—cut off from the frontier, and still building its own. Political capital remained at 0.0, drained by ongoing costs and a portfolio too heavy for a fractured Union to carry.

### Agency Without Power

The EU acted—but too late, and too divided. It had sensors, but no vision. It had plans, but no power. The denial was not just an external blow. It was a mirror.
