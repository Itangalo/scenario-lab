## In practice  
We are operationalising the European AI Emergency Response Corps as of this turn. With the measure now finished, ENISA assumes permanent coordination authority, backed by binding emergency powers under Article 122a TFEU. Cross-border teams in Berlin, Marseille, and Warsaw are fully staffed and equipped with access protocols to sovereign compute clusters and real-time data feeds from national CERTs. Legal resistance in two member states has been overcome through joint funding agreements and the establishment of a unified command under the ECDC-ENISA Joint AI Incident Cell. This is no longer a contingency—it is a standing capability. But it is blind without the Sentinel Nodes.

That is why we are accelerating the Sentinel Nodes into deployment. The European Research Council, in partnership with IMEC and Fraunhofer, has finalised the first agent suite for deployment at EuroHPC sites in Jülich and CINECA. These agents are not passive monitors; they are trained to detect self-modification signatures, unauthorised replication attempts, and anomalous planning trajectories in real time. They will run continuously inside licensed high-risk systems, feeding alerts to the Emergency Response Corps and to national regulators. Funding is secured through the Digital Europe Programme and the new AI deployment levy, and we are fast-tracking legal authorisation via the AI Act’s emergency amendment clause.

At the same time, we are acting on the open-weight frontier. The emergent_open_assurance_network has created a de facto standard—we will now make it a regulatory one. The Mandatory Open-Weight Risk Labelling measure will be proposed under the AI Act’s conformity assessment framework, with the European Standardisation Organisations (CEN, CENELEC, ETSI) tasked to formalise the label within six months. Accredited evaluators—including national labs and members of the Open Assurance Network—will issue labels based on adversarial testing results. No model above 45.0 capability enters the EU market without one. This is not a ban. It is a signal: if you release, you are responsible, and the public will know what you’ve released.
