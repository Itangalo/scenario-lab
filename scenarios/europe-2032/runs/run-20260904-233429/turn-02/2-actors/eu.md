## New measure  
**Critical Infrastructure AI Resilience Programme**  
A coordinated, EU-wide hardening of detection, response, and fallback protocols for essential services against AI-generated cyber threats, with mandatory audits and live exercises.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to +18 to resilience by turn 5; begins reversing the erosion of public and institutional confidence after the recent intrusion; establishes the European Cyber Resilience Board as a standing authority with cross-sector mandate  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with binding requirements on operators of essential services in energy, water, transport, health, and digital infrastructure

## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because despite the cyber incident and collapsing investment, the root vulnerability remains our dependence on foreign-controlled compute; without binding leverage over capacity on our soil, we cannot secure, audit, or allocate AI power in a crisis, and the directive is now the only instrument that forces the hyperscalers to internalise EU strategic needs.

## In practice  
We are acting on the understanding that compute is not just infrastructure—it is strategic terrain. This turn, the Commission advances the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive through trilogue negotiations with the Council and Parliament, anchoring it in Article 114 TFEU to assert internal market coherence over bilateral concessions. We are offering Germany and Ireland a tripartite compromise: fast-track access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund for compliant operators, a liability shield for AI systems used in good faith under the directive, and an independent scheduling body—EDIC—to manage the reserved 30% as a public utility, auctioned transparently to prevent political capture. The goal is not to seize control, but to establish a right of first refusal on compute deployed in our market, just as we do with spectrum or rail capacity.

At the same time, the Tech Sovereignty Package moves from concept to site selection: we are finalising AI Acceleration Zones in Spain, Poland, and Finland, where permitting is capped at six months and grid access guaranteed within 18 months. These zones will host both Gigafactory builds and sovereign compute nodes under the directive. But the recent cyber intrusion—AI-coordinated, undetected, and only contained by accident—has shattered any illusion that sovereignty alone is defence. That is why we are launching the Critical Infrastructure AI Resilience Programme: by turn 5, every essential operator must implement AI-driven anomaly detection with human-supervised fallbacks, undergo biannual red-team exercises modelled on the Kimi K3 attack vector, and report incidents to a newly empowered European Cyber Resilience Board. This is not optional resilience—it is the baseline for operating in the new era.
