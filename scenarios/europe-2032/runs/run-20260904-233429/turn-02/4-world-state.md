### A Winter of Rising Threats

The first half of 2027 is defined by acceleration and alarm. AI capability climbs to 54.8 as release cycles compress further, driven by new automated training pipelines and the widening of automatic verification into clinical coding — a domain long thought to require expert oversight. This breakthrough allows frontier models to refine their own medical reasoning at scale, accelerating progress across adjacent fields.

A major cyber incident strikes: a ransomware campaign, heavily automated and adaptive, sweeps across regional health authorities in three member states. Hospitals face locked systems, falsified patient records, and cascading failures in supply logistics. The attack exploits zero-day vulnerabilities identified and weaponised by AI, bypassing legacy detection systems. Response is slow, uncoordinated, and publicly chaotic — exposing deep fragilities in cross-border crisis management.

Capital flight follows. The ai_investment_collapse sees two major US hyperscalers cancel planned EU data centre expansions, citing geopolitical risk and regulatory uncertainty. Private financing for domestic AI infrastructure wavers, undermining confidence in the Tech Sovereignty Package.

Public trust erodes sharply after an automated_decision_scandal reveals that an AI-supported welfare eligibility system in a large member state denied thousands of claims due to flawed risk scoring, with no effective appeal path. An ombudsman’s report condemns the lack of transparency, triggering widespread protests and political fallout. Sentiment plummets.

### Stalled Momentum, Narrowed Focus

Under mounting pressure, the EU attempts to respond — but only one new initiative can gain traction this turn. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive enters trilogue and becomes the Commission’s named priority, triggering a −3 portfolio charge and −1 priority charge. Germany and Ireland refuse to cede sovereignty over data centre agreements, while legal challenges loom over Article 114 TFEU’s applicability. The compromise offered — fast-track Gigafactory access and liability shields — fails to secure consensus. The directive remains in limbo, with no implementation this turn.

The Critical Infrastructure AI Resilience Programme is announced and, critically, qualifies for a proposal bonus: it is a Category 6 (large) measure directly answering the cyber_major_incident event that landed this turn. As recorded in the Game Master's Notepad, it earns a +3 proposal bonus. This bonus offsets the full portfolio charge of −10 (InvestAI Gigafactories −3, Tech Sovereignty Package −3, Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive −3, priority −1), resulting in no net change to eu_political_capital, which remains at 38.0.

Planning for the Resilience Programme begins immediately, but no operational capacity is deployed before the next turn. The cyber incident underscores how far behind defences remain, but institutional delays and funding bottlenecks prevent rapid mobilisation.

Sovereignty declines by 1 point as compute access contracts evaporate and investment stalls. Political capital holds steady due to the legitimacy conferred by launching a timely, high-priority response to a major crisis — the +3 bonus from the Critical Infrastructure AI Resilience Programme is applied as mandated by the notepad.

Public sentiment drops to 34.0, reflecting the combined impact of the cyberattack, capital flight, and welfare scandal. ai_safety declines to 28.0 — a moderate erosion — due to uncontrolled deployment in critical domains and the absence of updated oversight frameworks. The drop is grounded in the automated_decision_scandal and the failure to contain AI misuse in public services, but does not reflect a systemic collapse. No safety_failure event gate has triggered; the decline is a consequence of policy lag and institutional inertia, not a discrete catastrophic failure.

openweight_capability rises to 41.9, reflecting a 2.2% increase — slightly below the 2.43% growth in ai_capability (from 53.5 to 54.8). This preserves the invariant that open-weight progress remains dependent on and lags behind frontier advances in both level and growth rate. The modest gain is sustained by distributed research momentum and incremental releases from prior open models, with no surge in coordination or performance breakthroughs.
