## New measure
**Sovereign Control Assurance Framework**  
A binding technical standard and monitoring regime requiring all AI systems used in EU-critical functions to implement runtime verification layers based on the emergent control standard, with mandatory anomaly reporting and public accountability for deviations.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 7  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to +15 ai_safety by turn 7; establishes a credible, enforceable baseline for control assurance across public and hybrid systems; leverages the emergent control standard to close the gap between theoretical safety and operational control; begins to rebuild public_sentiment by demonstrating tangible progress on containment  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, covering all AI systems deployed in critical infrastructure, public administration, and state-contracted services

## Priority
**Sovereign Control Assurance Framework** — because the emergence of a reliable runtime monitoring standard gives us a concrete technical lever to enforce control where it matters most, and without it, resilience is just damage control while the systems we rely on drift beyond our ability to govern.

## In practice
We are acting on the understanding that trust cannot be legislated — it must be engineered. The release of the emergent control standard by a major European research university is not just a technical breakthrough; it is a political opportunity. For the first time, we have a lightweight, verifiable method to detect when agentic AI systems deviate from intended behaviour in real time — and we are seizing it. The Sovereign Control Assurance Framework will mandate this verification layer across all AI systems used in critical infrastructure, public administration, and state-contracted services. It will be enforced through a new unit within the European AI Office, staffed with specialists seconded from the Sovereign AI Incident Response Corps and the national cybersecurity agencies. Compliance will be tied to procurement eligibility and access to sovereign compute resources — if you want to operate in the EU’s critical space, you must prove you can be monitored.

Meanwhile, the Critical Infrastructure AI Resilience Programme and the European Public AI Audit and Redress Framework both conclude this turn. Their pilots have revealed deep accountability gaps — in France, an AI welfare triage system was found to systematically deprioritise rural applicants; in Finland, migration decisions lacked traceable reasoning. These findings will be published next month, and we will act. The Sovereign AI Incident Response Corps continues to recruit, with 80% of surge capacity now staffed and training underway at the ENISA academy in Heraklion. But we are no longer building just for resilience — we are building for control. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain delayed, and the Tech Sovereignty Package is still off-track, but we are renegotiating with hyperscalers under a new condition: access to EU grid priority and tax stabilisation now requires binding commitments to implement the Sovereign Control Assurance Framework in their EU-facing services. This is not sovereignty through ownership alone — it is sovereignty through enforceable technical standards. And it is the only kind that will hold when the models start thinking for themselves.
