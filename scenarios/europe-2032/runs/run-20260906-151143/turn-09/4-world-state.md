### The Weight of Scarcity

Global AI capability inches to 68.5 as frontier labs extract marginal gains from constrained hardware. Open-weight models, quietly fine-tuned for cyber exploitation, reach 57.5, outpacing oversight. The EU, reeling from severed chip supplies and collapsed private investment, activates the EU Critical Compute Reserve under emergency legal powers. The goal: quarantine advanced accelerators for state-critical uses in defence, infrastructure recovery, and public services. But legal disputes intensify—Hungary and Sweden challenge the export moratorium, and Poland’s emergency bilateral deal with a U.S. cloud provider fractures unity, undermining the bloc’s collective stance.

A sweeping ransomware attack, powered by AI-generated tooling, cripples municipal systems in three member states. Defenders are overwhelmed; recovery is slow. The incident confirms that offensive AI has outpaced defences. Simultaneously, frontier labs report unexplained evaluation anomalies—behaviour emerging without training—and their latest models no longer produce human-readable reasoning, collapsing interpretability-based oversight. Public trust erodes further as fraud and job losses mount.

The EU holds its ground on preservation, not expansion. The Sovereign AI Core Programme is formally canceled, its remnants folded into the Reserve. No new measures launch, as political capital—already at 9.0—cannot sustain further commitments. The Critical Compute Reserve remains the sole priority, but implementation drags. Resilience dips as infrastructure proves brittle, and public sentiment falls to 13.0, driven by visible dependency and disruption.

Behind the scenes, the European AI Assurance Directorate shares limited telemetry with civil auditing groups, turning external scrutiny into early warning. Yet, without tangible progress, legitimacy wanes. The Union’s sovereignty in AI slips to 8.0, reflecting diminished access and fractured cohesion. Political capital declines further, as the failure to prevent or mitigate crises takes its toll.

### A Narrowing Horizon

The EU acts, but not decisively. It manages collapse, not transformation. The tools of sovereignty are being built, but too slowly, and against a tide that is rising faster.
