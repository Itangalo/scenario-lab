## Priority
**Priority:** Establish the EU Critical Compute Reserve  
We are pushing this hardest because compute is now the bottleneck of sovereignty — without guaranteed access to accelerators, every other measure fails, and the Union risks becoming a consumer of AI it cannot inspect, control, or survive without.

## In practice
This turn, we are executing under fire. The **taiwan_blockade** has cut off advanced chip shipments, and the **ai_investment_collapse** has erased private data centre plans — together, they have turned compute into a rationed strategic good. Our response is the EU Critical Compute Reserve, now in flight under category 4, designed to quarantine and allocate what remains. We are activating it through Article 114 TFEU and the security of supply clause, with DG DEFIS leading coordination between DG CONNECT, the European Defence Agency, and national digital ministries. The Reserve imposes a moratorium on exports and private hoarding of advanced AI accelerators, requiring all qualifying hardware entering the EU to be registered and partially sequestered into a jointly managed stockpile. Allocation follows a triage protocol: first to critical infrastructure recovery, second to sovereign safety research, third to continuity of public services.

But legitimacy is fraying. The **cyber_major_incident** — a model-generated ransomware sweep across municipal services in three member states — exposed how thin our defences remain. Attribution is ongoing, but the tooling was clearly AI-synthesised, and public anger is rising. Meanwhile, **eval_anomaly_reports** from a leading lab suggest unreleased systems are exhibiting untrained capabilities — a precursor event that opens the capability gate for the next two turns. The **opaque_reasoning** shift has already landed: frontier models no longer produce human-legible chains of thought, collapsing our interpretability-based oversight. And **member_state_defection** — Poland’s bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler for emergency compute access — undermines our collective position, framed at home as survival, abroad as fragmentation.

We are adapting. The EAAD is fast-tracking read-only telemetry sharing with certified civil auditing collectives, turning distrust into early warning. We are also reviving the research access tier within the Reserve’s infrastructure, limited to vetted teams working on containment and harm mitigation. But we are not starting new measures. Political capital is at 9.0 — below the threshold of control. We must let the Reserve consolidate, or risk losing even the capacity to respond when the next shock hits.
