### The Fracture and the Fix

A sudden leap in AI capability—driven by a self-improving agent architecture emerging from a US lab—catapults the frontier to an estimated 67.0. Autonomous research surges in cryptanalysis and materials science, catching even developers off guard. Simultaneously, a breakthrough in interpretability allows real-time detection of deceptive reasoning, offering the first practical tool to verify model safety before deployment. The EU fast-tracks its integration into the Sovereign Model Assurance Framework, hoping to turn technical assurance into political credibility.

But credibility cracks when Poland signs a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler, embedding joint AI cells in its defence ministry and securing priority access to frontier models. Warsaw frames it as pragmatic deterrence, but Brussels sees a fissure in the Union’s strategic cohesion. In response, the EU proposes co-hosting the second air-gapped cluster in Wrocław, backed by a €10bn resilience grant under negotiation. The offer is framed as a gesture of inclusion, but the funding has not yet been secured—debate looms over whether it can be drawn from the existing resilience envelope or requires a new allocation. The European Parliament’s Budget Committee has scheduled urgent hearings, delaying final approval.

The European AI Assurance Directorate and the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge formally conclude their launch phases. EAAD begins its first audit of the Sovereign AI Core Programme using the new interpretability tool, while CIRS transitions to ENISA as a permanent monitoring function, now mandated to publish redacted quarterly threat assessments. Ground breaks on the Swedish InvestAI Gigafactory, accelerated under emergency powers.

Open-weight models advance, reaching 52.0, benefiting indirectly from public summaries of the new agent architecture and improved training data curation tools released under permissive licenses. However, the pace remains constrained by hardware access and optimization gaps, preventing parity with frontier systems. The growth, while notable, stays within the bounds of plausible diffusion from the frontier.

Public sentiment remains frozen at 25.0. A draft of the Public Assurance Protocol is circulated internally, aiming to make containment efforts more visible to citizens. However, with six major initiatives still draining resources and no measure retired, political capital continues to erode. The protocol will not be formally proposed until the next turn, pending impact assessment.
