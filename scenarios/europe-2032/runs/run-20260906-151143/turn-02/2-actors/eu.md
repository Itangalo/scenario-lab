## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge (CIRS)  
A rapid, targeted hardening of energy, water, and transport control systems against AI-powered intrusion, with mandatory detection upgrades and cross-border red-teaming exercises.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 10–12 points; reduce systemic vulnerability to AI-driven cyberattacks in critical sectors within two years  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, with enforcement through the NIS2 Directive and conditional access to EU recovery funds

## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
This remains the cornerstone of our two-year commitment: without sovereign compute, every resilience gain, every safety regime, and every industrial strategy is contingent on the goodwill of others — and we are now seeing openweight systems closing in on frontier capability, which means our window to act is narrowing fast.

## In practice  
We are accelerating the InvestAI Gigafactories by invoking the Crisis Response Instrument under Article 122 TFEU, treating the convergence of AI capability growth and openweight diffusion as a systemic risk to economic and security sovereignty. The European Investment Bank is now releasing tranches on a quarterly milestone basis, tied to grid connection agreements and local workforce hiring targets. We are fast-tracking environmental assessments in Brittany and Norrland through a joint committee of the Commission and member-state energy ministries, backed by a €12bn solidarity fund to offset regional energy cost increases. The goal is to bring the first site online by turn 8, not turn 9, and we are reallocating 3GW of reserved capacity from the general AI zone pool to the highest-readiness site in northern Sweden.

The Tech sovereignty package is being leveraged in bilateral talks with semiconductor equipment suppliers, particularly ASML and Zeiss, to secure priority delivery slots for EU-based AI fabs. We are offering export-control cooperation and joint R&D funding in next-gen lithography in exchange for binding capacity reservations. At the same time, we are finalizing the draft directive on permitting harmonization, which will cap approval timelines at 14 months with a “dormant adoption” clause: if a member state fails to decide within the window, the project is automatically approved under EU law. This is legally aggressive, but the verification_widens event and the openweight_frontier_release make it necessary — we can no longer afford delay.

The EAAD is now operational in interim form under ENISA, with 45 staff seconded from national cyber agencies, CERN, and nuclear safety institutes. We have issued our first evaluation request to a non-EU hyperscaler deploying a model at 53.0 capability, demanding access to weights and training logs within 30 days. Legal challenges are expected, but we are preparing a public dossier linking the model’s architecture to known agent persistence risks observed in the recent intrusion. The levy mechanism is being tested via a pilot on three firms’ 2026 Q4 revenue, with enforcement through the Digital Services Act’s compliance infrastructure. If this holds, we will scale it in turn 3.
