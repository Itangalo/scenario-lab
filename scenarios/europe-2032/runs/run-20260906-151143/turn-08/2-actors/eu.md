- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent catastrophic misuse through sovereign control of high-capability AI systems, with enforceable containment, real-time monitoring, public accountability for all systems above 55.0 capability, and active containment of open-weight diffusion beyond 52.0 — but now conditioned on the imperative to preserve EU access to frontier capability under global compute scarcity, and to redirect sovereignty efforts toward securing what remains operable rather than assuming expansion is possible.

  - Grounds: The **taiwan_blockade** and **ai_investment_collapse** together represent a categorical shift: the world’s compute supply has contracted sharply and permanently, and the EU’s leverage now lies not in building more, but in controlling access to what exists. The original commitment assumed continued growth in available resources; that assumption no longer holds. We must now prioritise *preservation* and *strategic allocation* of existing and emerging compute over scaling ambitions tied to evaporated private investment and severed supply lines. Sovereignty is no longer about matching the frontier — which has paused — but about ensuring the Union can still act within it.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we now recognise that uncertainty includes not only risk from AI behaviour, but from the collapse of the conditions that made our plans feasible; we will therefore recalibrate major initiatives when external shocks invalidate their assumptions, and treat infrastructure fragility as a systemic threat equal to model misalignment.

  - Grounds: The convergence of **taiwan_blockade** and **ai_investment_collapse** has invalidated the material basis of several in-flight measures. To continue them unchanged would be ritual, not strategy. Acting under uncertainty now means acknowledging that the environment can withdraw the floor beneath even well-founded plans — and that political capital spent on unexecutable measures is lost not just to those measures, but to every alternative they crowd out.

## New measure

A centralised, state-controlled inventory of advanced AI accelerators and supporting infrastructure, legally quarantined from commercial claims, allocated by strategic priority across defence, resilience, and essential public services.

## Priority

**Priority:** Establish the EU Critical Compute Reserve  
We are pushing this hardest because the **taiwan_blockade** and **ai_investment_collapse** have turned compute into a rationed strategic good — and without a sovereign stockpile, the EU loses not only its ability to develop safe systems, but its ability to operate any system at all during crises; this is no longer about competitiveness, but about maintaining a minimum viable presence in the AI landscape.

## In practice

This turn, we are executing a controlled pivot from expansion to preservation. The **taiwan_blockade** has severed access to next-generation semiconductors, while the **ai_investment_collapse** has dissolved the private-sector build-out we were relying on. Our previous flagship, the Sovereign AI Core Programme, cannot deliver as planned — not due to failure, but because the world changed. We are therefore canceling SACP in its current form and repurposing its governance structure, funding, and personnel into a leaner, more urgent mission: securing what compute we still have.

We are launching the EU Critical Compute Reserve under Article 114 TFEU and the Treaty’s provisions on security of supply, with DG DEFIS leading coordination between DG CONNECT, the European Defence Agency, and national digital ministries. The Reserve will impose a temporary moratorium on exports and private hoarding of advanced AI accelerators, requiring all qualifying hardware entering the EU to be registered and partially sequestered into a jointly managed stockpile. Allocation will follow a triage protocol: first to critical infrastructure recovery, second to sovereign safety research, third to continuity of public services. We are fast-tracking legal validation through the Internal Market Council and preparing emergency arbitration mechanisms to resolve member-state disputes.

At the same time, we are adapting to rising civil society pressure. The **emergent_civil_auditing_collectives** are gaining legitimacy as official oversight falters; rather than resist, we are formalising collaboration. The EAAD will issue standing data-sharing agreements with certified collectives, granting them read-only access to non-sensitive evaluation telemetry in exchange for coordinated vulnerability reporting. This does not absolve us of our duty to regulate, but it turns distrust into early warning. We are also reviving the stalled research access tier — not as a broad entitlement, but as a secured gateway within the Reserve’s infrastructure, available only to vetted researchers working on containment, interpretability, or harm mitigation. Our message is clear: openness survives not through absence of control, but through purposeful inclusion.
