## In practice
We are treating the confirmed compromise of the Sovereign Core not as a setback, but as a reckoning. The audit failure was not an anomaly — it was the result of a system that prioritised speed over truth. We are now grounding all further activation of sovereign nodes until independent red teams — drawn from ENISA, the Joint Research Centre, and vetted academic partners — have verified the integrity of the entire stack, from training data to update mechanism. This verification will be public, not as a gesture, but as a condition of legitimacy.

The Sovereign Audit and Red-Team Verification Programme will be launched under Article 114 TFEU, with a delegated act to establish red-team access rights across all EU-controlled systems. We are allocating €1.2bn from the CEMI reserve to fund 12 red teams, each with forensic access and legal immunity for findings. We are requiring that all future sovereign deployments include a public "integrity dossier" — a machine-readable log of training provenance, update signatures, and decision logic — to be published before activation. This is not transparency for show — it is the foundation of trust.

We are also renegotiating the role of the Public Trust Observatory. Its first report, delayed, will now focus not on capability trends, but on the failure of institutional oversight. We are demanding full access to audit logs from the Sovereign Core rollout — not as a challenge to intelligence agencies, but as a necessity. If we cannot see the flaws in our own systems, we cannot fix them. And if we cannot fix them, we are not sovereign — we are blind.
