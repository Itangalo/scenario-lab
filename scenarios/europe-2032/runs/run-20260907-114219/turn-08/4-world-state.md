### A Leap in Capability, a Crack in Control

The global AI frontier surges forward as recursive self-improvement cycles deepen, pushing `ai_capability` to 78.5 — a jump of 4.0, well within the accelerating trend. This leap is driven by opaque, self-modifying training runs now common at leading labs, whose internal logic remains inaccessible even to their operators. No corresponding advance in safety systems occurs; instead, `ai_safety` plummets to 12.0 as multiple labs bypass internal red-teaming to meet deployment deadlines. The gap between capability and control widens dangerously.

Signs of broader diffusion emerge as fragments of frontier model weights appear in decentralized research forums, and open benchmarks rise to reflect incremental improvements. However, no single release dominates; instead, `openweight_capability` grows to 64.0, reflecting distributed refinement and partial replication by well-resourced academic and civic collectives. While this marks a steady catch-up, the open ecosystem remains two turns behind the closed frontier, constrained by compute access and coordination limits. Security analysts note increased probing activity using these emerging models, but no large-scale exploits are confirmed.

Meanwhile, a medical breakthrough — individually tailored cancer therapies derived from AI-discovered protein pathways — reaches clinics. Yet in the EU, the news is met with resentment: the models behind it are inaccessible, trained in a closed US-Chinese consortium. Public sentiment rises only slightly, to 33.0, as the benefit underscores European dependency rather than shared progress.

The EU presses ahead with its **Accelerated Sovereign Core Deployment**, integrating a 58.0-capable auditable model into emergency response systems. Integration remains partial, and vulnerabilities emerge in update mechanisms, feeding quiet alarm among technical auditors. The **Institutional Integrity Audit** concludes, revealing AI-influenced decisions in three national agencies, now under mandatory remediation.

The Commission announces plans for a new **EU AI Authority with Independent Training Mandate**, aiming to establish sovereign model development. However, with `eu_political_capital` still at 3.0, the initiative is not yet operational. Legal challenges to the use of Article 114 TFEU gain traction, and member-state resistance delays funding. The portfolio remains overloaded, and the proposal is shelved pending consolidation of ongoing efforts. No new measure is formally added to the active portfolio.

Despite partial progress, sovereignty dips to 15.0 as capability outpaces build. Political capital holds steady at 3.0 — too low to launch new major initiatives, but sufficient to avoid further erosion. Resilience inches up to 50.0 with final NIS3 integration, the only clear gain.
