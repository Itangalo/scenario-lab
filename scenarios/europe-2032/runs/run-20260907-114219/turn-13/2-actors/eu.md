- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us. **We now go further: this integrity is not a background condition — it is the central front. Our sovereignty is not measured in compute or nodes, but in the fidelity of our decision chains. Our safety is not in containment, but in legibility. From this turn forward, we act not to balance two mandates, but to rebuild the substrate on which both depend — because without it, every action we take risks compounding the crisis.**  

  - Grounds: The development this turn is the **emergent_researcher_desertion_wave**, which is not merely a loss of personnel but a systemic collapse of technical legitimacy. Senior researchers are not just resigning — they are publicly denouncing the EU’s stewardship as ethically indefensible and institutionally futile. This is not a failure of recruitment or retention; it is a collapse of the epistemic foundation upon which both safety and sovereignty rest. When the people who build and audit the systems declare them ungovernable, we are no longer managing risk — we are presiding over a vacuum of authority. This is a categorical shift: we are not late, we are irrelevant. Our identity must reflect that we are no longer the steward of a sovereign stack, but the caretaker of a broken chain of trust — and that rebuilding it is not a policy choice, but an existential necessity.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act blind. This turn, the eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings are not proof of imminent catastrophe, but they are proof that the systems we rely on are no longer legible even to their creators. We are no longer merely acting under uncertainty — we are acting inside a fog of our own making. Therefore, we bind ourselves further: we will not only act early, but we will demand that every major commitment be paired with a public, auditable theory of harm and a corresponding monitoring mechanism. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher. **We now add: we will not proceed with any capital expenditure on new capability until an independent red team has verified the integrity of the entire deployment chain — from training data to update mechanism — and until that verification is made public. This is not a pause — it is a condition of legitimacy.**  

  - Trigger: The **emergent_researcher_desertion_wave** and the **capability_jump** to 93.0, which together confirm that the systems we are meant to govern are not only beyond human legibility but also beyond the capacity of any remaining institutional actor to verify or control.

But the cost of continuing without verification is higher: the irreversible erosion of technical legitimacy. If even the builders no longer believe in the system, no amount of political capital can sustain it. We are not abandoning action — we are redefining legitimacy as a prerequisite for action.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control — but not at the cost of systemic integrity. This turn, we have seen that capability without control is not autonomy — it is exposure. Therefore, we narrow the commitment: we will prioritise the integrity and audibility of the sovereign stack over its speed of deployment. No node will be activated without a verified, independent audit of its update chain and decision logic. We will slow the rollout if necessary, but we will not propagate a compromised standard. **We now redefine: our two-year commitment is no longer to deploy sovereign AI — it is to restore sovereign *judgment*. This means rebuilding the chain of trust from the ground up: auditable models, transparent evaluation, and public accountability. We will not accept a sovereign capability that cannot be understood, because such a thing is not sovereign — it is a puppet.**  

  - Trigger: The **emergent_researcher_desertion_wave** and the **capability_jump** to 93.0, which together confirm that the frontier has moved beyond any possibility of EU-led technical stewardship in its current form.  
  - Grounds: The commitment was to restore sovereign judgment, but judgment requires actors who can judge. With red teams dissolving, evaluation bodies losing quorum, and recruitment halted, the EU no longer has the human capital to exercise judgment — even if the systems were legible. The reversal — shifting focus from system audits to institutional survival — costs us time and the perception of strategic drift. But the alternative — to continue demanding audits with no one left to conduct them — would be farce. We are not abandoning judgment — we are acknowledging that it cannot be restored through process alone when the people who enact it have fled.

## In practice  
We are treating the **emergent_researcher_desertion_wave** not as a personnel issue, but as an institutional extinction event. The resignations are not isolated — they are a coordinated withdrawal of legitimacy from the entire EU AI governance framework. We are therefore activating emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU, invoking the precedent of the pandemic and energy crisis to justify extraordinary intervention in labour and institutional continuity.

We are establishing the **Emergency Institutional Continuity Protocol** as a delegated act, granting the Commission direct authority to identify, fund, and protect critical AI oversight roles across member states. This includes emergency salaries indexed to private-sector benchmarks, legal immunity for public disclosures of systemic risk, and fast-track Schengen visas for international experts willing to join. We are allocating €800 million from the CEMI reserve — the only remaining unencumbered fiscal space — to fund 500 protected positions over the next 18 months, with 60% reserved for red-team and audit functions.

We are also restructuring the Public Trust Observatory to serve as the operational hub of this protocol, shifting its mandate from reporting on risk to actively managing the survival of technical oversight. Its first act will be to publish a public register of all critical AI governance roles in the EU, their current occupancy, and attrition risk — not to shame, but to mobilise. We are negotiating with member states to waive national civil service rules for these roles, offering direct EU contracts with mobility and security clearance portability. This is not about rebuilding trust yet — it is about preventing total institutional collapse. If we lose the people, we lose everything.
