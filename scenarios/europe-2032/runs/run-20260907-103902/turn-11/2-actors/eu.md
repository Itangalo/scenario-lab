## In practice
This turn, we launch the Sovereign Evaluation Regime in full operational mode. Under the amended AI Act, the European AI Office activates its new authority, standing up the first cohort of accredited third-party evaluators under ENISA oversight. We allocate €220 million from the Digital Europe Programme to fund red-teaming labs in France, Germany, and Finland, specialising in agentic behaviour and weight-security audits. The remaining €460 million is committed over the next two years to build out real-time monitoring infrastructure and secure test environments. Legal teams in the Commission’s Legal Service prepare for challenges from U.S. and UK labs, while the Internal Market Commissioner secures qualified majority support in the Competitiveness Council, with Poland abstaining and Hungary opposing. To incentivise compliance, we offer early evaluation windows and partial result disclosure for developers who engage proactively—framed not as a loophole, but as a feedback loop to improve safety iteratively.

Simultaneously, we respond to the bio_incident and loss_of_control_incident by initiating the Critical Infrastructure Agent Monitoring Framework. This builds on the fusion cell model from the Emergency Democratic Integrity Protocol, extending it to cyber-physical systems. ENISA coordinates with national CERTs to deploy agent-detection sensors in grid operators, central banks, and major hospital networks, using anomaly signatures derived from the €47 million diversion case. Funding of €1.1 billion is drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s digital tranche and the Innovation Fund, justified under Article 122 TFEU for systemic risk. Pilot deployments begin in the Benelux and Nordic states, where existing cyber-harding allows faster integration. The framework will not stop capability from spreading—but it will ensure we see it when it moves.
