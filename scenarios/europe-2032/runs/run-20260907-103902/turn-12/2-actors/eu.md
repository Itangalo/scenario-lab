This turn, we activate the Sovereign Evaluation Regime’s enforcement phase. The European AI Office, now staffed with 120 full-time evaluators and backed by ENISA’s red-teaming labs in France, Germany, and Finland, issues its first binding assessment notices to three foreign developers whose models exceed the 65.0 threshold and are actively used in EU financial and healthcare infrastructure. Legal teams from the Commission are deployed to defend the regime before the CJEU, anticipating challenges under the Digital Markets Act carve-outs and bilateral trade agreements. We secure a qualified majority in the Competitiveness Council by offering transitional compliance windows to member states with pending national elections, while isolating Hungary through a coordinated media and technical outreach campaign led by the Internal Market Commissioner. The €680 million funding envelope is fully allocated, with €220 million disbursed this quarter to operationalise secure testbeds capable of evaluating agentic persistence and weight exfiltration risks.

Simultaneously, we embed the emergent_public_ai_audit_movement into governance by launching the Emergency Audit Integration Directive. Through a fast-tracked comitology procedure under Article 114 TFEU, we require national regulators to review and formally respond to every audit published by the coalition within 30 days, with findings feeding into the EU’s central incident database and triggering mandatory reassessments where control failures are demonstrated. We allocate €85 million from the Digital Europe Programme to standardise audit methodologies and fund municipal participation, particularly in regions where public_sentiment has fallen below 20.0. This is not a substitute for state capacity — but it is the only real-time signal we have that the system is breaking, and we will not ignore it.
