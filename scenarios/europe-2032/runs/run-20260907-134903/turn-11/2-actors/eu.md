## In practice
This turn, we are driving the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* through the European Parliament’s Internal Market and Civil Liberties committees, anchoring it in Article 114 TFEU to frame it as internal market harmonization—a legal strategy intended to defuse claims of extraterritorial overreach. The Commission’s Legal Service is finalizing the draft, holding firm on non-negotiables: access to model weights, real-time telemetry, and verified kill-switch mechanisms. We are preparing to invoke Article 155(2)’s urgency clause if parliamentary resistance demands it, though we are aware this risks constitutional challenges in several member states and further erodes cohesion.

On the operational front, ENISA and national agencies are expanding detection pilots in France and Germany, deploying traffic fingerprinting and inference-pattern recognition to identify unauthorised frontier models in use. The first EAAN certification gateway in Brussels is completing its security audit and will go live this turn, offering a sovereign on-ramp for compliant systems. To counter US pressure, we are preparing to classify certain foundation models as core digital services under the DMA—a move that would compel compliance through existing antitrust leverage—while simultaneously offering early adopters liability shielding and preferential access to EU compute under the revised AI Liability Directive. The message is calibrated: cooperation is rewarded, defiance will be met with instruments we already control.
