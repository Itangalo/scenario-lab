### A Frontier That Stalls, a Gap That Stays

Global AI capability advances by +0.5, reaching 67.5, consistent with the fixed underlying rate of progress. The release of a major frontier model underperforms expectations, and premium pricing for advanced AI services has declined, suggesting developmental challenges. Despite this, no safety gains emerge: frontier systems remain opaque, and `ai_safety` holds at 7.0, near collapse. Open-weight capability rises to 56.0, pulled up by the prior capability jump and growing underground distribution of fine-tuned variants, even as official releases remain restricted.

### Border Enforcement Meets Resistance

The EU advances its *AI Deployment Boundary Act*, with ENISA expanding detection pilots and the Brussels certification gateway going live. Yet uptake remains minimal—only one developer has engaged—and US hyperscalers continue to resist reclassification under the DMA. Legal challenges in Germany, Poland, and Hungary gain traction, with national courts questioning the proportionality and legal basis of the border. The Commission’s reliance on Article 114 TFEU is now under scrutiny by the European Court of Justice, following a fast-tracked referral.

### Strikes Escalate, Sentiment Drops

Coordinated strikes by displaced technical workers spread across public-sector IT contractors, disrupting local government services in France, Italy, and Spain. The protests frame AI deployment as a policy failure, demanding retraining and transition funds. Media coverage intensifies public anxiety, and `public_sentiment` drops to 25.0. The EU’s focus on control over support fuels resentment, with critics accusing leaders of managing risk for institutions while ignoring worker displacement.

### Political Capital Edges Up, Just Barely

The completion of the AI Safety Emergency Investment yields a +4 boost to `eu_political_capital`. With no new measure proposed, portfolio charges are limited to the ongoing cost of the *EU Border for AI Systems* (−3) and the named priority (−1), totaling −4. The net effect is a slight rise to 13.0. However, with capital still below 20, the priority confers no acceleration. Sovereignty declines to 30.0 due to the capability rise of +0.5 this turn (−1) and deepening dependency (−2). Resilience drops to 37.0, penalized by rising open-weight threats and no active category 6 measure.
