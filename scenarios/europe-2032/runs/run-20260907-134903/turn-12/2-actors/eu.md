## In practice
This turn, we are finalizing the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* in the Council’s Internal Market configuration, relying on the Commission’s legal argument that Article 114 TFEU permits harmonization of rules governing the deployment of high-impact AI systems as a matter of internal market integrity. We are holding firm on access to model weights, telemetry, and kill switches, but have accepted a phased enforcement schedule to secure qualified majority support. The German delegation remains cautious, citing constitutional concerns, while Poland and Hungary continue to frame the measure as disproportionate; we are countering with bilateral assurances on national AI development funding and access to EAAN evaluation services for domestic innovators.

Operationally, ENISA is expanding detection pilots in France and Germany, deploying traffic fingerprinting and inference-pattern recognition to identify unauthorised frontier models in use. The first EAAN certification gateway in Brussels has passed its security audit and is now live, though only one developer has engaged. We are preparing to invoke the DMA’s core platform designation to compel compliance from US hyperscalers, drafting legal opinions on whether certain foundation models meet the gateway criteria for gatekeeper status. At the same time, we are offering early adopters liability shielding under the revised AI Liability Directive and preferential access to EU compute under the Chips+ framework — a calibrated mix of pressure and incentive. But with political capital at 8.0, we cannot afford missteps: every move must land, and nothing new can be risked until the current portfolio proves its worth.
