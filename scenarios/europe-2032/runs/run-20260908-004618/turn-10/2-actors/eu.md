### Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.  
- Grounds: The erosion of public sentiment, the member_state_defection, and the emergent_ai_talent_drain reveal that sovereignty without legitimacy is hollow. Our dual mandate must now explicitly encompass not only technical control and strategic independence but also the restoration of civic trust and institutional credibility. The frontier is advancing not just in capability but in influence — and if we lose the confidence of our citizens, we lose the Union’s capacity to act at all.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will now anchor such commitments in observable harm, not just plausible risk.  
- Grounds: The **election_annulled** event and the **verification_widens** breakthrough have changed the calculus: we now see domains once thought immune to automation falling rapidly, and democratic processes visibly destabilised. Acting under uncertainty remains necessary, but legitimacy demands that our interventions respond to real, documented harms — not only to feared ones. This turn, we shift from preparing for hypothetical breakdowns to repairing actual fractures.

- retire `two_year_commitment`  
- add `next_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public trust and democratic integrity by hardening institutions against AI-driven manipulation, ensuring sovereign access to transformative applications, and reversing the talent drain  
- Grounds: The previous two-year commitment ends this turn, having been overtaken by events. While sovereign compute was necessary, it proved insufficient without diffusion, control, and legitimacy. The **adoption_success** shows what is possible when public benefit is tangible; the **member_state_defection** and **public_sentiment** at 25.0 show the cost of failing to deliver it widely. The new commitment reflects a pivot from infrastructure alone to integration, impact, and inclusion.

## In practice
This turn, we are closing the Emergency Cyber Resilience Surge as planned: ENISA has confirmed compliance from 24 member states, and the Finnish failover drill succeeded in rerouting health registry queries within 12 minutes of simulated disruption. The exascale cluster held — but only just. The **emergent_compute_shortfall** remains acute, and we are now formally triaging access: energy grid operators receive priority over non-critical research, and public health AI models are capped at 80% utilisation to preserve headroom for incident response. This is not sustainable, but it is holding.

The **Lithography Revival Initiative** remains grounded — not for lack of will, but for lack of tools. The **supply_chain_coercion** event has frozen construction in Dresden and Grenoble; without access to calibrated metrology equipment from ASML, we cannot proceed. We are invoking Article 21 of the EU Treaty to explore countermeasures, but leverage is thin. In parallel, we are fast-tracking procurement from alternative suppliers in Japan and South Korea, but timelines stretch. Sovereignty without supply-chain autonomy is a castle on sand.

And so we launch the **Public Trust and Democratic Integrity Initiative** — not as a supplement, but as a successor in spirit to what came before. The **adoption_success** in public health gives us a foothold: we are expanding that pilot to three new regions, this time with sovereign deployment baked in. But more urgently, we are standing up a joint task force between the European Media and Information Fund and national election commissions to audit information flows ahead of the 2032 cycle. The **election_annulled** event is our warning shot: we will not wait for manipulation to be proven decisive. We act because doubt alone is enough to break a democracy.
