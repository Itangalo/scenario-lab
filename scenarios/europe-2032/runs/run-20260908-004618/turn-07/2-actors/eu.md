## In practice
We are acting on the leak not as a breach, but as a signal: the idea has weight. The emergent lithography initiative proves that the strategic logic of full-stack sovereignty has reached critical mass within the Commission’s technical services. This turn, we are converting that momentum into action. Under Article 172 TFEU and the European Defence Technological and Industrial Base framework, we are launching the Lithography Revival Initiative as a Category 4 measure with a six-turn horizon. The plan is to consolidate existing EU holdings in high-purity silicon, photoresists, and precision optics, and to reconstitute a pilot line in Dresden with parallel cleanroom development in Grenoble and Eindhoven. Funding will draw €45bn from the European Defence Fund, the Chips Act, and repurposed cohesion grants, with co-investment required from national champions like Zeiss and STMicroelectronics. We are fast-tracking legal exemptions for land use and environmental review under the same emergency protocols used for the Compute Corridor.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on the EU Sovereign Compute Corridor. COREPER’s objections on military oversight are being addressed through a revised statute that separates operational command from strategic access: the Authority will be civilian-led but embedded within the EU’s Permanent Structured Cooperation (PESCO) framework for crisis resilience. We are offering Austria and Finland enhanced data sovereignty guarantees and priority access to training capacity in exchange for accelerated grid integration. The first exascale cluster is scheduled to come online in Q1 2030, and we are already drafting access protocols with the ECDC, ENISA, and the European Grid Agency to ensure it is not idle upon arrival. The closure of the InvestAI Gigafactories marks a symbolic moment — the capital is spent, the sites are chosen, but the true test of sovereignty now lies not in how much compute we host, but in whether we can defend, control, and reproduce the means to produce it. That is why lithography is not a distraction from the Compute Corridor — it is its necessary foundation. Without it, we are building fortresses on rented ground.
