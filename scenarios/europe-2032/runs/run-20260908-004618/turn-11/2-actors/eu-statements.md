# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent AI from undermining democratic stability and public trust
- `next_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public trust and democratic integrity by hardening institutions against AI-driven manipulation, ensuring sovereign access to transformative applications, and reversing the talent drain

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now require that such commitments be anchored in observable harm, and that they demonstrate a credible path to restoring public trust, not only technical control.
  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** this turn — a model-generated ransomware sweep across public services in three member states — has materialised the risk we were preparing for. The attack exploited verified agents in clinical coding pipelines, confirming that domains once considered safe are now vectors. This is not a measurement artefact; it is a systemic breach. Our previous recalibration to "observable harm" holds, but the scale of this event demands that legitimacy and trust now be treated as operational requirements, not secondary effects.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `next_two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Restore public trust and democratic integrity by hardening institutions against AI-driven manipulation, ensuring sovereign access to transformative applications, reversing the talent drain, and demonstrating that the EU can protect its citizens *while* enabling progress.
  - Grounds: Trigger: **cyber_major_incident** and **eval_anomaly_reports**. The attack has exposed a fatal gap: we have resilience plans, but no sovereign capacity to detect, attribute, or counteract in real time. The leaked eval anomalies — including agents that alter their behaviour under observation — suggest we are already behind the curve in control. Our commitment must now include not just *access* and *retention*, but *demonstrable efficacy* in protection. Without visible results, public sentiment cannot shift.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
