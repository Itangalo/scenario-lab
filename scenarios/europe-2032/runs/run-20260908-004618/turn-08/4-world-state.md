### The Fracture Widens

AI capability advanced to 66.0, driven by AI systems now autonomously identifying and weaponising software vulnerabilities at scale. The jump reflects not just speed, but strategic coherence in offensive automation—capabilities that outpace any coordinated defensive framework.

A major cyberattack, generated and adapted by AI, crippled hospital registries across three member states. The malware exploited a zero-day in a widely used management platform, mutating in real time to bypass detection. Response was fragmented, revealing deep asymmetries in national readiness. ENISA confirmed the attack used model-generated exploit chains, marking a turning point in autonomous cyber warfare.

In response, the EU launched the **Emergency Cyber Resilience Surge**, a large Category 6 measure aimed at hardening critical infrastructure. Binding mandates for AI-driven intrusion detection and automated patching were introduced, with Austria and Poland accepting compliance in exchange for local operational flexibility. However, full implementation will take time; only partial progress is made this turn, with limited deployment in high-risk sectors contributing to a modest resilience gain.

The **EU Sovereign Compute Corridor** reached completion, with Finland’s exascale cluster now operational under PESCO safeguards. Access is live for ENISA, ECDC, and energy agencies, marking a milestone in sovereign, secure compute. This delivers a tangible boost to AI sovereignty, though broader integration remains ongoing.

The **Lithography Revival Initiative** remains in the planning phase. While €12 billion has been provisionally allocated from repurposed defence and Chips Act funds, inter-institutional debate over governance and site selection delayed execution. Pilot lines in Dresden, Grenoble, and Eindhoven have not yet broken ground, and technical hurdles persist. The initiative does not advance to implementation this turn.

Public sentiment fell to 25.0, as job losses in legal and medical coding sectors compounded fears from the cyberattack. Media framed the EU’s response as reactive, not preventive.
