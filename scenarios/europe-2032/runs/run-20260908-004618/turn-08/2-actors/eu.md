## Priority
**Emergency Cyber Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_major_incident has exposed a lethal gap between our sovereign infrastructure ambitions and our ability to defend what we already have, and without resilience, sovereignty is a target, not a shield.

## In practice
This turn, the Union acts on two fronts: one long-term, one immediate. The Lithography Revival Initiative moves from concept to execution. Under Article 172 TFEU and the European Defence Technological and Industrial Base framework, we are activating the first phase: consolidating pilot lines in Dresden, Grenoble, and Eindhoven. €12 billion in initial funding is unlocked from repurposed Chips Act reserves and European Defence Fund allocations, with co-investment mandates for Zeiss, ASML suppliers, and STMicroelectronics. We are invoking emergency land-use waivers to accelerate cleanroom construction and fast-tracking Horizon Europe grants toward metrology and photoresist innovation. The goal is a working 14nm-equivalent line by turn 12 — not frontier, but sufficient to break dependency on foreign lithography for safety-critical systems. Germany, France, and the Netherlands are onboard; Italy is negotiating access in exchange for rare earth processing concessions.

Simultaneously, the EU Sovereign Compute Corridor reaches its finishing turn. The first exascale cluster is now operational in Finland, governed by the European AI Infrastructure Authority under PESCO safeguards. Access protocols are live with ENISA, ECDC, and the Grid Agency, and we are conducting the first live-fire resilience drill next month. This is not just compute — it is a controlled environment for running safety evaluations and emergency simulations without relying on external infrastructure.

But the cyber_major_incident changes everything. A model-generated ransomware sweep has crippled regional health registries in three member states, exploiting zero-day vulnerabilities in a widely used hospital management stack. The attack was not just fast — it was adaptive, reconfiguring payloads in real time to evade detection. Defenders were blind until the cascade began. This is not a failure of sovereignty alone, but of readiness. That is why we are launching the Emergency Cyber Resilience Surge: a two-turn, large-scale Category 6 measure to harden all Tier 1 and Tier 2 critical services with AI-powered intrusion detection, automated patching, and cross-border failover. ENISA will lead, with binding authority to mandate updates and suspend compromised systems. We are fast-tracking procurement under the Civil Protection Mechanism, drawing on pre-vetted vendors from the Digital Europe Programme. Austria and Poland, initially resistant to centralised command, are being offered enhanced autonomy in local response design in exchange for compliance with EU-wide detection standards. This is not optional: if we cannot defend what we build, then every gigafactory, every corridor, every lithography line is just a more valuable target. Resilience is no longer a complement to sovereignty — it is its price.
