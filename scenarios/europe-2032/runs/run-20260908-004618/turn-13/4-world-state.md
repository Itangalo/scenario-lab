### The Breach and the Blockade

A wave of adaptive ransomware, self-modifying and AI-coordinated, sweeps across regional hospitals and transport hubs in three member states. The attack evades legacy defences, encrypting critical systems and demanding payment in untraceable assets. Attribution is murky, but forensic analysis confirms the tools were generated by high-capability models. This **cyber_major_incident** exposes a brutal truth: the EU lacks the sovereign compute to simulate, counter, or even fully analyse such threats in real time.

Simultaneously, Washington compels the Netherlands to extend export controls to older ASML lithography systems—machines vital not just to China but to European industrial maintenance and research. The move, enforced through jurisdiction over embedded U.S. components, severs a critical supply line. The **supply_chain_coercion** confirms a strategic reality: the EU’s chokepoint leverage has become a weapon turned inward.

The Lithography Revival Initiative formally concludes—on paper—with no functional fabrication achieved. Political momentum stalls as member states bicker over funding. The Public Trust Initiative falters further, with Hungary and Poland resisting central oversight. Legal challenges mount against the Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework, with U.S. developers arguing it overreaches.

In response, the Commission launches the **EU Sovereign AI Corridor**, a bold bid to build end-to-end sovereign infrastructure for training and deploying models up to 70.0. EuroHPC machines are earmarked, fast-track visas drafted, and research contracts proposed. But the ambition outpaces the moment: fiscal clearance is delayed, parliamentary debate drags, and the project remains in early drafting. It is not yet operational.

### The Cost of Absence

With no sovereign capacity to run or evaluate frontier models, oversight is blind. The gap between `ai_capability` and `ai_safety` widens as red-teaming collapses under strain. Public trust erodes further as medical records remain inaccessible. The Corridor is announced, but presence is not presence—yet.
