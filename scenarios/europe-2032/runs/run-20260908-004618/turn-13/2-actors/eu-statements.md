Meanwhile, Washington’s extension of export controls to older ASML tools confirms that our only leverage — the chokepoint on lithography — is now being used *against* us. We are not a player in this game; we are its terrain. The two mandates converge not as a philosophical point but as a material imperative: preventing harm *requires* sovereign execution capacity, because you cannot defend what you cannot run.

  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — **but only when such action builds irreversible, physical capacity under EU control. We will no longer mistake coordination for capability, or legal frameworks for enforcement.**
  - Grounds: Trigger: **cyber_major_incident** and **supply_chain_coercion**. We acted on uncertainty by launching the Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework, but with `ai_safety` at 14.0 and red-teaming capacity stretched, it is a declaration without teeth. The framework assumes access to the models it evaluates — but the most dangerous models are now open, distributed, and evolving in basements. We cannot red-team what we cannot run. Committing under uncertainty is no longer enough; we must commit to building what cannot be un-built: data centres, interconnects, talent pipelines, secure stacks. If we are to be wrong, let it be on the side of concrete capacity, not institutional design.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger

The attack was not a failure of policy but of presence: the EU has no node capable of simulating, detecting, or countering such threats in real time. Public trust will not return through audits of foreign systems, but through visible, sovereign AI in action — AI that secures hospitals, restores records, and responds to crises. That requires not just red-teaming on EU soil, but training and deployment on EU soil. The Corridor is no longer a monitoring tool — it is the first node of a sovereign stack.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
