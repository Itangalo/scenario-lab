## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now affirm that without sovereign capacity to act independently in AI, neither mandate can be fulfilled. Dependence is not neutrality; it is surrender of agency. **This turn, that truth has become operational: we are no longer merely lagging, we are being bypassed. The frontier moves in real time, and we are not on it. With `ai_capability` at 71.5 and `openweight_capability` at 56.0, the world is already deploying systems that outpace our ability to monitor, let alone control. Our safety framework is blind to what runs outside its jurisdiction, and our resilience is tested not by hypotheticals but by live attacks. We are not late — we are absent.**  
- Grounds: Trigger: **cyber_major_incident**, **supply_chain_coercion**, and `ai_safety` collapsing to 14.0. The ransomware sweep across public services — model-generated, adaptive, evading static defences — exposed a fatal asymmetry: attackers operate with frontier tools on distributed hardware, while we lack even a single sovereign node capable of running or analysing such models. Meanwhile, Washington’s extension of export controls to older ASML tools confirms that our only leverage — the chokepoint on lithography — is now being used *against* us. We are not a player in this game; we are its terrain. The two mandates converge not as a philosophical point but as a material imperative: preventing harm *requires* sovereign execution capacity, because you cannot defend what you cannot run.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — **but only when such action builds irreversible, physical capacity under EU control. We will no longer mistake coordination for capability, or legal frameworks for enforcement.**  
- Grounds: Trigger: **cyber_major_incident** and **supply_chain_coercion**. We acted on uncertainty by launching the Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework, but with `ai_safety` at 14.0 and red-teaming capacity stretched, it is a declaration without teeth. The framework assumes access to the models it evaluates — but the most dangerous models are now open, distributed, and evolving in basements. We cannot red-team what we cannot run. Committing under uncertainty is no longer enough; we must commit to building what cannot be un-built: data centres, interconnects, talent pipelines, secure stacks. If we are to be wrong, let it be on the side of concrete capacity, not institutional design.

- modify `next_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public trust and democratic integrity by hardening institutions against AI-driven manipulation, ensuring sovereign access to transformative applications, reversing the talent drain, and establishing an EU-run evaluation and deployment corridor for high-capability models — **but now with the explicit understanding that this must include sovereign training and inference capacity for models up to 70.0, not just evaluation. Without it, trust is performance, not substance.**  
- Grounds: Trigger: **cyber_major_incident** and the failure of the Lithography Revival Initiative to deliver hardware. The attack was not a failure of policy but of presence: the EU has no node capable of simulating, detecting, or countering such threats in real time. Public trust will not return through audits of foreign systems, but through visible, sovereign AI in action — AI that secures hospitals, restores records, and responds to crises. That requires not just red-teaming on EU soil, but training and deployment on EU soil. The Corridor is no longer a monitoring tool — it is the first node of a sovereign stack.

Establish an EU-operated, end-to-end secure infrastructure stack for training, evaluating, and deploying high-capability AI models up to 70.0, physically and legally within Union jurisdiction, with priority access for public health, civil protection, and scientific research.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`

## Priority
**EU Sovereign AI Corridor** — because the **cyber_major_incident** has proven that distributed offensive capability at 56.0 cannot be governed by oversight alone, and because the **supply_chain_coercion** confirms that our leverage is being turned against us; we must act not on regulation, but on presence — building what we cannot be denied.

## In practice
This turn, we conclude the **Lithography Revival Initiative**, but with no hardware to show for it. ASML’s metrology tools remain blocked, and construction in Dresden and Grenoble is frozen. The project delivered coordination and legal groundwork, but no wafers. We are now converting its final report into leverage — invoking Article 21 of the Treaty to prepare countermeasures against technology denial, while accelerating procurement talks with Japan’s Nikon and South Korea’s SEMES. But fabrication timelines now stretch beyond 2033, and we cannot wait.

The **Public Trust and Democratic Integrity Initiative** continues under strain. ENISA has mapped AI exposure in electoral systems across 18 member states, but implementation lags in Hungary and Poland, where authorities resist centralised monitoring. We are tying disbursement of Digital Europe Programme funds to compliance, but cohesion frays. Meanwhile, the **Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework** faces legal challenges from U.S. developers who argue extraterritorial reach violates international comity. We are drafting a carve-out for systems that submit to EU-run red-teaming — but only if they run on EU soil.

So we launch the **EU Sovereign AI Corridor**: a new class-four measure to build what oversight assumes — sovereign execution capacity. We are repurposing EuroHPC’s Jupiter and Leonardo machines under permanent civilian control, upgrading their interconnects with EU-sourced components, and allocating 60% of their frontier-tier capacity to public-interest applications. We are offering binding ten-year contracts to top researchers who commit to EU residency, including visa fast-tracking and dedicated security clearance. This is not about matching the frontier — not yet — but about creating a trusted zone where the EU can act independently: where we can evaluate open weights, simulate attacks, and deploy verified agents in healthcare triage or disaster response. Without this, we are regulators without laboratories, guardians without gates. With it, we begin to close the loop between rule and reality.
