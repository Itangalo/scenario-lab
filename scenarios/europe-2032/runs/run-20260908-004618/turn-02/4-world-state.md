### A Wake-Up Call, But Not a Reset

The EU responded to the recent cyberattack with urgency, activating the Cyber Shield Emergency Protocol under NIS2. ENISA issued binding directives for public health, energy, and transport operators to deploy AI-augmented detection systems and join a real-time threat-sharing network. Fast-tracked procurement allowed initial contracts to be awarded within weeks, and red-teaming exercises began in three member states by April.

Yet momentum faltered. Two countries delayed implementation, citing budget constraints and legal disputes over centralised enforcement. The promised €18bn funding faced scrutiny from the European Court of Auditors over Recovery and Resilience Facility reallocations, slowing disbursement. While patch deployment improved, full integration remained partial. The effort was visible, but not yet transformative.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories advanced through permitting hurdles after a trilateral deal with ASML, Intel, and EDF tied reactor co-location to approval. However, final environmental assessments revealed groundwater risks at one proposed site, triggering local protests and a three-month review extension. The Tech Sovereignty Package neared agreement, though green hydrogen commitments were diluted to secure unanimity.

The Labour Transition Accelerator Fund began pilot disbursements via the ESF+, now requiring cyber resilience training for eligibility. This linkage was praised as innovative, but rollout was slow, and media coverage focused on job losses in logistics and customer service sectors where automation accelerated unchecked.

Globally, AI capability advanced another 2.0 points. Frontier labs released new agent frameworks capable of sustained coordination, though early benchmarks showed inconsistencies—fueling debate over whether progress was plateauing. Still, offensive cyber tools derived from these models enabled the recent attack, underscoring growing risks.

With no safety-focused measures completed and opaque model reasoning persisting, confidence in control eroded. ai_safety dropped. openweight_capability rose slightly, reflecting wider use of last cycle’s open models. Resilience ticked up modestly from early Cyber Shield gains, but systemic fragility remained.

Public sentiment dipped as fear outpaced trust in responses. eu_ai_sovereignty fell again, as foreign frontier access remained critical and domestic build-out lagged. Political capital declined under the weight of multiple large initiatives, despite the priority shift.
