## New measure
**Cyber Shield Emergency Protocol Activation**

Activates and funds a binding, time-bound upgrade cycle for cyber defences across critical public infrastructure, with mandatory adoption by member states and direct EU-level coordination of threat intelligence, patch deployment, and red-teaming.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 8–12 points, reduce incident severity from cyber_major_incident by 40–60%, and stabilise public_sentiment by demonstrating a visible response to systemic risk.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, critical infrastructure operators, and EU agencies (ENISA, ECDC, CER).

## Priority
**Cyber Shield Emergency Protocol Activation** — because the cyber_major_incident has exposed a critical vulnerability in essential services, and failure to respond decisively now would collapse both resilience and public trust, undermining all other efforts, including our two-year commitment to sovereignty.

## In practice
We are treating the cyber_major_incident as a wake-up call that cannot be met with incrementalism. Under Article 12 of the NIS2 Directive, we are activating the Cyber Shield Emergency Protocol to mandate immediate hardening of public health, energy, and transport systems against AI-generated attack vectors. ENISA will coordinate a six-month sprint: every critical operator must deploy AI-augmented intrusion detection systems, undergo a red-team exercise modelled on the recent breach, and integrate into a real-time threat-sharing network. Funding of €18bn will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with 80% co-financing for member states that complete upgrades by turn 4. We are fast-tracking procurement through the EU’s centralised digital contracts framework to bypass national delays.

At the same time, we are pushing the InvestAI Gigafactories through the final permitting hurdles in the Competitiveness Council. We’ve brokered a trilateral agreement with ASML, Intel, and EDF to co-locate two gigafactories with next-gen nuclear microgrids, addressing both energy load and sovereignty concerns. Legal anchoring of compute and weights is now a condition of state aid disbursement, and we are preparing draft legislation under the Critical Entities Resilience Directive to make it irreversible. The Tech sovereignty package is entering its final negotiation phase in the Internal Market Council, where we are trading accelerated permitting for binding green hydrogen co-investment and water recycling commitments. On the social front, we are deploying the Labour Transition Accelerator Fund through the ESF+, but now linking disbursements to the Cyber Shield effort: retraining programmes must include cyber resilience skills to qualify, turning workforce transition into a dual investment in human and systemic resilience.
