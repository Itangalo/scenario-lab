## New measure
**Public Trust and Democratic Integrity Initiative**  
Launch a coordinated programme to secure democratic processes, public information ecosystems, and essential services against AI-driven manipulation and failure.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience from 46.5 to 60.0 by hardening electoral systems, public health registries, and media verification infrastructure; stabilise public sentiment by demonstrating tangible protection of civic life; reduce the societal blast radius of future AI incidents.  
`Applies to:` Member states, ENISA, European Media and Information Fund, national election commissions, public broadcasters, and critical public service operators.

## Priority
**Emergency Cyber Resilience Surge** — because the immediate threat to life and public order from adaptive, AI-generated cyberattacks demands full focus this turn, and delaying its implementation risks cascading failures that would undermine both the new commitment and every measure in flight.

## In practice
This turn, we are operating under the closing weight of our two-year commitment to secure sovereign AI capacity — but the world has shifted beneath us. The **Emergency Cyber Resilience Surge** is now live: ENISA has issued binding directives to all Tier 1 and Tier 2 critical infrastructure operators, mandating AI-powered intrusion detection systems and automated patching protocols within 90 days. We are fast-tracking procurement under the Civil Protection Mechanism, drawing on pre-vetted vendors from the Digital Europe Programme. Austria and Poland have agreed to compliance, with tailored implementation plans that preserve local operational control while ensuring EU-wide detection standards. The first live-fire drill for the Finnish exascale cluster will test failover procedures for health registries next month — a concrete step toward operational resilience.

Yet the **emergent_compute_shortfall** exposes a brutal truth: sovereignty without scalability is symbolic. Despite the completion of the EU Sovereign Compute Corridor, demand from health, energy, and cyber agencies already exceeds capacity. Non-defence functions are being deprioritised, threatening the very services we are trying to protect. We are now triaging access through the European AI Infrastructure Authority, but this is not sustainable. The **Lithography Revival Initiative** remains our long-term anchor — pilot lines in Dresden, Grenoble, and Eindhoven are now legally established under Article 172 TFEU, with €12 billion unlocked and co-investment mandates active. But construction has not yet begun, and the **supply_chain_coercion** event — Washington forcing ASML to restrict servicing of older lithography tools — threatens even mid-tier production. We are exploring emergency waivers and alternative metrology suppliers, but the leverage is not ours.

Meanwhile, the **ai_investment_collapse** has frozen several private compute projects the Union was counting on to scale capacity. The frontier is no longer accelerating — but neither is it ours. And while the **medical_breakthrough** offers hope, the models behind it are locked in U.S. clouds, inaccessible to EU clinicians on sovereign terms. The benefit is real, but it deepens dependence. Public sentiment remains at 25.0 — not because people reject AI, but because they see it as a force acting upon them, not for them. That is why we are launching the **Public Trust and Democratic Integrity Initiative** next turn: to shift from defending infrastructure to defending legitimacy. But this turn, we hold the line. The Surge is our priority — because if we cannot keep hospitals running, no commitment, no sovereignty, no future holds.
