### A Rising Gap, A Widening Fracture

AI capability advanced steadily, driven by closed-lab progress in multi-agent coordination and automated code generation. At 63.0, systems now routinely execute complex professional workflows over days, displacing junior roles across law and software at an accelerating pace. Open weights, while lagging, reached 50.0—enough to run supervised research and development tasks, further distributing offensive cyber tools and model fine-tuning capacity.

The EU launched the **Accelerated Reskilling Compact for Cognitive Sectors**, a small Category 7 measure targeting displaced graduates. Though symbolically significant, its rollout faced early resistance: national agencies in three member states delayed implementation, citing funding ambiguities, and corporate buy-in remained patchy. The narrative of "rearmament" struggled against perceptions of burden-sharing, dampening public sentiment slightly.

Meanwhile, the **Tech Sovereignty Package** formally concluded with mixed results. Private investment in data centres materialised below target, and permitting reforms stalled in national parliaments. Its closure freed political capital, but the pivot to prioritising the **EU Sovereign Compute Corridor** met legal headwinds. COREPER delayed approval of the European AI Infrastructure Authority’s statutes, demanding clarity on military oversight and cross-border data flows.

Sovereignty eroded further. Despite ongoing compute projects, the rise in AI capability without a corresponding leap in domestic infrastructure cost −1, and no capacity-securing event occurred. Political capital declined under the weight of multiple measures and the priority, despite the closure bonus of the Tech Sovereignty Package.
