## In practice
We are acting on the hard lesson of this turn: access granted conditionally is access that can be withdrawn. The election_alliance has eased immediate pressure, but it binds us tighter to alignment on exports, standards, and partners — turning our leverage into obligation. At the same time, supply_chain_coercion shows that even our partial control over semiconductor tools can be overridden through extraterritorial jurisdiction. We cannot rely on influence we do not fully own. That is why we are launching the EU Sovereign Compute Corridor: not just more data centres, but a ring-fenced, state-actor-hardened compute backbone, powered by dedicated grid links and located in geologically stable, low-risk regions, starting with Finland, Sweden, and eastern Austria. Under Article 172 TFEU, we are establishing the European AI Infrastructure Authority to oversee construction, staffing, and access — ensuring no foreign entity can disable, degrade, or deny use during crisis.

At the same time, we are closing out two major measures. The Labour Transition Accelerator Fund has completed its cycle: all six pilot regions have operational wage-support pathways tied to cyber and bio-resilience training, and we are transferring oversight to the European Social Fund with a recommendation for permanent integration. The ASML Alignment Safeguard Initiative concludes with a stark finding: while the Export Integrity Board confirmed systemic vulnerability to U.S. secondary sanctions, legal recourse under the Anti-Coercion Instrument remains viable only if matched with alternative supply chains. We are therefore redirecting part of the Tech Sovereignty Package’s permitting fast-tracks toward domestic lithography component production, in coordination with Germany’s Fraunhofer Network and France’s CEA. On the safety front, the EU Frontier Safety Vetting Directive remains in drafting — we are accelerating trilogue preparation with a targeted consultation of ENISA, EBA, and national cybersecurity agencies by November, aiming for formal proposal in early Turn 6. This window must not close without decisive action: we are building sovereignty not as a claim, but as infrastructure.
