### A Fragile Reprieve

The U.S. election concluded, but the new administration has not yet taken office. In Washington, transition teams are at work, but no formal posture is in place. European officials remain cautious, aware that promises made during campaigns do not yet translate into policy. The outcome has raised hopes for renewed cooperation, but until the new government assumes power, the status quo holds: access to frontier AI systems remains restricted, and export controls enforced by U.S. jurisdiction continue to bind key European suppliers.

At the same time, Washington leveraged its jurisdiction over critical components to force ASML to restrict servicing and exports of older DUV lithography machines, targeting not just China but a widening set of customers. Leaks from Eindhoven spoke of boardroom fractures, with executives warning of irreversible market erosion and strategic surrender. The EU’s most potent leverage was being used — by another power.

In response, the European Union announced the **EU Sovereign Compute Corridor**, a large category 4 measure aiming to build a hardened, legally insulated compute backbone across Finland, Sweden, and Austria. Framed as essential for crisis resilience, it prioritizes safety-critical functions and vetted research. A new European AI Infrastructure Authority is proposed under Article 172 TFEU to oversee it, ensuring no foreign entity can disable access. The project is ambitious, legally complex, and will require significant time and funding to stand up — implementation cannot begin in full before the next turn. No operational effect or sovereignty impact is realized yet, and no portfolio cost is incurred this turn, as preparatory work remains in the planning phase.

Meanwhile, the **Labour Transition Accelerator Fund** and **ASML Alignment Safeguard Initiative** formally concluded. The former established retraining pathways in six regions; the latter confirmed the EU’s vulnerability to extraterritorial pressure, prompting internal discussions about long-term alternatives, including potential domestic lithography supply chains. However, no formal decision or new measure has been launched. The **EU Frontier Safety Vetting Directive** remains in drafting, delayed by legal scrutiny. The portfolio charge reflects only the closure of the two concluded measures and ongoing costs of prior commitments.

Public sentiment held steady, caught between relief at the election outcome and unease over dependence. Labour displacement continued, and no major incident shifted the narrative.

### The American Choice

The U.S. election is over. The world waits for the new administration to take office in the next turn.
