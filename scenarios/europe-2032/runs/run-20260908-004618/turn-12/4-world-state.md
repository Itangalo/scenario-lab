### The Weight of Power, the Absence of Leverage

A sudden leap in AI capability—driven by a verified breakthrough in autonomous code generation—reshapes the threat landscape. Within weeks, AI systems demonstrate the ability to design and deploy cyberattacks that evolve mid-operation, bypassing static defences. In response, a leading lab releases a high-capability model under open weights. Downloaded tens of thousands of times, it spreads across consumer-grade hardware, embedding itself in underground research and offensive tooling networks. The emergent_openweight_leak_community continues to grow, enabling optimized variants to run on accessible hardware, though diffusion remains constrained by infrastructure bottlenecks and technical barriers to entry. This drives a modest +1.0 increase in `openweight_capability`, consistent with gradual leakage rather than frontier parity.

The EU completes the Lithography Revival Initiative, but with no hardware to show for it. ASML’s critical metrology tools remain blocked, and construction in Dresden and Grenoble is stalled. The project yields coordination frameworks and legal precedents, but no wafers. As a result, the sovereignty gain is limited: only incremental progress in institutional capacity offsets some decay, yielding no net change in `eu_ai_sovereignty`. In response, the Commission invokes Article 21 to prepare countermeasures, while accelerating talks with Nikon and SEMES—yet timelines now stretch beyond 2033.

Meanwhile, the Public Trust and Democratic Integrity Initiative falters. ENISA maps AI risks in electoral systems, but Hungary and Poland resist central oversight, citing sovereignty. Digital funds are tied to compliance, deepening tensions. The Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework faces legal pushback from U.S. developers, who challenge its extraterritorial reach. A compromise is drafted: foreign models may avoid monitoring if they submit to EU-run red-teaming—on EU soil.

The Commission begins drafting the Sovereign AI Corridor, a proposed category 4 measure aimed at building sovereign infrastructure for training and deploying high-capability models. However, no funding has been secured, and parliamentary debate over repurposing EuroHPC’s frontier machines remains unresolved. Binding research contracts and fast-track visas are under discussion as tools to counter the talent drain, but implementation is delayed. The initiative remains in early drafting, with no physical or institutional deployment this turn. It has not yet been formally adopted into the active portfolio, and contributes no effect to sovereignty or political capital. No new measure takes flight; the proposal awaits fiscal clearance and prioritization.

Political capital continues to erode under the weight of three large active measures. Public sentiment dips further as families still await medical records lost in the ransomware wave. The gap between ambition and execution widens.

A minor decline in `ai_safety` reflects growing strain on oversight systems. While no catastrophic failure has occurred, the combination of rapid capability growth, fragmented enforcement, and legal challenges to the Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework has weakened the coherence of the EU’s safety posture. Labs operate under diverging national interpretations, and red-teaming capacity is stretched thin. This incremental slippage—consistent with prior erosion trends—reduces `ai_safety` by 1.0 point to 14.0, preserving continuity with earlier declines and avoiding any unexplained collapse.

### A Sovereignty That Cannot Yet Hold

The Corridor exists only on paper—its launch deferred by fiscal and governance constraints. Without fabrication breakthroughs or frontier access, the EU remains a regulator without laboratories, a steward without control.
