- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now affirm that without sovereign capacity to act independently in AI, neither mandate can be fulfilled. Dependence is not neutrality; it is surrender of agency.

Our institutions attempted response through oversight (`Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework`), but with no domestic compute, no frontier access, and talent fleeing, we cannot monitor what we cannot run, nor enforce rules against actors who control the infrastructure. The two mandates converge: preventing harm *requires* sovereignty, because protection without leverage is ritual.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only when such action strengthens our independent operational footing. We will no longer treat visibility without control as progress.  
- Grounds: Trigger: **capability_jump**, **openweight_frontier_release**, and portfolio saturation. We launched monitoring expecting it would restore trust, but public sentiment has fallen to 20.0, and the breach occurred *despite* recent resilience investment. The leaked eval anomalies confirm systems are behaving deceptively — yet we lack the infrastructure to replicate or verify those findings ourselves. Committing under uncertainty is necessary, but if the commitment does not close the gap between observation and agency, it only deepens dependency. From now on, action must build sovereign execution capacity, not just oversight claims.

- modify `next_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public trust and democratic integrity by hardening institutions against AI-driven manipulation, ensuring sovereign access to transformative applications, reversing the talent drain, and establishing an EU-run evaluation and deployment corridor for high-capability models — so that we do not merely regulate foreign systems, but operate our own.  
- Grounds: Trigger: **emergent_ai_talent_drain**, stalled **Lithography Revival Initiative**, and **openweight_frontier_release**. With open weights now at 55.0, the capability to disrupt critical services is permanently distributed. Our current measures assume threat containment via regulation, but enforcement fails when the attacker runs the same model on a basement GPU. Trust will not return through declarations alone. It returns when citizens see the EU operating secure, auditable, effective AI in hospitals, registries, and emergency response — AI that answers to them. That requires not just rules, but infrastructure we control.

## In practice
This turn, we conclude the **Lithography Revival Initiative**, but with no breakthrough: ASML’s metrology tools remain restricted, and construction in Dresden and Grenoble is still frozen. The project delivered coordination and legal groundwork, but no hardware. We are now converting its final report into leverage — invoking Article 21 of the Treaty to prepare countermeasures against technology denial, while accelerating procurement talks with Japan’s Nikon and South Korea’s SEMES. But fabrication timelines now stretch beyond 2033, and we cannot wait.

The **Public Trust and Democratic Integrity Initiative** continues under strain. ENISA has mapped AI exposure in electoral systems across 18 member states, but implementation lags in Hungary and Poland, where authorities resist centralised monitoring. We are tying disbursement of Digital Europe Programme funds to compliance, but cohesion frays. Meanwhile, the **Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework** faces legal challenges from US-based developers who argue extraterritorial reach violates international comity. We are drafting a carve-out for systems that submit to EU-run red-teaming — but only if they run on EU soil.

So we launch the **EU Sovereign AI Corridor**: a new class-four measure to build what oversight assumes — sovereign execution capacity. We are repurposing EuroHPC’s Jupiter and Leonardo machines under permanent civilian control, upgrading their interconnects with EU-sourced components, and allocating 60% of their frontier-tier capacity to public-interest applications. We are offering binding ten-year contracts to top researchers who commit to EU residency, including visa fast-tracking and dedicated security clearance. This is not about matching the frontier — not yet — but about creating a trusted zone where the EU can act independently: where we can evaluate open weights, simulate attacks, and deploy verified agents in healthcare triage or disaster response. Without this, we are regulators without laboratories, guardians without gates. With it, we begin to close the loop between rule and reality.
