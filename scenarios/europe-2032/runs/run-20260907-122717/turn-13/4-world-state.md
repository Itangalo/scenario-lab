### A Surge in Capability, a Collapse in Control

AI capability advances rapidly, reaching 74.5 as automated verification spreads across law, engineering, and clinical coding—domains once thought immune to unchecked AI progress. The frontier surges ahead, but oversight collapses: leading systems now operate with opaque reasoning, rendering human-readable interpretability obsolete overnight. No replacement safeguards are ready, and the gap between what AI can do and what anyone can verify widens dangerously.

The EU completes the Loss-of-Control Containment Framework in core financial institutions, with live telemetry now feeding into prosecutorial systems. However, Hungary remains outside the network, and bilateral incentives fail to trigger re-engagement. Meanwhile, the EU-Wide Cognitive Infrastructure Hardening Directive inches forward, with ENISA launching audits and Siemens-Thales prototypes nearing delivery. Resistance persists in Germany and France, where operators argue override mandates disrupt live systems. Liability shields ease some concerns, but full compliance remains distant.

A wave of AI-driven job losses hits graduate markets across law, accountancy, and software. In response, the EU launches the Labour Market Shock Absorption Initiative, targeting Belgium, Ireland, and Finland first. Wage insurance and retraining pipelines begin setup, but employer uptake is slow, and public messaging struggles to frame the programme as strategic rather than reactive.

Public sentiment dips further, reflecting anxiety over opaque AI and vanishing entry-level jobs. Resilience rises slightly due to containment successes and the new labour initiative’s partial rollout. However, ai_safety plummets as interpretability fails globally. Sovereignty declines due to capability growth outpacing domestic capacity, and political capital erodes under the weight of multiple large measures and a fracturing response.

### Fragile Unity, Rising Stakes

A new coalition of middle powers strengthens supply-chain leverage, improving the EU’s bargaining position. Yet internal cohesion frays under the strain of implementation, and no breakthrough in trust occurs.
