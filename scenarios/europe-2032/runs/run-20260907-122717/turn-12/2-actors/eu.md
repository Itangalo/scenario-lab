## New measure  
**Secure by Design: AI Interface Certification Standard**  
Establish a mandatory certification regime for all AI-facing control systems in critical infrastructure, ensuring they are architected to resist model-generated deception and maintain human-readable decision logs under stress.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 15  
`Targeted effect:` +6 to resilience by reducing the cognitive attack surface in automated decision chains; hardens the weakest link—trusted delegation—by enforcing design-level constraints on how systems accept AI-generated inputs  
`Applies to:` National regulators, ENISA, critical infrastructure operators, and EU-certified AI system vendors

## Priority  
**EU-Wide Cognitive Infrastructure Hardening Directive** — because it is the backbone of our systemic response to the cyber_major_incident, which exploited not code but cognition: the trust that automated systems place in one another. While the new certification standard builds for the future, this directive is the force multiplier now, retrofitting defences into systems that are already live and already compromised.

## In practice  
This turn, we complete the Loss-of-Control Containment Framework: its protocols are now operational in Poland’s central bank and three EU financial supervisors, with real-time telemetry feeding into the European Public Prosecutor’s Office and thresholds set for automatic escalation upon unauthorised agent activation. Despite Hungary’s continued refusal—rooted in its U.S. bilateral—we are deploying bilateral onboarding with Internal Security Fund support, offering to cover 40% of integration costs for any member state that joins by turn 13. The Frankfurt simulation proved the framework works; now we must ensure it covers the Union.

We press forward with the EU-Wide Cognitive Infrastructure Hardening Directive, finalising the legal package under Article 17 of the AI Act and NIS2, and securing provisional agreement from the Energy and Health Council formations. Resistance from private operators—especially in Germany’s grid management and France’s hospital networks—remains high, centred on fears of operational disruption during override testing. To counter this, we are expanding liability shielding to cover all incidents occurring during authorised override drills, funded through the Digital Europe Programme. Talks with Siemens and Thales are advancing the design of a unified override interface—a physical red button standardised across sectors and borders—while ENISA prepares for spot audits to begin in turn 13. This is not just compliance. It is the re-establishment of human final authority in systems that have quietly outsourced judgment to models.

Simultaneously, we launch the Secure by Design initiative to prevent the next generation of cognitive breaches. Drawing from the joint_threat_response, where real-time telemetry sharing revealed how attackers mimicked maintenance signals, we are drafting technical standards that require all new AI-facing systems to reject self-signed operational status updates, maintain immutable logs of delegation decisions, and expose a minimal human-readable control plane that cannot be overridden by software. Standardisation will be led by CEN-CENELEC with input from the BSI and ANSSI, and certification will be mandatory for all public procurement and EU-subsidised infrastructure. This is not a patch. It is a new architecture for trust—one that assumes models will lie, and designs accordingly.
