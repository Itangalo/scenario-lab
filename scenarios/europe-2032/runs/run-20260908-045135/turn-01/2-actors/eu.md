## New measure
Cyber Resilience Emergency Programme  
A rapid, targeted hardening of critical infrastructure against AI-generated cyber threats in response to the autumn intrusion and the major incident.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +15, eu_political_capital -5 if incident escalates further, public_sentiment +5 if damage is contained visibly  
`Applies to:` EU member states, particularly energy, water, and public services

## Priority
Cyber Resilience Emergency Programme — because the cyber_major_incident has already landed, defenders are behind, and failure to act decisively now will collapse public_sentiment and expose systemic fragility that no amount of future compute can fix.

## In practice
The cyber_test_shot and cyber_major_incident have shattered the assumption that AI-enabled attacks would arrive gradually or be detectable by existing means. We are now in a period where offensive capability, especially from open-weight models like Kimi K3, has outpaced national and cross-border defences. The intrusion into grid operators and a water utility was not targeted at any single state, which suggests probing for systemic weaknesses rather than immediate sabotage — but the tooling was AI-generated, and its stealth indicates a new order of threat.

This turn, we activate the Cyber Resilience Emergency Programme under Article 214 TFEU and the Union Civil Protection Mechanism, with the European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) and the Health Security Committee as lead coordinators. We are fast-tracking a €12bn emergency fund — drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused margins and the Digital Europe Programme — to deploy AI-driven intrusion detection systems, mandate red-teaming of all critical nodes, and require real-time logging and sharing of anomalies across energy, water, and health infrastructure. Member states must submit national action plans within four months, or risk suspension of cohesion funding. Resistance is expected from smaller states citing cost and sovereignty, but the Commission will offer 80% co-financing and technical support from the newly expanded Joint Cyber Unit.

We are not waiting for perfect consensus. The cyber_major_incident has already demonstrated that our current resilience of 39.0 is insufficient, and with openweight_capability at 40.0, this level of offensive access is now permanent. While InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech sovereignty package remain foundational for long-term eu_ai_sovereignty, they are too slow to answer this turn’s crisis. The Emergency Programme buys time — and credibility — while we build.
