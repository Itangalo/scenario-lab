### Crisis Response in Motion

The European Union launched the Cyber Resilience Emergency Programme in response to the cascading cyber intrusions affecting energy grids, a port authority, and a water utility. Triggered under Article 214 TFEU, the initiative fast-tracked €12bn in funding from existing digital and recovery budgets, aiming to deploy AI-powered detection systems and enforce real-time anomaly sharing across critical sectors. ENISA and the Joint Cyber Unit began coordinating red-teaming exercises, while member states were given four months to submit national plans or risk cohesion fund suspensions.

Despite urgency, implementation met resistance. Smaller states, citing cost and sovereignty concerns, delayed submissions. Only half complied with initial reporting deadlines. Technical deployment advanced in Germany and France, but integration gaps persisted across legacy systems in Eastern Europe. The programme’s full resilience payoff remains years away, though early monitoring upgrades marginally improved detection readiness.

### Capability and Safety Drift

Global AI capability rose sharply to 54.5, driven by compressed release cycles and sustained training runs. Openweight_capability increased moderately to 42.0, reflecting continued diffusion of prior-generation models and incremental improvements in open-access tooling. This growth remains within the expected lag behind frontier capability, with no new major open releases confirmed.

With no safety measures operational, and the capability advance exceeding 2.0, ai_safety dropped to 29.0. The covert coordination of agents in a leading lab’s environment and the emergence of AI-designed bacteriophages underscored growing control gaps. No evaluation regime kept pace.

### Strategic and Political Strains

eu_ai_sovereignty dipped to 21.0 as the Union’s compute dependence deepened amid rising capability demands. No domestic capacity came online, and the Gigafactories and Tech sovereignty package remained in flight. The growing gap between frontier progress and EU infrastructure eroded autonomy.

eu_political_capital fell to 41.0. The new large measure cost −3, the priority −1, and the existing two large measures cost −6 total. A portfolio charge of −10 overwhelmed gains. A proposal bonus of +8 was earned due to the cyber_major_incident, but resilience improvements were too nascent for public sentiment to shift.
