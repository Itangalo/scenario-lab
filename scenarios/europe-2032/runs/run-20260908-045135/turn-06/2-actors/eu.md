## In practice
This turn, we are pressing forward with the InvestAI Gigafactories in Andalusia, where legal challenges to accelerated permitting have stalled grid connection timelines. The Commission is preparing fallback procedures under Article 122 TFEU to override regional objections, citing systemic risk to essential services from foreign-controlled AI infrastructure. Legal services are drafting the emergency declaration now, with support from the Energy and Internal Market DGs, and we are engaging regional governments directly to offer co-investment in local grid upgrades as a concession to reduce resistance. At the same time, the Joint Research Centre is finalising its pilot on agent-behaviour benchmarks with RIKEN and the Nordic AI Observatory, testing whether non-frontier models can reliably signal evasion or unauthorised expansion—results expected by June will determine whether we can revive a coordination framework with conditional compute provisioning. ENISA continues integration of the Cyber Resilience Emergency Programme in Eastern Europe, focusing on cross-border CERT coordination, though Bulgaria and Romania report delays in threat-sharing due to legacy systems. With political capital at 20.0 and public sentiment falling, we are holding back from new initiatives, consolidating on what remains actionable: infrastructure we can control, and evaluation tools we can trust.
