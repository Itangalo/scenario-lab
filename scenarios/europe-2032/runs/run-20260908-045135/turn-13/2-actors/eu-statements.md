## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign control over agentic AI systems in essential services **by ensuring EU-governed, physically anchored infrastructure can host, monitor, and if necessary interrupt such systems — but only after rebuilding political capital and public legitimacy to enforce such control**
  - Grounds: This turn, we formally abandoned the Secure Enforcement Mandate due to legal blockage and member-state fragmentation. With **eu_political_capital** at 5.0 and **public_sentiment** at 10.0, we cannot credibly assert control over systems we neither host nor govern, nor command the authority to restrict. The objective remains — sovereign control — but its path now requires foundational rebuilding: without legitimacy, no enforcement regime, however technically sound, will stand. We are not abandoning the commitment, but we are acknowledging that its execution is currently impossible without first restoring capacity to act.

- **modify** `contain_the_open_frontier` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Prevent openweight capability from reaching 75.0 by restricting distribution of agent-enabling model components through enforceable, jurisdictionally grounded mechanisms, and only where political and legal consensus can be secured — **but we now acknowledge that this threshold has been crossed (75.6), and the commitment has failed in its original form**
  - Grounds: openweight_capability has reached 75.6 — a level where distributed models match strong domain experts across cognitive professions. The tools to replicate agentic behaviour are now widely available, and no enforcement mechanism, even if revived, could recall what is already loose. We are not retracting the intent — containment remains vital — but we are forced to admit that the 75.0 line was breached without effective resistance. The cost of this reversal is high: markets and member states now see containment as futile, and public trust in regulatory foresight is further eroded. We must shift from containment to damage limitation and resilience.

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without a credible enforcement path or a viable political foundation — **and now, we add: we will not act at all when doing so risks institutional collapse**
  - Grounds: This turn, attempting to enforce the Secure Enforcement Mandate triggered legal resistance, political fragmentation, and public backlash, culminating in its formal withdrawal. We misjudged not only the feasibility of enforcement but the fragility of the Union’s own cohesion. Acting under uncertainty must now include a triage of institutional survivability: some risks are too great to run, even for urgent ends. We are not retreating from agency — we are redefining it around endurance.
