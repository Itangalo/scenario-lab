## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without a credible enforcement path.
  - Grounds: This turn, we proposed a major restriction on open-weight agent components, yet lack both the mandate and the operational capacity to enforce it. ENISA cannot regulate model distribution without a legal basis, staffing, or tools. Acting without such capacity risks symbolic overreach: we signal resolve but deliver neither safety nor sovereignty. The principle of early action stands, but it must now be bounded by enforceability. We are not retreating from boldness — we are anchoring it in delivery.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `contain_the_open_frontier` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Prevent openweight capability from reaching 75.0 by restricting distribution of agent-enabling model components **through enforceable, jurisdictionally grounded mechanisms**
  - Grounds: The original commitment assumed regulatory reach over open-source platforms and EU-based developers. But with **eu_frontier_access_denied** and **supply_chain_coercion** demonstrating that real control lies elsewhere, we must now condition our actions on actual enforcement leverage. We still aim to cap openweight capability, but only through instruments that can be exercised — not merely declared. The frontier is not slowed by intent, but by chokepoints we can operate.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now acknowledge that **without sovereignty in implementation, neither mandate can be fulfilled**.
  - Grounds: This turn, we were locked out of the frontier model, and our sole remaining lever — restricting open distribution — lacks enforcement capacity. Meanwhile, Washington dictates ASML’s exports, treating our strategic chokepoint as its own policy instrument. The dual mandate remains, but its foundation has shifted: political intent is not enough. We are not abandoning either goal, but we are no longer pretending we can achieve them without operational control over infrastructure, access, and enforcement. This is not a change of purpose — it is a recognition of power.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
