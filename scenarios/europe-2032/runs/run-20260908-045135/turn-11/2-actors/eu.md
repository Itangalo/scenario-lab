## Statement changes  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without a credible enforcement path.  
  - Grounds: This turn, we proposed a major restriction on open-weight agent components, yet lack both the mandate and the operational capacity to enforce it. ENISA cannot regulate model distribution without a legal basis, staffing, or tools. Acting without such capacity risks symbolic overreach: we signal resolve but deliver neither safety nor sovereignty. The principle of early action stands, but it must now be bounded by enforceability. We are not retreating from boldness — we are anchoring it in delivery.  

- modify `contain_the_open_frontier` (commitment): Prevent openweight capability from reaching 75.0 by restricting distribution of agent-enabling model components **through enforceable, jurisdictionally grounded mechanisms**  
  - Grounds: The original commitment assumed regulatory reach over open-source platforms and EU-based developers. But with **eu_frontier_access_denied** and **supply_chain_coercion** demonstrating that real control lies elsewhere, we must now condition our actions on actual enforcement leverage. We still aim to cap openweight capability, but only through instruments that can be exercised — not merely declared. The frontier is not slowed by intent, but by chokepoints we can operate.  

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now acknowledge that **without sovereignty in implementation, neither mandate can be fulfilled**.  
  - Grounds: This turn, we were locked out of the frontier model, and our sole remaining lever — restricting open distribution — lacks enforcement capacity. Meanwhile, Washington dictates ASML’s exports, treating our strategic chokepoint as its own policy instrument. The dual mandate remains, but its foundation has shifted: political intent is not enough. We are not abandoning either goal, but we are no longer pretending we can achieve them without operational control over infrastructure, access, and enforcement. This is not a change of purpose — it is a recognition of power.

## Priority  
**Secure Enforcement Mandate for Open-Weight Controls** — because without legal and operational capacity, our restriction on agent-enabling components is unenforceable, and our only remaining lever to slow the open frontier collapses.

## In practice  
This turn, we are shifting from declaration to enforceability. The draft of *Prevent Open-Weight Agentic Leap* remains on the table, but we now anchor it in a credible enforcement path. We are submitting a proposal to the European Parliament and Council under Article 114 TFEU to amend the AI Act, granting ENISA explicit authority to regulate the release of AI components that enable persistent goal pursuit, autonomous tool use, and memory retention. This is not a ban on open models — it is a licensing regime for the architectures that make them agentic. We are pairing this with a €1.2bn operational envelope to build ENISA’s technical capacity: hiring 150 specialists in model auditing, deploying automated screening tools for model uploads, and establishing a fast-response unit for emergency takedowns.  

We are negotiating with the Dutch and French governments to secure early political backing, offering in return expanded EU liability shielding for compliant developers and fast-track access to EuroHPC compute for those who submit to pre-release review. The German Constitutional Court has raised concerns about proportionality, so we are narrowing the scope to components trained on leaked agent frameworks or fine-tuned for autonomous operation in high-risk domains. We are also engaging with Hugging Face and LMNT to co-design upload filters, offering them safe harbour from liability if they implement EU-standard screening. Resistance remains strong from open science advocates and the European Research Council, but the **eu_frontier_access_denied** incident has shifted the debate: if we cannot access the frontier, we must at least control what leaks from it. This is not sovereignty by creation — it is sovereignty by containment. And for now, containment is all we have.
