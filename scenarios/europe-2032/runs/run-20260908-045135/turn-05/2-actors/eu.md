Secure sovereign access to controlled AI systems

## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign access to controlled AI systems``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The previous commitment to build sovereign AI capacity was necessary but insufficient. With global capability now at 63.5 and the U.S. consolidating strategic control over frontier systems, mere compute or infrastructure ownership no longer guarantees autonomy. What matters is access to *controlled*, *evaluated*, and *operationally secured* systems — not just raw capacity. The Union must pivot from building hardware to ensuring that the AI systems it depends on are both within its jurisdiction and under its authority, regardless of where they were developed.

## Priority
**InvestAI Gigafactories** — because with the Middle Power Pact now concluded and its enforcement still untested, the only lever the EU has to guarantee future access is its own physical infrastructure; and because the erosion of eu_ai_sovereignty to 18.0 means delay is no longer an option.

## In practice
This turn, we are finalising the agent-behaviour benchmark pilot with the Joint Research Centre, RIKEN, and the Nordic AI Observatory, using non-frontier models to test whether observable metrics can reliably predict autonomous expansion or evasion. The results will feed into a post-pact assessment of whether the Netherlands and other members are willing to condition compute provisioning on compliance. Simultaneously, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories in Andalusia, where the pilot for accelerated permitting under the Tech sovereignty package is now under legal challenge; we are preparing fallback procedures under Article 122 TFEU to override regional objections if grid delays threaten the 2030 deployment timeline. ENISA has completed handover of the Cyber Resilience Emergency Programme in Bulgaria and Romania, with national CERTs now operating the new detection modules, though integration with broader Eastern European networks remains patchy. Political capital remains strained at 27.0, and without a clear path to binding enforcement through the Middle Power Pact, we are consolidating our position around infrastructure we can build unilaterally — not because it is ideal, but because it is the only ground still under our control.
