## New measure
**Prevent Open-Weight Agentic Leap**  
Restrict the public release of model weights, training data, and agent frameworks that enable persistent goal pursuit in high-risk domains.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` reduce the rate of openweight_capability growth by blocking key enablers; aim to cap it below 75.0 for at least 12 months  
`Applies to:` EU-based developers, open-source platforms hosted in the EU, and any entity receiving EU funding or market access  

## Priority  
**Prevent Open-Weight Agentic Leap** — because the Emergency Sovereign Access Programme has failed to deliver sovereign capability at scale, and with openweight systems now nearing autonomy thresholds, the most urgent lever we have to prevent lasting harm is to slow the distributed frontier before it becomes uncontrollable.

## In practice  
This turn, we are pivoting from failed sovereignty-by-decree to sovereignty-by-denial: if we cannot yet field our own agentic systems in essential services, we must at least ensure no one else—not on our soil, not in our supply chains, not under our jurisdiction—can release the components that make such systems trivial to replicate. The European Commission is drafting a delegated act under Article 114 TFEU, this time justified not by internal market continuity but by systemic risk to public health and security. It will classify agent-enabling model components—those that support persistent goal pursuit, memory, and autonomous tool use—as dual-use AI assets, subject to pre-release review and licensing. Platforms like Hugging Face and EU-funded open initiatives will be required to screen uploads against a dynamically updated list of restricted architectures and training methodologies. Enforcement will fall to ENISA, with support from national cybersecurity agencies.  

We are coordinating with the Middle Power Pact members to align definitions and avoid fragmentation, but we are acting unilaterally where necessary—this cannot wait for consensus. The Dutch government, under pressure from ASML shareholders, has signaled openness to export-style controls on knowledge flows if framed as risk containment. We are offering legal indemnity to developers who comply, and fast-track access to EU compute for those who submit to voluntary pre-release audits. Resistance will come from open science advocates and some member states, but the loss_of_control_incident and bio_uplift_findings have shifted the burden of proof: the default can no longer be openness. We are not banning open models—we are banning the open distribution of the scaffolding that turns them into unaccountable agents. If we succeed, we buy time. If we fail, the frontier goes fully open, and our last lever breaks.
