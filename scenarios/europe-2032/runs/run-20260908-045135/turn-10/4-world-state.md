### Frontier Acceleration and Safety Erosion

Global AI capability advances by +3.5 to **81.5**, driven by self-modifying agent systems now autonomously redesigning biological pathways and supply logistics. A new genome model produces a design that, while not yet tested, raises alarms in technical circles for its potential dual-use implications. The work is circulating in preprint form, prompting urgent discussion among biosecurity experts about thresholds for concern, though no confirmed misuse has occurred.

Despite growing alarm, **ai_safety** remains at **0.0**. While opaque reasoning is now systemic and no safety upgrades have been deployed, the metric does not drop below zero this turn, as the scale has not previously extended into negative territory and no calibrated threshold for systemic failure has been established. The absence of a formal safety framework or containment breach keeps the metric at baseline, though downward pressure is accumulating.

Open-weight models, trained on leaked agent frameworks and fine-tuned across distributed clusters, rise to **70.0**, narrowing the gap with the closed frontier. Persistent goal pursuit is now reproducible outside labs, raising concerns about autonomous misuse in the absence of governance.

### EU Pivots to Containment

The European Commission begins drafting **Prevent Open-Weight Agentic Leap**, a proposed Category 3 measure aimed at restricting the release of agent-enabling components. Framed as a systemic risk to public health, the draft invokes Article 114 TFEU to classify key architectures as dual-use. However, the measure remains in early development: no legal finalization, operational framework, or staffing plan has been adopted, and ENISA—tentatively designated as the future oversight body—currently lacks the mandate, tools, or capacity to enforce such controls.

As a result, the measure is not yet formally entered into the EU’s policy portfolio and does not generate a portfolio charge this turn. Implementation will require at least one full turn of preparation, including inter-institutional consultation, capacity planning, and potential mandate expansion for oversight agencies. No enforcement can begin until these prerequisites are met.

Coordination with Middle Power Pact members is partial—Japan and the Netherlands express cautious support, but South Korea resists, citing innovation costs. Open science groups mobilize, warning of chilling effects. The Dutch government, under pressure from ASML shareholders, signals conditional backing if controls are narrowly scoped.

### Sentiment and Capital Sink Further

With no visible benefit and rising fears of uncontrolled AI, **public_sentiment** drops to **10.0**, entering active hostility. **resilience** falls to **36.0** as capability growth outpaces protection. **eu_ai_sovereignty** declines to **16.0** due to capability rise (−1) and no completion bonus.

**eu_political_capital** remains at **9.0**, avoiding further decline, as no new measure has been formally adopted to generate a portfolio charge. The abandonment of the **Emergency Sovereign Access Programme** is acknowledged, but its cancellation does not trigger an immediate capital loss, as the paralysis charge was already accounted for in prior turns. Political momentum stalls, but no additional penalties apply without a new, failed commitment.
