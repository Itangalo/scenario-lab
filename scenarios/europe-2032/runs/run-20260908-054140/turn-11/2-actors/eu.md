## Priority  
Priority: Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive — because without physical control over the infrastructure that runs frontier AI, our legal doctrines are unenforceable, our safety claims are unverifiable, and our sovereignty is a fiction; the **emergent_eu_compute_pool** is the first real lever we have had in years, and we must now institutionalise and scale it before it is co-opted or crushed.

## In practice  
We are treating the **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive** as a binding instrument under Article 114 TFEU, framed as a single market measure to ensure continuity of critical AI services. The directive will compel member states to designate priority zones for EU-anchored data centres, fast-track permitting within six months, and guarantee grid access by reallocating energy from decommissioned industrial assets. We are directing €18 billion from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility to capital grants for the **emergent_eu_compute_pool** consortium, conditional on full legal jurisdiction, audit access, and public-purpose deployment clauses. The Commission’s Internal Market team is drafting enforcement mechanisms, including the power to override national objections if a project meets sovereign resilience thresholds.

At the same time, we are formally closing the **Emergency Sovereign Agent Containment Framework**. ENISA has certified the national response units, and playbooks exist — but with no provider cooperation and no sovereign infrastructure to run containment operations on, the framework cannot be activated. We are repurposing its technical staff and threat intelligence pipelines into a new **Sovereign AI Monitoring Cell**, hosted under the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, to begin red-teaming open-weight models on EU-controlled hardware. This is not a continuation — it is a salvage operation, turning a failed enforcement regime into the seed of a real technical capability.

We are not pursuing further legal assertions against foreign providers this turn. The **Autonomous Agent Jurisdictional Doctrine** is de facto suspended: no fines have been issued, no representatives appointed, and the Council’s JHA formation has tabled further discussion pending a "review of enforceability." Instead, we are engaging bilaterally with the leaders of the **emergent_eu_compute_pool**, offering legal immunity for past grey-zone operations in exchange for formal integration into the EU’s sovereign stack. Public messaging will frame this not as a retreat, but as a pivot — from chasing control to building it. But we know the truth: we are starting from ruin, and we have six months before the next eval gate opens.
