## In practice
We are treating the cyber_test_shot as a wake-up call: the intrusions were not just deep but invisible to current monitoring, and their non-targeting of any single jurisdiction suggests an adaptive, possibly learning adversary. This cannot be left to national agencies alone. Under Article 114 TFEU, we are drafting a revised NIS3 Directive — calling it the Cyber Shield Directive — that mandates AI-augmented threat detection across electricity, water, ports and rail networks. ENISA will lead technical specifications, working with ETSI and CERT-EU, while the Commission fast-tracks funding from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU recovery funds to co-finance deployment in lower-capacity member states.

Resistance is expected from several member states citing cost and sovereignty, especially around red-teaming mandates that could expose systemic weaknesses. To overcome this, we are pairing the directive with a conditional access mechanism: full compliance unlocks preferential rates on EU-wide cyber reinsurance pools currently under negotiation in the Council. At the same time, we are engaging industry through the European Cybersecurity Industrial Alliance to ensure solutions are interoperable and not locked to single vendors. The proposal will go to COREPER II by month’s end, with a trilogue push scheduled before year-end — tight, but necessary given the six-month gap between detection failure and regulatory response.
