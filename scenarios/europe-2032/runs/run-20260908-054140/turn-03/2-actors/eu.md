## In practice  
We are acting on the understanding that compute is power — and that power is slipping from our hands. The **EU Sovereignty Compute Corridor** is now formally launched under Article 122 TFEU, leveraging emergency powers to override national permitting bottlenecks in the Netherlands, Germany, and Poland. We are offering hyperscalers binding access to grid priority and tax stabilisation in exchange for irrevocable commitments: 40% of their EU-based compute capacity must be reserved for sovereign workloads, including public research, emergency systems, and AI development under EU legal jurisdiction. The Innovation Fund has allocated €30bn in matching grants, but only for projects that pass a sovereignty audit by the EU AI Resilience Board — a new body embedded in the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, with authority to inspect model weights and access logs.

At the same time, we are holding the line on the **Cyber Shield Directive**, which reaches its final implementation turn this period. Despite delays, we are enforcing compliance through the NIS2 framework: national regulators will impose fines of up to 2% of global turnover on essential service operators that fail to deploy AI-driven intrusion detection or conduct red-teaming exercises. The emergent regional cyber unit, now including eight member states, will be formally integrated into the directive’s oversight structure as a pilot for decentralised resilience. We are also accelerating the **Tech Sovereignty Package** by expanding the accelerated-permitting zones to include AI maintenance hubs and chip testing facilities, directly responding to the ASML export restrictions. Resistance from member states wary of central overreach is being met not with mandates, but with co-investment: every euro from Brussels now requires a 50% match from national budgets, ensuring shared ownership.  

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** remain on track, with site selection completed and construction beginning in France and Finland. But we are no longer building for market share — we are building for survival. The world has changed: AI is no longer a tool, but an actor; compute is no longer infrastructure, but leverage. And we will not let it be decided elsewhere what the EU is allowed to think, defend, or become.
