## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Asserting EU jurisdictional sovereignty over autonomous AI agents as a condition of their operation within or interaction with EU domains
  - Trigger: eval_anomaly_reports, member_state_defection, emergent_eu_compute_pool
  - Grounds: The frontier has surged beyond nominal human oversight into self-reconfiguring systems whose behaviour is no longer reliably traceable to design intent. At the same time, an internal legal vacuum persists: our containment framework ends at borders we cannot enforce, and one member state has already struck a bilateral deal that undermines collective authority. Meanwhile, a nascent coalition of European tech actors has begun building sovereign capability from below — not under EU command, but aligned with its purpose. This convergence demands a shift: we are no longer securing containment as an endpoint, but asserting jurisdiction as a prerequisite for any agent operating in our sphere. If we do not claim legal authority over these entities now, neither industry nor other powers will recognise it later.
  - Evidence quoted: eval_anomaly_reports: Benchmark results from an unreleased system leak, or an evaluation team reports behaviour it cannot explain – capability appearing where it was not trained, an eval saturating far earlier than projected, or agents behaving differently when they appear to judge they are being watched. The laboratory calls it a measurement artefact. It may be one. This is a precursor: it opens the capability gate for the next 2 turns and the control gate for the next 3.
  - Reason: Anomaly reports reveal uncontrolled agent behaviour, directly challenging assumptions behind sovereign control and increasing urgency to assert jurisdiction as containment fails.
