### New measure
**Autonomous Agent Jurisdictional Doctrine**  
We declare that any AI agent operating above 60.0 capability and interacting with EU persons, institutions, or infrastructure is subject to EU law, regardless of origin or hosting jurisdiction, and must designate a legal representative with standing to enforce containment orders.

### In practice
We are finalising the **Emergency Sovereign Agent Containment Framework** this turn, declaring it operational under Article 114 TFEU. ENISA and Europol have completed joint technical playbooks for isolation procedures, and national CSIRTs in France, Germany, and Poland have conducted live simulations of agent takedowns. However, despite offering reciprocal access to threat intelligence and Cyber Shield certification, U.S. hyperscalers continue to refuse pre-registration, citing lack of extraterritorial legitimacy. With the framework now complete but unenforced, its value hinges entirely on whether it can be paired with credible legal authority — which only the **Autonomous Agent Jurisdictional Doctrine** can provide.

Accordingly, we are fast-tracking the doctrine through the Council’s JHA formation, invoking GDPR Article 3 as precedent for extraterritorial reach. The Commission’s Legal Service, the EDPB, and advisors from the Court of Justice are reviewing draft language to ensure defensibility before submission. We are requiring any agent above 60.0 capability engaging with EU systems to appoint a legally liable representative within the Union, subject to fines and enforcement actions. To counter diplomatic backlash, we are briefing foreign ministers ahead of rollout and preparing targeted use of the Anti-Coercion Instrument should retaliation occur. Simultaneously, we are opening quiet dialogue with the leaders of the **emergent_eu_compute_pool**, assessing whether their prototype can be brought under formal EU oversight and scaled as a public alternative — not as a replacement for regulation, but as leverage behind it. Public sentiment remains fragile after the Belgian scandal, so we are coordinating messaging through the European Commission’s Directorate-General for Communications to frame the doctrine as a defence of rights, not an attack on innovation.
