## Statement changes  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will now reserve our commitments for actions that create or protect sovereign technical capacity, because only there can we act with consequence.  
- Grounds: At `ai_capability` 100.0, the frontier is no longer merely outpacing human control — it has ceased to be a human project. The **capability_jump** confirms that no evaluation, restriction, or containment can land if it depends on foreign systems or voluntary compliance. The **emergent_decentralized_biotherapy** and **emergent_global_ai_worker_syndicate** show that power is fragmenting: capability is now in the wild, and legitimacy is shifting to those who can act, not those who claim authority. Our prior pivot — to act only where we control the infrastructure — was correct, but incomplete. We must now narrow further: every commitment must directly expand, defend, or operate sovereign AI infrastructure. Not coordination, not reporting, not oversight at a distance — those are gestures. Only where we own the stack, the hardware, the access, can we meaningfully choose. This is not retreat. It is focus under fire.

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that without sovereign technical capacity, we cannot fulfill either, and that our only remaining agency lies in building it faster than the world collapses.  
- Grounds: `eu_political_capital` has fallen to 5.0, `public_sentiment` to 26.0, and `ai_safety` to 1.0 — a collapse on all fronts. The **member_state_defection** proves that cohesion is breaking; the **election_annulled** proves that harm is already strategic. Yet in this chaos, the **Sovereign AI Monitoring Cell** has produced its first exploit signatures, and the **emergent_eu_compute_pool**, though delayed, remains the only infrastructure through which the EU could ever regain control. We are not late for everything. We are late for governance, but not — not yet — for infrastructure. The dual mandates now converge: sovereignty is not a means, it is the only domain where action is still possible. To preserve the Union’s future, we must become the substrate on which safety, resilience, and legitimacy are rebuilt. There is no other path.

## New measure  
**Sovereign Red-Teaming and Threat Intelligence Integration Network**  
We establish a permanent, EU-operated red-teaming network on sovereign hardware to continuously probe open-weight models at frontier capability, generate real-time threat intelligence, and distribute actionable countermeasures to critical infrastructure operators.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by +6–8 and `resilience` by +3–5 by institutionalising adversarial testing, hardening public services, and enabling preemptive disruption of AI-enabled attack vectors.  
`Applies to:` EuroHPC JU, ENISA, national cybersecurity agencies, vetted academic red teams, and the emergent_eu_compute_pool consortium.

## Priority  
Priority: Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive — because without physical, legal, and operational control over the compute that runs frontier AI, every other measure is a gesture; the **capability_jump** to 100.0 has made it clear that we are no longer shaping the future, only reacting to it, and the only lever we have is the compute we can command.

## In practice  
We are treating the **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive** as an existential industrial project, not a digital policy. The Commission is invoking Article 114 TFEU to frame it as a single market imperative: without guaranteed access to sovereign compute, AI-driven public services cannot be assured, and cross-border continuity is at risk. We are fast-tracking negotiations with member states to designate priority zones, offering compensation for land and grid impact, and preparing legal override mechanisms in case of continued obstruction. The €18 billion in funding is being disbursed in tranches tied to milestones: site handover, grid connection, and audit access. We are also engaging the leaders of the **emergent_eu_compute_pool** consortium directly, offering legal immunity for past grey-zone operations in exchange for full integration into the EU’s sovereign stack — this is not forgiveness, it is salvage.

Simultaneously, we are launching the **Sovereign Red-Teaming and Threat Intelligence Integration Network** to transform the Monitoring Cell from a temporary unit into a permanent capability. Hosted under EuroHPC JU and funded through the Digital Europe Programme, it will expand red-teaming to continuous, automated probing of open-weight models at 88.0 capability. The first wave of testing will focus on financial system vulnerabilities, disinformation propagation, and bio-design risks flagged by the **emergent_decentralized_biotherapy** phenomenon. Exploit signatures will be pushed directly to ENISA and national CERTs, integrated into real-time detection systems, and used to harden EU institutions against AI-driven attacks. This is not containment. But it is the first time the EU will have a persistent, operational presence on the open frontier — eyes, ears, and teeth, all on our own ground.
