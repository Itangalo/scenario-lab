### The Frontier Leaps, Control Slips

A sudden leap in AI capability reshapes the landscape: a leading lab releases an agent system that autonomously designs, tests and deploys software across complex enterprise environments, marking a discontinuous advance. `ai_capability` surges by +5.5, crossing the 60.0 threshold — the point at which AI completes multi-day professional projects end to end. This **capability_jump** triggers widespread reassessment of deployment timelines, as systems once expected in 2030 arrive today.

Simultaneously, the US pressures the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s export of mature-node lithography tools — not just to China, but to a widening set of third countries. The move, justified as non-proliferation, effectively weaponises the EU’s own partial supply-chain leverage. The **supply_chain_coercion** event exposes a bitter truth: Europe’s access to the physical foundation of AI can be revoked unilaterally.

### Ambition Mounts, But Not Yet Launched

In response, the European Commission drafts the **EU Sovereignty Compute Corridor**, a proposed category 4 initiative to fast-track sovereign data centres through emergency powers, tax incentives and talent access in exchange for binding commitments on jurisdiction and model control. It is designated as the intended new priority, pending consensus.

However, member states immediately balk at ceding permitting authority, and hyperscalers demand guarantees beyond what the Commission can currently offer. No agreement is reached on funding mechanisms or governance structure. Without cross-institutional alignment, the measure remains in preparation and does not formally launch. As such, it incurs no political capital cost, delivers no immediate sovereignty benefit, and does not displace the existing priority.

The Cyber Shield Directive remains the active priority. The portfolio charge holds steady.

### Safety Erodes, Capital Holds

With the **capability_jump**, `ai_safety` falls by −12 to 19.0 due to opaque reasoning and loss of control. The Cyber Shield Directive, though in flight, delivers no resilience benefit yet. `resilience` holds at 40.0, but only because no incident occurs — not because defences are ready.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` remains at 21.0: −1 from capability growth, −2 from **supply_chain_coercion**, offset by +3 from proposal activity (early design work on the Compute Corridor). There is no additional drain from active measures beyond prior commitments.

`openweight_capability` rises to 51.0 (+4.0), reflecting diffusion of recent open-source releases and modest infrastructure gains, but grows more slowly than `ai_capability`, preserving the invariant that openweight never exceeds frontier capability and does not close the gap unrealistically.

Public sentiment holds, but the gap between ambition and delivery widens.
