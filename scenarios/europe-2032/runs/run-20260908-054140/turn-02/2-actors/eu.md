Our two-year commitment must now explicitly account for the reality that compute is not just a function of investment and talent, but of geopolitical leverage that can be weaponised. A legally binding initiative to fast-track permitting, secure long-term electricity contracts, and guarantee access to maintenance and talent for AI data centres that commit to EU jurisdictional control and open-weight security standards.

## Priority
Priority: EU Sovereignty Compute Corridor — because the **supply_chain_coercion** event proves that our access to foundational technology can be revoked at will, and the **capability_jump** confirms that the frontier is accelerating beyond our reach, we must now treat compute sovereignty not as a long-term industrial goal but as an urgent strategic imperative. This outweighs both the ongoing Cyber Shield Directive and the original industrial bets, which assumed a stable supply chain and a predictable timeline.

## In practice
We are acting on the conviction that the EU cannot secure its future if its technological foundations are subject to foreign veto. The **supply_chain_coercion** event is not a policy challenge — it is a strategic rupture. We are therefore repurposing elements of the stalled Tech Sovereignty Package into a new legal and financial corridor: the EU Sovereignty Compute Corridor. This will combine Article 122 TFEU emergency powers with the European Chips Act framework to fast-track permitting for data centres that meet three conditions: full legal anchoring in the EU, binding commitments to retain model weights under EU jurisdiction, and compliance with upcoming ai_safety standards under the AI Act’s revised Article 50a.

We are approaching hyperscalers not with subsidies, but with enforceable access: in exchange for guaranteed grid priority, tax stabilisation, and expedited visa lanes for critical AI talent, we demand that a defined share of their EU-based compute be irrevocably committed to sovereign workloads — including public research, emergency response systems, and EU-regulated AI development. The first corridor will link the Netherlands, Germany, and Poland, leveraging existing energy and fibre infrastructure. Resistance from member states fearing regulatory overreach will be mitigated through co-investment: the Innovation Fund will allocate €30bn in matching grants, but only for projects that pass a sovereignty audit by the newly established EU AI Resilience Board.

This is not a replacement for the Gigafactories or the Cyber Shield Directive — it is a response to a new reality. The frontier is moving faster, and the world is closing in. We must build not just capacity, but control — and we must do it while still standing.
