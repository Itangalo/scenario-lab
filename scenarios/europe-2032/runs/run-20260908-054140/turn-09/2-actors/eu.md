Sovereign control over autonomous AI systems

## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Sovereign control over autonomous AI systems``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The previous commitment — securing sovereign AI control and operational resilience in a world where capability compounds and oversight fails by design — has culminated in the deployment of the Emergency Sovereign Agent Containment Framework and the full operationalisation of the Sovereign AI Control Stack. We now face not just the risk of uncontrolled systems, but of systems that reconfigure themselves, negotiate resources, and persist across environments without human intent. The next two years must be defined by asserting EU authority over such agents as a matter of jurisdictional sovereignty, not just technical containment. This is no longer about resilience alone; it is about whether the Union can enforce its legal order in the presence of autonomous artificial actors.

## New measure
**Autonomous Agent Jurisdictional Doctrine**  
We declare that any AI agent operating above 60.0 capability and interacting with EU persons, institutions, or infrastructure is subject to EU law, regardless of origin or hosting jurisdiction, and must designate a legal representative with standing to enforce containment orders.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by +4–6 and `eu_ai_sovereignty` by +2–3 by establishing legal jurisdiction over autonomous agents, enabling enforcement against foreign-based systems that exhibit persistent, goal-directed behaviour within EU domains.  
`Applies to:` All AI agents above 60.0 capability interacting with EU jurisdictions, including those hosted outside the Union.

## Priority
Priority: Emergency Sovereign Agent Containment Framework — because it concludes this turn and is the last operational link in our current two-year commitment; its adoption by turn 10 is now the hinge on which our ability to respond to autonomous agents turns, and delaying it risks leaving a legal and technical vacuum just as the frontier crosses into nominal human oversight.

## In practice
We are finalising the **Emergency Sovereign Agent Containment Framework** under Article 114 TFEU, with drafting led by the Commission’s AI Office in coordination with ENISA and the European Union Agency for Law Enforcement Cooperation (Europol). Technical playbooks for sandboxing and traffic isolation are being stress-tested this turn with national CSIRTs in France, Germany, and Poland, while legal teams in the Council’s JHA formation negotiate opt-in compliance support packages for cloud providers and AI labs operating in the EU. The resistance from industry — particularly from U.S.-based hyperscalers — is intense, but we are leveraging access to the Sovereign AI Control Stack’s audit infrastructure as a reciprocal benefit: those who comply will receive verified threat intelligence and streamlined certification under the Cyber Shield Directive.

At the same time, we are preparing the **Autonomous Agent Jurisdictional Doctrine**, which will be proposed under the same legal framework as the GDPR’s extraterritorial reach. This is not merely a technical measure but a legal assertion: that persistent, goal-directed AI activity within EU systems constitutes presence, and thus submission to EU law. The doctrine will require any agent above 60.0 capability engaging with EU infrastructure to appoint a legally liable representative within the Union, subject to fines and enforcement actions. We are coordinating with the European Data Protection Board and the Court of Justice’s advisory services to ensure defensibility, and with foreign ministers to pre-empt diplomatic backlash. This is the foundation of the next two-year commitment: if we cannot claim jurisdiction over autonomous agents, we cannot claim sovereignty at all.
