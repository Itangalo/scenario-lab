### Holding the line
July to December 2032 was a half-year of maintenance. Brussels proposed nothing new, and executed what it had.

The benefits-algorithm redress ground forward. The serious-incident procedure stayed open through autumn: ombudsmen and rights bodies worked through flagged cases, compensation payments trickled out, and the AI Office published further audit extracts showing logs unread and approvals timed in seconds. No rewrite of the AI law was tabled, as promised. Victim groups called the pace insulting; industry groups called the procedure a warning that enforcement could bite. Both kept the story alive.

Siting mediation produced the same split result. Grid-hardening work and rebate payments restarted construction at two contested computing sites, with auditable timelines posted at town halls. Two other sites stayed blocked, with access roads intermittently obstructed and councils keeping freeze motions on the agenda without passing them. Contractors billed delay costs from existing budgets.

Containment held. Separation gates and telemetry sharing stayed certified, and autumn drills again restored connected districts in days. Eastern and southern districts outside full coverage lagged, and the two-Europes commentary hardened. Accelerator stockpiling was quietly extended; synthesis screening stayed tight. No new law, no new posture.

Offices kept using AI assistants heavily, with junior productivity gains confirmed in new studies. It did not move public mood much. With no new shock and no visible payoff from repair work, trust stayed near the floor — slightly less hostile where compensation arrived, unchanged elsewhere.
