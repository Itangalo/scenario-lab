### Repair work under fire
January to June 2032 was the half-year Brussels tried to buy back trust while keeping the lights on.

The test was the benefits algorithm. An ombudsman and then a court found a social-insurance decision support system had systematically cut or flagged vulnerable claimants, with logs generated and never read and caseworkers clearing queues in under a minute. The Commission chose the enforcement-failure reading: the system was high-risk, the conformity papers were fiction, oversight had collapsed. It opened a serious-incident procedure, ordered case-by-case review and compensation through ombudsmen and rights bodies, and published audit extracts. It pointedly tabled no rewrite of the AI law.

That choice did not settle the argument. Press and parliaments replayed queues approved at forty seconds an item as proof enforcement was discredited; lawyers and NGOs argued the opposite lesson. Restriction became cheap. Two mayors facing municipal blackouts linked the scandal to planned computing sites, and coordinated blockades of surveyors and access roads delayed extensions through spring. Councils debated temporary freezes on new sites. No general moratorium passed, but contractors lost months.

Mediation bought partial relief. Host towns were offered grid-hardening, fee rebates from existing budgets and a share of local benefit in exchange for auditable timelines and lifting blockade threats. Some sites resumed; others stayed contested.

Meanwhile the isolation programme finished. Separation gates, emergency stops and telemetry sharing were certified across essential services, and restoration drills that had taken weeks now took days in connected districts. Engineers called it containment landed, not resilience solved.

The wider world intruded quietly. Extended exercises around the Taiwan Strait lifted shipping insurance and chip anxiety; Brussels extended spare-accelerator stockpiling without a public posture. Synthesis screening stayed tightened after the autumn genome-design paper, with no new bio law.

Ordinary offices told a different story. Studies across law, accountancy, administration and consulting showed clear productivity gains, largest for juniors, without matching job losses. Firms that had cut early rehired. It barely lifted mood against scandal and blackouts.
