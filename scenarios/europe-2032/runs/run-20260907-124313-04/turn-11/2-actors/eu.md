## New measure
**Secure Model Repository for Public Evaluators**  
Establish a legally protected, air-gapped environment where vetted researchers from EU institutions can access and evaluate frontier model weights under strict security protocols.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size:` small

## In practice
This turn, we consolidate our shift from trial to operational defence. The **Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme** concludes with mixed results: while Germany and Finland have deployed neuromorphic nodes in grid-control systems, delays in certification and supply-chain bottlenecks limited rollout elsewhere. Still, the programme delivers enough hardened local inference capacity to justify its closure on schedule — lessons learned will feed into future procurement rules under the Digital Markets Act review. We treat it as closed and do not extend it.

The **Autonomous System Boundary Trials** continue into their second year, now expanding from controlled environments to shadow-deployment scenarios where certified safeguards run alongside live systems without intervention. This phase focuses on stress-testing containment logic under real-world load and adversarial simulation. The AI Office coordinates with ENISA and national agencies in France, Poland, and Italy to integrate findings into the upcoming revision of the AI Act’s high-risk classification criteria. Industry pushback remains, particularly from cloud providers who argue that boundary enforcement degrades performance — we respond by publishing benchmark data showing sub-3% overhead in tested configurations.

Simultaneously, we launch the **Secure Model Repository for Public Evaluators**, a small but strategically vital step toward closing the knowledge gap between developers and regulators. Using secured facilities at the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking in Prague and Bologna, we enable trusted researchers to conduct deep-dive analyses of model internals under conditions that satisfy both intellectual property and national security concerns. Access requires dual clearance — one from the host member state, one from the AI Office — and all findings are subject to a 90-day embargo to allow for remediation. This builds directly on the sentinel deployment: anomalies detected in the field can now be traced back to structural features in the weights themselves, creating a feedback loop from operation to design. We fund this with €180 million from Horizon Europe, reprioritised from lower-urgency AI ethics projects.

Public sentiment remains low, and we face rising resistance from tech firms framing sentinels as “government surveillance.” To counter this, we launch a transparency campaign focused on the opt-in liability shield: operators who fully integrate the sentinels and pass six months of clean monitoring receive automatic qualification for reduced penalties under the AI Liability Directive. Early adopters in the Dutch healthcare sector report strong interest, seeing it as a way to manage risk amid growing malpractice concerns. We also open a fast-track channel for SMEs to receive integration support from the Digital Innovation Hubs network, reducing friction in adoption. Despite these efforts, rollout remains uneven — but the direction is irreversible. We are no longer preparing to act. We are acting.
