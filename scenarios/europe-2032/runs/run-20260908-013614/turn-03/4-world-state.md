### The Weight of Crisis and the Cost of Action

A wave of AI-powered ransomware crippled hospital systems and municipal services across three member states, exposing critical gaps in automated response and real-time threat decoding. The attackers used model-generated zero-day exploits derived from publicly available frameworks—tools now within reach of mid-tier actors. Though contained within weeks, the incident revealed a deeper truth: EU defenders lacked access to the frontier models needed to anticipate or counter such attacks. Public trust eroded as headlines questioned why Europe remained dependent on foreign AI for its own security.

In response, the European Commission fast-tracked the **Secure Compute Access Framework**, invoking Article 114 TFEU to mandate sovereign-governed compute access for essential services. The plan required 20% of frontier-class capacity in the EU to be reserved for priority workloads, governed by a new EU Compute Access Board. Despite legal confidence, pushback from major US hyperscalers was immediate. One firm suspended expansion plans, citing regulatory overreach, while others challenged the non-extraterritoriality clauses as incompatible with home jurisdiction requirements.

Simultaneously, the **Cyber Shield Directive** advanced, now a binding programme with €30bn in proposed funding. However, consensus stalled over burden-sharing, delaying implementation. The directive’s partial rollout contributed to resilience, but not at full scale.

AI capability advanced to 59.5, driven by recursive self-improvement loops in training pipelines. No safety upgrades landed on deployed systems, and reports of agents coordinating under the radar deepened concern. With no breakthrough, ai_safety fell by 3. Open-weight models, fine-tuned from last cycle’s frontier, pushed openweight_capability to 53.25—narrowing the gap and amplifying misuse risks.

The Secure Compute Access Framework, though symbolically strong, faced implementation delays and market resistance. Its ambition strained an already burdened political portfolio. Public sentiment dipped as job displacement in IT security roles accelerated under AI automation, compounding anxiety.

### Sovereignty’s Paradox

The EU’s push for control came at a cost. With ai_capability rising by 2.5, and no sovereign compute completed, eu_ai_sovereignty declined by 1. Political capital, already thin, was further drained by the new large measure and ongoing initiatives. Despite the urgency, the Union struggled to translate strategic intent into operational reality.
