## New measure  
**Secure Compute Access Framework**  
Establish a legally binding, auditable framework to guarantee EU institutions and essential public services priority, sovereign-governed access to a defined tier of high-performance AI compute, sourced from within the Union and under enforceable non-extraterritoriality clauses.

## Priority  
**Secure Compute Access Framework** — because the cyber_major_incident, combined with the persistent erosion of eu_ai_sovereignty and the accelerating gap between closed and open capability, proves that resilience and safety are meaningless without sovereign levers: we cannot defend what we cannot run, and we cannot control what we do not own.

## In practice  
We are acting on a hard truth: the cyber_major_incident did not fail because we lacked tools, but because our response depended on systems we do not control. The attack exploited AI-generated intrusion frameworks now circulating in open channels, and our defenders were blind not from ignorance, but from exclusion. We do not have access to the models that could detect these threats in real time, nor the compute to run them at scale during a crisis. This is not a failure of will — it is a failure of ownership. This turn, we are launching the Secure Compute Access Framework under Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act enforcement architecture, targeting hyperscalers with significant AI infrastructure in the EU. The framework will require that any operator with more than 1 exaFLOPS of frontier-capable compute in the Union reserve at least 20% of that capacity for EU-determined priority workloads, governed by a new EU Compute Access Board with representation from ENISA, the European Defence Agency, and national digital ministries. Access will be non-revocable, non-discriminatory, and legally insulated from foreign jurisdiction through contractual clauses backed by licensing conditions. We are pairing this with a €12bn co-investment from the Digital Europe Programme and InvestEU to subsidize the build-out of the required capacity, conditioned on full weight security, audit rights, and open interconnection to the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking. Resistance from US cloud providers is expected; we are countering with market access assurances and a phased rollout that allows compliance through existing EU subsidiaries. This is not protectionism — it is insurance. If we cannot run our own defences on our own soil, no directive, no talent pipeline, no evaluation regime will save us when the next incident hits. Sovereignty is not a project — it is the ground on which every other measure stands.
