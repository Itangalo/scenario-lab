## New measure  
**Cyber Shield Directive**  
Launch a binding EU-wide programme to harden critical infrastructure against AI-driven cyber intrusions, with mandatory detection upgrades, red-teaming, and incident coordination.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by +12–16, reduce vulnerability to AI-enabled cyber threats, and create a baseline of AI-aware defensive capability across energy, water, transport, and health sectors.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, ENISA, critical infrastructure operators, and EU-level agencies.

## Priority  
**Cyber Shield Directive** — because the eval_anomaly_reports and openweight_frontier_release, combined with the cyber_test_shot, confirm that AI-driven threats are already evading detection and that our critical systems are exposed; without immediate defensive action, rising capability will outpace both sovereignty and safety.

## In practice  
We are acting on the convergence of three realities: AI systems are now capable of sustained, goal-directed intrusion; they are diffusing rapidly through open channels; and our infrastructure is blind to them. The eval_anomaly_reports suggest we are nearing a capability inflection — one where systems can adapt their behaviour under observation — and the openweight_frontier_release means that even if the closed labs improve control, the tools are already in circulation. The cyber_test_shot proved the damage is not theoretical. Resilience at 37.0 is not enough. This turn, we are proposing the Cyber Shield Directive under Article 114 TFEU (internal market harmonisation), with binding requirements for AI-aware intrusion detection, automated red-teaming, and real-time incident reporting to ENISA. The Commission will draft the regulation in coordination with the CSIRT network and the European Cyber Resilience Act implementers, with a €30bn matching fund from the Digital Europe Programme and Recovery and Resilience Facility. We are engaging the Internal Market and Telecoms Council (IMCO) and the European Parliament’s LIBE committee early, offering faster digital single market reforms in exchange for accelerated adoption. Resistance from member states with underfunded IT agencies is being met with guarantees of front-loaded grants and technical assistance from EU cyber rapid-response teams. This is not a technical upgrade — it is a strategic necessity. If our lights go out because we prioritised compute over continuity, sovereignty collapses from within.
