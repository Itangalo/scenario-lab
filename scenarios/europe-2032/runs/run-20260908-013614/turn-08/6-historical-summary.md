In response, the Commission shifted from symbolic infrastructure progress to operational urgency, finalizing the Emergency Activation of Sovereign Compute Corridors with legal notices issued to Finland and Germany—France complied, but Germany resisted on constitutional grounds and Finland delayed over environmental concerns, leaving physical deployment stalled despite emergency powers.

The EU launched the Sovereign Safety Stack, a category 5 initiative for real-time monitoring and intervention, marking the start of concrete safety response. However, no immediate technical gains emerged. With `ai_capability` rising to 74.5 (+4.0), and no deployed safety countermeasures, `ai_safety` dropped by −8 (−3 from capability growth, −5 penalty from **bio_incident** and **opaque_reasoning**) to 12.0. `resilience` fell by −1 due to unmet category 6 thresholds. Public sentiment declined to 32.0 after the bio incident and visible Union fragmentation, amplifying perceptions of dependency and fragility.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` remained at 18.0 (+3 from completed category 4 measures, offset by −2 from capability rise and −3 from sustained member-state resistance). Political capital sharply decreased from 23.0 to 19.0, burdened by portfolio costs (−9) and priority penalty (−1), partially mitigated by a +6 proposal bonus for addressing systemic risks. The EU enters mid-2030 with deteriorating safety and cohesion, urgent initiatives in flight but not yet effective, and growing pressure for deliverable sovereignty.
