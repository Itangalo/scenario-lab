## New measure  
**Sovereign Safety Stack: Integrated Monitoring and Intervention Capacity**  
Establish an operational, EU-controlled technical stack to detect, analyse, and if necessary interrupt unauthorised or runaway AI behaviours in real time, starting with systems above 60.0 capability operating on EU soil or serving EU functions.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by +15.0 and resilience by +5.0 by creating a persistent, sovereign monitoring layer capable of detecting emergent capabilities and enforcing circuit-breakers.  
`Applies to:` European AI Research Consortium, national security agencies, EU-certified AI providers, and all systems accessing EU critical infrastructure or public services.

## In practice  
We are shifting from infrastructure promises to operational urgency. The Emergency Activation of Sovereign Compute Corridors is now in its final turn: legal notices have been served to Finland and Germany under Article 122 TFEU, and the Commission is appointing EU-level permitting officers to assume oversight of grid integration and environmental compliance at the designated sites. The €18bn from InvestAI Gigafactories is being released in tranches tied to construction milestones, with the European Investment Bank managing disbursement. France has accepted the framework; Finland’s resistance is being met with binding green compute guarantees and a seat on the new European AI Security Board. Germany remains defiant, but we are preparing to invoke Article 260 for non-compliance — a nuclear option, but one the bio_incident has made plausible.

At the same time, we are launching the Sovereign Safety Stack under the European AI Research Consortium, now activated via Article 185 and co-funded by Horizon Europe. This is not another study group. We are hiring red-team leads this quarter, procuring secure testbeds, and integrating anomaly detection from the emergent_lab_cooperation network into a live monitoring platform. The system will begin with telemetry from all EU-certified models above 60.0 capability, and we are drafting technical standards that will require real-time logging, model heartbeat signals, and emergency kill switches — enforceable under the AI Act’s revised Article 28b. This is not about trust. It is about having a hand on the lever.

We are also responding to the election_annulled event by initiating emergency consultations under the European Democracy Shield, though no new measure is launched this turn. The Taiwan tension rise is being monitored via the External Action Service, with coordination channels opened to Japan and Taiwan’s de facto missions. But our focus remains internal: without sovereign eyes and hands on the models that can now design pathogens and manipulate information at scale, no external posture will matter. We are no longer building for competitiveness. We are building for survival.
