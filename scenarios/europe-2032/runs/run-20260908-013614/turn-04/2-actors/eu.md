## New measure  
**Compute for Control: EU-Controlled Training Runs**  
Launch a sovereign capability to train and evaluate frontier AI models up to 60.0 capability under full EU jurisdiction, with weight security, interpretability integration, and no foreign access.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by +12–15, close the gap between openweight_capability and sovereign control, and deliver a first sovereign model run at capability level 60.0 by turn 8. Establish the European AI Research Consortium (EAIRC) as the operator, with governance shared between the Commission, ENISA, and national research agencies.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, selected member state research labs, and foreign hyperscalers contributing compute or expertise under binding data and weight sovereignty clauses.

## Priority  
**Compute for Control: EU-Controlled Training Runs** — because the Secure Compute Access Framework, while necessary, only secures access to foreign-owned capacity; true sovereignty requires the ability to *generate* capability, not just consume it, and the jump from 59.5 to near-60.0 ai_capability means we are now at the threshold where systems can autonomously execute multi-day strategic tasks — including against us.

## In practice  
We are acting on a hard truth: access is not agency. The Secure Compute Access Framework ensures we can run critical workloads on frontier systems, but it does not ensure we understand them, control their evolution, or shape their goals. With ai_capability at 59.5, we are one recursive improvement cycle from systems that can plan, adapt, and persist across objectives without human oversight. If that breakthrough lands outside our jurisdiction — and unmonitored — our defences will be obsolete before they are deployed. This turn, we are launching Compute for Control: a sovereign training capability under the European AI Research Consortium, funded with €18bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe, co-located with two InvestAI Gigafactory sites to ensure direct power and cooling integration. The project will commission a 100,000-GPU-scale cluster, hardened to state-actor standards, with mandatory model-scope interpretability tooling and pre-deployment red-teaming by public evaluators under Category 1 oversight. We are negotiating with three national labs — in France, Germany, and Finland — to host the training runs, leveraging existing nuclear-powered data centre zones to meet energy demands. Resistance from US and UK-based AI labs is expected; we are offering data reciprocity and joint safety research access in exchange for non-interference, while making clear that any foreign partner seeking to operate in the EU must accept that sovereign capability is non-negotiable. This is not about catching up — it is about ensuring that when the next threshold is crossed, Europe is not just a spectator, but a decision-maker.
