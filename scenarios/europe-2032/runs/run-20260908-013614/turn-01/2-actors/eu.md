## In practice  
We are acting on the reality that compute without competence is useless. The InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package are moving — site selections are underway, grid interconnects negotiated — but delays in staffing reveal a deeper bottleneck: we cannot deploy what we cannot staff. The cyber_test_shot and openweight_frontier_release have made clear that capability is diffusing faster than our capacity to govern or defend against it. The emergent_labor_shortage is not just a market failure; it is a sovereignty failure. U.S. hyperscalers are outbidding public-sector AI projects across Germany, France, and the Nordic states, while domestic training pipelines remain underfunded and fragmented.

This turn, we are launching the EU Talent Corridor Initiative through a joint legal basis under Article 122 (economic policy coordination) and Article 169 TFEU (research and innovation). The European Commission will propose a regulation to the Council and Parliament establishing fast-track visas, tax incentives for AI engineers taking public-sector roles, and a €15bn matching fund for member states that expand AI graduate programmes with security and public-interest curricula. We are negotiating with the European Parliament’s EPP and S&D groups to fast-track approval, offering concessions on data mobility in return for speed. Resistance from smaller member states concerned about brain drain is being met with binding commitments to co-locate training hubs and rotate placements. This is not just about recruitment — it is about making the EU a pole of attraction for talent committed to public sovereignty. Without it, our compute ambitions are monuments, not machines.
