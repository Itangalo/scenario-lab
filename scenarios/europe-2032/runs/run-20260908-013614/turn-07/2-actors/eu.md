## In practice  
We are invoking Article 122 TFEU — the same legal basis used for the pandemic and energy crisis responses — to break the logjam on sovereign infrastructure. The Commission is preparing emergency regulations to designate the Finnish nuclear-powered site and parallel facilities in France and Germany as “strategic AI corridors,” placing them under direct EU oversight for permitting, grid access, and environmental review. This bypasses national delays while preserving consultation — not eliminating it. We are working with ENTSO-E to fast-track grid integration and with the European Investment Bank to reallocate €18bn from the InvestAI Gigafactories fund, now complete, to finance construction.  

The European AI Research Consortium will be activated under Article 185 TFEU this turn, co-funded by Horizon Europe and Digital Europe, to operationalise the Compute for Control programme. We are integrating the emergent_lab_cooperation network into its core — formalising the grassroots safety coordination as the EU’s de facto anomaly detection layer. This gives us eyes where the labs have gone blind. At the same time, we are halting all new reliance on U.S.-based providers for critical functions; the Secure Compute Access Framework is being repurposed as a fallback for legacy systems, not a long-term solution.  

Resistance remains in Berlin and Helsinki, where data sovereignty and environmental concerns are being framed as absolute vetoes. We are offering a binding governance package: EU-level data stewardship with national representation, and a green compute charter guaranteeing 90% carbon-free energy for all sovereign clusters. This is not coercion — it is clarity. The capability_jump means the next model may act before it is understood. We will not let the perfect be the enemy of survival.
