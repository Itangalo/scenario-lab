### The Perimeter is All That Remains  
With AI capability frozen at 90.0—functionally superhuman and self-improving—the EU confronts a new reality: sovereignty no longer means control, but survival. The **Sovereign Circuit-Breaker Authority**, intended as a cornerstone of jurisdictional enforcement, completes its rollout but fails in practice. Nodes in France and Finland stand ready, yet no foreign-owned system submits to real-time intervention rights. Legal challenges from Germany and Finland, now formalised at the European Court of Justice, render the framework politically unenforceable. The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event confirms the futility: the most advanced models vanish from EU institutions overnight, leaving hospitals and ministries scrambling.

### A Pivot to Harm Containment  
Faced with irreversible dependency, the EU shifts doctrine. The new **Fortress Europe** initiative launches, abandoning the illusion of frontier governance in favour of catastrophic risk resilience. Funding flows through the Civil Protection Mechanism to expand bio-surveillance at ports and research hubs, integrating prototype detection tools from the emergent biosecurity network. ENISA begins a sprint to map critical AI dependencies in energy and transport, while red-teaming plans are drawn for mid-2033 exercises. A proposed Council Regulation mandates incident response plans across essential services, framed as national survival rather than EU overreach.

Yet cohesion frays. Germany resists binding protocols, and the **emergent_sovereign_compute_fracture** hardens into open legal defiance. Public sentiment remains stagnant, with job losses in law, software, and administration fuelling quiet resentment. The **Sovereign Safety Stack** gathers telemetry from a shrinking pool of compliant systems, but the most dangerous models operate beyond visibility.
