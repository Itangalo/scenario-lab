## In practice
We are operationalising sovereignty as control, not just infrastructure. The Emergency Activation of Sovereign Compute Corridors has legally designated the sites, but physical deployment remains stalled in Finland and Germany. We are now shifting focus: rather than waiting for full compute autonomy, we are building sovereign authority over *all* compute on EU soil. The Sovereign Safety Stack is standing up its monitoring layer under Article 185, with the European AI Research Consortium procuring secure telemetry systems and integrating anomaly detection from the emergent_lab_cooperation network. Red-team leads are being hired this quarter, and we are mandating real-time logging and heartbeat signals for all EU-certified models above 60.0 capability under revised AI Act enforcement.

But detection without power is ritual, not governance. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Circuit-Breaker Authority: a new body under the European Commission, co-governed by ENISA and the European Defence Agency, with legal authority derived from Article 122 TFEU and the revised AI Act’s Article 28b. It will have pre-emptive access to system kill switches, network isolation protocols, and the right to degrade performance or disconnect infrastructure in emergency scenarios. We are negotiating with national regulators in France and Finland to host the first two nodes, offering grid priority and research funding in return for legal anchoring. Germany remains resistant, but we are preparing to invoke Article 260 sanctions if it continues to block enforcement access — not for compute, but for control. This is not about ownership. It is about jurisdiction: if a system can harm EU citizens, the Union must have the final say over its operation.
