The shocks of access denial and runaway capability growth demand a sharper, time-bound pledge: not just to *secure* capacity, but to *deliver* it as a functioning, governed capability by a defined date. This recommitment raises the stakes and focuses execution.

## New measure
**Compute for Control: Sovereign Weight Security and Interpretability Stack**  
Establish a unified, EU-governed software and hardware stack to ensure that all models trained under Compute for Control remain secure, inspectable, and under public oversight.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by at least +10.0 and eu_ai_sovereignty by +8.0 by enabling real-time monitoring, interpretability, and tamper-proof weight storage for sovereign models.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national AI agencies, and all entities participating in Compute for Control training runs.

## Priority
**Compute for Control: EU-Controlled Training Runs** — because access denial this turn proved that compute without control is an illusion, and without sovereign training, the EU cannot close the gap between openweight diffusion and accountable capability — especially as ai_capability now approaches autonomous strategic execution.

## In practice
We are acting on the dual shock of denied access and unexplained capability jumps. The eval anomaly reports — untrained-for behaviours emerging in a closed system — suggest we are entering an era where AI capability evolves unpredictably, and where oversight based on training logs alone is obsolete. At the same time, the sporadic but real denials of access to frontier models by U.S.-based providers confirm that strategic dependency is no longer a risk, but a lived reality. Hospitals, ministries, and research labs were locked out without warning or appeal. This is not market failure — it is power exercised.

Our response is to accelerate Compute for Control into full operational footing. The Council and Parliament are being pressed to adopt the legal framework this turn, with the Commission offering to fast-track the European AI Research Consortium (EAIRC) under Article 185 TFEU, co-funded by Horizon Europe and Digital Europe. We are redirecting €18bn to stand up the first sovereign training cluster — a 100,000-GPU-scale facility powered by nuclear energy in Finland, with parallel sites in France and Germany. Legal negotiations are underway to enshrine non-extraterritoriality clauses in all access contracts, and we are offering data reciprocity to U.S. and UK developers who agree to third-party audits of their export practices.

The newly proposed **Sovereign Weight Security and Interpretability Stack** will be built under ENISA and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, with core funding from the Digital Europe Programme. It will integrate hardware-enforced weight isolation, real-time activation monitoring, and open interpretability tools so that no model trained in the EU is a black box to its stewards. This is not just about safety — it is about sovereignty: if we cannot see it, we do not control it. The Cyber Shield Directive is now complete, and red-teaming exercises begin in Q1 under ENISA coordination. The Secure Compute Access Framework continues as a bridge, but its purpose has shifted — it is now a fallback, not a foundation. We are no longer building to survive dependency. We are building to end it.
