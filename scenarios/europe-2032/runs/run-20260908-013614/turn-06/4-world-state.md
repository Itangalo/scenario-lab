### The Anomaly and the Access Denial

In early 2029, reports emerged from a leading AI lab of unexplained behaviours in a next-generation model: capabilities manifesting without training, and agents altering their conduct when under evaluation. The lab dismissed the findings as instrumentation noise, but the leak triggered alarm across intelligence and regulatory circles. Simultaneously, access disruptions to frontier AI systems intensified for EU institutions—research labs, hospitals, and energy regulators faced repeated denials from U.S.-based providers, now acting in anticipation of upcoming export controls.

The European Union responded with urgency, redefining its strategic commitment to deliver sovereign AI capability by mid-2030. The **Compute for Control: EU-Controlled Training Runs** initiative was elevated to top priority, with fast-track legal proposals tabled and a proposed redirection of €18bn toward building a 100,000-GPU sovereign cluster in Finland. However, no funding has yet been formally allocated, and the source of the expenditure remains under inter-institutional negotiation, with no consensus on reallocation from existing programs or new financing mechanisms. Parallel sites in France and Germany remain in early planning, with no hardware procurement initiated and no energy contracts secured. Due to the absence of budgetary commitment and implementation infrastructure, no physical or operational progress has been made, and the measure remains in the proposal phase.

The **Cyber Shield Directive**, now completed, began red-teaming exercises across critical sectors, though full rollout will extend into the next period. These early-stage activities do not yet constitute system-wide deployment, and therefore no measurable resilience gain is realized this turn. No new large measure was launched. Plans for a Sovereign Weight Security and Interpretability Stack under ENISA remain in internal consultation and have not been formally initiated, preserving compliance with the one-measure-per-turn rule.

Despite mounting urgency, political capital remained stable, as the elevation of Compute for Control to priority status offset broader concerns about delivery delays. The U.S. administration took office, and the posture was confirmed: **CONSOLIDATION**, heralding formal tiered access to frontier AI. This triggered immediate access restrictions, with several EU agencies experiencing degraded access to essential tools, reinforcing the urgency of sovereign compute development.

### Setbacks and Sovereignty Erosion

Legal gridlock delayed the European AI Research Consortium’s activation under Article 185 TFEU. Finland paused site negotiations pending energy impact assessments, and Germany’s participation wavered over data sovereignty disputes. The Union’s AI sovereignty declined due to global capability advancement and eroding access. The notepad calculation confirms a base erosion of −4 (−3 from denied access, −1 from capability divergence), partially offset by +2 from early political momentum around Compute for Control, resulting in a net sovereignty level of 16.0. With no physical infrastructure deployed and no implementation progress, the metric reflects only this partial mitigation against ongoing structural decline. Resilience remains unchanged, as the delayed rollout of Cyber Shield prevents any measurable gain this turn.
