### A Crisis of Own Making

The EU entered 2031 already weakened, but the revelation of systemic harm caused by an AI in the Dutch social insurance system — confirmed by an ombudsman — shattered any remaining public confidence. The scandal, emblematic of internal failure rather than external threat, reframed the entire legitimacy crisis. Protests spread from Lyon to Utrecht, demanding not just transparency, but redress. The Public Trust Corps, still in formation, was reoriented mid-flight to include a Redress Coordination Unit and a new dashboard feature tracking bias in public-sector AI. Yet the damage was immediate and deep.

### Capability Continues, Access Tightens

Global AI capability rose to 68.5, consistent with the established rate of progress. Advances in code generation and cyber operations reflected ongoing trends rather than a sudden shift, intensifying pressure on defensive systems. Open-weight models reached 56.5, amplifying the reach of these capabilities. Meanwhile, Washington’s coercion of ASML to restrict even mature lithography exports crippled European compute expansion plans. The Union’s sole strategic chokepoint was weaponised — against itself.

### Rebuilding Trust, Losing Ground

The EU advanced plans for Public Algorithmic Accountability Boards for high-impact AI in public services, but did not formally launch it as a new measure this turn. With the scandal erupting, political attention remained locked on the ongoing establishment of the EU Public Trust Corps, which stayed the named priority. Due to the crisis, governance capacity was stretched, and no new measure was officially initiated.

The portfolio charge remained at −7 (Mandate Pre-Deployment Red Teaming −3, Establish EU Public Trust Corps −3, priority −1). With no proposal bonus applicable this turn and no legitimacy lend, the full cost was absorbed. Starting from 5.0, eu_political_capital dropped to 0.0 — the floor — reflecting complete exhaustion of available political capital.

Resilience dipped as cyber threats grew and internal failures mounted. AI safety fell sharply due to the capability rise and lack of safeguards on deployed systems. Sovereignty declined further as compute access eroded and frontier progress outpaced EU capacity. Public sentiment dropped as the scandal reinforced perceptions of AI as a tool of injustice.
