## New measure
**Common Front on External AI Deals**
It requires member states to notify and submit bilateral hyperscaler and third-country AI infrastructure deals to joint review against Union security and single-market terms before signature.
This is category 8 International coordination and leverage, because this turn's defection shows sovereignty is being picked off bilaterally while the hardening pact and Gigafactories need a common line to hold, and a cheap discipline instrument now prevents further undercutting without opening a second compute programme.

## Priority
EU Critical Systems Hardening and Response Pact, because live persistence in transmission, ports and water plus unrecallable open-weight cyber still outranks the defection fight, which we contain with a small discipline track.

## In practice
We push the Hardening Pact through the Transport-Telecom-Energy Council and Justice-Home Affairs formation on NIS2 implementing acts: binding 24-hour reporting to ENISA, joint audits of transmission operators and major ports, and EU co-financed backup control centres. Finance ministry resistance on cost-sharing is met with Connecting Europe Facility and Digital Europe reprogramming plus mutual-aid crews, while permitting blockages for backup sites are taken to accelerated-permitting talks linked to the sovereignty zones.

On the defection we do not open a new compute fund. DG CNECT and DG COMP run the Common Front review from the existing tech sovereignty and Gigafactory mandates, with the Anti-Coercion Instrument and procurement leverage as the backstop: any external deal offering extraterritorial data access or preferential offtake that fragments EU grid-connection and siting terms is flagged to the European Council. Grid-connection priority for the four to five Gigafactory sites stays negotiated with energy regulators and ASML coordination kept quiet to avoid a further split.
