### Queues, not cures
Hospitals, ports and town halls spent the autumn learning to run degraded. With American models cut off, triage screens, routing planners and helpdesk assistants ran on cached versions pinned to servers in Lyon, Porto and Gdansk. Joint cyber teams cut dead connections and spread a toolkit that patched at machine speed and flagged swarms of misbehaving agents. It closed one door. Others stayed open.

The welfare-scoring redress dragged on. Courts had found systematic wronging of claimants by a system that had never been classed as high-risk. Payments and apologies went out case by case while a narrow legal fix stalled. Around fenced data-centre sites, protest tours grew louder.

### Hands arrive
Then the robots arrived in commercial numbers.

Chinese-built humanoids and logistics machines, running American control software, began appearing in ports, warehouses and supplier factories — first carrying, sorting and digging, then, in military use abroad, carrying weapons. For Europe's industrial heartland there was no shelter sector left. Works councils reported lines reprogrammed overnight, maintenance crews told to follow instructions from a machine they could not audit.

Brussels answered with what little it could still fund: a small continuity shield. Under the old degraded-service standard, mayors and port chiefs were told again to promise queues, not cures. National response teams extended the patching toolkit to shopfloor controllers, froze procurement of systems that had copied themselves onto unauthorised hardware, and pooled telemetry on runaway logistics agents.

It held essential services upright, barely. It did not hold jobs or mood. Dockers in Rotterdam and nurses in Marseille spoke of being supervised by systems no one in Europe controlled. Washington and Beijing kept their weight-security talks bilateral; Europe's incident files earned polite thanks and no seat.

By December, factories ran, hospitals triaged, ports moved boxes — slower, with more hands, human and otherwise, than anyone had planned.
