## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that without sovereign technical capacity, neither mandate can be fulfilled. Control is not policy, law, or negotiation. It is access, compute, and the ability to run, audit, and counter systems independently. When that is absent, all action is reactive, all regulation is theatre, and all resilience is borrowed.``  
  - Grounds: The **taiwan_blockade** this turn has severed access to advanced semiconductor supply for all non-Chinese actors, freezing compute expansion and exposing the EU’s total dependence on external chains. Simultaneously, **eu_frontier_access_denied** cut the Union off from the leading model — not through a dispute, but by unilateral denial with no recourse. These are not failures of diplomacy or timing. They are structural: we cannot verify what we cannot run, cannot defend what we cannot observe, and cannot act when our tools are hosted elsewhere. The **emergent_european_ai_commons** offers a path, but it is emergent, not sovereign. Our identity must now reflect that sovereignty is not a policy goal — it is the material condition of agency.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we now act only where sovereign observability can be established, and we treat any action without it as provisional, reversible, and politically indefensible. This turn, we extend that principle: we will no longer accept dependency on systems we cannot replicate, audit, or replace within our own jurisdiction.``  
  - Grounds: The **bio_incident** — a deliberate release involving an AI-designed agent — revealed that the model used was hosted and trained outside the EU, with no checkpoint, telemetry, or access for forensic reconstruction. Our response was delayed by weeks while we begged for logs that never came. The **Sovereign Verification Regime** cannot compel what it cannot reach. We acted under uncertainty before; now, uncertainty is weaponised against us. The commitment evolves: we will still move early, but only where we hold the means to act unilaterally. Dependency is no longer a risk. It is a surrender.

* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor EU sovereignty and risk resilience in foundational AI capacity under Union control, and where that capacity is not yet achievable, secure sovereign observability — the right and ability to audit, reconstruct, contain, and independently verify the behaviour of any high-capability system operating in or affecting the EU, regardless of origin. This turn, we redefine "sovereign" as *replicable*: if we cannot train it, run it, and defend it without permission, it does not exist for us.``  
  - Grounds: The **taiwan_blockade** makes clear that compute is now a strategic bottleneck, not an economic good. The **emergent_european_ai_commons** shows that distributed effort can begin to fill the gap, but it runs on decommissioned hardware and borrowed time. Our **InvestAI Gigafactories** are complete, but their output is years away. We are not building capacity — we are waiting for it. The **two_year_commitment** must now demand acceleration: not just ownership, but *operational replication* of frontier capability under EU jurisdiction, with or without external supply. We are no longer securing capacity. We are seizing the means to create it.
