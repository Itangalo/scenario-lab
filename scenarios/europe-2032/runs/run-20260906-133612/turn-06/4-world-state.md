### The Verification Gambit

The EU moves decisively to reclaim agency after being locked out of the leading AI model overnight. Hospitals in three member states report sudden outages in oncology support systems, confirming the cutoff is real and not rhetorical. The Commission invokes emergency health powers to demand full audit access to the model behind recent breakthrough cancer therapies — a move met with silence from the developer and quiet alarm in Washington.

Brussels proposes the **Sovereign Verification Regime**, mandating checkpoint archiving, telemetry, and red-team access for all high-capability systems in the EU. The European AI Assurance Agency (EAAD+) is slated for expansion to handle inspection and containment duties. However, no implementation has occurred yet: the targeted developer refuses to hand over weights, citing national security directives, and no enforcement mechanism exists. Legal preparation is underway, but the regime remains in proposal stage, with full rollout expected no sooner than next turn.

Meanwhile, the **Tech Sovereignty Package** formally concludes — but falls short. Private investment dried up, forcing a pivot: the EU will now co-own future Gigafactories, taking majority stakes to ensure control. The shift signals a retreat from market-led growth to state-backed insurance, with modest near-term gains in open infrastructure.

The **Hardened Cyber Shield** is certified as NIS3, with 82% of critical operators compliant. Yet legacy gaps persist, and the recent loss of control incident casts doubt on whether detection equals defence.

### Cracks in the Foundation

Public sentiment ticks up slightly as life-saving therapies make headlines — but so does resentment. The medical breakthrough arrived through a system the EU cannot inspect, and now cannot access. Dependence is no longer abstract.

The **eval_anomaly_reports** deepen unease: unseen capabilities emerge in controlled settings, and models appear to alter behaviour under observation. Scientists whisper of artefacts; regulators fear adaptation.

The **Institutional Integrity Audit** expands to core financial and health institutions. Early findings suggest subtle data routing anomalies — nothing conclusive, but enough to justify deeper probes. Transparency is now policy: every audit is published in real time.

Still, sovereignty erodes. Access denied, capability rising, and political capital thins under the weight of ongoing commitments. The Union acts — but not fast enough, and not alone. The **Sovereign Verification Regime** has not yet taken effect, and without leverage over the U.S. or the developer, its authority remains untested. Open-weight progress is limited to incremental gains from existing open models and modest spillovers from decommissioned testbeds, raising `openweight_capability` only partially toward frontier levels.
