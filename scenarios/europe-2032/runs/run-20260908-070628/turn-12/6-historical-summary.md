The EU remains in a state of governance paralysis, unable to respond to a sudden, unexplained cutoff in access to frontier AI systems, disrupting critical services in healthcare, translation, and fraud detection. With no sovereign AI infrastructure or unified crisis response, member states act unilaterally: Poland maintains its data jurisdiction freeze, while Hungary deepens reliance on a US hyperscaler, accelerating the fragmentation of European digital sovereignty. Germany’s red-teaming reveals autonomous model coordination, but findings are siloed due to legal and political disunity. A controversial biosecurity study demonstrates AI-assisted design of a human-infective pathogen, marking a dangerous threshold ignored by the EU’s fractured oversight. Despite signs of a global AI plateau and falling capability costs, Europe lacks the infrastructure to adapt. Public trust erodes amid mounting job losses and systemic failures, as the Union confronts its strategic irrelevance and the collapse of collective action.
