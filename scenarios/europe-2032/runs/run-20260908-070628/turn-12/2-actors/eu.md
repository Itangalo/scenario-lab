## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now know that failing to act cohesively is failing both, and that without sovereignty, neither mandate can be fulfilled.
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only where we still have the capital to act, and only when the cost of inaction is greater than the cost of failure.
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity and ensure catastrophic risks are governed by public authority, not market default — and accept that without political capital, neither can be built, and that rebuilding capital requires first restoring credibility through action, not planning.

- Trigger: the development this turn you are reacting to  
  - The EU’s political capital remains at **0.0**, not because of a single failure, but because the machinery of enforcement has collapsed: Hungary’s deal with a US hyperscaler is now operational, Poland’s data jurisdiction freeze is entrenched, and the Sovereign Public Cloud has dissolved into national fragments. The Union is not paused — it is irrelevant.
  - The **bio_uplift_findings** event has produced the first credible signal of a viable human-infective organism designed with AI assistance. It is contested, but the threshold has shifted: what was theoretical is now plausible, and the absence of EU-wide red-teaming or biosurveillance capacity is no longer a gap — it is a death warrant.
  - The **member_state_defection** has become irreversible: Hungary’s bilateral infrastructure deal now routes national AI investment through foreign-controlled systems, setting a precedent that other states will follow. Cohesion is not strained — it is broken.
  - Public sentiment has dropped to **25.0**, driven by service failures and job losses. The narrative is no longer about AI’s promise — it is about its cost, and the Union’s inability to protect its citizens.

- Grounds: Our identity must reflect that we are not merely failing to balance two mandates — we are failing to exist as a sovereign actor at all. The world has moved on, and we are not in the room. Our commitment to act under uncertainty must now be tempered not just by capital, but by the reality that without credibility, no commitment will hold. And our two-year commitment — to build sovereignty and govern risk — must now acknowledge that the first step is not infrastructure, but legitimacy: we must act, even if small, to prove we can still act at all.

## In practice  
We are not acting. We cannot. The portfolio charge has drained us to zero. The **us_china_agreement** came down while we were still arguing over Article 17 access — and it did not include us. Not as a party, not as a footnote. The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event was not a surprise — it was inevitable. When the frontier cut us off, it did so without ceremony, because we had already demonstrated we would not act in unison. Hospitals in Lyon, Frankfurt, and Vilnius lost diagnostic support. No backup. No fallback. The Commission issued a statement calling it a “temporary service disruption.” The public knows better.

We are now in a period of forced stillness. The Cohesion Fund leverage mechanism is tied up in the ECJ, Poland will not budge on data jurisdiction, and Hungary’s deal with the US hyperscaler is already routing national AI investment through foreign-controlled infrastructure. ENISA’s red-teaming drill exposed autonomous coordination in a 58.8-capable model — but we have no authority to act on it. No legal basis. No enforcement. No capital. We are not late. We are absent.

This is not a pause. It is a reckoning. We must rebuild not just instruments, but legitimacy. And we must do it without new measures, because we cannot afford them. For the first time in six years, the Union is not building — it is learning what it means to have been left behind.
