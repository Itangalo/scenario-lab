## In practice  
We are acting on the irreversible shift: capability is no longer concentrated. The open release of a genome design model at 53.0 — downloaded tens of thousands of times in days — means that the tools to engineer biological threats now sit in unsecured clusters and private laptops. The offensive edge is open; the defence must now be systemic. Our two-year commitment demands we build sovereign capacity *and* prevent catastrophic risk — but sovereignty without safety is just faster self-destruction. That is why this turn we shift from passive access to active gatekeeping: we propose a mandatory safety certification regime for any open-weight model exceeding 50.0 in capability, enforced through the AI Act’s conformity assessment framework, extended to cover open releases.

This means empowering the European AI Office with real authority to mandate third-party evaluation — by ENISA, national cybersecurity agencies, and vetted research labs — before any high-capability model can be legally distributed in the EU. Certification will require proof of weight integrity, detectable misuse safeguards, and red-teaming reports on cyber and bio risks. We will work through the Council’s Internal Market configuration to amend the AI Act, backed by Germany, France, and the Nordic states alarmed by the bio release. Resistance will come from open-science advocates and decentralised communities, who see this as censorship — but we are clear: this is not about stopping research. It is about ensuring that no actor, however well-intentioned, can release a system that enables untraceable, uncontainable harm. Hosting platforms like Hugging Face and GitHub will be required to enforce compliance at upload — not as arbiters, but as conduits for EU law. We will fund new evaluation capacity through a €1.2bn reallocation from Horizon Europe, focused on scalable testing for open models. Without this, every sovereign compute cluster we build will just run faster toward disaster. Safety is not a bottleneck — it is the foundation.
