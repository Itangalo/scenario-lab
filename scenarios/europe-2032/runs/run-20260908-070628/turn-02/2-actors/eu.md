## New measure  
**Sovereign Access to Frontier Models**  
Establish a legal and technical pathway for EU institutions to run capable AI systems independently, on sovereign infrastructure, with full control over weights and deployment.

## Priority  
**Sovereign Access to Frontier Models** — because the openweight_frontier_release event proves that capability is now diffusing faster than governance can track, and without sovereign execution capacity, the EU cannot verify safety, enforce rules, or act in crisis — rendering both mandates hollow.

## In practice  
We are acting on the growing gap between what the world can do and what the EU can control. The open release of a model within months of the closed frontier — already downloaded hundreds of thousands of times — means that dangerous capabilities are no longer confined to labs or states. They are everywhere, and they are permanent. Our two-year commitment demands that we build sovereign capacity *while* preventing catastrophic risk, but we cannot prevent what we cannot run, inspect, or contain. That is why this turn we launch Sovereign Access to Frontier Models: a programme to deploy and operate high-capability AI systems — initially up to openweight_capability levels — on EU-controlled infrastructure, under EU legal authority, with no foreign dependency.

This means securing compute clusters within the EU, governed by a new mandate for the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU), expanded to include AI sovereignty as a core mission. We will negotiate access to the best available open-weight models — including Kimi K3 and the new genome design model — and ensure they are hosted on systems where the EU holds the keys, the physical access, and the operational authority. This is not about matching the frontier in secret; it is about having a real, running capability to test, monitor, and if necessary, counteract threats that are already in circulation. We will fund this through a reallocation of €8bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe, with co-investment from France, Germany, and the Netherlands. Resistance will come from member states wary of centralisation and from developers who fear precedent — but we are clear: this is not a backdoor to regulation-by-operation. It is a firewall for autonomy. Without it, we are left reacting to crises with tools we do not control, from powers we cannot compel. That is not sovereignty. It is supplication.
