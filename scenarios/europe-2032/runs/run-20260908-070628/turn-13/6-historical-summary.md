The EU, paralyzed by disunity and lacking sovereign AI infrastructure, invokes emergency powers under Article 80 to launch the Emergency Talent Anchoring Initiative—a symbolic move to signal agency through fast-tracked visas and repurposed legal frameworks, without new funding or structural support. The initiative yields no immediate hires or capabilities but earns a nominal +1 sovereignty boost for demonstrating intent. Meanwhile, member states deepen fragmentation: Hungary finalizes a deal with a US hyperscaler, Poland blocks cross-border data flows, and Germany’s critical red-teaming findings remain isolated. A biological incident in Southeast Asia involving an AI-designed, treatment-resistant pathogen exposes global institutional weaknesses, though core AI safety mechanisms hold. In Europe, anti-AI protests escalate into physical sabotage of infrastructure in Bavaria and Brittany, straining public order but not collapsing systemic resilience. Public trust wavers but does not collapse; political capital remains depleted. Despite motion, there is no momentum—Europe’s response remains fragmented, reactive, and institutionally constrained.
