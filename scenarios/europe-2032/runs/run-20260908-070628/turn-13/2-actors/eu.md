## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now know that without political capital, sovereignty, or public trust, neither mandate can be exercised, and that our first duty is not to choose between them, but to restore the Union’s capacity to act at all.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only where we still have the capital to act, and only when the cost of inaction is greater than the cost of failure. This turn, the cost of action may still exceed what remains — but inaction now risks permanent irrelevance.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity and ensure catastrophic risks are governed by public authority, not market default — and accept that without political capital, neither can be built, and that rebuilding capital requires first restoring credibility through action, not planning. This turn, credibility must be earned not through scale, but through signal: we must act, even if small, to prove we still can.

- Trigger: the development this turn you are reacting to  
  - The **bio_incident** has materialised: a real biological release involving an AI-designed agent has caused casualties and required weeks of containment. This is not theoretical risk — it is harm. The EU had no coordinated detection, no response protocol, and no authority to intervene.  
  - The **backlash_physical** has escalated: data centre occupations and grid sabotage are now occurring, with injuries. Policing costs exceed damage, and the public sees not protection but repression.  
  - **emergent_global_ai_talent_drift** accelerates: Swiss and Nordic institutions are actively recruiting frontier researchers from the US and China. This is a window — not for grand infrastructure, but for agile, high-leverage recruitment of talent into EU jurisdiction.  
  - Political capital remains at **0.0**, public sentiment has fallen to **20.0**, and member-state fragmentation is now operational reality. Hungary’s foreign-controlled AI infrastructure is live; Poland’s data freeze is law. The Union does not act — it watches.

- Grounds: The identity must reflect that we are not merely failing to balance two mandates — we are failing to exist as a decision-making body. The bio_incident proves that risk is no longer hypothetical; the backlash proves that legitimacy is evaporating. Our commitment to act under uncertainty must now confront the fact that we have no margin for error. And our two-year commitment — to sovereignty and public governance of risk — must now begin not with compute or law, but with signal: a single, credible act that proves the Union can still move.

## Priority

**Emergency Talent Anchoring Initiative** — because while we lack capital for grand infrastructure, we still have the legal and institutional space to act as a sanctuary for researchers fleeing fragmented or militarised systems, and this is the only lever left that can restore both capability and credibility within two turns.

## In practice

We are not rebuilding the Union with a flagship directive or a sovereign cloud launch. We are starting with a signal: the EU is still a jurisdiction that can act. We are activating Article 80 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU — emergency powers for the protection of public order and security — to fast-track residency, research funding, and legal immunity for AI researchers fleeing jurisdictions where their work is subject to state capture or militarisation. This is not open to all: we are targeting those with verifiable experience in red-teaming, alignment, or biosecurity-relevant AI work.

The European Research Council will lead, with support from Frontex for rapid visa processing and Europol for background vetting — not to restrict, but to enable. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme’s crisis reallocation clause, €300 million redirected from stalled AI4PublicGood projects that never left pilot phase. We are offering: full legal anchoring in EU jurisdiction, access to Schengen-wide research networks, and guaranteed publication rights — no gag orders. Host institutions in Tübingen, Paris-Saclay, and Aalto have already pledged lab space.

No new infrastructure, no new law, no vote in the Council. This is executive action under existing legal bases, using the only capital we have left: the EU’s residual reputation as a neutral, rules-based space. We are not building compute. We are building trust — with researchers, with the public, and with ourselves. If even ten frontier researchers accept, we will have done more this turn to restore sovereignty than in the last six years.
