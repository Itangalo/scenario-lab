## New measure  
**Accelerated Evaluation Capacity for Open-Weight Models**  
Establish a pan-European network of public evaluation labs with authority and tools to independently assess high-capability open models, focusing on scalable, automated red-teaming and misuse detection.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`

## Priority  
**Mandatory Pre-Deployment Safety Certification for High-Capability Open Models** — because without credible, independent evaluation capacity, the certification regime risks becoming a paper exercise, and the open frontier at 53.0 is already capable of enabling catastrophic misuse; this turn, we must ensure the enforcement mechanism can actually function.

## In practice  
We are acting on a hard truth: our certification mandate cannot stand on its own. The proposal is law in principle, but without the technical muscle to verify compliance, it is a rule without teeth. This turn, we move to close that gap by launching a €2.1bn programme to build public evaluation capacity across the Union — not just in Brussels, but in national labs from Toulouse to Tallinn. We will expand the European AI Office’s remit to coordinate a distributed network of red-teaming hubs, each specialising in a domain: cyber, bio, disinformation, autonomous agents. These will be staffed with vetted researchers and equipped with sovereign compute to run high-fidelity evaluations without relying on private labs. Funding will come from a reallocation of Horizon Europe and cohesion funds, with matching contributions required from member states to ensure buy-in. We will negotiate through the Research Council and the Competitiveness Council, backed by Germany and the Netherlands, who have already committed national labs to the effort. Resistance will come from private developers who see this as overreach, and from smaller states wary of new obligations — but we are clear: this is not about replacing private innovation. It is about ensuring that when a model capable of designing pathogens is released under an open licence, someone in the public interest can test it before it spreads. The Sovereign Access initiative ensures we can run models; this ensures we can understand them. Together, they form the core of a sovereign safety stack — because without both, we are either blind or powerless.
