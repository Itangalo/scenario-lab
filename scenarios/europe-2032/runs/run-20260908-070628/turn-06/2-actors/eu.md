## New measure
**Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure**  
Launch a time-bound, high-intensity programme to harden essential public services against AI-powered cyber threats in the wake of a major incident, with binding standards, rapid deployment of detection and failover systems, and mandatory incident response drills.

## Priority
**Accelerated Evaluation Capacity for Open-Weight Models** — because the cyber_major_incident confirms that offensive AI tooling is already in circulation at capability levels near 57.0, and without an integrated, sovereign evaluation network, we cannot determine whether open models are weaponizable before they are embedded in public systems.

## In practice
We are acting under fire. The cyber_major_incident — a self-propagating ransomware strain exploiting zero-day vulnerabilities in municipal and healthcare networks, generated by a model at or near openweight_capability 56.5 — has exposed a fatal gap: we have rules for deployment, but no sovereign capacity to test what those models can actually do before they are loose. This turn, we are forcing convergence on the **Accelerated Evaluation Capacity**, using the Competitiveness Council to mandate unified red-teaming protocols across the seven national nodes. Germany and France will host the central cyber and bio clusters; Poland and Hungary are being offered technical assistance packages tied to Cohesion Fund disbursements to secure buy-in. The goal is operational integration by the end of the turn: a single, auditable pipeline that can stress-test any model above 50.0 for autonomous exploit generation, deception, and chain-of-thought evasion.

At the same time, the **Sovereign Cloud for Public Audit and Civic Use** is being reoriented in response to the incident. It will now require, as a condition of hosting, that all models undergo full evaluation through the Accelerated Evaluation Capacity — not just certification, but live red-teaming. We are working with the European AI Office to fast-track integration of the first two national clouds (France’s *Sphère* and Germany’s *BürgerNetz*) into the federated framework, using InvestAI compute and EDIC governance. This is no longer optional: if public AI is to gain trust, it must be demonstrably safer than the wild west of open weights.

But trust means nothing if the lights go out. The **Emergency Resilience Surge** is our response to the attack. Under Article 12 of the Digital Operational Resilience Act and the new Crisis Protocol activation powers, we are mandating that all critical infrastructure operators implement AI-driven intrusion detection and automated failover within six months, funded through a reallocation of the Digital Europe Programme and supported by ENISA rapid-response teams. Drills will be conducted under live-fire conditions by turn’s end. This is not precaution — it is repair. We failed to harden in time. Now we must catch up before the next wave hits.
