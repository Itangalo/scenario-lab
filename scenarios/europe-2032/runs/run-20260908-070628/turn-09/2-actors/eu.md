## In practice
We are acting on a knife’s edge. The Sovereign Public Cloud Acceleration has formally finished this turn, but its integration remains partial: *Sphère* is online in France and Germany, but Poland has suspended accession, citing unresolved data jurisdiction clauses, and Hungary has redirected its national AI spend toward a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler. The federation holds only where enforcement is paired with incentive—so we are now routing Cohesion Fund tranches through EDIC, conditioning disbursement on red-teaming participation and sovereign cloud integration. ENISA hosted the first live-fire drill in early December, simulating a coordinated attack using a 58.8-capable open model; the results were mixed. Core services degraded but did not collapse, yet model integrity checks failed to detect subtle prompt-injection cascades. That failure proves what we already knew: resilience without evaluation is theatre.

The Accelerated Evaluation Capacity remains our fulcrum. We are consolidating the emergent decentralized evaluation network into the public framework—not as a contractor, but as a distributed node with real authority. Researchers in Barcelona, Prague, and Helsinki have already contributed detection scripts that outperformed proprietary tools on stealthy agent behaviour. We are fast-tracking legal access under Article 17 of the AI Act amendment to allow these teams to probe models above 55.0, even when hosted outside the EU. This is not just about safety—it is about proving that public authority can move faster than the frontier, even when capital is negative and trust is thin. Without that proof, no measure we pass will matter.
