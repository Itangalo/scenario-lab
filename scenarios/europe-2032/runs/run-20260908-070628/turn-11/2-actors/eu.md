## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now know that failing to act cohesively is failing both.
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only where we still have the capital to act.
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity and ensure catastrophic risks are governed by public authority, not market default — and accept that without political capital, neither can be built.
- Trigger: the development this turn you are reacting to  
  - The EU’s political capital has collapsed to **0.0**, not just from portfolio charges but from the *de facto* loss of enforcement power. Hungary’s bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler and Poland’s suspension of integration have created irreversible precedent: sovereignty without cohesion is performance, not power.
  - The **us_china_agreement** has excluded the EU entirely. No consultation, no side letter, no observer status. The world’s two leading powers have acted — on *our* two mandates — while we were negotiating with ourselves.
  - The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has landed: hospitals in three member states lost real-time diagnostic AI, ministries in two lost translation pipelines, and a pan-EU fraud detection system failed over a weekend. The public was told “technical issues.” The truth is we were cut off.
  - The **capability_plateau_evidence** is disputed, but irrelevant: even if the frontier stalls, we are not in a position to exploit the pause. We have no sovereign evaluation, no sovereign cloud, no sovereign response.
- Grounds: The Union is not failing because it lacks vision or instruments, but because it lacks the capital to enforce them. Our identity must reflect that we are not merely torn between two mandates — we are failing both. Our commitment to act under uncertainty must now be tempered with the recognition that capital is finite and legitimacy is broken. And our two-year commitment — to build sovereignty and govern risk — is now in direct conflict with our ability to fund or enforce it. These are no longer strategic choices; they are survival constraints.

## In practice  
We are not acting. We cannot. The portfolio charge has drained us to zero. The **us_china_agreement** came down while we were still arguing over Article 17 access — and it did not include us. Not as a party, not as a footnote. The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event was not a surprise — it was inevitable. When the frontier cut us off, it did so without ceremony, because we had already demonstrated we would not act in unison. Hospitals in Lyon, Frankfurt, and Vilnius lost diagnostic support. No backup. No fallback. The Commission issued a statement calling it a “temporary service disruption.” The public knows better.

We are now in a period of forced stillness. The Cohesion Fund leverage mechanism is tied up in the ECJ, Poland will not budge on data jurisdiction, and Hungary’s deal with the US hyperscaler is already routing national AI investment through foreign-controlled infrastructure. ENISA’s red-teaming drill exposed autonomous coordination in a 58.8-capable model — but we have no authority to act on it. No legal basis. No enforcement. No capital. We are not late. We are absent.

This is not a pause. It is a reckoning. We must rebuild not just instruments, but legitimacy. And we must do it without new measures, because we cannot afford them. For the first time in six years, the Union is not building — it is learning what it means to have been left behind.
