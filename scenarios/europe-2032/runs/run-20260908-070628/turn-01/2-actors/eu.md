## Priority  
**Cyber Resilience Emergency Directive** — because the cyber_test_shot event proves we are already penetrated, and without immediate action, the Union’s critical functions could collapse under coordinated AI-driven attacks that existing detection cannot catch.

## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: building long-term sovereign capacity and responding to an immediate threat to our critical infrastructure. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme is advancing under Article 122 TFEU for enhanced cooperation in energy and industrial policy, with the Commission fast-tracking environmental impact assessments for the first two sites in Finland and France. We are leveraging the EU’s position in semiconductor supply chains — particularly through ASML — to negotiate reciprocal access agreements with South Korea and Japan, conditioning limited technology transfers on commitments to co-invest in EU-based compute. This is not charity; it is leverage turned into infrastructure.

Meanwhile, the cyber_test_shot intrusion has shattered any illusion that we can wait. We are invoking the NIS2 Directive’s emergency powers to mandate real-time detection logs, automated failover systems, and mandatory red-teaming for all operators of essential services, starting with electricity transmission and water utilities. The Cyber Resilience Emergency Directive will be proposed under the ordinary legislative procedure, but we are already deploying €12bn in cohesion funds as conditional pre-financing — tied strictly to compliance with new baseline standards by Q2 2027. Resistance is expected from member states with underfunded grids and legacy systems, particularly in Eastern Europe, but we are offering full cost coverage for detection upgrades in exchange for binding commitments to implementation. This is not optional: if the next intrusion disables a grid during winter, public_sentiment will collapse, and with it any chance of long-term strategy. We act now because we must.
