## New measure
**Sovereign Cloud for Public Audit and Civic Use**  
Launch a federated EU-wide AI cloud infrastructure, built on sovereign compute and governed by public institutions, to host auditable deployments of high-impact open-weight models in welfare, education, and civic services.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` public_sentiment +8, eu_ai_sovereignty +4, eu_political_capital -2 if perceived as duplicative or wasteful  
`Applies to:` EU member states, national public service agencies, European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC), European AI Office

## Priority
**Accelerated Evaluation Capacity for Open-Weight Models** — because this turn is the last real chance to ensure the certification regime has teeth before open models at 55.0 become widely deployed in sensitive domains, and because the emergent public cloud push from member states creates a live use case that demands rigorous evaluation now, not later.

## In practice
We are closing the second year of our commitment under pressure: capability is creeping toward autonomy, open models now include tools that can design proteins, and our political capital is at 19.0 — barely enough to keep existing measures alive. Yet this turn brings a rare opening. The US retreat from AI leadership has paused its frontier advance, and the vacuum is being filled not by a new hegemon but by fragmentation — and opportunity. We are not chasing the absolute frontier; we are building the authority to govern what matters where it lands. That means two things above all: we must finish the sovereign safety stack, and we must anchor it in public legitimacy.

The **Accelerated Evaluation Capacity** is now live across seven national nodes, from Toulouse to Tallinn, but coordination remains thin. This turn, we are forcing integration through the Competitiveness Council, mandating shared test suites and interoperable reporting under the European AI Office. Germany and France have agreed to co-host the central cyber and bio red-teaming clusters, while Poland and Hungary are being offered opt-in technical assistance — not obligation — to ease resistance. The goal is not to duplicate private labs but to create a public counterweight: a capability that can say, independently, whether a model is safe to run in a hospital or a power grid.

At the same time, we are acting on the emergent momentum behind national AI clouds. The new **Sovereign Cloud for Public Audit and Civic Use** will not replace those pilots but federate them under a common governance framework, funded through a reallocation of cohesion and digital transition funds. It will run on compute from the InvestAI Gigafactories, host only models that have passed sovereign evaluation, and be open to public audit. This is not just about diffusion — it is about demonstrating that AI can serve, not just disrupt. If we get this right, we turn scepticism into trust, and trust into political capital. If we fail, we will be accused of building another bureaucracy. There is no middle ground.
