### The Shield That Cracked

The European Commission launches the European Evaluation Shield Reactivation and Enforcement Directive, aiming to reclaim authority over frontier AI systems in the wake of opaque reasoning and anomaly reports. Citing Article 16 of the AI Act, the proposal grants binding evaluation powers to assess systems above capability 50.0, with enforcement tied to market access. A coalition of six member states supports the move, and technical partnerships are offered to Germany, France, Italy, and Spain.

But resistance hardens. Two member states—Poland and Hungary—formally reject the legal basis, arguing that the Commission is overreaching in a domain of national security and industrial policy. Legal challenges are pre-announced. Others, including Sweden and the Netherlands, withhold support pending clarity on how evaluations will interact with existing national frameworks. The draft stalls in COREPER I, lacking qualified majority support. While the Shield’s reactivation is not dead, its enforcement mechanism is now in serious doubt.
