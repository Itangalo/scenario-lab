## New measure  
**European Evaluation Shield Reactivation and Enforcement Directive**  
Revive and legally empower the European Evaluation Shield to conduct binding pre-deployment evaluations of high-impact AI systems, with authority to delay or condition deployment based on safety findings, and mandate compliance from all providers operating in the single market.

- `Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
- `Size: large`  
- `Finishes on turn: 15`  
- `Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by 10–15 points; strengthen `eu_ai_sovereignty` by establishing enforceable authority over frontier systems; counteract `emergent_institutional_fracture` by reasserting EU-level legitimacy in AI governance  
- `Applies to:` All AI providers with significant presence in the EU, including foreign hyperscalers and national subsidiaries

## Priority  
**European Evaluation Shield Reactivation and Enforcement Directive** — because the collapse of sovereign compute and the fracturing of oversight have left the Union without credible control levers, and because the `eval_anomaly_reports` and `opaque_reasoning` events demand that we re-establish authority over systems whose behaviour we can no longer interpret or predict — even if we cannot yet build our own frontier models.

## In practice  
We act now not because the conditions are favourable, but because they are failing. The `emergent_institutional_fracture` is real: two member states have rejected the Evaluation Shield’s authority, and others are drafting parallel frameworks. If we do not reassert a common standard, the single market for high-stakes AI will fracture into national silos — and with `ai_capability` at 62.0 and `openweight_capability` rising, that is a risk we cannot absorb. We will not rebuild the Shield as a recommendation or a coordination body. This time, it will have teeth.

Using Article 16 of the AI Act as legal base, we propose a Directive to amend the Shield’s mandate: it will gain binding pre-deployment evaluation powers for any system exceeding 50.0 on the capability index, or any open-weight release above 45.0. Evaluations will include stress tests for emergent behaviour, misuse potential, and opacity of reasoning — directly responding to `eval_anomaly_reports` and `opaque_reasoning`. The Shield will have authority to impose deployment delays, require third-party monitoring, or mandate interpretability proxies where full chain-of-thought review is no longer possible. Non-compliant providers will face suspension of market access under the Digital Markets Act enforcement machinery.

We take this to COREPER I with a draft regulation already drafted by the Commission’s AI Office and ENISA, backed by a coalition of six member states that have experienced recent AI-enabled cyber incidents. We offer Germany and France a joint seat on the Shield’s technical panel, and promise Italy and Spain fast-tracked access to its evaluation data for national critical infrastructure screening. The cost is high — 3 political capital per turn for four turns — but we have 12, and we must spend it before the US administration resets the global posture. This is not sovereignty through compute, not yet. It is sovereignty through standards — the only leverage we still hold.
