### A Directive Ends, a Strategy Shifts

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation formally concludes without a single new facility activated. Germany and Ireland never ratified implementation, and the directive lapses, marking a symbolic end to the EU’s top-down push for sovereign infrastructure. Yet the ambition mutates: the Commission invokes digital fairness doctrine to require dominant AI providers to disclose energy use and spare capacity. The move sidesteps stalled construction by turning market power into leverage—hyperscalers must now open their books or risk antitrust scrutiny. However, enforcement remains conditional on member state cooperation, and no binding access has yet been secured.

Meanwhile, the Municipal Resilience Backchannel gains quiet momentum. Lyon, Barcelona, and Helsinki complete interoperability testing under ENISA, earning provisional certification as Trusted Resilience Nodes. Bologna, Ljubljana, and Tallinn are selected for rapid integration, funded through repurposed disaster recovery envelopes. The model proves adaptable—local legitimacy replaces central mandate.

### Cracks in Oversight, Doubts at the Frontier

The European Evaluation Shield fractures further as two member states reject its authority to enforce safety remediations, calling its legal basis opaque. With no unified enforcement, developers face patchwork compliance, and public trust in EU oversight erodes. At the same time, a major model release underperforms expectations, and prices for top-tier access drop—signs the frontier may be plateauing, though labs dispute this.

Open-weight systems advance modestly. Some capabilities from the earlier leak continue to diffuse into consumer-grade agent frameworks, enabling incremental improvements. However, without a major new open release or coordinated development surge, progress remains bounded by the pace of general advancement. The gap with the closed frontier does not widen.

### Political Capital at the Edge

With no new measure proposed and one large initiative formally closing, portfolio charge decreases slightly. The political capital burden eases from −13 to −7, allowing for minimal recovery. However, with legitimacy still unearned—capital (7.0) well below sentiment (34.0)—and no breakthrough success, gains are limited. The EU remains below the threshold to reliably launch new initiatives. Sentiment dips as institutional drift and fragmented oversight dominate headlines.
