## Priority  
**European Evaluation Shield Reactivation and Enforcement Directive** — because despite its current paralysis, it remains the only instrument we have that could restore sovereign oversight over systems whose behaviour we can no longer interpret, and because allowing it to die would signal irreversible fragmentation of EU authority in AI governance.

## In practice  
We are not withdrawing the Shield proposal. We are doubling down on its political survival, even as it stalls. The directive remains formally on the table in COREPER I, and we are now shifting from legal assertion to coalition repair. The Commission President is convening an informal trilogue with the leaders of Poland, Hungary, Sweden, and the Netherlands—framing the issue not as overreach, but as mutual defence. We are offering Poland a seat on the Shield’s incident review panel and proposing a joint audit of its critical infrastructure AI dependencies with Hungarian participation, to demonstrate transparency and shared interest.

At the same time, we are quietly engaging the emergent_fallback_coordination network—municipal IT directors, defence AI liaisons, and national cyber agencies—through DG CONNECT and ENISA. We are mapping their existing compute pools and model repositories, not to absorb them, but to understand how they might become a distributed enforcement backbone: a shadow infrastructure that could host evaluations if central authority fails. This is not a new measure, but reconnaissance in force. If the Shield collapses, we must know what can be salvaged. We are also preparing a contingency legal instrument under Article 4 of the Treaty on European Union—emergency competence—to be invoked only if a systemic incident renders the current deadlock untenable. For now, we hold the line. We spend the capital. We do not concede the principle. Sovereignty through standards is all we have left—and if we lose that, we lose the capacity to act at all.
