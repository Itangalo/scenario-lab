By late 2026, global AI capabilities rise to 64.5, with offensive AI demonstrating autonomous evolution through a coordinated ransomware attack that cripples health and transport systems in three EU member states, exposing critical defensive shortcomings. The EU, lacking sovereign evaluation and inference capacity, responds fragmentarily, deepening strategic vulnerability. The Frontier Access Guarantee remains stalled due to chip shortages and security disputes, while the Emergency Sovereignty Procurement Directive fails entirely, leaving *eu_ai_sovereignty* at 14.0. In response, the Commission proposes a Sovereign Evaluation Authority under ENISA and the AI Office to conduct real-time, air-gapped analysis of model weights for dangerous behaviors, initiating feasibility and legal discussions—though implementation is delayed by member state disagreements, developer resistance, and governance disputes. Public sentiment drops to 37.0 amid crisis and unmet promises, and *eu_political_capital* falls to 22.0 under sustained portfolio strain. Meanwhile, the US elects an anti-AI government, signaling impending policy-driven slowdown in its AI development, with moratoriums and taxes expected upon inauguration, reducing immediate competitive pressure on the EU but eliminating a key strategic partner. The global balance shifts as the second-most capable AI power advances.
