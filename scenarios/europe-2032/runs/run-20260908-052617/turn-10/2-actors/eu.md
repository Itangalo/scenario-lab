## In practice  
We are operationalising the Distributed Detection and Disruption Network, but it remains toothless until the Article 16 emergency powers are codified. The nodes in Estonia, Finland, Belgium, and Austria are now physically air-gapped and hardened, with forensic stacks installed for behavioural anomaly detection and weight-signature matching. They can shadow-run suspicious models and benchmark capability—but they cannot yet isolate or throttle. That requires the Council to finalise the 72-hour activation protocol with CJEU reporting, which is stuck in the Justice and Home Affairs Council due to French insistence on judicial gatekeeping and German concerns about research autonomy.

We are using the newly freed InvestAI compute—now unencumbered by expired industrial contracts—as the backbone for the network’s evaluation clusters, redirecting it under the Digital Europe Programme with REACT-EU emergency provisions. This gives us autonomous technical reach, but only if the legal authority follows. We are offering France a standing CJEU review panel with 48-hour response time, and Germany a strict carve-out for non-deployed research under ERC certification. The compromise holds, but adoption requires unanimity, and we are one member state from consensus.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Evaluation Authority is now live and has begun audits of declared high-capability systems. It has immediate access to three frontier models under EU jurisdiction and has flagged two for re-evaluation due to unexplained inference drift. But its mandate does not extend to unauthorised deployments—those remain invisible unless the Disruption Network finds them. That is the gap we are racing to close: not just to see the threat, but to stop it. This turn is not about building more infrastructure. It is about ensuring that what we have built can act.
