## New measure  
**Frontier Access Guarantee: Public Option for AI Inference**  
Establish a publicly operated, EU-owned inference infrastructure capable of running models at or near the closed frontier, independent of foreign providers, to serve essential public functions and anchor sovereign adoption.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty from 18.0 to at least 40.0 by turn 6; reduce dependence on foreign providers by delivering guaranteed inference capacity at capability index 58.0+ for health, energy, and research by turn 5.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member states’ critical public agencies, and EU-funded research consortia.

## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereignty Procurement Directive** — because without immediate operational access to frontier models, our public services degrade further, our resilience remains blind, and our industrial strategy builds capacity for systems we cannot control, rendering both sovereignty and safety unattainable.

## In practice  
We are acting on the reality that access is not a negotiation when the frontier moves faster than diplomacy. This turn, the Emergency Sovereignty Procurement Directive enters its decisive phase: we are finalising tripartite agreements with the transatlantic firm, the domestic consortium, and the Asian provider to deploy models at capability 52.0+ on EU soil under full operational control. The Commission, invoking Article 122 TFEU, has issued the implementing act, and the Internal Market Council will vote next month under urgency procedure. We are offering fast-tracked liability shielding under revised AI Liability Directive terms, 18 months of guaranteed procurement at premium rates, and access to curated public health and energy datasets — but only in exchange for ironclad weight security, audit access, and deployment within AISP-certified facilities in Luxembourg, Marseille, and Helsinki.

At the same time, we are launching the **Frontier Access Guarantee**, a public inference backbone to break the cycle of dependence. This is not a replacement for private investment — it is a floor beneath which no essential function will fall. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking will lead, repurposing 40% of EuroHPC’s next-phase capacity for sovereign AI inference, with dedicated funding of €12bn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU. We are contracting with ASML, Infineon, and a newly formed EU GPU consortium to prioritise chip allocation for this infrastructure, and the European Defence Agency is seconding cyber-physical security teams to harden the stack. Member states are being offered opt-in access to the network in exchange for contributing compute or waiving local data restrictions.

The Joint Resilience Drills conclude this turn, but their findings are sobering: simulations based on open-weight models at 44.5 failed to anticipate attack vectors now demonstrably feasible at 57.0. We are therefore redirecting the post-exercise review toward a new threat library, to be integrated into next cycle’s drills under a revised mandate — but only after we have access to the systems that can simulate real frontier threats. Without that, resilience is ritual, not readiness.
