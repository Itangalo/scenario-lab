### The Frontier Arrives Overnight

A leading open-weight model, functionally matching the closed frontier just months prior, spreads globally within days of release. It runs on consumer hardware, powers autonomous cyber agents, and is already repurposed for offensive use. The EU’s long-standing buffer between closed and open systems evaporates—what was once a year’s lead for frontier labs is now gone in weeks.

### A Union Grasps for Control

In response, the European Commission proposes emergency enforcement powers for its nascent Sovereign Evaluation Authority, shifting its mandate from oversight to active intervention. The plan would allow the Authority to halt deployments, demand model changes, or initiate containment protocols—but full legal adoption is delayed by national disagreements. Germany resists, demanding national override rights, while France pushes for co-leadership of the review panel. A compromise is reached in principle: suspensions will require 72-hour justification and joint review, with immunity for developers who submit to early audits. However, final ratification is pushed to the next turn due to procedural hurdles.

The €1.4bn REACT-EU fund is redirected from infrastructure to enforcement readiness, with air-gapped nodes in Estonia and Finland beginning setup for automated containment systems. These will not be operational until full legal authority is granted.

### Industrial Gains, Strategic Losses

The InvestAI Gigafactories officially complete, marking a symbolic victory for EU industrial policy. But the compute they deliver arrives too late to offset dependence: access to frontier models remains conditional, and the US posture of RETRENCHMENT continues to limit coordination. Sovereignty erodes further as global capability outpaces domestic control.

### Fragile Unity, Fading Trust

The EU joins a new joint cyber command, improving resilience through shared intelligence. Yet public sentiment declines as enforcement proposals spark fears of overreach. Media frame the Authority’s proposed powers as “AI martial law,” and tech workers protest in Paris and Berlin. Political capital, already strained, drops further under the weight of five large measures and a high-stakes priority.

The Sovereign Evaluation Authority’s expansion is counted as the single new measure this turn, absorbing the proposed emergency powers as part of its scope. No additional measure is introduced.
