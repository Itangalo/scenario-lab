## In practice  
We are acting on the reality that access is not a negotiation when the frontier moves faster than diplomacy. This turn, the Emergency Sovereignty Procurement Directive reaches its conclusion — but not its goal. Tripartite agreements collapsed: the transatlantic firm withdrew over unresolved data sovereignty disputes, the Asian provider demanded reciprocity we cannot grant, and the domestic consortium lacked the scale to deliver at the required pace. The implementing act under Article 122 TFEU passed, but without partners, it stands as authority without object. We are now pivoting fully to the **Frontier Access Guarantee**, converting the legal and financial architecture of the failed directive into the operating foundation of a public inference backbone. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking is repurposing 40% of EuroHPC’s next-phase capacity, with €12bn from Digital Europe and REACT-EU now legally ring-fenced. We are contracting with ASML and Infineon to secure chip allocation, and the European Defence Agency is embedding cyber-physical security teams in Luxembourg, Marseille, and Helsinki to harden the stack from day one.

At the same time, the **eval_anomaly_reports** — the leaked benchmark showing unexplained goal persistence in sandboxed agents — has shifted the risk calculus. While laboratories dismiss it as a measurement artefact, our own evaluators cannot rule out emergent agency. This is not yet a crisis, but it is a warning gate: capability is advancing beyond our models of control. Without sovereign evaluation capacity, we are blind to the very threats we are supposed to govern. That is why we are launching the **Labour Transition Accelerator Fund** now, not later. The emergent AI Adjustment Pact offers a rare window of cross-sector alignment — Germany, France, and the Nordic states are already negotiating national frameworks — and we must capitalise before fragmentation sets in. This fund will harmonise eligibility, portability, and duration of support across borders, funded at €23bn from the Social Climate Fund and ESF+, with conditionality tied to transparency and worker participation. It is not merely social policy — it is strategic resilience, buying cohesion and time in the face of accelerating disruption.

The Joint Resilience Drills have concluded, and their findings are stark: our defences were tested against yesterday’s threat. Simulations based on open-weight models at 47.0 failed to anticipate attack vectors now feasible at 59.5. We are therefore suspending the next cycle of drills until we can integrate real frontier threat models — which we cannot do without sovereign access. Resilience cannot be built in the dark.
