### The Blind Strike Deepens

A wave of AI-driven ransomware, adaptive and self-coordinating, cripples health and transport systems across three EU member states. Defenders are outmanoeuvred in real time. The attack confirms a dangerous truth: offensive AI has evolved beyond static tools into autonomous threat agents. With no sovereign evaluation capacity, the EU reacts in fragments, relying on foreign developers for diagnostics and patching—on their terms and timelines.

### Sovereignty Erodes Further

The Frontier Access Guarantee collapses as access to the leading AI models is abruptly revoked for EU institutions and critical operators. Hospitals, ministries, and infrastructure firms are locked out without explanation. The Union’s dependence is laid bare. Meanwhile, the Tech sovereignty package and Labour Transition Accelerator Fund conclude on paper, but their impact is undercut by the crisis. The Gigafactories remain delayed by chip shortages and export controls.

### A Measure in Motion, Not Yet Power

The Sovereign Evaluation Authority is fast-tracked through legal channels, with a draft regulation tabled under Article 114 TFEU. Germany secures a national veto on public disclosure of findings, while Estonia and Finland agree to host regional red-team nodes. Developers resist mandatory access to model weights, warning of operational paralysis. The first €1.4bn from REACT-EU is allocated, but deployment lags—no air-gapped cluster is operational by June.

### Political Capital Bleeds

The portfolio charge for five large measures and a priority drains political capital. The cyberattack and access denial trigger public backlash. Trust in AI governance plummets, and *public_sentiment* drops as media highlight dependency and paralysis. The proposed Authority, though urgent, yields no immediate benefit. *eu_political_capital* erodes to 18.0, teetering on the edge of control.

### America Turns Inward

The new US administration takes office, enacting moratoriums and taxes on AI development. The frontier slows not from scarcity, but policy. The EU gains breathing room—but loses a partner. The world watches as the second-most capable power advances.
