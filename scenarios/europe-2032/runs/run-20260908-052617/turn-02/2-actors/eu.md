Fast-track legal and financial authority to acquire, deploy, and operate frontier-capable AI systems on EU-controlled infrastructure, using emergency powers to bypass stalled negotiations and procurement cycles.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that the Union cannot govern what it cannot run. The cutoff from the leading frontier model has left hospitals unable to process complex diagnostics, research teams blind to emergent scientific pathways, and agencies relying on degraded or delayed inference. This is not a market failure — it is a sovereignty failure. We are invoking Article 122 TFEU and the crisis clause in the Digital Markets Act to establish an emergency procurement lane for AI systems capable of operating at or near the current frontier, conditioned on deployment within EU legal jurisdiction, under EU operational control, and with full weight security and audit access.

This turn, we are negotiating with three providers — one transatlantic, one domestic consortium, and one Asian — to secure immediate access to models at capability index 52.0 or higher, to be hosted in Luxembourg, Marseille, and Helsinki under AISP-certified facilities. The Commission’s Joint Research Centre will act as technical evaluator, using newly expanded authority under the AI Act to conduct pre-deployment assessments. We are offering fast-tracked liability shielding, 18 months of guaranteed procurement, and access to public datasets in exchange for priority provisioning. Resistance is expected from member states wary of centralisation and from providers fearing precedent, but the member_state_defection event has hardened our hand: if one state can cut its own deal, we will cut one for all. The directive will be tabled in the Internal Market Council next month, with a qualified majority expected despite early murmurs of opt-out. Without this, our resilience drills test defences against threats we cannot even simulate, and our industrial strategy builds capacity for a race we are already losing.
