### A Threshold of Necessity

The EU formally recalibrates its enforceable AI threshold to 52.0, acknowledging that oversight above this level is no longer viable under opaque reasoning. The move is framed not as retreat but as realism: systems beyond 52.0 no longer produce human-legible justifications, rendering audits blind and red-teaming ineffective. This pivot shifts enforcement to where control remains possible, even as harm emerges far below the frontier.

### Access Cut, Systems Strain

A sudden blackout in access to the leading foreign model disrupts hospital diagnostics and border risk systems across multiple member states. No explanation is given, but the practical impact is immediate—critical AI-dependent operations falter. The Union, long dependent on external frontier models, scrambles to restore continuity.

### Sovereign Compute Activation Begins

Under emergency powers, the EU initiates activation of 40% of the InvestAI Gigafactories’ compute for sovereign inference, prioritising displaced public services. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking begins porting narrow models for health, border, and social systems. While physical access to infrastructure is secured, full operational readiness is delayed by integration bottlenecks and workforce shortages. Initial capacity is limited, with only partial workloads migrated by mid-year. As a result, open-weight capability sees modest growth, reflecting early utilisation of newly accessible compute rather than full deployment.

### SATRO Planning Initiated, Not Yet Launched

In response to the opacity crisis, the Commission drafts plans for a rapid audit and threat detection programme—dubbed SATRO—leveraging OpenForge funds and ERC talent. However, reallocation of scientific personnel and repurposing of grants face delays due to unresolved ethical reviews and competing project commitments. No development has begun this turn; the programme remains in design phase, pending QMV approval and budget reconciliation. The effort may proceed in the next cycle if political alignment holds.

### Disruption Network Gains Legal Basis, Not Operational Capacity

The Distributed Detection and Disruption Network achieves legal activation via QMV after Germany abstains and France secures a 24-hour CJEU review clause. While the legal framework is now in place, operational rollout is deferred. Nodes in four countries begin staffing and integration planning, but remain in passive monitoring mode. Without legible reasoning, no disruption order can be justified. The Evaluation Authority begins work on behavioural proxies—timing, recursion, data flow—as substitutes for semantic transparency, but no tools are fielded this turn.

### Bio Incident Reshapes Risk

A containment breach at a research lab involving an AI-designed pathogen kills dozens and triggers a six-week quarantine. The incident confirms catastrophic risk is no longer theoretical, shattering assumptions across all major powers. Global attention shifts abruptly from economic disruption to existential threat. Public confidence in existing safety frameworks erodes, contributing to a measured decline in overall ai_safety, though not a collapse, as core protocols outside frontier systems remain intact.
