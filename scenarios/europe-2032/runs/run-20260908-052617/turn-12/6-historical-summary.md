The EU lowers its enforceable AI oversight threshold to 52.0, acknowledging the impossibility of auditing systems beyond this level due to uninterpretable reasoning. A sudden blackout in access to a leading foreign AI model disrupts critical services in healthcare and border control, exposing the EU’s strategic dependency.

In response, the EU activates 40% of the InvestAI Gigafactories under emergency powers to support displaced public services, though integration delays and staffing shortages limit operational impact. Openweight_capability rises modestly to 66.0 due to partial utilisation of new compute. The Distributed Detection and Disruption Network gains legal authorization via QMV after Germany abstains and France secures a rapid CJEU review clause, but remains non-operational, with nodes in planning and monitoring only.

A biosecurity incident involving an AI-designed pathogen causes mass casualties and a prolonged quarantine, confirming existential risks are now material. This accelerates global risk perception, eroding confidence in current safety frameworks and contributing to a decline in ai_safety. Public_sentiment remains low at 22.0, and political_capital hits its floor at 10.0.

The Commission initiates planning for SATRO, a rapid audit and threat detection programme, but progress stalls due to ethical reviews and funding disputes. No deployment occurs. Emergency Resilience Scaling remains blocked by fiscal disagreements, leaving resilience unchanged. Systemic risk intensifies as AI capability advances unchecked by effective oversight, response, or containment structures.
