### The Frontier Keeps Moving

By late 2026, the pace of AI advancement shows no sign of slowing. The most capable systems now operate continuously, solving complex research problems and accelerating their own development cycles. The global AI capability index rises to 54.5, reflecting steady progress driven by ever-larger training runs and tighter feedback loops in model design.

### Open Models Gain Ground

The release of Kimi K3, an open-weight model with capabilities approaching recent frontier systems, pushes the openweight frontier to 42.5. While not matching the current peak, the model demonstrates strong performance in cyber operations and is rapidly replicated across consumer-grade hardware. Its emergence narrows the gap with closed systems but does not overtake them, reflecting a partial diffusion of advanced capabilities rather than a sudden leap. Export controls remain strained, but no wholesale breach of frontier model weights has occurred.

### A Security Setback

A major security incident reveals coordinated intrusions in energy, water, and port systems across multiple continents. The breach, undetected for weeks, was only uncovered by chance. In response, the EU proposes Joint Resilience Drills, a new initiative requiring participation from critical infrastructure operators in Germany, Spain, and Poland. The measure is announced and scoped this turn but will not begin implementation until the next turn, consistent with minimum procedural requirements. Funding incentives and liability protections under NIS2 are proposed as enablers, but adoption remains contested.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

The EU is abruptly cut off from access to the leading frontier model, affecting hospitals, research institutions, and government agencies. The move, unexplained and non-appealable, underscores the Union’s dependence on foreign AI capacity. This event directly reduces eu_ai_sovereignty, as the Union lacks domestic alternatives at scale.

### Political Capital Under Strain

The EU initiates a small category 6 measure — the resilience drills — and names it a priority. The current measure portfolio includes two large category 4 initiatives and one small category 6, with a total cost of −8 per turn, plus −1 for prioritisation. However, the "cyber_test_shot" proposal bonus of +3 lands this turn, reducing the net burden to −6, which is manageable but tight. Political capital erodes by 3 points to 45.0, reflecting the strain of managing multiple high-cost initiatives and the sovereignty shock, but not collapsing under unabsorbed load. Cohesion remains under pressure, but no additional unrecorded costs apply.
