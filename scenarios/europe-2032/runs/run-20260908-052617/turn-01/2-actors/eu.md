## In practice  
We are responding to the intrusion revealed this autumn by launching the first EU-wide live-fire cyber resilience campaign focused on AI-empowered threats. Under the authority of the Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) and in coordination with the Energy, Transport, and Internal Security Council formations, we are mandating participation from national grid operators, major port authorities, and water utilities in three pilot member states—Germany, Spain, and Poland—with full rollout planned by turn 3. The exercises will simulate multi-week AI-coordinated intrusions modelled on the recent breach, testing detection, cross-border alerting, and manual override protocols under real-time pressure.

This turn, we are finalising the exercise design with national CERTs and securing political cover through the Permanent Representatives Committee, where resistance has emerged over sovereignty concerns and liability in case of accidental disruption. To overcome this, we are offering liability shielding under Article 4 of the NIS2 Directive and tying participation to enhanced EU funding for cyber hardening under the Digital Europe Programme. The drills are not optional: they are the first binding test of whether our critical systems can withstand what is already loose in the wild. Without this, every investment in compute or control is built on sand.
