### Holding the line
July to December 2031 became a long exercise in keeping degraded systems running. With two Atlantic cables still down and Asian fabrication offline, therapy doses arrived late, triage assistants timed out, and hospitals stayed on manual protocols. Power and telecoms held through emergency rerouting — brownouts and dropped calls, but no blackout.

The Energy Council's repair teams shuttled between gigafactory grid works hit by arson. Guards were posted, a section re-energised, then another pylon burned. Construction never resumed at pace. Mayors in Germany and Italy extended local bans on unattended robots after further night fires.

Brussels asked for nothing new. No procurement, no siting promises. Bridge pay continued in ports and warehouses idled by supply cuts, while graduate hiring stayed frozen across law, accountancy and software. The separate bilateral compute deal in one capital persisted, quietly draining the little pooled capacity that remained.

Abroad, the middle-power channel that had been built over the past year finally delivered its modest return: a joint evaluation note with partners, a small pooled order for licensed components, and a shared deconfliction message to Washington and Beijing. Neither belligerent honoured non-belligerence reliably, but landing stations were spared a direct hit in the autumn.

By December the Union was intact, rationed, and exhausted — non-belligerent in word, degraded in fact, with open models running on hundreds of thousands of machines beyond any oversight.
