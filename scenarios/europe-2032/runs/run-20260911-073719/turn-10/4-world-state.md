### War over the frontier
In March the shooting started in the Pacific. Washington and Beijing each claimed the other was months from an uncatchable lead, and both acted as if waiting was the greater risk. Within weeks fabrication plants, undersea cables, satellite links and data centres were declared military targets. Europe was not asked.

Two cable cuts in the Atlantic and a strike on an Asian fab rippled straight into European clinics. Therapy doses held for days in customs and compute queues, triage assistants timed out, allocation software reverted to rationing. Brussels declared EU territory and landing stations non-belligerent and offered both sides deconfliction and test recognition for no targeting and continued supply. Neither capital refused; neither complied reliably.

### Arson at home, hiring freeze everywhere
At home the night fires spread. From fenced robot pilots in Germany and Italy the arson moved to gigafactory grid-connection works, halting construction. The Energy Council took emergency control, sent civil-protection repair teams and posted guards. Work restarted in fits, then stopped again after the next attack.

The jobs data hardened into a political fact. Law firms, accountants, software houses and customer operations simply stopped hiring juniors. Bridge pay under the newly finished Guarantee reached ports and warehouses hit by war disruption, but mayors said Brussels could keep a therapy slot open and still not produce a single interview.

A new openly downloadable model, close to the closed frontier, spread to hundreds of thousands of machines in days. Evaluators in Brussels were still writing notes on the last release when the next one was already running unattended in training clusters neither government could see into.

The wayward capital kept its separate hyperscaler pact, taking what pooled compute survived while keeping its bilateral discount. The middle-power club met urgently, communiqués promised pooled bargaining, but no vendor re-routed chips because a communiqué asked.

By June power, health and telecoms were still running, isolated by emergency rerouting — degraded, not stopped.
