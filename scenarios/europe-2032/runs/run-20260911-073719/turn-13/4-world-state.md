### The queue lengthens
The second joint order of tailored blood-cancer cures never reached the scale promised. Doses arrived in Lyon, Barcelona and Krakow on public prescription, with cold-chain vans photographed at hospital doors, while clinics in Turin, Ghent and Brno stayed on waiting lists. Health ministers spoke of erasing postcode rationing; nurses spoke of triage screens freezing and returning to paper.

Graduate hiring did not return. Law firms, accountants, software houses and customer centres confirmed what students already knew: entry posts were simply not re-advertised. Bridge pay was extended to idle logistics halls, where the new machines stood behind shutters.

### Hands that do not stop
Chinese-built humanoids running American control software moved from pilots to night shifts in Rotterdam, Antwerp and Gdansk. Mayors enforced bans on unattended operation; owners claimed the night runs were supervised. After two more substation fires and a pylon blaze near a battery plant spur, Europol opened formal sabotage probes while distributors insisted the fires were arson by opponents.

Brussels tabled no new robot law. Inspectors enforced the old safety accord site by site, and evaluation of the control models was pushed into the joint note with middle powers.

### Dark cables
The neutral consortium hired to repair the two dark Atlantic cables withdrew in October after further interference and loss of insurance. Clinics and compute queues went onto prolonged rationing. Brownouts held without blackout, the repair barge stayed insured through the middle-power channel, but landing stations ran degraded.

In response the Union signed accession to the offered joint cyber command and biosurveillance pact, pooling telemetry and samples it could not collect alone. Connection rooms were staffed in Tallinn and Lisbon; hospital power and landing-station monitoring stayed running, barely.

By December the Union was intact, degraded, and visibly divided between those inside the cure pilots and those outside them.
