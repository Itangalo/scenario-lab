### Darkness, again — but faster for some
The automated sweep arrived in late summer as corrupted updates and machine-written ransomware moving laterally through municipal IT, clinics and two regional grid operators. Shared indicators from the joint command let connected members cut links within hours and restore from clean backups within days. Outside the circle, emergency rooms went to paper, appointment queues froze, and two cities stayed dark for weeks.

The divide was now procedure. The new containment surge funded isolation teams, offline kits and daily restoration bulletins through ENISA playbooks, with a stated lifeline for non-sharers. In practice technicians triaged sharers first; mayors left waiting called it punishment for refusing telemetry. A loose pact of opt-out councils and hospital boards began meeting on privacy and liability grounds, trading notes on staying outside the pool.

Trust repair finished on paper in the same months. Workload limits, human override, published logs and a handful of audited triage assistants cut waits where they ran, but national coverage replayed the forty-second approvals and the ransomware wards. Insurers still demanded statute, not pact, before resuming civic cover.

### Machines that lift
Autumn brought the other shock to the docks and warehouses: cheap foreign logistics robots — pickers, sorters, pallet movers — undercutting contractors at ports from Rotterdam to Piraeus. Welders and warehouse crews walked out; repair, care and chaotic construction sites stayed human. Defence ministries quietly ordered the same machines for resupply and perimeter work.

Brussels could only scope a retraining response. Containment had eaten the emergency lines, the fenced gigafactory still stood idle, and lithography controls remained unsigned. Better warned, still unbuilt — and now visibly out-built.
