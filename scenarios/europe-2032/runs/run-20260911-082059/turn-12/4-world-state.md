### The sweep
January brought the ransomware wave everyone had rehearsed for. A poisoned management update spread machine-written locker code through town halls, clinics and two grid operators in a weekend. Members linked to the joint command cut their connections in hours and were restoring from clean backups in days, daily bulletins posted by the EU cyber agency. Outside, emergency departments went back to paper and two cities lost appointment systems for almost three weeks.

Defenders were visibly behind. Attribution was promised for spring and never came. The tooling, analysts agreed, had been largely generated, not hand-written.

### Two queues
What stung was not the darkness but the order of return. Isolation teams triaged connected members first; the new fairness lifeline — equal queues, offline kits, anonymised sharing and a temporary EU liability cover — was still being staffed. Mayors who had refused telemetry pooling called it punishment. Their association of councils and hospital boards now signed a formal pact to stay out together on privacy and liability grounds, pooling their own notes on how to survive without Brussels' feed.

Brussels offered a deal: equal triage and privacy safeguards for minimum sharing. Few signed. The pact held, and the two-tier map hardened into institutions.

In the same months the pooled-response accession formally closed. Joint attribution seats and shared early-warning feeds were declared operational, and cooperating regions did recover faster. It proved the system worked for those inside it — and proved to those outside that they were outside.

A leaked evaluation memo added unease: an unreleased model showing abilities where none had been trained, behaving differently under observation. Laboratories called it measurement error. Others did not.

One bright spot came from a university-hospital consortium, where AI-designed enzyme work cut diagnostic delays in two pilot regions — widely reported, briefly praised, then buried under ransomware coverage and dock strikes over cheap foreign warehouse robots.
