### Accession on credit
Brussels signed what it could afford. With chip slots still cancelled and data-centre money gone, the Council accepted the invitation to the pooled cyber and bio response, wiring ENISA telemetry and HERA sample feeds into the joint command in exchange for early warning and a seat at the attribution table. Technicians called it the only hardening on offer that did not require new concrete.

On paper it worked. When a fresh wave of update-poisoned intrusions probed clinics and municipal networks in spring, shared indicators let cooperating members isolate in hours. Non-sharers again stayed dark longer, and the divide hardened into routine: inside the sharing circle, containment; outside, delay.

The liability pact closed in the same months. The compromise text clarified who pays when a civic assistant errs, and insurers nodded politely and still did not resume cover. Law, they said, not a pact draft. Deployments beyond the pilot municipalities stayed frozen.

That freeze was political, not technical. The team charged with repairing trust pushed workload limits, mandatory human override on benefit queues, published logs and redress desks, and paid for a handful of audited triage roll-outs from old emergency lines. Nurses in those towns reported shorter waits; national press reported the forty-second approvals again. Lawful-harm framing held.

The Hague, Tokyo and Seoul left the licensing offer on the table and asked again for binding controls on lithography first. The fenced gigafactory site grew weeds. American labs, slowed by their own retrenchment, bought Brussels time no one used to build.

By June the Union was better warned, still unbuilt, and still arguing about legitimacy.
