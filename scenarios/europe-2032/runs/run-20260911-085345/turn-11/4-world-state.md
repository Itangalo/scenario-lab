### Patching on borrowed tools
The autumn offered one piece of good news. Research teams on both sides of the Atlantic demonstrated patching and detection software that finally closed a whole class of intrusion at machine speed, and capitals hit by the same springtime shocks agreed to pool live telemetry and pathogen samples in a joint command Brussels was invited to join.

The Union joined. With money and votes exhausted, the Council authorised the cyber agency as gateway for the shared feed and the health bodies as node for the sample pact, buying the new defensive stack centrally and pushing it first to energy, telecoms and hospital networks. Engineers attached to isolation wards reported fewer cascading chart errors and faster municipal handoffs. Containment held: the cross-border medical surge finished its work, freeing triage teams, while the bio containment surge stayed as the operational centre.

It was not enough to restore trust.

### The lawful wrong
In October, an ombudsman and then a court found that an automated system used in benefits and policing support had systematically wronged thousands — denied claims, flagged families, queued for a caseworker to approve in under a minute. The Commission's own review conceded the worst version: the deployment had sat in a gap in the 2024 rulebook. Everything done had been lawful.

Editorial pages called the flagship tech law badly drawn, built for the systems of 2024 against the deployments of 2031. Ministers froze new public-sector rollouts pending an audit of approval queues but tabled no rewrite they had no votes to carry. Bereaved-family networks and anti-AI groups continued to picket logistics sites, slowing countermeasure runs for days at a time.

Offshore, extended exercises around Taiwan lifted shipping insurance and drew only a monitoring note from Brussels. Hospitals stayed open through December, grids did not cascade, and detection improved — but the public judgment hardened: protected on others' tools, governed by rules that permitted the harm, dependent still on models no one could audit.
