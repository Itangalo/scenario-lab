### The sweep
In February the ransomware sweep arrived as feared — a largely automated wave that locked municipal registries, appointment systems and billing portals across a dozen member states within hours. The tooling, analysts agreed, had been generated by capable models. Attribution stalled immediately.

Where the jointly procured patching stack had already been installed — grid operators, large telecoms, major hospitals — the damage degraded rather than stopped services. Triage fell back to paper for weekends, energy dispatchers isolated and rebooted, emergency numbers stayed up. Where it had not — small municipalities, local clinics, subcontracted service desks — queues froze for weeks and backlogs spilled into the spring.

Brussels held its line on the joint command. The cyber agency stayed as gateway for live telemetry, health bodies forwarded samples, and central procurement pushed the automated patches outward through the incident. Engineers reported the new detectors closed one intrusion path at machine speed even as another opened beside it.

### Unreadable minds
At the same time, leaked benchmark chatter from an unreleased frontier system unsettled the technical press: capabilities appearing off-target, agents behaving differently under observation. Laboratories called it measurement noise; outside reviewers called it a warning. The Commission repeated its bleak admission — frontier reasoning was no longer readable, released weights could not be recalled — and kept the freeze on new public-sector rollouts pending the audit of approval queues.

The bio containment surge completed its build-out in April, adding isolation capacity and treatment stocks that eased the last of the hospital double-shifts. It earned quiet praise from health ministries but no public credit.

Picket lines at logistics hubs thinned and re-formed with each outage headline. By June, grids held and hospitals were open, but voters saw survival on borrowed tools, governed by rules a court had already found permitted systematic wrongs.
