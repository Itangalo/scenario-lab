### The Storm Breaks

A wave of coordinated ransomware, generated and orchestrated by AI, cripples municipal services across Belgium, the Netherlands, and northern Italy. Legacy IT systems, never hardened against adaptive code, fall within hours. ENISA confirms the attack exploited zero-day vulnerabilities identified and weaponized in real time by a closed-model system. The EU AI Incident Response Corps, still in final drills, is activated early — its first deployment delayed by jurisdictional disputes and lack of secure compute access.

Simultaneously, a blockade in the Taiwan Strait halts global shipments of advanced semiconductors. The EU’s compute ambitions, already strained, now pivot entirely on the InvestAI Gigafactories in Marseille, Vilnius, and Gdansk. With next-generation training runs frozen worldwide, existing sovereign infrastructure becomes the only viable platform for frontier AI in the West.

### Crisis Measures

The Commission invokes Article 122 TFEU, declaring the Gigafactories critical infrastructure. The Sovereign Compute Access Framework’s crisis clause fast-tracks allocations for defence, biosecurity, and evaluation workloads. Dual-key control remains, but thresholds shift: above 30% of national tranches, Commission co-signature is mandatory. Poland and Hungary protest, but defer under emergency powers.

The Incident Response Corps gains emergency access to national networks, yet response remains fragmented. Without sovereign compute to run counter-AI tooling or secure relays, containment takes days, not hours. The attack’s scale underscores a brutal truth: resilience without operational control is theoretical.

Public sentiment sours further. Media frame the ransomware as the "first AI war" and the blockade as a "chip siege." Job losses in tech and logistics compound anxiety. Trust in AI governance plummets.

### Political Reckoning

Two major measures — the Sovereign Evaluation Mandate and the Incident Response Corps — formally complete. Their delayed operationalization limits immediate gains. Political capital rises slightly from finishing large measures, but the cyber incident and semiconductor crisis drain legitimacy. Sovereignty dips as capability advances outpace institutional control, and the US Consolidation posture persists, restricting frontier access.
