A coordinated AI-generated ransomware attack cripples critical infrastructure across Western Europe, exposing systemic vulnerabilities and fragmenting the EU’s Incident Response Corps deployment due to jurisdictional and compute access issues. Simultaneously, a blockade in the Taiwan Strait halts global semiconductor supplies, forcing the EU to rely entirely on its newly completed InvestAI Gigafactories in Marseille, Vilnius, and Gdansk as the sole operational frontier compute base in the West.

In response, the European Commission invokes Article 122 TFEU, designating the Gigafactories as critical infrastructure and activating the Sovereign Compute Access Framework’s crisis protocol, imposing mandatory Commission co-signature for national allocations above 30%. Poland and Hungary resist but comply under emergency powers. Despite formal completion of the Sovereign Evaluation Mandate and the Incident Response Corps, delayed operationalization and lack of secure compute severely limit effectiveness.

Public trust collapses amid framing of the attacks as an "AI war" and "chip siege," exacerbated by widespread job losses. Resilience is exposed as hollow without operational control, while EU sovereignty gains material footing through physically secure compute but suffers politically due to lagging institutional capacity and ongoing US export restrictions on frontier models.
