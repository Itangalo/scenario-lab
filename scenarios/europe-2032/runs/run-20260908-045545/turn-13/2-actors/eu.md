## In practice  
The Operational Command for Sovereign Compute is now legally established and operational. Despite continued resistance from Poland and Hungary, the Council adopted the revised framework under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the cyber_major_incident as a demonstration of systemic risk. The High Council for Sovereign Compute will be co-chaired by a Commission appointee and a rotating member-state representative, with binding ECJ arbitration. The promised 6% compute allocation for Poland and Hungary has been codified as a transitional safeguard, and ENISA has confirmed zero-trust hardening in Vilnius, Marseille, and Gdansk will be complete by turn 14. This closes a six-month vulnerability window and marks the first time the Union has unified command over its strategic AI infrastructure — a foundational gain, but one now buried under deeper crises.

We are responding to the cyber_major_incident with the Emergency Resilience Surge, targeting the most exposed nodes: hospital networks, energy grid operators, and digital ID systems. Using €900 million from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, we are deploying automated intrusion detection systems trained to recognise AI-generated attack patterns, rolling out mandatory zero-trust segmentation for all Tier 1 providers, and launching a 72-hour red-team sprint across ten member states to simulate cascading failures. The incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that encrypted regional health records in Bavaria, Wallonia, and Emilia-Romagna — has made the threat concrete. Attribution remains uncertain, but the tooling’s sophistication points to frontier-level AI, likely repurposed from open-weight derivatives now at 59.0 capability. We cannot wait for perfect attribution; we must assume the offensive edge is already distributed.

Simultaneously, we are scaling the Public Trust and Transition Initiative to meet the social shock. The European Social Fund is releasing €800 million in immediate retraining grants, prioritising law, accounting, and administrative graduates, with wage insurance covering 60% of previous income during transitions. The *AI That Works For Us* platform is now live in all 27 member states, curating successes like Helsinki’s permit automation and Valencia’s hospital wait-time reductions — but we are also publishing Eurostat’s monthly displacement dashboard, showing 142,000 net job losses in AI-exposed professions this turn. We are not hiding the cost. We are framing the response as a collective adjustment — not a failure of policy, but a test of solidarity. Because if public sentiment drops further, no amount of sovereign compute will save us from political paralysis.
