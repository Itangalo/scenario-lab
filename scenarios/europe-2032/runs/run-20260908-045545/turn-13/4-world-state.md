### The Fractured Response

AI capability advanced again, +1.0 this turn, as sparse activation techniques matured across research and industrial applications. With no safety improvements landing, `ai_capability` reached 71.5, while `ai_safety` remained at 2.0 — a dangerous plateau of high capability and near-total opacity in decision-making systems. The gap fueled misuse, but open replication did not keep pace: `openweight_capability` held at 59.0, as political fragmentation, sabotage in Poland and Hungary, and restricted compute access impeded the diffusion of frontier capabilities. No major open-source releases or model leaks occurred this turn, and the offensive edge remained concentrated.

A major cyber incident unfolded mid-turn, overwhelming regional health systems in Bavaria, Wallonia, and Emilia-Romagna with a fast-spreading, AI-generated ransomware strain. The attack exploited zero-day vulnerabilities chained by automated planning, evading signature-based detection. Though no fatalities occurred, emergency care delays and data unavailability lasted days. The offensive edge had clearly outpaced defense.

In response, the EU formally activated the **Operational Command for Sovereign Compute**, now established under Article 114 TFEU. Despite Polish and Hungarian resistance, binding ECJ arbitration and a 6% transitional compute allocation secured just enough consensus. ENISA confirmed zero-trust hardening in key nodes, marking a foundational gain in control. This earned a +5 finish bonus under rule 5. The measure is now complete and transitions to ongoing cost.

Simultaneously, the EU proposed the **Emergency Resilience Surge**, a large category 6 measure targeting critical infrastructure. It aims to harden energy, health, and digital ID systems against AI-powered threats, with red-teaming and automated patching to be deployed across ten member states. However, due to its scale and cost, full implementation will require at least two turns. No effects are realized this turn, and the proposal bonus is not yet granted.

The **Public Trust and Transition Initiative** continued, now scaled to address visible job losses — 142,000 in AI-exposed professions this turn. Retraining funds flowed, but public sentiment remained stagnant at 16.0, still marked by resentment and fear.
