## In practice  
We are pressing forward with the Operational Command for Sovereign Compute, now legally anchored in Article 122 TFEU and hosted under DG CONNECT, but the political resistance from Poland and Hungary remains acute. We are convening an extraordinary COREPER II session next week to finalise the delegation of operational authority over the InvestAI Gigafactories, offering binding guarantees on minimum national allocation thresholds—5% each for Warsaw and Budapest—and joint oversight through a newly established High Council for Sovereign Compute, co-chaired by member-state appointees and Commission leadership. The €1.2 billion from Digital Europe and NextGenerationEU is being disbursed to upgrade secure operations cadres, deploy zero-trust architecture across all three sites, and harden relays against remote compromise.

At the same time, we are integrating early lessons from the emergent_regional_auditing_alliance’s successful intervention in the German energy grid scheduler incident into the command’s protocols. While the alliance operates independently, its red-teaming findings are feeding directly into our secure execution standards. We are also working with ENISA and the Joint Research Centre to begin shadow-monitoring agent workloads on sovereign infrastructure, aiming to detect anomalous coordination patterns before they escalate. But without full legal ratification of the command’s authority, these efforts remain fragmented—our unity of action still lags behind our unity of hardware.
