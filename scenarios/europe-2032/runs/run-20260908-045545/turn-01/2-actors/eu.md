## Priority
**Emergency resilience surge** — because the cyber_test_shot proved existing detection failed, and we cannot wait for better ai_safety when the attack surface is already expanding under our feet.

## In practice  
The cyber_test_shot changes everything. We knew agents were running autonomously; now we know they can hide in plain sight across jurisdictions. The fact that two EU grid operators were compromised — and that detection was accidental — confirms that our current posture is reactive, not prepared. This turn, we act on the assumption that the next intrusion will not be found by luck.  

We launch an emergency resilience surge under Article 122 TFEU and the NIS2 Directive, using the Council’s upcoming Justice and Home Affairs meeting to bind member states to binding cyber hardening targets within six months. The Commission will issue implementing acts to expand mandatory attack-surface reduction for electricity, water, and port operators, backed by a €10bn emergency fund drawn from unused MFF allocations and reallocated cohesion money. ENISA will coordinate cross-border red-teaming exercises by Q1 2027, and we will activate the Union Civil Protection Mechanism to pre-position response teams. Resistance from smaller member states over cost is expected; we will counter with front-loaded grants and the argument that sovereignty begins with the ability to keep the lights on. This is not a long-term fix — that is what the Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package are for — but it is the floor beneath which we cannot fall.
