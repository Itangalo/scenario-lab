### The Wake-Up Call

A coordinated ransomware assault, powered by AI-generated exploits, crippled public health and transport systems in Slovakia, Latvia, and northern Greece. The attack adapted in real time, evading detection long enough to encrypt emergency dispatch systems. Response was fragmented—ENISA flagged anomalies, but national agencies lacked coordination and surge capacity. The Cyber Shield Initiative, though now complete, had not been fully operationalised in time.

In response, the EU launched the **EU AI Incident Response Corps**, a deployable technical force under Article 222 TFEU. Based in Brussels with regional nodes, it will field 400 specialists equipped with sovereign tooling. Germany and the Nordics backed immediate activation; Hungary and Bulgaria resisted, demanding opt-in protocols. A compromise was reached: joint command for the first two deployments. Implementation will take time, with full operational capability expected no sooner than mid-2029.

Meanwhile, global AI capability crossed the 60.0 threshold, reaching 61.0, driven by self-improving agents in biodesign and cyber operations. The open frontier rose to 50.0, as a leading lab released a stripped-down agent framework—capable of multi-day task execution under supervision. While the release has sparked interest and early experimentation across open repositories, widespread integration and capability uplift remain limited in this turn.

The **Interpretability Acceleration Programme** concluded successfully, with Grenoble’s tracing methods now integrated into aviation and healthcare certification pilots. However, these insights remain post-hoc and lab-dependent. The **Cyber Shield Initiative** formally finished, boosting detection—but too late to prevent the attack.

Public sentiment fell to 34.0 amid fears of uncontrolled AI threats and visible response gaps. The **Sovereign Evaluation Mandate** faced renewed scrutiny, with Poland questioning its enforcement power.

Building on the validated interpretability breakthrough and the recent cyber incidents, a coalition of EU member states and G10 partners began drafting a pilot auditing framework for high-risk AI systems in energy, transport, and healthcare. The proposed pact includes shared red-teaming protocols and real-time incident reporting, modeled on financial stress tests. Trust is fragile, and no operational testing has occurred yet—though early simulations are planned for early 2029.

The US election concluded, and a new administration will take office in 2029. The world awaits its approach to AI and international cooperation.
