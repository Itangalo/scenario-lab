**EU AI Incident Response Corps**  
A standing, cross-border technical unit trained and equipped to deploy within hours to major AI-enabled incidents, conducting forensic analysis, containment, and recovery support.

## Priority
**EU AI Incident Response Corps** — because the cyber_major_incident this turn proves that offensive model-generated tooling is now operational and effective, and without a standing, deployable capability to respond, our sovereignty is performative, not real.

## In practice
The cyber_major_incident has changed the ground beneath us. A model-generated ransomware sweep has crippled public services in three member states, exploiting zero-day vulnerabilities in legacy infrastructure. The attack was not novel in form, but its speed, targeting precision, and autonomous adaptation under countermeasures were — hallmarks of agent-level coordination. Slovakia and Latvia, still lagging in Cyber Shield integration, were hit hardest. ENISA’s detection tools flagged anomalies, but response was fragmented, delayed, and under-resourced. We now know that resilience is not just about hardening — it is about having hands on the console when the system fails.

We are therefore launching the EU AI Incident Response Corps under Article 222 TFEU and the Union Civil Protection Mechanism, adapting its governance model to include technical surge capacity. This is not another study or framework — it is a deployable force, headquartered in Brussels with regional nodes in Warsaw, Ljubljana, and Lisbon, staffed by 400 cross-trained engineers, cryptographers, and incident commanders, equipped with sovereign tooling and pre-authorised access to critical systems. Funding of €3.2bn will come from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused allocations and redirected cohesion funds, with national contributions in kind. Germany and the Nordic states support immediate activation; Hungary and Bulgaria express concern over sovereignty, so we are offering opt-in deployment protocols and joint command for the first two incidents. The Corps will train quarterly, respond under ENISA coordination, and report publicly after every activation. Without this, every future incident will be a scramble — and the next one may not stop at disruption.
