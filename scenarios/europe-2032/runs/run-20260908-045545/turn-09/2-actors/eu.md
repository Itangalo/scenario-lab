## In practice
We are closing out the Sovereign Compute Access Framework under emergency conditions triggered by the Taiwan Strait blockade and the ransomware wave. The Gigafactories in Marseille, Vilnius, and Gdansk are now legally designated critical infrastructure under Article 122 TFEU, and their allocation protocols have shifted: national reservations below 30% of compute capacity require only host-state sign-off; above that threshold, Commission co-signature is mandatory for any allocation. This dual-key system has held through early resistance from Poland and Hungary, who view it as an overreach, but emergency powers under the Treaty have allowed implementation without unanimity. We are treating this not as a temporary override but as proof-of-concept for permanent crisis-capable governance: sovereignty is not just about owning chips, but about being able to run what matters when the world shuts down.

At the same time, the ransomware attack has exposed how thin our operational resilience remains despite formal completion of the Incident Response Corps and the Evaluation Mandate. The Corps was activated within hours but delayed by jurisdictional friction and lack of secure compute relays—meaning no encrypted coordination channel, no trusted execution environment for counter-AI analysis, and reliance on commercial cloud backbones that were themselves degraded. ENISA’s preliminary report confirms the attack used model-generated payloads adapting in real time to patch attempts. Our own defensive tools, where they worked, relied on AI-driven anomaly detection trained locally—but ran at half speed due to contested access to sovereign GPUs. We are now compiling a lessons-learned dossier across DG CONNECT, the JRC, and Frontex (which faced parallel cyber intrusions), to inform next turn’s decisions on whether to harden, expand, or restructure compute sovereignty into a fully autonomous operational layer. Until then, we hold the line: the Framework finishes, but its successor must begin not from ambition, but from the wreckage of what failed to respond.
