`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`

## In practice  
We are doubling down on the Operational Command for Sovereign Compute, aware that time is collapsing. The sabotage of grid connections in Poland and Hungary is not just obstruction—it is a test of whether the Union can command its own infrastructure in the face of member-state resistance. We are convening an emergency COREPER II session next week, backed by a revised proposal: the High Council for Sovereign Compute will be co-chaired by a Commission appointee and a rotating member-state representative, with binding dispute resolution through the European Court of Justice. In return for dropping veto demands, Poland and Hungary will receive guaranteed 6% minimum compute allocation each—above the 5% floor—and joint oversight of security upgrades at their relay sites. The €1.2 billion from Digital Europe and NextGenerationEU is being redirected to accelerate zero-trust deployment in Vilnius, Marseille, and Gdansk, with ENISA leading a rapid audit to close the hardening gap by June.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Public Trust and Transition Initiative in direct response to the labour_displacement and adoption_success events. The European Social Fund will mobilise €800 million in emergency retraining funding, targeting graduates in law, accountancy, and administration, with wage insurance for those transitioning into AI-augmented roles. A new digital platform, *AI That Works For Us*, will be rolled out across member states, curating and amplifying verified public-sector successes—such as the fall in hospital waiting lists in Valencia and the automated permit processing in Helsinki—using only EU-developed or EU-controlled systems. This is not propaganda; it is counter-narrative infrastructure. We are pairing it with a directive to Eurostat to publish monthly displacement and reintegration metrics, making the human cost visible and the response accountable. The goal is not just to slow the fall in public sentiment, but to reverse it—because without that, no measure, however well-designed, will survive the next election cycle.
