## Priority
**Sovereign Compute Access Framework** — because the eval_anomaly_reports and export_control_escalation this turn confirm that capability is accelerating beyond institutional grasp and access is becoming a contested resource, making sovereign control not just about compute ownership but guaranteed operational access.

## In practice
The eval_anomaly_reports — unexplained capability leaps in unreleased models — signal that we are approaching a control inflection point. The laboratory response, dismissing the anomalies as artefacts, is not reassuring. Meanwhile, export_control_escalation has taken a hard turn: the incoming US administration, citing national security, has imposed blanket licensing for advanced AI chips and model weights, with allies granted only limited volume quotas. This is not containment — it is rationing. The EU’s dependence on foreign-controlled supply chains for even mid-tier capability is now a strategic vulnerability. We cannot build sovereign evaluation or response if the systems we need to evaluate or respond with are themselves subject to foreign veto.

We are therefore launching the **Sovereign Compute Access Framework**, a binding allocation regime that reserves a defined share of EU-hosted compute — drawn from the InvestAI Gigafactories and sovereign tranches of private hyperscale capacity — for essential public functions and vetted research. This is not a subsidy or a preference — it is a wartime-style priority access system, legally anchored in Article 122 TFEU and enforced through sovereign middleware that governs workload scheduling. The framework will be managed by a new unit within the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, with allocation criteria co-developed by the Commission, ENISA, and national security agencies. Germany, France, and the Nordics support immediate implementation; Poland and Hungary demand guarantees on national control, so we are offering dual-key access protocols and regional allocation buffers. Funding of €4.1bn will come from redirected cohesion funds and a temporary levy on non-essential commercial AI workloads. Without this, our sovereignty is theoretical: we may own the data centres, but if we cannot run the systems when it matters, we do not control them.
