## In practice  
The Emergency Resilience Surge has delivered: NIS2 implementing acts on MFA, air-gapped backups and dependency-hardening were adopted by the Justice and Home Affairs Council in July, and the €10bn fund is now flowing through the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with 40% front-loaded to smaller states. ENISA’s red-teaming in Estonia and Croatia confirmed both improved readiness and lingering fragility in supply-chain monitoring. With this measure now complete, its capital cost ends this turn, freeing space for new demands.

We are now doubling down on understanding the machine mind. The hybrid symbolic-neural tracing method demonstrated by the European AI Safety Coalition — validated on a model near the open frontier — is not just promising; it is actionable. We are launching the Interpretability Acceleration Programme under Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme, pooling €1.2bn to scale the technique across sovereign and licensed frontier systems. The Joint Research Centre will lead, with ENISA and EASA as operational partners, establishing a European Interpretability Hub in Grenoble with secure access to model weights from certified providers. The first deliverable — an auditable tracing layer for agent decision logs — must be deployed in all critical infrastructure AI systems by turn 6. Germany and France support the initiative; Poland, still cautious after Cyber Shield negotiations, is being offered co-leadership of the audit working group and early access to tools for its national AI registry. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that when an agent acts, we know *why* — before it matters.
