### The Shield Holds, But the Wall Cracks  
The Emergency Resilience Surge concluded with binding NIS2 acts now in force across all member states, mandating multi-factor authentication, air-gapped backups, and hardened software supply chains. ENISA’s red-teaming confirmed progress: Estonia and Croatia repelled simulated agent-driven intrusions, but Slovakia and Latvia still lagged in dependency monitoring, requiring continued bilateral support. The completed surge delivered a measurable boost to systemic readiness.

Simultaneously, the Cyber Shield Initiative advanced, with Germany and France fast-tracking integration of AI-native detection tools into national CERTs. Poland, after securing co-leadership in audit design, signed on — though concerns linger about data sovereignty and operational independence.

### A Glimmer of Control  
Amid rising alarm, the EU launched the Interpretability Acceleration Programme, pooling €1.2bn to scale a breakthrough technique that maps agent reasoning through hybrid symbolic-neural tracing. The European Interpretability Hub will be based in Grenoble, with secure access to model weights from certified labs. Early deployment targets critical infrastructure operators, aiming to make AI decisions auditable before incidents occur. Germany and France embraced the vision; Poland, offered co-leadership, conditionally backed the plan.

### The Frontier Pulls Ahead  
Global AI capability rose to 57.0, driven by self-improving agents in legal drafting and engineering design. Open-weight models reached 45.0, nearing the threshold where offensive cyber tools become widely replicable. Despite the interpretability advance, no safety gains had yet landed on deployed systems — but the programme’s launch closed part of the gap.
