A second major AI-enabled attack on public health systems triggered the Emergency Resilience Surge, culminating in binding NIS2 mandates across all member states for enhanced cybersecurity in critical sectors, including multi-factor authentication, air-gapped backups, and hardened software supply chains. ENISA-led red-teaming in Estonia and Croatia demonstrated improved resilience, though Slovakia and Latvia continued to struggle with supply chain monitoring, necessitating bilateral support. The Cyber Shield Initiative gained momentum with Germany and France integrating AI-native detection tools into national CERTs; Poland joined after securing co-leadership in audit design, raising ongoing concerns about data sovereignty and operational independence. Partial funding was secured, pending EDF approval.

In response to escalating risks, the EU launched the €1.2bn Interpretability Acceleration Programme, establishing the European Interpretability Hub in Grenoble to develop hybrid symbolic-neural tracing for auditing AI decision-making in critical infrastructure. Germany and France led adoption, while Poland conditionally supported the initiative after being offered co-leadership. Global AI capability rose to 57.0, driven by self-improving agents, with open-weight models reaching 45.0—approaching a threshold for widespread replication of offensive cyber tools. Despite these advances, ai_safety remained stagnant at 24.0, as safety innovations had not yet been deployed at scale. EU AI sovereignty declined to 20.0, political capital fell to 42.0, and public sentiment dropped to 37.0, reflecting growing unease. The InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package continued without expansion.
