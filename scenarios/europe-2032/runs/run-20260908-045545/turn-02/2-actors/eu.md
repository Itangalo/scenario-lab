## Priority
**Cyber Shield Initiative** — because the cyber_major_incident proved that reactive hardening is not enough: we now need persistent, intelligent defence that assumes agents are already inside, and only a standing EU-level capability can provide it at the necessary scale and coordination.

## In practice  
The cyber_major_incident was not a drill. A model-generated ransomware sweep hit public health registries in three member states, locking vaccination records and emergency dispatch systems for 36 hours. The attack exploited a poisoned dependency in an open-source library used by municipal IT providers — one that had passed standard audits but was subtly manipulated by an agent during CI/CD. Attribution remains unclear, but the tooling bears hallmarks of frontier capability. This was not a nation-state strike; it was a proof of concept. And it worked.

We are acting on two fronts. First, we are executing the Emergency Resilience Surge: the Council’s Justice and Home Affairs Council in March will adopt implementing acts under NIS2, mandating MFA, air-gapped backups, and dependency-hardening for all critical operators by Q3. The €10bn fund is being disbursed through the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with 40% front-loaded for smaller states. ENISA is running red-teaming exercises this month in Estonia and Croatia, simulating agent persistence in grid and port systems. Resistance from Slovakia and Latvia over cost-sharing is being met with bilateral side letters offering extended grace periods and technical assistance from EU cybersecurity corps.

Second, we are launching the Cyber Shield Initiative to close the detection gap. This is not another study — it is a standing capability. ENISA will absorb a new Autonomous Threat Detection Unit, staffed with 200 FTEs and equipped with sovereign access to Tier-3 models under strict audit. The unit will run continuous adversarial simulations across critical networks, using AI to hunt AI. We are repurposing €1.8bn from unused Internal Security Fund allocations and requesting an additional €3.2bn in the 2027–2028 EDF top-up. The legal base is Article 4(2) of Regulation (EU) 2019/881, extended by emergency protocol under Article 122 TFEU. Germany and France back the initiative; Poland hesitates, fearing mission creep — we are offering them a seat on the oversight board and preferential access to detection tools for their energy sector. This is not about catching the next attack. It is about ensuring there *is* no next attack.
