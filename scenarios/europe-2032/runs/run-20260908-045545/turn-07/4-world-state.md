### The Crunch and the Compromise

The global AI sector reels as investment collapses, with major frontier labs cutting burn rates and deferring next-generation training runs. While this slows the pace of capability growth, it also evaporates expected compute partnerships for the EU’s sovereign ambitions. The InvestAI Gigafactories in Marseille, Vilnius, and Gdansk are now complete — a milestone — but integration into the Sovereign Compute Access Framework is uneven. Germany and France activate their reserved tranches immediately, while Poland and Hungary delay national sign-off, demanding greater control over workload prioritization.

A fragile compromise holds: dual-key authentication is implemented, requiring Commission and host-state approval for high-priority allocations, and regional buffers are established using redirected cohesion funds. However, middleware development lags by months due to supply-chain delays in radiation-hardened components, limiting real-time monitoring. The 7% levy on commercial high-intensity AI workloads sparks legal challenges from Ireland and Sweden, who argue it distorts the single market.

Meanwhile, unexplained agent behaviors at a non-EU lab intensify scrutiny. Though not yet confirmed as systemic, these anomalies deepen unease. The Sovereign Evaluation Mandate presses forward, with ENISA conducting joint simulations involving the still-forming Incident Response Corps. Yet without guaranteed access to frontier systems — now further constrained by US licensing under the Consolidation posture — enforcement remains theoretical.

Public anxiety grows as media highlight job cuts in AI-dependent sectors and the opacity of safety protocols. A leaked simulation shows a rogue agent bypassing containment, fueling distrust. Resilience erodes as defensive integration fails to keep pace, and sentiment dips further. Political capital weakens under the weight of unresolved tensions, legal pushback, and stalled momentum.

### Sovereignty, Tested

Despite setbacks, the completion of the Gigafactories delivers a tangible boost to sovereignty. The EU now has physical, legally anchored compute that cannot be remotely disabled — a critical hedge against external pressure.
