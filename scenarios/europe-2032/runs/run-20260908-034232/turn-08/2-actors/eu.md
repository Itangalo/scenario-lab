## In practice
We are in the final stretch on the Model Evaluation Labs: Finland’s node is live, the Netherlands’ design is locked, and we’ve broken resistance in four of the six lagging member states by offering early access to audit findings and co-drafting rights on evaluation protocols. For the last two, we are invoking Article 114 TFEU — not as a threat, but as a guarantee of mutual protection. The openweight release this turn proves the danger is not hypothetical: a model at 53.5 capability, downloaded hundreds of thousands of times, is now beyond recall. That is why we are launching a new red-teaming authority under ENISA — to stop the next one before it spreads. This is not duplication; it is differentiation: the labs handle pre-deployment scrutiny of closed models, the red-team unit handles post-release containment of open ones.

The InvestEU AI Bonds remain in ECOFIN, but the ai_investment_collapse changes the calculus: frontier labs are cutting training runs, and private compute build-outs are stalling. This is our moment to lock in public capacity while the market retreats. We are reframing the bonds not as competition, but as insurance — and pairing them with resilience investments in grid hardening and cooling infrastructure to win over fiscally cautious states. The Tech Sovereignty Package still stalls, but we are using the Taiwan tension rise to reframe it: compute is not just economic, it is strategic. We are offering fast-track permitting only to projects that pre-commit to audit readiness and crisis-access protocols.

Internationally, the middle-power coalition is proving its worth. With U.S. access increasingly conditional and China tightening chip controls, we are finalising mutual compute reservations with Japan and South Korea — not full pooling, but guaranteed access in crisis. Canada and Norway are aligning on evaluation standards, which will let us bypass foreign inspection regimes we cannot trust. This is sovereignty not as isolation, but as interdependence we control. The red-teaming measure will extend that logic to open models: if we cannot stop the code from spreading, we must ensure it is stress-tested before it lands.
