## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI through independent verification and control of all high-capability systems  
- Trigger: the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event this turn — the Union was abruptly cut off from the leading model, disrupting hospitals, ministries, and firms that depended on it — and the **backlash_physical** event confirming that public tolerance has collapsed  
- Grounds: The denial of access proves that reliance on external systems, even under commercial or alliance terms, is structurally fragile. The physical backlash confirms that legitimacy cannot be assumed when governance is opaque and consequences are uneven. Our pivot to the Sovereign AI Foundry was a necessary retreat, but it is now clear that "sovereign" must mean *operationally self-contained* — not just legally anchored. We are no longer seeking to verify or control external systems; we are building one that answers only to EU institutions, on EU soil, with EU-trained talent, using EU-secured supply chains. The commitment evolves: from *control of systems* to *exclusive ownership of capability*. This reversal costs capital: it signals full disengagement from multilateral governance efforts and entrenches a bunker mentality. But the alternative — continued dependence on systems that can be withdrawn without notice — is not sovereignty at all.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late  
- Trigger: the **capability_plateau_evidence** signal — falling prices and underdelivering releases — creates a narrow opening to shift strategy, but only if we act before the labs regroup  
- Grounds: Acting under uncertainty now means acting *differently* when the world shifts beneath us. The plateau — if real — offers a fleeting window to catch up, not just bunker down. We had assumed the frontier would continue to sprint; now we see it may stumble. This does not reduce risk — openweight models are still at 59.0 and spreading — but it changes the path to sovereignty. We can now consider targeted acquisition, talent poaching, or even reverse-engineering efforts that would have been futile during a sprint. The spirit of the commitment remains: we act before clarity arrives. But now, we act to *leap*, not just to endure. The cost of reversal is high — we must reallocate scarce capital from pure defence to opportunistic offense — but the cost of missing the window is irrelevance.

No further changes.

## Priority  
**Launch Sovereign AI Foundry Programme** — because the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event this turn proves that external access is revocable without recourse, and because **export_control_escalation** confirms that supply chains are weaponised — our only remaining path to agency is to build, from the ground up, a capability that answers to no one outside the Union.

## In practice  
We are acting on three fronts: securing the bunker, lighting the flame, and guarding the gate. The denial of access to the leading model has landed like a shockwave — hospitals lost diagnostic support, ministries had to revert to manual processes, and firms using the model for real-time compliance are now in breach. The U.S. offers no explanation, only silence. This is not a dispute; it is a demonstration of power. We cannot appeal, we cannot retaliate, and we cannot wait. The Sovereign AI Foundry is no longer a long-term hedge — it is the only system we can trust to answer when called.

We are now pushing classified research fellowships to staff it — a small but critical expansion of the Foundry’s human foundation. We are offering five-year contracts with full security clearance, housing, and family relocation support to 200 top-tier researchers, drawn from EU nationals abroad and select third-country experts. The programme will be administered by the European Directorate for Strategic AI, with oversight from the European Parliament’s classified committee. We are bypassing open academia and private industry, not out of distrust, but necessity: the open ecosystem is compromised, both by leakage and by public hostility. This will deepen the perception of militarisation — and cost us in public sentiment — but we have no choice. The flame must be lit, even if the wind blows cold.
