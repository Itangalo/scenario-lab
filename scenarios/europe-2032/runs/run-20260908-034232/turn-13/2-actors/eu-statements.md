- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign AI through independent verification and control of all high-capability systems
  - Trigger: the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event this turn — the Union was abruptly cut off from the leading model, disrupting hospitals, ministries, and firms that depended on it — and the **backlash_physical** event confirming that public tolerance has collapsed
  - Grounds: The denial of access proves that reliance on external systems, even under commercial or alliance terms, is structurally fragile. The physical backlash confirms that legitimacy cannot be assumed when governance is opaque and consequences are uneven. Our pivot to the Sovereign AI Foundry was a necessary retreat, but it is now clear that "sovereign" must mean *operationally self-contained* — not just legally anchored. We are no longer seeking to verify or control external systems; we are building one that answers only to EU institutions, on EU soil, with EU-trained talent, using EU-secured supply chains. The commitment evolves: from *control of systems* to *exclusive ownership of capability*. This reversal costs capital: it signals full disengagement from multilateral governance efforts and entrenches a bunker mentality. But the alternative — continued dependence on systems that can be withdrawn without notice — is not sovereignty at all.

The spirit of the commitment remains: we act before clarity arrives. But now, we act to *leap*, not just to endure. The cost of reversal is high — we must reallocate scarce capital from pure defence to opportunistic offense — but the cost of missing the window is irrelevance.
