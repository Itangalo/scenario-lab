## New measure
**Launch Openweight Security Audit Programme**

A new programme to assess and secure the most capable openly released models, ensuring they do not become uncontrolled vectors of harm as they approach frontier performance.  
`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +8–12, openweight_capability -5 (via mitigation of misuse), resilience +3–5  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member states, and developers of open-weight models above 45.0 capability

## Priority
**Establish EU-Controlled Model Evaluation Labs** — because the gap between closed and open capability is closing fast, and without sovereign capacity to inspect, test, and if necessary restrict models before deployment, every other measure risks being bypassed by what is already loose.

## In practice
We are finalising the legal architecture for the EU-Controlled Model Evaluation Labs under Article 114 TFEU, with DG CONNECT and the Joint Research Centre co-designing the first operational node in Finland. The Netherlands will host a second site focused on cyber-resilience testing, with CNA and TNO leading technical integration. We are offering member states a seat on the oversight board in exchange for early ratification of the access mandate, and we are fast-tracking security clearances for EU-contracted researchers. This is not optional: any model above 55.0 capability deployed in the EU must submit weights and training data for inspection, or face market exclusion. The U.S. election and the bilateral deal with the hyperscaler prove that access will be weaponised — we must not be left without eyes.

The InvestEU AI Bonds remain in legal review, but we are now advancing a pilot tranche under the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s digital window, using cohesion funds as collateral. This allows us to begin construction on two Gigafactory sites — Bavaria and Wallonia — before the full bond programme clears ECOFIN. We are offering regional governments co-drafting rights on allocation rules and guaranteed grid priority only if they meet ENISA audit readiness by Q1 2029. This turns resistance into ownership. The Tech Sovereignty Package is nearing completion, with the Commission finalising the list of accelerated-permitting zones — but we are conditioning access on public oversight integration, not just speed.

Meanwhile, the AI Incident Reporting Regime is now law: COREPER has adopted the directive, and ENISA is deploying the dashboard ahead of the first audit into the benefits scandal. We are using that incident to stress-test the system, feeding findings directly into the evaluation labs’ test suite. But we see a new danger: the open-weight release this turn reaches 50.0 capability — enough to run autonomous agents with real-world impact — and it is already being fine-tuned in unregulated environments. So we are launching a parallel audit programme under the same legal framework, targeting open models above 45.0. This is not about blocking innovation — it is about ensuring that what is open does not become what is weaponised. We are tasking ENISA with running red-team evaluations, and we are offering open developers a fast-track compliance path in exchange for early cooperation. Without this, sovereignty ends at the firewall.
