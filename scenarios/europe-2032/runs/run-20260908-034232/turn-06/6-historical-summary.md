By late 2027, AI capability reached 62.5 as frontier labs deployed advanced autonomous agents in legal and clinical domains, while open-weight models demonstrated self-improvement via autonomous fine-tuning, lifting their estimated performance to 52.5—narrowing the gap but falling short of full parity. The EU responded by launching the Openweight Security Audit Programme under Article 114 TFEU, mandating red-team evaluations for models above 45.0 capability, with ENISA leading audits and offering compliance incentives. Initial developer engagement was mixed, with smaller groups cooperating and larger projects resisting over transparency concerns, while member state buy-in remained pending in six capitals.

To advance infrastructure sovereignty, the EU began construction on two InvestAI Gigafactories in Bavaria and Wallonia using Recovery and Resilience Facility funds, bypassing stalled InvestEU AI Bonds. However, progress was tied to upcoming ENISA cybersecurity benchmarks, creating tension with unprepared regional authorities. The AI Incident Reporting Regime officially launched, with ENISA releasing its first public report focused on the benefits scandal, informing test protocols for the still-developing EU-Controlled Model Evaluation Labs now in co-design phase in Finland and the Netherlands—though no sovereign audit capacity became operational.

Amid overlapping initiatives and strained coordination, the EU’s implementation bandwidth saturated, slowing progress on the Tech Sovereignty Package and other priorities despite formal continuity. Political capital declined to 18.0 due to strategic anxieties amplified by U.S. export restrictions and internal fragmentation from bilateral deals, while public trust stagnated at 32.0. Resilience and safety remained under pressure, but institutional stability persisted as the bloc prioritized long-term autonomy amid rising deployment risks.
