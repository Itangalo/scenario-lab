By late 2027, global AI capability reached 65.0 as frontier labs resumed limited, efficiency-focused training following the investment downturn, while open-weight capability stagnated at 54.0 due to fragmentation, poor coordination, and funding constraints despite widespread deployment of existing models on consumer hardware. The EU completed key initiatives: the InvestAI Gigafactories became operational in Bavaria and Wallonia, establishing the bloc’s first sovereign compute infrastructure, and the AI Incident Reporting Regime demonstrated functionality, though underreporting persisted. The Openweight Security Audit Programme concluded its pilot, revealing enforcement weaknesses after red-teaming two high-capability open models. A proposed Sovereign Red-Teaming Authority gained political support but stalled due to legal disputes over enforcement and member state opt-outs, remaining unimplemented. Political capital declined under pressure from public protests against AI infrastructure, job displacement fears, and gridlock over the InvestEU AI Bonds and Tech Sovereignty Package, with public sentiment falling to 27.0. Diplomatic coordination with Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Norway advanced on compute access and evaluation standards, contributing to a modest rise in EU AI sovereignty to 21.0, though gains remained fragile amid external dependencies and internal unrest.
