### Frontier Creeps Forward, Openweights Stall on Fragmentation  
Global AI capability rises to 65.0, as frontier labs resume limited training following the investment downturn, focusing on efficiency over scale. The open ecosystem shows increased adoption of existing models, with a widely distributed model at 53.5 capability now running across consumer-grade hardware. However, no significant technical advancement occurs: fragmented tooling, lack of coordination, and insufficient funding prevent meaningful progress in openweight capability, which remains at 54.0. The gap with frontier systems persists, sustained by structural disadvantages in compute and talent.

### EU Completes Key Measures Amid Mounting Pressure  
The **InvestAI Gigafactories** initiative formally completes, with construction launched in Bavaria and Wallonia, marking the EU’s first sovereign compute foothold. The **AI Incident Reporting Regime** proves functional, with ENISA’s first report highlighting underreporting in weaker digital administrations. The **Openweight Security Audit Programme** concludes its pilot, red-teaming two high-capability open models—one cooperative, one resistant—exposing enforcement fragility.

### Red-Teaming Authority Delayed by Legal Disputes  
Proposals for a **Sovereign Red-Teaming Authority** under ENISA gain political traction, aiming to compel disclosure and block non-compliant releases of models above 50.0 capability. Framed as containment, not censorship, the measure is added to the portfolio. However, legal debates over enforcement scope and member state opt-outs prevent final approval. Implementation cannot begin until consensus is reached, and no progress is made this turn. The EU remains at capacity with its existing priorities, and the new proposal remains in limbo.

### Political Capital Erodes Amid Backlash and Gridlock  
Physical protests against AI infrastructure escalate across several member states, disrupting grid connections and straining public order. The backlash, combined with stalled **InvestEU AI Bonds** and unresolved disputes over the **Tech Sovereignty Package**, drains political capital. The **Model Evaluation Labs** push forward, but at high cost. Public sentiment dips to 27.0, driven by job displacement fears and distrust in AI governance.

### Coalition Diplomacy Advances, But Sovereignty Gains Are Fragile  
Negotiations with Japan and South Korea on mutual compute access deepen, framed as crisis contingency. Canada and Norway align on evaluation standards, but Singapore hesitates, citing U.S. ties. Sovereignty rises modestly to 21.0, buoyed by Gigafactories’ completion and diplomatic gains, but remains vulnerable to external access restrictions.

Tensions grow across multiple fronts, with unrest in labor sectors and quiet fractures emerging in open-source governance, though no decisive rupture has yet occurred.
