## In practice
We are closing the first phase of the Incident Reporting Regime under Article 16 TFEU, with COREPER now finalising the draft directive. The legal basis holds, but resistance remains in several capitals over scope and enforcement. To break the logjam, we are offering member states a dual-track implementation: full compliance by Q2 2029 for high-risk operators, with phased reporting for mid-tier systems, and we are assigning ENISA to build the technical backbone of the dashboard in parallel with adoption. The first audit — on the benefits scandal chain — is underway, and its findings will shape the regime’s mandatory thresholds. This is not just transparency for its own sake: we are designing the system to feed directly into our new evaluation labs, turning incident data into test cases.

On sovereignty, the Bonds remain in legal review, but we are shifting strategy: rather than wait for ECOFIN’s full endorsement, we are preparing a pilot tranche under the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s digital window, using cohesion funds as collateral. Bavaria and Wallonia have been offered not just board seats but co-drafting rights on the allocation mechanism, turning resistance into co-authorship. The Gigafactories continue under revised permit conditions: each site must now demonstrate ENISA audit readiness and public oversight integration by Q1 2029, or lose access to grid priority. This is not just about building compute — it is about ensuring that what is built answers to EU law.

And now, we act on the lesson of the openweight release: capability is no longer something that can be contained by borders or contracts. We are launching the EU Model Evaluation Labs — a sovereign technical capability to test, understand, and if necessary block, frontier systems before deployment. The legal basis will be Article 114 TFEU, with operational delivery split between a new unit in DG CONNECT and a re-tasked arm of the Joint Research Centre. We will require weight-sharing with these labs for any model above 55.0 capability deployed in the EU, under strict liability and data protection safeguards. This is not a request: it is a condition of market access. We are already in talks with the Netherlands and Finland to host the first nodes, with physical security at a level comparable to central bank vaults. Without this, every other sovereignty measure is just scaffolding around empty space.
