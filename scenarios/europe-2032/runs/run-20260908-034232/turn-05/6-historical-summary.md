By mid-2027, AI capability rose to 61.5 as frontier labs enhanced autonomous agents across professional domains, while open-weight models narrowed the performance gap but lagged due to compute and fine-tuning constraints. A major EU member state struck a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler for preferential model access, undermining EU unity and signaling growing fragmentation in AI sovereignty. The U.S. election ushered in an administration poised to treat frontier AI as critical infrastructure, restricting access by geopolitical alignment and deepening European dependency.

In response, the EU launched the EU-Controlled Model Evaluation Labs under Article 114 TFEU as a priority initiative to build sovereign auditing capacity, though implementation remains months away and faces developer and member state skepticism. The AI Incident Reporting Regime advanced to final draft stage, with ENISA designing the technical dashboard and planning audits, but remains non-operational. 

Despite mounting pressures—including stalled InvestEU AI Bonds, eroding safety at 17.0, and rising cyber threats—political capital declined by 3 points to 21.0 without triggering collapse. Resilience dipped slightly to 31.0, and public trust remained low amid governance delays, but institutional stability held as the Union pursued long-term technical independence.
