## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign AI through independent verification and control of all high-capability systems
  - Trigger: the Pre-Emptive Intervention Authority failing to gain ratification traction despite emergency invocation, and openweight_capability now at 56.0 with no mechanism to recall or restrict deployed models
  - Grounds: Verification is meaningless without enforcement; control is illusory without reach. We have built evaluation labs but cannot act on their findings. The U.S. export controls under CONSOLIDATION have severed access to frontier weights, while open models—now functionally equivalent to what was once cutting-edge in closed labs—are spreading uncontrolled. Our sovereignty is not just weak—it is structurally inverted: we inspect what we cannot stop, and depend on what we do not own. The commitment must shift from *control of systems* to *sovereign capacity to act*: we will no longer rely on external compliance or goodwill. From this turn forward, the aim is not to govern distributed models—but to outpace them with EU-controlled, secure, and operationally autonomous AI capability. This reversal costs capital: it abandons a regulatory dream for an industrial one, and signals retreat from global governance leadership. But survival comes first.

  - Tier: commitment

  - Trigger: two major incidents enabled by open-weight models, yet our pre-emptive authority remains unenforced due to legal resistance and member-state fragmentation
  - Grounds: Acting under uncertainty has become indistinguishable from acting alone. We committed early, yes—but the institutions that must carry those commitments forward are not moving. Emergency powers were invoked, but COREPER deadlocked, ECOFIN refused fast-track, and the Court of Justice signaled skepticism over extraterritorial enforcement. The cost of action is no longer just political—it is constitutional. We will still move before clarity arrives, but now we must also build the instruments that make movement possible. The spirit of the commitment evolves: we will not just act before the picture is clear—we will ensure the Union can act at all. That means grounding decisions in deployable infrastructure, not just directives. The price of being late is catastrophe; the price of acting too soon without power is irrelevance.
