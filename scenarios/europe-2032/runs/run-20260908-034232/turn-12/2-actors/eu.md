## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI through independent verification and control of all high-capability systems  
- Trigger: the Pre-Emptive Intervention Authority failing to gain ratification traction despite emergency invocation, and openweight_capability now at 56.0 with no mechanism to recall or restrict deployed models  
- Grounds: Verification is meaningless without enforcement; control is illusory without reach. We have built evaluation labs but cannot act on their findings. The U.S. export controls under CONSOLIDATION have severed access to frontier weights, while open models—now functionally equivalent to what was once cutting-edge in closed labs—are spreading uncontrolled. Our sovereignty is not just weak—it is structurally inverted: we inspect what we cannot stop, and depend on what we do not own. The commitment must shift from *control of systems* to *sovereign capacity to act*: we will no longer rely on external compliance or goodwill. From this turn forward, the aim is not to govern distributed models—but to outpace them with EU-controlled, secure, and operationally autonomous AI capability. This reversal costs capital: it abandons a regulatory dream for an industrial one, and signals retreat from global governance leadership. But survival comes first.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late  
- Trigger: two major incidents enabled by open-weight models, yet our pre-emptive authority remains unenforced due to legal resistance and member-state fragmentation  
- Grounds: Acting under uncertainty has become indistinguishable from acting alone. We committed early, yes—but the institutions that must carry those commitments forward are not moving. Emergency powers were invoked, but COREPER deadlocked, ECOFIN refused fast-track, and the Court of Justice signaled skepticism over extraterritorial enforcement. The cost of action is no longer just political—it is constitutional. We will still move before clarity arrives, but now we must also build the instruments that make movement possible. The spirit of the commitment evolves: we will not just act before the picture is clear—we will ensure the Union can act at all. That means grounding decisions in deployable infrastructure, not just directives. The price of being late is catastrophe; the price of acting too soon without power is irrelevance.

## New measure  
**Launch Sovereign AI Foundry Programme**  
To establish a fully EU-owned, end-to-end frontier AI development and deployment capability, insulated from foreign supply chains and accessible only through secure government channels.  
`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 18`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +25 (by creating independent EU-controlled frontier capability), ai_safety +10 (via closed, audited development environment), resilience +5 (through hardened public-sector AI applications)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, selected member-state agencies, and contracted research consortia operating under classified conditions

## Priority  
**Launch Sovereign AI Foundry Programme** — because the failure of the Pre-Emptive Intervention Authority reveals that we cannot regulate our way to safety when the capability is already distributed, and because U.S. export controls mean we cannot depend on external access—our only path to agency is to build our own.

## In practice  
We are shifting from governance to founding. The dream of controlling open models died in COREPER this quarter. Despite two catastrophic incidents, despite invoking emergency powers, we could not secure consensus to act on systems we do not own. The U.S., under CONSOLIDATION, has made clear: their frontier is not ours. And the open frontier—now at 56.0—is everywhere. So we turn inward. The Sovereign AI Foundry Programme will be launched under Article 172 TFEU, framed as a strategic research and innovation priority, and funded through a reallocated tranche of the InvestEU AI Bonds—diverted from general infrastructure to a singular, classified effort. It will sit within a newly established European Directorate for Strategic AI, hosted jointly by DG CONNECT and the European Defence Agency, with physical nodes in France, Germany, and Finland leveraging the existing Gigafactory sites.

We are negotiating with ASML, STMicroelectronics, and Thales to reconfigure chip supply lines for non-commercial AI accelerators, bypassing U.S.-controlled tooling where possible. Talent recruitment will be fast-tracked through Schengen Blue Card waivers and security-clearance streamlining. Access to the Foundry’s outputs will be restricted to EU institutions and vetted national agencies working on critical continuity: cyber defence, bio-surveillance, energy grid stability. This is not about competing with the global frontier—we cannot win that race. It is about ensuring that when the next incident hits, the EU has one system it can trust, that answers to no foreign power, and that operates beyond the reach of open distribution. We are no longer trying to stop the fire. We are building a bunker—and lighting our own flame inside.
