By 2032, the EU’s credibility had eroded under the strain of technological upheaval and institutional paralysis. Despite completing the InvestAI Gigafactories, chronic underfunding persisted due to the blocked InvestEU AI Bonds, while the Pre-Emptive Intervention Authority collapsed under legal and political resistance. In response, the Commission launched the classified Sovereign AI Foundry Programme under Article 172 TFEU—a closed, end-to-end AI development initiative led by DG CONNECT and the European Defence Agency, with nodes in France, Germany, and Finland—marking a strategic retreat from open governance toward tightly controlled self-reliance.

The move came at a steep cost: it consumed dwindling political capital, deepened public distrust amid fears of militarized AI, and triggered sharper U.S. export restrictions under CONSOLIDATION, cutting off access to advanced chips and updated model weights. Public sentiment remained low, fueled by job losses, bio-lab incidents, and protests against opaque governance. As global ai_capability approached 59.0 and openweight_capability surged, the EU’s ability to regulate or even keep pace deteriorated. The Foundry offered a vision of autonomy, but by 2032, it remained incomplete—a fragile refuge in the face of accelerating global AI advancement.
