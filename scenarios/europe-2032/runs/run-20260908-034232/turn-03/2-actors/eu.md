## In practice

We are acting on two fronts: one forward, one corrective. On the sovereignty front, we continue to defend the InvestEU AI Bonds proposal as strategic infrastructure, pressing ECOFIN for endorsement in October and preparing legal arguments under Article 122 TFEU by analogy to SURE and the Recovery Instrument. We are offering Bavaria and Wallonia a 15% cohesion top-up and priority access to bond-financed projects in exchange for dropping their legal challenges — but we are no longer treating sovereignty as a purely technical project. The Gigafactories continue, but now with embedded audit rights for ENISA and a new mandate: no site receives final approval without a public oversight framework in place.

At the same time, we are pivoting hard toward accountability. The social benefits scandal cannot be answered by apology alone. We are launching an independent ombudsman for public-sector AI, hosted jointly by the European Data Protection Board and the Fundamental Rights Agency, with powers to compel data access, suspend deployments, and recommend compensation. The first case will be the benefits system in question, but the mandate is permanent. We are funding it from a reallocation of 10% of the Tech sovereignty package’s conditionality enforcement budget — a signal that sovereignty includes not just control over hardware, but over outcomes. This is not a retreat from capability; it is a redefinition of what control means. We are building oversight into the foundation, because the next jump in capability will not wait for us to catch up.
