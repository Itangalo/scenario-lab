## In practice

We are consolidating our corrective front: the ombudsman initiative launched last turn is now operational in drafting mode, hosted jointly by the EDPB and FRA, with a secretariat standing up in Brussels by Q3. We are insisting that ENISA conduct a parallel audit of the benefits scandal’s technical chain, not just its outcomes, to ground the ombudsman’s first mandate in forensic clarity. That work feeds directly into this turn’s new measure — the Incident Reporting Regime — which we are designing as a legal instrument under Article 16 TFEU, to be adopted by COREPER and the Justice and Home Affairs Council. The regime will require operators of critical AI systems to report anomalies, failures, and misuse attempts within 72 hours, with a public dashboard publishing non-sensitive summaries. We are exempting small providers and research entities to preserve innovation space, but not hyperscalers or public agencies — no more opacity behind public-private partnerships.

We are also defending the sovereignty portfolio, though it is under strain. The InvestEU AI Bonds remain in legal limbo, but we are preparing a revised proposal for October’s ECOFIN, backed by a legal opinion from AG Campos Sánchez-Bordona arguing that Article 122 TFEU *can* justify strategic investment in digital sovereignty, by analogy to pandemic response — not a perfect fit, but enough to break the deadlock. We are offering Bavaria and Wallonia not just a 15% cohesion top-up, but a seat on the bond’s oversight board and guaranteed project allocation, to convert resistance into co-ownership. The Gigafactories continue on schedule, but we are tightening their conditionality: final permits now require proof of ENISA audit readiness and public oversight frameworks in place. Sovereignty is not just compute — it is control, and control must be seen to exist.
