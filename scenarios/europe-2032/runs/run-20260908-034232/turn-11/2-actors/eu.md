## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI through independent verification and control of all high-capability systems  
- Trigger: the failure to launch the Sovereign Red-Teaming Authority and the cyber_major_incident involving open-weight model tooling  
- Grounds: The commitment to verification and control remains valid, but its foundation has collapsed. Without legal authority to act on open models, and with a major cyber incident now demonstrating the real-world harm of uncontrolled distributed capability, the gap between inspection and intervention is fatal. We can see the threat, but we cannot stop it. The commitment must now shift from verification as an end to verification as a trigger: the next phase must be operational, not observational. This reversal costs political capital — developers will resist compulsion, member states will balk at enforcement — but inaction costs more.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late  
- Trigger: the bio_incident and the cyber_major_incident occurring in the same turn  
- Grounds: We acted under uncertainty — and were still too late. The picture is no longer unclear; it is catastrophic. Two major incidents, one biological and one cyber, both enabled by unsecured, widely distributed models, have reset the global risk calculus. Waiting for full attribution or consensus on causality is no longer a form of prudence — it is complicity. The commitment stands, but its spirit must harden: we will now act not just before the picture is clear, but before the damage spreads. That means pre-emptive authority, not just retrospective response.

## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: containment and credibility. The cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across public services, powered by AI-generated exploit chains from a 55.0-capability open model — has exposed the fatal gap between knowing and acting. The bio_incident — a modified respiratory agent released from a compromised research lab, its design traced to model-assisted synthesis — has shattered any illusion that open models are only a digital threat. We are no longer facing hypotheticals. We are in crisis.

The Sovereign Red-Teaming Authority failed because we asked for too much, too fast, without the legal spine to back it. This time, we are building narrower, sharper authority: a Pre-Emptive Intervention Authority under the European Commission, empowered by a new Article 114 TFEU directive, that can issue binding orders to modify or remove high-risk open models upon verified risk assessment from the EU-Controlled Model Evaluation Labs. This is not red-teaming — it is enforcement. We are not waiting for consensus. We are invoking emergency procedure under the Lisbon Treaty, backed by the evidence of two simultaneous incidents, to fast-track adoption in COREPER and ECOFIN.

The InvestEU AI Bonds are now framed as crisis infrastructure: we are pairing them with mandatory resilience upgrades for all funded sites, offering fiscally conservative states a trade — sovereignty for security. The Tech Sovereignty Package will be unblocked only if private developers accept audit hooks and crisis-access protocols. And the Middle-Power Coalition is being repositioned: we are offering Japan and South Korea joint authority over intervention decisions, turning sovereignty into shared responsibility. This is not the Union acting alone — it is the Union acting first, so others can follow.
