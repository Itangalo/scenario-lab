### Crisis and Authority in the Shadow of Catastrophe

Two major incidents in quick succession—widespread ransomware crippling public services and a bioengineered agent released from a compromised lab—shook global confidence. Both were enabled by AI tools derived from open-weight models at 55.0 capability. The EU, already reeling from institutional paralysis, responded with a bold but divisive move: the proposal of a Pre-Emptive Intervention Authority, aimed at granting the Commission power to suspend or modify high-risk open models.

Despite invoking emergency procedures and framing the measure as essential for survival, resistance was immediate. Legal scholars questioned extraterritorial reach, developers decried censorship, and several member states balked at enforcement overreach. The measure entered the portfolio, but momentum stalled—no ratification progress was made, and no pull-in of the finish date occurred.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** formally completed construction in Bavaria and Wallonia, marking a symbolic win. However, without the **InvestEU AI Bonds** approved, long-term funding remained uncertain. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** stayed blocked, its passage still tied to unresolved audit and resilience conditions.

Public sentiment worsened sharply. Media coverage focused on chaos, job losses, and perceived helplessness. Protests grew, now including scientists and healthcare workers fearing AI-driven lab accidents. Trust in EU leadership eroded further.

Meanwhile, under the **CONSOLIDATION** posture, the U.S. tightened export controls further, limiting EU access to updated model weights and advanced chips—another blow to sovereignty. The Middle-Power Coalition negotiations made no headway, with allies waiting to see if the EU could act coherently before committing.

### Fractured Response, Fading Leverage

The EU’s attempt to assert control over open models exposed its limits: it could declare intent, but not compel compliance. The world moved faster than its institutions could adapt.
