## Priority
InvestEU AI Bonds — because the capability_jump at 57.0 and the failure of detection mechanisms demand immediate, irreversible investment in EU-controlled infrastructure, and because without sovereign access to compute, all future resilience, safety, and adoption depend on permission we may not be granted.

## In practice
We are pressing forward on the legal and financial foundations of the InvestEU AI Bonds, treating AI compute as critical infrastructure under Article 122 TFEU. The Commission has drafted the proposal and will present it to ECOFIN in October, arguing by analogy to SURE and the Recovery Instrument: when strategic interdependence becomes fragility, the Union must act as one. Legal services are preparing for a potential CJEU referral, but we are proceeding on the basis that delay is a greater risk than overreach.

Simultaneously, we are enforcing conditionality on the Tech sovereignty package: access to accelerated-permitting zones now requires 51% EU ownership and full audit rights for ENISA. This is being negotiated bilaterally with Bavaria and Wallonia — we are offering bond priority and a 15% cohesion top-up in exchange for compliance. The message is clear: sovereignty is not optional infrastructure. On the ground, the first tranches of the Gigafactories fund are being redirected to grid and modular nuclear upgrades in Nordic and Iberian clusters, with cyber-hardening protocols from the Agency for Defence Innovation already embedded in site design. We are building not just faster, but harder — because the next jump may come before our defences are ready.
