### The shield holds on paper
Brussels spent the spring trying to make its authorisation demand stick. Trade officials filed notifications, ministers repeated that no servicing withdrawal from chip-making equipment could proceed without Union approval, and diplomats tried to fold talks with Japan and Korea into a single European licensing position to protect The Hague from bilateral pressure.

In practice little moved. Servicing schedules continued to thin, spare parts arrived slower, and maintenance windows stretched. The Dutch government acknowledged the Union line without enforcing it, while suppliers complied quietly with the foreign orders they could not contest. No plant closed, but no capacity returned. The fenced gigafactory stayed fenced.

The pooled cyber command's formal accession gave the Commission something to point to. Municipalities inside the arrangement shared telemetry, kept locked backups and rehearsed cutting cloud links; a spring round of intrusions left them degraded but working. Outside, smaller councils and clinics again struggled to restore systems. The contrast was real, but so was the fatigue: membership had not brought hardware, insurance cover, or new compute.

### Audited assistants
The new diffusion programme landed more visibly. Procured through the AI Office and SME agencies, audited office assistants running on existing European-hosted capacity were offered to small firms, law offices, accountants and town halls, with published logs, redress procedures and co-funding tied to backup discipline.

Uptake was steady where trust already existed — the same clinics and administrations that had used triage tools — and cool elsewhere. Councils that had refused data-centre permits cited the same reasons to decline new deployments, even without new sites required. Business press noted higher output per junior employee and rehiring after early cuts, but voters experienced it as more work per person rather than a public benefit.

By June the Union was less exposed to cascading outages, no less dependent for its machines, and still searching for a benefit the public could feel.
