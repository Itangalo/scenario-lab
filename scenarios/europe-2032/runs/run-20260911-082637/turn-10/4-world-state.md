### The invitation taken, the hardware still missing
Brussels said yes to the pooled cyber command. With domestic chip slots still cancelled and data-centre money gone, the offer of shared attribution and live telemetry was the only protection on the table that did not require building anything. Accession papers moved quickly through foreign affairs and telecom ministers, and ENISA linked its playbooks to the common feed.

On the ground it worked, narrowly. Cooperating clinics and town halls that already kept locked backups and practised cutting cloud links stayed up through a spring wave of intrusions that crippled a few unlinked neighbours. Auditors again credited containment, and the joint feed gave faster warning than national centres alone.

Everything else confirmed dependence. The Hague, Tokyo and Seoul left the joint licensing offer unsigned, still demanding binding controls on lithography before quotas. The fenced gigafactory site stayed idle. Insurers praised the liability draft and still refused to resume civic cover until there was law. Trade officials kept the file alive but spent no leverage pretending silicon would return this half-year.

At home the trust repair crawled. The frozen benefits and policing system stayed frozen, logs were published with a redress window, and only audited triage sites were allowed to expand. Where they ran, waiting lists fell and nurses spoke well of them. Where the scandal had run, press and opposition kept the phrase lawful harm alive, and most councils refused new deployments. The gap-closing act advanced in draft but did not become enforceable, and workload limits were unevenly applied.

Finance did not return. American labs trained more slowly, second-tier builders boasted of catching up, and open models crept closer to the frontier. Systems grew more capable without becoming more legible, and safety teams warned they were falling further behind.

The Union ended June protected by borrowed eyes, still without its own capacity, and still arguing whether its machines could be trusted.
