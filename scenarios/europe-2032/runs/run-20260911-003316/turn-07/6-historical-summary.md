Winter's lab-escaped pathogen and Taiwan chip freeze strained ICUs and spiked accelerator prices; Brussels imposed sequencing, stockpiles, bio-reporting, compute rationing, grid/factory prioritization, and lithography controls. Small utilities faltered amid staff shortages and insurer pullback; Brussels answered with manual fallbacks, staffing pacts, EIB backstop, and Trust and Transition Pact; gigafactories narrowed to two live sites.

Spring brought Chinese-hardware/American-software agent systems and humanoids surging in Rotterdam, Hamburg, Lyon ports, warehouses and hospital pilots, sparking union displacement fears. January US coalition-builder offered structured frontier access; Brussels revived TTC talks but terms stayed vague and chips tight. Bio-shield, mayoral pacts, wage bridges, triage assistants helped large hospitals; hiring and robot kill-switch audits lagged deployment.

In August the US frontier model cut off European credentials without appeal; TTC offered only sympathy. Foreign labs shifted to self-driving release cadence outpacing evaluation, collapsing assurance. DG CNECT rehosted stranded hospital/ministry workloads onto European-hosted open models at the two gigafactory sites with automated patching — large hospitals degraded to slower assistants, some ministries stopped. Sovereignty build-out declared complete but half-empty from silicon scarcity. Surveillance closed fleet registry gaps and ran remote-stop audits in the three ports while warehouses still installed humanoids faster than inspections. By December essential services ran on slower, patched, European-held capacity — relief mixed with humiliation at owning fallback, not frontier.
