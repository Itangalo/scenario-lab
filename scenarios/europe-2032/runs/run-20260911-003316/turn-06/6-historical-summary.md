Winter's lab-escaped pathogen and Taiwan chip freeze strained ICUs, spiked accelerator prices; Brussels imposed sequencing, stockpile releases, bio-reporting, compute rationing, factory/grid prioritization, and lithography controls. Large hospitals/grids held, small utilities faltered amid staff shortages, blind isolations, insurer pullback, including loss of chlorination monitoring and boil notices. Brussels answered with manual fallbacks, staffing pacts, EIB insurer backstop, and Trust and Transition Pact; gigafactories narrowed to two prioritized sites.

Spring brought foreign long-horizon agent systems and humanoid fleets — largely Chinese hardware with American software — surging in Rotterdam, Hamburg, Lyon ports, warehouses and hospital pilots, sparking union fears of second displacement. January US coalition-builder took office offering structured frontier access, joint evaluation, aligned controls; Brussels revived TTC talks but terms vague and Taiwan-frozen chips still tight. Sovereignty permitting/power went live at two gigafactory sites despite silicon scarcity; bio-shield sequencing/stockpiles declared operational, helping large hospitals. Mayoral pacts, wage bridges, insurer backstop and triage assistants aided some towns/clinics, but hiring lagged and robot kill-switch audits/registries trailed rapid warehouse deployment. By June, US partnership relief mixed with anger Europe owned neither robots nor models.
