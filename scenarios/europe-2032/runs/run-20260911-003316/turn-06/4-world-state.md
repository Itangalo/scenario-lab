### The machines arrive
Spring began with a demonstration that made last year's benchmarks look quaint. A foreign lab released an agent system that planned, coded and coordinated across long horizons with little supervision. Within weeks, logistics firms were ordering humanoid fleets for ports and warehouses, and hospitals were piloting care assistants that lifted, sorted and ferried.

For Europe the shock landed on its factory floor. The robots were largely Chinese-built, running American control software. Orders surged in Rotterdam, Hamburg and Lyon while domestic robotics firms warned they could not match price or scale. Unions called it a second displacement wave with no trade to learn in, and tabloids ran footage of machines stacking crates beside idle workers.

Washington changed hands in January. The new coalition-building administration offered structured allied access to frontier models, joint evaluation and aligned export controls. Brussels welcomed the opening — officials flew to revive the Trade and Technology Council — but access terms remained vague, and allocations of advanced chips stayed tight after the Taiwan freeze.

At home, two long-running programmes finally paid out. The sovereignty package's permitting zones and power connections went live at two gigafactory sites, cranes moving though silicon was still scarce. The bio-shield regime's sequencing network, stockpiles and blockade playbooks were declared operational, and large hospitals weathered late-winter absences better than before.

The Commission pushed its limited capital into towns. Mayoral staffing pacts, wage-bridge payouts and a renewed insurer backstop kept some utilities and clinics staffed, and triage assistants cut waiting lists where they were deployed. But small municipalities still struggled to hire, and a mandated kill-switch audit for robot fleets moved slowly — registries filled, exercises were rehearsed in a few ports, while most warehouses installed machines faster than inspectors could visit.

By June, relief at American partnership coexisted with anger that Europe owned neither the hands nor the minds now working in its warehouses.
