## New measure  
**Sovereign Inference Initiative**  
A binding mandate to ensure that all AI systems used in EU critical functions — healthcare, justice, border management, and core infrastructure — run on sovereign compute infrastructure, with full model provenance and no dependency on foreign-hosted inference.

## Priority  
**Sovereign Inference Initiative** — because access denial this turn was not a failure of policy but of posture: we treated interdependence as resilience, and it was dependence. Without sovereign inference, every other measure — from labour transition to cyber shield — rests on foreign permission. This is now the fulcrum of agency.

## In practice  
We are treating the access cutoff not as an incident but as an inflection. The Sovereign Inference Initiative will be implemented through a revised Article 122a TFEU legal basis, invoking strategic infrastructure status for inference capacity. We are directing the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) to fast-track procurement of sovereign GPU clusters in Luxembourg, Finland, and Marseille, with deployment tied to the decommissioning of foreign-hosted services in sensitive domains. By Q3, all EU-level health data queries must run on EU-anchored systems; by Q4, member states must certify their justice and border systems as sovereign-inference compliant. We are not banning foreign models — we are banning reliance on foreign inference. This is not autarky; it is auditability.  

The Cyber Shield directive, now finished, lands as a partial victory: the red-teaming framework is adopted, and ENSISA will lead the first cross-border exercise in July. But its impact is blunted — without sovereign infrastructure, red teams cannot access the full stack, and cloud providers still resist logging mandates. We are converting this weakness into leverage: the Sovereign Inference Initiative will condition public procurement on full red-team access, closing the loop. Meanwhile, the Labour Transition Accelerator continues bilaterally, but pressure mounts — Poland delays again, citing budget strain, and German unions demand sectoral carve-outs. We are responding by tying early disbursement to sovereign inference milestones: no foreign-hosted AI in retraining platforms, and all digital upskilling tools must run on EU soil. The message is clear: transition must not deepen dependence.  

The Openweight Governance Framework remains in legal review, but we are already seeing pushback from cloud providers who see it as a backdoor restriction. We are adapting: instead of demanding pre-release evaluation, we are piloting a "trusted release" tier — those who submit to ENISA audits gain faster cloud provisioning and liability shielding. It is not as strong as we wanted, but it is a wedge. On sovereignty, we are no longer asking for consensus — we are building. When the first sovereign inference cluster powers on, the narrative shifts from vulnerability to viability. That shift must come fast.
