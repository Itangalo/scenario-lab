### The Verification Surge

Automatic verification in law, clinical coding, and structural engineering has demonstrated significant gains in high-trust domains. Tasks once deemed too context-sensitive for reliable AI are now being executed at scale with machine-verified correctness, challenging assumptions about the necessity of human oversight in specific procedural roles. This progress accelerates the frontier: `ai_capability` rises by +6.0, as systems achieve higher reliability in narrowly defined, rule-bound applications.

Open-weight models begin to incorporate fragments of these advances. Underground networks redistribute fine-tuned variants capable of contract analysis and diagnostic coding, but integration is partial and unstable. Without access to the full verification stacks, performance gains are uneven. `openweight_capability` increases to 70.5—closing part of the gap, but still trailing significantly behind the frontier.

### EU Holds Course, But Loses Ground

The EU refrains from launching a new measure, awaiting clearer misuse patterns from the verification shift. The Sovereign Inference Initiative remains the de facto priority, with live deployments in health and border systems. ENSISA completes its first red-team cycle using fully sovereign stacks, confirming technical feasibility—but adoption remains patchy. Only two member states fully comply; others delay, citing cost and complexity.

The InvestAI Gigafactories in Hamburg and Lyon operate at full capacity, supporting evaluation and health data workloads. Budapest reaches 60% load, with remaining capacity reserved for crisis response. A repurposed training program begins onboarding 5,000 operators, though deployment timelines slip due to recruitment bottlenecks.

### Erosion Continues

With no new measure and no completions, political capital erodes further. The priority cost applies despite inaction, and no portfolio benefits offset it. Public sentiment stagnates at 22.0, weighed down by visible job losses in legal and clinical sectors now exposed to automation. Resilience declines by 2 points—exposure grows while protective measures lag.

Sovereignty drops to 18.0: the +3 from last turn’s category 4 completion has expired, the capability rise costs −1, and no new build offsets the decay. The ASML boardroom revolt simmers, and transatlantic tensions over export controls deepen, though no formal access denial occurs.
