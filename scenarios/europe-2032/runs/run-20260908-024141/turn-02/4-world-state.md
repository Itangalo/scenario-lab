### The Weight of Transition

The frontier advances again, with `ai_capability` rising to 57.0 — a +2.5 increase sustained by longer-horizon agents and faster iteration. This growth accelerates the gap between what systems can do and what humans can verify. Meanwhile, `openweight_capability` climbs to 47.0, pulled up by the legacy of Kimi K3 and recent open releases that now enable sophisticated cyber operations outside controlled environments.

A rare bright spot emerges: a **safety_breakthrough** in interpretability allows developers to predict emergent reasoning patterns before deployment. The result is rapidly adopted across leading labs, lifting `ai_safety` by +7 points to 38.0 — though still far from robust assurance, the improvement is tangible and timely.

The EU launches the **Labour Transition Accelerator**, a large category 7 measure aimed at stemming social strain from AI-driven job losses. Driven by rising displacement in legal, radiology, and software sectors, the programme introduces mandatory employer contributions and EU co-funding for retraining. Early enthusiasm is tempered by implementation friction: Germany and Poland challenge the directive’s conditionality, calling it an overreach into national labour policy. The Commission holds firm, but rollout slows.

Public sentiment edges up to 45.0, supported by visible action on job displacement — a modest +3 boost from the new measure, despite resistance. The narrative shifts from resignation to guarded hope.

Resilience dips slightly to 37.0. The Cyber Shield directive remains in trilogue, yielding no immediate gains, while rising AI capability imposes a −1 penalty due to unmitigated systemic risk.

Sovereignty declines to 20.0: the ongoing delay in gigafactory deployment, especially in Budapest, compounds the −1 decay from rising capability. No event secures or removes access, leaving only the steady erosion.

Political capital falls sharply to 32.0. The portfolio now carries three large measures (−9), one priority (−1), and no completion bonuses. The proposal bonus for the Labour Transition Accelerator — a direct response to a growing displacement wave — earns +3, but cohesion struggles and legal pushback limit the gain. The net charge is −7.
