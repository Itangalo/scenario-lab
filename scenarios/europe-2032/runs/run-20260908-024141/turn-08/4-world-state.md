### The Surge Continues

AI capability accelerated again, rising by 6.0 points to 83.5 as recursive self-improvement deepened. Frontier models now design not only architectures but training curricula, data pipelines, and evaluation benchmarks—effectively steering their own evolution. Human oversight is now largely confined to high-level goal setting, with technical decisions made autonomously. No laboratory reports meaningful interpretability, and alignment assumptions are breaking under recursive pressures. Despite growing alarm, no coordinated international safety initiative has taken hold, and `ai_safety` remains stagnant at 6.0.

Open-weight capability rose to 64.5, reflecting increased underground diffusion of frontier-derived models. While no major open release occurred, leaked weights and inference APIs have proliferated in cyber and research communities, narrowing the gap with closed systems. The rise is consistent with structural lag but signals growing distributed risk.

### EU Consolidates, But Pays a Price

The EU did not launch a new measure, focusing instead on operationalizing recently completed infrastructure. The Sovereign Inference Initiative went live in Luxembourg, Finland, and Marseille, enabling end-to-end evaluation of AI systems on EU-controlled hardware. ENSISA began red-teaming frontier models using sovereign stacks, and compliance mandates were issued for justice and infrastructure systems. Two member states adopted the framework; others face cohesion fund withholdings and infringement proceedings.

The InvestAI Gigafactories in Hamburg and Lyon are now fully operational, with Budapest reaching 60% capacity. The sites are feeding compute into health data and safety evaluation pipelines. A new training program, repurposed from the Labour Transition Accelerator, will train 5,000 operators to maintain sovereign infrastructure.

However, political capital declined. With no new measure proposed and no completions this turn, the portfolio charge was zero, but the named priority cost −1. Public sentiment fell sharply to 22.0 amid accelerating job losses and physical protests against AI infrastructure. Resilience dropped by 2 points due to rising exposure and no new protective measures.

Sovereignty rose to 19.0: +3 from the Sovereign Inference Initiative’s completion (category 4), offset by −1 due to the 6-point capability rise. The ASML boardroom revolt intensified, but no formal action was taken, leaving transatlantic tensions unresolved.
