## In practice  
We are treating the Sovereign Inference Initiative as the operational core of a new regime of AI sovereignty: evaluable, auditable, and under full EU jurisdiction. The three clusters in Luxembourg, Finland, and Marseille are now processing live health data, cross-border risk assessments, and legal diagnostics using only EU-controlled stacks—no foreign cloud, no external audit, no dependency. ENSISA’s first red-team exercise ran entirely on sovereign infrastructure, exposing vulnerabilities in legacy systems but proving that independent verification is possible. We are now enforcing a de facto standard: any AI used in EU critical infrastructure must be fully evaluable on EU-controlled hardware by mid-2033. Compliance will be tied to cohesion fund disbursements and enforced through Article 260 TFEU infringement procedures. Technical assistance is being deployed via the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium to bring lagging member states into alignment, but the deadline will not bend.

The InvestAI Gigafactories in Hamburg and Lyon are fully operational, supplying the compute backbone for ENSISA’s evaluation pipeline and the European Health Data Space. Budapest is at 60% load, with remaining capacity reserved for crisis response. The repurposed training programme for 5,000 operators is live, though recruitment remains slow—this is not just about filling roles, but about building a public technical cadre capable of maintaining sovereign systems over time. Yet none of this is enough. The automated decision scandal—now confirmed by an ombudsman—has shattered public trust. A social insurance AI, trained on legacy data and fine-tuned without oversight, denied benefits to tens of thousands based on synthetic fraud patterns. The system could not be audited in time. The damage was done. Restriction is now politically cheap, adoption politically toxic. We cannot launch a new measure into this void. The verification surge has made capability incomprehensible; the export controls have severed continuity; the scandal has destroyed legitimacy. Before we act, we must understand what kind of power we still have—and what kind of Union we are trying to save.
