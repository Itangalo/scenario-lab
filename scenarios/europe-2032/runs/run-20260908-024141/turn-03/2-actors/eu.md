## New measure  
**Openweight Governance Framework**  
A binding regime to condition access to EU cloud infrastructure and digital markets on adherence to safety thresholds for open-weight model releases, leveraging the Digital Markets Act and Cyber Resilience Act as enforcement levers.

## Priority

## In practice  
We are pushing the Labour Transition Accelerator through national labour ministries with a mix of leverage and partnership. The European Social Fund+ is unlocked only when member states designate AI-driven job loss as a qualifying condition for active support — a legal nudge, not a full harmonisation. We are working bilaterally with Germany and Poland: to Berlin, we offer co-design of the retraining pathways for legal and engineering technicians; to Warsaw, we tie early disbursement to regional pilot zones in Silesia and Lublin, where green retrofitting can absorb displaced workers. Cedefop is fast-tracking modular curricula in AI stewardship and public digital auditing, and the European Labour Authority is building a real-time dashboard to track not just participation but placement — because we will be judged on outcomes, not effort. This is flexicurity under acceleration: we are not blocking restructuring, but we are making it cost more to automate without reinvestment.

At the same time, we are acting on the rising openweight threat. With `openweight_capability` at 47.0 and offensive cyber tools now within reach of mid-tier actors, we cannot wait for the closed frontier to catch up. The new Openweight Governance Framework will require any entity releasing model weights above 45.0 capability to submit to pre-release evaluation by a newly mandated unit within ENISA, and to restrict distribution via contractual terms enforced through cloud providers. We are using the DMA’s gatekeeper provisions to bind hyperscalers: no AWS, Azure or OVHcloud instance for those who distribute unchecked, high-capability open models. The legal base is Article 114 TFEU, and we are coordinating with the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre to operationalise the technical verification. This is not a ban — it is a conditionality, and it is designed to be enforceable without needing to inspect every download.

Cyber Shield remains in trilogue, but we are breaking the deadlock by offering national CSIRTs rotating leadership of red-team exercises — sovereignty of execution, not exemption. We are also fast-tracking the cohesion fund leverage: countries adopting the open-logging standard by Q1 2028 unlock 20% of their allocation, and we are pairing it with joint procurement of detection stacks via the European Defence Agency’s emerging cyber unit. On the industrial front, InvestAI Gigafactories moves forward in Hamburg and Lyon, but Budapest remains stalled. We have invoked Article 122 TFEU to unlock emergency grid funding, and we are now treating the gigafactory build as a strategic infrastructure priority, equivalent to energy interconnectors. The Tech Sovereignty Package gains ground in Spain and Ireland, where permitting is tied to green hydrogen commitments, but Germany remains cautious. We are not forcing consensus — we are demonstrating momentum. When the first factory powers on, the narrative shifts from cost to necessity.
