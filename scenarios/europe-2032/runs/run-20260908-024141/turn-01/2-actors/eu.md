## In practice  
We are acting on the breach in transmission grids and port systems not as an isolated failure, but as a symptom of a deeper fragility: our critical infrastructure runs on systems we cannot secure because they are shaped by decisions made elsewhere, with pace prioritised over visibility. This turn, the Commission tables a draft Cyber Shield Directive under Article 114 TFEU, mandating interoperable detection stacks, live logging to national CSIRTs, and quarterly cross-border penetration exercises led by ENISA. The legal base is the NIS2 framework, but we are stretching it — this is not just reporting, it is operational interdependence. Resistance is expected from member states with weaker cyber posture and from operators who see compliance costs as prohibitive. To counter that, we are bundling €12bn in cohesion funds for detection modernisation, tied to adoption of open, auditable AI monitoring tools — not black-box systems, but ones whose logic flows can be traced.  

The two inherited measures remain on track. InvestAI Gigafactories is in early site selection: Hamburg, Lyon, and Budapest are shortlisted, but grid connection timelines are the bottleneck. We are invoking the EU Emergency Infrastructure Provision to fast-track substation upgrades, but that requires Council approval under the EUCI framework — a fight we will win only if member states see this as strategic, not industrial. Meanwhile, the Tech sovereignty package is drawing private capital, but slowly: the accelerated-permitting zones are stalled in national parliaments. We are now offering bilateral energy-for-access deals: countries that fast-track data centre permits gain priority in EU renewable hydrogen allocations. It’s not clean, but it’s how things move.  

Public sentiment remains ambivalent. The phage news did not land in mainstream coverage, but the cyber intrusion did — framed as a near-miss, not a catastrophe. That buys us a narrow window: if we act as if resilience were the precondition of everything, not the afterthought, we may yet align capability with control. But the eval_anomaly_reports whisper something else — that the next jump may not be incremental. We are not ready. And so, we build both faster and safer, knowing that for now, only the first gets celebrated, while only the second keeps us standing.
