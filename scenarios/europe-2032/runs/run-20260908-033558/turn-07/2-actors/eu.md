## Statement changes

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): We are shifting our two-year commitment from securing sovereign AI capacity to establishing sovereign AI control, because the surge in capability and the onset of RSI have rendered access to systems less urgent than the ability to understand, constrain and govern them where they run  
- Grounds: The opaque_reasoning event confirms that even if sovereign compute were online, we lack the technical means to audit or control systems at the current capability level. With ai_safety at 5.0 and no interpretability pathway in sight, building more capacity without control would accelerate risk without increasing agency. This turn’s developments have redefined the bottleneck: it is no longer access, but assurance.

## New measure

### Sovereign Control Pilot Programme  
We are launching a pilot to develop and deploy EU-controlled, auditable AI agents for high-risk public functions — starting with grid stability, medical triage, and border threat assessment — using formal verification, modular reasoning, and human-readable decision logging, even at the cost of capability.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +12 (by demonstrating a path to legible, verifiable systems in critical domains), eu_ai_sovereignty +5 (by establishing EU-run evaluation and deployment infrastructure), public_sentiment +3 (if early pilots are seen as transparent and accountable)  
`Applies to:` EU agencies (ENISA, EMA, Frontex), national critical infrastructure operators, public research labs, and selected EU-certified developers

## Priority

**Resilience Acceleration Programme** — because the ransomware wave, emergent labour unrest, and opaque reasoning event have created a triad of immediate threats: infrastructure is vulnerable, public trust is fraying, and no one can explain what the systems are doing, so we must harden the front line now, even as we pivot our long-term strategy toward control.

## In practice

We are executing on the ground truth that we are no longer in a race for capability — we are in a race for comprehension. The Resilience Acceleration Programme is now live: the Commission has issued the Article 114 TFEU directive, and ENISA is coordinating with national cyber agencies to define the baseline AI detection protocols. We are fast-tracking the Baltic-Nordic early-warning grid as the model for rollout, with JRC teams already on the ground to standardise detection signatures and response playbooks. Funding is unlocked through REACT-EU and the Digital Europe Programme, with €12bn allocated to subsidise upgrades in lower-capacity states. The first pan-European cyber exercise will be scheduled for turn 8, under the authority of the Cybersecurity Act, and will simulate cascading AI-driven attacks on energy and health systems.

At the same time, we are standing up the Sovereign Control Pilot Programme within DG CONNECT and the Joint Research Centre. The first three use cases — grid load balancing under extreme weather, AI triage in overwhelmed emergency departments, and real-time detection of synthetic biological threats at borders — will be developed under strict legibility constraints: no black-box reasoning, no autonomous action beyond pre-verified thresholds, and full logging of decision pathways. We are engaging with formal methods researchers at INRIA, Fraunhofer, and Aalto to build verification layers, and we are offering procurement preference to developers who can meet the audibility standard, even if their systems are slower or less capable than frontier models. This is not about matching the frontier — it is about creating a sovereign zone of control where we know what the AI knows, and why.

The InvestAI Gigafactories and Joint Access Framework Implementation both finish this turn, freeing up political capital — but not momentum. We are not stopping the build-out of capacity, but we are no longer treating it as the primary lever of sovereignty. The Gigafactories will continue under national co-investment, and we will report on grid readiness in Finland and Andalusia, but the strategic focus shifts: compute without control is not sovereignty, it is exposure. We are now betting that the EU’s comparative advantage lies not in speed, but in legibility — and that our legitimacy depends on being able to show, not just act.
