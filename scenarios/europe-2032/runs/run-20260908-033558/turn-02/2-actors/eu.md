## New measure  
### Open-Weight Model Regulation Initiative  
We will establish a legal threshold for open-weight AI models, requiring registration, safety testing, and licensing for any release above a defined capability level.

## Priority

## In practice  
We are finalising the binding technical standards under Article 18 of the Cyber Resilience Act, with ENISA issuing the first set of AI-specific anomaly detection protocols by the end of Q1. The Commission is activating emergency disbursement mechanisms under the RRF, pairing front-loaded grants with pre-approved vendor packages to compress implementation timelines. We are engaging the Council Presidency to fast-track qualified majority adoption of the implementing regulation, isolating legal objections from operational delivery — this is not a negotiation over principles, but a race against convergence between open-weight capability and closed-frontier offensive tools.

Simultaneously, we are launching preparatory work on the Open-Weight Model Regulation Initiative, convening JRC, BEREC, and national digital agencies to define the capability threshold at which open releases trigger mandatory evaluation. We are coordinating with the European Parliament’s informal working group to align draft amendments to the AI Act, ensuring legal continuity. The message is clear: openness is not an excuse for ungoverned risk, and the EU will treat distributed models as seriously as corporate deployments when they cross the line into autonomous exploitation. Industry pushback is expected, but the coalition of researchers and civil society behind emergent_openweight_scrutiny gives us political cover — and the loss_of_control_incident gives us urgency.
