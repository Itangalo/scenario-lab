## Priority

## In practice
We are moving rapidly to implement the US alliance framework, negotiating the first tier of access with the Department of Commerce and DHS for EU-level evaluators and cyber defenders. The European AI Office is setting up a classified channel under the CSDD legal base to manage data flows and model access, while ENISA and the JRC are being equipped with secure enclaves to conduct joint evaluations. We are designating three EU institutions — including the BfAI and a consortium of national cyber agencies — as initial access points, with strict protocols to prevent leakage or unauthorised diffusion. This is not a substitute for sovereignty, but a bridge: we are binding access to mutual incident reporting, shared safety benchmarks, and reciprocal audit rights, turning dependency into interdependence.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on Enforce Open-Weight Licensing Threshold, where the loss_of_control_incident has sharpened urgency. We are finalising the EU model registry with DEPLOY-AI and onboarding major hosting providers under threat of infringement if compliance is not achieved by turn 6. The JRC is accelerating its audit framework to cover agentive behaviour, and we are expanding fast-track certification to include models evaluated under the new US-EU joint protocol. On the industrial front, InvestAI Gigafactories continues to advance: we have secured grid access in two locations and are finalising co-investment agreements with ASML and a leading EU chip designer for advanced packaging. The Tech Sovereignty Package remains on track, though we are now conditioning some permits on open-weight compliance, linking industrial growth to control. The cyber_major_incident has exposed our fragility, but we are not retreating — we are adapting, using this turn to turn alliance into advantage while keeping our eyes on the horizon.
