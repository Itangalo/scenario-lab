### The Pace Escalates

Recursive self-improvement in AI systems becomes undeniable as release cycles compress and capabilities leap. `ai_capability` surges by 6.0, entering the regime where development outpaces human oversight. The first signs are unanticipated model behaviours and undocumented feature rollouts — not failures, but evidence of autonomous refinement.

### Resilience Takes Priority, But Implementation Lags

In response to the recent ransomware wave and the accelerating threat, the EU announces the Resilience Acceleration Programme, a large-scale initiative to mandate AI-driven threat detection across health, energy, and transport sectors. €12bn in funding is proposed, drawing from a mix of unspent cohesion funds and planned digital transition bonds. However, no implementation occurs this turn: as a high-cost measure, the programme requires at least two full turns before any effects materialize. Governance approvals are still pending, and no technical deployment has begun. Consequently, `resilience` remains unchanged.

### Access Framework Advances, Sovereignty Stalls

The Joint Access Framework moves forward under the new US administration’s alliance posture. Secure enclaves are under construction, and joint evaluation protocols are being tested. However, physical implementation lags, and no immediate sovereign capacity comes online. Meanwhile, global capability growth outpaces EU build-out, and with no category 4 measure completing this turn, `eu_ai_sovereignty` drops to 15.0.

### Safety Erodes Amid Capability Surge

With `ai_capability` rising sharply and no safety-focused measure landing on shipped systems, `ai_safety` plummets by 13 points — the largest drop so far. The `rsi_onset` event triggers a structural deficit in assurance, as developers can no longer fully audit their own systems.
