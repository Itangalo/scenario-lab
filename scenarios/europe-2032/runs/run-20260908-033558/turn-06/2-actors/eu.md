## New measure
### Resilience Acceleration Programme  
We are fast-tracking the integration of defensive AI into critical infrastructure by leveraging the emergent regional resilience network, scaling proven coordination models, and mandating AI-driven threat detection and response capabilities across health, energy, and transport sectors.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +15 (by institutionalising rapid detection and coordinated response), eu_political_capital -2 (due to resistance from member states on mandatory upgrades), public_sentiment +5 (as visible progress restores confidence in institutional response)

## Priority  
**Resilience Acceleration Programme** — because the rsi_onset and the cyber_major_incident confirm that defensive lag is now existential, and the emergent_regional_resilience_network offers a ready-made model to scale, this measure addresses the most immediate vulnerability in our posture: we cannot wait for sovereign capability or allied access to defend what is already under attack.

## In practice  
We are acting on the ground truth that resilience is no longer a backstop but the front line. The emergent regional resilience network, born from local initiative after the ransomware wave, has demonstrated faster detection and cross-border coordination than any central directive has achieved. We are now formalising it: the Commission, in coordination with ENISA and the European Health Agency, is issuing a binding directive under Article 114 TFEU requiring all critical infrastructure operators to integrate AI-powered intrusion detection and automated response protocols by turn 9. Funding will flow through the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with €12bn allocated to subsidise upgrades in lower-capacity member states. We are deploying JRC teams to map the most effective regional models — particularly the Baltic-Nordic early-warning grid and the Rhine Corridor incident-sharing protocol — and standardising them into a EU-wide framework, to be stress-tested in a pan-European cyber exercise by turn 8.

At the same time, we are advancing the Joint Access Framework Implementation: secure enclaves are being built at the BfAI and the Joint Research Centre, with classified connectivity to DHS and NIST counterparts. The first evaluation cycle under the joint protocol will begin in April, focusing on model integrity and zero-day generation risk. We are also finalising the closure of the Tech Sovereignty Package and Enforce Open-Weight Licensing Threshold — both now complete. The former unlocked €183bn in private investment and established three accelerated-permitting zones; the latter led to the takedown of 47 non-compliant model repositories and the registration of 210 high-capability open models. We are carrying forward InvestAI Gigafactories with renewed urgency: ASML and our EU chip packaging partner have confirmed co-investment, and grid connections in Finland and Andalusia are on schedule. But the truth remains — we are building for 2032 while the threat is here in 2029. That is why resilience, not just capacity, must now lead.
