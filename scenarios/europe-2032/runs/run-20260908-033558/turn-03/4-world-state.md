### The Offensive Surge and the Defensive Window

A large-scale, automated cyber intrusion sweeps across public service portals in three member states, disrupting healthcare scheduling, tax filings, and municipal communications. The attack, rapidly attributed to a novel AI-generated exploit chain, exploits zero-day vulnerabilities at machine speed, overwhelming human-led response teams. The breach confirms fears: offensive AI capabilities now outpace legacy defences. In the aftermath, a breakthrough in defensive AI—capable of real-time patching and behavioural swarm detection—emerges from a European research consortium. The EU seizes the moment, launching **Cyber Shield Rapid Deployment** as a large category 6 measure, aiming to harden critical infrastructure by turn 5.

### Rapid Mobilisation, Uneven Compliance

The European Commission activates emergency powers under NIS2, tasking ENISA with issuing binding guidelines for adoption of the new defensive tools. A €15 billion disbursement is unlocked from the Digital Europe and Recovery and Resilience Facility budgets, reprioritising funds already allocated for strategic digital infrastructure. Member states are required to match 30% of costs from national budgets, with disbursement tied to verified procurement milestones. While most states initiate planning, procurement delays and legal challenges in two countries slow initial rollout. Pilot deployments are scheduled for early 2028, with limited systems expected to come online by the next turn.

Meanwhile, the **Emergency Resilience Surge** concludes with mixed results. Legal disputes blocked full funding, but regional cooperation—spurred by earlier cyber incidents—delivers partial upgrades. ENISA’s detection standards are now operational in 60% of targeted sectors, a modest gain that contributes to early confidence but falls short of full coverage.

### Regulation and Resistance

The **Open-Weight Model Regulation Initiative** advances, with the Commission proposing amendments to the AI Act to license powerful open models. Pushback from open-source advocates and startups intensifies, warning of innovation flight. The EU holds firm, arguing accountability must accompany openness.

Public sentiment stabilises, reassured by visible defensive progress despite the earlier breach. However, job losses in cybersecurity—replaced by automated defences—fuel quiet discontent.

### Sovereignty Under Strain

AI capability advances to 60.5, driven by recursive self-improvement now spanning multi-day research cycles. Openweight capability rises to 49.0, reflecting continued diffusion of capable models but growing more slowly than overall AI capability, consistent with structural constraints. No safety gains accompany this leap, and the gap between capability and control widens. The EU’s political capital erodes under the weight of multiple concurrent initiatives, including the new priority. While defensive action earns cautious praise, the strain of simultaneous efforts reveals limits. Resilience improves modestly to 43.0 as planning and early infrastructure setup lay the groundwork for future gains, but operational impact remains limited in this turn.
