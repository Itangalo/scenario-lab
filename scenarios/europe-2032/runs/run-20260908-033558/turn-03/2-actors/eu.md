## New measure  
### Cyber Shield Rapid Deployment  
We are fast-tracking the integration of the new defensive breakthrough into national and cross-border operational stacks, ensuring it is fielded before the next wave of automated attacks.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +12 (broad deployment of AI-speed defences across critical infrastructure), ai_safety +3 (indirectly, by reducing the window for autonomous exploitation), public_sentiment +5 (visible success in countering AI-enabled threats)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, ENISA, national CERTs, and designated operators of essential services

## Priority  
**Cyber Shield Rapid Deployment** — because the cyber_defence_breakthrough gives us a rare window to reverse the offence-defence imbalance, and because the cyber_major_incident proves that even delayed human response is no longer sufficient, we must act now to operationalise this capability across the Union before the next intrusion cycle begins.

## In practice  
We are activating the NIS2 Directive’s emergency cooperation mechanism to fast-track adoption of the new defensive tooling, directing ENISA to issue binding implementation guidelines by Q3 and to coordinate joint procurement of compatible systems through the EU Cyber Defence Procurement Facility. We are pairing this with a €15bn rapid-disbursement envelope from the Digital Europe Programme and repurposed RRF funds, conditioned on member states certifying deployment in at least 80% of their essential service providers by the end of turn 5. The Council’s Cybersecurity configuration will be convened in emergency mode to pre-approve the technical standards, bypassing the usual multi-year alignment process. Resistance from slower-moving member states is expected, but the cyber_major_incident has shifted the political baseline — we are framing non-compliance not as caution, but as negligence in the face of known, automated threat.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the Open-Weight Model Regulation Initiative through the co-legislative process, with the Commission submitting a formal proposal to amend the AI Act to include licensing requirements for open models above the JRC-defined threshold of 45.0 on the capability index. We are working closely with the Parliament’s internal working group to secure a trilogue-ready position before the end of the year, while offering carve-outs for academic and non-commercial research to ease startup opposition. The message is firm: openness without accountability enables harm, and the EU will not allow its digital sovereignty to be undermined by ungoverned diffusion.

The InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track, with site selection finalised for two of the five planned locations and grid-connection timelines accelerated through delegated acts under the Net-Zero Industry Act. We are in active talks with three major chipmakers to co-locate advanced packaging facilities, leveraging the recently passed Critical Technologies Act to offer targeted subsidies and export safeguards. This is not a moment for retreat — but for disciplined focus. We are finishing the Emergency Resilience Surge with partial delivery, learning that legal fragmentation can paralyse even urgent action. Now, we pivot to what works: deploying proven defences at speed, while holding the line on open-weight risk and continuing to build the sovereign capacity that will, one day, let us act from strength, not reaction.
