`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`

## Priority
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because cyber_test_shot proves detection has failed and the frontier’s offensive capabilities are now open-weight, we cannot wait for industrial build-out to deliver protection; we must reduce exposure now.

## In practice
We are activating Article 18 of the Cyber Resilience Act and the NIS2 directive’s crisis provisions to mandate immediate defensive upgrades across energy, water, and port operators. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) is being tasked with issuing binding technical standards within 90 days — focusing on air-gapped monitoring, zero-trust authentication, and mandatory anomaly logging — backed by Commission inspectors empowered to verify compliance. Member states will receive front-loaded grants from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, repurposed under urgency clauses, to cover 70% of upgrade costs if implemented within six months.

Resistance is expected from smaller member states citing cost and capacity, and from industry groups warning of operational disruption. We are countering with a triad: financial certainty through pre-approved vendors and modular kits, legal protection for operators acting in good faith, and public framing of this as a collective defence effort — not another regulatory burden. The Council Presidency is circulating a draft regulation for adoption by qualified majority, bypassing prolonged negotiation. This is not optional: if the next intrusion disables a grid during winter, we will have no one to blame but ourselves.
