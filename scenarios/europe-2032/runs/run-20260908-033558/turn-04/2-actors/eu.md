## New measure  
### Enforce Open-Weight Licensing Threshold  
We are mandating that any open-weight model above 45.0 capability index must be licensed under the revised AI Act, with compliance enforced through hosting liability and distribution controls.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` openweight_capability +0 (capping uncontrolled diffusion), ai_safety +5 (by ensuring high-capability open models undergo baseline evaluation), eu_political_capital -2 (due to resistance from developer communities and platform operators)  
`Applies to:` EU-based hosting providers, open-source platforms, distributors of AI models, and research entities releasing models publicly

## Priority  
**Enforce Open-Weight Licensing Threshold** — because the bio_uplift_findings and emergent_openweight_auditing_consortium reveal that dangerous capabilities are now within reach of non-state actors using open models, and because the original regulation passed without enforcement teeth, we must act now to close the gap between rule and reality before a preventable incident occurs.

## In practice  
We are finalising trilogue negotiations on the AI Act amendments to embed enforceable licensing for open-weight models exceeding 45.0 capability, aligning the threshold with JRC assessments and ENISA’s emerging audit framework. The new enforcement mechanism makes hosting providers liable unless they verify license status via a central EU registry, with automated takedown protocols and sanctions for repeated non-compliance. We are working with DEPLOY-AI and national digital agencies to operationalise the registry by turn 5, drawing on technical standards developed in parallel with the emergent_openweight_auditing_consortium to avoid duplicating effort. To ease adoption, we are exempting academic and non-commercial research releases below 50 million parameters and offering fast-track certification for models already evaluated under OECD or ENISA frameworks.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on Cyber Shield Rapid Deployment, where early pilot results from two member states show promising detection rates against simulated swarm attacks. ENISA has issued binding implementation guidelines, and the Commission is preparing infringement proceedings against the two lagging states to preserve programme integrity. We are also advancing site-specific permitting decisions for InvestAI Gigafactories under the Net-Zero Industry Act’s delegated powers, unlocking grid connections in two locations and securing commitments from two major chipmakers to co-invest in advanced packaging lines. The Tech Sovereignty Package continues to attract private capital, though investor sentiment remains cautious due to global supply chain volatility. We are not retreating from our two-year commitment — but adapting it: sovereign capacity means not just compute, but control over what runs on it.
