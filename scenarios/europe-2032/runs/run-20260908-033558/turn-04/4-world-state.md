### Closing the Open-Weight Gap

The European Union doubles down on enforcement of open-weight model licensing, finalising amendments to the AI Act that impose hosting liability and automated takedown protocols for unlicensed high-capability models. The new regime targets models above a 45.0 capability threshold, aligning with JRC assessments and leveraging technical standards from an emergent pan-European auditing consortium. While academic and small-scale research releases are exempted, commercial platforms and public distributors now face compliance obligations backed by sanctions.

Despite strong political will, resistance mounts. Developer communities denounce the rules as overreach, warning of fragmentation and innovation flight. Implementation delays emerge as some cloud providers challenge the scope of liability in national courts, and cross-border enforcement remains patchy. The measure lands partially — a signal, not a seal.

### Defending Critical Infrastructure

Cyber Shield Rapid Deployment advances, with two member states reporting strong pilot results using defensive AI against simulated swarm attacks. ENISA tightens oversight, issuing binding implementation guidelines and threatening infringement proceedings against lagging nations. Progress is tangible but uneven: procurement bottlenecks and legal disputes continue to slow nationwide rollout.

Meanwhile, AI capability climbs steadily to 63.0, driven by recursive self-improvement cycles now spanning multiple days. Openweight models reach 51.0, aided by diffusion from frontier systems and bolstered by growing accessibility of consumer-grade training clusters. No safety gains accompany this leap — the gap between power and control widens further.

### Strain Across the Union

Political capital erodes under the weight of overlapping initiatives. The new enforcement push adds strain, while sovereignty slips to 17.0 as global AI progress outpaces domestic build-out. Public sentiment dips slightly, reflecting unease over both unchecked capabilities and heavy-handed regulation. Resilience improves modestly to 45.0, supported by incremental defensive deployments and regional coordination networks gaining operational maturity.
