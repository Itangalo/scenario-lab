### The Weight of Inaction

The EU’s diplomatic push to anchor the Global AI Safety Initiative advanced in technical talks, with Brussels emerging as the likely host. Draft protocols now reflect EU-style audit logic, and the High Representative secured verbal support from Canada, South Korea, and Singapore. But at home, momentum stalled. Germany’s constitutional court announced it would hear the challenge to the expired bio mandate, rejecting the political trade as legally irrelevant. France withheld commitment, demanding concrete guarantees on data sovereignty that the Commission could not deliver. Only six member states now back the initiative—short of the threshold to avoid a blocking minority.

With no new measure proposed and the portfolio unchanged, the EU’s political capital eroded further under the weight of ongoing commitments. The Global AI Safety Partnership, a large measure, continued to cost −3 per turn. The named priority added another −1. No finishing measure provided relief, and no proposal bonus applied. The legitimacy lift from public sentiment was absent—capital remained above sentiment—leaving the final balance at 1.0, barely above paralysis.

### Fractures Deepen

Public silence backfired. In Lyon and Gdansk, protests intensified, now joined by municipal IT workers whose departments were fully automated. Rumours spread of a coordinated AI-driven disinformation campaign targeting regional energy grids, though no breach was confirmed. The emergent regional resilience network detected anomalous activity but lacked authority to act. Trust in institutions continued to decay.

The truth commission on the bio incident missed its deadline for an interim report. Internal leaks revealed disputes between ENISA and EASAC over responsibility for the original failure. With no visible accountability, the perception of drift hardened.

### Sovereignty Erodes

AI capability remained at 100.0, but the open frontier held at 75.0—no new leaks or releases occurred. Still, the mere persistence of terminal capability cost the EU −1 on sovereignty, as did the ongoing enforcement vacuum. No event secured or removed access, but dependence deepened in practice. With no domestic compute breakthrough or supply-chain gain, sovereignty fell to 6.0.
