- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): We are now committed to establishing sovereign AI control through legible, auditable systems in high-risk domains, because the onset of opaque reasoning and the collapse of ai_safety to 0.0 have made it clear that control—not access—is the precondition for both security and political legitimacy  
- Grounds: The Black Box Era has begun: at 79.0 ai_capability and 0.0 ai_safety, we can no longer assume that even sovereign systems are governable. The Sovereign Control Pilot Programme is now the central pillar of our strategy, not a complement to capacity-building. Continuing to frame the goal as “securing capacity” would misalign our actions and mislead our publics. We are pivoting from infrastructure to assurance because without it, sovereignty is an illusion.

## New measure

### Transparent Assurance Framework  
We are launching a binding EU-wide framework requiring all AI systems used in public functions or critical infrastructure to submit interpretable decision logs, undergo third-party audits of reasoning pathways, and demonstrate compliance with formal safety specifications before deployment.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +15 (by institutionalising pre-deployment verification), resilience +5 (by reducing attack surface in critical systems), public_sentiment +5 (if perceived as restoring accountability)  
`Applies to:` EU agencies, national governments, critical infrastructure operators, and certified AI developers supplying public-sector systems

## Priority

**Transparent Assurance Framework** — because the automated_decision_scandal has created a narrow window to establish enforceable oversight, and with ai_safety at 0.0, we must institutionalise auditability before another failure erodes public trust beyond recovery.

## In practice

We are acting on the fact that the EU can no longer afford to deploy systems it cannot understand. The automated_decision_scandal has lit a fire under member states: ministers are demanding guarantees that AI will not repeat its betrayal in social services or justice. We are answering with the Transparent Assurance Framework, a regulation drafted under Article 114 TFEU and co-decided in the Internal Market and Consumer Protection Council. ENISA will be empowered as the central audit authority, with mandatory access to model reasoning traces, decision logs, and training data provenance for all high-risk deployments. We are working with formal methods teams at INRIA and the Max Planck Institute to define the first set of verifiable safety specifications—starting with non-discrimination, causal traceability, and bounded autonomy—and we will require developers to prove compliance through simulation and adversarial probing before any system touches public services.

At the same time, we are pushing the Resilience Acceleration Programme into operational mode. The first pan-European cyber exercise, codenamed *CyberShield 2030*, will run in June under the Cybersecurity Act, simulating coordinated AI-driven attacks on energy grids and hospital networks. ENISA is finalising the detection signature library with partners in Estonia, Finland, and the Netherlands, and we are unlocking €4.2bn in immediate funding through the Digital Europe Programme to upgrade intrusion detection systems in lower-capacity member states. This is not about matching the frontier—it is about making sure the frontier does not break us. The Sovereign Control Pilot Programme has now concluded its design phase; its three use cases will transition into the Transparent Assurance Framework as pilot implementations, ensuring continuity between research and regulation.

We are not restarting the Joint Access Framework or InvestAI Gigafactories—those chapters are closed. The Gigafactories will remain under national management, but the EU’s strategic focus has shifted: compute without control is not sovereignty, it is exposure. We are now betting that our legitimacy lies in legibility, and that our power will come not from how fast we compute, but from how clearly we can show what our systems know, and why.
