### A fallback still in build
By autumn the Commission reported the Fallback Stack still in phased build rather than delivered: reserved supercomputing slices were being prepared for hospital and ministry inference, procurement lists were being drafted toward European-hosted open models, and French-Dutch exercise playbooks were in draft implementing guidance. In districts that already shared telemetry, limited pilot switchovers during the autumn drills worked. Elsewhere migration stalled — private co-financing never arrived, connectors to legacy appointment and payment software remained half-built, and several regions kept the fallback as a pilot rather than a production path, with full effect still turns away.

The isolation regime became the daily grind. Auditors from the EU cybersecurity agency and the AI Office toured essential operators, checking gates, kill-switches and logging, releasing recovery funds only where installation was verified. Joint containment teams answered faster than in winter, but attribution for the spring intrusions never landed and evening bulletins repeated that attackers using model-built tooling were still moving faster than defenders. Safety practices and service resilience held roughly steady rather than improving, as audits and drills contained but did not reverse underlying pressure.

### Paychecks against resentment
The new continuity income launched through reprogrammed social funds: a temporary EU top-up to short-time and transition benefits tied to automating employers, plus fast retraining vouchers aimed at municipal clerks, hospital administrators and logistics back-office staff. Rollout was uneven. A few employment services paid within weeks; others tangled over eligibility proof and employer certification. Unions called it too small to calm fear of displacement, while some finance ministries called it an open-ended subsidy.

Public mood barely lifted. Wards stayed open more reliably where gates held, and junior staff kept productivity gains from assistants, but town halls still linked every outage to dependence on foreign models that could be withdrawn. Opposition to new data-centre sites persisted, slowing the very hosting the fallback needed.

With cohesion thin and budgets tight, Brussels held the line rather than widening it: no new lockdown scope, no forced migration deadlines, just audits, drills and first income payments while the underlying capability gap widened and the fallback remained incomplete.
