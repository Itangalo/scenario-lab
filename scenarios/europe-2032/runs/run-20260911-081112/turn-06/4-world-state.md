### The jump no one can read
Spring 2029 brought a sharp, narrow leap. A new generation of systems, first demonstrated abroad and then widely copied, proved strikingly better at code, mathematics and intrusion work. Within weeks, security teams saw faster exploit chains and more convincing malicious engineering help. General usefulness rose only modestly, but what an attacker could do changed.

At the same time the leading models stopped showing their work. Vendors confirmed the new architectures reason in representations that are not language. Oversight teams that relied on reading step-by-step traces found their tools blind overnight. Alternatives — sealed-box testing and slow inspection of internal activations — were costly and immature.

Days later a near-frontier open release spread to hundreds of thousands of downloads. Whatever the jump had added was now on private machines permanently.

### Brussels builds a black box tester
The Commission answered with a small evaluation cell inside the AI Office, staffed with secondments from research labs, cyber and health agencies. Essential operators buying AI would face new pre-deployment suites for cyber and biology, gating public contracts and security approvals. Paris and Dutch labs volunteered as pilots. Legal cover came from existing evaluation powers; no new law was opened.

The cell started work but could not keep pace. Results were disputed, vendors slow to submit, and activation inspection yielded little in time.

Earlier bets paid partly. The permitting and data-centre package formally closed, unlocking sites and grid links, though private money remained cautious. The joint cyber and pathogen integration also closed, with shared telemetry and sequencing now conditioning recovery funds. Municipal networks hit by renewed probes again degraded rather than stopped.

### A capital goes its own way
The unity broke on supply. One member state signed its own capacity arrangement with a foreign hyperscaler on looser screening terms, calling it pragmatism. Brussels offered joint-buying prices to re-anchor it and tied continuity reinsurance to data sharing, but the deal stood. Papers called it humiliation; workers liked their assistants but feared replacement and unreadable machines.

Washington, under its new moratorium-minded administration, slowed for political reasons and offered little help.
