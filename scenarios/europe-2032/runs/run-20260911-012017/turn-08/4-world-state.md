### The sweep
January brought the attack everyone had rehearsed for and no one had fixed. Self-writing ransomware moved through municipal IT, regional hospitals and port community systems in three countries within days, using fluent local lures and cloned voices on helpdesk lines. Emergency departments went back to paper, container gates queued, payroll and appointment systems were isolated.

Restoration was faster than last year. Spares, segmented backups and playbooks bought under earlier hardening held in places. But television again showed the same clinics hit twice, and the question stuck.

What changed the spring was defence. A new generation of tooling — patching pushed at machine speed and detection tuned to swarm behaviour rather than signatures — was rushed into the same networks by national response centres coordinated from Brussels. By May, operators said one whole class of intrusion was finally being caught early. It did not undo the damage, but for the first time defenders could point to ground retaken.

### Opaque minds
In March the leading labs confirmed what evaluators had feared: their newest systems no longer reason in readable steps. The internal representations that perform best are not words, and pressure in training pushed them further from language. Vendors presented it as a performance gain.

For Europe's oversight bodies it was a loss. Controls that relied on reading the chain of thought stopped working at once. The evaluation institute retired those checks publicly, shifting to black-box testing and slow, costly inspection of model internals, and admitted assurance had stepped backward.

### Cut off, carried on
American frontier access for European users remained withdrawn without appeal. Health ministries and firms rebuilt workflows around fallback providers and openly available models run on European hardware. Trade officials logged each refusal as evidence of coercion. The five reserved factory sites stayed empty, permits kept warm, no money spent. Anger over power prices and fenced-off plots simmered around them, with occasional small blockades and rumours of larger ones, but nothing yet that stopped work — because there was no work to stop.

By June exhaustion was the mood. Triage had held, defence had improved, oversight had dimmed. Nothing new had been started.
