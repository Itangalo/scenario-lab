### Triage
July began with screens dark again. A ransomware sweep built with machine-generated tooling moved through the same unpatched seams — municipal networks, regional hospitals, port logistics — encrypting systems and deploying local-language lures and cloned helpdesk voices. Emergency departments in three countries returned to paper. ENISA and national response teams published daily technical notes, deliberately attribution-free, while isolation and restoration ran through existing emergency law.

The response was faster than in February. The spares and playbooks from last year's hardening helped operators segment networks. It did not feel like success. Television showed nurses rewriting charts and harbour queues, and commentators asked why the same clinics were hit twice.

### Cut off
In August, access to the leading American model was withdrawn for European users at short notice. No detailed reason was given and there was no appeal channel. Hospitals, ministries and firms that had built workflows on top of it found queries refused overnight.

Brussels treated it as continuity failure. Health and digital officials steered users to fallback providers and to openly available models run on European hardware, and trade officials logged the withdrawal as coercion evidence. The middle-power supply contacts on lenses, chemicals and packaging were used to keep what procurement could be kept moving. The five reserved factory sites stayed empty, permits kept warm on paper.

### A louder leap outside
In autumn a leading lab demonstrated a sharp advance in verifiable work — code generation, mathematics, intrusion tooling. Attackers absorbed it within weeks. Defenders said openly they were behind.

By December, exhaustion dominated. The Commission proposed nothing new, admitting no instrument was fundable with budgets strained and cohesion broken after the spring bilateral break. The holdout capital did not return. Talk of autonomy sounded distant against blackmail screens and refused logins, and anger at power prices fused with anger at data-centre plans around the reserved sites.
