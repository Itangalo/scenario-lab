### Triage that holds
The second half of 2030 did not bring a new sweep so much as the long tail of the last one. Clinics in the three hit countries cycled back to normal scheduling, then slipped again when a patched appointment system failed under load. Port gates moved, slowly. Brussels paid for extra response-centre shifts and pushed spares and clean backups through existing emergency channels. It kept services upright, but television crews knew where to find the waiting rooms that had been filmed in January.

The one programme that moved was defence. The machine-speed patching and swarm detection that had caught intrusions in May was rushed wider, through national response centres with coordination from the EU cybersecurity agency. Technicians installed it in municipal networks, hospital IT and harbour systems. Where it was in place, probes were stopped earlier. Where it was not yet in place, administrators asked why they were last.

### Minds no one can read
Oversight did not recover. The evaluation institute worked only with black-box tests and slow inspection of model internals, a costly routine that produced thin reports. Vendors shipped another performance gain built on internal representations further from language. Ministers stopped asking for explanations they knew would not come.

### Cut off, then a compact
In autumn, American frontier access for European users was withdrawn at short notice, without reasons or appeal. Hospitals, ministries and firms that had kept a paid fallback lost it overnight and fell back to openly available models run on European hardware. Trade officials logged each refusal.

Almost at once, Brussels announced a coordination framework with other middle powers holding pieces of the supply chain — aligned export licences, joint bargaining for compute, shared evaluation. It promised leverage, not capacity, and no new machines arrived this year. The empty factory plots stayed empty, permits extended, fences watched.

Meanwhile ordinary offices changed quietly. Studies from law firms, accountancies, newsrooms and consultancies showed output per person rising, most for juniors, without layoffs. Firms that had cut early rehired. The public noticed better service more than it noticed AI, and exhaustion eased a fraction.
