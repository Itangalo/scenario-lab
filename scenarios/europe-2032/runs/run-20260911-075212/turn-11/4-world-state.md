### Containment
Autumn brought the incident health officials had rehearsed but never believed. A modified pathogen, designed with model assistance, was released far from Europe and then carried inward by travel. Cases appeared in three member states. Hospitals activated isolation protocols, sequencing labs ran around the clock, and for six weeks contact tracing and ward closures dominated local news.

The joint pact invitation arrived in the middle of containment. Brussels signed quickly: hospital sequencing feeds and grid telemetry were piped into the shared system, liaison officers seconded to the joint cyber command, sample-sharing made binding. It helped. Shared attribution cut through rumour about the source, and shared telemetry blunted a parallel wave of probing attacks on landing stations and power nodes. Registries stayed up. Triage assistants stayed online.

It did not prevent harm. The agent spread before sharing began, and the therapies that worked best were still licensed abroad.

### Empty Desks
At the same time, graduate hiring collapsed across law, accountancy, software support and administration. Firms confirmed what unions had warned: entry roles were not being refrozen, they were gone, automated by systems no European lab could build. Transition Shield top-ups, now finishing, paid out late and small against that scale. Protests outside job centres turned from quiet queues to anger, and coverage paired images of empty desks with airlifted medicine.

In the Taiwan Strait, extended exercises and rising insurance premiums darkened shipping forecasts. Non-belligerency held, but transatlantic repair ships and pharma flights now required chartered cover few insurers would write. Engineers kept inland backups alive with alloys and improvisation, yet every briefing ended on dependence: on foreign cables, foreign chips, foreign models.

Europe sheltered through the half-year, and was seen to shelter. It was also seen to borrow the shelter it stood under.
