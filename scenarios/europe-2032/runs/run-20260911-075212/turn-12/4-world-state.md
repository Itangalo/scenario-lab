### Holding
January to June was a half-year of maintenance. No new law was tabled in Brussels. Hospital sequencing feeds and grid telemetry kept flowing to the shared biosurveillance and cyber system, with reserve liaison teams from Estonia, France and Germany covering shifts. Isolation wards stood down from autumn containment to routine vigilance, and sample-sharing protocols stayed binding.

The trusted-care network formally completed. Detection kits, isolation playbooks and continuity staffing reached district hospitals and care homes in most member states. Nurses reported faster triage; administrators reported hiring freezes unchanged.

### Austerity Continuity
The shelter programme ran as austerity. Inland mirrors of registries held, triage assistants ran on smaller models that could be operated locally, and remaining transition payments were disbursed late. Council meetings avoided new procurement or siting fights. Non-belligerency held, though repair ships and pharma charters still struggled to find cover, and single-source reports spoke of insurers quietly refusing transatlantic data and medical flights.

Graduate joblessness did not lift. Law and accountancy firms confirmed entry cohorts would not return. Protests outside job centres persisted, smaller but angrier, while coverage again paired empty offices with foreign-licensed therapies.

By June, services had stayed up. Dependence had not narrowed, and no one in Brussels claimed otherwise.
