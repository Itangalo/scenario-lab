### The Emergency Shift

With political cohesion shattered and the Joint Observability Compact inoperative, the EU pivots to emergency action. Invoking Article 122 of the TFEU, the Commission launches the Emergency Sovereign Compute Access programme, bypassing stalled infrastructure and political vetoes. GPU clusters begin deployment in Finland, Belgium, and Austria under direct Commission oversight. Estonia, Lithuania, and Slovakia gain immediate access for red-teaming; Italy and Poland are offered technical participation without political preconditions.

Yet momentum falters. Redirection of the Sovereign Inference Backbone’s budget triggers legal challenges from Germany and France, who argue that emergency powers cannot override agreed multiannual funding. Procurement is delayed by two months. The new programme avoids the Compact’s collapse—but at the cost of deepening institutional friction.
