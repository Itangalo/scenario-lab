## Priority

**Priority:** Emergency Sovereign Compute Access  
We are prioritising this over the Joint Observability Compact because the Compact has failed to achieve buy-in from key member states, and with `ai_capability` at 89.0 and `ai_safety` at 1.0, we can no longer afford to condition sovereign capacity on political consensus—we must deliver it where we can, to whom we can trust, before the control gap becomes irreversible.

## In practice

We are invoking Article 122 of the TFEU on safeguarding essential interests to bypass normal procurement and launch emergency compute provisioning under the authority of the European Commission’s Directorate-General for Communications Networks, Content and Technology. We are redirecting 80% of the Sovereign Inference Backbone’s near-term budget—not to verification, as previously planned, but to fast-track deployment of GPU clusters in Finland, Belgium, and Austria, where grid access and physical security are already certified. These will be operated by a new unit within the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, reporting directly to the Commission, with access granted to national cybersecurity agencies in Estonia, Lithuania, and Slovakia that have demonstrated red-teaming capability and telemetry compliance.

We are decoupling compute access from Compact membership. Italy and Poland will be offered participation in the Emergency Sovereign Compute Access programme on a technical basis—no political veto, no delegation of emergency authority required, only adherence to telemetry and incident reporting under the AI Act. This is not a retreat from collective action; it is a shift to *operational* sovereignty: we will build the foundation where the ground is firm, not where it is claimed. The Replication Foundry Initiative’s completion this turn means we now have distillation pipelines ready—we will use them, even if only in fragments, to seed sovereign capability where it can take root.
