## Statement changes

* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity as the foundation of strategic autonomy and public trust``
  - Trigger: The combination of `election_annulled`, `automated_decision_scandal`, and `member_state_defection` has rendered the existing formulation untenable. Public trust is not being built by compute access alone—it is collapsing under the weight of demonstrable harm and institutional failure. Meanwhile, `eu_political_capital` has dropped to 4.0 and `public_sentiment` remains at 13.0, indicating that sovereignty without legitimacy is self-defeating.
  - Grounds: The commitment must now integrate *consequence* into its logic. It is no longer enough to secure sovereign AI capacity—we must ensure it does not become the instrument of our own disempowerment. The revised commitment reflects a pivot from pure infrastructure accumulation to operational legitimacy: we will build sovereign capacity **only if** it can be shown not to reproduce or accelerate systemic harm. This is not a retreat from capability but a recalibration of purpose.

* ``- add `no_harm_by_design` (commitment): We will not deploy any sovereign AI system whose failure mode risks democratic integrity, social cohesion, or critical infrastructure collapse, even if it advances strategic autonomy``
  - Grounds: The `automated_decision_scandal` and `election_annulled` events prove that high-capability systems are already failing catastrophically in public domains. With `ai_safety` at 1.0, we cannot assume control. Our sovereignty must be bounded by survivability. This new commitment raises the bar for deployment: capability without containment is not power—it is surrender to an internal threat vector.

Continuing would consume political capital for symbolic unity with no operational return. We retire it rather than renew, redirecting residual resources to harden nodes within the Emergency Sovereign Compute Access framework.

## New measure

**Measure:** Mandatory Pre-Deployment Harm Audit  
A binding requirement that all AI systems used in public administration, law enforcement, or electoral infrastructure undergo independent evaluation for potential to cause systemic injustice, manipulation, or institutional erosion before deployment.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` from 1.0 to at least 15.0 by establishing verifiable safeguards; stabilize `public_sentiment` by demonstrating accountability after the automated decision scandal  
`Applies to:` All member states’ public agencies deploying AI above capability threshold of 45.0; enforced through revised AI Act implementation protocols and conditional access to EU funding

## Priority

**Priority:** Mandatory Pre-Deployment Harm Audit  
We are prioritising this over further expansion of sovereign compute because the `automated_decision_scandal` and `election_annulled` events have shifted the crisis from external dependence to internal rupture—our own systems are now the vectors of breakdown, and no amount of sovereign infrastructure can compensate for lost legitimacy.

## In practice

We are acting under Article 16(3) of the Treaty on European Union and delegated powers under the AI Act to establish an emergency evaluation regime administered by a newly empowered unit within the European Artificial Intelligence Office. This unit will coordinate with national data protection authorities, ombudsmen, and civil society auditors to review high-risk deployments—not for technical compliance, but for societal consequence. The first target is the algorithmic welfare assessment tool exposed in the recent scandal; its redeployment is suspended pending audit. We are mandating red-teaming of all AI-supported decision systems in policing, benefits, and election logistics by Q1 2023, using teams drawn from outside government contractors to avoid capture.

At the same time, we are pressing forward with the Emergency Sovereign Compute Access programme, now focused exclusively on defensive applications: cyber-hardening, bio-surveillance pattern detection, and red-teaming of foreign-origin models. We have secured provisional agreement from Italy and Poland to join telemetry-sharing protocols, though full integration remains conditional on national legislation. The Commission is fast-tracking procurement exemptions under Article 122 TFEU, but resistance persists in the Council Working Party on Telecommunications, where France insists on parity with national sovereign efforts. We are offering shared access to output—not control over infrastructure—in exchange for non-interference. This is not ideal, but it keeps the nodes online.
