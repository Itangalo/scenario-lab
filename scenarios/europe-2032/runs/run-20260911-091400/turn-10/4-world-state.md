### The recipe that would not go away
In February a preprint claimed a genome model had helped sketch a viable human-infecting organism. Virologists fought over methods, editors fought over publication, but security labs quietly circulated the files. Weeks later a new openly downloadable model landed close to the closed frontier. Hundreds of thousands of copies spread in days. Whatever it could do was now on private machines for good.

Brussels answered with a civil-protection shield: pooled sequencing screens, hospital lab triage, emergency hardening for hospitals and water controls run through health emergency and cyber agencies using existing continuity teams.

### Screens up, concrete still silent
The shield started fast where kit already existed. Showcase hospitals got screening protocols and backup stocks; health operators rehearsed referrals for suspicious samples. Elsewhere procurement stalled, labs waited for reagents, and municipal utilities said the hardening teams arrived with checklists but no staff to run them.

The construction freezes held. Courts kept water and power suspensions in place, the two poured sites stayed taped, and no new ground broke. Energy mediators shuttled draft water-and-power compacts between councils and ministries without a signature. Washington repeated sympathy. The capital with its separate supply deal kept it.

The access pact agreed the previous year was declared complete. Ministers praised unity and pooled volumes, but volumes did not grow and lead times did not shorten.

By June wards were open and routine waits held where European helpers worked, while fear of engineered biology and unrecallable models seeped into local news. People used the triage tools and resented them in equal measure.
