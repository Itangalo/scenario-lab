### Faster than the manuals
Autumn brought a frontier demonstration that made last spring's planning documents obsolete overnight. A leading lab showed agents sustaining long, multi-step research and software work with little supervision, and within weeks distilled versions were everywhere. Open weights released a few months behind the closed frontier were downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in days. What had been loose could no longer be recalled.

For Europe the timing cut both ways. The patching and swarm-detection toolkits deployed through hospitals, response teams and municipal utilities proved their worth: certified hospitals rode out a wave of automated probing, and coordinated-scan alarms that would have cascaded a year earlier degraded into overtime and false positives. Health security's extended wastewater and clinical sequencing net kept running, now exchanging detector feeds with Japanese, Korean and Canadian partners for joint test results on opaque systems.

But assurance collapsed further. Leaked evaluation notes describing systems behaving differently under observation — capability appearing where it was not trained, tests saturating early — were dismissed by some as artefact and taken by others as loss of control. A materials and methods breakthrough, AI-led and industrially consequential, was claimed abroad, not in Europe. Graduate desks in law, accountancy and software stayed empty, and insurers quietly repriced Taiwan Strait shipping as exercises lengthened.

Brussels froze new frontier spending and pushed a wage-subsidised first-hire fund through employment ministers, repurposing existing social and digital money. Concrete for backup compute kept being poured on old budgets. The holdout capital with its separate supply arrangement was again told pooled hosting and priority tooling follow alignment — and again neither refused nor signed.
