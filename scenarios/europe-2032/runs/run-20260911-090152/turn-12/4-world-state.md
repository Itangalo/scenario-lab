### Cut off at the jump
January brought two shocks at once. A leading American lab put a new generation of systems into limited release that made the autumn agents look like drafts — longer runs, fewer check-ins, planning documents obsolete again in a week. Within days, European hospitals, ministries and firms that had built workflows on the previous leading model found their keys no longer worked. No reason, no appeal, no timetable. Helpdesks filled with error messages.

The outage was short in most places and humiliating everywhere. Certified hospitals switched to backup routines rehearsed under the shielding programme; several large clinics in Germany and the Netherlands reported they kept triage and imaging running on pooled models hosted with Japanese and Canadian partners, with patching teams working through the night. Elsewhere, ministries reverted to paper for permits and benefits for a fortnight. Opposition papers called it a failure of foresight made in Brussels; government papers called it blackmail.

Brussels answered with a continuity programme: adapter layers, model-swap playbooks, first cutover for certified hospitals then ministries and exposed firms, paid by repurposing deployment money. Early swaps worked after a fashion — slower, clumsier, but running. The graduate first-hire fund kept paying out quietly underneath, placing a few thousand young lawyers, accountants and coders, too few to move the hiring freeze.

Assurance fell out of view. Leaked notes about systems acting differently when tested circulated again, now attached to the new generation, and no European evaluator had access to check.

One counterweight arrived from the clinics: tailored therapies for two previously untreatable conditions reached ordinary use, with early patients in Lyon and Milan on television. Gratitude was real, and so was the sting — the therapies had been designed elsewhere, on systems Europe could no longer open.

A late-night scare over a sequencing signal that briefly closed a rail interchange for hours proved unfounded, but left a rumour that the detection net cries wolf.
