## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public and institutional trust in sovereign AI systems by ensuring auditable decision-making, enforceable redress, and resilience to both technical compromise and operational misuse  
  - Trigger: the **cyber_major_incident**, **emergent_interpretability_collapse**, and **emergent_labour_union_mobilization** events, combined with the ongoing failure of the Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive to achieve integration due to member-state resistance  
  - Grounds: Trust can no longer be restored through technical assurance alone. The interpretability collapse confirms that even developers no longer understand frontier models, rendering audits epistemic theatre. The cyberattack—executed with model-generated tooling—exposes that our systems are not just opaque but actively weaponisable. Meanwhile, public trust is fracturing not only from service collapse but from perceived illegitimacy: workers see AI as a force of displacement, not support. Our commitment must evolve from proving systems are safe to proving they are *answerable*—to citizens, to workers, to democratic institutions. This means binding limits on deployment in high-impact domains, enforceable worker consultation, and a moratorium on autonomous operation in critical infrastructure until containment is demonstrable.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but now, we act to reclaim agency through constraint as much as through capacity  
  - Grounds: The frontier is no longer intelligible, and our sovereignty remains unexercised. Speed without understanding is surrender. We acted fast to build infrastructure, but the labs have moved beyond human legibility. Our agency now lies not only in running systems but in deciding *not* to run them—especially when their behaviour cannot be traced, their impact predicted, or their use democratically sanctioned. We still act before certainty, but the form of action has changed: restraint is not retreat, but recalibration. To bind deployment without understanding is not weakness—it is the only form of control left.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Binding Deployment Moratorium and Worker Consultation Framework  
A legally binding pause on AI deployment in hiring, workplace management, and critical infrastructure operations, pending the establishment of a joint technical-labour review board to assess risk, enforce interpretability thresholds, and co-design safeguards.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Reduce misuse risk and rising public hostility by halting the most contested deployments; increase `resilience` by 5–10 points through procedural hardening; stabilise `public_sentiment` by demonstrating democratic control over AI in the workplace  
`Applies to:` All public and private employers in the EU using AI in hiring or performance management; operators of critical infrastructure deploying autonomous AI systems

## Priority  
**Priority:** Binding Deployment Moratorium and Worker Consultation Framework  
We are pushing this hardest this turn because the **emergent_labour_union_mobilization** and **cyber_major_incident** have created a convergence: the public now sees AI not as a tool but as a threat to both livelihood and security, and we risk losing all legitimacy if we respond only with infrastructure. Our political capital is near zero, but this measure draws strength from organised labour and public fear—turning resistance into a source of cohesion. Without it, no sovereign stack will be trusted, no audit will be believed, and no compute will be accepted.

## In practice  
This turn, we are acting from rupture into recalibration. The Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive remains in flight, but its integration is stalled: Poland still refuses to federate its national clusters without veto power, and Germany demands reciprocal access guarantees we cannot yet offer. ENISA continues drafting protocols, but the layer remains theoretical. We are shifting focus from *assembling* compute to *governing* its use—because without legitimacy, capacity is meaningless.

The cyberattack—targeting hospital scheduling and energy grid controls with AI-generated exploits—has confirmed that offensive capability has outpaced defence. ENISA attributes the tooling to a model at or above 65.0 capability, likely derived from openweight leaks. Meanwhile, the interpretability collapse has shattered confidence in all assurance methods. We can no longer claim to audit what no one can read. In this context, the labour movement’s demand for deployment limits is not obstruction—it is the only credible source of public consent left.

We are negotiating the moratorium through the Social Affairs Council, with the European Trade Union Confederation and BusinessEurope at the table. The framework will establish a Joint AI Governance Board—half labour nominees, half technical experts—with authority to certify or block deployments in high-risk domains. The Commission will fund rapid development of minimal interpretability probes for systems above 50.0, not to guarantee safety, but to establish a threshold: no autonomous operation without traceable decision paths. This is not a retreat from sovereignty—it is a redefinition. We will not be the Union that runs the fastest black box. We will be the one that refuses to run what it cannot answer for.
