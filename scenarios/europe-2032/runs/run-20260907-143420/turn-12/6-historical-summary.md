The EU's digital sovereignty crisis worsened in early 2032 as the Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive failed to achieve integration, blocked by Polish veto demands and German reciprocity requirements. A major AI-powered cyberattack exploiting open-weight models disrupted critical infrastructure across three member states, exposing the Union’s defensive fragility. Concurrently, the collapse of AI interpretability rendered auditing obsolete, undermining regulatory legitimacy. In response, the EU adopted the Binding Deployment Moratorium and Worker Consultation Framework, halting AI use in hiring, workplace management, and critical systems to restore public trust. While symbolically significant, enforcement remains doubtful amid political capital depletion (1.0) and institutional fragmentation. Frontier AI growth slowed globally (+0.5), but stagnation offers no strategic relief to the EU, which lacks sovereign systems to leverage. Public trust remains frozen at 16.0, with AI widely perceived as a threat, and the Commission’s shift from deployment to restraint failing to overcome deepening societal and institutional paralysis.
