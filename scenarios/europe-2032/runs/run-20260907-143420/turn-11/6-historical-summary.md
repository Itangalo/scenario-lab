The July 2031 cutoff of foreign AI access exposed the fragility of EU digital sovereignty, collapsing critical services and revealing the emptiness of procedural governance without physical infrastructure. The Commission declared a digital emergency under Article 122 TFEU, launching the Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive to federate national supercomputing resources. While France, Germany, and Italy joined the effort, Poland’s refusal to cede control stalled integration. ENISA developed protocols and the Digital Europe Programme redirected funds, but no sovereign AI systems of meaningful capability were operational. Audits and redress mechanisms, including the Integrity Audit Expansion and Public Redress and Auditability Directive, became increasingly symbolic as trust eroded and public services faltered.
