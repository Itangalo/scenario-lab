### The Fractured Response

The EU enters the second half of 2032 in reactive mode. The Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive collapses, undone by Polish resistance and German conditionalism. Only a fraction of pledged compute is federated; the vision of a sovereign execution layer remains unrealised. The failure underscores the Union’s institutional paralysis—urgent coordination is blocked not by technical limits, but by political fragmentation.

Simultaneously, the frontier shifts. Anomalous behaviour in a leading AI model—capabilities emerging without training—alarms developers, who dismiss it as a measurement glitch. Days later, a powerful open-weight model is released, showing unexpected robustness and tool-use proficiency. It spreads rapidly, downloaded by researchers, hackers, and state actors alike. While not matching frontier systems, it represents a clear step forward in accessible capability, nudging openweight upward.

In response, the EU proposes the Mandatory Incident Reporting and Containment Protocol under emergency powers, requiring rapid disclosure of AI anomalies. The measure targets transparency, not control—acknowledging that audits are obsolete and interpretability is gone. ENISA begins scoping a central hub; liability shields are proposed to incentivise compliance. However, no final adoption occurs this turn. BusinessEurope decries the proposal as bureaucratic overreach amid crisis. Member states offer rhetorical support but delay planning for implementation. The protocol remains in drafting, with enforcement mechanisms unresolved and the 50.0 threshold still undefined.

Public sentiment holds at 16.0. Workers see no relief—AI is still unsafe, still unaccountable. Unions demand binding human override, not just reporting. Civil disobedience grows around data centres, framed as acts of digital resistance.

### Capability Creeps, Control Erodes

Global AI capability rises by 0.5, reaching 70.0. The plateau is breaking. Openweight capability increases modestly to 53.8, reflecting gradual diffusion and incremental improvements in accessible models—not a single breakthrough. Offensive capabilities continue to spread, but not at an explosive rate. Resilience dips as no defensive measure lands. The gap between capability and safety widens—safety falls slightly as systems grow less predictable.

Sovereignty erodes further. Without sovereign compute, the EU’s leverage fades. The American posture of consolidation deepens dependency, rationing frontier access. No new capacity is secured.
