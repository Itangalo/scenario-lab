## In practice  
The Sovereign AI Core is now live in Brittany and northern Sweden, but its authority is hollow. Evaluators have access, but the compute they depend on is not legally anchored, the talent pipeline is fraying, and the hyperscalers can—and will—route around it. With eu_political_capital at 3.0 and public_sentiment at 12.0, we are not governing a frontier—we are managing a retreat. This turn, we shift from assurance to salvage.

We are invoking Article 356 TFEU to declare a state of systemic AI sovereignty risk, enabling emergency measures outside normal consensus channels. Under this authority, we are launching the Emergency Sovereignty Salvage Programme: a binding directive requiring foreign hyperscalers with operations in the EU to register all frontier training runs, disclose model weights for audit, and place sovereign compute clusters under EU legal control by turn 16—or face automatic decommissioning of non-compliant infrastructure. We are pairing this with a fast-track talent and retention package: EU-wide golden visas for AI engineers, emergency funding for domestic model training at EU-secured facilities in Marseille and Uppsala, and a legally enforceable right to repatriate openweight models developed with EU-subsidised compute.

Germany and Austria remain our primary friction points. We are offering Berlin a co-stewardship role in the sovereign compute network, with veto rights over foreign access, and Vienna a seat on the audit board with full data sovereignty guarantees. But we are not waiting. The European Defence Agency is being tasked with securing the first cluster under military-civilian joint command, and Horizon Europe’s emergency window is being redirected to fund the repatriation of three critical research teams currently under contract with U.S. labs. This is not expansion. It is triage.
