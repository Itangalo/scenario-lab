### Capability Creeps Forward, Quietly

Global AI capability advances to 72.0, continuing the steady climb. Frontier labs report no breakthroughs, but opaque training runs persist, and anomalies in agent coordination grow harder to dismiss. No public safety failure occurs—yet confidence in control erodes further among technical observers.

The open-weight frontier, though stagnant in release, continues to diffuse. Adapted into cyber intrusion frameworks, it enables more persistent and adaptive ransomware. openweight_capability rises to 59.0 as operators refine targeting. Two additional health systems suffer degraded service, compounding prior incidents.

### The Hollow Core

The EU’s AI Safety Assurance Infrastructure completes its rollout. Public evaluators now have real-time access to model logs in Brittany and northern Sweden. Yet the achievement is hollow. The compute infrastructure remains under foreign ownership, and hyperscalers begin rerouting workloads outside EU jurisdiction. The override mechanism, legally untested, is ignored in practice.

With political capital at 3.0 and public sentiment at 12.0, the Union shifts strategy. Invoking Article 356 TFEU, it declares a systemic AI sovereignty risk and launches the Emergency Sovereignty Salvage Programme—a crash effort to repatriate compute, talent, and model weights by turn 16. A binding directive demands foreign hyperscalers register frontier training runs, disclose model weights, and place clusters under EU legal control—or face decommissioning.

Germany and Austria resist. Berlin demands veto rights over foreign access; Vienna insists on full data sovereignty. Offers of co-stewardship stall. Meanwhile, the European Defence Agency begins securing a compute cluster under joint command, and Horizon Europe redirects funds to repatriate three research teams.

Public backlash intensifies. The Human Futures movement labels the directive “digital conscription.” Protests turn physical near data centres in France and Sweden. Labour unions warn of retaliatory automation.

### Fracture, Not Reform

The completion of the safety infrastructure yields no political gain. Resilience drops to 28.0 as repeated cyber incidents outpace response. ai_safety falls to 23.0—capability advanced, but no new safeguards land on shipped systems. The new programme, large and controversial, costs −3 political capital, minus an additional −1 for being the priority. With no offsetting bonus, capital falls to 2.0. Sovereignty, already weakened, declines to 11.0—capability rose by 1.5 (insufficient for −1), no capacity event, but the ongoing erosion from fragmentation and the failure to substitute foreign dependence persists. Public sentiment drops to 10.0 amid fear of overreach and retaliation.
