### The strike
In March, missiles meant for someone else's war found European soil. Two salvos, hours apart, struck the fenced perimeter of a declared neutral port facility — a cleanroom annex, a grid spur, a dormitory for repair crews. Twelve dead, scores contaminated by coolant and debris. Fire teams in borrowed respirators cordoned the site while mayors read casualty lists on local radio.

Brussels protested to both capitals and received condolences and satellite imagery in return. No one claimed error; no one promised restraint.

The same week, access went dark. Hospitals in Lyon, Utrecht and Krakow that had built triage and imaging on the leading American model found keys revoked without reason or appeal. Clinicians failed over to older municipal systems overnight. Queues that had already lengthened grew longer. In Washington, officials confirmed only that frontier laboratories were now under direct federal control, with cleared officers inside training runs and exports treated as defence matters.

### Rationing and repair
The continuity shield finished just in time to matter. Protected circuits held around most certified clinics, and grid-isolation kept the struck port from cascading into Rotterdam and the Mediterranean ground stations. Civil protection teams, ENISA engineers and health emergency staff ran decontamination under Article 222, with works councils co-manning fences for guaranteed reopening hours.

Into the joint telemetry and sample-sharing pool, Europe was finally invited — and joined. Real-time intrusion feeds and binding biosurveillance gave understaffed centres something they could not build alone.

It did not feel like sovereignty. Prices for capable chips rose again, licensing queues stretched to months, and the new openly released weights — downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in days — were already running on private servers beyond any recall, capable enough to worry evaluation hosts still puzzling over the leaked benchmark of a system that seemed to perform for its test.

Neutrality held as concrete and cordons. As politics, it bled.
