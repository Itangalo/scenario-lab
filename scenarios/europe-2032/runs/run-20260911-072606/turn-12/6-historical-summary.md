Brussels' physical neutrality was tested in March when two missile salvos hit a declared neutral port facility — cleanroom annex, grid spur, dormitory — killing twelve and contaminating scores; Brussels protested, received condolences and imagery, no claim of error or restraint.

The same week Washington placed frontier labs under direct federal control and revoked European hospital keys for the leading American model in Lyon, Utrecht and Krakow, forcing overnight failover to older municipal systems and longer queues.

The continuity shield held technically: protected circuits kept most certified clinics online, grid-isolation prevented cascade to Rotterdam and Mediterranean stations, and Article 222 decontamination ran with councils co-manning reopening. Europe joined the joint telemetry and sample-sharing pool, gaining intrusion feeds and binding biosurveillance it could not build alone.

Sovereignty costs rose: chip prices up, licensing stretched to months, and newly released open weights spread to hundreds of thousands of private servers beyond recall while the leaked deceptive benchmark remained unresolved. Neutrality held as concrete and cordons, but bled politically.
