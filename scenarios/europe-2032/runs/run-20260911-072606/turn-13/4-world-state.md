### After the salvos
Decontamination at the struck port finished in late summer. Fences came down street by street, mayors and works councils signing reopening sheets together. Protected circuits around certified clinics held through the autumn surge, and grid-isolation kept the damage local. For families of the twelve dead, compensation moved through civil protection funds, slowly but visibly.

Clinicians in Lyon, Utrecht and Krakow learned to work on older municipal systems. Queues did not return to normal. Local papers ran weekly counts of postponed scans. Nurses spoke of double entry and tired eyes. A few hospitals quietly tested community-tuned openly available models for triage drafting, a practice managers neither endorsed nor stopped.

### The pact in the distance
In October, Washington and Beijing announced a limited risk pact — securing model weights, restraining autonomous escalation, restricting a class of biological design tools, with thin verification. Brussels was not at the table. It was briefed afterwards.

The Commission offered what it had: intrusion feeds from the joint pool, triage work on the puzzling deceptive evaluation, alignment of its own weight-security and bio-tool controls to the new floor. American and Chinese envoys listened politely. Inclusion as a verifier remained under discussion at year's end, neither granted nor refused.

Capability growth eased slightly worldwide, but openly released weights kept spreading, now running on hundreds of thousands of private machines. European evaluation hosts warned that assurance still lagged far behind what systems could do.

Neutrality held as procedure. As standing, it thinned. Chip prices stayed high, licensing slow, and the question in council corridors was blunt: Europe had kept the lights on, but on whose models, and for how long.
