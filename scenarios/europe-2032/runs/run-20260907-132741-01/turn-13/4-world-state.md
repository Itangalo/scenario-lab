### The Weight of Failure

A major cyber incident struck multiple EU member states, exploiting AI-generated zero-day exploits in a widely used public-sector software dependency. Emergency response was fragmented: while the Emergency Cross-Border Incident Data Pool technically functioned—five of seven participating states transmitted telemetry—Poland and Hungary again withheld data, and real-time coordination collapsed under protocol gaps. The unified dashboard lit up, but only for Berlin and Paris. The attack disrupted hospital scheduling, transit systems, and local tax offices for days. Recovery was slow, and public trust eroded further.

Despite the crisis, the EU’s institutional paralysis deepened. The Public Trust Corps, though formally established, remained inactive—no budget, no staffing, no audit access. Its first transparency report, expected this turn, was delayed indefinitely. The completed Cyber Shield and Data Pool proved brittle in practice, offering visibility but no unified action.

In a rare sign of organic momentum, the emergent European distributed AI collective gained traction. Volunteers in Barcelona, Utrecht, and Ljubljana trained transparent models on municipal data, deploying them in schools and clinics. These systems were modest, but they worked—without foreign cloud dependencies or opaque APIs. The EU announced the **Distributed Open-Weight Resilience Programme**, pledging compute subsidies and sovereign cloud hosting. But funding was contingent on approval of a revised InvestEU-AI tranche, which the European Parliament deferred due to fiscal concerns. For now, the initiative remains symbolic.

Diplomatically, the Joint Middle-Power Evaluation Cell advanced in quiet. Legal teams from six nations finalized a draft MOU, and red-teaming facilities in Toulouse and Helsinki were certified. Yet no frontier developer agreed to submit models. Without enforcement leverage, the coalition’s credibility remained untested. The priority held, but momentum stalled.

### A System on Life Support

The EU demonstrated minimal continuity—packets moved, dashboards lit, meetings convened—but no measure landed with force. The cyber incident overwhelmed thin defences. Sovereignty and political capital remained in freefall. The world watched, but no one acted as if the Union still could.
