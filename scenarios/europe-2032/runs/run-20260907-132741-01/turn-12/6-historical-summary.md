The EU entered 2032 in a state of near-total institutional paralysis, but two factors provided fragile hope: signs of a plateau in frontier AI advancement and emerging alignment among middle powers in the global AI supply chain. The Emergency Cross-Border Incident Data Pool achieved technical completion, demonstrated during ENISA’s “Exercise Heraklion-2,” where four member states successfully transmitted encrypted telemetry, and Portugal followed after manual intervention. The unified dashboard activated for the first time, though analytics were selectively shared with Germany and France under a bilateral arrangement, while Poland and Hungary abstained.  

Preliminary negotiations began on a Joint Middle-Power Evaluation Cell with Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Nordic states, aiming to establish pre-release evaluations of frontier AI models through secure red-teaming hubs in Toulouse and Helsinki. A draft MOU circulated under the Digital Partnerships Council, but legal reviews delayed adoption, with no lab compliance achieved and first commitments expected only by mid-2032.  

The Public Trust Corps was formally created but remained inactive due to lack of funding, personnel, and audit access, postponing its first transparency report. Open-weight AI models advanced through diffusion of legacy techniques, narrowing the gap with frontier systems, while AI safety and interpretability saw no progress. Despite minimal functionality restored in cross-border data sharing, no policy or institutional measure delivered tangible improvements in resilience or public trust. Political capital remained at critical lows, though the partial success of Heraklion-2 halted further erosion, marking the first visible sign of systemic function in years—fragile, incomplete, but consequential.
