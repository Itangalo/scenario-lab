### A Fragile Pulse

The EU entered 2032 with near-zero operational capacity, but two developments shifted the atmosphere: signs that AI progress may have plateaued, and a quiet alignment among middle powers controlling key nodes in the global AI supply chain. This created just enough space to attempt stabilization.

The Emergency Cross-Border Incident Data Pool reached its scheduled completion date. ENISA launched “Exercise Heraklion-2,” a simulated ransomware strike targeting five member states. Belgium, Estonia, Finland, and the Netherlands transmitted encrypted telemetry within the 12-hour window. Portugal’s upload failed due to certificate mismatch, but was manually corrected and accepted after intervention by a Commission liaison. Five packets were verified — barely meeting the threshold for technical success. The unified dashboard lit up for the first time, though only Germany and France received early analytics under their side agreement. Poland and Hungary did not participate.

Negotiations began on a Joint Middle-Power Evaluation Cell with Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Nordic partners. A draft MOU circulated under the Digital Partnerships Council proposes coordinated pre-release evaluations of frontier models using secure red-teaming facilities in Toulouse and Helsinki. However, no lab has yet complied, and formal adoption remains months away. The initiative is under review by legal councils in all participating states, with first commitments expected no earlier than mid-2032.

The Public Trust Corps was formally established but remains in activation limbo, lacking staffing and budget. Its mandate is clear — to validate the integrity of cross-border data flows and issue public transparency reports — but it cannot operate independently until audit access is granted by national agencies. It will not issue a report on Heraklion-2 next turn, as originally hoped; instead, it will begin internal coordination and resource mapping.

Despite stalled capability at the frontier, open-weight models advanced significantly through diffusion of older techniques, closing the gap. No safety gains occurred organically, and no new policy or measure has yet produced measurable improvement in ai_safety. The evaluation initiative remains in negotiation and does not qualify as implemented oversight.

### Signs of Structure

For the first time in years, the EU demonstrated that something still functions across borders — narrowly, incompletely, but visibly. The data flow, however minimal, broke a cycle of total failure. Political capital remains critically low, with no new legitimacy lends or completed high-impact actions to offset prior charges. The coalition event and partial delivery prevented further erosion, but no recovery has begun.
