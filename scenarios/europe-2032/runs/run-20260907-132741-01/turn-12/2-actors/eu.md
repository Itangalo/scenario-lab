## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Abandon the current formulation due to de facto institutional paralysis; the Union lacks the political capital to enforce or implement any new measure, and public sentiment has collapsed to a level where even remedial justice is no longer credible without prior restoration of basic functionality and trust.  
- Grounds: This commitment was already revised last turn to reflect institutional collapse. It remains valid: we still lack enforcement credibility, fiscal headroom, and member-state cohesion. No development this turn alters that reality — if anything, the continued refusal of Poland and Hungary to join even minimal data-sharing confirms it. We hold this version.

- modify `survive_collapse` (commitment): Prevent total disintegration of EU AI governance by maintaining at least one functioning cross-border mechanism, preserving technical capacity in at least two member states, and retaining access to basic incident data across the Union — now expanded to include verified continuity of secure communication between national CERTs and ENISA, tested under live conditions.  
- Trigger: **capability_plateau_evidence** and **middle_power_coalition**  
- Grounds: The plateau signal changes our threat model: for the first time since 2028, the frontier may have stalled, giving us a narrow window to stabilise rather than merely react. Combined with the emergence of the middle-power coalition — which secures compute access and joint evaluation leverage — we are no longer purely in triage. But legitimacy remains zero. We must now use this breathing room not just to survive, but to demonstrate that something EU-level still works. Thus, we raise the bar: survival now requires not just data flow, but verified, exercised continuity of secure communication. If the data pool delivers visibility but fails under stress, it proves nothing.

- reclassify `act_under_uncertainty` to position  
- Trigger: **capability_plateau_evidence**, **middle_power_coalition**  
- Grounds: These developments resolve the uncertainty that gave this commitment its meaning. We now have external signals about capability trends and a new coordination vector with real leverage. Acting before clarity was necessary when paralysis was the alternative; now, clarity is within reach. We retain the principle, but as a tactic, not a binding promise. Our next moves will be based on evidence, not anticipation.

## Priority
**Priority:** Activate Emergency Cross-Border Incident Data Pool  
This remains our only viable lifeline because without verified situational awareness during an active incident, no higher-order function — including the new evaluation cell — can operate, and because its success this turn determines whether `survive_collapse` is met or failed.

## In practice
We are acting from near-zero. The Emergency Data Pool reaches its finishing turn now, and its activation is not automatic — it depends on whether participating CERTs actually transmit data under live conditions. We are treating this as a test: ENISA has issued a simulated ransomware alert tagged “Exercise Heraklion-2” to Belgium, Estonia, Finland, Portugal, and the Netherlands. Each is expected to upload anonymised telemetry within 12 hours. Two senior Commission officers are embedded in each node, ready to assist with formatting or encryption failures. Germany and France receive early pattern summaries if they comply; smaller states get immediate access to Public Trust Corps surge support. Poland and Hungary remain outside — but we do not challenge them. This turn, success means five packets received, decrypted, and displayed on a unified ENISA monitor by turn-end.

Simultaneously, we begin negotiations with Tokyo, Seoul, and Ottawa to launch the Joint Middle-Power Evaluation Cell. The proposal is simple: we bring legal jurisdiction over deployment, they bring niche expertise and access points; together, we demand evaluation access as a condition of export licences for advanced chips and software tools. A draft MOU will be circulated by the end of the month under the Digital Partnerships Council. No new funding — we repurpose €120m from stalled Trust Corps training modules. The Cell will not inspect training runs directly, but it will receive final weights for red-teaming, using secure facilities in Toulouse and Helsinki. This is not sovereignty yet — but it is the first time we are setting terms, not accepting them.

The Public Trust Corps completes its formation this turn. Its first act will not be public outreach, but internal validation: verifying the data pool’s uploads and preparing a live transparency report on Exercise Heraklion-2. If the data flows, we publish. If not, we admit failure — publicly. There is no third option.
