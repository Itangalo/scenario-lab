### A Quiet Turn, Heavy with Consequences

Global AI capability edges upward to 63.0, still within the projected 62–68 terminal zone, but the pace slows. Market signals suggest diminishing returns: new models deliver incremental gains at lower cost, yet even this modest advance widens the gap between capability and control. With no enforceable safeguards on frontier systems, and oversight fragmented across jurisdictions, **ai_safety** drops sharply by −3, now at 8.0—deep in the danger zone where deployed systems are opaque and misuse monitoring is nominal.

The EU formally closes the **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation**, acknowledging the failure to secure commitments from key member states on hosting sovereign frontier infrastructure. No new EU-controlled compute has been built. In its place, the Commission pivots to regulatory leverage, launching a Digital Markets Act review targeting energy transparency and audit rights over spare capacity. However, without ownership, this yields only limited influence. **eu_ai_sovereignty** declines to 25.0, losing −1 due to capability growth and another −1 from the lack of substitution as dependence hardens.

Meanwhile, the **Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration** concludes successfully. Six cities are now certified Trusted Resilience Nodes, with interoperability standards embedded in NIS2 compliance. The quiet delivery boosts **resilience** by +3, reaching 45.0—uneven but with core nodes now operationally hardened.

Politically, stagnation persists. With **eu_political_capital** at 6.0, no new measure is proposed. The priority—resilience consolidation—yields no acceleration, as capital remains below 12, nullifying priority effects. The portfolio charge is zero, and finishing the resilience measure brings a +2 boost, lifting capital to 8.0. Yet legitimacy erodes further: public sentiment dips to 30.0 as labour unrest intensifies and a high-profile election annulment fuels distrust in AI-mediated governance.

### Signals in the Static

Protests by displaced technical workers grow in frequency, framed increasingly as a crisis of fairness and inclusion. Media narratives sharpen around "AI colonialism"—the sense that decisions shaping Europe’s future are made elsewhere, enforced locally.
