### A Directive Ends, Not a Project

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation formally expires after failing to secure binding commitments from key member states. No new EU-controlled compute has been built. Germany maintains silence on military entanglement concerns; Ireland cites unresolved grid risks. The directive ends in legal form but not material effect.

Yet the EU signals a strategic pivot. Rather than abandoning leverage over compute, it begins exploring antitrust authority as a backdoor path to oversight. The Commission announces a preliminary Digital Markets Act review targeting dominant AI providers, with a focus on transparency around EU energy use and spare capacity. DG COMP, ENISA, and ACER are directed to begin scoping audit protocols, but no formal mandates are issued. The initiative remains in early drafting stages, with legal challenges anticipated and no immediate operational effect.

### Bottom-Up Resilience Gains Ground

With top-down sovereignty stalled, the EU consolidates momentum at the municipal level. Lyon, Barcelona, and Helsinki achieve formal interoperability under NIS2, earning certification as “Trusted Resilience Nodes.” These cities gain preferential access to future Digital Europe funds and emergency data flexibilities. Bologna, Ljubljana, and Tallinn are identified for integration using existing disaster recovery envelopes—no new budgets, no votes—but onboarding is delayed by governance gaps and pending risk assessments.

### Fractures Deepen, Safety Erodes

Two member states reject the European Evaluation Shield’s authority to impose safety mandates, citing legal overreach. Enforcement across the bloc freezes. Public trust in central oversight erodes further. With no finished safety measure landing on shipped systems and capability advancing, ai_safety declines, though not catastrophically—existing evaluation frameworks still provide partial containment, and open-weight models have not yet advanced significantly beyond prior baselines.

Frontier progress shows signs of plateauing—major releases underperform, and prices for top-tier models fall. Open-weight capability sees modest gains as prior leaks consolidate into more stable agent frameworks, but deployment remains constrained by infrastructure and integration bottlenecks.

Political capital rises slightly from completed portfolio relief and limited resilience progress, though still deep in paralysis range. No new initiative can be risked.
