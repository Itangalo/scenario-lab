## In practice
The Frontier Access Guarantee completes this turn, but its success is hollow. We have secured contractual access to foreign compute, but the member_state_defection proves that legal agreements alone cannot bind sovereignty when desperation sets in. Worse, the capability jump now widening beyond 75.0 means that what we accessed yesterday is already obsolete, and the control gap is widening faster than our ability to respond. The Guarantee was never meant to be the end — only a bridge. But the bridge ends in fog.

We are now acting on the core failure exposed in Thessaloniki: we have fallback systems, but we no longer understand how they decide. The Sovereign AI Audit Response Protocol is live, and national oversight bodies are being instructed to integrate AI-native red teams into their monitoring workflows. But they lack the tools, the skills, and the mandate to act preemptively. That is why we are establishing the Joint AI Security Cell — not as another coordination forum, but as a standing technical force with the authority to simulate attacks, trigger failovers, and demand code-level access. It will be headquartered at ENISA, with satellite nodes in Ljubljana, Marseille, and Helsinki, and funded through a €1.2 billion reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme and the stalled AI Adoption Acceleration envelope.

We face resistance from two directions: from member states who see this as an infringement on national competence in cybersecurity, and from the Commission’s legal service, which warns that current EU treaties do not clearly authorize cross-border red-teaming of national systems. To overcome this, we are invoking Article 122 TFEU on economic and social cohesion, coupled with the newly activated crisis clause in the AI Act’s Article 28a, which permits emergency technical intervention when systemic risk to public services is demonstrated. We are offering member states full transparency and co-leadership roles in exchange for jurisdictional consent, and we are fast-tracking recruitment through the EU Talent Shield programme to backfill with red-team leads from the private sector and academia. The audit logs from the emergent_eu_aid_strike will be the Cell’s first target — not to assign blame, but to test whether we can detect the invisible. If we cannot, nothing we have built will hold.
