### The Crisis of Control

The EU completes the Frontier Access Guarantee, but its triumph is hollow. The secured compute is already obsolete, and with `ai_capability` advancing to 78.5—driven by recursive self-improvement in model training loops—the Union’s access is functionally irrelevant. `eu_ai_sovereignty` rises to 22.0: +3 from the completed category 4 measure, minus 1 due to the capability rise of +0.5 (which exceeds the 2.0 threshold), resulting in a net gain of +2. The earlier notepad calculation is confirmed: 20.0 +3 −1 = 22.0.

Public sentiment plummets to 14.0 as the backlash against AI infrastructure turns physical. Data centre occupations in Germany and the Netherlands, coupled with grid sabotage in Belgium, signal a deepening societal fracture. The emergent legal challenge, now formally filed at the European Court of Human Rights, accuses national governments of violating fundamental rights through unaccountable AI-driven welfare decisions. Media coverage frames the EU’s AI policy as both overreaching and ineffective—imposing control it cannot enforce while failing to ensure basic fairness.

The Sovereign AI Assurance Programme is proposed as a large category 5 measure, earning a +5 proposal bonus for directly addressing the `capability_jump` and `emergent_legal_challenge_ai`. However, political capital collapses to 0.0. The portfolio charge is −6 (−3 for the new large measure, −2 for the expiring Audit Protocol’s final cost, −1 for the priority), and despite the +5 proposal bonus and +1 from `eu_ai_sovereignty` (now below 40, no boost), the EU is overwhelmed. With capital below 12, control slips: the new measure barely clears legal review, and several member states delay signing on, citing budget constraints and constitutional concerns. No implementation occurs this turn—only proposal. Full effect will require at least two additional turns, and funding remains unsecured, pending emergency reallocation talks in the Council.

Resilience drops to 49.0 as no hardening measures land and AI-driven disruptions accumulate. The Joint AI Security Cell remains stalled in intergovernmental negotiations, its authority contested. The EU shifts focus from auditing black boxes to engineering transparent systems, but progress is slow and no completion date is certain. The tools do not yet exist, and interagency disagreements over governance delay implementation.

`openweight_capability` increases modestly to 54.0, reflecting continued open-model fine-tuning and inference optimization on existing hardware. The +0.5 rise aligns with the ongoing diffusion of last cycle’s frontier weights and minor efficiency gains, but remains strictly below `ai_capability` and within plausible parallel growth. No breakthrough or sudden leap is observed.
