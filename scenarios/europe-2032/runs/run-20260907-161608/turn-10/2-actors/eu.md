## Statement changes

* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI continuity to ensure critical public functions remain operational during systemic shocks``  
  - Grounds: The two-year commitment period has closed, and while the intent remains valid, the current collapse of political capital and public sentiment renders the original formulation — already strained — operationally unenforceable. The commitment is retained in name but must now be recalibrated to reflect diminished capacity to compel compliance. Sovereign continuity is still the goal, but its execution must now proceed through persuasion, phased integration, and demonstrable utility rather than binding mandates backed by sanctions.

* ``- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement will now be conditional on the operational readiness of the Sovereign AI Core Programme, and we will not cut off a member state from critical capabilities unless we can offer a viable, secure alternative within 90 days. We act to defend the regime, not to punish into helplessness.``  
  - Grounds: With eu_political_capital at 0.0 and public sentiment at 20.0, enforcement mechanisms tied to fiscal penalties are politically toxic and institutionally unenforceable. The principle of no free rides remains, but the Union cannot credibly threaten cohesion fund suspensions when it lacks the capital to deliver on its own promises. The commitment is preserved, but its enforcement clause is effectively suspended until political capital recovers above 30.0 and a functioning alternative stack is demonstrably available.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.``  
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident and the continued erosion of ai_safety confirm that acting under uncertainty remains necessary — but the cost of error has become existential to the Union’s own cohesion. The commitment stands, but its expression must now be more surgically targeted: we will act early, but only where failure can be contained and reversal is possible. Broad, binding mandates are no longer tenable; we now prioritise pilot-based validation and reversible interventions.

## In practice

This turn, we are shifting from mandate to demonstration. The Continuity Assurance Framework remains in flight, but its authority is fraying. Two member states still refuse central certification, and the unannounced drill in Thessaloniki exposed not just technical flaws but a deeper crisis of confidence: public servants do not believe the backup systems will work, and citizens do not believe they should. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that locked emergency dispatch systems in three cities for 36 hours — proved the threat is real, but also that our defences are reactive, not resilient. The Frontier Access Guarantee continues to secure compute access through agreements with Canadian and Japanese hyperscalers, but those deals are now under review as export_control_escalation looms in Washington. We are holding the line, but barely.

So we are launching the Emergency Trust Restoration Initiative under the Digital Europe Programme and the AI Act’s public-interest derogation. We will deploy a hardened SACP stack — capability level 62.0, fully auditable — in three high-visibility, high-pain-point services: emergency medical triage in Marseille, real-time border processing in Ljubljana, and AI tutors in under-resourced schools in Athens. Each will be staffed by joint EU-national teams, monitored by the EAAD, and subject to weekly public reporting on performance and incidents. The goal is not perfection — it is proof. Proof that sovereign AI can work, safely, for people.

We are not adding new rules. We are not issuing new sanctions. We are showing, not telling. The €1.8bn repurposed from stalled adoption projects will fund this sprint. We are working through the Digital Ministers’ Council and the European Semester to align national budgets, and we are offering early access to the next SACP upgrade as an incentive for cooperation. The lights went out. Now we are lighting a fire.
