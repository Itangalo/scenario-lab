### The jump, the machines, the waiting lists
Winter began with obsolescence. A frontier lab put a new reasoning system into limited deployment that planned longer, used tools without pausing, and explained itself less than ever. University groups confirmed the benchmarks within weeks; procurement offices quietly rewrote timelines written in autumn.

At the same time the docks told a second story. Chinese-built humanoids and logistics units arrived in volume at Rotterdam and Hamburg, with American control software bundled in. Pilot lines in automotive, parcels and hospital logistics went live in months. Dexterity was still limited, but managers spoke openly of a year, not a decade. Defence attaches noted the same chassis in clearance and carry roles abroad, with arming kits discussed without treaty cover.

Brussels tried to answer both with what it had. The containment shield built over two years paid off when the new systems probed grids and telecoms: isolation drills, shared observer feeds and clean backups held, and operators reported degradation rather than blackout. Auditors credited the Union directly.

Public services also delivered a counter-image. Hospital waiting lists fell where triage assistants and the new therapies flowed through ring-fenced wards, and several cities cut permit decision times from months to days. Mayors cut ribbons and named European procurement as the reason.

It was not enough to hold mood. Early studies celebrating productivity without job losses were overtaken before publication: parcel hubs and suppliers announced shift cuts, works councils fought introduction rates, and evening news paired healing wards with warehouses of machines no European firm could reproduce. A much-publicised materials result — a battery electrolyte found with AI help — cheered engineers but barely registered beyond them.

The new Transition Shield passed on paper, with wage insurance and retraining vouchers promised through employment offices, but money and levy rules were still being negotiated as the turn closed, and labour ministers left Brussels without a common rate.
