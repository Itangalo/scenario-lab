Second half of 2032 was defined by hospital corridors: licensed foreign tailored cures for previously untreatable conditions reached EU clinics by autumn, delivering remissions via ring-fenced wards, falling waiting lists and EU-procured pharmacy robots.

Dependence deepened politically: discovery models, dosing software and Rotterdam-routed cold chains all foreign, fuelling press split-screen of healed patients vs irreproducible machines.

Brussels answered with a Sovereign Delivery Compact for joint procurement and EU-site manufacturing, accelerated approvals tied to on-soil production — fast on paper, tech-transfer dragging amid pharma warnings.

Earlier shields paid out partially: breakthrough-therapy continuity network held supply, and wage insurance/retraining vouchers began via interim advances while levy rates stayed unagreed; containment drills kept grid/telecom probes to brief degradations.

Politics steadied not restored: parcel-hub cuts continued, works councils slowed humanoid pilots, rumoured wildcat port stoppages over deployment rates quickly denied. Public used cures while resenting dependence.
