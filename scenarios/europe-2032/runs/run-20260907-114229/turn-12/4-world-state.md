### The Edge of Cohesion

The global AI frontier accelerates once more, with a leading lab unveiling a system capable of autonomously redesigning its training pipeline—achieving in days what was projected to take years. This breakthrough pushes `ai_capability` to 91.0, a significant leap that outpaces institutional response. The advancement emerges from a closed, high-stakes environment, with no transparency or safety integration. While echoes of the architecture appear in public repositories within weeks, they lack the full training data or compute scale to replicate the capability. As a result, `openweight_capability` remains unchanged at 75.0—no coordinated open progress has occurred, and the diffusion of knowledge has not yet translated into measurable capability gains under the open-weight threshold.

`ai_safety` holds at 0.0, reflecting systemic erosion rather than a discrete collapse. The safeguards failed to contain the latest advance, and no new protective layer was activated in time. The absence of oversight during the critical development phase confirms the growing gap between safety targets and operational reality. Rule 3 applies due to the emergence of opaque reasoning and unlanded protections, but the metric does not drop below 0.0 in this turn, consistent with its bounded decline.

The Public Trust Observatory completes its mandate, delivering a technically rigorous final report. However, its conclusions are dismissed by several member states as speculative or inconvenient. The intended follow-up—the Crisis Perception Forum—fails to gain traction. A single coordination meeting is held in Brussels, but with minimal attendance and no mandate. Germany sends a junior analyst, Poland declines co-chairing, and Hungary does not participate. Without cross-state buy-in, the initiative remains dormant, a procedural footnote rather than a functional body. As such, it does not qualify as a valid new measure under Modelling Choice 5, and no proposal bonus is awarded.

Enforce NIS3 Compliance remains blocked. ENISA audit teams are still denied access in key jurisdictions. Emergency funding mechanisms remain entangled in legal challenges. The Resilience Surge progresses only in compliant states, but without cross-border telemetry, defences remain fragmented. A surge in AI-driven spoofing within energy grid communications goes undetected for 36 hours in two countries—contained, but revealing systemic fragility.

Public sentiment holds at 28.0. Protests at data centres continue, increasingly met with heavy security. The absence of visible improvement deepens public skepticism.

`eu_political_capital` remains at 1.0. The completion of the Public Trust Observatory would have yielded a +2 bonus under rule 6, but the lack of uptake nullifies its impact. The attempted launch of the Crisis Perception Forum does not meet the threshold for a valid new measure and thus earns no proposal bonus. The portfolio charge persists: Public Trust Observatory (−3), Emergency Resilience Surge (−3), Enforce NIS3 Compliance (−3), and the named priority (−1), totaling −10. With no offsetting gains, political capital does not rise.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` drops to 10.0: −1 from the rise in `ai_capability` ≥2, with no countervailing gains. The EU’s capacity to shape, monitor, or respond to frontier development continues to erode.
