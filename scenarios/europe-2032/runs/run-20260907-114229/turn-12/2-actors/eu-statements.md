# Statements: The European Union (turn 12)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control
- `last_stand_for_cohesion` (commitment): From this moment, our primary objective is to reconstitute the European Union as a functioning political entity capable of collective action on existential threats. All measures must serve, or at minimum not undermine, this end. We will prioritise interoperability, enforce compliance with mutual aid obligations, and suspend contested legal interpretations that block coordinated defence. If we cannot act as one, we will fall as many.

The cost of this reversal is real: it concedes that enforcement cannot precede consensus, and will be read as retreat by those demanding decisive action. But the alternative — continued unilateralism in a context of zero trust — is not just ineffective; it accelerates disintegration.

  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us. **We now go further: we acknowledge that the Union itself is no longer operating as a coherent strategic actor. Fragmented data policies, divergent national risk tolerances, and paralysed decision loops mean that even when we act, we do not act as one. Thus, we redefine our identity not just around dual mandates, but around the imperative to *reconstitute the Union as a decision-making body capable of acting at the speed and scale of the threat*. Without this, no policy endures, no investment lands, and no commitment holds. And we now add: this reconstitution is not a precondition we wait for — it is the central act of governance in this period. Every measure, every instrument, every euro must be judged by whether it rebuilds the capacity for collective judgment and action. We are not preserving a system — we are rebuilding one.**
  - Grounds: The situation has moved categorically: the Union is no longer a unitary actor even in self-defence. The cyber_major_incident exposed not just technical flaws but a breakdown in the very possibility of shared situational awareness. The Public Trust Observatory’s findings are ignored not because they are weak, but because the epistemic infrastructure of the Union — the shared basis for knowing — has collapsed. This is not a policy failure but a civilisational one. The identity shift reflects that we are no longer managing a crisis within the existing framework — we are attempting to restore the framework itself.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
