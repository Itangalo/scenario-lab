## Statement changes  
- modify `last_stand_for_cohesion` (commitment): From this moment, our primary objective is to reconstitute the European Union as a functioning political entity capable of collective action on existential threats. All measures must serve, or at minimum not undermine, this end. We will prioritise interoperability, enforce compliance with mutual aid obligations, and suspend contested legal interpretations that block coordinated defence. If we cannot act as one, we will fall as many. **But we now accept: this cannot be enforced from the centre alone. The resistance in Germany, Poland, and Hungary is not merely legalistic — it reflects a deeper collapse of shared perception of risk. Therefore, we bind ourselves to a new condition: we will not only demand compliance, but we will establish a Crisis Perception Forum by turn-end, bringing together national intelligence, scientific, and civil protection leaders to jointly validate threat assessments before any further enforcement action is taken. No state will be compelled to act on a threat it does not see — but it will no longer be allowed to block action because it refuses to look.**
  - Grounds: Trigger: The political collapse following the attempted NIS3 enforcement — Germany’s constitutional challenge, Poland’s refusal to participate in Operation Iron Gate, Hungary’s full exit from mutual assistance — has not been overcome but deepened. The eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings are treated as speculative in key capitals, despite the Public Trust Observatory’s findings. This is not a failure of authority but of epistemic alignment. Without a shared understanding of what is happening, no legal instrument, however strong, will hold. The cost of this reversal is real: it concedes that enforcement cannot precede consensus, and will be read as retreat by those demanding decisive action. But the alternative — continued unilateralism in a context of zero trust — is not just ineffective; it accelerates disintegration.

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us. **We now go further: we acknowledge that the Union itself is no longer operating as a coherent strategic actor. Fragmented data policies, divergent national risk tolerances, and paralysed decision loops mean that even when we act, we do not act as one. Thus, we redefine our identity not just around dual mandates, but around the imperative to *reconstitute the Union as a decision-making body capable of acting at the speed and scale of the threat*. Without this, no policy endures, no investment lands, and no commitment holds. And we now add: this reconstitution is not a precondition we wait for — it is the central act of governance in this period. Every measure, every instrument, every euro must be judged by whether it rebuilds the capacity for collective judgment and action. We are not preserving a system — we are rebuilding one.**
  - Grounds: The situation has moved categorically: the Union is no longer a unitary actor even in self-defence. The cyber_major_incident exposed not just technical flaws but a breakdown in the very possibility of shared situational awareness. The Public Trust Observatory’s findings are ignored not because they are weak, but because the epistemic infrastructure of the Union — the shared basis for knowing — has collapsed. This is not a policy failure but a civilisational one. The identity shift reflects that we are no longer managing a crisis within the existing framework — we are attempting to restore the framework itself.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Establish Crisis Perception Forum  
A permanent, rotating council of national technical, intelligence, and civil protection leads to jointly validate AI-related threat assessments before Union-level enforcement or emergency action can proceed.

`Category: 2 (Transparency and reporting)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_political_capital by 5–8 points by rebuilding epistemic trust; reduce obstruction to enforcement by grounding action in shared perception; lay groundwork for future binding decisions  
`Applies to:` All member states, with initial focus on Germany, Poland, and Hungary

## Priority  
**Priority:** Enforce NIS3 Compliance Across Member States  
Because the Union’s ability to defend itself hinges on real-time data flow, and because every day without it deepens the technical and political fragmentation that makes us collectively ungovernable — this measure remains the operational fulcrum, even as we acknowledge it cannot succeed without the Crisis Perception Forum to legitimise it.

## In practice  
We are acting on two tracks: enforcement and epistemic repair. On the first, the Enforce NIS3 Compliance measure continues. ENISA has issued formal non-compliance notices to Germany, Poland, and Hungary, citing Article 14 TEU and the ongoing declared emergency under Article 172 TFEU. The Commission has initiated the process to suspend CEF Digital and Horizon Europe funds tied to cybersecurity cooperation, though legal services warn disbursement mechanisms remain contested. Mobile audit teams are on standby, but access is denied. The Emergency Resilience Surge proceeds in compliant states — France, the Netherlands, and Finland have implemented zero-trust upgrades in health and energy sectors — but the lack of cross-border telemetry means detection remains siloed and slow.

On the second track, we are standing up the Crisis Perception Forum by turn-end. The General Secretariat of the Council is convening a special session of the Cybersecurity Board and the EU Intelligence and Situation Centre (INTCEN), with mandatory attendance from national AI safety coordinators, CERT leads, and biosecurity directors. The first agenda item: joint review of the Public Trust Observatory’s final report on eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings. The goal is not consensus — it is *recognition*: that the data exists, that the risks are plausible, and that unilateral denial cannot block collective preparation. We are offering Germany a bilateral protocol on data anonymisation in exchange for participation; Poland will be invited to co-chair the bio-risk working group; Hungary will be offered reintegration into Europol pipelines if it rejoins mutual assistance on a provisional basis. This is not capitulation — it is the only path to restoring the Union as a decision-making body. Without it, every instrument we hold becomes a weapon in an internal war, not a shield against external threat.
