# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control
- `last_stand_for_cohesion` (commitment): From this moment, our primary objective is to reconstitute the European Union as a functioning political entity capable of collective action on existential threats. All measures must serve, or at minimum not undermine, this end. We will prioritise interoperability, enforce compliance with mutual aid obligations, and suspend contested legal interpretations that block coordinated defence. If we cannot act as one, we will fall as many.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us. **This turn, we go further: we acknowledge that the Union itself is no longer operating as a coherent strategic actor. Fragmented data policies, divergent national risk tolerances, and paralysed decision loops mean that even when we act, we do not act as one. Thus, we redefine our identity not just around dual mandates, but around the imperative to *reconstitute the Union as a decision-making body capable of acting at the speed and scale of the threat*. Without this, no policy endures, no investment lands, and no commitment holds.**
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident exploited not only technical vulnerabilities but institutional ones: Germany and Poland blocked access to logs needed for coordinated defence; Europol withheld data citing national protocols; Operation Iron Gate was delayed despite emergency powers. These were not failures of intent — they were structural fractures in the very mechanism of Union action. The emergent_researcher_desertion_wave compounds this: talent flees not because of ideology, but because the EU cannot offer a functional research environment. This is not a failure of policy design — it is a failure of governance coherence. The situation has moved categorically beyond managing trade-offs between autonomy and safety. We are now fighting for the survival of the actor that must make those choices.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act blind. This turn, the eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings are not proof of imminent catastrophe, but they are proof that the systems we rely on are no longer legible even to their creators. We are no longer merely acting under uncertainty — we are acting inside a fog of our own making. Therefore, we bind ourselves further: we will not only act early, but we will demand that every major commitment be paired with a public, auditable theory of harm and a corresponding monitoring mechanism. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher. **We now extend this: from this turn forward, any measure that costs more than 2 political capital per turn must include a built-in sunset clause tied to demonstrated effectiveness within 12 months, or lose funding automatically. We will still move early, but we will no longer subsidise inertia in the name of urgency.**
  - Grounds: The Emergency Resilience Surge is costing precious capital in an environment where eu_political_capital = 1.0 — yet early implementation reports show patchy compliance, legal delays, and resistance from member states invoking sovereignty. Without a mechanism to terminate failing initiatives, we risk locking in dysfunction under the guise of crisis response. The commitment evolves not because we doubt the need to act, but because we can no longer afford unaccountable action. The cost of reversal is real — it signals hesitation to allies and emboldens obstructionists — but the cost of perpetual drift is annihilation.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger

There is no credible path to independent advancement while the Union cannot coordinate its own emergency response. To continue claiming this commitment would be performance, not policy. We abandon it not because the goal was wrong, but because the conditions for its fulfillment have been destroyed. The cost is legitimacy: markets will read this as retreat, voters as surrender, and developers as irrelevance. But pretending otherwise costs more.
