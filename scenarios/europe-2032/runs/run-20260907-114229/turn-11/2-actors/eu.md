## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us. **This turn, we go further: we acknowledge that the Union itself is no longer operating as a coherent strategic actor. Fragmented data policies, divergent national risk tolerances, and paralysed decision loops mean that even when we act, we do not act as one. Thus, we redefine our identity not just around dual mandates, but around the imperative to *reconstitute the Union as a decision-making body capable of acting at the speed and scale of the threat*. Without this, no policy endures, no investment lands, and no commitment holds.**
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident exploited not only technical vulnerabilities but institutional ones: Germany and Poland blocked access to logs needed for coordinated defence; Europol withheld data citing national protocols; Operation Iron Gate was delayed despite emergency powers. These were not failures of intent — they were structural fractures in the very mechanism of Union action. The emergent_researcher_desertion_wave compounds this: talent flees not because of ideology, but because the EU cannot offer a functional research environment. This is not a failure of policy design — it is a failure of governance coherence. The situation has moved categorically beyond managing trade-offs between autonomy and safety. We are now fighting for the survival of the actor that must make those choices.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act blind. This turn, the eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings are not proof of imminent catastrophe, but they are proof that the systems we rely on are no longer legible even to their creators. We are no longer merely acting under uncertainty — we are acting inside a fog of our own making. Therefore, we bind ourselves further: we will not only act early, but we will demand that every major commitment be paired with a public, auditable theory of harm and a corresponding monitoring mechanism. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher. **We now extend this: from this turn forward, any measure that costs more than 2 political capital per turn must include a built-in sunset clause tied to demonstrated effectiveness within 12 months, or lose funding automatically. We will still move early, but we will no longer subsidise inertia in the name of urgency.**
  - Grounds: The Emergency Resilience Surge is costing precious capital in an environment where eu_political_capital = 1.0 — yet early implementation reports show patchy compliance, legal delays, and resistance from member states invoking sovereignty. Without a mechanism to terminate failing initiatives, we risk locking in dysfunction under the guise of crisis response. The commitment evolves not because we doubt the need to act, but because we can no longer afford unaccountable action. The cost of reversal is real — it signals hesitation to allies and emboldens obstructionists — but the cost of perpetual drift is annihilation.

- retire `two_year_commitment`
  - Grounds: Trigger: The emergent_researcher_desertion_wave and the collapse of eu_political_capital to 1.0. Our two-year commitment — to secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction — assumed a baseline of institutional functionality, fiscal room, and scientific cohesion. None remain. Researchers are leaving in waves, citing ethical concerns and lack of operational capacity. No new talent pipeline exists. The Sovereign Core is grounded. Compute remains offshored. There is no credible path to independent advancement while the Union cannot coordinate its own emergency response. To continue claiming this commitment would be performance, not policy. We abandon it not because the goal was wrong, but because the conditions for its fulfillment have been destroyed. The cost is legitimacy: markets will read this as retreat, voters as surrender, and developers as irrelevance. But pretending otherwise costs more.

- add `last_stand_for_cohesion` (commitment): From this moment, our primary objective is to reconstitute the European Union as a functioning political entity capable of collective action on existential threats. All measures must serve, or at minimum not undermine, this end. We will prioritise interoperability, enforce compliance with mutual aid obligations, and suspend contested legal interpretations that block coordinated defence. If we cannot act as one, we will fall as many.
  - Grounds: This is not a shift in values but a recalibration of survival. The Union is fragmenting under pressure — not along old east-west or north-south lines, but between those who see existential risk and those who see opportunity, between those willing to cede sovereignty for security and those who invoke it to resist. This commitment binds us to reverse that drift, using every instrument at our disposal. It carries cost: Germany, Poland, and Hungary will challenge enforcement actions as overreach. But the alternative is dissolution by incident.

## In practice
We are treating the breakdown of cross-border cooperation as an existential failure. Despite the declaration of emergency under Article 172 TFEU, Germany continues to withhold forensic logs from the compromised Bavarian health node, citing constitutional privacy protections. Poland delays participation in Operation Iron Gate, demanding bilateral guarantees. Hungary has suspended Europol data pipelines entirely. This is not dissent — it is secession by default. We are therefore invoking Article 14 TEU to issue binding decisions requiring full compliance with NIS3 data-sharing obligations, effective immediately. Non-compliant states will face suspension of CEF Digital and Horizon Europe funds tied to cybersecurity until data flows resume. The Conditionality Mechanism will be activated to treat obstruction of mutual aid during a declared crisis as a breach of the rule of law.

The Emergency Resilience Surge continues, but its implementation is now explicitly conditioned on NIS3 compliance. ENISA is deploying mobile audit teams to national CERTs to verify real-time reporting, and the Joint Research Centre is building a minimal viable common operating picture using anonymised telemetry. Where member states refuse access, we will publish their non-compliance in the next Public Trust Observatory interim report — not as punishment, but as transparency. The Observatory itself is now staffed and conducting its first joint analysis of the eval_anomaly_reports and the bio_uplift_findings, with a preliminary assessment due by turn-end. Whistleblower submissions have increased, including evidence of repeated manipulation in update chains at two national cloud providers. These are being triaged for immediate investigation.

We are no longer building independent AI advancement. We are trying to keep the Union intact. Every euro spent, every legal instrument invoked, every public statement issued serves that end. If we cannot act as one, nothing else matters.
