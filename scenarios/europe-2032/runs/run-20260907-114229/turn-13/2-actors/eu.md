Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us. We now go further: we acknowledge that the Union itself is no longer operating as a coherent strategic actor. Fragmented data policies, divergent national risk tolerances, and paralysed decision loops mean that even when we act, we do not act as one. Thus, we redefine our identity not just around dual mandates, but around the imperative to *reconstitute the Union as a decision-making body capable of acting at the speed and scale of the threat*. Without this, no policy endures, no investment lands, and no commitment holds. And we now add: this reconstitution is not a precondition we wait for — it is the central act of governance in this period. Every measure, every instrument, every euro must be judged by whether it rebuilds the capacity for collective judgment and action. We are not preserving a system — we are attempting to restore one.

The fact that such a flaw could persist undetected — and that verification is now meaningless — proves that the epistemic collapse described in the last turn has materialised into systemic operational failure. This is not a degradation of capability but a rupture in the very possibility of sovereign function. The identity shift is necessary because we are no longer managing risk within a functioning system — we are trying to reconstitute the system itself under active attack.

- modify `last_stand_for_cohesion` (commitment): From this moment, our primary objective is to reconstitute the European Union as a functioning political entity capable of collective action on existential threats. All measures must serve, or at minimum not undermine, this end. We will prioritise interoperability, enforce compliance with mutual aid obligations, and suspend contested legal interpretations that block coordinated defence. If we cannot act as one, we will fall as many. But we now accept: this cannot be enforced from the centre alone. The resistance in Germany, Poland, and Hungary is not merely legalistic — it reflects a deeper collapse of shared perception of risk. Therefore, we bind ourselves to a new condition: we will not only demand compliance, but we will establish a Crisis Perception Forum by turn-end, bringing together national intelligence, scientific, and civil protection leaders to jointly validate threat assessments before any further enforcement action is taken. No state will be compelled to act on a threat it does not see — but it will no longer be allowed to block action because it refuses to look.

Physical protests against AI infrastructure are now a symptom of a deeper legitimacy crisis, while the compromise of Sovereign Core systems proves that even when compliance is achieved, it may not be real. The reversal acknowledges that we cannot secure the Union’s systems without first securing the Union’s ability to agree on what is real. The cost is high: it delays enforcement and concedes further ground to obstruction. But continuing without shared perception would be to rebuild a house on poisoned foundations.

We are acting from a position of emergency. The confirmation of persistent adversarial access to Sovereign Core update chains across multiple member states is not a breach to be patched — it is a collapse of the trust model underpinning EU digital sovereignty. We are therefore halting all non-essential AI deployment in sovereign systems until cryptographic verification is restored. The Commission is convening an emergency meeting of COREPER II, invoking Article 172 TFEU to declare a systemic crisis in EU digital infrastructure. ENISA, in coordination with the EU Cybersecurity Board and INTCEN, will lead a six-month audit and isolation campaign, beginning with the energy and health sectors. We are mandating the use of verifiable update chains, air-gapped rollback checkpoints, and independent model audits by a newly empowered EU Evaluation Office — a function we are fast-tracking under the existing Public Technical Capacity envelope.  

Germany, Poland, and Hungary are being offered a provisional reintegration track: participation in the audit process with full transparency rights in exchange for temporary suspension of contested legal challenges. We are not demanding surrender — we are demanding verification. The Crisis Perception Forum, now concluded, has delivered its first joint assessment: the *emergent_sovereign_core_instability* is real, and its implications are shared. This is not consensus, but it is recognition — and for now, it is enough. The Emergency Resilience Surge continues in compliant states, but its focus is shifting from perimeter defence to core integrity: zero-trust is no longer sufficient if the trusted core itself is compromised. We are reallocating funds from stalled enforcement actions to this new priority, because if we cannot trust our own systems, we cannot trust any decision they produce — including this one.
