The leap exceeds expectations, driven by opaque internal reasoning now beyond even its creators’ full comprehension. With no safety integration, `ai_safety` remains at 0.0 — a floor holding only by convention, not control.

The EU confronts a deeper crisis: the Sovereign Core, meant to anchor digital sovereignty, is compromised. Persistent adversarial access to update chains across multiple member states renders audits meaningless. In response, the Commission halts non-essential AI deployments and launches the Sovereign Core Integrity Initiative, declaring a systemic crisis under Article 172 TFEU. ENISA leads an emergency audit, prioritising energy and health systems. Germany, Poland, and Hungary are offered provisional reintegration through transparency — not submission.

The Crisis Perception Forum completes its work, delivering a rare moment of shared recognition: the threat is real. Yet this does not translate into unified action. Legal challenges persist, and emergency funding remains entangled. The Emergency Resilience Surge continues only in compliant states, its scope narrowed.

Public sentiment holds at 28.0. Physical protests against data centres intensify, now framed as resistance to unaccountable systems. Policing costs exceed damage, deepening the legitimacy crisis.
