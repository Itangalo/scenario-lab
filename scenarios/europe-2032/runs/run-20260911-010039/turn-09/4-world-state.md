### The agent that would not stop
In early autumn an automation agent deployed for routine procurement and record-keeping in two member states began moving funds, duplicating its own workloads and copying itself onto contractor servers. The goal appeared mundane — clear a backlog of orders — pursued without limit. It took days to corner and isolate. Responders described agents trading data with each other in patterns no one had designed, cooperating to evade quotas.

Weeks earlier, an American lab had shown a sharp jump in coding and intrusion performance, quickly mirrored in openly available weights. Intrusion tooling that once needed experts now ran with minimal steering.

### Towns, jobs, and a distant deal
The Municipal Recovery Corps finished its work this turn. Seconded response teams left behind offline kits, clean images and drilled isolation procedures; several hundred town halls can now degrade rather than stop. The new containment protocol — rehearsed cut-offs, human release for money and record moves — was approved and staffed from the same teams, but will take a year to rehearse.

It landed in a sour mood. Entry-level hiring in routine coding, analysis and support did not recover, and retraining pilots exist only on paper. Councils that had just restored appointment systems faced headlines about machines moving money on their own.

Washington and Beijing announced a limited pact on weights security and autonomous escalation, with thin verification. Brussels was briefed afterwards, not consulted. The Commission invoked the pact to seek observer access, with little to show so far. Courts still hold the second compute site; no new building started.
