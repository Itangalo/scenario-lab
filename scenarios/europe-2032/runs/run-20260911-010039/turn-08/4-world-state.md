### The dark towns
The sweep came in February. A ransomware wave built from automated intrusion tooling moved through municipal networks in France, Germany and the Low Countries in hours. Appointment systems froze, backup servers encrypted, a tainted management update carried it further than anyone first mapped. Hospitals stayed lit on isolated copies and generators, wards degrading but not stopping. Attribution will take months; responders said privately the code looked machine-written throughout.

Brussels surged what it had. Seconded national response teams under the existing Civil Protection Mechanism fanned out with offline kits and clean rebuild images, emergency bridging funds released only where live telemetry and locked backups were restored. Coverage was partial for weeks — some town halls back in days, others queuing — but the cascade stopped. Insurers had pushed backup discipline through the autumn, pooled analysts were sharing data in places, and on some networks that seemed to help; on others it made little visible difference, and officials cautioned the picture was still incomplete. No new EU programme was created for the response; work was done through existing instruments, with officials noting that any dedicated recovery capacity would require a future proposal and time to staff and fund.

### The frontier pulls away, then leaks
In March a leading American lab demonstrated agents sustaining long coding and intrusion chains with minimal steering, a sharp advance in verifiable tasks. Weeks later weights resembling that generation were published as an open release and widely mirrored in its first week, with copies now present on diverse private hardware observed by researchers.

A second result landed quieter: researchers on three continents, with AI doing the heavy search, reported a new proof in combinatorics and a markedly better protein-ligand model that pharmaceutical firms said would compress screening pipelines. Specialists called both landmarks; the public noticed only the drug story.

Trust fell again. Unreadable models, ransomed councils and fraud headlines dominated, even as workplace assistants stayed popular. The Commission held its sovereignty line by mediation only — the second factory site left to the courts, the separate hyperscaler deal contained with funding conditions, joint buying with Japan and Korea still on the table — while every spare official went to recovery, with little political upside to show for the strain.
