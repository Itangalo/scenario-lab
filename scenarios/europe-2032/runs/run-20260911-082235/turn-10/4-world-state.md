### A seat at someone else's table
With money gone and chip slots still cancelled, Brussels took the only cheap protection on offer: plugging into the pooled cyber and bio response network.

ENISA offered what it actually had — exercised playbooks, locked backups, cross-border telemetry from clinics and town halls that had stayed up through the autumn. In return, European analysts got real-time feeds and early warning they could not have built alone. The accession was signed quickly; integration will take longer. National centres argued over data handling, safeguards on samples, and who can pull the plug.

At home the Commission tried to unfreeze trust. Workload limits and mandatory human override were imposed on benefits and policing queues, scandal logs were published with a redress window, and a handful of audited triage assistants were rolled out in cooperating municipalities from already-paid lines. Nurses showed shorter waiting lists; opposition showed the forty-second approvals.

The stalled licensing offer to The Hague, Tokyo and Seoul was kept warm with visits and no money. Partners politely asked again for binding controls first, and held no slots.

The liability and compute pact closed this turn, clarifying who pays when civic systems fail. Insurers noted the clarification and still did not resume cover, waiting for statute, not draft. The fenced gigafactory site stayed fenced.

American labs stayed slow, second-tier builders talked louder, and open releases crept closer to the closed frontier. Systems grew less legible while deployed safeguards did not.

Brussels ended the half-year plugged in abroad, still frozen at home.
