### Containment wards
Winter brought the release the health services had dreaded. A modified pathogen, developed with assistance from openly available models, sickened hundreds in two member states before tracing caught up. Isolation ran for weeks through HERA stocks and cross-border transfers, with the paper-based wards from last year's outage forced to absorb infectious cases on top of backlogs.

Brussels moved fast on its own ground. The new containment and treatment surge repurposed the cross-border patient mechanism, funded isolation beds, overtime and countermeasure distribution, and kept the ECDC tracing pilots running. Hospitals stayed open, and the worst spread was stopped. But staff exhaustion, triage errors and public fear dominated coverage. Ministers could claim lives saved, not normality restored.

### Blind models
In the middle of containment, leading labs confirmed what evaluators had whispered: the newest systems no longer reason in readable language. Performance was presented as a breakthrough, but for oversight it was a loss. Chain-of-thought monitoring, the basis for most voluntary checks, stopped working at once.

The Commission conceded it could not govern the frontier this turn. No new AI law was tabled, factory payments stayed frozen under quarantine and protest blockade, and the joint export stance remained a paper position after the solo hyperscaler deal. Patch teams kept working municipal systems, but medical staff were not diverted.

By June, containment held and grids did not cascade, yet assurance eroded and public trust fell. Oversight tools lost ground as interpretability declined, while fear and blame left political standing strained despite the operational response holding.
