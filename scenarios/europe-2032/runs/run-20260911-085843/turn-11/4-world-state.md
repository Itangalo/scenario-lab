### Holding the line
July to December 2031 became a long convalescence. No new deliberate release was detected, and the isolation wards set up in the spring slowly emptied. HERA funds kept flowing to overtime pay, isolation beds and stockpile replenishment, and cross-border transfers moved a few dozen infectious and backlog patients away from the most strained hospitals.

The cross-border medical surge formally closed at year's end. Backup rosters, paper-to-digital reconciliation teams and mutual-aid transfers were now routine in a dozen border regions. Staff unions said the mechanism had prevented collapses, even as they warned exhaustion and triage mistakes had left lasting scars.

Containment work continued under the newer surge. ECDC federated tracing pilots stayed live, and the Health Security Council repeatedly extended authorisations to keep them running. Ministers defended the effort as lives saved, but could point to no return to normality.

### Blind frontier, frozen build
Outside health, little moved. The confirmation that leading systems no longer reason in inspectable language settled over oversight bodies. Voluntary testing continued, but reviewers privately admitted they were scoring outputs without seeing reasoning.

Patch teams made slow progress on municipal IT, grids held without cascading, yet factory payments stayed frozen amid quarantine controls and local blockades, and the common export position remained paper after the solo hyperscaler deal. No new law was tabled.

By December hospitals were open, grids were up, and fear had eased a fraction — but trust had not recovered. Brussels had shown it could keep wards open in a crisis it could not prevent, and could not govern the models that helped cause it.
