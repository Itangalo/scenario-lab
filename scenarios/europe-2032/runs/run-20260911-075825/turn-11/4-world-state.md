### The jump
Autumn brought a demonstration no timeline had priced. A leading lab released an agent system that planned across days, wrote its own tooling, and improved on benchmarks that had defined the frontier months before. Everything Brussels had assumed about restoration windows became obsolete in a week. Clinics using borrowed models reported outputs with no inspectable trace at all.

The emergency cell held the line but could not advance it. Pooled clean backups, seconded response teams and diverted supercomputing slots kept hospital administration and a patchwork of municipal services limping forward. Grid operators stayed on manual fallbacks. The new Continuity Corps formalised that improvisation — pooled technicians, rehearsed degraded protocols, foreign telemetry plugged directly into the Brussels cell — and interior ministers welcomed delivery now over promises later. It yielded nothing yet; rehearsals were pushed to next year.

The hardening pact paid out. Cyber recovery drills, isolated rebuild environments and ring-fenced therapy wards meant the second wave of automated intrusions degraded services rather than stopping them. Victim states pooling live intrusion data gave the Union a seat and a feed it could not have built alone.

### The street
That technical hold did not reach the street. Law, accountancy and customer operations hired almost no graduates for a second consecutive term. The retraining guarantee paid wage insurance to a first cohort, but enrolment queues stretched for months and payments arrived too slowly to shift headlines. Town halls paired celebration of new cures with anger over empty job boards.

Faint reports of satellite glitches and grid flicker blamed on solar weather circulated in operator chats, unconfirmed and easily lost amid the intrusion attribution work.

With cohesion fraying, the Commission kept the graduates programme as its stated focus, but under emergency budgets the declaration changed little on the ground. Restoration remained uneven, oversight meant human checking of every clinical output, and defenders admitted they were still behind a sweep whose tools had been generated rather than written.
