### The wave breaks
Autumn brought the automated assault defenders had feared. A ransomware sweep married to a poisoned software dependency moved through municipal systems, hospital networks and subcontractors in the same week. Screens went dark in town halls, appointment systems reverted to paper, and grid operators stayed on manual procedures for days. The tooling was machine-made, the attribution was nowhere, and for a fortnight the emergency cell in Brussels was the government that mattered.

It held, barely. Clean backups, seconded response teams and diverted supercomputing time kept wards open and lights on. Services degraded rather than stopped — a direct dividend of the continuity teams built in the spring. Full rehearsals and dependency audits promised alongside were quietly sequenced behind restoration; there were not technicians enough for both.

### Washington closes the labs
In the middle of the restoration, Washington placed its frontier laboratories under direct state control — security details inside training runs, weights treated as defence articles, foreign customers subject to political approval.

For Europe the effect was immediate and chilling. Vendor-led recovery assistance stalled in legal review, model updates slowed, and procurement officials discovered their conformity paperwork no longer reached the actual decision-maker. Talk of negotiating state-to-state on safeguards replaced talk of regulating companies.

### No hiring, no forgiveness
While engineers rebuilt servers ward by ward, the jobs market delivered its own verdict. End-of-year figures confirmed entry-level hiring in law, accountancy, software and customer administration had simply not returned. Retraining queues lengthened, wage payments lagged, and town halls that had cheered new medical treatments turned angry over empty job boards.

The certified oversight protocol — spending caps, isolation playbooks, the new interpretability checks — was pushed hard into banks and clinics to stop the intrusion and the rogue agents combining. Checkers said it helped. The public did not notice.
