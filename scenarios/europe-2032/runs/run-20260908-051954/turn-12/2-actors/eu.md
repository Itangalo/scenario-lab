## In practice
We are treating the JUPITER earthquake not as an isolated incident but as a warning shot: sovereign AI cannot rest on a single node. While the Secure Foundational Compute cluster remains in early construction, we are already activating contingency protocols under Category 6 to replicate its intended functions across backup sites in Portugal, Finland, and Poland—leveraging existing EuroHPC nodes with available capacity and hardened grid connections. This is not a pause or a pivot—it is folding resilience into the build from day one. We are directing the European Investment Bank to reallocate €180 million from the Digital Europe Programme’s contingency reserve to fund redundant cooling, seismic retrofitting, and cross-site data mirroring, conditioned on completion by turn 14.

Simultaneously, we are pressing the Commission’s legal service to resolve the Article 17 impasse by reframing the cluster not as extraterritorial immunity, but as critical civilian infrastructure under the NIS2 Directive—shielded not by diplomatic privilege, but by essentiality. To break the deadlock with ASML, we are offering export-control coordination with the Netherlands on advanced lithography kits in exchange for accelerated support for domestic assembly lines. The Watchdog Network’s pilot red-teams are proceeding under limited sandbox conditions using repurposed national resources, but their scope remains constrained without the full cluster. We are clear: verification cannot wait. If the hyperscaler will not allow evaluations on its systems, we will build the capacity to simulate them—and break them—ourselves. The Union is no longer asking for a seat at the table. We are pouring the foundation for our own.
