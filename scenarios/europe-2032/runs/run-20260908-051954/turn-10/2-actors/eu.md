## In practice
We are acting on the lesson that jurisdiction without technical parity is ritual. The enforcement units stand up this turn, but their authority is already being tested: one U.S. provider has suspended updates, citing national security exemptions, and telemetry access remains contested. Without our own evaluative capacity, we are forced to either trust or block — and neither is sustainable.

So we begin building what should have come earlier: the European AI Watchdog Network. Under Horizon Europe’s redirected safety strand, we are funding a pilot involving six national labs — from France’s ANSSI to Finland’s FCAI — to develop red-teaming protocols and secure evaluation environments. The network will not rely on voluntary cooperation; access will be mandated under revised Article 11 of the AI Act for any system above 50.0 capability operating in the EU. We are negotiating hosting through EuroHPC, using existing legal infrastructure to bypass new approvals, and securing long-term funding through the Digital Europe Programme’s resilience window.

This is not a replacement for enforcement — it is its necessary foundation. The anomaly reports from unreleased systems suggest we are losing visibility into the frontier. We cannot regulate what we do not understand. By turn 13, we will have evaluators who can see — and speak — truth to power.
