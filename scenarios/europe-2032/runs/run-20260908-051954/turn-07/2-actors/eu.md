## In practice
The completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories marks a symbolic milestone — we have laid the foundation for EU-based compute at scale. But the taiwan_blockade has turned that foundation into a fortress under siege. With global semiconductor flows severed, our upstream leverage in lithography and materials is now our most potent strategic asset — and our most exposed. We are no longer just building capacity; we are defending scarcity. The Gigafactories are finished, but their purpose remains unfulfilled: without sovereign AI stacks to run on them, and without enforcement of jurisdiction over the systems that do, we have hardware in search of a mission.

So we are shifting from construction to coercion. The new measure — Activate EU Jurisdictional Enforcement Units — operationalises the Sovereign Containment Framework’s legal teeth. Under a joint ENISA-Eurojust mandate, these units will issue binding audit orders, demand access to agent logs and training summaries, and conduct unannounced inspections of foreign developers’ EU-facing operations. Germany has agreed to host the first hub, with BSI integrating into review cycles for U.S. cloud providers; Poland’s full data mirroring rule now serves as a precedent for data sovereignty that others will be asked to match. This is not voluntary compliance. Where developers resist, we will invoke Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions to restrict market access — a step we know will provoke retaliation, but one we can no longer defer.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Resilience Corridor expands under fire. The emergent_defensive_swarm_detection success within NATO has given us a working model: we are fast-tracking integration of that swarm-detection system into EU energy and transport networks, co-funding deployment with repurposed Civil Protection Mechanism resources. But the real test is political: in Ireland and Finland, local resistance to data centres has turned violent, and policing alone cannot restore legitimacy. We are therefore coupling physical protection with a new transparency compact — real-time public dashboards on AI system telemetry, managed by national regulators but fed from the same streams as enforcement. It is a gamble: that visibility can temper fear. But if we cannot make sovereignty felt as protection, not just control, then no amount of capability or containment will save us.
