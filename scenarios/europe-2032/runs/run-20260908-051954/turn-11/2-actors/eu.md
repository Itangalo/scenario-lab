## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Consolidate sovereign operational capacity through independent evaluation and resilient integration``
  - Trigger: the completion of the Jurisdictional Enforcement Units and the shift from enforcement assertion to technical foundation-building
  - Grounds: The previous commitment to "sovereign operational control through enforceable jurisdiction and resilient integration" has reached its limit: jurisdictional claims have been tested and found wanting without technical parity. The enforcement framework exists, but it lacks teeth where access is denied. The new phase must pivot from asserting control to *exercising* it — starting with independent evaluation as the core lever. Sovereignty will not come from legal fiat alone, but from the ability to see, test, and speak truth about AI systems independently. This reframe acknowledges that operational control must be built from capability, not just law.

* ``- modify `containment_first` (position): Until we can independently verify system behavior, no expansion of AI integration is net positive — but we now accept that verification must be operational before it is complete, and we will build it in parallel with containment, not after.``
  - Grounds: The failure to secure telemetry from a major hyperscaler proves that containment based on compliance is fragile. With `ai_capability` at 63.0 and anomalies emerging, waiting for perfect verification means surrendering agency. We must now treat independent evaluation not as a support function but as the central pillar of containment. The Watchdog Network is not a supplement to enforcement — it is its necessary successor in substance, if not in name. We begin operationalising verification even as legal disputes over access continue.

## New measure
**Measure:** Secure Foundational Compute for Public Evaluation  
Establish a sovereign, secure, and scalable compute cluster dedicated exclusively to public technical evaluation of high-capability AI systems, hosted within EuroHPC and legally insulated from external interference, enabling sustained red-teaming, interpretability research, and weight audits.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`

## Priority
**Secure Foundational Compute for Public Evaluation** — because the Watchdog Network cannot fulfill its mission without the compute to run high-capability models in secure environments, and because every day without sovereign infrastructure deepens our dependence on foreign-controlled systems that can withhold access at will.

## In practice
We are acting on the truth exposed by the enforcement push: jurisdiction without infrastructure is empty. The Watchdog Network is our eyes, but it needs a body — a compute base that is ours, on our soil, under our legal and physical control. We are now launching the first real bid for technical parity: a 16-petaflop sovereign cluster, funded through a reallocation of the Digital Europe Programme’s infrastructure window and co-invested by three member states already part of the regional pact — France, Germany, and the Netherlands — who see this as a bridge back into EU-led coordination.

Hosting will be under EuroHPC’s JUPITER site in Germany, leveraging existing security accreditation and grid access, with a dedicated partition walled off for evaluation work. Legal insulation is being drafted under Article 17 of the EuroHPC Regulation, treating the cluster as critical infrastructure immune to extraterritorial data access demands. We are negotiating with ASML and Infineon to fast-track lithography support for domestic chip assembly, and with the European Investment Bank to underwrite power redundancy.

Resistance is expected from the same hyperscaler blocking telemetry, which has already signaled opposition to any EU-run evaluation of its frontier models. But we are no longer asking. This cluster will not train frontier models — it will test them. And if access is denied, the EU will treat that as a compliance failure under the AI Act. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that, for the first time, the EU has a machine it can trust — because it owns the metal.
