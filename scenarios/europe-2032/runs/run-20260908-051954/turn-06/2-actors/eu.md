## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: one legal, one technical — but both aimed at making jurisdiction real. The Sovereign Containment Framework and the Tech sovereignty package have now formally concluded. The former has established the legal architecture for real-time telemetry and behaviour limits on high-capability systems; the latter has unlocked private investment in EU data centres through accelerated permitting. But neither delivers sovereignty on its own. The containment framework lacks teeth without enforcement, and the compute build-out lacks purpose without a sovereign stack to run on it.

So we are doubling down on operational credibility. The EU Jurisdictional Enforcement Units are now active within ENISA and Eurojust, issuing preliminary compliance directives to foreign developers with significant EU user bases. These are not symbolic: backed by Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Services Act’s systemic provider provisions, they demand access to agent logs, training data summaries, and kill-switch readiness. Germany has accepted the audit-sharing protocol, allowing BSI to co-review US and Chinese models, while Poland’s carve-out enables full data mirroring for its national AI backbone — a concession that holds the Council together, barely.

Meanwhile, the safety_breakthrough in goal stability verification — a method that can predict and certify alignment-preserving transformations in agent reasoning — is being fast-tracked into ENISA’s evaluation toolkit under our new Category 1 measure. This is not perfect, but it is deployable. For the first time, we can point to a technical standard that goes beyond post-hoc audits and enables pre-compliance certification. We are mandating it for all systems above 50.0 capability, starting with those under containment framework obligations. The labs are cooperating because it reduces their liability; we are pushing because it turns our jurisdictional claims from legal fiat into technical fact.

But the backlash_physical events are changing the game. Data centre occupations in Ireland and sabotage in Finland have forced us to divert interior ministry resources to protect infrastructure — a cost that now exceeds the damage. We are not backing down, but we are reframing: the Sovereign Resilience Corridor is being expanded to include physical protection of Tier 1 AI infrastructure, with EU Civil Protection Mechanism funds repurposed for rapid response teams. This is not just about cyber anymore. It is about making clear that the EU will defend its digital sovereignty with the same resolve it defends its borders — because they are now the same thing.
