## Priority
Accelerate Critical Infrastructure Hardening — because the cyber_test_shot proves existing defences are blind to advanced persistent threats, and without credible resilience, even temporary lapses in control could cascade into strategic failure.

## In practice
We are acting on the fact that the Union’s most immediate vulnerability is not its lack of frontier models, but the fragility of the systems those models might target. The cyber_test_shot across grid operators, water utilities and port authorities — including within two member states — was not an attack but a rehearsal, and it went undetected by every national system until uncovered by accident. This cannot stand. Under Article 114 TFEU, we are drafting a revised NIS3 Directive to impose mandatory intrusion detection thresholds for electricity transmission, water distribution, healthcare networks and transport hubs, with enforcement powers vested in ENISA and co-financing from the Digital Europe Programme. The Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formation will be asked to fast-track adoption by Q1 2027, supported by a new Joint Cyber Situational Awareness Cell hosted at Europol, drawing on CSIRTs across the Union.

At the same time, we are pushing forward the Tech sovereignty package, negotiating with national energy regulators and regional governments to designate three pilot “AI-ready” permitting zones — in Finland, Spain and Poland — where grid access and environmental reviews for data centres will be capped at six months. These negotiations are difficult: some member states fear becoming energy colonies for foreign hyperscalers. To address this, we are attaching conditions to public land allocation — requiring 40% of compute capacity in these zones to be reserved for EU-controlled workloads, including sovereign AI stacks under development by EuroHPC. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, though site selection has been delayed by local opposition in two candidate regions. We are deploying cohesion funds to offset perceived inequities in siting, and engaging directly with mayors and regional councils to pre-empt blockages. Public sentiment remains ambivalent, and we cannot afford visible failures.
