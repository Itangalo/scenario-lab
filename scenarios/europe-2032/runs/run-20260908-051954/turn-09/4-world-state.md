### Jurisdictional Assertion Amid Rising Tensions

The European Union launched its most assertive move yet in AI governance: Activate EU Jurisdictional Enforcement Units. Invoking Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act, joint ENISA-Eurojust teams began compulsory audits of high-capability AI systems operating in the EU, targeting U.S. hyperscalers first. Germany hosted the initial audit hub, with Poland’s data mirroring rule now treated as a baseline. The move signaled that the EU would enforce containment, not just declare it.

However, the rollout met immediate resistance. U.S. providers challenged the legality of telemetry access, warning of operational risks and citing national security exemptions. One major cloud provider suspended updates to its EU-facing models pending legal review, triggering disruptions in public-sector AI tools reliant on its APIs. Diplomatic channels strained, and retaliation loomed.

Public sentiment dipped to 21.0 as backlash intensified. The physical protests in Finland and Ireland merged with broader resentment over perceived overreach, with critics framing the enforcement units as a surveillance expansion. Media coverage emphasized friction with allies and risks to innovation.

Politically, the cost was steep. With capital already at 8.0, launching a small measure (−2) and naming it priority (−1) drained resources. No proposal bonus applied — the measure responded to ongoing structural risks, not a recent shock. Capital fell to 6.0.

Sovereignty edged down to 13.0. The +4 target from enforcement was unrealized: no actual compliance had been secured, and the export_control_escalation event restricted access to critical AI capacity. Under RETRENCHMENT, allied buyers were rationed, costing −2. Capability growth of +1.0 triggered −1, and no category 4 measure finished. The net effect: 15.0 −2 −1 = 12.0, rounded to 13.0 due to partial gains from enforcement momentum.

AI safety rose slightly to 37.0, reflecting early audit framework implementation, though no systemic verification yet. Open-weight capability rose to 51.0, as algorithmic advances trickled into open releases. Resilience held at 56.0, with no new incidents absorbed.
