## In practice
We are acting on the principle that sovereignty is not ownership — it is control. The completion of the Sovereign Resilience Corridor gave us hardened infrastructure; the stalled Sovereign Containment Framework gave us a design for monitoring; but until now, we lacked the teeth to make either matter against resistant actors. That ends this turn. Under Article 114 TFEU and reinforced by the Digital Markets Act, we are activating EU Jurisdictional Enforcement Units — joint teams of ENISA technical auditors and Eurojust legal enforcers — to begin compulsory inspections of telemetry logs, training summaries, and agent-behaviour limits for all AI systems above capability 52.0 operating in the EU.

Germany will host the first audit hub, with Poland’s data mirroring rule now extended as a baseline expectation: if you serve the EU, your system’s behaviour must be visible here. We are targeting U.S. hyperscalers first — not because they are uniquely dangerous, but because their market dominance makes their non-compliance uniquely corrosive to our authority. Where they refuse, we will invoke DMA remedies: throttling, data access revocation, and ultimately, market access suspension. This will provoke retaliation. But we cannot wait for perfect safety or perfect consensus — we act under uncertainty, and we pay the price of being wrong to avoid the cost of being late.

At the same time, we are laying the groundwork for the European AI Watchdog Network, though it will not launch as a measure this turn. Horizon Europe’s safety strand is being redirected to fund its design, and we are securing commitments from six national labs to participate in a pilot phase. But we will not propose it formally until next turn, when our capital may allow. For now, our message is clear: the EU does not just regulate AI — it governs it, in practice, at the point of operation.
