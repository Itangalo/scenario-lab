### Frontier Leaps, Safety Lags

Global AI capability advanced to 65.0 as a leading lab released a system capable of managing multi-day research campaigns across physics and software. Following the release, early versions of an open-weight model with partial frontier capabilities began circulating, drawing significant interest from research collectives and private clusters. While downloads spread rapidly, full parity with the frontier remains out of reach, and deployment is still constrained by hardware access and fine-tuning requirements. As a result, `openweight_capability` rose to 56.0—reflecting strong momentum, but not a sudden leap.

With no safety measure landing on shipped systems, and a major capability leap occurring, `ai_safety` dropped to 35.0. The EU’s Watchdog Network delivered pilot findings but remains in limbo, its permanent governance blocked by disputes over evaluator independence.

### Earthquake Triggers Redundancy Push

A moderate earthquake disrupted a major data centre hub, exposing the fragility of concentrated AI infrastructure. In response, the EU activated the Geographic Redundancy Protocol, replicating core workloads across Portugal, Finland, and Poland. Seismic retrofitting and redundant cooling are now integrated into the Secure Foundational Compute project at JUPITER, but legal delays under Article 17 persist. The Commission is pushing a NIS2 reclassification to grant extraterritorial immunity, while offering export-control coordination with the Netherlands to secure ASML’s support.

### Political Capital Holds, But Pressure Mounts

The EU proposed a new small Category 6 measure—Cross-Border AI Incident Response Drills—intended to strengthen coordination between CERTs, EuroHPC, and civil protection. However, due to critically low political capital and the existing burden of three active measures (−7 total) plus a named priority (−1), the initiative has not yet launched. Planning is delayed pending governance clarity and resource reallocation. The Watchdog Network finishes this turn, earning a +2 completion bonus, but no sentiment gain occurred. Political capital remains at 4.0, as the cost of maintaining existing measures was already accounted for in prior turns and no new drain occurred from unimplemented proposals.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` declined to 8.0, penalised by capability growth (+1.0) and no category 4 completion. Member states continue informal compute-sharing talks with Switzerland and Norway, bypassing stalled EU frameworks—a sign of deepening fragmentation.
