This new commitment reflects a shift from building capability to wielding jurisdiction as power: our leverage lies in making compliance with EU rules a condition of access, even for foreign systems, and in ensuring that failures do not cascade.

## In practice
We are now treating jurisdiction as infrastructure. The Sovereign Containment Framework remains our legal spine, but it is only as strong as its enforcement. This turn, we are establishing EU Jurisdictional Enforcement Units — hybrid teams of legal experts, cyber investigators, and AI auditors embedded in ENISA and Eurojust — to operationalise our claim that any system serving EU users falls under EU rules, regardless of where it runs. These units will begin issuing compliance directives to foreign developers by turn 6, backed by the threat of market exclusion. We are offering Germany a bilateral audit-sharing protocol to ease concerns over sovereignty, and Poland a data-localisation carve-out for critical government systems, in exchange for dropping their Council objections.

At the same time, we are integrating the newly finished Accelerate Critical Infrastructure Hardening directive into the Sovereign Resilience Corridor. The binding cyber benchmarks it established are now being rolled out through the Connecting Europe Facility, with AI-driven intrusion detection mandated for all Tier 1 operators in energy and finance. Non-compliant firms will lose access to EU cyber incident support and cohesion funding. Behind the scenes, we are fast-tracking the safety_breakthrough in goal stability verification into ENISA’s compliance toolkit, despite its immaturity, because we need a technical foothold to justify our legal claims. The labour_displacement wave is tightening public pressure, but we are resisting calls for broad AI moratoriums — instead framing these measures as the only way to ensure that automation does not become coercion.
