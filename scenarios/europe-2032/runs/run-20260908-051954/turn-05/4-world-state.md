### Jurisdiction as Leverage, Sovereignty Under Strain

The EU doubled down on legal jurisdiction as its primary lever of sovereignty, launching the **EU Jurisdictional Enforcement Units**—hybrid legal-technical teams within ENISA and Eurojust—to assert extraterritorial authority over foreign AI systems serving EU users. The move aimed to operationalise the stalled **Sovereign Containment Framework**, offering Germany a bilateral audit-sharing deal and Poland a data-localisation carve-out to break the Council deadlock. Yet the concessions only deepened internal fractures: Warsaw accused Brussels of rewarding obstruction, while Berlin remained wary of diplomatic overreach.

Despite political maneuvering, enforcement credibility remained unproven. No foreign developer acknowledged the new units’ authority, and legal challenges loomed over jurisdictional reach. The **Sovereign Containment Framework** was technically advanced to final negotiation, but without a clear enforcement mechanism, its deterrent value remained speculative.

Meanwhile, the completed **Accelerate Critical Infrastructure Hardening** directive was folded into the **Sovereign Resilience Corridor**, mandating AI-driven intrusion detection for Tier 1 operators. Early adopters in finance and energy reported improved threat visibility, bolstering resilience. However, the **labour_displacement** wave intensified, with entry-level roles in law, accountancy, and software vanishing—fueling public backlash. Protests against AI hiring tools spread, and sentiment dipped as automation’s human cost became undeniable.

The **supply_chain_coercion** event struck hard: Washington forced ASML to restrict exports of older lithography tools, demonstrating that the EU’s sole chokepoint in semiconductor supply was not its own to wield. This direct erosion of strategic autonomy triggered a sovereignty penalty.

In Washington, the **election_retrenchment** outcome shifted the global landscape. The incoming administration turned inward, imposing data centre moratoriums and sector-wide restrictions, slowing US frontier progress. While this eased transatlantic pressure, it also removed a key coordination partner—leaving the EU to act alone at a moment of weakening capital.

### Capability Creeps Forward, Safety Stalls

Global **ai_capability** rose to 59.5, driven by recursive training, though at a slightly reduced pace due to US retrenchment. **ai_safety** fell to 32.0 as theoretical advances in goal stability failed to deploy at scale, and no containment measure had yet landed on frontier systems. **openweight_capability** edged up to 47.0, reflecting wider diffusion of mid-tier models.

Resilience gained +5 from the completed hardening directive and integration into the broader corridor. But **eu_ai_sovereignty** dropped to 15.0, hit by capability growth and the ASML coercion event. **eu_political_capital** fell to 12.0 under sustained portfolio strain and no offsetting gains. With capital below 20, the named priority conferred no acceleration. **public_sentiment** dropped to 31.0 amid job losses and distrust in AI governance.
