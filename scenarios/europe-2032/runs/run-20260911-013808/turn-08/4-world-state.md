### The sweep
It came as a rolling outage, not a single blast. A poisoned update in a widely used back-office package, stitched to automated ransomware that moved at machine speed across municipal systems, clinics and a logistics operator. Screens locked with identical notes. Referral software already running on throttled European fallbacks slowed further.

Emergency crews from the Union cyber agency, national response teams and hospital IT staff worked ward by ward. Where the new behaviour-based detection and machine-speed patching had been installed, intrusions were cut off in hours. Where they had not, recovery took days of rebuilds from backups. No blackout, no deaths attributed, but weeks of cancelled appointments and leaked administrative data. Attribution remained open; analysts noted privately the tooling bore the marks of model-written attack code.

### The quiet offices
At the same time, a different story settled in white-collar work. Studies from law firms, consultancies and administrations showed the same pattern: assistants lifted output, especially for juniors, employment held steady, and then gains flattened. No second wave of automation arrived. Managers stopped budgeting for transformation and started budgeting for licences.

For governments it was a reprieve — no displacement wave to pay for — and a disappointment for anyone who had promised a productivity boom to fund other plans.

### The deal in the distance
Washington and Beijing announced a limited understanding on frontier risks — securing model weights, restraining autonomous escalation, controlling a class of biological design tools — with thin verification. Brussels was not at the table. The External Action Service and the new Safety Institute shuttled between embassies offering evaluation capacity and pooled incident telemetry as a ticket to observer status.

Both capitals listened politely. Neither opened the text. With American commercial services still blocked for export reasons and shipping insurers repricing routes amid larger naval drills around Taiwan, European leverage felt theoretical.

By June, services ran, slower and patched. Staff who had demanded working tools in January now asked only that they not break again. Relief that the attack had not cascaded mixed with anger that it had landed at all.
