### Wards and town halls
The second half of 2030 belonged to repair crews. Money left over from the cut-off patching deployment was reprogrammed into the worst-hit municipalities and hospital groups. National teams seconded 24-hour patching crews; engineers rebuilt administrative networks from clean backups and replaced the poisoned back-office package with segmented alternatives.

Where crews arrived, the change was tangible. Cancelled appointments were rebooked, referral queues began to move, ransomware notes stopped reappearing. Where they had not yet arrived, mayors complained loudly about a two-speed recovery. Health ministers toured clinics presenting the funds as repair money, not Brussels centralisation, and for exhausted staff that framing mostly held.

### The quiet diplomacy
On the US-China understanding, Brussels kept a low burn. Diplomats and the new Safety Institute continued to offer evaluation capacity and pooled incident telemetry in exchange for observer status. Washington and Beijing acknowledged the offers without opening the text. With export restrictions still in place and shipping insurers quietly repricing routes around larger naval drills near Taiwan, European officials spoke privately of being kept in the waiting room.

### A slower, patched normal
By December, services ran — slower, patched, but running. Behaviour-based detection caught several smaller follow-on attempts in hours rather than days. No blackout, no attributed deaths. Relief mixed with resentment: gratitude that the cascade had been avoided, anger that dependence had allowed the sweep at all.

White-collar work stayed flat. Assistants helped juniors, licences were renewed, but no second automation wave arrived to fund grand plans. Staff asked only for stability, and for the first time in a year, in the repaired wards, they partly got it.
