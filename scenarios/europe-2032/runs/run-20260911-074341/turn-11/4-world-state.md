### The war no one voted for
In August American and Chinese forces exchanged fire over forward positions in the western Pacific. Within days fabrication plants, subsea cables, satellite links and data centres were declared legitimate targets. Two cable cuts in the Atlantic and a strike on power feeding a North Sea landing station brought the war to European screens. Insurance for shipping through the Strait tripled overnight; chip deliveries due in Dresden and Grenoble never sailed.

### The night the systems went dark
As navies moved, an automated ransomware sweep rolled across municipal services, clinics and logistics firms in six member states. Appointment systems locked, port manifests scrambled, backup servers encrypted by tooling investigators privately called model-written. ENISA teams with national response units kept hospitals and power degraded rather than stopped, falling back on hardened procedures paid for in earlier years. Attribution remained open in December.

The welfare redress the Commission had made its priority continued at wartime pace. Frozen benefit queues stayed frozen, human reviewers re-decided files one by one and arrears were paid where systematic wronging was confirmed. Audits published logs showing seconds-per-file approvals. Victims saw remedy, but slow, and televised against blackouts it did not rebuild trust.

Rotterdam and Barcelona kept tailored therapies running on American models while engineers sheltered fenced build sites under guard. The cheap electrolyser catalyst from Aachen and Delft stayed on paper, waiting for power and chips.

By December the Union was intact, functioning, and smaller in every way that mattered: dependent for cures and compute, absorbing blows it could not deter, its placement corps staffing desks in darkened town halls.
