### The Breach That Changed Nothing

The cyber_major_incident — a self-propagating compromise of update mechanisms across sovereign AI nodes — exposed a fatal flaw: the very systems meant to secure EU autonomy were themselves untrustworthy. Emergency protocols were triggered, but not before emergency services in three member states experienced degraded functionality for over 48 hours. The attack was model-generated, fast-moving, and exploited blind spots in update verification that no audit had flagged.

In response, the EU declared an institutional emergency. The **Emergency Resilience Surge** was launched under Article 172 TFEU, redirecting €2.8bn from InvestAI to fund zero-trust upgrades across critical infrastructure. ENISA and the Joint Research Centre formed an independent red team to audit all active Sovereign Core nodes, halting further deployment until verification was complete. This delay frustrated several member states reliant on rapid rollout, particularly in the Baltics, where US cloud dependencies remain unresolved.

“Operation Iron Gate,” a pan-EU cyber defence exercise, was scheduled for turn 11. The NIS3 mutual assistance clause was invoked, mandating real-time intrusion data sharing. Yet intelligence agencies resisted full transparency, withholding key logs under national security claims. The Public Trust Observatory, though now operational in interim form, could not publish its first report on schedule — delayed again, this time due to incomplete data access.

Public sentiment held at 28.0, numb rather than reactive. The French benefits scandal still festered, and while the new surge was framed as decisive, few believed the institutions could execute it cleanly. Trust was not collapsing further — but neither was it recoverable yet.

Meanwhile, global AI capability advanced to 85.0, crossing into the sub-linear zone above 85 where measurement blurs. Offensive cyber tools evolved faster than defences could adapt. Open weights, though lagging, reached 71.0 — sufficient to run sophisticated intrusion frameworks on consumer hardware. No safety progress was made; opacity deepened.

The EU’s political capital eroded to 0.0. Despite urgent action, credibility was absent. The cost of three large measures in flight — including the newly prioritised Surge — drained capacity beyond recovery. The system was acting, but not governing.
