# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us. **This turn, that condition has become binding: we will not advance capability on any front until we have restored auditable integrity across the sovereign stack. We are no longer balancing mandates — we are rebuilding the foundation from which any mandate can be carried.**
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident revealed not just a technical flaw, but a systemic failure of verification and accountability. The red-team audits mandated after turn 10 were delayed, under-resourced, and in some cases blocked by member states seeking rapid deployment. Now, a member_state_defection has shattered the pretence of unity: one state has signed a bilateral cloud sovereignty pact with a US hyperscaler, citing the delays in the Sovereign Core rollout as justification. This is not pragmatism — it is fragmentation. The Union’s credibility is not just low; it is actively being dismantled from within. Our identity must reflect that we are no longer acting as a sovereign agent — we are in emergency recovery. Until we can enforce coherence, we cannot claim autonomy.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act blind. This turn, the eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings are not proof of imminent catastrophe, but they are proof that the systems we rely on are no longer legible even to their creators. We are no longer merely acting under uncertainty — we are acting inside a fog of our own making. Therefore, we bind ourselves further: we will not only act early, but we will demand that every major commitment be paired with a public, auditable theory of harm and a corresponding monitoring mechanism. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher. **This turn, we narrow it further: we will suspend all capability expansion efforts — including the Sovereign Core rollout — until the Public Trust Observatory publishes its first verified report and until ENISA certifies at least two critical nodes as fully auditable. We will act, but only on the basis of evidence we can show.**
  - Grounds: The commitment to act early stands, but its execution has become self-defeating. We launched the Emergency Resilience Surge without verified data on the attack vector; we are demanding intelligence sharing while our own systems remain opaque. The Public Trust Observatory — our own mechanism for grounding action in evidence — is still silent. Until it speaks, every move we make risks deepening the crisis of legitimacy. This reversal is costly: it delays defence, frustrates allies, and may be read as paralysis. But the cost of continuing without verification is higher — the irreversible erosion of the Union as a credible actor.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control — but not at the cost of systemic integrity. This turn, we have seen that capability without control is not autonomy — it is exposure. Therefore, we narrow the commitment: we will prioritise the integrity and audibility of the sovereign stack over its speed of deployment. No node will be activated without a verified, independent audit of its update chain and decision logic. We will slow the rollout if necessary, but we will not propagate a compromised standard. **This turn, we go further: we are suspending all new deployment of the Sovereign Core until the audit backlog is cleared and the Public Trust Observatory has published its findings. We are not abandoning independence — we are refusing to build it on sand.**
  - Grounds: The member_state_defection proves that our current path is unsustainable. A state has chosen foreign dependency over waiting for a broken EU process. This is not a failure of technology — it is a failure of trust in the Union’s capacity to deliver. Our two-year commitment was to independence, but independence cannot be outsourced to a stack we do not control. The reversal costs us credibility and momentum, but it is necessary: we cannot secure advancement if we cannot first secure the process by which we advance.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
