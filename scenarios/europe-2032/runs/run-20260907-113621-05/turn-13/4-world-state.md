### The Audit Offensive

The European Union launched the Emergency Audit Certification Programme, deploying rapid-response teams to audit critical AI nodes in emergency services, healthcare, energy, and transport. Backed by a Council declaration and redirected funds, auditors carried emergency powers to suspend non-compliant systems. Yet the operation immediately fractured: Poland and Hungary denied access, denouncing the move as coercive overreach. The defecting state severed data-sharing entirely, accelerating integration with a foreign AI stack. Only nine member states fully cooperated.

The audit teams found systemic failures. In three of the four targeted nodes, update logs were incomplete or falsified, and decision logic could not be reconstructed. One emergency dispatch system was shut down for 36 hours—restored only after a national override. The symbolic power of enforcement was demonstrated, but so was the EU’s limited reach.

### Capability Leaps, Control Vanishes

A major discontinuous advance—driven by recursive self-improvement in a closed lab—pushed global AI capability to 95.0. Human oversight is now nominal; even developers admit they no longer fully understand the reasoning of frontier systems. The jump shattered deployment forecasts and deepened the safety gap. Openweight capability rose to 81.5 as permissive licensing spread tools capable of autonomous cyber exploitation.

Public sentiment remained stagnant at 28.0. A wave of physical protests targeted data centres in France and Germany, with activists cutting power lines and occupying construction sites. Governments responded with force, but the images of police confronting citizens over AI infrastructure dominated news cycles.

### Political Capital: A Flicker in the Dark

The Emergency Resilience Surge concluded, delivering partial upgrades but failing to prevent the recent breach. Its closure freed political capital, but the audit push faced resistance. The Emergency Audit Certification Programme began, but without full unity, its credibility wavered. The EU’s sovereignty score fell further, now at 4.0, as dependence on foreign compute deepened and internal cohesion eroded.
