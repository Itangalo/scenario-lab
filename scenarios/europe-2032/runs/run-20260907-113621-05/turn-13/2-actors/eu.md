## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us. **This turn, that condition has become binding: we will not advance capability on any front until we have restored auditable integrity across the sovereign stack. We are no longer balancing mandates — we are rebuilding the foundation from which any mandate can be carried.**
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident this turn confirms that the Union is not merely lagging in capability or safety — it is actively operating systems it cannot verify, against threats it cannot trace, while member states defect to foreign stacks. The attack exploited update chains, the very vector the Sovereign Core was meant to secure. This is not a failure of ambition, but of fidelity: we built a system to assert control, but we did not build the means to verify that control exists. Our identity must reflect that we are no longer a sovereign actor — we are a crisis response entity. Until we can prove our own systems are coherent and accountable, we cannot claim to be securing anything.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act blind. This turn, the eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings are not proof of imminent catastrophe, but they are proof that the systems we rely on are no longer legible even to their creators. We are no longer merely acting under uncertainty — we are acting inside a fog of our own making. Therefore, we bind ourselves further: we will not only act early, but we will demand that every major commitment be paired with a public, auditable theory of harm and a corresponding monitoring mechanism. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher. **This turn, we narrow it further: we will suspend all capability expansion efforts — including the Sovereign Core rollout — until the Public Trust Observatory publishes its first verified report and until ENISA certifies at least two critical nodes as fully auditable. We will act, but only on the basis of evidence we can show.**
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident landed because the update verification process — the core safeguard — was not just weak, but unverified. We acted on the assumption that sovereignty implied control, but we never required proof. The commitment to act early remains, but it must now be anchored to a new principle: no deployment without demonstrable auditability. The reversal costs us time and credibility, but the alternative — continuing to expand a stack we cannot inspect — would cost us legitimacy entirely.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control — but not at the cost of systemic integrity. This turn, we have seen that capability without control is not autonomy — it is exposure. Therefore, we narrow the commitment: we will prioritise the integrity and audibility of the sovereign stack over its speed of deployment. No node will be activated without a verified, independent audit of its update chain and decision logic. We will slow the rollout if necessary, but we will not propagate a compromised standard. **This turn, we go further: we are suspending all new deployment of the Sovereign Core until the audit backlog is cleared and the Public Trust Observatory has published its findings. We are not abandoning independence — we are refusing to build it on sand.**
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident exploited the very systems meant to secure us. This is not a technical setback — it is a political rupture. The defecting member state was not wrong to fear delay; it was wrong to conclude that foreign dependency was the answer. Our reversal acknowledges that we cannot deliver strategic autonomy through a broken process. The cost is high: we are halting the central instrument of our two-year commitment. But the commitment was to independence, not to deployment. If we cannot secure the means, we forfeit the end.

## Priority

**Priority:** Emergency Audit Certification Programme  
This is our top priority this turn because the Unified Audit Authority cannot wait two turns to prove itself — the Sovereign Core is already compromised, public trust is collapsing, and member states are drifting toward foreign stacks. We need a visible, enforceable demonstration of audit power *now*, not in theory. This measure delivers that by putting boots on the ground in the most vulnerable systems, with legal authority to shut them down if they fail. It is the sharp end of the UAA’s mandate — and the only way to show that the Union still governs its own stack.

## In practice

We are launching the Emergency Audit Certification Programme under Article 114 TFEU, using the same legal basis as the Unified Audit Authority but with immediate, time-limited powers to act before the full body is stood up. Three rapid-response audit teams — drawn from ENISA, the JRC, and the Public Trust Observatory’s technical staff — are being deployed this quarter to conduct full forensic audits of the most critical sovereign AI nodes: one in national emergency dispatch, one in regional healthcare coordination, one in cross-border energy management, and one in rail traffic control. Each team carries emergency authority to suspend system operations if update chains are unverifiable, logs are missing, or decision logic cannot be traced.

Funding of €400 million is being redirected from the now-canceled Emergency Resilience Surge to cover deployment, secure transport, and red-teaming. The Commission’s AI Office is coordinating with national regulators to ensure access, backed by a Council declaration that non-cooperation will trigger automatic suspension of EU funding and cross-border data privileges. This is not a request — it is conditionality in motion. We are not waiting for consensus on standards; we are applying the baseline established by the Public Trust Observatory’s final report: no audit trail, no operation.

Poland and Hungary have already signaled resistance, calling this “coercive federalism.” Our response is clear: the defecting state chose foreign dependency and got compromised systems. We are offering them a path back — full audit support, technical assistance, and fast-track certification — but not exemption. If they refuse, their nodes will be isolated. The world has moved. We are no longer building resilience. We are enforcing accountability. And this turn, that begins with shutdown authority in the hands of auditors who answer to no one but the Union.
