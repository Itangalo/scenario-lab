## In practice  
This turn, we are not passing laws — we are burying roots. The binding mandate dies in law, but we are not letting it die in practice. In Berlin, Stockholm, and Marseille, we are converting pilot integration kits into permanent fallback nodes: lightweight, open-weight models trained to run on commodity hardware, capable of接管 protein folding, grid stability checks, and biothreat triage if the foreign models go dark or rogue. These are not replacements for frontier systems — they are tripwires and life rafts. We are funding them through the Digital Europe Programme’s resilience window, reprogramming €180 million from stalled deployment grants, and routing it through the Joint Research Centre to bypass national resistance. No new legal base, no new vote — just quiet technical embedding.

We are also responding to the backlash_physical by changing the story: not “AI must be controlled,” but “AI must be owned.” In Bavaria and Andalusia, where data centre expansions were blocked, we are offering local governments co-ownership of small-scale, energy-efficient inference clusters running only open-stack models — tied to regional hospitals and grid operators. The clusters are slower, but they are inspectable, recallable, and under local jurisdiction. This is not the frontier — but it is soil. And in the wake of the ai_investment_collapse, when the big labs are cutting training runs and laying off safety teams, we are quietly hiring. Through the European Innovation Council, we are offering five-year contracts to 40 auditable AI researchers and engineers from shuttered labs — not to build new models, but to harden the fallback stack. Sovereignty is no longer a mandate. It is a migration path.
