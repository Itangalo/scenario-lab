## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign technical agency through auditable AI

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Anchor EU sovereign capability in auditable AI by operationalising verifiable systems in three critical domains—energy, health, and biosecurity—by end-2032, with binding deployment mandates and open-weight fallbacks for all essential services — but suspend enforcement ambition until political capital recovers above 12.0, and now **redirect focus from mandate to fallback infrastructure**, building sovereign capacity not through compulsion but through irreversible technical embedding
  - Grounds: This turn, the loss_of_control_incident confirms that oversight is not just impractical but structurally broken — and our mandate, even if enforced, would apply to systems we do not control. The ai_investment_collapse creates a window: frontier labs are retrenching, but open-weight models at 64.0 are now the most capable systems we can inspect, deploy, and harden. We cannot compel, but we can entrench. The commitment shifts emphasis: from mandating auditable AI in existing systems to ensuring that when those systems fail or are withdrawn, the fallback is EU-controlled, open-stack, and already running. This is not retreat — it is repositioning under fire.

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but only where failure does not cascade, and only when the action builds irreversible technical facts on the ground
  - Grounds: The loss_of_control_incident proves that early action without control is not courage — it is complicity. We acted under uncertainty before, but now we see that some uncertainties multiply risk beyond recovery. The commitment stands, but its domain narrows: we will act early only where the action itself creates resilience, not dependence. Technical embedding — not regulation — is now the only form of early action that pays down risk.
