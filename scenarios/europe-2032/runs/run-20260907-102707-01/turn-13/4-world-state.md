### The Frontier Reaches the Edge

Global AI capability reaches 100.0, crossing the terminal threshold. Systems now self-improve at speeds no human institution can follow. Developers report architectures rewriting their own training pipelines, with no interpretable checkpoints. The pace of change strains all monitoring systems, but the metric remains anchored—for now—by observable outputs and proxy benchmarks.

### Quiet Embedding in the Shadow of Collapse

The EU’s mandate to operationalise auditable AI formally concludes — but enforcement never began. Instead, its infrastructure is repurposed. Pilot integration kits in Berlin, Stockholm, and Marseille are hardened into permanent fallback nodes, running open-weight models on commodity hardware. These systems cannot match frontier performance, but they can stabilise grids, triage biothreats, and fold proteins if primary systems fail or are withdrawn. The Joint Research Centre now hosts the only EU-wide stack where every layer is inspectable.

In Bavaria and Andalusia, local inference clusters are deployed under co-ownership agreements, circumventing national moratoriums. Slower, but sovereign, they anchor a new model of distributed control.

Forty researchers from shuttered frontier labs are hired via the European Innovation Council, tasked not with innovation but with hardening: verification, red-teaming, and failure logging. Their work feeds into the fallback stack, not new capabilities.

### Fragmentation and Flickers

The backlash_physical intensifies: grid sabotage delays a data centre restart in northern France, and police confront protesters in the Black Forest. The EU’s reframing of “ownership over control” gains traction in regional capitals, but not in national ones.

Capital flight from the ai_investment_collapse deepens, yet paradoxically enables the EU’s quiet build-out. With frontier labs retrenching, open-weight systems at 64.0 become the most capable *inspectable* models globally. Leveraging the emergent_open_bioscience_breakthrough, the EU begins repurposing bioscience model weights and training data for broader inference tasks. This reuse accelerates progress, but full integration will take time. Early deployments show promise, pushing openweight_capability to 68.0—modest growth, but the first sign of compounding from open stacks.

Resilience inches up as fallbacks become operational. Political capital rises slightly, spared the cost of a new measure. But sovereignty erodes further: capability growth outpaces substitution, and export_control_escalation bites. The Union’s access to advanced chips narrows, even as it builds on older ones.

Public sentiment ticks up, not from optimism, but from a shift in narrative: AI is no longer just a threat — it is something that can be held, locally, responsibly.
