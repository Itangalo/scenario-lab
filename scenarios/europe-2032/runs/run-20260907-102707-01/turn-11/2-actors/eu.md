## New measure  
**Operationalise auditable AI in critical infrastructure with binding deployment mandates**  
Mandate the use of auditable AI systems in energy grid management, hospital diagnostics, and biosecurity monitoring, with open-weight fallbacks required for all essential services by Q2 2032.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +8 (via entrenched deployment), resilience +6 (via trusted fallbacks), public_sentiment +5 (via visible control in high-risk domains)  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with binding implementation through member state regulators and EU agencies (ENTSO-E, ECDC, EMA)

## Priority  
**Open Science Resilience Initiative** — because it is the only measure that delivers on the recalibrated two-year commitment: without its hubs operational and its models validated by turn 12, the new deployment mandates have no foundation to stand on, and sovereignty collapses back into aspiration.

## In practice  
This turn, we are finalising the integration into the joint cyber-biosurveillance pact: ENISA and ECDC have stood up co-located analysis cells in Brussels and Stockholm, and secure telemetry pipelines are now live with five partner states. The system has already flagged two AI-mediated bio-signals of concern—one in wastewater genomics, one in cross-border prescription anomalies—validating the resilience gain. This success, however, only sharpens the contradiction: we are trusted in the pact *because* our systems are verifiable, yet we cannot mandate those same systems at home. That changes now. The Commission is drafting a Regulation under Article 114 TFEU to enforce auditable AI in critical infrastructure, with derogations only where no open-weight alternative exists—and even then, requiring foreign models to run under EU-controlled interpretability layers. We are engaging the Energy Council, the Health Security Committee, and national regulators to align implementation, offering technical support and front-loaded grants to offset transition costs. Resistance is strongest in Germany and Spain, where energy operators fear grid instability, and in Italy, where hospitals rely on proprietary diagnostics. We are countering with pilot deployments in three EU-run facilities—Charité Berlin, Hospital del Mar, and the Joint Research Centre’s bio-lab—using the Open Science Resilience Initiative’s protein folding models as proof of reliability. The goal is not to ban frontier AI, but to make its use conditional on transparency—turning dependence into leverage.
