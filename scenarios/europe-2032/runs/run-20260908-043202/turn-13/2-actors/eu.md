## In practice  
This turn, we are not acting — we are surviving. The Union has no fiscal authority, no legal standing to compel, and no political capital to spend. InvestAI remains frozen. Article 114 is under judicial review, and with six member states contesting the legitimacy of emergency AI powers, any attempt to spend would be struck down. ENISA’s operational mandate has collapsed; its couriers no longer carry rollback keys, only archive manifests. The Sovereign Command Stack is formally withdrawn, not because the threat has passed, but because the response infrastructure became a symbol of overreach, not protection.

We are conducting informal soundings through the General Secretariat, not the Commission, in Dublin, Berlin, Madrid, and Dublin again — meetings held under Chatham House rules, with no agenda, no minutes, no binding outcome. We are asking only: *Under what conditions would you re-engage?* We speak to national CIOs, to health ministry technocrats, to energy regulators, to civil society leaders — not as a governing body, but as a convener with no power left to enforce. These are not negotiations. They are triage. If the Union is to act again, it must first be seen not as a threat to national sovereignty, but as a vessel for shared survival. Until then, we hold still — not by choice, but because to move now would be to fracture what little remains.
