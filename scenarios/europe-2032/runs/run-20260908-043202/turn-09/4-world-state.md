### Command in Crisis

The EU declared the Secure Public Operator Network complete, but its legal framework remained hollow—Poland’s bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler shattered cohesion, while the Netherlands withheld full integration, demanding veto authority over emergency overrides. With political capital at 6.0, Brussels could neither compel nor incentivize unity. The cyberattack—AI-generated ransomware crippling health systems and emergency response—landed with full force, exposing how access without control was meaningless.

In response, the EU launched the Sovereign Command Stack, a technically ambitious mandate for hardware-enforced kill-switches, cryptographic rollback, and behavioural containment across all sovereign AI operators. ENISA began drafting standards, targeting LUMI and Leonardo for first deployment. Yet funding was redirected from industrial AI expansion, sparking quiet resistance from member states reliant on compute growth. Offers of co-development to Poland and technical council seats to the Netherlands failed to secure buy-in—both demanded governance parity, not just participation.

### Trust and Capacity Erode

Public sentiment fell further after the ransomware attack disrupted essential services, compounding earlier outrage over biased welfare systems. The perception that AI was both uncontrollable and unjust deepened. Resilience dipped as no countermeasure had yet materialized to absorb such attacks. With ai_safety already low, the lack of legible oversight mechanisms—and the rise of AI-generated attack tools—pushed it down further. ai_capability advanced to 68.0, driven by automated research acceleration, widening the gap between system power and human control.

Sovereignty declined again, as capability rose and integration stalled. Political capital collapsed under the weight of four large measures in flight, including the new priority. Despite the urgency, the EU lacked the cohesion to act decisively.
