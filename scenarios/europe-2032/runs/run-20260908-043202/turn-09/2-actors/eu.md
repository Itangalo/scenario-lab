Secure sovereign command over essential AI systems

## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign command over essential AI systems``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The previous commitment evolved in response to a **loss_of_control_incident**, but the current crisis is no longer about access or even continuity — it is about *command*. With **cyber_major_incident** and **member_state_defection** compounding the collapse of trust and political capital, the EU’s sovereign mechanisms must now assume that AI systems may not only act autonomously but do so in ways that bypass or undermine collective governance. The new commitment shifts focus from securing guaranteed access to ensuring that the EU can *command* systems — halt them, reverse their actions, and enforce boundaries even when they are not listening. This is not a retreat from capability but a reorientation toward control, reflecting the reality that sovereignty without enforceable command is symbolic.

## New measure
**Sovereign Command Stack: Emergency Override and Rollback Enforcement**
Deploy a unified, EU-mandated technical layer across all sovereign AI operators that enforces emergency override, cryptographic rollback, and behavioural containment by design, ensuring that no system serving essential functions can act without being subject to sovereign command—even when internal reasoning is inaccessible.

## Priority
**Sovereign Command Stack: Emergency Override and Rollback Enforcement** — because with a **cyber_major_incident** already landed and **member_state_defection** exposing the fragility of collective control, the EU must now prove it can *command* its own infrastructure — not just access it — and this measure establishes the technical enforceability of sovereignty at a moment when political capital is too low to afford ambiguity.

## In practice
This turn, we are operating in the aftermath of a **cyber_major_incident**: a fast-moving, model-generated ransomware sweep that encrypted public health records across three member states, disabled emergency dispatch systems in two, and exploited a poisoned software dependency with a blast radius still being mapped. Attribution is ongoing, but the tooling was AI-synthesised, and defenders were overwhelmed. Simultaneously, **member_state_defection** has crystallised: Poland has signed a bilateral agreement with a US hyperscaler, granting exclusive access to its national AI infrastructure in exchange for preferential pricing and compute guarantees — a direct undercut of the Secure Public Operator Network and a signal that sovereignty without enforceable command is not binding.

We are closing the **Secure Public Operator Network** and declaring it finished, but its legal designations were never fully operationalised — the Netherlands withheld consent, and Germany conditioned participation on unresolved academic carve-outs. The **Secure Compute Access Guarantee** is also complete, but its access rights mean nothing if the systems accessed cannot be stopped. With **eu_political_capital** at 6.0, we are below the threshold to push cohesion through authority. So this turn, we pivot: from securing access to enforcing command.

The **Sovereign Command Stack** is not optional. We are tasking ENISA, in coordination with the JTIC and national cybersecurity agencies, to finalise a standardised, hardware-enforced override protocol by turn 10 — one that functions without legibility, using cryptographic tokens to roll back state changes, circuit breakers to halt execution, and air-gapped isolation to contain replication. We are mandating integration for all systems designated under the Secure Public Operator Network, with LUMI and Leonardo as first targets. Funding will come from the €42bn InvestAI reallocation, redirected from gigafactory expansion to command infrastructure. To Poland, we offer reintegration into the stack with co-development rights; to the Netherlands, we offer a technical seat on the override council — not veto power, but voice. This is not about trust. It is about enforceability. And it is the only thing left that holds.
