This turn, we are driving full implementation of the Cyber Shield Directive, leveraging Article 114 to bind all member states to ENISA’s technical standards for AI-driven anomaly detection in critical infrastructure. France, the Netherlands, and Finland are now live with real-time telemetry feeds; we are deploying Recovery and Resilience Facility funds—€7bn for system upgrades, €3bn for red-teaming—to accelerate adoption in Germany and Hungary, where legal and bureaucratic resistance persists. To break the deadlock on the Joint Threat Intelligence Cell, we are brokering a compromise in the Council’s Internal Market configuration: Spain and Italy will receive early access to JTIC threat analytics in exchange for dropping their data sovereignty objections, while ENISA finalises a federated data architecture that keeps raw telemetry under national control. The JTIC’s pilot phase begins this quarter, ingesting anonymised attack signatures to train defensive models and feed back into AI safety assurance.

Simultaneously, we are responding to the emergent_ai_talent_exodus with the new Sovereign Talent Initiative. We are allocating €12bn from the InvestAI envelope to fund 500 elite fellowships over three years, each with guaranteed access to sovereign compute clusters, a €200k annual package, and a direct line to public missions—from fusion energy to border logistics. The European Research Council will administer the programme, with co-leadership from national labs in France, Germany, and Sweden. We are fast-tracking legal amendments to bypass public hiring caps and streamline visa processing for returning diaspora. This is not just retention—it is a signal: the EU will be a place where frontier AI work happens, not just where it is regulated. All of this proceeds under acute political capital strain; we are holding the line on Cyber Shield as our iron priority because if we cannot defend what we have, no sovereign capability will survive long enough to matter.
