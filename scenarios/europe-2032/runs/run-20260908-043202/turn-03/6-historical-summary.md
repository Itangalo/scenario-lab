A major AI-powered cyber intrusion disrupted critical infrastructure, accelerating deployment of the binding Cyber Shield Directive in France, the Netherlands, and Finland, where real-time telemetry enhanced threat detection. Germany and Hungary delayed implementation despite €10bn in RRF incentives, citing data sovereignty concerns. A compromise enabled a limited pilot launch of the Joint Threat Intelligence Cell (JTIC) using a federated architecture, granting Spain and Italy early access. In response to escalating talent losses, the EU launched the €12bn Sovereign Talent Initiative to retain 500 top AI researchers by 2030, though structural inefficiencies weakened its credibility. Despite improved cyber defenses, incomplete rollout limited resilience gains. Political capital declined sharply due to portfolio strain from multiple large initiatives, including Cyber Shield, JTIC, InvestAI Gigafactories, and the new talent program. Public sentiment dipped slightly amid growing awareness of systemic vulnerabilities. The EU's strategic autonomy remained under pressure from advancing global AI capabilities and restrictive US export controls on key technologies.
