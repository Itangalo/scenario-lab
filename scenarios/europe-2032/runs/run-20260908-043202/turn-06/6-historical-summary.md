The EU intensified efforts to secure technological sovereignty, launching the Secure Public Operator Network with LUMI and Leonardo as core nodes, though integration stalled due to resistance from Germany’s NHR alliance and funding demands from the Netherlands. Legal concerns over state aid and research autonomy delayed the Secure Compute Access Guarantee, while the expired Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive left persistent capacity gaps. France and Finland advanced alignment, but Italy and others withheld support, exacerbating north-south divides. Public backlash surged as AI-driven job losses in law, accountancy, and tech triggered protests, undermining confidence in the AI agenda; a proposed transition fund lacked funding and credibility. Amid deteriorating sentiment, the new US administration classified frontier AI as a strategic asset, placing the EU in a conditional access tier, reinforcing urgency for autonomous capabilities despite ongoing fragmentation and implementation shortfalls.
