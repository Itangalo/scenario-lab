### A Hardening of Resolve Amid Mounting Strains  
The EU doubled down on institutional sovereignty, launching the Secure Public Operator Network to anchor AI continuity in legally bound public entities. Five core operators were identified, including LUMI and Leonardo, with mandates to maintain frontier systems under EU governance. However, Germany’s NHR alliance resisted integration, citing academic independence, and the Dutch Hub demanded additional funding, delaying full alignment.

### Legal and Political Friction Mount  
The Secure Compute Access Guarantee’s draft advanced, but legal services flagged tensions with state aid rules and national research sovereignty. The emergency compute directive was formally retired, its partial implementation leaving lingering gaps. While France and Finland committed to the new network, Italy withheld support, demanding reciprocal access guarantees. The northern regional aid pact quietly expanded, deepening fragmentation.

### Labour Displacement Ignites Public Backlash  
Widespread hiring freezes in law, accountancy, and tech entry roles—blamed on AI—sparked protests in Paris, Berlin, and Barcelona. Media coverage framed the EU’s AI push as benefiting elites while displacing graduates. A scoping study for a transition fund began, but with no budget attached, it was dismissed as symbolic. Public sentiment eroded sharply.

### US Posture Takes Shape  
The new US administration took office, declaring frontier AI a national strategic asset. Tiered access rules were previewed, reserving top-tier models for allied states with verified control frameworks. The EU was placed in a conditional tier, pending review. This confirmed fears of dependency—and accelerated the push for sovereign operation, though no immediate access cuts occurred.
