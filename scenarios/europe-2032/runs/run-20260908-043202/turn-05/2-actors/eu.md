## In practice
This turn, we are acting on the conviction that sovereignty is not a plan—it is a condition we must create in real time. The denial of access to the leading AI model was not a policy failure; it was a system failure, and it revealed that our critical functions run on borrowed brains. The Secure Compute Access Guarantee remains our anchor: we are advancing the legal draft under Article 114 TFEU through DG CONNECT and the Legal Service, with fallback protocols to be tested by December using repurposed clusters in France and Finland. But that work will take until turn 8 to land. In the gap, we are vulnerable.

So we are launching the Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive now—small, fast, and targeted. We are instructing EuroHPC and national compute directors in Germany, the Netherlands, and Italy to identify idle or underutilised capacity in publicly funded clusters and reassign it immediately to 50 priority entities: national health agencies, grid operators, and central banks. This is not about building new infrastructure; it is about using what we already own. We are waiving standard procurement rules under Article 122 TFEU, citing exceptional circumstances, and deploying emergency funding from the InvestAI reallocation to cover integration and security upgrades.

We face resistance—particularly from research institutions worried about losing training runs—but we are offering guaranteed reinjection of compute time in turn 6, once the northern regional aid pact’s shared reserves come online. We are also using the momentum from the successful cross-border cyber drill to push for a Union-wide compute reserve, to counterbalance the informal northern pact and prevent a two-tier Europe of AI access. The message is clear: we will not let strategic paralysis deepen while the world moves. We are securing the floor, not waiting for the ceiling.
