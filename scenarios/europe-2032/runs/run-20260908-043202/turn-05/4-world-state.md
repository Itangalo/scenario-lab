### A Fractured Response to Escalating Pressure  
Despite the Commission’s push for unity, the EU’s AI posture remains strained. The Joint Threat Intelligence Cell formally launched, enhancing cross-border cyber coordination among early adopters. Yet, its limited integration—Spain and Italy only—underscores persistent fragmentation. Meanwhile, benchmark anomalies from a leading lab suggest unexplained capabilities in a next-gen model, raising quiet alarms among regulators.

### Emergency Compute Push Faces Headwinds  
The Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive was launched to repurpose public clusters for critical agencies. France and the Netherlands swiftly redirected capacity, but Germany delayed implementation, citing legal concerns over Article 122 use. Italy stalled over research community backlash. Only half the targeted institutions gained access. Still, the move eased pressure on health and grid operators, marginally improving resilience.

### Talent and Trust Hang in the Balance  
The Sovereign Talent Initiative faltered as compute shortages deepened. Researchers in smaller institutions reported training runs delayed by months. The northern regional aid pact’s shared reserves offered no immediate relief, widening the north-south divide. Public sentiment ticked up slightly on news of action, but scepticism grew over delivery.

### US Election Reshapes the Landscape  
The US elected a president who treats frontier AI as a strategic asset. Access for foreign entities will now be tiered and security-reviewed. Though the administration takes office next turn, the signal is clear: Europe’s dependence is now a lever in Washington’s hands.
