**Joint Threat Intelligence Cell (JTIC)**  

Establish a permanent, AI-enabled fusion centre under ENISA to aggregate, correlate, and act on real-time cyber threat telemetry from critical operators across the Union.

## In practice  
This turn, we are operationalising the Cyber Shield Directive under Article 114 TFEU, with ENISA issuing binding technical standards for AI-driven anomaly detection systems across energy, water, and digital infrastructure. The Joint Threat Intelligence Cell is being designed in parallel, with staffing commitments secured from six member states’ national cyber agencies and a provisional mandate to ingest telemetry from pilot operators in France, the Netherlands, and Finland. We are fast-tracking the legal basis through the Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications configuration, offering Spain and Italy expedited access to JTIC analytics in exchange for dropping their reservations on data sovereignty. The €12bn disbursement from the Recovery and Resilience Facility is now split: €7bn for detection system upgrades, €3bn for red-teaming capacity, and €2bn ring-fenced for JTIC setup — all conditional on national implementation plans submitted by June.

At the same time, we are defending the InvestAI Gigafactories against deepening fiscal scrutiny. The export_control_escalation this turn — which now restricts even mature-node chip tools to volume licences, excluding the EU — has hardened our resolve but weakened our leverage. We are renegotiating the funding tranches with the European Investment Bank to require 60% private co-investment, and anchoring demand through a new public procurement initiative: sovereign AI clusters for nuclear safety simulation (with EDF), smart-grid optimisation (with TenneT), and emergency logistics (with Frontex). The Tech Sovereignty Package’s accelerated permitting zones are being piloted in Ireland, Poland, and Sweden, but local resistance persists. In response, we are activating the cohesion fund “sovereignty bonus,” releasing €400m in early disbursements to municipalities that approve data centre zoning by Q3 — a move that has already unlocked three sites in eastern Germany. All of this proceeds under severe political capital strain, which is why Cyber Shield remains our iron priority: if we cannot secure what we have, nothing we build will survive to matter.
