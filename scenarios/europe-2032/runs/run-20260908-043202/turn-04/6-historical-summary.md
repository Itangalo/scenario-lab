A severe AI-driven cyberattack disrupted hospital systems in early 2028, exploiting a poisoned software dependency, while the EU was abruptly locked out of a critical frontier AI model, exposing deep dependencies in essential services and eroding public trust. The Cyber Shield Directive was fully implemented in France, Finland, and the Netherlands, enhancing real-time threat detection, but Germany and Hungary maintained partial non-compliance over sovereignty concerns. The Joint Threat Intelligence Cell (JTIC) remained in a limited pilot phase with Spain and Italy due to federated architecture delays. In response to the crisis, the Commission proposed the Secure Compute Access Guarantee—a Category 4 initiative under Article 114 TFEU—to ensure uninterrupted sovereign AI access for critical functions, but it lacks funding and faces member state divisions. €18bn was reallocated from the InvestAI Gigafactories, slowing that project, though no formal reprioritization has occurred. With five major initiatives underway, including the ongoing Sovereign Talent Initiative, political capital declined due to systemic strain. Public sentiment turned negative amid healthcare and energy disruptions, growing compute shortages, and persistent talent losses. Open-weight model capabilities improved slightly through distributed fine-tuning, but the EU’s sovereign AI gap with global leaders narrowed only marginally.
