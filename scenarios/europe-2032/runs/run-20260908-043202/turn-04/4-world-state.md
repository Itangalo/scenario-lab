### A Blow to the Core  
The first half of 2028 opened with a cascade of shocks. A sophisticated, AI-generated cyberattack crippled hospital triage systems in three countries, exploiting a poisoned software dependency. Attribution remains murky, but the tooling bore the hallmarks of frontier model misuse. Simultaneously, without explanation, the EU was locked out of the leading AI model—overnight, ministries, research hubs, and grid operators lost access. The outage exposed a dangerous dependency: critical functions faltered, and public trust eroded.

### Cyber Shield Completes—Too Late  
The **Cyber Shield Directive** formally concluded this quarter, mandating AI-driven intrusion detection across all critical infrastructure. France, Finland, and the Netherlands now boast real-time telemetry and red-teaming, but Germany and Hungary remain partial holdouts, citing sovereignty concerns. While the Joint Threat Intelligence Cell (JTIC) launched a limited pilot with Spain and Italy, its federated model delays full integration. The completed directive modestly improved resilience, but the recent attacks revealed its limits.

### Sovereignty Under Siege  
In response, the European Commission proposed the **Secure Compute Access Guarantee**, a Category 4 initiative to ensure uninterrupted access to sovereign AI systems for essential services. Framed as a legal right under Article 114 TFEU, it aims to prevent future blackouts. However, no funding has yet been secured. The proposal remains in early consultation, with member states divided over cost and scope. The reallocation of €18bn from the InvestAI Gigafactories has slowed that project, but those funds have not been formally redirected to the new initiative. With five large measures already in flight—including the **Sovereign Talent Initiative** as the current priority—the political system is stretched. Launching another priority is not feasible without de-scoping an existing one, which has not occurred.

### Sentiment Turns Negative  
Public sentiment dipped sharply. The denial of AI access, combined with visible disruptions in healthcare and energy, fueled resentment. The talent exodus continues, and the emerging compute shortage pressures research. Despite the Cyber Shield’s completion, the string of incidents left the public questioning whether the EU can protect its digital foundations. Political capital declined moderately, reflecting the strain of ongoing efforts and the inability to respond decisively to the crisis. Open-weight capability rose slightly due to distributed fine-tuning efforts on existing models, but the gap with frontier AI narrowed only marginally, consistent with the broader trend of constrained sovereign compute growth.
