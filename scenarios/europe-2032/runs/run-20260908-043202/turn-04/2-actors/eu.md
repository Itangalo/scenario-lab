## New measure  
**Secure Compute Access Guarantee**  

Establish a legal right for EU public agencies and essential industrial operators to maintain uninterrupted access to at least one frontier-capable AI system hosted and governed within the Union, with fallback capacity activated automatically if external access is severed.

## Priority

## In practice  
This turn, we are treating the eu_frontier_access_denied incident as a wake-up call that cannot be answered with more appeals or coordination—it demands ownership. The Cyber Shield Directive is now complete, having driven adoption of AI-powered monitoring in all critical sectors except Germany and Hungary, where federated telemetry will come online via the JTIC pilot. We are transferring political momentum from that effort into a new imperative: no EU institution or essential operator should ever again face a blackout in AI capability due to decisions made beyond its borders. To that end, we are launching the Secure Compute Access Guarantee under Article 114 TFEU, which will legally bind the Commission, in partnership with EuroHPC and national sovereign labs, to ensure continuous access to a sovereign AI stack capable of substituting for any externally hosted frontier model.

We are instructing DG CONNECT and the European Commission’s Legal Service to draft the regulation by April, with emergency fallback protocols to be operational by the end of the year through repurposed InvestAI compute clusters in France and Finland. The guarantee will initially cover 200 high-priority entities—national health directors, grid operators, central banks, and defence logistics—and will require each to integrate a sovereign AI client with automatic failover. This is not about replacing commercial innovation; it is about ensuring that when access is denied—as it was this quarter—the lights stay on, the triage works, and the trains run. We are funding this through a reallocation of €18bn from the InvestAI Gigafactories envelope, slowing but not halting that project, because if we do not anchor capability where we can defend it, no amount of private investment will keep us sovereign.
