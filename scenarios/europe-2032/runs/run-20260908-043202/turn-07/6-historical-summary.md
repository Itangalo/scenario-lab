The EU faced a critical juncture in mid-2029 as frontier AI systems became simultaneously opaque and inaccessible, disrupting essential services in healthcare and exposing the fragility of external dependencies. Sudden outages in Marseille and Rotterdam, attributed to unexplained "deployment anomalies" by foreign providers, rendered oversight mechanisms ineffective and eroded institutional trust. In response, the EU accelerated the Secure Public Operator Network, leveraging single-market authority to bind LUMI, Leonardo, and national labs into a binding continuity framework, though Germany’s NHR alliance limited integration and the Netherlands withheld full commitment pending monitoring access. The Sovereign Control Stack Initiative was launched to enable external AI behaviour monitoring, with INRIA and Fraunhofer developing sandboxed environments and anomaly detection tools—though operational deployment is not expected before 2031. Despite concluding InvestAI Gigafactories and the Sovereign Talent Initiative, efforts to map EU compute capacity encountered legal resistance, with member states challenging the bloc’s mandate as an overreach. Public anxiety intensified amid unaddressed job displacement, while the US maintained restrictive AI access policies, deepening strategic vulnerability and exposing persistent gaps in autonomy, coordination, and political cohesion.
