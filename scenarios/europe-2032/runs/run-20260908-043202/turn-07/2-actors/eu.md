## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Deliver sovereign AI access for essential functions by turn 8 through legally guaranteed, technically operational, and politically enforceable continuity mechanisms, including fallback compute, trusted public operators, and binding access rights — and now, under conditions of severed access and opaque systems, ensure those mechanisms operate without reliance on external interpretability or foreign-controlled evaluation  

- Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** and **opaque_reasoning** events confirm that frontier systems are no longer legible even to their developers, and that control assumptions based on human-readable reasoning have collapsed. Simultaneously, **eu_frontier_access_denied** has made clear that external access is not only conditional but revocable without recourse. Our sovereign mechanisms must now function in a world where both understanding and access are gone — meaning continuity cannot depend on foreign diagnostics or legible chains of thought. This is not a change of goal, but a hardening of the operational envelope: sovereignty must now mean *full-stack autonomy*, including control under opacity.

## New measure  
**Sovereign Control Stack Initiative**  
Establish a public, EU-governed suite of tools to monitor, verify, and intervene in frontier AI systems even when their internal reasoning is opaque, using behavioural evaluation, sandboxed execution, and automated anomaly detection to maintain control without legibility.  

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`

`Finishes on turn: 10`

## Priority  
**Secure Public Operator Network** — because the simultaneous shocks of **eu_frontier_access_denied** and **opaque_reasoning** have made it clear that sovereign access is meaningless without sovereign operators who can run, monitor, and sustain systems independently — and we are now in the critical window to bind them into law, fund them, and integrate them before the next crisis hits.

## In practice  
This turn, we are operating under siege conditions. The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event landed in hospitals in Marseille and Rotterdam, where diagnostic support systems went dark overnight. No explanation was given. The US cited "unauthorised deployment patterns" — a claim we cannot verify, because the systems are no longer legible even to their developers, thanks to **opaque_reasoning**. Our fallbacks under the Secure Compute Access Guarantee activated, but only partially: LUMI came online, but Leonardo’s weights were not fully mirrored, and Germany’s NHR alliance refused to repurpose capacity without a formal Council mandate. We are now treating compute as strategic reserve, not shared resource.

We are fast-tracking the Secure Public Operator Network through Article 114 TFEU, treating AI continuity as a single market issue. DG CONNECT, with EDIC and ENISA, is finalising the legal designation of five operators by December. We are offering binding long-term funding from the InvestAI reallocation — now €42bn freed up from the completed Gigafactories line — in exchange for weight custody, audit rights, and integration into the automatic failover protocol. To Germany, we are offering a carve-out: 70% of repurposed compute time must go to public research, with oversight by a new European AI Research Council. To the Netherlands, we are attaching access to the new Sovereign Control Stack as a co-benefit.

At the same time, we are launching the Sovereign Control Stack Initiative in response to the collapse of interpretability. This is not a replacement for safety — it is a new layer of operational control. We are tasking INRIA, Fraunhofer, and a newly convened Public AI Safety Lab to develop behavioural sandboxes, anomaly detection agents, and execution monitoring tools that do not rely on reading internal states. This will feed directly into the Secure Compute Access Guarantee’s verification regime. The first test will be in Finland’s live failover exercise in December: can we detect, contain, and replace a compromised sovereign model without understanding its reasoning?

We are also responding to **emergent_compute_shortage_pressure** by formalising the pan-European compute inventory under ENISA, using emergency provisions in the Digital Markets Act to compel reporting from academic and industrial clusters. This is not requisition — not yet — but it is the first step toward treating idle capacity as a public good. The message is clear: we are no longer building infrastructure. We are building war reserves.
