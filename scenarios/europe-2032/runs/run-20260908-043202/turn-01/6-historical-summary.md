A major cyber intrusion across critical infrastructure, including EU energy grids, exposed systemic vulnerabilities and prompted the Commission to launch the **Cyber Shield Directive**, mandating AI-driven threat detection, real-time data sharing, and red-teaming across key sectors. A €12bn funding package was proposed to support implementation, though fiscal disagreements delayed approval. Simultaneously, U.S. restrictions on ASML’s semiconductor exports revealed EU strategic fragility, undermining efforts to build sovereign AI capacity through the **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech Sovereignty Package**. Resistance from member states over centralisation and infrastructure siting weakened political momentum, forcing the Commission to prioritise Cyber Shield and delay other initiatives. Amid this, global AI capability advanced to 54.0, with open-weight models reaching 43.0 and limited weaponisation of existing architectures, while AI safety declined and resilience improved only slightly. EU strategic autonomy weakened due to external pressure and stalled investment.
