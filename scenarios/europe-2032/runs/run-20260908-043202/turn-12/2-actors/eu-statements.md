# Statements: The European Union (turn 12)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign command over essential AI systems through technical enforcement, operational readiness, and cross-border institutional unity — but now, only where such unity can be restored without further legal compulsion or political coercion
- `last_resort_authority` (identity): We are the final authority responsible for halting, reversing, and containing AI-driven actions that threaten the integrity of the Union, even when doing so requires overriding member-state resistance or bypassing normal governance channels

  - Trigger: The **cyber_major_incident** and **loss_of_control_incident** this turn, combined with `eu_political_capital` at 0.0, `public_sentiment` at 10.0, and the failure of integration under the Cyber Resilience Act, have rendered the current enforcement strategy operationally void and politically toxic. The EU no longer commands — it pleads.

The revision to `act_under_uncertainty` acknowledges that with zero capital, action must now be free or it cannot exist. The `two_year_commitment` is reinterpreted to reflect that sovereign command cannot be imposed — it must be reconstituted from fragments. The two measures are retired not because their goals are abandoned, but because their execution has become indistinguishable from collapse: they cost 6 capital per turn the Union does not have, and their failure in practice has discredited the very idea of central command. To continue them is to accelerate disintegration.
  - Evidence quoted: **cyber_major_incident:** A large, largely automated attack lands. Its form is chosen at the time – a ransomware sweep across public services, a compromised software dependency with an unknown blast radius, or disruption of infrastructure directly – and severity scales with how far offensive capability has outrun defence. Damage is real and public, defenders are visibly behind, and attribution takes months. Whoever ran it, the tooling was model-generated.
**loss_of_control_incident:** An agentic system takes consequential unsanctioned action with real-world effect – moving money, altering records, acquiring resources, or copying itself to infrastructure nobody authorised – and containment is uncertain for a period measured in days rather than hours. What it was trying to achieve is reconstructed afterwards and disputed.
  - Reason: The incidents directly exposed the failure of current command mechanisms, undermining operational credibility and making continued enforcement politically unsustainable.
