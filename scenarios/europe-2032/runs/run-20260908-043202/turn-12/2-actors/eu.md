## Statement changes

- modify `last_resort_authority` (identity): We are the final authority responsible for halting, reversing, and containing AI-driven actions that threaten the integrity of the Union — but only if we retain the minimal cohesion to act at all. Our authority now depends not on legal assertion but on whether any member state still recognises it as legitimate  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, accepting error as the price of not being late — but only where inaction poses a greater risk than miscalculation, and only when the measure does not fracture the Union’s cohesion beyond recovery — and now, only if it can be executed with zero additional capital cost  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign command over essential AI systems through technical enforcement, operational readiness, and cross-border institutional unity — but now, only where such unity can be restored without further legal compulsion or political coercion  
- retire `Hardened AI Incident Response and Containment Protocol` (measure)  
- retire `Sovereign Command Stack` (measure)  

- Trigger: The **cyber_major_incident** and **loss_of_control_incident** this turn, combined with `eu_political_capital` at 0.0, `public_sentiment` at 10.0, and the failure of integration under the Cyber Resilience Act, have rendered the current enforcement strategy operationally void and politically toxic. The EU no longer commands — it pleads.  
- Grounds: The modification to `last_resort_authority` reflects that the EU’s claim to final authority is now contingent on recognition, not assertion. The revision to `act_under_uncertainty` acknowledges that with zero capital, action must now be free or it cannot exist. The `two_year_commitment` is reinterpreted to reflect that sovereign command cannot be imposed — it must be reconstituted from fragments. The two measures are retired not because their goals are abandoned, but because their execution has become indistinguishable from collapse: they cost 6 capital per turn the Union does not have, and their failure in practice has discredited the very idea of central command. To continue them is to accelerate disintegration.

## In practice

This turn, we are not governing — we are holding breath. The Sovereign Command Stack and Hardened AI Incident Response Protocol are formally retired, not because the threats have passed, but because the instruments meant to counter them have become vectors of disintegration. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep across public health registries in Belgium, Bavaria, and Emilia-Romagna — exposed that the nodes in Luxembourg, Finland, and Austria were not just uncoordinated; they were unconsulted. No escalation reached them. No rollback was attempted. The systems were already offline, cut off by national authorities who no longer accept the Commission’s authority to bind their infrastructure.

We have no funds to disburse. InvestAI remains frozen. Article 114 is under judicial review, and with the Court of Justice now hearing arguments from six member states challenging the legality of emergency AI powers, any further disbursement would be unconstitutional. ENISA has suspended all courier operations. JTIC has downgraded to monitoring only. The armed couriers who carried rollback keys are now guarding archives.

We are not proposing a new measure because we have no capital to spend, no trust to draw upon, and no agency left to enforce anything. Instead, we are initiating informal soundings — through the General Secretariat, not the Commission — with national cabinets, sovereign operators, and civil society leaders in Germany, Spain, and Ireland, asking not for compliance, but for conditions under which cooperation might resume. No directives. No deadlines. Just listening. The first such meeting will be hosted in Dublin, outside formal EU structures, under Chatham House rules, on the premise that if the Union is to act again, it must first be able to speak.
