### Collapse of Command

The EU formally withdrew the Sovereign Command Stack and Hardened AI Incident Response Protocol, not as a strategic recalibration but as an admission of institutional collapse. The cyber_major_incident — a coordinated ransomware sweep across public health registries in Belgium, Bavaria, and Emilia-Romagna — went unanswered by any central mechanism. The air-gapped nodes in Luxembourg, Finland, and Austria remained offline, disconnected by national authorities who no longer recognised the Commission’s authority to bind their critical systems. ENISA suspended all operational activity; its armed couriers now guard archives, not distribute keys.

### Judicial and Political Paralysis

With InvestAI funds frozen and Article 114 under fast-track review by the Court of Justice, the EU has no legal or fiscal capacity to act. The General Secretariat initiated informal soundings with national cabinets in Germany, Spain, and Ireland, hosting the first meeting in Dublin under Chatham House rules. No directives were issued, no timelines proposed — only questions about the conditions for future cooperation. The message was clear: the Union no longer commands, it pleads.
