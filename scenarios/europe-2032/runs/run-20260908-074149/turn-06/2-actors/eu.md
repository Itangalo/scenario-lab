## New measure
**Sovereign AI Testbed for Critical Functions**  
Establish a live, secure environment where EU institutions and member states can run high-stakes workloads — clinical diagnostics, infrastructure planning, threat analysis — on sovereign AI stacks without relying on foreign API access or cloud infrastructure.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases eu_ai_sovereignty (+10–15), strengthens resilience (+5), and indirectly supports public_sentiment by demonstrating sovereign utility in high-trust domains.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, selected member states, and designated public operators in health, energy, and security.

## Priority
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation (ESCM)** — because without immediate progress on sovereign compute, no other measure — not even the new testbed — can land, and because legal resistance and supply delays are now actively blocking the EU’s ability to act in its own interest.

## In practice
We are pressing ESCM forward under emergency legal frameworks, even as the Court of Justice weighs the data access mandate. With two member states still resisting full dataset pooling, we are activating the fallback: a federated learning architecture coordinated through DG CONNECT and ENISA, using synthetic data generation to train baseline models while preserving jurisdictional boundaries. This is not ideal — capability will be reduced and convergence delayed — but it keeps the programme alive. We are also finalising the €12bn GPU procurement under JSECI’s national security carve-out, with direct negotiations now underway with AMD and NVIDIA for emergency waivers on A100-class chips, citing Article 122 TFEU on public health and infrastructure continuity. The goal is to have at least one sovereign stack operational by turn 7, even if initially narrow in scope.

At the same time, we are launching the **Sovereign AI Testbed for Critical Functions**, a new measure to ensure that when sovereign capacity *does* arrive, it can be used immediately for high-stakes public missions. This is not a research project — it is a live environment, air-gapped where necessary, where frontline institutions can run real workloads: cancer screening triage in EU hospitals, grid stress simulations during heatwaves, real-time analysis of biothreat signatures. The testbed will be hosted on repurposed HPC infrastructure in France, Germany, and Finland, with strict access controls and audit trails. It will be funded from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe surplus, and governed by a joint board of the Commission, ENISA, and the European Health Data Space authority.

We are also integrating the emergent **cyber_collective_response** into our planning: the new joint rapid-response unit from EU states, Switzerland, and Norway is being invited into the testbed’s security perimeter, with shared red-teaming protocols and AI-driven intrusion detection models. This strengthens resilience without waiting for top-down mandates. But we are not letting coordination substitute for sovereignty. The US alliance offer remains pending, and while it may bring temporary relief, the supply_chain_coercion event — Washington forcing ASML to cut even mature-node exports — is a stark reminder: leverage is not shared; it is held. We are not building capacity to say yes to allies — we are building it so we do not have to ask.
