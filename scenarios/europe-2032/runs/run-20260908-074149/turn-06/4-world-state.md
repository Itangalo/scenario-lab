### A Surge in Capability, a Slippage in Control

AI capability advances to 62.0 as automation spreads in engineering and clinical workflows, with systems now handling multi-day projects in logistics and diagnostics. Yet assurance lags: no safety breakthrough occurs, and the gap between capability and control widens, eroding ai_safety to 18.0. Open-weight models, benefiting from leaked tooling, approach offensive cyber parity, pushing openweight_capability to 50.0.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

The EU presses forward with the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation (ESCM), adapting to legal resistance with federated learning and synthetic data. However, delays reduce expected capability, and full deployment slips. The Sovereign AI Testbed for Critical Functions is launched, targeting high-stakes public workloads, but it will take time to yield results.

Meanwhile, Washington forces ASML to restrict exports of mature-node lithography equipment, extending supply-chain pressure beyond frontier chips. This act of supply_chain_coercion undermines the EU’s leverage and directly hits its sovereignty, as its sole supply-chain chokepoint is used against it.

### Fragmented Response, Fragile Gains

A major cyber incident — an AI-generated ransomware sweep — disrupts public services across three member states. Defenders are overwhelmed, and attribution remains unclear. In response, a coalition of EU states, Switzerland, and Norway launches an emergent cyber rapid-response unit, pooling red teams and defensive AI. This joint effort strengthens resilience, which rises to 47.0.

The Tech Sovereignty Package and JSECI conclude successfully, but their impact is muted by external pressures. Public sentiment dips to 30.0 as job losses in law, accountancy, and software mount, and media spotlight dependency on foreign AI infrastructure.

The US election outcome — a technological alliance — takes effect. The new administration offers structured access to frontier AI, but on terms requiring alignment. The Commission welcomes cooperation but insists: sovereignty, not dependence, is the goal.
