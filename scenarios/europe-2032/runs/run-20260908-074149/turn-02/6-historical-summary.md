A devastating AI-coordinated cyberattack, exploiting a poisoned open-source library, disrupted critical public services across three EU member states, overwhelming defences and exposing the severe lag in protective capabilities. The attack demonstrated autonomous adaptation and leveraged tools matching frontier AI, underscoring the offensive lead of adversarial AI systems. Simultaneously, a near-frontier open-weight model spread rapidly, downloaded over 300,000 times, and was weaponized for exploit generation and synthetic biotoxin design, triggering urgent regulatory response.

In reaction, the European Commission drafted the **Open-Weight Containment Framework (OWCF)** under Article 114 TFEU, proposing a 90-day scrutiny period for high-capability open models, mandatory red-teaming by ENISA, and restrictions on autonomous or dual-use functions. However, fierce opposition from civil society, developers, and academic platforms like Hugging Face stalled the proposal, which entered co-decision without timeline or guarantee of adoption.

Efforts to advance **Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)** continued amid heightened resistance from Central European states, further delaying implementation. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech Sovereignty Package** remained frozen, weakening strategic autonomy. **ai_capability** rose to 55.0, driven by diffusion and offensive innovation, while **openweight_capability** reached 43.0. **ai_safety** stagnated at 29.0, and **ai_sovereignty** declined to 19.0 as dependency on external AI deepened. Political capital held at 43.0, but public sentiment fell to 38.0, eroding trust and eliminating legitimacy gains. The EU’s response, though initiated, was fragmented and reactive, widening the gap between emerging threats and effective governance.
