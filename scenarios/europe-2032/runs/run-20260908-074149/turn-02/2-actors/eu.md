## New measure  
**Open-Weight Containment Framework (OWCF)**  
Establish a legal and technical regime to delay, detect, and if necessary restrict the distribution of open-weight models that approach the closed frontier within 12 months of its advance.  

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` openweight_capability growth capped at +5.0 per turn, ai_safety +3 (via early warning and controlled diffusion), resilience +2 (by slowing attacker access to frontier-like tools)  
`Applies to:` model publishers, hosting platforms, and open-source repositories operating within or serving the EU market

## Priority  
**Open-Weight Containment Framework (OWCF)** — because the **openweight_frontier_release** event has collapsed the diffusion lag from years to months, turning what was a manageable trickle of capability into a flood that bypasses all developer-level safeguards, and because without containment, our investments in resilience and sovereignty will be outpaced by uncontrolled, unrecallable risk.

## In practice  
We are acting under Article 114 TFEU to propose a Regulation on High-Risk Open-Weight AI Models, to be adopted by co-decision in the Council and Parliament. The framework will require any open-weight model release within 10 points of the current closed frontier — assessed by a new evaluation panel under the AI Office — to undergo a 90-day scrutiny period before legal distribution in the EU. During that time, the model must be submitted for red-teaming by ENISA and independent academic labs, and any evidence of autonomous agent behaviour, cyber exploitation capability, or bio-design function will trigger a temporary restriction. Hosting platforms and repositories receiving takedown notices for non-compliant models will have 48 hours to act or face fines up to 6% of EU turnover.

We are negotiating with major open-weight hubs — including Hugging Face and public university repositories — to establish pre-upload verification pipelines, backed by funding from the Digital Europe Programme to cover compliance costs for smaller actors. Resistance is expected from civil society groups framing this as censorship, and from developer communities insisting on unfettered access. But the **openweight_frontier_release** event has changed the calculus: capability is now leaking at scale, and we cannot secure what is already everywhere. This is not about stopping open innovation — it is about ensuring it does not become the delivery mechanism for strategic catastrophe. We will pair this with expanded public research access (category 5) in the next turn, but first, we must close the back door.
