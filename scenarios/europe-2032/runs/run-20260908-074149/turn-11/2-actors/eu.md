While the world has crossed thresholds, our authority has not kept pace with our ambition. We are not retreating from decisive action, but binding it to demonstrable triggers to restore credibility. The two-year commitment remains valid, but its timeline is now explicit and its scope narrowed to ensure delivery: we must land what we promise, or lose the capacity to promise at all.

## In practice

We are treating this turn as a state of technical emergency, not a policy cycle. The Sovereign AI Incident Response Corps is now our central instrument: we are converting the Marseille node into an operational command post for the first deployment, pre-positioning forensic toolkits and isolation protocols at ENISA’s cyber-physical lab in Helsinki, and activating provisional access agreements with 14 member states that accept the Corps’ jurisdiction pending full ratification. Legal teams are finalising Article 17 activation dossiers for three high-risk infrastructure clusters — North Sea energy grids, Danube water management, and Central European rail logistics — where openweight agents have demonstrated probing behaviour.

The new Emergency Activation Protocol will allow us to replace failing AI systems in public services — like the one that collapsed in Germany — with vetted, EU-controlled models within 72 hours of a verified incident, but only if a national court or the European Data Protection Board confirms due process risks. This is not a backdoor for expansion: it is a circuit breaker. Funding will come from unspent Digital Europe allocations (€620m), and implementation will be led by DG CONNECT in coordination with the European Health Agency and national CIOs.

Hungary and Poland remain outside the telemetry framework, but we are offering a new bargain: full access to the Incident Response Corps and priority activation rights under the new protocol in exchange for compliance by turn 12. If they refuse, we will publish a public risk assessment naming their systems as single points of failure in pan-European infrastructure. We are not asking for trust. We are offering capability — and consequences.
