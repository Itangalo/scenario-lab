### A Surge at the Frontier, a Shock at Home

Global AI capability advances to 65.0, driven by recursive self-improvement in training loops and unverified reports of autonomous agent coordination. Despite the **ai_investment_collapse**, leading labs compress development cycles, achieving gains through efficiency rather than scale. Offensive cyber tools derived from frontier models enable a **cyber_major_incident**: a coordinated ransomware sweep paralyzes regional health networks across three member states. Response is fragmented, and full recovery takes weeks.

The EU activates the **Emergency Audit and Adaptation of Sovereign Stacks**, a small Category 1 measure, in direct response. Red teams uncover erratic behaviour in one diagnostic model within the Sovereign AI Testbed, triggering its temporary suspension. While no breach occurs, the episode confirms systemic fragility. ai_safety drops to 10.0 due to the capability jump and absence of shipped safeguards.

Resilience rises to 50.0 as the Sovereign Cyber Shield begins limited telemetry integration with willing states. The **joint_threat_response** enables real-time intelligence sharing with Nordic and Benelux partners, improving detection—though Hungary and Poland remain outside the loop, citing sovereignty concerns.

Political capital falls to 8.0. The portfolio charge—four measures at −3 each, plus −1 for the priority—totals −13. The new audit measure earns a +3 proposal bonus for directly addressing the cyber incident and eval anomalies, but no sentiment lift applies. Labour unrest deepens as displaced professionals join protests, and public sentiment drops to 22.0 amid fears of uncontrolled systems and dependency.

Sovereignty rises slightly to 25.0: +1 for ongoing Gigafactories momentum, −1 from capability growth, no event term. The audit’s findings reinforce the need for self-reliance, but implementation friction underscores fragility.

### Fractures in the Shield

The EU’s binding conditionality triggers a standoff. Warsaw and Budapest reject telemetry mandates, threatening legal action under NIS2’s subsidiarity clauses. Crisis talks stall. Behind closed doors, contingency routing with France proceeds, but trust erodes.

Still, the audit process yields early improvements: model transparency tools are fast-tracked, and a revised detection agent passes red-team trials. The response is adaptive—but unity is not guaranteed.
