## New measure
**Emergency Audit and Adaptation of Sovereign Stacks**  
Activate a real-time review and stress-testing protocol for all sovereign AI systems in operation, triggered by the **cyber_major_incident** and **eval_anomaly_reports**, to assess control integrity, detect emergent behaviour, and adapt safeguards within 90 days.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases ai_safety (+5–10) by forcing transparency into live systems, detects control failures before they cascade, and strengthens public trust by demonstrating responsiveness to anomalies.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, ENISA, national AI oversight bodies, and operators of sovereign AI systems under the Testbed and Cyber Shield.

## Priority  
**Sovereign Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure** — because the **cyber_major_incident** has made it the only measure directly confronting an active, large-scale threat, and because its failure would collapse both public confidence and the credibility of the entire sovereign stack.

## In practice  
We are activating emergency protocols under Article 17 of the AI Act and the NIS2 Directive to fast-track the audit mandated by our new measure. ENISA, in coordination with the European Defence Agency and national cybersecurity agencies, will conduct live evaluations of the Cyber Shield’s detection models and the underlying testbed stacks, using red-team agents derived from the leaked eval anomalies. This is not a paper exercise: any model found to exhibit uncontrolled behaviour or to degrade under adversarial load will be taken offline, and its developers—public or private—will be required to submit revised versions under stricter monitoring. We are allocating €1.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility to fund rapid iteration, with implementation overseen by a crisis cell reporting directly to the Commission’s Executive Vice-President for A Europe Fit for the Digital Age.

At the same time, we are enforcing integration of the Cyber Shield across all critical operators who receive EU funding. Germany and the Netherlands have already signed on; resistance remains in Hungary and Poland, where national leaders frame the Shield as an infringement on sovereignty. We are countering with binding conditionality: access to cohesion funds and critical infrastructure grants now requires full telemetry sharing and integration with the Shield’s detection layer by turn 9. No opt-outs. This is non-negotiable: if a member state will not defend the network, it cannot expect to be defended by it. Behind the scenes, we are also accelerating contingency planning with France and Belgium for alternative routing of critical data flows, in case disruption forces isolation of compromised nodes. The moment of testing has arrived. We will not outsource our defence, and we will not pretend resilience is optional.
