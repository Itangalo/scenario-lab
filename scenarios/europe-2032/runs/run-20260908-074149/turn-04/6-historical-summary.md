The EU entered crisis mode after being locked out of a critical AI model, disrupting healthcare and government operations. In response, the Commission launched the **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation (ESCM)** to build a sovereign alternative within 18 months, pooling public compute resources and accelerating procurement. However, legal challenges from two member states over data access and voluntary participation limited expected capacity to 60%. The **Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)** was fully implemented across key infrastructure, and a cross-border red-team exercise improved coordination, though readiness gaps persisted. Public anxiety surged over technological dependence, job displacement from automated law and clinical coding, and the spread of a biotoxin design via open models.

Despite the emergency, institutional fragmentation deepened: the **Open-Weight Containment Framework (OWCF)** stalled as Parliament rejected pre-upload screening, even with liability protections. Germany ratified the **Joint Semiconductor Export Coordination Initiative (JSECI)** under pressure, but Taiwan’s role remained informal, and grey-market chip flows continued unabated.
