### Emergency Mobilisation, Fractured Response

The EU declared crisis mode after being abruptly locked out of the world’s most capable AI model. Hospitals using it for diagnostic support and ministries relying on its planning functions faced immediate disruption. The Commission invoked Article 122 TFEU to launch the **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation (ESCM)**, aiming to deploy a sovereign alternative within 18 months by consolidating public compute and fast-tracking procurement. However, legal challenges emerged immediately: two member states contested the mandate for dataset access, citing national sovereignty over health and transport data. Participation in the compute pool remained voluntary, and early estimates suggest only 60% of targeted capacity will be secured.

Meanwhile, the **Cyber Shield for Critical Operators** was formally completed, with all designated energy, water, and port operators implementing baseline detection protocols. A cross-border red-team exercise revealed uneven readiness — some systems failed within minutes — but the drill itself boosted coordination. Still, resilience gains were partial.

Public sentiment worsened as news of the access denial sparked headlines framing Europe as technologically dependent and exposed. Automation advances in law and clinical coding, now verifiable and rapidly iterated, accelerated job displacement fears. The biotoxin design circulating online deepened anxiety about uncontrolled open models.

The **Open-Weight Containment Framework** negotiations stalled despite the public health argument; the European Parliament refused to accept pre-upload screening even with liability shields. Germany finally ratified JSECI under pressure, but Taiwan’s involvement remained informal, limited to technical exchanges via IMEC. Grey-market chip flows continued.

AI capability advanced to 59.0, driven by automated improvement in newly verifiable domains. With no safety breakthrough and rising capability, assurance eroded further. Open-weight models gained ground, reaching 46.5, as leaked tools enabled sophisticated misuse.

Political capital fell sharply under the weight of five large measures, including the new priority. The emergency move did not unify the bloc — instead, it highlighted fragmentation.
