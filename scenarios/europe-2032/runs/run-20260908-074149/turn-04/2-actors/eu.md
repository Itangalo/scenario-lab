## New measure  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation (ESCM)**  
Launch an emergency programme to deploy at least one EU-controlled, high-capability AI system within 18 months by repurposing existing public compute, fast-tracking procurement, and mandating access to restricted datasets for public interest models.

## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation (ESCM)** — because the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has exposed a fatal vulnerability in the Union’s strategic posture, and because without sovereign access to capable AI, every other measure — containment, coordination, resilience — becomes contingent on the goodwill of others.

## In practice  
We are invoking Article 122 TFEU and the crisis clause of the Digital Europe Programme to launch the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation. The plan is simple: consolidate all available public compute — EuroHPC clusters, national research facilities, and decommissioned commercial hardware — under a unified sovereign stack, managed by a new taskforce hosted at DG CONNECT and co-staffed by the AI Office and ENISA. We will fast-track procurement exemptions for GPU imports under the JSECI carve-out for public security, and mandate dataset access from health, transport and energy sectors for training public-interest models under strict governance. Our legal basis is emergency continuity of state function, and we are offering participating member states first right of use for domestic critical services.

Simultaneously, we are pressing forward on the Open-Weight Containment Framework through trilogue negotiations, using the biotoxin incident wave to justify pre-upload screening as a public health measure rather than censorship. We are offering Hugging Face additional liability shielding and co-governance on the evaluation panel in exchange for operational compliance. On JSECI, we are leveraging the grey-market surge to demand full ratification from Germany and formalise Taiwan’s role through a back-to-back research agreement with IMEC, sidestepping diplomatic recognition. The Cyber Shield for Critical Operators is now complete — we are shifting from rollout to exercise, with the first cross-border red-team drill scheduled for June. But make no mistake: this turn, everything bends toward restoring what was lost when the plug was pulled. We are no longer building autonomy. We are reclaiming it.
