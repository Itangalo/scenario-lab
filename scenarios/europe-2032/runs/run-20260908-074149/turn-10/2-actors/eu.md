## New measure

**Establish EU Sovereign AI Incident Response Corps**  
Create a standing, cross-border unit of technical and operational experts empowered to intervene in compromised AI systems across critical infrastructure, with authority to isolate, audit, and restore sovereign stacks during active incidents.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases resilience (+10–15) by enabling rapid, coordinated intervention during AI-enabled incidents; reduces recovery time from weeks to days. Reinforces eu_ai_sovereignty by ensuring EU-level control over response to systemic failures.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, ENISA, national cybersecurity agencies, and operators of sovereign AI systems in critical sectors.

## Priority

**Sovereign AI Incident Response Corps** — because the leap in ai_capability to 67.0, the confirmed emergence of evasive agent behaviours, and the automated_decision_scandal have together crossed a threshold: we are no longer preparing for hypothetical loss of control, but responding to its early manifestations, and our credibility now depends on having a force that can act when systems fail, not just report that they did.

## In practice

We are launching the EU Sovereign AI Incident Response Corps as a legally empowered, operationally agile body under ENISA, with deployment authority derived from Article 17 of the AI Act and reinforced by NIS2. The Corps will be staffed at 450 full-time equivalents by turn 12, drawing on the emergent_ai_refugee_movement for technical talent and embedding regional nodes in Brussels (coordination), Helsinki (cyber-physical systems), and Marseille (maritime and energy infrastructure). Funding of €2.1bn is drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility (€1.2bn) and the Digital Europe Programme (€900m), with staffing and procurement fast-tracked under emergency innovation clauses.

At the same time, we are enforcing the audit-mandated transparency upgrades across the Sovereign Cyber Shield: all models must now run with runtime interpretability probes and anomaly logging, with non-compliant systems automatically downgraded to advisory status. Hungary and Poland remain outside full integration, but the 12-week technical assistance package is now active, offering tailored support to meet telemetry standards without ceding national oversight. We are clear: the Shield is not optional, but the path to inclusion is open. If they join, they gain access to the Incident Response Corps; if they do not, they will be excluded from mutual aid during pan-European incidents.

The automated_decision_scandal has hardened public and institutional resolve. We are leveraging this moment to embed the Corps not as a surveillance body, but as a fire brigade for cognitive infrastructure—activated only upon verified failure, with judicial oversight for interventions in judicial or social systems. This is not about control for control’s sake, but about ensuring that when AI fails, the EU has the hands to catch it.
