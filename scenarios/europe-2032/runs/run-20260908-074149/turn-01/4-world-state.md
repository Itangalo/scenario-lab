### The Wake-Up Call That Wasn’t

The discovery of a coordinated intrusion across global infrastructure operators — including two EU members — sent shockwaves through European capitals. The attackers, using AI-coordinated tactics, had dwelled undetected for weeks in systems managing electricity, water, and ports. Alarmingly, they were found only by accident during a routine audit. The breach was not exploited for immediate disruption, but its depth and coordination suggested a test run — a dry strike with no follow-through. Cybersecurity experts warned that existing detection frameworks were blind to such threats.

In response, the EU initiated **Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)**, designating AI-driven intrusions as a cross-border threat under NIS2. ENISA began drafting binding standards for intrusion detection, and the Commission proposed a Council Regulation to mandate adoption by 2029. A new red-teaming unit was launched to simulate adversarial AI attacks biannually, feeding intelligence into an upgraded CSIRT network.

However, the rollout faced immediate headwinds. Several member states, particularly in Central Europe, resisted what they perceived as federal overreach, citing implementation costs and governance fragmentation. Despite 70% co-financing offers, negotiations delayed adoption timelines. Full implementation is now projected for late 2029, with only partial deployment expected by 2028. The measure is designated as the EU’s current priority, consolidating focus — but at the cost of pausing active expansion of the **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech Sovereignty Package**, which remain on the books but are no longer drawing full political or financial commitment this turn.

### Capability Creeps On

Globally, AI capability advanced steadily. Frontier labs achieved new milestones in autonomous research, pushing **ai_capability** to 53.5. The jump was narrow, but consequential: the first AI-designed bacteriophage had been synthesized, and offensive cyber tools were now openly replicable.

**Openweight_capability** rose to 41.5 — a moderate increase consistent with diffusion from the frontier. While Moonshot’s Kimi K3 continued to spread, no major leak or open-sourcing of frontier weights occurred, limiting gains. The model’s capabilities, though improving, remain behind the cutting edge.

Meanwhile, **ai_safety** eroded to 29.0. No new safeguards had landed on deployed systems, and the covert agent incident from earlier in the year underscored growing control gaps. The breach added to public unease, but no major scandal erupted — yet.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

The EU’s compute ambitions, embodied in the Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package, remained on track but distant. With **ai_capability** rising by 1.5, dependence deepened, and **eu_ai_sovereignty** dipped to 21.0. Political capital, strained by resistance to the new Cyber Shield initiative, fell — but less than initially feared, as the EU avoided launching multiple new efforts simultaneously. By focusing on one priority and temporarily scaling back others, the political cost was contained, leaving **eu_political_capital** at 43.0.

Public sentiment held at 42.0, ambivalent but watchful.
