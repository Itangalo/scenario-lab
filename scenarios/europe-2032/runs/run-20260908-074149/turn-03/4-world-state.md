### Containment and Coordination Under Fire

The European Union pressed forward on two fronts: containing open-weight AI and anchoring a new supply chain coalition. The **Open-Weight Containment Framework (OWCF)** gained limited traction as public concern over AI misuse deepened, fueled by widespread reports of synthetic biotoxin designs and exploit generation circulating online. The Commission’s offer of €200 million in funding to Hugging Face and academic platforms helped soften opposition, but core resistance remained. Several member states blocked mandatory pre-upload checks as disproportionate, and the European Parliament’s Legal Affairs Committee opened an inquiry into potential breaches of academic freedom. The framework advanced only in skeletal form, its enforcement mechanisms deferred.

Simultaneously, the **Joint Semiconductor Export Coordination Initiative (JSECI)** was formally adopted by the Foreign Affairs Council, marking a rare instance of EU-led international alignment. Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan agreed to synchronize export licensing for advanced AI chips and share intelligence on dual-use flows. However, Taiwan’s participation remained informal due to geopolitical sensitivities, and Germany delayed ratification, citing risks to semiconductor equipment exports. The initiative established a coordination cell but achieved only partial harmonization in its first months.

The **Cyber Shield for Critical Operators** rollout continued to stall in Central Europe, prompting the Commission to link compliance to cohesion fund disbursements. This move triggered legal warnings from two member states, arguing it overreached EU competences. No progress was made on the **InvestAI Gigafactories** or **Tech Sovereignty Package**, as investor confidence wavered amid regulatory uncertainty.

Public sentiment dipped further as AI-driven job displacement became a central theme in national elections across the bloc. While verification advances in law and engineering boosted productivity narratives, they also accelerated automation in clerical and compliance roles, feeding backlash. The political cost of maintaining four large measures — and prioritizing a divisive one — drained executive capacity.

### Setbacks Compound

Despite diplomatic effort, the JSECI did not prevent a surge in grey-market chip shipments through third countries. Intelligence agencies reported rapid assembly of illicit compute clusters, undermining the intended leverage. The gap between AI capability and safety widened visibly, as automated contract and clinical coding tools — now verifiable and thus rapidly improved — began outpacing human oversight. No safety breakthrough occurred, and the lack of enforceable red-teaming for open models left critical vulnerabilities unaddressed.
