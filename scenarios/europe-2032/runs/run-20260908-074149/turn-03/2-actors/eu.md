## In practice  
We are advancing the Open-Weight Containment Framework through co-decision under Article 114 TFEU, with the AI Office now staffing a rapid evaluation panel to assess model capability thresholds and ENISA preparing red-teaming protocols for the first submissions. We are offering Hugging Face and university repositories a €200 million Digital Europe Programme fund to build pre-upload verification pipelines, with legal liability shielding for good-faith compliance — a compromise meant to defuse the censorship narrative while preserving operational teeth. The Council’s Internal Market and Industry formation is our primary venue, but we are also briefing the Justice and Home Affairs Council on the biotoxin and cyber exploitation findings to anchor the security rationale. Resistance remains fierce, but the **campaign_backlash** event has shifted public sentiment leftward on restriction, and we are leveraging that window.

Simultaneously, we are formalizing the emergent **Joint Semiconductor Export Coordination Initiative** through the Foreign Affairs Council and the Competitiveness Council, with the Commission’s Joint Research Centre providing technical coordination. This is not alignment with U.S. controls, but a middle-power alternative: we are insisting on independent assessment of dual-use risk, mutual recognition of licensing decisions, and a shared early-warning system for compute cluster assembly. The goal is to make the EU a node, not a follower, in global supply chain governance. We are offering Taiwan reciprocal access to EU research-grade fabrication tools in exchange for transparency, and we are using the threat of unilateral EU export audits to bring reluctant member states on board.

The **Cyber Shield for Critical Operators** continues its rollout, though delays in Central Europe persist; we are now tying cohesion funding disbursements to compliance milestones, a move that risks legal challenge but reflects the urgency of the **cyber_test_shot** event. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech Sovereignty Package** remain on track, though their political cover is thinning — which is why this turn’s priority must be not just containment, but credibility. We are acting under fire, and we are choosing to be seen.
