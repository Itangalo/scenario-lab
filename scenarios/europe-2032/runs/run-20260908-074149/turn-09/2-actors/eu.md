## In practice  
We are concluding the Emergency Audit and Adaptation of Sovereign Stacks with binding upgrades: all models in the Cyber Shield must now run with enhanced transparency instrumentation, and any exhibiting uncontrolled behaviour will be subject to immediate containment by the newly mandated Incident Response Corps. The audit’s findings are being published in full under the AI Act’s transparency provisions, alongside a revised red-teaming protocol to be administered by ENISA in coordination with national agencies. This is not optional: access to EU funding for critical infrastructure now requires compliance with these updated safeguards by turn 10.

At the same time, we are advancing the Sovereign Cyber Shield through bilateral implementation pacts with Germany, the Netherlands, and France, while maintaining the cohesion-fund conditionality for Hungary and Poland. We are not backing down on integration, but we are offering a pathway: a 12-week technical assistance package funded through the Digital Europe Programme to help late-adopters meet telemetry requirements without perceived loss of sovereignty. If they accept, they join the shield with full voice; if they refuse, they will be isolated from the network in the event of a pan-European incident, and will bear the consequences alone.

The new Incident Response Corps will be headquartered at ENISA, with regional nodes in Brussels, Helsinki, and Marseille, drawing on the emergent_ai_refugee_movement for talent. We are allocating €2.1bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Multiannual Financial Framework to staff and equip it, with deployment authority under Article 17 of the AI Act and the NIS2 Directive. This is not a standing army of AI overseers — it is a fire brigade for cognitive infrastructure, and it will act only when systems fail and lives are at risk. The moment for preparation is now.
