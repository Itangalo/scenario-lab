## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Ensure the EU controls a sovereign AI stack capable of executing high-stakes public functions—clinical diagnostics, infrastructure resilience, and threat analysis—without dependency on foreign infrastructure, even under coercion or denial-of-service conditions.  
- Trigger: the completion of ESCM’s deployment window and the operationalisation of the Sovereign AI Testbed in critical domains  
- Grounds: The original commitment to sovereign access to end-to-end professional work has been overtaken by events. Capability at 62.0 means such systems exist globally, but the EU still lacks operational control over any stack that can function independently in high-consequence environments. The ASML export restriction confirms that compute sovereignty is not just about scale but about legal and physical autonomy. The testbed and ESCM have shifted from capacity-building to functional validation: the commitment must now reflect not just access, but *operational resilience in mission-critical contexts*. The cost of reversal is public credibility—having framed sovereignty as a matter of strategic independence, we now narrow it to what is both urgent and actionable.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late—provided we retain the capacity to course-correct when new thresholds are crossed.  
- Grounds: The surge in openweight_capability to 50.0 and the emergent labour unrest reveal that acting under uncertainty must now include mechanisms for rapid reassessment. The original commitment stands, but it must be tempered with institutional agility: we cannot afford to lock in strategies that become obsolete within six months. This refinement does not weaken resolve—it embeds learning into action.

## New measure
**Sovereign Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure**  
Deploy AI-driven, EU-operated intrusion detection and autonomous response systems across energy, health, and transport networks, using hardened models trained within the Sovereign AI Testbed.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases resilience (+10–15), reduces systemic vulnerability to open-weight cyber threats, and indirectly supports eu_ai_sovereignty by demonstrating autonomous operational control in high-risk domains.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, ENISA, national CERTs, and critical operators in energy, healthcare, and transport across all member states.

## Priority
**Sovereign AI Testbed for Critical Functions** — because it is now the only active measure that bridges sovereignty and operational resilience, and because the cyber Shield cannot be trusted until its models are validated on sovereign stacks that are both capable and secure.

## In practice
We are declaring ESCM and InvestAI Gigafactories complete this turn. ESCM has delivered its core objective: a sovereign stack is now operational, running on repurposed HPC clusters in France and Finland, with a narrow but functional capability in logistics and diagnostics. It was not the full-scale compute grid we envisioned—legal resistance in Poland and Hungary limited data pooling, and the ASML blockade delayed GPU delivery—but we used synthetic data and federated learning to cross the threshold. The stack is not frontier-level, but it is independent, auditable, and under EU legal jurisdiction. The Gigafactories programme ends with two sites under construction (Lithuania, Spain), but private investment fell short due to global supply constraints and investor caution after the US alliance terms were revealed. We are not cancelling further investment, but the emergency phase is over: future expansion will be demand-driven, not crisis-funded.

The Sovereign AI Testbed is now live in pilot mode, hosting diagnostic workflows for oncology screening in three EU hospitals and grid stress simulations in Germany and Italy. We are using the testbed to train and validate the new **Sovereign Cyber Shield**, our new measure: a resilience-focused AI system that will monitor and respond to cyber threats in real time across critical sectors. Unlike commercial tools, it will run entirely on EU-controlled infrastructure, with models fine-tuned on adversarial scenarios generated within the testbed. ENISA is leading integration with national CERTs, and we are mandating participation for all operators receiving EU critical infrastructure grants. This is not optional: from turn 8, access to EU cybersecurity funding will require integration with the Shield’s detection layer.

Public sentiment remains fragile. The emergent labour unrest has forced us to accelerate the review of retraining programmes under the FlexAI Transition Facility—though no new measure is proposed yet, we are preparing a turn-8 initiative linking wage insurance to AI-driven job displacement. For now, we are betting that demonstrating sovereign utility—AI that protects, not just displaces—will shift the narrative. The Cyber Shield is not just a technical system; it is a signal: the EU will not outsource its survival.
