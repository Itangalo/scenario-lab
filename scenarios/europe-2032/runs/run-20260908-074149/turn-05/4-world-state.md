### US Election Outcome

The United States elects a new president committed to technological alliance. The administration pledges structured access to frontier AI systems for allied governments, joint safety evaluations, and shared incident reporting. Alignment on export controls and standards will be required, but the immediate promise of access offers relief — and a strategic dilemma.

### Emergency Compute Under Legal Strain

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation (ESCM) remains the EU’s top priority, but its legal foundation is fracturing. The Court of Justice has fast-tracked hearings on whether the Commission can mandate access to national health and transport datasets. With two member states holding firm, full data pooling is unlikely. In response, the Commission activates fallback protocols: synthetic data generation and federated learning will substitute where direct access fails. However, these workarounds will delay deployment and reduce initial capability.

Meanwhile, €12 billion in Digital Europe and Horizon funds have been redirected to GPU procurement under JSECI’s national security carve-out. Negotiations with NVIDIA and AMD for export-limited chips are ongoing, but progress is slow.

### Joint Response, Fragile Gains

The EU joins a new biosurveillance pact and shared cyber command, gaining real-time intelligence and coordinated response capacity. This joint threat response strengthens defences and lifts resilience, even as open-weight models cross a biological threshold — a viral vector design derived from leaked tools has been validated as feasible.

Verification widens further, with clinical coding and structural engineering now admitting automated checks. AI capability jumps past 60.0, accelerating progress in once-supervised domains. With no safety breakthrough, assurance erodes further.

Public sentiment holds steady despite growing anxiety. The Open-Weight Containment Framework has formally expired without renewal, leaving a governance gap. The US alliance eases pressure, but the Commission insists ESCM must continue — dependence, even on allies, is not sovereignty.
