## Priority
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation (ESCM)** — because the denial of frontier access remains an active crisis, and because without sovereign control over at least one capable stack, the Union cannot execute its own decisions in healthcare, security, or infrastructure planning, regardless of other coordination gains.

## In practice
We are doubling down on the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation, treating it as the operational core of our survival in this period of technological coercion. The legal challenges from two member states over data access are now before the Court of Justice, and we are preparing fallback protocols: if full dataset pooling is blocked, we will activate tiered training regimes using synthetic data and federated learning across national silos, coordinated by a new taskforce under DG CONNECT with embedded ENISA security oversight. We have redirected €12bn in Digital Europe and Horizon funds to fast-track GPU procurement under the JSECI carve-out for national security, and we are negotiating with NVIDIA and AMD for emergency waivers on export-limited chips, citing the public health and infrastructure continuity grounds activated under Article 122 TFEU.

At the same time, we are capitalising on the **joint_threat_response** event: the biosurveillance pact and shared cyber command offer real-time intelligence and mutual response capacity we could not build alone. We are embedding EU agencies in both structures, ensuring that our red-teaming and incident response now run in lockstep with allies. This boosts resilience and reduces the immediate pressure — but we are not letting it dilute the urgency of ESCM. The **election_alliance** shift in US posture offers access, yes, but on terms that demand alignment on export controls and standards — and that makes building our own capacity, not dependence on access, the only path to strategic autonomy. We are using the biotoxin incident to rally support for stronger open-weight controls, but the OWCF has stalled; we will not waste capital forcing a vote this turn. Instead, we are focusing on making ESCM irreversible — because if we lose this window, we lose the Union’s right to decide its own future.
