### Grey screens, thinner supplies
Spring brought a new American model family that made last year's benchmarks look dated overnight. The same week, Washington tightened chip and model licences again. Under the standing alliance terms, European buyers kept volume access on paper, but institutional quotas were rationed harder and delivery times slipped. For hospitals in Lyon and Gdansk already on fallback, there was no path back to the frontier system — only slower EU-hosted tools and manual double-checks.

Joint teams kept the degraded service running. Diagnostics continued with higher error rates, ministries worked around cut-off assistants, medical stockpiles were rationed to therapy queues. Officials repeated nightly what still ran. It held, but visibly worsened.

### Lawful again, and larger
A second welfare and insurance review widened the earlier court affair. Caseworkers described queues approved in seconds, logs generated and never read. The Commission again conceded the hardest point: the deployments sat outside the 2024 high-risk categories. Lawful, and wrong.

Redress offices paid first tranches through social funds while new queues formed. Restrictionist campaigns fused the affair with fenced construction sites: automated decisions beside fenced machines. Graduate-led protests blocked a site road for hours; police moved them on. Rumours of longer coordinated shutdowns circulated in local press without confirmation.

Placements as care aides, desk aides and night guards grew slightly in dividend cities, nowhere else. Trust, already collapsed, fell further. Brussels husbanded its last resources to finish the continuity and redress effort rather than start anything new — a holding action while capability leapt and supply narrowed.
