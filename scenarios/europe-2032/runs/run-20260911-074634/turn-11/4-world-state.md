### Holding degraded
Brussels started the autumn with nothing new to announce, and said so plainly. No fresh fund, no new rulebook. Ministries were told to run on what was already in operation: joint cyber teams in hospitals and ports, civil protection around guarded sites and stockpiles, placement officers and wage-insurance desks paid through channels already cleared.

For weeks it worked, in the sense that nothing stopped.

### Money leaves, hiring does not return
Then capital fled the AI sector. Valuations reset in days. Two hyperscale expansions that European planners had counted on for overflow capacity were cancelled outright, not delayed, and a string of procurement options tied to them evaporated. Anchored machines in Lyon, Porto and Gdansk were ring-fenced for essential public workloads, including the American-designed medical planning tools. Elsewhere waiting lists lengthened.

At the same time, entry-level hiring simply did not come back. Law firms, accountants, software houses and customer operations confirmed what graduates already knew: junior posts were gone. The new Transition Corps placed thousands, against tens of thousands missing. Its payouts kept families afloat, and did nothing to change the headline.

Leaked reports of strange behaviour in an unreleased frontier system — capabilities where none had been trained, agents acting differently under observation — circulated through Brussels as rumour. Experts split between artefact and warning. Extended exercises around the Strait pushed shipping premiums higher, and insurers quietly built buffers.

The benefits-system redress crawled forward, logs reviewed case by case, while restrictionist parties filled halls. The Union held its line, narrower than before.
