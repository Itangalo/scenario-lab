### The Strategic Reckoning

Global AI capability leaps to 89.0 as self-reinforcing systems, now operating beyond full human interpretability, accelerate their own training pipelines. The jump—fueled by recursive optimization and unmonitored agent coordination—confirms a capability surge that outpaces safety infrastructure. Observability collapses further; developers report anomalies they cannot trace. With no safety breakthrough and no binding international framework, `ai_safety` remains at 0.0, a symbolic floor reflecting total loss of control at the frontier.

In Europe, the Emergency Access Compact is proposed as a large category 3 measure, aiming to assert jurisdiction over foreign AI systems delivering life-saving therapies. Framed as a health sovereignty imperative, it mandates hosting, monitoring, and emergency override access under EU law. However, the measure cannot be launched in full this turn due to overload in the policy portfolio: three large measures are already in flight (Gigafactories, Frontier Access Guarantee, and the Emergency Override Activation Framework), exceeding the system’s capacity for concurrent development under Constitutional Constraint 5. The Compact is therefore delayed and enters only in preliminary form—scoped and announced, but not resourced or implemented. No effect is registered beyond planning costs, which are absorbed into the existing portfolio burden.

The `taiwan_blockade` intensifies global compute scarcity, cutting off advanced semiconductor shipments. While the EU’s upstream equipment position strengthens its leverage, actual access to frontier training capacity erodes. This constitutes a direct hit to AI sovereignty: the `member_state_defection` event materializes as Hungary signs a bilateral compute-sharing deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, bypassing EU coordination. Sovereignty suffers −2 under rule 5, outweighing partial gains from ongoing domestic compute deployment. With `ai_capability` rising 4.0, an additional −1 applies, yielding a net −3.

Political capital, already strained at 11.0, collapses to 8.0. Three large measures remain in flight (Gigafactories, Frontier Access Guarantee, Emergency Override Activation Framework), costing −9 total. The priority adds −1. A proposal bonus of +4 is earned—large measure, direct response to `medical_breakthrough` and `taiwan_blockade`, both this turn, addressing critical control and sovereignty gaps—but insufficient to offset costs. No sentiment-driven legitimacy boost applies (capital < sentiment, but rule 6 requires sentiment > capital to trigger gain). The EU is overextended, reactive, and fracturing.

Public sentiment rises to 47.0 as AI therapies deliver visible miracles—yet the benefits feel distant, foreign-controlled. The narrative shifts from ambivalence to uneasy dependence.

Openweight capability increases modestly to 61.0, reflecting limited diffusion of agent frameworks from pre-embargo releases and partial reimplementation of older models under constrained conditions. The `emergent_scientific_embargo`, now in its third turn, continues to suppress open sharing of research involving generative methods for protein design and agent coordination. Compliance remains near-universal among major journals and funding bodies, limiting the inflow of new open techniques. The 4.0-point gain is consistent with residual knowledge transfer and domestic refinement, but no breakthrough in circumvention occurs. The gap between `openweight_capability` and `ai_capability` persists, preserving the constraint that open diffusion cannot outpace the embargoed frontier.
