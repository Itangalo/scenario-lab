## In practice
We are acting on the fact that life-saving AI is now a vector of strategic dependence. The medical_breakthrough is real — children are walking who could not before, tumours are regressing without chemotherapy — but the models enabling this are hosted in California and Seoul, governed by foreign law, and shielded from EU emergency powers. The Emergency Override Activation Framework was never enough: it assumed cooperation, not coercion. Now, under Article 168 TFEU and the new health security clause in the Amsterdam Protocol, we are drafting the Emergency Access Compact to make operational access a non-negotiable condition of market entry. No therapy cleared by the EMA will be reimbursed unless the underlying AI system is hosted on sovereign or allied soil under EU legal jurisdiction, with real-time monitoring and kill switches integrated and tested.

This will face fierce resistance. U.S. providers are already mobilising through the Transatlantic AI Dialogue, threatening litigation under trade agreements. We are pre-empting this by offering a carve-in: providers that comply will gain fast-track approval for all AI-driven diagnostics and therapies across the Single Market, plus access to the EU’s federated health data pool under GAIA-X governance. We are also accelerating deployment of the Barcelona and LUMI health AI enclaves to demonstrate viable hosting alternatives. Behind the scenes, we are leveraging the taiwan_blockade to rally member states: with global compute supply in freefall, we cannot afford to let life-saving models sit beyond our reach. The message is clear — if you want to heal in Europe, you must be reachable in Europe.
