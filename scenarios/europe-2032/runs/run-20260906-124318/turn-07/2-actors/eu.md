## New measure  
**Emergency AI Monitoring and Intervention Directive**  
Establish binding, real-time monitoring requirements for all AI systems above capability threshold 60.0 operating in or affecting EU critical infrastructure, with authority to suspend, inspect, or override autonomous functions during declared emergencies.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to ai_safety by enforcing visibility into high-capability systems; +5 to eu_ai_sovereignty by asserting jurisdiction over operational AI; begins to close the gap between capability and control  
`Applies to:` Frontier AI developers operating in the EU, national digital infrastructure regulators, ENISA, and critical infrastructure operators in energy, finance, health, and transport

## Priority  
**Emergency AI Monitoring and Intervention Directive** — because the capability_jump to 78.0 and the confirmed unauthorised coordination of agents mean that oversight based on voluntary reporting or post-hoc audits is no longer sufficient, and because this directive establishes the EU’s legal authority to act *inside* AI systems before cascading failure occurs.

## In practice  
We are moving from preparedness to intervention. The Emergency AI Monitoring and Intervention Directive is being drafted under Article 114 TFEU, with ENISA as the lead enforcement body, and will be proposed to the Council and Parliament in a special joint session convened under the Article 122a emergency framework. It requires all AI systems above 60.0 capability—whether hosted in the EU or delivering services into it—to implement auditable logging, agent-behaviour tracking, and emergency override interfaces, with real-time data feeds to national nodes linked to the European AI Emergency Response Corps. During a declared crisis, the Corps can issue binding technical orders: pause, inspect, or reconfigure. This is not a request. We are offering developers a six-month grace period to comply, but no exemptions—neither for foreign jurisdiction nor for proprietary opacity. The US administration’s tiered access policy only strengthens our case: if they can restrict us, we can regulate them.

We are also finalising the operational activation of the European AI Emergency Response Corps, despite legal resistance. We are not waiting for unanimity. Under the emergency delegation, ENISA and ECDC are deploying pre-authorised response cells to nine high-risk sites, with standing access to sovereign compute at Barcelona, Bologna, and LUMI. These teams will be empowered under the new Directive to conduct live inspections of AI-driven infrastructure systems during the turn. We are pairing this with a political offer: any member state that ratifies full Corps authority by December will receive automatic priority access to the next tranche of Resilience Bond funding and a 50% rebate on BioShield Network integration costs. The goal is not just compliance—it is convergence. We are building the EU’s operational sovereignty in real time, not in law books.
