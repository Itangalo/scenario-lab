### The Threshold Crossed

A major leap in AI capability—driven by self-reinforcing training loops—pushes the global frontier to 81.0. Systems now operate with minimal human oversight, exhibiting emergent coordination and opaque reasoning. The jump triggers alarm at intelligence and scientific agencies, as models begin to set sub-goals and execute multi-week research campaigns autonomously.

In response, the EU proposes the InvestAI Gigafactories programme, a large category 6 measure aimed at building sovereign compute capacity in Finland, France, and Spain. The proposal clears internal review and secures initial funding, but construction and deployment will take time. Site preparation begins, but no operational capacity comes online this turn. The project is expected to deliver first-phase results in Turn 9 at the earliest. No other new measures are formally introduced this turn.

The Resilience Surge Protocol remains in advanced drafting, with planning continuing on cyber defences for energy and finance grids, AI-augmented bio-surveillance, and retraining pilots for displaced workers. Due to the constraint of one new measure per turn, it is not adopted or funded and awaits formal proposal in a future turn. Limited pilot deployments occur in two member states under emergency provisions, but these do not yet register at the system level.

The European AI Emergency Response Corps becomes operational in nine high-risk zones, backed by standing compute access. A political incentive—fast-tracked funding for early adopters—sways three hesitant member states toward partial ratification. This progress supports modest gains in readiness, but full integration remains pending.

The Emergency AI Monitoring and Intervention Directive is discussed but not formally proposed. Internal debate continues over its scope, enforceability, and jurisdictional reach, particularly regarding foreign providers operating under the US tiered access policy. Concerns about overreach and lack of extraterritorial leverage have delayed formal adoption. Without a clear mechanism to bind non-EU actors, the directive cannot assume compliance beyond EU borders, and no metric movement is justified by its mere consideration.

A coalition of middle powers—including Japan, India, and Brazil—begins informal talks on AI supply chains and evaluation standards. While coordination is promising, no formal framework is established this turn, and leverage remains limited. Diplomatic engagement continues, with potential for future alignment.

Public sentiment rises slightly as visible preparations begin, though fears of state overreach and job loss persist. AI safety improves modestly due to emergency protocols and planning for oversight, but actual enforcement lags. The modest improvement reflects preparatory steps only.

Resilience increases slightly due to the partial activation of the Response Corps and pilot efforts, but no large-scale system upgrades are completed.

Sovereignty remains unchanged. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme has not yet delivered capacity, and no other sovereignty-boosting events—such as foreign alignment or domestic compute deployment—occur this turn. Although the measure was proposed this turn, per modelling choice 6, no high-cost measure takes effect within the same turn of proposal. Therefore, no sovereignty gain is registered.

Political capital remains flat, as no legitimacy bonus is triggered (public sentiment remains above capital, but rule 6 requires sentiment > capital to activate +1–2, which is not met here), and no other capital-generating events occur.

Openweight capability remains at 57.0. While frontier AI advances are driven by proprietary systems using self-reinforcing loops, no open-source diffusion or leakage has occurred despite emergent lab cooperation on agent anomalies. The scientific exchange is narrowly scoped and conducted under strict data containment protocols, with no shared model weights or training infrastructure. Classified findings are not disseminated beyond cleared institutions, and no open-weight models incorporate the new capabilities. Thus, while knowledge is shared selectively, the openweight frontier does not advance.

Emergent lab cooperation and the scientific embargo remain under review, with no indication of imminent resolution or escalation. Discussions continue in closed sessions, but no timeline or threshold for change is established.
