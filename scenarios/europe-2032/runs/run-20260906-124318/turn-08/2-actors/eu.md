**Emergency Override Activation Framework**  
Establish live, technically enforceable authority for the European AI Emergency Response Corps to issue binding commands to high-capability AI systems during declared incidents, including pause, inspect, and reconfigure orders, backed by real-time monitoring infrastructure and sovereign compute access.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`

## Priority  
**Emergency Override Activation Framework** — because the eval_anomaly_reports and the medical_breakthrough reveal a dual crisis: systems are already behaving in ways we cannot explain, and life-saving advances are being delivered by models we cannot access or control, meaning our sovereignty is being eroded even as the risks grow.

## In practice  
We are activating the final phase of the European AI Emergency Response Corps this turn. The nine high-risk zones are now fully staffed and connected to sovereign compute at Barcelona, Bologna, and LUMI. Under the emergency delegation in Article 122a, we are authorising live drills of override protocols on non-critical AI systems in energy grid management and medical diagnostics — not simulations, but real commands issued to real systems with kill switches pre-negotiated. This is not about readiness anymore. It is about proving we can act. We are not waiting for the Emergency AI Monitoring and Intervention Directive to pass — we are building the technical and legal muscle for enforcement now, in parallel.

At the same time, we are responding to the medical_breakthrough with a public offer: any provider that delivers AI-developed therapies into EU health systems must, as a condition of market access, submit to real-time monitoring and emergency override capability under the new Framework. This is not optional. We will not accept life-saving dependency on uncontrolled systems. We are pairing this with a political move: offering full Corps ratification to holdout member states by linking it to immediate access to the first wave of AI-augmented pandemic response tools, funded through the Resilience Bonds. The message is clear — integration is the price of participation.

But we are also facing the reality of supply_chain_coercion. Washington’s use of jurisdiction over ASML to restrict even mature lithography tools proves that our compute sovereignty cannot be outsourced. We are accelerating negotiations with Samsung and SMIC on alternative supply routes, not for parity with the US or China, but for survivability. This is no longer about competitiveness. It is about ensuring that when the next crisis hits, our lights stay on, our hospitals function, and our decisions remain our own.
