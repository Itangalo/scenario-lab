### The plug pulled
In February, access to the leading American model went dark for European users with almost no warning. No detailed reason, no appeal channel. Hospital planning tools, ministry copilots and contractor workflows built on the foreign service failed at login. The Commission told ministries to fall back to European-hosted systems and locally run openly available models, with paper procedures where nothing else worked.

The fallback mostly held where the municipal repair teams were present, and failed where they were not. In the two regions that had cut waiting lists, young corps staff kept triage desks running on stripped-down local models, typing around what the machines could no longer do. Elsewhere clinics cancelled planning sessions and firms lost weeks of integration work. Evening news split between anger at Washington and anger at Brussels for having built critical routines on a switch someone else owned.

### A shared watchtower
The same spring offered a partial shelter. After months of parallel intrusions and the unsettling genome-model paper that rattled biosecurity circles, a group of states pooled live intrusion telemetry and pathogen-sample sharing, inviting the Union in. The Council accepted through conclusions, naming its cyber agency and disease centre as nodes, feeding in logs from rebuilt town networks and samples from hospital labs.

Technicians said the shared feed caught scanning campaigns faster than national centres had alone. It did not restore lost model access, and it did not answer the biosecurity argument — methodologists quarrelling over whether a non-expert could really follow the published design — but isolation drills in corps municipalities gave mayors something to point to.

### The lever tightens
In the Netherlands the servicing dispute ground on. Shipments slowed further, maintenance lapsed on older machines, and executives openly warned of production breaks while ministers warned of industrial-policy collapse. The Council repeated its common line and delayed decisions where it could.

By June queues moved in places where people had been sent, and stopped where only software had been promised. A widely copied toolkit for running open models disconnected on city servers spread among communes, useful and unvetted, outside any common standard.
