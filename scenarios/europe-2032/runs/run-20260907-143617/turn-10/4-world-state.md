### Stalled Trust, Deepening Opaqueness

The EU enters 2031 in crisis mode. Public trust in AI continues to erode after the welfare scandal, and the Commission’s pivot toward accountability fails to gain traction. The **Public Redress and Auditability Directive** is formally proposed, aiming to restore legitimacy through enforceable redress mechanisms. But with political capital at just 6.0, the measure struggles to command attention. Member states resist new compliance burdens, and legal services warn that the directive may clash with national administrative procedures.

The **Integrity Audit Expansion**, now the named priority, continues from the previous turn—no new measure is launched alongside it, preserving compliance with the one-new-measure limit. However, its foundations are undermined by the sudden collapse of human-readable reasoning in leading systems. The **opaque_reasoning** event renders traditional audit methods obsolete overnight. ENISA and EAAD scramble to adapt, but no alternative verification framework is ready. Audits continue in name, but their practical value diminishes, contributing to a slow erosion of systemic safeguards.

Meanwhile, signs of a **capability plateau** emerge: a flagship model release underperforms expectations, and prices for high-end inference drop sharply, suggesting stagnation at the frontier. Yet this does not halt the diffusion of existing capabilities. With no major open-weight release blocked, and containment stalled in court, **openweight_capability** rises modestly to 54.0—driven by wider deployment and fine-tuning of previously available models, not a leap in base capability. The gap between closed and open narrows, but not abruptly.

Resilience declines as no new category 6 measure lands, and the **capability_plateau_evidence** does not offset ongoing risks. Cyber defences remain patchy, especially in healthcare and local government. A wave of AI-powered phishing attacks hits mid-tier firms, overwhelming national CERTs.

Sovereignty continues its slow erosion. No new compute comes online, and US **CONSOLIDATION** posture ensures continued dependency. The Polish cluster delay becomes permanent as funding is diverted, deepening fragmentation.

Political capital sinks further. The completion of prior measures brings no relief—only fatigue. The portfolio charge remains high, and the new small measure offers no immediate payoff. Public sentiment dips again as media frames the redress proposal as “too little, too late.”

### A Union in Retreat

The EU is no longer shaping the AI era—it is reacting to it. Its tools are outdated, its unity fraying, and its legitimacy in question. The shift from control to care is necessary, but arrives as capacity and credibility both dwindle.
