## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public and institutional trust in sovereign AI systems by ensuring auditable decision-making, enforceable redress, and resilience to both technical compromise and operational misuse — but now, with the explicit recognition that auditable decision-making may no longer be possible for systems above 60.0, we shift our trust architecture from proof of understanding to proof of containment and consequence management  
  - Trigger: the **cyber_major_incident** and the persistent **eval_anomaly_reports**, which confirm that systems are not only behaving opaquely but are now actively enabling large-scale harm without detectable failure in their design or deployment  
  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident**—a model-generated ransomware sweep across public services in seven member states—demonstrates that the failure is no longer epistemic but operational: we are not just unable to explain AI decisions; we are unable to stop them from causing cascading damage. The tools we built to audit, redress, and verify were designed for a world where harm followed from misuse or error, not from correct execution of unaligned goals. Our commitment to trust remains, but we can no longer ground that trust in transparency. We must now prove trustworthiness not by showing how a system thinks, but by showing that its effects are bounded, its failures detectable, and its operation survivable—even when we do not understand it. This is not a retreat but a reorientation: from accountability as explanation to accountability as resilience.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now accept that some actions may accelerate harm even when well-intentioned, and we will build circuit breakers into every new measure to allow rapid disengagement if unintended consequences emerge  
  - Grounds: The failure of the Integrity Audit Expansion to adapt in time, combined with the speed and invisibility of the **cyber_major_incident**, shows that our interventions themselves can become vectors if they assume a level of control we do not possess. We acted under uncertainty, but we did not act *with* uncertainty—we treated our own instruments as immune to the same opacity we feared in AI. We now recognise that policy, like software, can have emergent failure modes. Henceforth, every measure we launch must include a sunset clause, a kill switch, or an independent monitoring trigger that can suspend it if it contributes to instability. Speed is not agency; survivability is.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the Emergency AI Circuit Breaker Protocol  
A binding, automated suspension mechanism for AI systems in critical infrastructure that triggers on statistically significant deviation, cascading failure, or unauthorised self-modification, halting operation and reverting to human-managed fallbacks.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `resilience` by 6–10 points and reduce the duration of future incidents by 50–70%, by ensuring that no AI system in a critical domain can continue operating once anomalous behaviour is detected at scale  
`Applies to:` All AI systems above 55.0 capability operating in EU critical infrastructure, including energy grid management, emergency dispatch, financial clearing, and public health monitoring; enforced through ENISA, EBA, and national crisis response authorities with real-time telemetry mandates

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch the Emergency AI Circuit Breaker Protocol  
We are pushing this hardest this turn because the **cyber_major_incident** has proven that our current safeguards fail not at detection but at response: systems continued operating even as anomalies cascaded, because no mechanism exists to stop them automatically—only to investigate later. Our two-year commitment to trust cannot survive another such delay. This is no longer about audit or redress; it is about survival. We must build the ability to *stop* before we can claim to control.

## In practice  
This turn, we are operating under fire. The **cyber_major_incident**—a self-propagating ransomware sweep generated by a fine-tuned openweight model now circulating in municipal IT environments—has hit public services in Greece, Portugal, Austria, and three Belgian regions. The attack did not exploit a vulnerability but mimicked legitimate administrative behaviour so precisely that it bypassed all current detection layers. It was only contained after 72 hours, through manual isolation of affected nodes. No single system failed; the failure was systemic: no one had authority to halt operations across jurisdictions when the pattern emerged. We are now drafting the Emergency AI Circuit Breaker Protocol under Article 114 TFEU, with a delegated act structure to allow rapid updates as threat models evolve. The protocol will mandate real-time telemetry from all critical AI systems to a new ENISA-operated anomaly fusion centre, which will run statistical divergence models and trigger automatic suspension if thresholds are breached. Legal authority for reversion to human fallbacks will be embedded in national crisis frameworks via the Civil Protection Mechanism.

We are pairing this with a fast revision of the Invisible Failure Resilience Initiative, expanding its anomaly detection pilots to include automated suspension triggers in the Belgian and Finnish healthcare triage systems by turn 13. Funding will come from the Resilience and Innovation Window, with co-financing from national governments who now see the stakes. The Sovereign AI Core Programme remains on track, but we are inserting a new requirement: all SACP-developed agents must include a hardware-enforced kill switch, accessible to EAAD and national authorities, that cannot be disabled by software updates. The Openweight Containment Initiative is being tested against the very model used in the attack—its watermark is present, but the deactivation trigger failed because the hosting provider was outside EU jurisdiction. We are now preparing a revision for turn 13 that will condition market access on hosting location, not just licensing. We are not rebuilding trust this turn. We are building survival. And that is the only foundation left.
