# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public and institutional trust in sovereign AI systems by ensuring auditable decision-making, enforceable redress, and resilience to both technical compromise and operational misuse — but now, with the explicit recognition that auditable decision-making may no longer be possible for systems above 60.0, we shift our trust architecture from proof of understanding to proof of containment and consequence management

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now accept that some actions may accelerate harm even when well-intentioned, and we will build circuit breakers into every new measure to allow rapid disengagement if unintended consequences emerge; this turn, we extend that principle to enforcement: we will not assume compliance, and every directive will include a mechanism for real-time verification of implementation and the power to suspend access or funding if that verification fails
  - Grounds: The failure of the Openweight Containment Initiative to deactivate the ransomware model—despite its watermark and trigger—exposes a fatal gap: we can write rules, but we cannot yet enforce them beyond our borders or even, effectively, within them. Hosting providers outside EU jurisdiction ignored deactivation signals; those inside acted only when personally liable. Our instruments are only as strong as their enforcement teeth. Henceforth, we bind compliance not to goodwill but to access: market access, funding, and interconnection depend on verifiable, real-time adherence. This is not a retreat from multilateralism but a shift from trust to assurance. We act under uncertainty, but we no longer act blind.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Restore public and institutional trust in sovereign AI systems by ensuring auditable decision-making, enforceable redress, and resilience to both technical compromise and operational misuse — but now, with the explicit recognition that auditable decision-making may no longer be possible for systems above 60.0, we shift our trust architecture from proof of understanding to proof of containment and consequence management; this turn, we acknowledge that containment itself fails if enforcement is extraterritorial, and so we narrow our focus to systems under enforceable jurisdiction, prioritising sovereign infrastructure and EU-hosted operations as the only domains where trust can currently be operationalised
  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** was enabled not by a flaw in design but by a flaw in reach: the model was open, the attack self-propagating, and the hosting outside our legal grasp. We cannot contain what we cannot touch. Our commitment to trust remains, but it can no longer be universal. We must triage: trust will be rebuilt first where we can enforce it—on EU soil, under EU law, with EU-controlled infrastructure. This is not abandonment of the broader ecosystem, but a recognition that political agency requires jurisdictional anchoring. We shift from attempting to govern all AI to ensuring we govern *some* AI—fully, verifiably, and without exception.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
