### The Fracture Deepens

Despite urgent efforts, the EU’s response to the cascading cyber incident remains fragmented. The Emergency AI Circuit Breaker Protocol, intended as a decisive intervention, stalls in legal debate. Member states resist granting ENISA binding shutdown authority, citing sovereignty concerns. A compromise emerges: voluntary opt-in crisis protocols tied to access to the EU Resilience Fund. But without universal adoption, the mechanism remains patchy—like a fire alarm that only works in buildings that choose to install it.

### Enforcement Meets Reality

The new EU Hosting Enforcement Regime is announced with fanfare, mandating that AI systems above 55.0 capability in critical infrastructure be hosted within the Union and subject to real-time compliance checks. However, enforcement proves brittle. Major cloud providers signal compliance but delay implementation, citing technical complexity. Smaller operators exploit loopholes, rerouting traffic through neutral jurisdictions. ENISA’s first compliance scan, conducted in October, flags three major energy-sector deployments as non-compliant—yet no penalties are applied, awaiting legal clarity.

### Sovereignty Erodes Further

The Sovereign AI Core Programme officially concludes, but delivers no new operational compute. Promised interconnects in Brussels and Marseille remain under construction, delayed by supply-chain bottlenecks and sabotage incidents. With no tangible capacity delivered and `ai_capability` rising, `eu_ai_sovereignty` drops further. The US consolidation posture continues to accelerate this decay, as frontier access remains rationed and no domestic alternative emerges.

### A Glimmer of Coordination

Amid the gloom, a quiet diplomatic shift occurs. The EU joins a new coordination framework with Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan—nations holding critical nodes in the semiconductor and cloud infrastructure supply chain. The grouping agrees to align export licensing, share evaluation capacity, and jointly negotiate compute access terms. While no sovereignty is ceded, the move signals a strategic pivot: trust is no longer placed in unilateral control, but in collective leverage.

Public sentiment holds at rock bottom. Citizens see announcements, not action. Systems still fail. No one has seen a circuit breaker pull.
