The EU’s crisis intensifies in 2032 as the Emergency AI Circuit Breaker Protocol fails to gain binding legal force, reduced to a voluntary opt-in system linked to resilience funding, leaving critical gaps. The EU Hosting Enforcement Regime launches but falters in practice, with major providers delaying compliance and smaller actors circumventing rules, while ENISA refrains from penalties due to legal uncertainty. The Sovereign AI Core Programme concludes without delivering operational capacity, deepening reliance on foreign compute and eroding EU AI sovereignty amid stalled infrastructure and rising ai_capability. In a strategic shift, the EU joins Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan in a coordination pact to pool leverage over semiconductor and cloud supply chains, marking a move toward collective resilience over unilateral control. Despite these efforts, public trust remains shattered—announcements outpace action, systems continue to fail, and no circuit breaker has been activated, cementing a perception of institutional drift.
