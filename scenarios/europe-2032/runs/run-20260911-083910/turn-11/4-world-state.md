### Wards, codes and cures
Autumn 2031 did not feel like recovery. The pathogen cordons from winter held, but only just, and clinics stayed on paper triage for weeks while sequencing labs and wastewater monitors chased late chains. Emergency money kept nurses paid and repair crews in town halls rebuilding from offline backups.

Then the ground shifted twice. A newly demonstrated system sharply improved automated coding and intrusion. Hospital IT staff described machine-written probes arriving within hours of each patch, hitting dispatch and pharmacy systems already fragile. Defenders held the line by isolating wards and patching relentlessly, but confidence in control slipped further.

At the same time tailored cures for previously untreatable conditions reached ordinary use elsewhere. For European patients the news arrived bitterly: the supporting models ran on foreign infrastructure that had already gone dark for Europe months before. Brussels rushed a procurement drive to license the therapy protocols for inference on domestic servers, with joint purchasing and conditional approvals meant to deliver the cures on European capacity.

That drive was undercut from inside. One member state signed its own access deal with a foreign provider on cheaper, separate terms, presenting it as pragmatism for its patients. Others called it a break in the common line, and solidarity credits offered to pull it back failed to close the gap.

By December, the first EU-hosted treatments trickled into a handful of clinics — enough for cameras and grateful families, not enough to change waiting lists. Fear eased a fraction, but anger at dependence deepened. Survival held; strategy did not.
