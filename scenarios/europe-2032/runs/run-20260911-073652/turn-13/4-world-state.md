### The sweep
Autumn brought the attack operators had feared all year. A largely automated ransomware sweep, assembled with machine-generated tooling, moved across municipal systems, hospital IT and logistics contractors in several member states at once. Appointments were cancelled, emergency dispatch fell back to radio in two regions, and billing and records systems locked.

Defenders were visibly behind for weeks. Attribution took months to narrow, and never produced a name the public believed. What kept lights and wards alive was degradation, not prevention: live feeds from power, telecom and hospital networks flowing to the joint investigation team, faster tracing of malicious traffic, cleaner isolation of stressed grids.

### Protection without cure
The shared defence pact finished landing just as the sweep peaked. Health authorities held to published triage lists in Lyon, Barcelona and Krakow, and to deploying only treatments and tools they could run themselves. Candour steadied staff, but queues lengthened and the leaked separate discount in one capital turned exhaustion into resentment.

Brussels sought a seat at the table after Washington and Beijing announced a limited risk agreement on frontier safeguards and certain biological design tools. European envoys were briefed late, offered observer language on verification, and came home with little to show. In laboratories, the contested genome-model claim split specialists between hype and recipe, while screening of incoming samples tightened.

By December cohesion held on procedure, not delivery. No gigafactory restarted, no commercial compute returned, bridge payments thinned further. The Union had proved it could absorb a blow together, but not yet rebuild from one.
