### Holding the line with nothing to spend
The spring began with rationing made routine. In Lyon, Barcelona and Krakow, triage lists set by clinical need decided who received stockpiled care and who waited. Health officials published the criteria daily, and the honesty steadied wards even as queues lengthened. The separate discount channel kept by the wayward capital leaked into press, and anger at dependence hardened into anger at division.

Brussels put all remaining weight on the shared defence pact. Live feeds from power, telecom and hospital networks flowed outward to the joint investigation team, and samples flowed inward under reassigned staff. Operators credited faster tracing of the jamming and cleaner isolation of stressed grids for keeping lights on through renewed strikes. It was protection, not cure: no additional therapy doses arrived.

### A paper that frightened the specialists
Mid-turn a contested genome-model paper claimed a non-expert aided by assistance could reach a viable human-infecting design. Methodologists attacked the result; editors were accused both of hype and of printing a recipe. Inside the joint team, the Union pushed for calm assessment and tighter screening of poisoned samples, holding its rule of deploying only doses and triage tools it could run itself. The public heard little beyond rumour, but laboratories heard a great deal.

### When the money left
Then capital fled the sector. Valuations reset violently, announced data-centre expansions were cancelled outright, and several commercial compute arrangements European planners had counted on evaporated with them. Repair crews at the stalled gigafactory grid links held their pylons, but no contractor returned to build. Bridge payments thinned. Talk of mirrored models sheltered in neutral states grew louder in corridors, but nothing reachable materialised.

By June the Union was keeping services alive through shared vigilance while its capacity to build anything of its own shrank. Cohesion held on procedure and candour, not on delivery.
