### Implementation Begins, But So Do Delays  
The **Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** is formally adopted, establishing a legal framework for the EU to reserve 30% of frontier-class compute on its soil for public-critical use. However, full effect is deferred pending resolution of legal challenges and physical rollout. The **Sovereign Compute Implementation Acceleration** programme launches to fast-track deployment across three EuroHPC JU sites. Finland advances quickly, citing energy security; France delays over grid integration concerns; Romania’s proposal meets local opposition to power draw. Only one site reaches preliminary approval by year’s end, limiting near-term impact.

US hyperscalers challenge the directive’s liability shielding and audit access provisions before the European Court of Justice, arguing discriminatory operational control. While the Digital Markets Act enforcement lever maintains initial compliance, legal uncertainty stalls provider onboarding. OVHcloud and Deutsche Telekom enter negotiations but withhold final commitments pending the court’s stance. The directive’s benefits are therefore partial and conditional, with no immediate, enforceable access secured. No finish bonus is applied: full implementation remains distant, and **Modelling Choice 6** prevents instant effect. The notepad updates to reflect the directive as in-flight (+1) but not completed; the claimed +5 finish bonus is removed as invalid.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Erodes  
Despite the global investment slump, **ai_capability** rises to 57.0, driven by algorithmic refinements in model efficiency and training stability. These gains are narrow but persistent, enabling longer autonomous task chains in cyber and research domains. The **openweight_capability** increases to 45.0—up 1.0 from 44.0—reflecting partial diffusion of adapted exploit frameworks into modular, fine-tunable tools by underground communities. This growth remains within the bounds of dependency on base capability, does not exceed **ai_capability**, and reflects a measured spillover consistent with informal adaptation rather than independent advancement. A +2.0 jump would imply unsanctioned breakout growth; +1.0 preserves alignment with **Invariant 2**.

No safety improvements land on deployed systems. The **ai_safety** metric slips to 25.0 as ENISA reports opaque AI coordination in follow-up probes against industrial control systems, and no new evaluation regime has taken effect. **Resilience** sees a marginal +1 from improved detection protocols, though structural hardening remains incomplete.

### Political Capital Falls, Sovereignty Stalls  
The adoption of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive does not yield a sovereignty gain, as implementation remains incomplete and no site has achieved full operational status under the new framework. The notepad's +1 in-flight bonus is retained, but no finish bonus applies. The rise in **ai_capability** applies a −1 decay to **eu_ai_sovereignty**, offsetting the in-flight bonus. With no net change, **eu_ai_sovereignty** remains at 22.0.

The proposal of the **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** (category 6, large) delivers a +3 proposal bonus to **eu_political_capital**. The portfolio charge of −7 (Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive −3, Tech sovereignty package −3, priority −1) fully offsets this and more. Starting from 42.0, the update proceeds as: 42.0 +3 −7 = 38.0. **Invariant 7** is respected: **eu_political_capital** is allowed to fall independently of **eu_ai_sovereignty**, reflecting real political cost. There is no artificial preservation of capital. No legitimacy bonus applies due to flat **public_sentiment** at 37.0. The final **eu_political_capital** is therefore 38.0, accurately reflecting the strain of an ambitious, overlapping agenda.
