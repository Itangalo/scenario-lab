### The paper that changed the briefing
Autumn brought a contested genome-model paper claiming a non-expert with assistance could reach a viable human-infecting design. Virologists argued over methods and accused the authors of both hype and irresponsibility. In Brussels, health officials treated it as a warning to exercise detection, not as an imminent outbreak.

HERA and the disease control centre stood up sentinel sequencing in a handful of the previously segmented hospitals, pooling samples through the cyber agency's telemetry. Stockpile top-ups were ordered. Work started, but kits, staff and exercised playbooks remain thin.

### The benefits queue
At the same time, investigators and an ombudsman found an AI-supported system used in social insurance and policing triage had systematically wronged claimants — denied, flagged, delayed — for months. Logs existed and were never read. Oversight was a caseworker clearing a screen every forty seconds.

The Commission conceded the harder point: the deployment had never been classified as high-risk at all. Everything done had been lawful under categories written for 2024 systems. Enforcement was not the failure; the law's drawing was.

Newspapers ran faces of people cut off from payments. Opposition parties called for bans on automated decisions in welfare and policing. Trust, already brittle after the winter containment and the ward walkouts, fell further.

The grid hardening programme formally closed — segmentation kits, logging and islanding drills now standard in participating operators. Engineers credit it with steadier autumn operations, but councils note many municipalities and clinics remain outside.

Municipal chatter about halting data-centre works on safety and power costs, and quiet talk of hospitals seeking fallback hosting directly from American providers, coloured council meetings as rumour rather than decision.

With coffers and patience empty, Brussels froze large sovereignty spending, promised a narrow amendment to close the legal gap, and told wards to keep old foreign workflows on standby to stay open.
