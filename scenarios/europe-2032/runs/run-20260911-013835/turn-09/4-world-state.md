### Wards on borrowed time
Autumn brought tighter American volume licences. Two hospital suppliers received re-licensing notices with no delivery dates; procurement chiefs were told medical workloads would be considered favourably. In Brussels, trade ministers requested written protection for contracted clinical and utility inference. The answer restated the licensing principle and promised case-by-case review.

Meanwhile the freely downloadable frontier model spread to university servers, maker labs and municipal IT basements within days. Hospital guidance warned against connecting it to clinical or grid control loops, but audits were voluntary.

### The quiet fallback
By October, journalists in two member states reported that municipal utilities and hospital groups had signed direct fallback hosting with American hyperscalers to keep wards and billing running through winter. The Commission neither authorised nor blocked the deals, asking only for notification and data-residency clauses. Managers said they could not wait for European substitution that had no funding date.

The detection sprint formally closed. Sentinel sequencing stayed in a handful of hospitals, telemetry continued to pool through the cyber agency, and stockpiles remained topped up under health agency operating funds. Engineers reported faster flagging of anomalous samples; ministers cited it as proof that low-cost continuity worked.

One bright spot was claimed: two regions reported shorter triage backlogs where assisted scheduling tools were used, presented as European public-sector adoption working. Families waiting on welfare back-payments marched again, and councils extended pauses on data-centre construction over power and safety fears. By December, services held, but on foreign quota and foreign backup.
