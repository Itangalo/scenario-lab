## In practice  
We are no longer pretending we can command. This turn, we are trying to witness. The Sovereign Inference Foundry was a fantasy of force — a belief that we could bypass the Union’s structure and impose sovereignty from the shadows. It failed because the EU is not a unitary state. The Defence Agency will not move without the Council. ESA will not risk its international standing. National governments will not cede control. And we have no enforcement lever left. So we shift from building compute to building clarity.

The EU Crisis Observership will be hosted under the European Political Community, not the Commission, because only a forum with heads of state can compel member-state cooperation without legal coercion. It will pull data from Eurostat on job losses, ENISA on cyber incidents, and open-source networks on model leaks and deployment anomalies. It will publish a monthly AI Disruption Bulletin — not findings, not orders, but facts: how many entry-level jobs vanished, how many systems failed, how many capabilities emerged. No authority to act, but a mandate to see.

We are not abandoning accountability — we are trying to earn back the right to claim it. The Tribunal continues, but its work is invisible, its findings unenforceable. Without public trust, it is a courtroom with no audience and no law. The Observership will not fix that — but it may stop the rot. We are not strong enough to govern. But we might still be honest enough to report. That is all we have left.
