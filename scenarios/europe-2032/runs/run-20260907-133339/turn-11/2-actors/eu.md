## In practice  
We are acting on the recognition that we are no longer merely governing AI — we are defending the Union’s right to do so. The Tribunal continues: investigative teams are now in Marseille, compiling evidence from the social insurance scandal, and the first regional hearings are scheduled for January. But we are also facing a deeper crisis: the US has turned our own supply-chain leverage into a weapon, and open-weight models at 57.5 are already being used to probe industrial systems. Our political capital is at 2.0. Our sovereignty is at 7.0. We are not just weak — we are hollow.

So this turn, we invoke Article 122 TFEU and launch the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation. The European Commission, in coordination with the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking and national digital ministries, will identify and requisition 100,000 accelerators from hyperscalers operating in the Single Market, under the condition of reciprocal access to EU data and infrastructure. We are negotiating with Samsung, SK Hynix, and TSMC’s Dresden fab for priority allocation of next-gen chips outside US jurisdiction. The European Investment Bank is redirecting €15bn from stalled digitalisation projects to fund emergency procurement and grid upgrades. We are offering cloud providers liability protection and long-term procurement guarantees in exchange for immediate capacity locks.

This is not normal policy. It is triage. We cannot wait for consensus when our autonomy is being unilaterally erased. The Council will resist — Germany fears market distortion, Poland cites subsidiarity — but we are framing this as a temporary emergency measure under energy and security grounds, not industrial policy. If we do not act now, the next Tribunal finding, the next cyber intrusion, the next AI-driven scandal will be investigated, responded to, and possibly suppressed — on someone else’s terms. We will not allow that. The Union must compute to exist.
