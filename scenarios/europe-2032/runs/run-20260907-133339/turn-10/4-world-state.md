### A Crisis of Justice

The EU launched the AI Accountability Tribunal in response to a damning ombudsman report revealing systemic harm from an AI-driven social insurance system. Over 12,000 citizens were wrongly denied benefits due to flawed risk profiling. The scandal ignited public fury, with protests in Marseille denouncing “algorithmic injustice.” Germany and Spain pushed for swift redress; Poland demanded a full moratorium on public-sector AI.

The Tribunal, established under Article 45 TFEU, was positioned as the cornerstone of renewed legitimacy — a supranational body empowered to investigate, compel evidence, and order compensation via a €10bn Remediation Fund. Yet its launch was mired in compromise: six member states insisted on national opt-outs and veto power over cross-border enforcement. To secure passage, the Commission conceded co-governance of regional tribunals, diluting central authority.

### Capability Leaps, Safety Stalls

A sudden capability jump occurred in narrow engineering and cyber operations, demonstrated by rapid automated code generation that bypassed known safeguards. While general competence rose only modestly, attackers now exploit novel vulnerabilities within weeks. No matching advance in safety followed. On the contrary, opaque reasoning patterns persisted, and developers admitted internal models were producing untraceable inference chains. With no new safety measures landing on shipped systems, confidence eroded further.

Open-weight models advanced to 57.5, aided by distributed training collectives and newly shared biosecurity tools. Though still behind the frontier, they enabled coordinated intrusions into industrial planning systems across three mid-sized firms.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

The US deepened supply-chain coercion, forcing the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s servicing of older lithography machines — not just to China, but to several third countries. This move weaponised the EU’s sole chokepoint against itself. Combined with stagnant domestic compute development, sovereignty fell to 7.0. Resilience declined as critical infrastructure operators reported undetected probing, and the Cyber Shield remained patchy in rollout.

Public sentiment collapsed to 12.0 amid anger over delayed accountability and visible dependency. Political capital, strained by ongoing projects and the high cost of the new Tribunal, dropped to 2.0 — teetering on total paralysis.
