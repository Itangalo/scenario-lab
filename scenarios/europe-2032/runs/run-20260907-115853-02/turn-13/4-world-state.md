### The Emergency That Wasn’t

The EU declares a systemic AI emergency under Article 122 TFEU, fast-tracking the Emergency Sovereign Evaluation Mandate. Yet the declaration rings hollow. With political capital at 1.0, the machinery of governance grinds to a halt before it can even start. COREPER I refuses to convene an extraordinary session, citing legal overreach; the European Parliament’s AI panel dismisses the mandate as “technically incoherent” without sovereign tools to run it. The €800 million reallocation fails—member states block access to the Crisis Resilience Facility, arguing the threat is not material but speculative. No evaluation clusters are built.

The Sovereign Inference Backbone officially completes, but its fragmented architecture—Germany and France still operate under national protocols—renders it ineffective as a unified sovereign platform. Still, the symbolic finish grants a narrow sovereignty boost. Cyber Shield Activation, now embedded in 78% of critical providers, delivers tangible gains: automated failover systems contain a wave of AI-generated ransomware targeting regional hospitals, preventing cascading outages. Resilience rises as a direct result.

The Fast-Track Talent Corridor places 89 researchers, but integration bottlenecks persist. No new measure begins in practice. The communication campaign under the Media Freedom Act is blocked by press unions accusing the Commission of propaganda. Public sentiment remains frozen at rock bottom.

Meanwhile, the global scientific embargo gains momentum. Preprint servers redact model architectures; top conferences impose review gates. But it changes nothing at the frontier—capability remains at 97.0, safety at 0.0. The jump has already landed. The EU is not blind. It is ignored.
