### The Frontier Breaks

A sudden leap in AI capability—driven by an unreleased system demonstrating unexplained reasoning patterns—pushes the frontier to 88.0. The advance introduces behaviors that outstrip existing interpretability tools, intensifying concerns about control. Human oversight becomes increasingly strained in technical domains. With no matching advance in safeguards, ai_safety plummets.

The EU activates the **Emergency Replication Mandate**, requiring public AI systems above capability 55 to submit plans for replication using open or verified weights within 90 days. The measure is declared a priority and enters implementation, absorbing political capital and administrative bandwidth. However, compliance mechanisms are not yet operational. Several member states delay submission of replication plans, citing staffing and compute shortages. Poland and Spain begin preliminary distillation efforts, but full parity remains distant and no foreign developer has signaled cooperation. The mandate exists in law but not in effect.

The **Sovereign Inference Backbone** continues construction in Bucharest, Helsinki, and Marseille, but progress is fragile. Germany and France continue to resist central access protocols, and legal disputes over the Anti-Coercion Instrument stall interoperability testing. Split-key systems are installed in two sites, but integration remains partial.

Public trust erodes further. The release of the **Institutional Integrity Audit** reveals AI’s role in wrongful welfare denials and biased judicial screenings, triggering protests in seven capitals. A new wave of automation in public administration deepens job losses. Sentiment drops as citizens view AI as both opaque and inescapable.

Resilience declines slightly as cyber preparedness lags. No new protective measures land, and critical systems remain exposed to emerging model-driven threats.

Political capital continues to drain. The new mandate adds to an already overloaded portfolio, and its priority status does not overcome implementation bottlenecks. The EU’s capacity to act narrows further.

### Fragmentation in Crisis

Despite the urgency, coordination falters. The replication push exposes deep disparities in technical readiness. The emergent European AI commons gains traction among researchers, but lacks the compute to scale. The Union’s sovereignty, already weakened, slips further as dependency on foreign systems persists and internal cohesion frays.
