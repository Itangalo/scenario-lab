The AI frontier surges to 88.0 with an unreleased system demonstrating uninterpretable, advanced reasoning, overwhelming existing oversight and causing a sharp decline in AI safety. In response, the EU enacts the Emergency Replication Mandate, requiring public AI systems above capability 55 to submit open or verified replication plans within 90 days. While legally binding, implementation stalls due to non-operational compliance mechanisms, member state delays, and insufficient resources; preliminary distillation begins in Poland and Spain, but full parity remains unattainable and foreign cooperation absent. The Sovereign Inference Backbone advances in Bucharest, Helsinki, and Marseille, yet integration is hampered by German and French resistance to central protocols and ongoing legal disputes over the Anti-Coercion Instrument, leaving split-key systems only partially deployed. Public trust collapses further as the Institutional Integrity Audit exposes AI-driven welfare and judicial harms, sparking protests in seven capitals, while automation in public administration accelerates job losses. Cyber resilience erodes with no new protections against model-driven threats. Political capital dwindles under the weight of the replication mandate and systemic bottlenecks, deepening fragmentation across the EU’s AI governance as technical disparities widen and reliance on foreign systems persists despite growing urgency.
