## In practice
We are acting on the breaking point: the genome model leak has revealed that the frontier is now capable of generating actionable, recreatable threats at low cost, and our defences are still structured for slower, visible escalation. The Resilience Surge Protocol is our legal and financial backbone, but it is not enough. This turn, we are operationalising the European AI Emergency Response Corps with binding cross-border access rights under Article 122a TFEU, funded through a €4.2bn draw from the Resilience Bond facility. Recruitment begins immediately for 500 technical operators — AI red teamers, biosecurity analysts, and cyber incident commanders — embedded in ENISA and ECDC with pre-authorised intervention powers during declared incidents. The Council’s legal working group is finalising the deployment protocol, and we are offering member states opt-in liability shielding for rapid action taken under corps mandate.

At the same time, we are launching the Mandatory Pre-Deployment Biosecurity Audit Regime to stop the next leak before it happens. This is not voluntary testing — it is a condition of market access. We are tasking a newly empowered EU AI Evaluation Agency, working with the Paul Ehrlich Institute and SciLifeLab, to run adversarial probes on all models above capability threshold 65.0, simulating pathogen design, synthesis planning, and lab protocol generation. Results will be public, and any model found to produce viable blueprints will be blocked from deployment in the EU unless and until mitigations are verified. We are negotiating with OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta to align their internal red teams with our standards, but the regime will apply extraterritorially: if you sell here, you test here. This is not sovereignty through compute — it is sovereignty through standards, exercised at the only chokepoint we still control: market access.
