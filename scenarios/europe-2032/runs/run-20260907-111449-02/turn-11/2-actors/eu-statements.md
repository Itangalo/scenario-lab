# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign technical agency through auditable AI

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer accept acting in isolation from the operational reality of our member states.
  - Grounds: The **emergent_regional_compute_pact** reveals a deeper failure than access denial or technical opacity: the erosion of central legitimacy in crisis response. While we acted at the Union level to mandate auditable AI in critical infrastructure, three key member states bypassed Brussels entirely, pooling sovereign compute under a neutral scientific consortium in Switzerland. This is not defiance — it is adaptation to our inability to deliver sovereign capability at speed. Our commitment to act under uncertainty stands, but its cost has changed. We can no longer afford to assume that binding mandates from the centre will land without prior alignment, technical readiness, or political trust. From this turn forward, decisive action must be co-constructed — not imposed.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign technical agency through auditable AI, now co-developed with and distributed via a resilient, interoperable network of member-state and neutral scientific hubs.
  - Grounds: The **emergent_regional_compute_pact** proves that the path to sovereignty no longer runs solely through central institutions. The Nordic-Benelux initiative has already secured 18% of Europe’s remaining frontier-accessible compute under joint control, with a mandate to train open-weight models tailored for public-sector resilience. These models are not frontier-capable, but they are auditable, deployable, and politically sustainable — precisely the kind of capability we sought to build. Our two-year commitment must now absorb this reality: sovereignty will not be decreed from Brussels, but woven from distributed nodes of trust, capacity, and shared standards. We pivot from ownership to orchestration.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
