## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign technical agency through auditable AI, now co-developed with and distributed via a resilient, interoperable network of member-state and neutral scientific hubs — but only if those hubs remain under enforceable EU legal and security jurisdiction.
  - Grounds: The **supply_chain_coercion** event reveals that sovereignty is not just about distributed compute or open models—it is about control over access, continuity, and security. While the regional compute pact under Swiss stewardship offers technical promise, its legal insulation from EU authority now poses a systemic risk: a strategic asset built outside enforceable jurisdiction can be disrupted or denied just as easily as foreign frontier models. Our commitment to co-development stands, but we must now insist that no node in this network operates beyond the reach of EU law, security audits, or crisis mobilisation. This is not a retreat from decentralisation—it is a recalibration of sovereignty as both distributed *and* unified under a single legal and operational envelope.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer accept acting in isolation from the operational reality of our member states or from the coercive realities of external power.
  - Grounds: The **supply_chain_coercion** event—Washington forcing ASML to cut off not just advanced but also mature-node lithography exports—proves that even foundational layers of our industrial base are subject to foreign jurisdiction. Our earlier adjustment acknowledged political fragmentation; now we must acknowledge strategic vulnerability. Acting under uncertainty now includes acting under duress. We cannot assume that compute, talent, or supply chains will remain available just because they are technically within reach. Our decisions must be faster, more integrated, and legally airtight—because the window for building sovereign capacity is closing not just due to internal delays, but because others are actively narrowing it.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
