## Statement changes

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent uncontrolled AI systems from operating inside EU borders  
- Trigger: The cyber_major_incident and emergent_labour_unrest, combined with the collapse of eu_political_capital to 5.0, reveal that the current enforcement-first strategy is failing to secure either public confidence or operational control — the border exists in law but not in fact, and harm is now materialising on EU soil.  
- Grounds: The commitment as stated assumes that sovereignty over deployment is achievable through legal assertion alone. But with US hyperscalers defying audits, certification nodes still incomplete, and political capital exhausted, the EU cannot enforce the boundary it has declared. Continuing to treat "preventing uncontrolled systems" as the priority without first restoring capacity to act risks legitimising a fiction. The commitment must evolve to reflect that control cannot be asserted from weakness.

- add `rebuild_capacity_to_act` (commitment): Re-establish the EU’s ability to enforce its rules by restoring political capital, accelerating sovereign capability, and anchoring public support — because without the means to act, no boundary holds.  
- Grounds: With resilience falling, safety at crisis levels, and sovereignty declining, the immediate threat is not just uncontrolled AI but the Union’s loss of agency. This turn’s events prove that enforcement without capacity is provocation without power. The new commitment redirects the two-year arc from symbolic boundary-setting to rebuilding the real, material and political foundations of control.

## Portfolio

- `AI Safety Emergency Investment (category 5, costs 3 per turn, started turn 8, finishes on turn 12): A €40bn programme to rapidly scale up the EU’s independent technical capacity to monitor, audit, and secure high-capability AI systems, establishing the European AI Assurance Network (EAAN) with secure inspection enclaves and red-teaming capabilities` — **finished this turn**
- `EU Border for AI Systems (category 3, costs 3 per turn, started turn 10, finishes on turn 14): Establish a legal and technical boundary at deployment: no AI system above capability 60.0 may operate in the EU without sovereign evaluation, runtime controllability, and continuous monitoring by EAAN` — **in flight**
- `Accelerate EAAN Certification Nodes (category 5, costs 2 per turn, started turn 11, finishes on turn 13): Fast-track deployment of sovereign evaluation infrastructure in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich to operationalise the EU Border for AI Systems by turn 14, ensuring the EU can independently verify compliance` — **in flight**

## New measure

**Rebuild Public Trust and Labour Transition Capacity**  
Launch a visible, targeted programme to support workers displaced by AI and restore public confidence in the Union’s ability to manage the transition.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 10–15 points and public_sentiment by 10–15 points by demonstrating tangible support for those harmed by AI-driven disruption; indirectly stabilise eu_political_capital by restoring legitimacy.  
`Applies to:` EU-wide, with focus on member states experiencing emergent_labour_unrest and high rates of labour_displacement in law, accountancy, software, and administration.

## Priority

**Rebuild Public Trust and Labour Transition Capacity** — because the collapse in political capital and the surge in labour unrest show that the EU is losing the social licence to govern AI, and without it, no enforcement, no border, no certification node will hold.

## In practice

This turn, we shift from confrontation to consolidation. The AI Safety Emergency Investment finishes, delivering core EAAN infrastructure — but not access, not compliance, and not trust. We have drawn the line, but the ground beneath it is crumbling. The cyber_major_incident exploited model-generated vulnerabilities in public service software dependencies, exposing hospitals and transport systems across three member states. The attack was not state-scale in origin, but in effect — and it landed because our defences were designed for human hackers, not AI-scaled automation. Simultaneously, strikes in Berlin, Paris, and Amsterdam by mid-level professionals — lawyers, auditors, junior developers — signal that the social contract around technological progress is fraying.

We cannot enforce a border we cannot staff, nor command loyalty we do not earn. So this turn, we pivot. Under the European Social Fund Plus and the newly activated Crisis Instrument for Technological Disruption, we launch a €22bn Reintegration and Transition Facility for AI-Affected Workers, targeting displaced professionals with wage insurance, accelerated retraining in AI-augmented roles, and mobility support. The programme links directly to national employment agencies and social partners, with conditionality on participation from employers automating at scale. We pair it with a public communication campaign — “AI with Guardrails” — showcasing both the new EAAN nodes coming online and the first disbursements to affected workers, aiming to reframe AI not as an external force but as a transformation the Union is actively managing.

We do not abandon the border. The EU Border for AI Systems remains in flight, and we continue to demand access under its terms. But we now recognise that enforcement legitimacy flows from public confidence, not just legal authority. The Accelerate EAAN Certification Nodes measure continues, with Munich now fast-tracked under emergency procurement rules. Yet without political capital, even finished capabilities gather dust. This turn, we rebuild the base from which all else must rise: the belief that the EU can protect its people, not just its principles.
