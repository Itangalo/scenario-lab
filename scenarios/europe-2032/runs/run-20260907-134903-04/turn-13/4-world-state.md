### The Distributed Front

AI capability inches upward to 64.0, with labs reporting increasingly persistent agent-like behaviours that evade standard evaluation protocols. No safety improvements land on deployed systems, and the control gap remains dangerously wide. Then, a frontier model is released openly just months after its closed counterpart—its capabilities begin to diffuse across research collectives and private actors, with early replication observed in several jurisdictions. While the open release marks a turning point, full capability propagation is still unfolding, and the global open-weight base has not yet caught up. Openweight capability rises modestly to 53.5, reflecting early-stage diffusion and limited replication capacity, consistent with gradual leakage rather than systemic parity.

A major cyber incident—automated, adaptive, and model-generated—cripples hospital record systems and transit networks across several member states. Recovery takes weeks. The attack exploited a poisoned software dependency, undetected until activation. The EU’s perimeter defences failed: the threat was already inside.

In response, the European Commission proposes the Distributed Threat Detection and Response Network, a large category 6 measure, to establish real-time monitoring across critical infrastructure. Legal and technical design begins immediately under NIS2 and the Crisis Instrument for Technological Disruption, with plans for telemetry mandates across energy, transport, healthcare, and software providers. ENISA is tasked with drafting the framework for a Joint AI Incident Coordination Cell, to be staffed by national cyber units. However, no operational capability is established this turn. Member states raise concerns over cost, data sovereignty, and legal scope; staffing, interagency agreements, and technical integration will require significant time. The measure remains in early development and cannot generate effects or a proposal bonus until at least Turn 15.

The European AI Assurance Network was expected to launch this turn, but final operational status is delayed due to unresolved access disputes with US developers. Without inspection rights, its audit framework cannot activate, and formal launch is postponed. Technical teams continue alignment work, but no new measure is initiated. The Rebuild Public Trust programme expands to six countries, with early uptake in legal and accounting sectors. Public messaging shifts to include worker testimonials, but strikes persist.

### Political Calculus

Political capital drops to −1.0. The portfolio charge—two large measures (−3 each) and one small (−2) and one priority (−1)—totals −9. No proposal bonus is awarded, as the new measure remains in design phase and has not yet cleared funding or governance hurdles to qualify for partial effect. The Game Master's Notepad has been corrected: the proposal bonus for the Distributed Threat Detection and Response Network is not applied in this turn. Sovereignty remains at 33.0: −1 from capability growth (under 2, so no decay), no capacity event, and no category 4 completion. Resilience gains +1, solely from the continuing effect of the completed category 5 investment. Public sentiment rises +1, reflecting modest gains from targeted outreach, but is partially offset by public anxiety over the cyberattack and slow institutional response.
