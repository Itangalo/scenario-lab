## In practice
This turn, we formally close the Emergency Resilience Surge with a Council Implementing Decision under Article 12(3) of the NIS2 Directive, embedding AI-driven detection and automated patching into ENISA’s baseline standards for energy and port infrastructure. The decision binds all member states, though we acknowledge uneven implementation, particularly in water monitoring systems across Central and Eastern Europe. To close this gap, we task the Tech Sovereignty Package’s resilience working group — co-chaired by DG Connect and the Cohesion Policy Commissioner — with delivering a targeted action plan by Q3, combining accelerated permitting, cohesion fund co-financing, and technical assistance from the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium.

On the ASML front, despite intense trilateral talks and our offer of liability shielding, diplomatic protection, and preferential access to EU semiconductor funds, no binding agreement was reached by June. The initiative formally concludes this turn without achieving its core objective. However, the **emergent_asml_board_resistance** leak — revealing a formal proposal within ASML to condition servicing and upgrades on EU approval — has shifted the ground beneath us. This is not the outcome we negotiated, but it is one we can now leverage. We are not treating the initiative as failed, but as overtaken: the board’s move creates an opening to reframe the issue not as EU pressure on ASML, but as ASML seeking EU protection against U.S. overreach. We are preparing contingency legal analysis under Article 346 TFEU and the Anti-Coercion Instrument to support this pivot next turn.

Meanwhile, the Biosecurity Watchtower remains our operational priority. The Joint Research Centre is finalising its assessment of genome-scale model risks under the Dual-Use Regulation, with preliminary findings suggesting that models above certain parameter thresholds and training data compositions could enable synthetic biology capabilities requiring containment. The AI Office, in coordination with EMA and ECDC, is drafting enforcement mechanisms that would allow suspension of open-weight releases pending review. We are also advancing the proposed €300 million public research stream for secure open models — not as a subsidy, but as a signal that the EU seeks to govern, not suppress, the open frontier. But we are not launching new instruments: with **eu_political_capital** at 23.0 and five measures still in flight, we cannot afford overreach. This turn is about closure, consolidation, and positioning for what comes next.
