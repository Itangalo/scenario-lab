In late 2026, a major AI-aided cyber intrusion exposed critical infrastructure across energy, water, and ports in multiple regions, revealing severe gaps in detection capabilities. Though no operational disruptions occurred, the breach triggered emergency responses within the EU. Simultaneously, the Union lost access to a key frontier AI model without explanation, crippling sectors reliant on its reasoning capabilities.

In response, the European Commission launched the Emergency Resilience Surge under DORA and NIS2, fast-tracking binding AI-driven security standards through ENISA, with €12bn redirected to fund rapid implementation. Germany and France initially resisted mandatory retrofits but were secured through financial and energy incentives. A pan-European resilience exercise was scheduled for March 2027.

Amid the crisis, a southern EU member state struck a bilateral AI deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, undermining the Union’s unified strategy and eroding trust in collective action. Public confidence waned due to economic fallout and perceived technological dependency. In parallel, informal coordination began among the EU, Japan, South Korea, and the Netherlands to strengthen AI supply chain resilience, laying early groundwork for strategic alignment.
