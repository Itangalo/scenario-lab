### A Turn of Shock and Response

The second half of 2026 ends in crisis and motion. A coordinated cyber intrusion—discovered only by chance—exposes critical infrastructure across energy, water, and ports to persistent AI-aided threats. The breach, spanning three continents, reveals detection systems blind to novel attack patterns. In two EU countries, grid operators were compromised, though no disruption occurred. The incident is not yet public, but panic simmers in security councils.

Days later, the Union is abruptly cut off from the leading frontier model. Hospitals using its reasoning engine for diagnostics, ministries relying on it for crisis simulation, and firms building next-gen tools are left stranded. No justification is given, no appeal allowed. The denial reads not as policy but as power—naked and arbitrary.

### EU Mobilises Under Fire

The European Commission activates emergency powers under DORA and NIS2, launching the **Emergency Resilience Surge**. Binding baselines for AI-driven intrusion detection and failover protocols are fast-tracked. ENISA leads a sprint to set standards by January 2027, with a pan-European exercise planned for March. €12bn is redirected from digital and recovery funds, bypassing normal fiscal gates.

Germany and France resist mandatory retrofits, citing cost and complexity. Concessions—80% co-funding, priority access to future gigafactory power—secure reluctant buy-in. The measure is seen as essential but thin: a six-month sprint against a decade-long deficit.

### Fragmentation and Fallout

Amid the chaos, a southern member state finalises a bilateral AI access deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, undercutting the Union’s unified stance. Framed as “pragmatic continuity,” it is widely read as defection. Trust in collective action frays.

Public sentiment dips as media spotlight job losses in AI-dependent sectors and the humiliation of dependency. A quiet but growing chorus questions whether sovereignty is achievable at all.

Behind the scenes, a coalition of middle powers—EU, Japan, South Korea, Netherlands—begins informal talks on supply chain coordination. No agreement is signed, but the alignment gains quiet momentum.
