## New measure
**Emergency Resilience Surge**

A six-month sprint to harden detection and response across critical infrastructure, triggered by the cyber test shot and access denial.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 2`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases resilience by 10–15 points; begins closing the gap between detection capability and offensive AI tools now in the wild. Slows cascading failure in energy, water and port systems.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with binding minimums for critical operators and joint exercises led by ENISA and the European Commission.

## Priority
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyber intrusion proves we are already in a contested operational environment, and no amount of sovereignty matters if the lights go out before it arrives.

## In practice
We are activating Article 12a of the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) and the NIS2 Directive’s emergency provisions to mandate immediate cyber hardening across electricity transmission, water utilities and major ports. ENISA will issue binding technical baselines by January 2027 for AI-enabled intrusion detection, anomaly response, and air-gapped failover protocols, with a joint exercise scheduled for March 2027 involving all member states. Funding of €12bn is being redirected from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, leveraging the crisis clause in the MFF to bypass normal approval lags. The German and French energy ministries are resisting mandatory retrofits on cost grounds, but we are offering 80% co-financing and fast-track access to gigafactory power allocations in return for full compliance. This is not a long-term fix, but it buys time—because the next intrusion may not be found by accident.
