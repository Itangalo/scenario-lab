### A Frontier That Stalls, a Union That Struggles

Despite signs of a plateau in frontier capability—evidenced by underwhelming model releases and falling prices—the most advanced systems creep upward, with `ai_capability` now at 60.5. This marginal gain, though below prior trends, still widens the gap with open models, where reconstruction efforts push `openweight_capability` to 50.0. The frontier’s inertia slows, but does not stop.

The EU presses forward with its Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation, finalising hosting agreements in France, Germany, and Poland under emergency powers. Legal mandates are issued, and EuroHPC bypasses tendering to accelerate procurement. Yet grid constraints delay full deployment, and the clusters remain offline for now—symbolic rather than operational.

A quiet but pivotal moment arrives: the AI Office initiates the first binding review under the Biosecurity Watchtower framework, targeting a genome-scale open-weight model. If it results in restrictions, it could validate the mechanism. But internal legal debates intensify over whether the Commission overstepped in invoking Article 122 TFEU, risking a challenge to the legitimacy of the entire mobilisation.

Meanwhile, public sentiment dips to 26.0, dragged down by visible job losses and growing resentment over emergency powers. The Council Recommendation on technology servicing stalls in working groups, with member states divided. Sovereignty gains a slight boost from continued infrastructure momentum, but the ongoing `ai_safety` collapse—deepened by the prior breach and now worsened by inaction—drives `ai_safety` down to 9.0. Resilience erodes further as threats evolve faster than defences.

### Fractures Beneath the Surface

The EU’s political capital, already at 10.0, plummets under the weight of ongoing measures and failed legitimacy tests. No new measure is introduced, but the portfolio remains costly. The priority—Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation—adds further strain, while the unmet promise of immediate infrastructure weakens trust.

The narrative of sovereignty as survival gains traction in policy circles, but not in public discourse. The Union acts, but not convincingly.
