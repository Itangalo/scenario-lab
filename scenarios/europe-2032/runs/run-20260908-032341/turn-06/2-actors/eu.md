## In practice
This turn, we formally conclude the Tech Sovereignty Package with a Communication from the Commission affirming the target of €200bn in private investment for AI infrastructure by 2036 and the establishment of six accelerated-permitting zones across the Union, co-managed by the European Investment Bank and national digital ministries. While the target remains ambitious, early commitments from three major hyperscalers — secured through binding grid-access guarantees and tax incentives under the revised State Aid Digital Framework — suggest momentum is building. However, the persistent lag in sovereign compute deployment, now compounded by the supply_chain_coercion event, underscores that access to infrastructure cannot be taken for granted.

The Biosecurity Watchtower, though formally finished, transitions into operational status under the AI Office with a new standing mandate: the Joint Research Centre’s risk thresholds for genome-scale models are now codified in a Commission Implementing Act under the Dual-Use Regulation, granting the EU authority to suspend open-weight releases pending safety review. This is not yet enforcement — no model has been blocked — but the legal gate is open. We are pairing this with the new Secure Open-Model Research Initiative, which will be funded through a reallocation of the unfunded €300M proposal, now repurposed from subsidy to sovereign capability-building. The initiative will be hosted by a consortium led by the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, with participation from Fraunhofer, INRIA, and the Dutch National Cyber Security Centre, and will focus on developing open models with built-in interpretability, weight security, and misuse detection.

We are also advancing legal analysis under Article 346 TFEU and the Anti-Coercion Instrument in light of the ASML board’s leaked proposal. While the Joint Export Governance Initiative did not yield a treaty, it has revealed a new reality: ASML is no longer resisting EU coordination but seeking it as a shield. We are preparing a Council Recommendation for next turn that would formalise EU-level oversight of critical servicing decisions, not as a takeover, but as a mutual protection framework — one that aligns with the emerging middle_power_coalition logic now gaining traction among Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan. This is not sovereignty as self-sufficiency, but sovereignty as coordinated leverage — and it is the only path left as the U.S. posture shifts toward containment.
