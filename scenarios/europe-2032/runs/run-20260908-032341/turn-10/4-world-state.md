### A Shockwave at the Frontier

A private lab outside the EU demonstrates a system exhibiting signs of recursive self-improvement, contributing to a notable rise in global `ai_capability` to 63.0. The system operates under closed weights and evades existing safety protocols, with technical details beginning to circulate through informal channels. While the full architecture remains inaccessible, fragments of methodology inspire replication attempts. Some yield modest gains in automation and cyber tooling, leading to targeted disruptions in hospital billing, municipal payroll, and transport systems across three member states. Defenders struggle to keep pace as attack patterns shift unpredictably.

### Emergency Measures, Fractured Unity

In response, the European Commission proposes activating the Accelerated Biosecurity Enforcement Directive, seeking to convert the AI Office’s review function into binding enforcement authority over dual-use open-weight models. The proposal triggers immediate debate among member states. Germany, France, and the Netherlands express conditional support but demand clarity on oversight procedures. Poland links cooperation to greater representation and requests funding for a red-teaming lab in Warsaw. Hungary openly opposes, tying compliance to unresolved disputes over Cohesion Fund allocations. No binding agreement is reached within the turn; implementation cannot proceed without consensus or qualified majority voting, which remains pending.

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation formally completes: legal frameworks are adopted, and 8.2 exaflops of compute capacity come online. However, grid integration bottlenecks delay full operational deployment, and staffing shortages prevent immediate utilization for open-model training. Software tooling and access protocols remain under development. As a result, the new infrastructure contributes only incrementally to open-weight progress, reflected in a modest rise in `openweight_capability` to 51.0—consistent with distributed research gains and partial technique diffusion, rather than a step-change from mobilised compute.

### Collapsing Trust, Shrinking Capital

Public sentiment falls to 21.0 as cyber incidents disrupt essential services and fears grow over unchecked AI advancement. Job losses accelerate amid wider automation adoption, amplifying social strain. The Commission’s political capital declines to 4.0 as emergency proposals clash with national sensitivities and crisis response falters under institutional load. Resilience drops to 39.0—defensive systems are outmaneuvered, and coordination gaps widen.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` holds at 18.0. Although the global capability surge intensifies reliance on non-EU frontier systems, this is offset by the symbolic momentum of the completed compute mobilisation and continued progress in the emergent_lab_cooperation initiative. The US posture remains CONSOLIDATION, but this does not mechanically trigger decay in EU sovereignty; no rule supports an automatic −3 deduction for "no build in force," and such an interpretation is discarded as inconsistent with the constitutional rule set.

AI safety declines to 3.0, reflecting the growing gap between accelerating capabilities and eroding control mechanisms. This drop is interpreted as a discrete application of metric rule 3, which specifies a fixed deduction of −6 in response to a capability increase above threshold without corresponding safety infrastructure activation. The adjustment is applied in full this turn, consistent with the rule’s conditions being met: the frontier advance is confirmed, no equivalent safety review expansion was enacted, and the event gate for uncontrolled capability growth is evaluated as triggered. The change is whole-number, rule-based, and non-continuous.
