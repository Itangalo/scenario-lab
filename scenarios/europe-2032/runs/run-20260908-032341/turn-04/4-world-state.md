### The Surge Ends, the Front Opens

The **Emergency Resilience Surge** concludes with formal endorsement, embedding AI-driven detection and automated patching into ENISA’s baseline standards for energy and port infrastructure. Pilot integrations in three member states adopt new defensive tooling that exploits a global **cyber defence breakthrough**, closing vulnerabilities at machine speed. Yet gaps remain: water monitoring in several Central and Eastern European regions still lacks real-time coverage, now assigned to the Tech Sovereignty Package’s resilience working group.

Public sentiment edges up, buoyed by visible AI improvements in healthcare and the defensive progress. However, a sudden **ai_investment_collapse** rattles confidence. Major compute build-outs across the Atlantic are scrapped, and European plans relying on those partnerships evaporate. The frontier’s training capacity shrinks — a first — but progress persists, with **ai_capability** rising by 1.0.

An **openweight_acceleration_event** is observed: a coordinated release of advanced open-weight models from multiple research labs, including early outputs from the emergent_lab_cooperation initiative, begins circulating in developer communities. While not yet matching frontier performance, these models demonstrate unexpectedly rapid capability gains, pushing **openweight_capability** to 45.0. This reflects diffusion and optimization rather than a single breakthrough, consistent with gradual accumulation under Invariant 2. The gap to the closed frontier narrows, challenging export control assumptions, but no model exceeds the current closed frontier threshold.

In response, the Commission proposes tightening biosecurity thresholds under the Dual-Use Regulation, accompanied by a *proposal* for €300 million in research funding for secure open models. However, no funding is released this turn. The expenditure remains contingent on upcoming budget negotiations and requires trade-offs against existing commitments. Without new revenue or reprioritization, the allocation cannot proceed, preserving fiscal coherence amid eroding political capital.

The **ASML Joint Export Governance Initiative**, now the named priority, enters intensified negotiations. Discussions explore invoking Article 346 TFEU to justify joint export decisions, with exploratory talks on liability shielding and potential future funding mechanisms. However, these elements remain under discussion — not agreed. No binding commitments are made. ASML’s board continues to resist, citing market risks and U.S. pressure, while Dutch officials hesitate. Trilateral coordination advances technically, but no policy agreement is reached by June.

### Capital Erodes, Sovereignty Slips

With five large measures in flight and no completions this turn, political capital erodes. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech sovereignty package** yield no sovereignty gains, while rising capability and no category 4 completion drive **eu_ai_sovereignty** down. The investment collapse and open-model acceleration deepen dependency — a quiet but structural setback. Political capital falls to 23.0, reflecting constrained bandwidth and delayed decisions, but avoids deeper loss as no irreversible commitments are made without funding or consensus.
