We have appointed General Elisa Mäkinen, former head of Finland’s cyber command, as Crisis Implementation Coordinator, and she is already in Brussels cutting through procurement delays, bypassing national bottlenecks with direct EU procurement under Article 122 TFEU, and enforcing peer-reviewed timelines for zero-trust rollouts. ENISA is now conducting weekly progress reviews with national CERTs, and we are threatening to suspend Cohesion Fund disbursements to any member state that fails to meet the 90-day exercise deadline. France’s nuclear opt-out remains a gap, but we are negotiating a parallel red-teaming protocol with the EU’s nuclear safety agency to preserve oversight without violating sovereignty claims.

Meanwhile, the Accelerated Biosecurity Enforcement Directive is reaching its final turn. Despite Poland’s demand for veto power and Hungary’s boycott, we have secured a qualified majority by offering binding seats on the Enforcement Oversight Board and linking Warsaw’s red-teaming lab to the new sovereign compute programme. The directive will pass, but enforcement will depend on whether national agencies have the capacity to act — and right now, they do not. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme: not as a response to today’s crisis, but to ensure we are not helpless in the next. We are repurposing €3.2 billion from unused Digital Europe Programme funds and leveraging the Anti-Coercion Instrument to secure priority access to EU-fabbed chips. We are fast-tracking permitting for three new sovereign data centres — in Finland, Spain, and Romania — with direct grid connections and hardened physical security. This is not about catching up to the frontier; it is about ensuring that, when the next rupture comes, the EU still has a seat at the table — and a switch it can control.
