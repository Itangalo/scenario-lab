### Fractured Gains Amid Mounting Pressure

The **Accelerated Biosecurity Enforcement Directive** narrowly passes in COREPER II, securing a qualified majority after last-minute concessions to Poland linking its red-teaming ambitions to the new compute programme. However, the victory is hollow. Enforcement mechanisms remain unstaffed, and national agencies lack the technical capacity to conduct real-time model audits. Judicial oversight demanded by Germany delays implementation, and Hungary continues to block funding access, citing unresolved disputes.

Meanwhile, the **Emergency Resilience Surge** shows flickers of progress. General Elisa Mäkinen’s appointment energises coordination, and ENISA begins weekly reviews with national CERTs. Direct procurement under Article 122 TFEU bypasses some delays, but France maintains its nuclear exclusion, and Spain’s personnel shortages persist. Only 28% of the €1.8 billion is deployed by mid-year. A limited cyber intrusion at a regional grid operator in Belgium is contained quickly—suggesting marginal gains in resilience—but no full incident exercise is completed.

The **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme** is launched with fanfare, repurposing €3.2 billion from the Digital Europe Programme. Permitting is fast-tracked in Finland, Spain, and Romania, and antitrust scrutiny of foreign cloud dominance intensifies. Yet, the programme is still in early stages: no new compute is online, and EU access to next-generation chips remains constrained by U.S.-pressured supply chains.

Public sentiment holds at crisis levels. Sabotage fears grow after a near-miss at a Swedish substation, and job displacement continues unchecked. The EU’s political capital remains at 1.0—strained but not yet depleted—despite the cumulative burden of three active measures. The portfolio charge of −10 is partially offset by a proposal bonus of +3 for directly addressing a severe, recent cyber incident, but no further draw on political capital occurs this turn. The launch of a major sovereignty initiative offers no immediate relief, and no sentiment boost materialises.

AI capability climbs to 64.0 as frontier labs refine recursive techniques. Openweight capability inches up to 51.5, reflecting minor model leaks, but no breakthrough is released. Safety remains stagnant at 3.0—no new safeguards are deployed. Sovereignty holds at 17.0: the 0.5 rise in AI capability is below the 2.0 threshold, so no decay penalty applies, and no capacity event or completion bonus occurred. The metric remains unchanged, consistent with the rules.
