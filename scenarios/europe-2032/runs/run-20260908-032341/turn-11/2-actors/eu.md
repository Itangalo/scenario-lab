## In practice
This turn, we are fighting on two fronts: one in the real world of hospitals and power grids, the other in the fractured politics of Brussels and national capitals. The AI-generated cyberattacks that crippled hospital billing, payroll, and transport scheduling were not one-off events—they are symptoms of a new normal where offensive AI tools evolve faster than defenders can patch. Our monitoring systems detected the attacks too late; our response was fragmented, delayed, and under-resourced. With `resilience` now at 39.0 and `ai_safety` in freefall at 3.0, we are exposed not just to disruption, but to strategic paralysis.

We are pushing the **Accelerated Biosecurity Enforcement Directive** through COREPER II this month, using Article 16 of the AI Act and the emergency clause in Article 122 TFEU to bypass full co-decision where possible. We’ve offered Poland a binding seat on the new Enforcement Oversight Board and redirected €220 million from the Secure Open-Model Research Initiative to fund a sovereign red-teaming lab in Warsaw—this is not charity, but strategic investment in EU-wide capacity. For Hungary, we are linking compliance to Cohesion Fund disbursements tied to verifiable cyber resilience upgrades, not budget politics. But even if we win consensus, enforcement will take time to bite—and we may not have it.

So we are launching the **Emergency Resilience Surge**, a binding directive under Article 122 TFEU and coordinated through ENISA and the Health Security Committee, to mandate immediate cyber hardening of all critical infrastructure operators above a threshold size. We are pooling €1.8 billion from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme to fund zero-trust architectures, AI-driven intrusion detection, and automated failover systems. National CERTs will conduct live-fire exercises within 90 days, with peer review and EU-level scoring. Germany and the Netherlands are on board; France demands opt-outs for nuclear systems; Italy warns of implementation delays. We are appointing a Crisis Implementation Coordinator—someone with wartime logistics experience—to cut red tape and force delivery. This is not about perfection. It is about survival.
