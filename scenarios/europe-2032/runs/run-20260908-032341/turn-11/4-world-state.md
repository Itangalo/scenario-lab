### Fractured Response to Escalating Threats

Despite the Commission’s push, the **Accelerated Biosecurity Enforcement Directive** stalls in COREPER II. Germany and the Netherlands demand judicial oversight mechanisms before granting enforcement powers; Poland, though offered funding and representation, insists on veto rights over red-teaming targets. Hungary refuses to engage, citing unresolved Cohesion Fund disputes. With no qualified majority, the directive remains in limbo—symbolic but unenforced.

The **Emergency Resilience Surge** is formally adopted under Article 122 TFEU, but implementation falters immediately. France invokes national security exemptions for nuclear infrastructure, Italy reports a three-month delay due to procurement bottlenecks, and Spain lacks technical personnel to deploy the mandated zero-trust systems. ENISA scrambles to coordinate, but the Crisis Implementation Coordinator arrives late—appointed only in November, after legal disputes over mandate scope. Funding is approved, but disbursement is slow, and only 12% of the €1.8 billion reaches operators by year-end.

Meanwhile, **supply_chain_coercion** intensifies. Washington’s pressure on ASML to restrict older lithography tools hits EU semiconductor fabs reliant on Dutch servicing. SMIC-linked firms are the stated target, but ripple effects delay maintenance for European mid-tier manufacturers. The EU’s sole leverage—its market—is unused, as internal divisions prevent a coordinated countermeasure. The episode underscores strategic vulnerability, reinforcing dependence on external technological control points.

**backlash_physical** escalates across the bloc. Data centre occupations occur in Ireland and Finland; a transformer sabotage in Sweden causes a regional blackout blamed on anti-AI activists. Policing surges strain budgets, and media coverage frames the unrest as both symptom and accelerant of societal breakdown. Public trust erodes further as automation-linked job losses mount and cyber disruptions persist.
