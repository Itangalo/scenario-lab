### Patch Tuesday, every day
The winter brought one piece of genuinely good news for defenders. Research groups on both sides of the Atlantic demonstrated patching and detection tools that finally kept pace with automated probing — closing off a whole class of swarm-like intrusions that had plagued municipalities. ENISA moved quickly, pushing the updates through certified hospitals and larger utilities where the earlier containment drills had already taken hold. There, exercises that had failed a year before now passed quietly.

Where certificates had never arrived, little changed. Smaller town networks and subcontractors could not deploy the new tooling, and two ransomware waves in March still forced manual shutdowns. Engineers spoke of a two-speed continent: protected cores, brittle edges.

### A recipe argument
At the same time a contested biosecurity paper rattled health officials. A genome model, with non-expert prompting, had produced what reviewers called a viable design for a human-infecting organism. Authors were accused both of hype and of publishing too much. Inside Brussels, the file went to health emergency planners and to the new compact partners for joint scrutiny of opaque models. No incident followed, but stockpile orders and detector audits quietly rose.

### Empty desks
Neither development touched the dominant grievance. Graduate hiring in law, accountancy, software and customer operations stayed frozen. The Commission's answer — a co-financed guarantee of income bridging, six-month retraining vouchers and hiring credits in the four sectors, with reporting obligations on large deployers — cleared labour ministers quickly but stumbled in finance talks. Two net-contributor capitals demanded a capped, temporary envelope and delayed disbursement rules until late spring. First payments reached only pilot cohorts by June, and student unions called it a voucher for jobs that no longer existed.

The middle-power compact formally entered into force, pooling hosting quotas and swapping incident feeds. The holdout capital still did not unwind its separate supply arrangement, leaving cohesion cracked even as Brussels claimed leverage.
