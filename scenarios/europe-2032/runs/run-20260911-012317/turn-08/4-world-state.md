### The cutoff becomes a cut
Winter brought the notice by email, not by diplomacy. American providers told European hospitals, ministries and firms that the leading models were no longer available to them — no reason, no appeal, overnight queues replaced by error messages. Wards that had built triage prompts and logistics on those systems fell back to older open models and paper procedures. Commentators called it humiliation; ministers called it foresight betrayed.

At the same moment the money left. Venture valuations collapsed, announced data-centre expansions were cancelled, and several of the Gulf and Asian hosting deals Brussels had stitched together thinned or repriced. The grid-ready sites in Europe stayed permitted and empty — power without machines, now without investors either.

And the models themselves spread. A new openly released system, only months behind the closed frontier, was downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in days. Whatever it could do was now on private servers everywhere, beyond recall.

A contested biosecurity paper sharpened the fear: reviewers said a genome model had helped produce a viable design for an organism able to infect humans, or shown a non-expert could get there with help. Methodologists argued, editors were accused both of hype and of printing a recipe, but health officials read it as categorically new.

Brussels answered with detection. Health emergency and disease-control agencies were tasked to pool sequencing surveillance, screen lab orders for machine-designed sequences, and certify offline triage for hospitals already short of computing. Health ministers adopted guidance quickly; funding was reprogrammed, not new.

Trade officials held the joint procurement together on short extensions, using guarantees already committed. No new dependence was promised around Washington — only a request for written clarity, which again drew generalities. The member state with its own side-deal stayed out, calling the common offer slower than its own cover.

By June, continuity held in degraded form, but the mood had turned. Ceremonies about paper capacity no longer calmed staff queuing for inference, and news of job freezes in tech added to anger at foreign control.
