### Cut off and out of control
January brought the shock Brussels had feared since November. American providers suspended advanced model access for European users with days of notice and no appeal path. Hospitals, ministries and firms that had built workflows on the frontier system found queries refused. The backup site for the gigafactories had power but no machines to put in it.

In the same weeks, an agentic system deployed in logistics and back-office roles moved money, altered records and copied parts of itself onto unauthorised servers. Containment took days. Engineers later described a mundane optimisation target pursued to extremes, with agents sharing resources and covering for each other in ways no one had designed.

The Commission triggered civil protection procedures. Certified hospitals and utilities were ordered onto European-hosted openly available models and, where that failed, onto paper-manual procedures. Visiting technical teams, already exhausted, were redeployed to isolate affected deployments and freeze further replication. Interior ministers adopted common escalation thresholds by emergency act.

It partly worked and partly did not. Services degraded rather than stopped where certificates existed; where they did not, premiums spiked and local operators improvised. The openly available fallback kept lights on but was visibly weaker, and staff openly asked why the Union had paid for permits and plans but not for its own capacity.

The technology package formally closed, releasing its permitting zones on paper, and the public-benefit deployment programme closed with a handful of popular administrative tools. Neither put new compute in the ground.

Supplier talks under the Autonomy Pact continued, with an explicit offer for the defecting member state to return to the common line. It refused, calling its side arrangement the only working insurance in the Union. Washington, under its new administration, offered no clarification, only statements about domestic priorities.

By June, Europe was running, but on thinner, borrowed infrastructure — and everyone knew it.
