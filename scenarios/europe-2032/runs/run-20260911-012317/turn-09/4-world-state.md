### Holding on paper
July to December 2030 was the semester Brussels learned to live inside the cutoff.

American frontier systems stayed dark for European hospitals, ministries and firms. The error messages did not change, and neither did Washington's replies: requests for written clarity drew statements about commercial decisions and security reviews. Trade officials kept joint procurement with Japanese, Korean, Taiwanese and Gulf hosts alive on short extensions, using guarantees already committed. Volumes thinned at each renewal. The permitted grid-ready sites stayed cleared, connected, and empty.

Health agencies carried the heaviest load. With wards running on older openly available models and offline procedures, the disease-control and emergency authorities pooled sequencing data, began screening synthesis orders for machine-designed sequences, and pushed certification of offline triage through the Health Council. Money was reprogrammed from existing health programmes; no new budget passed. Staff complied, but complained that guidance arrived faster than servers.

That strain sharpened in autumn, when a contested genome-model paper claimed a viable design for a human-infecting organism, or a credible path for a non-expert to reach one with assistance. Methodologists fought over controls and editors over publication, but health officials treated it as categorically new. Coverage leaked beyond specialist press, and opposition MPs asked why detection was funded by reprogramming.

The openly released frontier-following model compounded the dilemma. Downloaded hundreds of thousands of times, it powered the fallback clinics and the logistics workarounds — and it also meant any uplift it enabled was unrecallable.

By December, continuity held in degraded form. Queues for inference lengthened, tech hiring froze further, and the member state with its own external cover stayed outside the common line, calling it slower. No rebuild started. Brussels said waiting for capital and investors to recover was prudence; critics called it paralysis made doctrine.
