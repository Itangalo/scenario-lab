## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity as the foundation of strategic autonomy and public trust  
- Trigger: the continued collapse of eu_political_capital to 1.0 and the failure of all centralised sovereignty initiatives to land  
- Grounds: Two years ago, we committed to sovereign AI capacity as a matter of ownership, control, and infrastructure. Today, that vision has no ground beneath it. The Sovereign Inference Backbone is stalled, the Replication Foundry is barely functional, and political capital is too depleted to enforce compliance. Yet the European AI Commons—unplanned, unowned, but aligned—has become the only living expression of EU AI sovereignty. Our commitment to sovereignty stands, but sovereignty is no longer a thing we build alone; it is a thing we steward, protect, and legitimise in others. To hold the commitment, we must redefine how it is fulfilled.

- `Replication Foundry Initiative` (category 4, costs 3 per turn, started turn 8, finishes on turn 12): Establish a distributed EU capacity to replicate and distill frontier models from open weights, using repurposed hardware and emergency grid access to close the capability gap — **finished**
- `Emergency Observability Directive` (category 3, costs 3 per turn, started turn 11, finishes on turn 11): Mandates full, real-time telemetry and model checkpoint access for all high-capability AI systems operating in or affecting EU critical infrastructure, with immediate decommissioning authority for non-compliant providers — **finished**
- `Support for the European AI Commons` (category 5, costs 3 per turn, started turn 12, finishes on turn 14): Provides coordinated funding, legal protection, and interoperability standards to the emergent European AI Commons, enabling it to scale evaluation frameworks, defensive tooling, and replication pipelines as a distributed alternative to state-controlled infrastructure

Canceled measure: *Institutional Integrity Audit*. The audit was completed in turn 8 and delivered no actionable findings; continuing its funding would misallocate scarce capital in a moment of systemic fragility.

## New measure
**Measure:** Mandate Minimum Security Baseline for Public AI Infrastructure  
Requires all public-sector AI deployments connected to critical infrastructure to meet a defined set of security, monitoring, and fail-safe standards—lightweight but enforceable—and mandates public reporting of compliance status to the European AI Commons.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: small  
Finishes on turn: 13  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 3–5 points by hardening the weakest links in public AI use; modestly increase ai_safety by creating a baseline of observable, auditable practices in government systems  
Applies to: EU institutions, member state agencies, and public contractors deploying AI in health, energy, transport, and social services

## Priority
**Priority:** Support for the European AI Commons  
This remains our top priority because it is the only functioning network through which the EU can still act—bypassing both corporate gatekeeping and intergovernmental paralysis—while the state’s own infrastructure initiatives have failed to land.

## In practice
We are finalising the legal framework for the “Public AI Steward” designation, which will grant participating institutions in the European AI Commons immunity from extraterritorial pressure and binding obligations to maintain public access to their models and tools. The European Research Council is now operationalising the emergency grant facility, with the first call for proposals focused on interpretability tooling and red-teaming automation. ENISA has delivered its gap analysis, confirming that the Commons lacks structured pipelines for bio-risk screening and long-horizon agent evaluation—two areas we will fund immediately from redirected backbone resources.

The Replication Foundry Initiative, having officially finished this turn, delivered only partial replication of open-weight models at 77.0 capability, and failed to scale beyond niche academic clusters. No further funding will be allocated. Its intellectual assets—distillation techniques, energy optimisation protocols—will be transferred to the Commons’ replication working group.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Inference Backbone remains legally and technically stalled: member states are unwilling to cede control, and the bilateral deal signed with the U.S. hyperscaler has further eroded trust. We are no longer pursuing enforcement; instead, we are offering participating labs in the Commons priority access to any future EU-controlled inference capacity as an incentive to align. The new small measure—Mandate Minimum Security Baseline—will be proposed through the Committee for Internal Market and Industry, leveraging Article 114 TFEU as legal basis, with a fast-track procedure requested at COREPER. Resistance from smaller member states is expected on grounds of cost and sovereignty, so we are offering co-financing through the Digital Europe Programme and technical support from the Commons’ security working group. This is not a fortress; it is a floor. And right now, the floor is all we have.
