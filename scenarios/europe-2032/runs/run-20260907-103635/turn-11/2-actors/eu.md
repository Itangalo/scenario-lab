## Priority
**Priority:** Support for the European AI Commons  
This is our top priority because the emergent European AI Commons is the only functioning expression of EU AI sovereignty left — and it exists outside our control. While the Emergency Observability Directive failed, this grassroots effort has already begun pooling models, tools, and expertise in a way that bypasses both corporate gatekeeping and intergovernmental paralysis. We must act now to legitimise, resource, and scale it before it fragments or gets co-opted — not as owners, but as enablers.

## In practice
We are redirecting 70% of the Sovereign Inference Backbone’s unspent Q3–Q4 budget to an emergency grant facility for the European AI Commons, hosted by the European Research Council and governed by a rotating council of participating institutions. Legal teams at DG CONNECT are drafting a framework recognition status — the “Public AI Steward” designation — that will shield participating labs from extraterritorial pressure and ensure their work cannot be unilaterally withdrawn from public access. We are tasking ENISA with mapping the Commons’ current evaluation pipelines and identifying three critical gaps — interpretability, red-teaming automation, and bio-risk screening — to focus the first wave of funding.

At the same time, we are formally acknowledging that the Emergency Observability Directive has failed: no major provider complied, and the two U.S. firms have now moved all EU-facing queries through Singapore and Dubai. The directive is being withdrawn, and enforcement efforts are being redirected toward supporting member states that choose to adopt the Commons’ standards voluntarily. We are calling a special meeting of the Research Ministers under Article 172 TFEU to affirm the Union’s role not as a builder of monolithic infrastructure, but as a guarantor of open, resilient, and accountable AI capacity — one that lives in the network, not in the state. The message is clear: if the EU cannot command, it must convene.
