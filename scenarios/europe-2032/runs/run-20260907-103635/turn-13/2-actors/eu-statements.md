## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer mistake motion for progress when the ground has shifted beneath us
  - Trigger: the cyber_major_incident and bio_uplift_findings this turn, combined with `ai_safety` at 0.0 and `eu_political_capital` frozen at 1.0
  - Grounds: We have acted under uncertainty for two years, but our actions have not landed. The Sovereign Inference Backbone is stalled, the Replication Foundry failed, and our safety instruments are absent where they are most needed. The world is now moving faster than our measures can follow, and the cost of failure is no longer political — it is systemic. To act under uncertainty now means to stop pretending that legacy instruments can contain recursive, distributed, weaponisable capability. We must accept that being wrong is not the only price of being late — irrelevance is worse.
  - Evidence quoted: cyber_major_incident: A large, largely automated attack lands. Its form is chosen at the time – a ransomware sweep across public services, a compromised software dependency with an unknown blast radius, or disruption of infrastructure directly – and severity scales with how far offensive capability has outrun defence. Damage is real and public, defenders are visibly behind, and attribution takes months. Whoever ran it, the tooling was model-generated.

bio_uplift_findings: A genome model produces a viable design for an organism able to infect humans, or a credible study shows a non-expert reaching that point with model assistance. It is contested on methodology, the authors are accused of both alarmism and of publishing a recipe, and the argument stays inside the biosecurity community – but it is a categorically stronger signal than anything published so far. This is a precursor: it opens the bio gate for the next 4 turns.
  - Reason: The cyber and bio developments reveal that threats are outpacing EU responses, directly challenging the value of acting under uncertainty without effective follow-through.
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Defend and deepen the European AI Commons as the only functioning expression of EU AI sovereignty, redirecting all feasible resources to harden, secure, and scale its evaluation, replication, and defensive capacity
  - Trigger: the formal conclusion of the Replication Foundry Initiative with subpar results, the absorption of its assets into the Commons, and the designation of three Public AI Stewards under its umbrella
  - Grounds: Our original commitment to sovereign AI capacity assumed state-led infrastructure could be built in time. It cannot. The only capacity that still functions — the only network still aligned, still operating, still producing — is the European AI Commons. It is not owned, but it is ours. To keep our commitment, we must stop trying to replace it with something more controllable and instead double down on what already works: distributed, resilient, open-but-secured technical stewardship. This is not retreat — it is realignment under fire.
  - Evidence quoted: The Replication Foundry Initiative formally concluded, but its output fell far short of expectations—limited to distilling models at the 77.0 level, with no operational integration into public services. Its assets were folded into the European AI Commons, now the sole locus of EU-aligned technical effort. The Commission granted provisional "Public AI Steward" status to three research hubs, offering legal shielding from foreign data demands.
  - Reason: The failure of the Replication Foundry and absorption into the Commons directly undermines the original sovereign capacity assumption, forcing realignment on what constitutes viable EU AI sovereignty.
