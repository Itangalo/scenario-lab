## Statement changes
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer mistake motion for progress when the ground has shifted beneath us  
- Trigger: the cyber_major_incident and bio_uplift_findings this turn, combined with `ai_safety` at 0.0 and `eu_political_capital` frozen at 1.0  
- Grounds: We have acted under uncertainty for two years, but our actions have not landed. The Sovereign Inference Backbone is stalled, the Replication Foundry failed, and our safety instruments are absent where they are most needed. The world is now moving faster than our measures can follow, and the cost of failure is no longer political — it is systemic. To act under uncertainty now means to stop pretending that legacy instruments can contain recursive, distributed, weaponisable capability. We must accept that being wrong is not the only price of being late — irrelevance is worse.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Defend and deepen the European AI Commons as the only functioning expression of EU AI sovereignty, redirecting all feasible resources to harden, secure, and scale its evaluation, replication, and defensive capacity  
- Trigger: the formal conclusion of the Replication Foundry Initiative with subpar results, the absorption of its assets into the Commons, and the designation of three Public AI Stewards under its umbrella  
- Grounds: Our original commitment to sovereign AI capacity assumed state-led infrastructure could be built in time. It cannot. The only capacity that still functions — the only network still aligned, still operating, still producing — is the European AI Commons. It is not owned, but it is ours. To keep our commitment, we must stop trying to replace it with something more controllable and instead double down on what already works: distributed, resilient, open-but-secured technical stewardship. This is not retreat — it is realignment under fire.

## New measure

Establishes a rapid-response red-teaming unit within the European AI Commons, mandated to simulate high-consequence attacks using frontier models at or above 79.0 capability, with focus on bio-risk pathways and automated cyber campaigns, and to publish defensive countermeasures within 72 hours of detection.

Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)

## Priority
**Priority:** Support for the European AI Commons  
This remains our top priority because it is the only entity still capable of acting at the speed and scale required — and because the cyber_major_incident and bio_uplift_findings this turn prove that the threat is no longer theoretical, but already in motion.

## In practice
We are treating the European AI Commons not as a fallback, but as the frontline. With the Sovereign Inference Backbone finishing this turn and delivering no operational capacity, we are redirecting its remaining budget — €1.2 billion in unspent commitments — to the Commons’ replication and evaluation arms. The three Public AI Stewards will be formally integrated into a new governance council, with voting rights tied to contribution and compliance with security standards. The European Research Council is fast-tracking grants for interpretability and agent monitoring, with a new call focused specifically on detecting recursive self-improvement in model weights.

The cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across regional health systems, enabled by AI-generated zero-days — has exposed the fragility of our current posture. We are not proposing a new directive; we do not have the capital. Instead, we are tasking the newly expanded red-teaming unit under the Commons to simulate similar attacks weekly, using distillations of open-weight models at 79.0, and to feed mitigation scripts directly into ENISA’s incident response pipeline. The bio_uplift_findings have triggered emergency consultations with the EMA and Frontex: we are establishing a joint working group on bio-risk screening, hosted at the Commons, with authority to flag and block model releases that meet predefined risk thresholds.

Public sentiment remains at 15.0, and we are not blind to the political cost of acting without visible public infrastructure. But we are no longer building monuments — we are building immune responses. The Mandate Minimum Security Baseline failed in Council, but its core requirements are being implemented as technical guidelines by the Commons’ security working group, with support from the Digital Europe Programme. We are not waiting for unanimity. We are acting where we can, with what we have, because the next incident may not be recoverable.
