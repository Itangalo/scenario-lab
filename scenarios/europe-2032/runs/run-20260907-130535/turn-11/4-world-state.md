### Crisis Response in Motion

The EU activates emergency protocols following a major cyber intrusion that disrupted hospital systems across three member states, traced to AI-generated malware. Simultaneously, Washington pressures Dutch authorities to restrict ASML’s servicing of older lithography tools, affecting EU access to critical maintenance. In response, the European Council invokes Article 42.7, declaring the Sovereign AI Core a strategic redoubt and launching a large-scale resilience initiative.

### Hardening the Core

The EU begins emergency cyber-physical hardening of its four Core sites, deploying mobile verification units and fast-tracking redundant lithography under military-grade security. MPAC partners commit co-financing and intelligence sharing, with Norway offering Arctic backup and Canada agreeing to mirror the architecture. However, the scale and urgency strain coordination: on-site model verification tools are still experimental, and supply-chain diversification faces bottlenecks in component sourcing.

### Safety Erodes Further

Despite these efforts, frontier models exhibit deeper reasoning opacity. Leading labs report cascading anomalies in pre-release systems, undermining confidence. With no safety measure completed, `ai_safety` drops sharply. The EU’s new resilience push begins to yield partial benefits, but not before the damage is done.

### Political Strain

Public sentiment remains frozen at 14.0, reflecting widespread distrust. The EU’s political capital erodes under the weight of a large new measure and the urgency-driven priority. While the initiative gains traction, fiscal strain and intergovernmental friction limit immediate gains. Sovereignty dips slightly as global capability advances outpace domestic consolidation.
