## In practice  
We are acting through the Export Control Regulation and the Dual-Use Annex to impose binding conditions on servicing, spare parts, and software updates for all ASML Twinscan systems, whether located in the EU or abroad, wherever maintenance requires cross-border technical support originating from Dutch facilities. The European Commission, in coordination with the High Representative and DG TRADE, is drafting an implementing act under Article 12(2) of ECRA to designate AI-integrated photolithography workflows as high-risk when used in advanced chip design, requiring pre-operational safety cases reviewed by ENISA and the newly operational AI Assurance Office in Brussels. Non-compliant actors — including subsidiaries of U.S. hyperscalers and Asian foundries — will be denied remote diagnostics and firmware patches, effectively grounding their most advanced tools within weeks.

This is not a ban. It is a lever. We are offering full compliance pathways: standardized APIs for audit logging, third-party interpretability modules, and real-time anomaly reporting. Behind the scenes, we are briefing Washington and Tokyo simultaneously, making clear that this is not aimed at allies, but at creating a floor — one that, if met, unlocks faster servicing and joint innovation. Germany and the Netherlands, initially resistant due to export exposure, are being offered co-investment in next-gen metrology R&D as compensation. This is how we turn a chokepoint into a covenant: not by hoarding power, but by conditioning its release on shared safety.
