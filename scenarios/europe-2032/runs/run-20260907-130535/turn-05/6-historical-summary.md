The EU advances the Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme, redirecting SCAP funds to nuclear and geothermal AI infrastructure with 5GW of low-carbon power allocated by turn 6. Legal challenges from Germany and Austria, along with ENTSO-E’s refusal to bind grid allocations without unanimity, delay implementation despite Commission action under Article 122 TFEU. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) becomes operational, certifying first models and mandating automated verification in public-sector contract and clinical coding. Real-time logging for critical systems is blocked by Italy and Hungary in COREPER II, undermining enforcement. Public trust declines further amid accelerating job losses in professional sectors, growing protests, and skepticism toward EAAD as symbolic. A new resilience communications effort fails to gain traction. Pre-exercised cyber response protocols for critical sectors are delayed by legal and coordination hurdles, stalling formal launch. AI capability reaches 60.0, driven by advances in autonomous code generation, while open-weight models achieve 48.5, aided by increased EU access to non-US frontier models. The US elects an anti-AI administration, signaling forthcoming restrictions and job guarantees; global implications await its inauguration next turn.
