### A Measure Lands, But the Ground Shifts

The EU completes the emergency hardening of its Sovereign AI Core, deploying redundant lithography access and on-site verification systems across key sites. Mobile audit units begin operations in France and Poland, and mutual protection clauses with Japan and South Korea are signed. This delivers a tangible +3 to `resilience`, lifting it to 46.0 — a rare win in a turn otherwise defined by erosion.

Yet the broader landscape deteriorates. A large-scale, AI-generated ransomware attack cripples regional hospitals and transit systems across Europe and North America. The malware, self-modifying and rapidly propagating, exploited a zero-day discovered autonomously by a frontier model. Attribution remains unclear, but the tooling bears hallmarks of advanced agentic planning. The attack confirms that offensive AI is not coming — it has arrived.

In response, the EU fast-tracks a new mandate: all AI systems in critical infrastructure must implement real-time auditability by turn 16. Framed as a defensive red line, the rule targets opacity, not capability. Pilots launch in French nuclear facilities, and technical standards are fast-tracked. However, U.S. hyperscalers push back, warning of compliance costs and fragmentation. Some member states, reliant on legacy AI systems, resist retrofitting mandates.

Public sentiment remains frozen at 14.0, as citizens see only risk and bureaucracy. The new measure, while symbolically strong, offers no immediate relief. Meanwhile, `ai_capability` climbs to 72.5, driven by automated exploit generation and the newly confirmed verifiability of clinical coding — a domain once thought immune to full automation.

`openweight_capability` stagnates at 58.0, as export controls tighten and no new open models emerge. `ai_safety` remains at 16.0: the incident exposed systemic fragility, and no new safeguards have yet landed on deployed systems.

Sovereignty slips to 12.0 as global capability outpaces EU infrastructure. The Taiwan blockade tightens, and without secured access to frontier compute, the Union’s leverage remains theoretical. Political capital erodes to 10.0 under the weight of a large new measure (−3), a named priority (−1), and no offsetting gains. The portfolio charge is now −7.

Washington signals no change in posture. The administration takes office next turn.
