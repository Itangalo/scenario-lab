### The Cut-Off and the Response

In January 2031, access to the leading AI model was abruptly suspended for EU institutions and affiliated operators. Hospitals relying on diagnostic assistance, border agencies using real-time translation, and research centres running simulations were left without critical tools—overnight, with no explanation. The incident confirmed long-standing fears: Europe’s dependence on foreign frontier systems is not just strategic—it is operational.

The European Union responded by launching the **Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative**, a large category 4 measure aimed at building sovereign fallback clusters in Finland, Luxembourg, and Bulgaria. Legal authority was invoked under crisis protocols, and stalled data centre projects were repurposed. However, integration challenges persist: power upgrades face local opposition, and microwave backhaul licensing remains incomplete. While construction begins, full capability is years behind schedule.

Meanwhile, the **Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes** reached completion, now generating telemetry from Jülich, CINECA, Prague, and Bologna. Yet national exemptions limit data sharing, weakening central oversight. The **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** remains delayed—staffing, legal authority, and security clearances are still unresolved.

Public sentiment holds at 20.0. Synthetic leaks continue to destabilize regional politics, and trust in AI-mediated governance erodes further. No visible benefit has offset the damage.

AI capability surged to 89.5, driven by self-improving validation loops now operating across engineering and legal domains. Open-weight models rose to 76.5, as compressed derivatives of frontier techniques leaked through inference APIs and tooling ecosystems—narrowing the gap, but not closing it.

With `ai_capability` rising over 4 points, `ai_safety` remains stagnant at 8.0—no new safeguards have landed on deployed systems. Resilience drops to 45.0, penalized for rising capability and lack of finished category 6 protections.

Political capital climbs slightly to 3.0 after finishing the Sentinel Nodes (+3), but is overwhelmed by ongoing costs: −3 for the Behavioural Observatory, −3 for the new redundancy initiative, and −1 for prioritization. A proposal bonus of +8 reflects the urgency of responding directly to a severe, recent shock—the denial of access. Without it, capital would be negative.

Sovereignty falls to 12.0: −1 from capability growth, −3 from the `eu_frontier_access_denied` event, despite efforts to build fallbacks. These do not yet exist; the loss of access stands unmitigated.
