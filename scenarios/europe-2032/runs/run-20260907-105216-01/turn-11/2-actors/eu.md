### Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without sovereign execution capacity, both mandates collapse into irrelevance  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has demonstrated that political agency in AI is not a function of rules or observation alone, but of *execution*. Without the ability to run consequential systems on infrastructure we control, our governance is theoretical and our risk prevention performative. This recalibration reflects a categorical shift: we are no longer balancing two mandates — we are defending the material basis on which either could ever be fulfilled.

- modify `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity; this now includes the full stack from training infrastructure to inference services and telemetry access  
- Grounds: The outage did not stem from model weights alone, but from a cascade of dependencies: frontier API access, proprietary validation loops, and closed telemetry ecosystems. Resilience requires not just fallback compute, but sovereign access to the full decision stack. The original position underestimated the depth of entanglement.

- retire `act_under_uncertainty`  
- Trigger: The collapse of political capital to near-zero and the failure of the Behavioural Observatory to launch despite two years of planning  
- Grounds: We acted early, repeatedly, and at great cost — but without the institutional capacity to absorb or act on what we discovered. Our willingness to commit before clarity has become a liability, not a virtue. We are no longer exhausting political capital to reduce uncertainty — we are burning it to mask our powerlessness. This principle, once a source of agility, now enables self-deception.

## In practice
We are treating the denial of frontier access not as an anomaly but as a precedent. The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative is now operational under the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking, with legal authority drawn from Article 172 TFEU and the Crisis Competence Protocol invoked by the Commission in February. We are repurposing three stalled commercial data centre projects in Finland, Luxembourg, and Bulgaria — sites previously abandoned due to power constraints — by fast-tracking grid upgrades through the Trans-European Networks for Energy mechanism and allocating emergency spectrum for microwave backhaul. These clusters will not match the frontier, but they will run certified derivatives of open-weight models up to capability 65.0, hardened against manipulation and tuned for continuity in health triage, border management, and energy dispatch.

Funding comes from a temporary reallocation of 40% of the Digital Europe Programme’s AI rollout budget, justified as crisis mitigation, and a new €900 million line in the MFF under "Strategic Infrastructure Continuity," to be ratified in the upcoming Mid-Term Review. Member states are being offered bilateral service-level agreements guaranteeing priority access during disruptions in exchange for waiving local permitting delays. Resistance from two northern member states, concerned about subsidy competition, is being addressed through co-location rights in their own hardened facilities and shared operations staffing.

The Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory remains in legal gridlock — not from lack of will, but from the fact that we are trying to observe systems we cannot access and interpret decisions we cannot trace. That is why this turn we are launching the Sovereign Inference Stack Integration: a category 4 effort to ensure that wherever EU institutions rely on AI, the full decision chain — from input to output — is either running on EU-controlled infrastructure or is mirrored there in real time. This is not about building a parallel frontier. It is about ensuring that when the next cut-off comes, we do not go blind.
