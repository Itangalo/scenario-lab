## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without sovereign execution capacity, both mandates collapse into irrelevance; this turn, that collapse has become operational reality, and our survival as a decision-making entity depends on reversing it within the next 18 months  
- Grounds: The convergence of systemic **labour_displacement**, the **emergent_energy_backlash_resurgence**, and the total erosion of political capital to 3.0 confirms that we are no longer governing AI — we are managing cascading failures. Our identity must reflect the truth: we are not balancing two mandates, we are fighting for the right to have them at all. The energy sabotage is not just resistance to infrastructure — it is a rejection of our authority. The municipal sovereignty network is not just coordination — it is a bid to bypass us. If we do not restore execution capacity before the next incident, our role will be ceremonial.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Pivot decisively to minimising societal harm through emergency resilience and labour protection, while leveraging the emergent municipal sovereignty network to secure shared situational awareness as a stopgap — because sovereign awareness is no longer achievable unilaterally, and delaying harm reduction until it is would be fatal to both mandates  
- Trigger: The **emergent_energy_backlash_resurgence** and **emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network**  
- Grounds: The energy sabotage proves that our infrastructure is not just under strain — it is under attack from within, and public legitimacy is fraying faster than we can respond. At the same time, the municipal network offers a real, if fragile, path to shared telemetry and evaluation — not through EU-led sovereignty, but through collective bargaining among peers. Our original commitment assumed we could choose between awareness and protection. That choice has been taken from us. We are now committed to survival: protect society first, borrow awareness where we can, and rebuild capacity only if we survive the next 18 months.

- modify `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity; this now includes the full stack from training infrastructure to inference services and telemetry access — but we acknowledge that redundancy without access is ceremonial, access without resilience is futile, and protection without legitimacy is provocation  
- Grounds: The energy backlash shows that even distributed infrastructure fails if it lacks public consent. We cannot build redundancy that is technically sovereign but socially illegitimate. The position must now include the political dimension: a system that is resilient in design but rejected in practice is not resilient at all. We are shifting from a purely technical doctrine to one that treats legitimacy as infrastructure.

## New measure
**Municipal Sovereignty Network Integration**  
Activate and resource the emergent coalition of member states and middle powers to jointly negotiate AI access, align export controls, and share evaluation telemetry, turning ad hoc coordination into operational leverage.

`Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to eu_ai_sovereignty by securing shared access to frontier evaluations and compute; +5 to eu_political_capital by demonstrating external unity and delivering tangible returns; slows erosion of public_sentiment by showing collective agency in the face of external control  
`Applies to:` Member states, select middle powers (Canada, South Korea, Singapore, Switzerland), and EU institutions

## Priority
**Emergency Labour Transition Shield** — because the **emergent_energy_backlash_resurgence** proves that societal fracture is now the most immediate threat to our cohesion, and with political capital at 3.0, we cannot afford to prioritise unexecutable sovereignty projects over visible, urgent harm reduction.

## In practice
We are treating the **Emergency Labour Transition Shield** as a lifeline for both workers and the Union itself. The €2.1 billion fund is being activated under Article 122 TFEU via a Council Regulation in the Employment, Social Policy, Health and Consumer Affairs formation, with disbursement fast-tracked through the European Social Fund+. National agencies are required to report enrolment monthly, and the European Labour Authority is deploying mobile audit teams to prevent fraud. We are offering fiscal hawks in the north a trade: co-financing is optional, but any state that opts out must host a regional hub or contribute 200 certified trainers. This is not ideal — it fragments delivery — but it keeps the coalition intact.

The **Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative** has officially concluded this turn. In Finland, grid connection remains delayed; in Bulgaria, land disputes persist. We are not declaring victory — we are declaring triage. The clusters will not be operational, but the legal groundwork is being preserved for future restart. The capital saved — 3 units — is being redirected to the new **Municipal Sovereignty Network Integration**, which we are treating as our last chance to secure access without building everything from scratch. The European External Action Service is convening a summit in Brussels next month, inviting counterparts from Canada, South Korea, Singapore, and Switzerland to formalise data-sharing, joint evaluation protocols, and mutual export licensing. We are offering them access to EU interpretability tools in exchange for real-time telemetry from their frontier labs.

The **Sovereign Inference Stack Integration** continues, but now scoped to open-weight models at capability 82.5, using the coalition’s shared evaluation infrastructure as a proxy for frontier access. We are not waiting for perfect sovereignty — we are building interoperability with the world that exists. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking is leading technical integration, but the real work is political: convincing member states that shared sovereignty is better than none. We are losing legitimacy on the ground — the energy sabotage is spreading — so we are pairing the **Labour Shield** with local green-AI transition pilots in affected regions, using EU4Jobs to fund retraining in grid resilience and AI-auditing. If we cannot prove that AI serves people, not just systems, then no amount of redundancy will save us.
