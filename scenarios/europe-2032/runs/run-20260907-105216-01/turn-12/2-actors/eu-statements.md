# Statements: The European Union (turn 12)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience — but only if it can be operationalised within existing political capital constraints; otherwise, pivot to minimising societal harm through emergency resilience and labour protection
- `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity; this now includes the full stack from training infrastructure to inference services and telemetry access — but we acknowledge that redundancy without access is ceremonial, and access without resilience is futile

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without sovereign execution capacity, both mandates collapse into irrelevance; this turn, that collapse has become operational reality, and our survival as a decision-making entity depends on reversing it within the next 18 months
  - Grounds: The convergence of **labour_displacement** at systemic scale, **bio_uplift_findings** as a credible precursor to catastrophic misuse, and the total erosion of political capital to 1.0 confirms that we are no longer governing AI — we are managing our own obsolescence. The identity statement must reflect the urgency: this is not a long-term strategic shift but an existential race. If we do not restore execution capacity before the next bio or cyber incident, our mandates will be decided by others.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience — but only if it can be operationalised within existing political capital constraints; otherwise, pivot to minimising societal harm through emergency resilience and labour protection
  - Trigger: The **labour_displacement** event and **middle_power_coalition** development
  - Grounds: The coalition provides a lifeline: for the first time, we can secure access not through unilateral sovereignty (which we cannot afford) but through collective leverage. This changes the calculus. Our commitment was to awareness, not to a specific path. With political capital at 1.0, continuing to fund unexecutable sovereignty measures would be ritual, not strategy. We are now committed to achieving awareness *if possible*, but our binding obligation is to prevent collapse — and that may require reallocating from observation to protection.
  - Evidence quoted: **labour_displacement:** Measurable job losses attributed to AI in named sectors, with the graduate market worst hit: entry-level positions in law, accountancy, software, customer operations and administration are not replaced. The numbers are argued about; the absence of hiring is not.

**middle_power_coalition:** A coordination framework among the Union and other middle powers holding pieces of the AI supply chain — export-licence alignment, joint bargaining over compute access, shared evaluation capacity. Nobody cedes sovereignty to it, but together its members can withhold things even the great powers need. It counts as securing access on the terms of metric rule 5, and moves `eu_political_capital` on the terms of metric rule 6.
  - Reason: The labour_displacement event directly challenges the societal stability the EU must uphold, and the coalition alters the feasibility of achieving situational awareness without unilateral sovereignty, changing the cost and path of the commitment.
- **modify** `no_single_point_of_failure` — applied
  - Tier: position
  - Text: No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity; this now includes the full stack from training infrastructure to inference services and telemetry access — but we acknowledge that redundancy without access is ceremonial, and access without resilience is futile
  - Grounds: The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative remains years from operation, and the Sovereign Inference Stack cannot function without the data it is meant to monitor. The position must reflect reality: we are not eliminating single points of failure — we are trying to survive them. The principle holds, but its application must shift from infrastructure-first to risk-first: where failure is imminent, we protect; where it is distant, we build.
