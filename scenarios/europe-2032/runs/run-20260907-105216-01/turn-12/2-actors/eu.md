## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without sovereign execution capacity, both mandates collapse into irrelevance; this turn, that collapse has become operational reality, and our survival as a decision-making entity depends on reversing it within the next 18 months  
- Grounds: The convergence of **labour_displacement** at systemic scale, **bio_uplift_findings** as a credible precursor to catastrophic misuse, and the total erosion of political capital to 1.0 confirms that we are no longer governing AI — we are managing our own obsolescence. The identity statement must reflect the urgency: this is not a long-term strategic shift but an existential race. If we do not restore execution capacity before the next bio or cyber incident, our mandates will be decided by others.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience — but only if it can be operationalised within existing political capital constraints; otherwise, pivot to minimising societal harm through emergency resilience and labour protection  
- Trigger: The **labour_displacement** event and **middle_power_coalition** development  
- Grounds: The coalition provides a lifeline: for the first time, we can secure access not through unilateral sovereignty (which we cannot afford) but through collective leverage. This changes the calculus. Our commitment was to awareness, not to a specific path. With political capital at 1.0, continuing to fund unexecutable sovereignty measures would be ritual, not strategy. We are now committed to achieving awareness *if possible*, but our binding obligation is to prevent collapse — and that may require reallocating from observation to protection.

- modify `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency—technical, infrastructural, or informational—will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity; this now includes the full stack from training infrastructure to inference services and telemetry access — but we acknowledge that redundancy without access is ceremonial, and access without resilience is futile  
- Grounds: The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative remains years from operation, and the Sovereign Inference Stack cannot function without the data it is meant to monitor. The position must reflect reality: we are not eliminating single points of failure — we are trying to survive them. The principle holds, but its application must shift from infrastructure-first to risk-first: where failure is imminent, we protect; where it is distant, we build.

## New measure
**Emergency Labour Transition Shield**  
Provide immediate income support and retraining pathways for workers displaced by AI in high-impact sectors, starting with graduates entering collapsed job markets.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to public_sentiment by demonstrating tangible support for displaced workers; +5 to resilience by reducing social shock from job losses; slows erosion of eu_political_capital by addressing visible harm  
`Applies to:` Member states, with implementation through national employment agencies and EU4Jobs funding

## Priority
**Emergency Labour Transition Shield** — because the **labour_displacement** event has made clear that societal fracture is now the most immediate threat to our cohesion and legitimacy, and with political capital at 1.0, we cannot afford to prioritise unexecutable sovereignty projects over visible, urgent harm reduction.

## In practice
We are treating the collapse of the graduate labour market as a systemic emergency. The **Emergency Labour Transition Shield** will activate immediately through a Council Regulation under Article 122 TFEU, authorising a €2.1 billion fund to co-finance national retraining and wage insurance schemes for workers displaced by AI in law, accountancy, software, and administration. Member states will receive matching grants for every displaced worker enrolled in accredited reskilling programmes, with priority for digital, green, and care economy transitions. The European Labour Authority will coordinate eligibility standards, and the European Social Fund+ will fast-track disbursement.

This is not a long-term solution — it is triage. We are leveraging the **middle_power_coalition** to secure access to evaluation tools and compute that may yet enable the Sovereign Inference Stack, but we cannot wait for that. The Shield buys time and legitimacy. We are offering bilateral side-deals to reluctant member states: those resisting co-financing will instead contribute skilled trainers or host regional upskilling hubs. Resistance from fiscal hawks is being met with a simple argument: if we do not stabilise the social base now, there will be no political capital left to spend on anything.

Meanwhile, the Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative continues in Finland and Bulgaria, but with scaled expectations. We are no longer promising operational clusters by turn 13 — that was unrealistic. Instead, we are focusing on securing the legal and grid access that will allow construction to accelerate *if* capital recovers. The Sovereign Inference Stack Integration is being re-scoped to work first with open-weight models at capability 79.5, using the middle-power coalition’s shared evaluation infrastructure as a proxy for frontier access. We are not building sovereignty from scratch — we are borrowing it, collectively, to survive long enough to build it alone.
