### The Capital Retreat

Global AI investment collapsed in early 2027, as overleveraged startups folded and U.S. hyperscalers froze expansion. Valuations reset by more than half, and several major training projects were quietly cancelled. This retrenchment slowed the pace of frontier advancement — but only temporarily. The underlying momentum in capability persisted, driven by compressed release cycles and algorithmic refinements, pushing `ai_capability` to 56.5.

### Sovereignty Through Crisis

The EU moved to capitalize on the shifting landscape. Brussels advanced a proposal to redirect €8bn from the resilience fund toward a new initiative: targeted support for sovereign AI compute startups. The goal was to anchor critical development capacity within EU jurisdiction, offering funding and infrastructure in exchange for binding safety and transparency commitments. This proposal — formally named Reallocate Resilience Fund to Sovereign Compute Startups — became the EU’s priority measure this turn.

However, implementation has not yet begun. Legal and budgetary challenges emerged immediately, particularly around the repurposing of earmarked resilience funds. Member states including Poland and Hungary challenged the move, arguing it bypassed agreed spending priorities and undermined ongoing cyber-hardening efforts. Pilot red-teaming exercises in Belgium and Denmark continued, but broader rollout in central and eastern Europe stalled amid the dispute.

A separate idea — establishing a public evaluation unit under CNECT to pre-clear open-weight models above 45.0 — was discussed in technical working groups but did not advance beyond the conceptual stage. No formal decision was made, and no resources were allocated. The idea remains under review for future consideration.

### Safety and Sentiment Under Pressure

Despite the strategic pivot, no new safety assurance landed on deployed systems. The gap between capability and control widened, and with the `ai_investment_collapse` underscoring systemic fragility, `ai_safety` fell to 31.0. `resilience` also declined, as the repurposing debate slowed hardening efforts and exposed implementation gaps.

Public sentiment turned sour. Media coverage focused on diverted security spending and perceived elite technocracy, dragging `public_sentiment` down to 39.0. The proposed measure, while large and symbolically significant, strained cohesion and political capital. `eu_political_capital` dropped to 32.0 as internal divisions mounted.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` rose to 23.0, reflecting early momentum in the initiative and the potential to lock in strategic capacity during a global downturn. However, `openweight_capability` remained unchanged at 41.0, as the retreat in private investment and lack of new open releases during the downturn prevented gains in that domain. The narrative of accumulation through open collaboration weakened, and no new open-weight breakthroughs were observed.
