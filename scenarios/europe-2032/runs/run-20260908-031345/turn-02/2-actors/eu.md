## In practice  
We are acting on three fronts, but the ground has shifted. The *Hardened Critical Infrastructure Zones* directive continues its legislative crawl through national transposition processes. ENISA has issued the first audit templates, and pilot red-teaming exercises have begun in Belgium and Denmark. But resistance in Poland and Hungary remains firm — they see top-down mandates as infringement, not protection. We are responding with differentiated implementation: offering national teams co-design rights over fallback protocols in exchange for faster adoption. The €12bn fund is being split — half released only after zero-trust certification — to maintain pressure without provoking defiance. Still, progress is halved where it matters most.

The *InvestAI Gigafactories* programme is now in advanced negotiation with ASML, TSMC, and Intel on packaging co-location. Legal anchoring clauses are being tested against WTO rules in a quiet opinion process led by DG Trade. Site selection favours Finland and Spain, but Romania’s candidacy gains weight as political cover for eastern member states otherwise alienated by centralisation. Meanwhile, the *Tech sovereignty package* faces renewed scrutiny in the Council’s Environment formation, where green groups argue that fast-tracking permits risks bypassing ecological assessments. Our compromise — tying each zone to 150% renewable procurement and local upskilling — holds France and Germany, but Italy demands a site or will block.

Now, the collapse in private AI investment changes everything. Valuations have reset; startups are folding, talent is flooding the market, and U.S. hyperscalers are pausing expansion. What was once unattainable — attracting world-class teams to build under EU jurisdiction — is suddenly possible. We cannot match Silicon Valley’s peak pay, but we can offer stability, purpose, and access to Europe’s compute backbone. So we are redirecting €8bn from the resilience fund — not cutting hardening, but compressing its timeline and focusing on highest-risk nodes — to launch a targeted acquisition-and-anchoring initiative. It will fund five to seven EU-rooted labs, selected for technical ambition and control-aware design, with grants that convert to equity if safety conditions are breached. Crucially, any open-weight release above 45.0 must be pre-cleared by a new public evaluation unit under CNECT. This turns a moment of global retrenchment into a leap for eu_ai_sovereignty — and ensures that when the next wave rises, Europe is not just resilient, but generative.
