## New measure  
**Mandate Binding Pre-Release Security Certification for Open-Weight Models**  
Require all open-weight models above capability 45.0 to obtain a verifiable security certificate from the EU Public Evaluation Unit before legal distribution within or into EU jurisdiction, with penalties for non-compliance including blocking of hosting, bandwidth throttling, and liability for damages.

## Priority  
**Mandate Binding Pre-Release Security Certification for Open-Weight Models** — because the openweight frontier is now functionally within reach of autonomous replication and offensive cyber use, and the US election consolidation means we can no longer rely on access to closed systems — our only remaining leverage is jurisdictional control over what is released, and this measure turns our evaluation capacity into an enforceable gate.

## In practice  
We are converting the Public Evaluation Unit from a monitoring body into an enforcement node. With the Unit now operational — though minimally staffed — we are drafting a delegated act under Article 16 of the AI Act to establish mandatory certification for open-weight models above 45.0, using the Unit as the certifying authority. This is not a soft standard but a hard stop: no certificate, no distribution. We are working with Germany, France, and the Netherlands to ensure immediate technical enforcement capacity — coordinating with national CERTs to block uncertified model hosting and with ISPs to throttle bandwidth for known distribution channels. The legal basis is tight, and we expect a challenge, but we are prepared to defend it as a public security imperative.

The Hardened Critical Infrastructure Zones directive has formally concluded this turn. While only 40% of member states implemented red-teaming on schedule, the linkage to cohesion funding created enough pressure to establish baseline protocols, and we are now transitioning to a monitoring phase under ENISA. The finished measure leaves behind a binding framework, but its uneven rollout shows the limits of top-down coercion without buy-in — a lesson we are applying to the new certification regime by building in fast-track pathways for academic and non-commercial releases.

The Reallocate Resilience Fund remains in legal limbo, but we are using the ring-fenced €4bn to fund pilot audits of three sovereign startups under ENISA supervision, creating a paper trail to demonstrate accountability to the ECJ. Meanwhile, we are detaching the Sardinia green-compute project from the stalled Tech sovereignty package and re-proposing it as a cohesion-funded green transition pilot, aiming to break the Council deadlock by offering Poland a lead role in a parallel Central European node. This is not the sovereignty we wanted, but it is the ground we can still hold.
