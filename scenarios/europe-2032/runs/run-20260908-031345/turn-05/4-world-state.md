### The Pivot to Control

With global AI capability advancing to 65.0, driven by algorithmic efficiency and tighter training loops, the frontier edges into sustained agent-like behaviour—systems now autonomously debug code, coordinate simple cyber operations, and simulate infrastructure attacks over multi-hour horizons. The EU, facing a hardening US stance after the election of a consolidation-aligned administration, shifts strategy: unable to build sovereign compute at scale, it turns to jurisdictional leverage. The newly operational Public Evaluation Unit, though still under-resourced, is empowered via a delegated act under the AI Act to mandate pre-release certification for open-weight models above 45.0 capability. Distribution within the EU now requires a verifiable security certificate, enforced through ISP coordination and hosting blocks.

The move is met with resistance. Developers warn of fragmentation, and legal challenges loom, but early enforcement actions—throttling a high-risk model linked to automated phishing campaigns—signal resolve. The measure is small but direct, responding to the openweight surge and rising misuse risks.

### Hardened, But Not Whole

The **Hardened Critical Infrastructure Zones** directive formally concludes, achieving a +3 resilience boost. Despite patchy red-teaming adoption, the cohesion fund linkage ensured baseline protocols were adopted across essential services. ENISA now leads monitoring, though disparities persist between northern and southern states.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Holds

Sovereignty drops to 18.0 as capability growth outpaces access security, and the US election consolidates a tiered access regime—Europe is now a client, not a peer. The Reallocate Resilience Fund remains blocked, and the Sardinia pilot, though rebranded, lacks momentum. Political capital stabilizes at 21.0, aided by finishing two measures and avoiding overreach. Public sentiment rises slightly to 35.0, as the certification regime is seen as a tangible response to uncontrolled AI risks.
