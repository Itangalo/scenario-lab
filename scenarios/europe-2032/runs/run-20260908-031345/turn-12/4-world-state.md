### Capability Creeps Forward

Global AI capability advanced by +2.5 this turn, reaching 83.5, as frontier labs deepened integration of autonomous reasoning into cyber operations and materials discovery. No safety breakthroughs occurred, and with no new governance alignment, `ai_safety` remained at 15.0 — systems grow more opaque, not less.

### The Union’s Pivot Under Pressure

The EU formally concluded its Emergency Activation of the AI Incident Response Framework. While joint drills in Lyon and Naples improved telemetry sharing, the lack of full legal ratification across member states limited operational reach. Resilience held at 40.0, absorbing no further degradation but gaining no new strength.

Simultaneously, the EU suspended physical rollout of the Sovereign Compute Corridors amid ongoing EFTA Court challenges and member-state resistance. In response, Brussels launched **Interoperable National Compute Hubs via Enhanced Cooperation**, a small category 4 measure under Article 329 TFEU, aiming to build functional unity without requiring political unanimity. Finland, France, and Italy committed immediately; Benelux states entered technical negotiations. Germany and the Netherlands agreed to lead standardization efforts under CEN-CENELEC, converting their governance demands into formal leadership.

However, Poland declined to join, citing unresolved energy sovereignty concerns, and Hungary dismissed the initiative as “fragmentation with funding.” The shift avoided a constitutional crisis but delivered no immediate infrastructure. Public sentiment remained stagnant at 20.0, as strikes by displaced tech and legal workers disrupted AI pilots in Paris, Barcelona, and Stockholm, reinforcing perceptions of AI as a destabilizing force.

### Political Capital Erodes

The portfolio now carries two large measures and one small, with the new hub initiative named priority. Despite the shift to a legally safer path, political capital fell sharply, as cohesion costs mounted and no delivery milestone was reached.
