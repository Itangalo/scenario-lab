## In practice
We are treating the current conjuncture as a hinge: the plateau in capability prices may signal a temporary stall, but the safety breakthrough—real, deployable, and rapidly adopted—offers a rare alignment between security and scalability. Still, we cannot bank on either lasting. The Middle-Power Coalition Framework is now operationalised through a joint declaration under the Foreign Affairs Council, with legal anchoring in Article 21 of the Treaty on European Union (strategic autonomy) and Article 173 TFEU (industrial policy). The Commission’s Trade and Internal Market DGs are finalising mutual recognition protocols: if Sweden’s AI Safety Institute certifies a model above 50.0 as compliant with shared baselines, it clears in Lyon and Helsinki alike. In return, we are offering access to our chokepoints—ASML’s non-US-controlled lithography nodes, our rare earth refining in Estonia, and our high-mobility talent corridors under Erasmus+. France’s condition—AI Act compliance as a floor—has been accepted reciprocally; no member state may exempt its national champions from audit. Germany and the Netherlands have signed on, seeing this as essential to semiconductor resilience. Poland remains the pivot: we’ve offered a regional compute node in Wrocław and fast-tracked €1.2bn in cohesion-linked green-compute funding, but only if Warsaw drops its resistance to the broader green transition package. The cost in political capital is steep—this is the largest coordination lift we’ve attempted—but the alternative is fragmentation just as the world demands cohesion.
