## New measure
**Fund and operationalise the emergent data sovereignty network as a complementary EU data integrity layer**

This measure integrates the grassroots movement into the Union’s governance architecture by providing technical and financial support to decentralised data nodes that audit AI data flows, ensuring transparency and local control while maintaining interoperability with EU-wide safety frameworks.

## Priority
**Launch Interoperable National Compute Hubs via Enhanced Cooperation** — because while the new data sovereignty initiative responds to civic energy, the compute fragmentation crisis strikes at the Union’s capacity to act collectively, and only a functioning network of hubs can preserve that capacity in the absence of unanimity.

## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: one of institutional adaptation, the other of civic absorption. On the first, we are advancing the Interoperable National Compute Hubs through the Enhanced Cooperation framework under Article 329 TFEU, with Germany and the Netherlands now formally leading standardisation via CEN-CENELEC. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking is deploying €4.2 billion in co-financing, tied to compliance with EU safety interfaces and reciprocal access agreements. For France, Italy, and Finland, we are fast-tracking semiconductor supply allocations; for Poland, we are linking hub participation to TEN-T rail-grid interconnection funding, turning energy sovereignty concerns into a bridge rather than a barrier.

On the second front, we are recognising that legitimacy is no longer granted from the top down. The emergent data sovereignty revolt is not an insurgency — it is a distress signal. We are therefore redirecting €900 million from the Digital Europe Programme to fund and integrate decentralised data nodes into a new EU Data Integrity Layer, governed jointly by EDPB and ENISA. These nodes will use open-weight models to audit data provenance and model inputs, feeding into the same telemetry pipelines as certified providers. This is not surrender to fragmentation — it is a bid to make resilience *participatory*. We are offering civil society groups technical support, legal recognition, and access to EU-wide incident response protocols, turning resistance into infrastructure. If we cannot command unity, we will cultivate interdependence.
