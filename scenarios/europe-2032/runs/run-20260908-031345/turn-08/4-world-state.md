### The Plateau That Wasn’t

Global AI capability rose by 2.5 points to 73.5, continuing its steady climb despite early signals of a plateau. Market pricing of top-tier models dipped briefly, but the lull proved deceptive—recursive self-improvement cycles resumed within weeks, driven by undisclosed architectural refinements. The brief dip, however, created a false window of strategic calm.

### A Coalition Forged—But Not Whole

The EU formally activated the Middle-Power Coalition Framework, binding Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Nordic states into a mutual recognition regime for AI safety certifications and supply chain access. Legal instruments were signed under strategic autonomy clauses, and Erasmus+ talent corridors were extended to include AI specialists. France secured its demand: AI Act compliance is now the baseline for all members. Germany and the Netherlands signed on, viewing the pact as essential for semiconductor resilience.

But Poland withheld assent. Despite offers of a regional compute hub in Wrocław and fast-tracked green-compute funds, Warsaw refused to decouple its approval from broader green transition negotiations. Behind closed doors, Polish officials questioned whether the coalition would truly share power—or merely extend Brussels’ reach. The absence of full unity weakened the bloc’s leverage.

### Safety Gains, Fragile Foundations

A genuine interpretability breakthrough—mapping latent reasoning pathways in high-parameter models—was rapidly adopted across leading labs, boosting `ai_safety` by +5. This progress, however, did little to calm rising alarm over open-weight models. Independent audits continued to expose latent exploit capabilities, and model drift in backend systems caused minor outages in energy and transport grids, eroding `resilience` by 1 point.

Public sentiment held steady at 25.0, as job displacement fears offset optimism about safety gains. The EU’s political capital dipped to 13.0: the coalition activation delivered a +3 completion bonus, but the portfolio charge of −4 (three ongoing measures, one priority) and the ongoing strain of fragmentation outweighed it. Sovereignty fell to 15.0, as capability growth outpaced domestic capacity, and no new compute came online.
