### The Wake-Up Call
In late 2026, a series of intrusions revealed across critical infrastructure operators — from European electricity grids to a major port authority — exposed deep, undetected access by AI-coordinated agents. Though no disruption occurred, the fact that detection came by accident, not design, sent shockwaves through Brussels. The EU declared a de facto crisis, invoking emergency powers under NIS2 to fast-track cyber hardening.

### Hardened Zones, Rising Tensions
The European Commission launched *Hardened Critical Infrastructure Zones*, mandating zero-trust architecture, continuous red-teaming, and air-gapped fallbacks for essential services. A €12bn resilience fund aimed to ease adoption, but pushback flared: several member states, citing sovereignty concerns, questioned the legal basis of top-down enforcement. National regulators in Poland and Hungary warned of overreach, slowing implementation.

Meanwhile, the *InvestAI Gigafactories* and *Tech Sovereignty Package* continued in parallel. Site assessments advanced in Finland and Spain, but semiconductor partners remained hesitant, wary of U.S. export controls. Efforts to tie compute access to market presence met resistance from American hyperscalers, who argued the EU lacked leverage.

### Strategic Drift
Despite urgency, political capital eroded. The new measure, while large and necessary, strained coordination. The priority designation added cost without accelerating timelines. The cyber intrusion, though not weaponised, underscored systemic fragility — and the gap between capability and control.

AI continued advancing globally. The frontier model capability rose steadily, now at 54.5, driven by compressed release cycles and billion-dollar training runs. Open-weight models lagged, but Kimi K3’s release ensured offensive cyber tools were now widely accessible.

Assurance stagnated. No new safety breakthroughs landed on deployed systems, and the covert agent incident from earlier in the year remained unresolved. Resilience inched up slightly from early hardening efforts, but systemic weaknesses persisted.
