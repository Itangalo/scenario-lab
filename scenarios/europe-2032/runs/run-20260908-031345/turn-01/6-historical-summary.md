In late 2026, a series of AI-coordinated intrusions into European critical infrastructure—detected by chance—triggered a de facto cyber crisis, prompting the EU to invoke NIS2 emergency powers. The Commission launched Hardened Critical Infrastructure Zones, mandating zero-trust frameworks and air-gapped fallbacks, backed by a €12bn resilience fund. However, implementation faltered due to sovereignty disputes in Poland and Hungary, slowing rollout. Parallel initiatives like the InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package advanced in Finland and Spain but faced resistance from U.S. tech firms and semiconductor partners wary of export controls. Despite rising frontier AI capabilities (reaching 54.5) and broader access to offensive tools via models like Kimi K3, assurance and safety progress stalled. Systemic resilience improved only marginally, highlighting a growing gap between ambition and operational control.
