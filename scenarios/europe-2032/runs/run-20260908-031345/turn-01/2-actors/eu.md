## New measure
Hardened Critical Infrastructure Zones  
A binding directive requiring cyber hardening and continuous red-teaming of essential services, with mandatory fallback protocols.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 5  
Targeted effect: resilience +15, ai_safety +5  
Applies to: particular member states (those hosting critical energy, water and port infrastructure)

## Priority
Hardened Critical Infrastructure Zones — because the cyber_test_shot proves existing defences are blind to advanced persistent threats, and resilience is now a precondition of sovereignty.

## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: building our own capacity and hardening what we already depend on. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme is moving through the European Investment Bank and EIB Global, with site selection underway in Finland, Spain, and Romania. Legal anchoring clauses are being drafted to ensure that any system trained on these clusters falls under EU jurisdiction, even if operated by a foreign entity. We are negotiating with three semiconductor firms to co-locate advanced packaging facilities near the sites, leveraging ASML’s position to extract reciprocal access guarantees. Resistance from member states over subsidy concentration is being managed through side commitments on regional development funds.

The Tech sovereignty package is advancing through the Council’s Competitiveness formation, where some northern members are pushing back against accelerated permitting as environmental deregulation in disguise. We are countering by tying each fast-track zone to a binding green-energy procurement clause and a local skills academy, funded from the same envelope. Early signals suggest Germany and France will support it if Italy secures one of the five sites. Meanwhile, engagement with hyperscalers has shifted from persuasion to leverage: we are making clear that market access at scale requires compute commitments that cannot be remotely disabled.

This turn, however, is defined by exposure. The cyber_test_shot was not an attack but a warning: the agents were not weaponised, yet they had deep, undetected access across systems the Union relies on. We cannot wait for proof of intent. Under Article 12 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU and the newly invoked crisis clause in the NIS2 Directive, we are launching Hardened Critical Infrastructure Zones. This will bind operators of essential services in energy, water and ports to implement zero-trust architectures, continuous adversarial simulation, and air-gapped fallbacks for control systems. ENISA will lead audits every six months, and non-compliant operators lose liability protection. Some national regulators see this as overreach, so we are pairing it with a €12bn resilience fund from the Multiannual Financial Framework, conditioned on adoption. Without hardening, sovereignty is an illusion — and without resilience, even the most capable AI cannot save us when the lights go out.
