### The Acceleration

AI capability continued its steady upward trajectory, reaching 71.0 as recursive self-improvement incrementally accelerated development cycles. This progress, while significant, unfolded at a pace consistent with prior trends—no sudden leaps or unannounced shifts occurred. Nevertheless, interpretability continued to degrade under the strain of increasingly complex architectures, with monitoring tools struggling to keep pace. Some labs reported partial blind spots in model reasoning, raising quiet concerns but not yet triggering systemic failure.

### Fractures in the Coalition

The EU’s effort to operationalise the Middle-Power Coalition Framework encountered institutional friction. Germany and the Netherlands advanced planning talks, but Poland withheld formal commitment, citing unresolved disputes over green funding and data governance. Behind closed doors, Warsaw sought stronger mutual access guarantees, wary of asymmetries in reliance. France maintained its insistence on strict AI Act compliance for all participants, a position that, while principled, slowed consensus.

Meanwhile, independent audits of open-weight models continued to reveal troubling dual-use potentials, including latent capabilities that could be repurposed for offensive functions. These findings fueled debate among regulators and civil society, with calls growing for binding oversight. The EU’s certification regime remained technically robust, but questions mounted about its enforceability at scale.

### Strained Foundations

Resilience dipped slightly as backend AI systems exhibited subtle model drift, leading to isolated outages in critical infrastructure. Investigations pointed to compounding edge-case failures rather than coordinated attacks, but response capacity was stretched. Public sentiment declined, driven by renewed fears of job displacement and eroding trust in AI-mediated services.

The EU’s political capital diminished under the weight of overlapping initiatives. The completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories marked a milestone, yet sovereignty gains were delayed—domestic frontier capacity remains offline, and dependence persists. Coordination efforts intensified, but tangible results lagged. The Middle-Power Coalition Framework remains in negotiation, its activation pending resolution of key disputes.
