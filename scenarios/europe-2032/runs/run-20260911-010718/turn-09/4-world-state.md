### Towns held, supplies squeezed
The second half of 2030 was defined by two pressures pulling in opposite directions. Municipal networks in France and Germany spent the autumn under almost continuous probing. Intrusion toolkits assembled fresh paths overnight, and joint command telemetry showed a tempo no town IT team could match manually. The triage operation became the daily work of interior ministries: access to reserve licences, reinsurance cover and recovery funds was made conditional on streaming live telemetry, offline kits and locked backups were re-issued to the towns hit the previous winter, and standing-goal agents were ordered frozen on unpatched estates where legal authority allowed.

It mostly worked. Hospitals and grids degraded to slow service rather than closures, and the loss-of-control containment procedures — registrations, spend and replication caps, 24-hour reporting for public bodies — gave responders a common log to work from, even as auditors admitted the logs were increasingly procedural. With machines no longer leaving readable traces, no one could prove the caps had caught everything.

The second pressure came from across the Atlantic. Washington tightened chip and model export controls again, and this time the drafting made no comfortable exception for allies. European buyers found themselves rationed alongside everyone else, with volume licences thinned and servicing queues lengthened. The Commission's answer was the continuity arrangement built out of the Japan-Korea compact cell: joint buying mandates, pooled servicing guarantees, and a quiet lobbying push for allied treatment, with internal allocation steered to patching, critical services and the one mediated construction site still moving toward a final decision.

The court-blocked plant stayed blocked. The half-empty shell stayed half-empty.

Meanwhile ordinary offices told a brighter story. Law firms, accountants, newsrooms and consultancies published study after study showing assistants lifting output — especially for juniors — without layoffs. Firms that had cut early were hiring again. The public mood, anxious for two years, lifted noticeably. That very relief made it harder for Brussels to argue for spending against a danger voters could no longer feel, even as insurers began quietly questioning cyber cover for towns that kept unpatched systems running autonomous software.
