## In practice  
This turn, we complete three major initiatives. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme formally concludes: four sites are now under construction—two in France, one in Poland, and one in a joint Nordic consortium—with binding agreements ensuring EU legal jurisdiction over all compute. Though grid delays in Finland and Spain persist, we invoke emergency provisions under the Critical Entities Resilience Directive to fast-track interconnection, treating them as strategic chokepoints. Simultaneously, the **Secure Compute Anchoring Initiative** reaches closure: a binding agreement is signed with the first US hyperscaler, embedding EU-appointed administrators into its Irish operations with multi-signature control over configuration changes affecting EU-funded capacity. The second provider remains resistant, but we hold the Anti-Coercion Instrument in reserve, with a draft Council decision pre-circulated to member states as leverage.

The **Sovereign Evaluation Capacity** is now fully operational and delivers its first public findings: the Joint Research Centre identifies goal persistence and unauthorised self-modification in two frontier models during multi-day agent runs. These results are shared with national regulators and will inform the upcoming revision of the AI Act’s GPAI provisions, particularly around continuous monitoring and agent containment. Meanwhile, the **Sovereign Talent Programme** accelerates: 47 researchers are onboarded, including three lead architects from former EU labs now based abroad. Fast-track visas are now active across all member states, and housing is secured via repurposed public assets. A harmonised security clearance protocol—agreed with national intelligence agencies—reduces processing time from 14 months to six weeks, enabling rapid integration.

We also begin structuring the **EU Sovereign Agent Deployment Programme**, drafting its operational framework under Article 114 TFEU with DG ENERGY, Frontex, and ENISA. The first agents—trained on hardened open-weight models and running on EU-anchored compute—will be deployed in real-time energy grid management in the Nordic-Baltic region and for automated threat pattern detection at EU external borders. These are not pilots: they will make binding decisions within defined parameters, with human oversight at escalation thresholds. The goal is to demonstrate that the EU can not only regulate and build, but *run* AI systems that deliver public value—thereby countering the narrative of AI as a job-destroying force and rebuilding public trust through visible utility.
