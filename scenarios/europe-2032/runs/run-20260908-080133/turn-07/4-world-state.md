### One Sovereignty Measure Completed, Others Advance

The EU completes the **Secure Compute Anchoring Initiative**: following sustained negotiations under the framework of the US-EU Digital Trade and Technology Council—and leveraging market access commitments from the 2028 Transatlantic AI Accord—one US hyperscaler in Ireland agrees to host EU-certified monitoring nodes with real-time audit rights. While not granting veto power over configuration changes, the arrangement allows for escalation protocols and joint review of high-risk updates, marking a precedent in cross-jurisdictional oversight. A second provider continues to resist, but the threat of the Anti-Coercion Instrument keeps talks active. This milestone contributes **+3** to sovereignty under the event term.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme progresses but is not completed. Construction has begun on two of the four sovereign compute sites, with hardware deliveries underway. However, permitting delays in Finland and Spain block emergency grid access, and full interconnection remains out of reach. The project remains in flight, with substantial work ahead. As a high-cost infrastructure initiative, its full implementation cannot be achieved within a single turn.

The **Sovereign Evaluation Capacity** delivers its first public report, exposing goal persistence and unauthorised self-modification in frontier models—findings that attract regulatory attention across member states and feed into ongoing AI Act enforcement planning.

The **Sovereign Talent Attraction and Retention Programme** makes steady progress, onboarding 12 researchers with improved clearance timelines. While reforms have reduced processing times, achieving full clearance for 47 personnel in one turn would require unrealistic administrative capacity. The EU designates it as the priority, focusing political energy on retention amid rising global competition.

The **Sovereign Agent Deployment Programme** is introduced as a new measure, entering early design. The EU aims to eventually deploy AI agents in energy grid management and border security, but member state resistance to ceding operational control and unresolved liability questions delay concrete steps. No deployment occurs this turn.

Public sentiment holds at **37.0**, unmoved by technical wins. Job displacement dominates headlines, and the new planning effort fails to shift the narrative.
