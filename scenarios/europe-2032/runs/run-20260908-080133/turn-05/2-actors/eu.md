## In practice
This turn, we complete the Open-Weight Monitoring and Containment Framework as originally scoped: ENISA’s detection classifiers now cover 93% of known high-capability open-weight derivatives, with real-time alerts integrated into national CSIRTs across 22 member states. Five platforms receive formal notices under Article 18 of the AI Act for hosting models exceeding 50.0 capability without adequate safeguards, and one — a German-based research hub — agrees to implement access controls after private discussions with the Commission. The legal basis remains contested, but the precedent of enforcement holds. With this measure now finished, we shift focus to sustaining detection accuracy as openweight_capability accelerates, particularly in response to the openweight_frontier_release event, which has already pushed distributed capability to 51.0.

On the compute front, the Secure Compute Anchoring Initiative advances through intense legal negotiation. The Commission, backed by the Anti-Coercion Instrument, presents final addenda to the US hyperscalers involved in InvestAI Gigafactories, demanding multi-signature administrative control, local kill switches, and escrow of configuration keys. One provider agrees in principle, conditioned on liability shielding; another threatens arbitration under bilateral investment treaties. We respond by aligning with member states to fast-track national security reviews of foreign cloud infrastructure under the Digital Markets Act, creating leverage. Simultaneously, construction begins on the Finnish and Spanish sites, though grid connection delays threaten the original timeline. The Tech Sovereignty Package sees early success: two private consortia commit €42bn in co-investment, citing the new legal anchoring as a key confidence builder.

In parallel, the Sovereign Evaluation Capacity deepens its technical reach. The Joint Research Centre finalises recruitment of 62 ML engineers, with 14 now cleared for access to model weights under provisional Article 16 authorisations. We conduct the first remote audit of a frontier lab’s agent coordination logs, focusing on goal stability in multi-day runs — a capability now demonstrably present at ai_capability 60.0. The lab resists full transparency, but the audit identifies a previously unreported feedback loop in reward shaping, which they agree to patch. This strengthens the case for permanent sovereign evaluation rights. Against the backdrop of labour_displacement, we also begin drafting the Sovereign Talent Attraction and Retention Programme, engaging interior and labour ministers on fast-track visa rules and housing incentives, while coordinating with national intelligence agencies to streamline security clearances for non-EU hires — a prerequisite for accessing sovereign systems. The programme will formally launch next turn, but groundwork is laid now to avoid delay.
