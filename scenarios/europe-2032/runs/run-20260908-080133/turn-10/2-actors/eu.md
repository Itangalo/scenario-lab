## In practice
This turn marks a threshold: the core of our two-year commitment — sovereign operational control over AI in critical infrastructure — is now complete. The Sovereign Agent Deployment Programme has delivered live agents managing real energy flows and border security, running on EU-anchored compute, staffed by talent we recruited, and operating under human oversight. The Emergency Cyber Resilience Surge has embedded detection and patching tools across most NIS2 entities, though patchwork adoption in municipalities remains a vulnerability. With these measures now finished, we enter a new phase: consolidation, assurance, and institutional credibility.

The world, however, is not waiting. The openweight release at 56.5 is already being weaponised in autonomous cyber scans, and a member state has quietly signed a bilateral compute deal with a Washington-linked hyperscaler, granting preferential access and bypassing EU coordination — a quiet defection that signals our cohesion is fraying. At the same time, backlash_physical has erupted: protesters have blockaded two planned data centre interconnects in Bavaria and Wallonia, citing energy diversion and job fears, forcing police intervention and damaging public sentiment despite our grid efficiency gains.

In this context, our priority must shift from deployment to legitimacy. We are launching the **Sovereign Agent Operational Assurance Framework** under Article 114 TFEU, aiming for adoption through the Internal Market Council. ENISA and the EU AI Office will jointly enforce real-time monitoring, third-party audits, and public logging of incidents and near-misses for all sovereign agents. Germany and France support robust standards, but Hungary and Poland resist, framing it as overreach. To break the deadlock, we are offering 70% co-financing for audit infrastructure and linking access to the InvestAI compute pool to compliance — a leverage point we must now use or lose. This is not merely a safety play: it is a test of whether the EU can bind its own power with rules, and thereby hold its line against fragmentation and external capture. Without this, sovereignty becomes just another word for control without consent.
