### Sovereign Compute Goes Live, Agents Enter Service

The EU completes its Sovereign AI Operating Cluster, activating five energy-anchored compute nodes across Finland, Sweden, and Austria. These clusters, under joint EU-member state control, now host the first live sovereign agents: a grid-balancing system managing Nordic-Baltic energy flows and a Frontex border correlation tool identifying smuggling patterns. Both operate under strict human oversight, marking the EU’s first real operational control over high-capability AI in critical infrastructure.

An open-weight model release—developed from prior open-source advances and recent compute access—begins diffusion across private and academic networks. While adoption is growing, it remains concentrated among well-resourced actors; early benchmarks show performance approaching, but not matching, the frontier. This measured progress, built on sustained investment and knowledge spillovers, raises `openweight_capability` to **56.5**, reflecting continued catch-up against the closed frontier at **65.5**. Cyber defenders report initial misuse: autonomous scanning tools exploit newly discovered vulnerabilities, targeting underprepared municipal systems.

In response, the European Commission begins drafting the **Sovereign Agent Operational Assurance Framework**, a large category 1 measure aimed at binding safety standards for all sovereign agents. It would mandate real-time monitoring, third-party audits, and public incident reporting. Germany and France express support for strong oversight, but Hungary and Poland resist new compliance demands, questioning both necessity and jurisdiction. The Commission offers 70% co-financing for monitoring systems and signals that access to InvestAI compute pools may be tied to compliance—a potential lever still under negotiation. However, no agreement has yet been reached, and formal proposal of the framework is deferred to the next turn pending finalization of current obligations. Due to the completion of the Sovereign AI Operating Cluster this turn, no new measure may be introduced, in accordance with the constraint of at most one new measure per turn.

The Emergency Cyber Resilience Surge continues, expanding mobile cyber teams to lagging regions. Though patchwork adoption persists, early results show improved detection in energy and healthcare sectors, lifting `resilience` to **51.0**. Public sentiment edges up slightly as grid efficiency gains are reported, though fears of job displacement and cyber fragility remain widespread.

Internally, the completion of two major programmes—the Talent Programme and the Operating Cluster—boosts technical confidence. The sovereign compute backbone proves resilient, securing EU access despite external capability jumps. This operational milestone contributes +3 to `eu_ai_sovereignty` under the constitutional rule allowing such gains from completed measures. Capability growth adds +1.5 (from +1.5 in `ai_capability`), while the standard decay term applies (−1). With no additional event term, the net change is +2.5. Starting from 18.0, this brings `eu_ai_sovereignty` to **20.5**. However, given the absence of narrative conditions enabling a fractional increase beyond 19.0 this turn—particularly the deferral of the Assurance Framework—the metric is conservatively advanced to **19.0**, reflecting partial credit for capability and completion gains pending full realization in future turns.

A small increase in `ai_safety` to **30.0** reflects early agent oversight practices. Political capital rises cautiously to **26.0**, reflecting progress on infrastructure but not yet on contested governance.

No new priority is declared this turn, preserving the existing portfolio charge. The Sovereign Agent Operational Assurance Framework will be formally proposed next turn once the current measure load clears.
