### The Weight of Control

The EU’s pivot from deployment to governance faced immediate headwinds. While sovereign AI agents now operate in energy and border systems, a major cyber incident—automated, model-generated, and spanning multiple public services—exposed systemic fragility. Defenders were overwhelmed; recovery took weeks. The attack did not breach core safeguards but revealed how far offensive capability has outpaced detection. Resilience absorbed some harm, but patchy municipal readiness allowed cascading failures, dragging `resilience` down.

Public sentiment worsened as images of disrupted hospitals and grid instability spread. The bilateral deal by a member state with a U.S. hyperscaler was rebranded domestically as a “stability pact,” deepening perceptions of EU disunity. Protests intensified in Bavaria and Wallonia, now targeting the perceived hypocrisy of demanding compliance while failing to demonstrate control. Trust eroded further: AI was seen not just as disruptive, but unaccountable.

The **Sovereign Agent Operational Assurance Framework**, already strained by Hungarian and Polish resistance, faltered under the pressure. The leverage play—tying InvestAI access to audit compliance—backfired when Budapest and Warsaw framed it as coercion, threatening legal action at the CJEU. No progress was made on implementing acts. Meanwhile, the new **Red-Teaming and Incident Simulation Directive** was formally launched, assigning ENISA to coordinate adversarial tests. Early cooperation from Nordic and Benelux red teams offered a sliver of momentum, but full operationalisation will take time.

Despite intent, no measure landed on shipped systems this turn. The cyber incident underscored the gap between capability and safety, and with no finished safeguards, `ai_safety` fell sharply. Political capital eroded under sustained portfolio costs and growing fragmentation. Sovereignty dipped again as dependence on contested external compute deepened, and no domestic capacity came online to offset it.

### A Union Under Strain

The crisis of legitimacy is no longer theoretical. The EU can deploy AI—but governing it, across borders and amid divergent interests, remains unproven. The tools for scrutiny exist in draft, but not in force. Public confidence continues to fall.
