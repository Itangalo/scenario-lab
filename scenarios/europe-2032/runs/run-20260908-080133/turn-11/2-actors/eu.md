## In practice
We are in the eye of a threshold moment. The Sovereign Agent Deployment Programme has delivered: AI agents are balancing the grid and screening border threats under human oversight, running on EU-anchored compute, staffed by talent we recruited. The Emergency Cyber Resilience Surge is embedded in most high-risk sectors, though municipal patchwork remains a liability. But delivery alone does not confer legitimacy — and legitimacy is now in freefall. Public sentiment has dropped to 28.0, dragged down by blockades in Bavaria and Wallonia, where protesters denounce AI as a machine that consumes energy and jobs while answering to no one. A member state’s bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler has deepened the perception that EU coordination is optional, and now a major cyber incident — automated, model-generated, and widespread — has landed with real damage. Defenders were behind, attribution is months away, and the public sees only chaos.

In this context, our actions must pivot from deployment to demonstration: we must show that we can govern what we have built. The Sovereign Agent Operational Assurance Framework is our central instrument, launched under Article 114 TFEU through the Internal Market Council. ENISA and the EU AI Office are drafting implementing acts for real-time monitoring and third-party audits, with public logging of incidents and near-misses. To break resistance from Hungary and Poland, we are offering 70% co-financing for audit infrastructure and conditioning access to the InvestAI compute pool on compliance — a leverage play that risks backlash but is necessary. Germany and France support strict oversight, but cohesion hinges on whether we can frame this not as centralisation but as a shared shield: sovereignty with accountability.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Red-Teaming and Incident Simulation Directive under the same legal umbrella, targeting energy, healthcare, and border systems. This will task independent red teams — drawn from Nordic-Benelux networks and vetted research institutions — with stress-testing sovereign agents under realistic failure scenarios, including model-generated exploits like those just seen. The results will feed into the Assurance Framework and be reported annually to the European Parliament. This is not merely technical: it is political. We must rebuild trust not by promising safety, but by proving it — through transparency, through independent scrutiny, and through the willingness to expose our own systems to adversarial challenge. The cost is high, but the alternative — a Union that can deploy AI but cannot answer for it — is no Union at all.
