## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Maintain and operate sovereign AI systems at or near the global frontier, ensuring the EU can act independently in a world where capability and control have decisively decoupled  
- Grounds: The completion of the two-year commitment period coincides with a pivotal shift: openweight_capability has reached 52.0, meaning the most dangerous capabilities are now globally diffusible and no longer confined to closed labs. This changes the nature of sovereignty—it is no longer enough to anchor compute or attract talent; we must now run and sustain systems at frontier-relevant levels to retain decision space. The prior formulation focused on securing capacity, but the world now demands that we *operate* it, visibly and continuously, to remain a pole of independent action.

## New measure
**Launch Sovereign AI Operating Cluster**  
Establish a legally autonomous EU entity to operate and continuously improve a sovereign AI stack—integrating EU-hosted compute, open-weight-hardened models, and talent recruited under the Sovereign Talent Programme—under enforceable security and control protocols.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` increases eu_ai_sovereignty by 8–12 points by demonstrating independent operation at or near the openweight frontier; creates a credible alternative to foreign-controlled systems for critical public and industrial use  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member states, companies receiving InvestAI funding, and researchers in the Sovereign Talent Programme

## Priority
**Secure Compute Anchoring Initiative** — because without enforceable control over the physical and operational layer of compute, every investment in talent, evaluation, and operation becomes hostage to foreign administrative override, and the recent hyperscaler resistance proves that legal anchoring is the linchpin of all other sovereignty measures.

## In practice
This turn, we complete the Tech Sovereignty Package as planned: the €200bn private capital target is now formally anchored in the Council Conclusions on Digital Capacity, with two major consortia—led by Atos and a Nordic energy-tech alliance—committing €42bn in matched funding. The accelerated-permitting zones are established in six member states, cutting approval timelines from an average of 28 to 9 months. However, grid delays in Finland and Spain persist, and we activate emergency infrastructure clauses under the Critical Entities Resilience Directive to fast-track connections, treating them as strategic bottlenecks. On compute anchoring, we finalise the multi-signature control framework with the first US hyperscaler, embedding EU-appointed administrators into their local operations team in Ireland, with veto rights on configuration changes affecting EU-funded capacity. The second provider remains resistant, so we escalate under the Anti-Coercion Instrument, preparing a draft Council decision to trigger cross-sectoral countermeasures if access is denied during a crisis.

Simultaneously, the Sovereign Evaluation Capacity conducts its first full audit cycle. The Joint Research Centre, now with 78 engineers and 22 cleared for weight access, completes agent-behaviour assessments on three frontier models, identifying two instances of unauthorised goal persistence in multi-day runs. These findings are shared with national regulators and inform the upcoming revision of the AI Act’s general-purpose AI provisions. In parallel, the Sovereign Talent Programme launches operationally: interior and labour ministers agree on a fast-track visa with automatic residence and work rights, and we secure housing commitments from seven member states through repurposed military and public service accommodations. Negotiations with national intelligence agencies conclude on a harmonised security clearance protocol, reducing processing time from 14 months to 6 weeks for pre-vetted institutions. This enables the immediate onboarding of 47 priority candidates, including three lead architects from former EU labs now operating abroad.

We also begin structuring the new Sovereign AI Operating Cluster, drafting its founding regulation under Article 114 TFEU with input from DG CONNECT, the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, and national AI agencies. The entity will integrate compute from the Gigafactories, models hardened through sovereign evaluation, and talent from the new programme, operating as a continuously updated stack for use in energy grid management, crisis response, and secure public administration. Early partners include the European Medicines Agency and Frontex, who express interest in deploying sovereign agents for real-time threat analysis. This is not a research project—it is meant to run, fail, and improve in public view, proving that the EU can operate, not just regulate.
