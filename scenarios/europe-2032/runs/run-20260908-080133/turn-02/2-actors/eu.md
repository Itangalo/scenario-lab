## New measure  
**Open-Weight Monitoring and Containment Framework**

Establish a real-time monitoring system for open-weight model deployments that pose systemic risk, using automated detection of high-capability derivatives and legal authority to compel takedown or modification of distributed models that exceed safety thresholds.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` slows growth in openweight_capability by 1–3 points per turn, increases ai_safety by forcing accountability onto open-source developers and distributors, and signals EU capacity to enforce boundaries even in uncontrolled domains  
`Applies to:` developers and distributors of open-weight models within EU jurisdiction, and platforms hosting model repositories (e.g., Hugging Face, GitHub EU instances)

## Priority  
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators** — because the intrusion pattern revealed a critical gap in our ability to detect advanced persistent threats, and without real-time visibility into attacks, no amount of containment or compute sovereignty can protect our essential services or maintain public trust during an incident.

## In practice  
This turn, we advance the Cyber Shield by finalising the implementing acts under Article 12a of the NIS2 Directive, mandating AI-augmented intrusion detection systems across energy, water, transport, and digital infrastructure providers. The Joint Threat Analysis Cell in Luxembourg begins staffing with seconded analysts from national CERTs and ENISA, while we secure provisional agreement from COREPER II on telemetry sharing — conditional on the 70% co-financing from the Digital Europe Programme and the exclusion of live red-teaming authority. We accept the compromise on oversight, knowing that even partial integration now builds the foundation for deeper integration later, when operational success strengthens our hand.

Simultaneously, we accelerate the InvestAI Gigafactories by convening the site selection council and initiating environmental impact assessments under fast-track procedures via the TEN-E framework. We assign Vice-President Vestager to mediate local opposition, linking project approval to binding regional development packages — including water recycling infrastructure and job guarantees — to secure social licence. The Tech sovereignty package advances in trilogue, where we trade guaranteed access nodes in future data centres to smaller member states in exchange for expediting the accelerated-permitting zones legislation, aware that without it, even funded compute projects will stall in national bureaucracies.

Externally, the **export_control_escalation** moment arrives: we choose to align with allied volume licensing, not universal rationing. We condition continued access for third-country hyperscalers on binding commitments to locate and anchor model training within EU jurisdiction, using the Anti-Coercion Instrument as leverage. This preserves strategic flexibility while avoiding the backlash of unilateralism. At the same time, the **verification_widens** development — confirmed in clinical coding and structural engineering — proves that automatic validation is spreading beyond code and math. We respond by launching the Open-Weight Monitoring and Containment Framework, tasking BEREC and ENISA with developing automated classifiers to detect high-risk derivatives of open models, and preparing legal notices to platforms hosting unsecured super-threshold releases. This is not containment by denial — that ship has sailed — but containment by accountability: we will not stop distribution, but we will make it costly to distribute dangerously.
