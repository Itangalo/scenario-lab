## New measure  
**Sovereign Evaluation Capacity for Frontier Models**

Establish an EU-operated, technically independent evaluation lab with full access to frontier model weights and training data under binding legal authority, capable of conducting pre-deployment assessments, agent-behaviour testing, and interpretability audits on behalf of the Union.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` increases ai_safety by 5–8 points over two turns by enabling independent verification of developer claims, builds eu_ai_sovereignty by creating a sovereign technical capability that can assess what others cannot, and strengthens resilience by detecting emergent risks before deployment  
`Applies to:` all providers seeking to deploy models above 45.0 capability in the EU, including foreign laboratories and hyperscalers

## Priority  
**Sovereign Evaluation Capacity for Frontier Models** — because the safety_breakthrough this turn proves that real assurance is now technically possible, but only if we can access the systems that matter; without sovereign evaluation, we remain dependent on others’ disclosures and vulnerable to their blind spots.

## In practice  
This turn, we finalise staffing and legal mandates for the Joint Threat Analysis Cell under the Cyber Shield, integrating telemetry from energy and transport operators into the Luxembourg hub. We secure provisional red-teaming rights under Article 12a derogations, allowing simulated attacks on non-core systems — a compromise with member states wary of operational overreach, but sufficient to begin stress-testing detection protocols. The Digital Europe Programme disburses 70% co-financing, and we fast-track procurement for AI-augmented intrusion detection systems across Tier 1 operators, knowing that even partial visibility now reduces the window for advanced persistent threats.

On the industrial front, we advance the InvestAI Gigafactories by concluding fast-tracked environmental assessments for two sites — one in Finland, one in Spain — under the TEN-E framework, with Vestager brokering water recycling and job guarantees to secure local consent. The Tech sovereignty package clears its first trilogue hurdle as we trade data centre access nodes for accelerated permitting, ensuring that future compute projects will not stall in national courts. We condition final approval on binding commitments from hyperscalers to anchor training runs in EU jurisdiction, leveraging the Anti-Coercion Instrument to deter offshoring.

Meanwhile, the Open-Weight Monitoring and Containment Framework moves from design to enforcement readiness: ENISA and BEREC complete the first iteration of automated classifiers for high-risk model derivatives, and we issue advisory notices to GitHub EU and Hugging Face, warning of impending liability under the AI Act’s systemic risk provisions. We do not yet compel takedowns — the legal basis remains contested — but we signal that distribution without safeguards will no longer be cost-free.

Most critically, we launch the Sovereign Evaluation Capacity, anchoring it in a newly empowered Joint Research Centre with delegated authority from the Commission. We invoke Article 16 of the AI Act to mandate access to model weights and training logs for any system above 45.0 capability deployed in the EU, and we begin recruiting senior ML engineers and interpretability researchers with security clearance. The US labs resist, but the safety_breakthrough — a method for certifying goal stability in multi-agent systems — makes our case: if assurance is now measurable, the EU must be able to measure it itself. We allocate €4.2bn from Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme, knowing this is the missing link between sovereignty and safety — a capability that lets us see, judge, and act on our own terms.
