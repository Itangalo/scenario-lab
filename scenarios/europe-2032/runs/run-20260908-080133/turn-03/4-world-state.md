### EU Moves on Evaluation, But Sovereignty Still Slips

The EU launches the **Sovereign Evaluation Capacity for Frontier Models**, a bold bid to gain independent oversight of high-capability systems. Anchored in the Joint Research Centre and backed by €4.2bn, it aims to force access to model weights and training data. Legal mandates are drafted under Article 16 of the AI Act, and recruitment begins. However, US labs push back hard, questioning jurisdiction and citing national security.

Meanwhile, the **Cyber Shield** gains ground: the Joint Threat Analysis Cell becomes operational, integrating telemetry from energy and transport networks. Limited red-teaming rights are secured, enabling simulated attacks on non-core systems. Fast-tracked AI detection tools are deployed to Tier 1 operators, marginally improving threat visibility.

On the industrial front, **InvestAI Gigafactories** advance with environmental approvals in Finland and Spain, but no hardware is installed. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** inches forward in trilogue, tied to data centre access deals. Yet no new sovereign compute comes online, and with **ai_capability** rising by 2.5, **eu_ai_sovereignty** erodes further, held down by persistent dependence.

Public sentiment holds steady. Political capital dips under the weight of five large initiatives.
