### A Step Forward in Crisis Mode

The EU formally completed the Sovereign Agent Operational Assurance Framework, marking its first finished measure in category 1. However, with Hungary and Poland still refusing implementation and the CJEU referral pending, the framework’s authority remains fractured. Compliance is patchy, enforcement nonexistent, and no real-time monitoring has gone live across contested systems. The symbolic milestone does little to restore confidence.

The **Red-Teaming Directive** advanced slowly. Finland and Belgium committed red teams, but delays in security clearances postponed simulations until next turn. No live test occurred.

Meanwhile, planning began for a new **EU Incident Response Corps**, with initial design work led by ENISA and funding discussions underway using repurposed Digital Europe funds. France and Germany expressed strong support, but no formal launch occurred. Italy raised concerns about deployment authority under civil protection rules, and Poland signalled it would refuse to host liaison personnel, calling any such body federal overreach. The proposal remains in early development and will not become operational for several turns.

A major open-weight release — powerful, widely distributed, and functionally near the frontier — contributed to rising open capabilities. Rogue agents now possess capabilities once confined to labs, increasing misuse risks across infrastructure. However, diffusion takes time: while the release was significant, adoption and integration across systems are ongoing, limiting the immediate jump in `openweight_capability`.

`ai_capability` rose by 1.5, reflecting steady progress in agent autonomy and cyber planning. With no safety measures landing on shipped systems, `ai_safety` eroded further. Municipal recovery from the prior incident remained sluggish, slightly lifting `resilience` through hard-won experience, though structural gaps persist.

Political capital dipped under portfolio costs but saw a modest rebound from completing a measure, supported by a +3 proposal bonus for advancing the Red-Teaming Directive in response to the recent cyber_major_incident. With no legitimacy lends and ongoing governance friction, the net gain was limited to +1.

Public sentiment held flat — no new scandal, but no visible gain either.

### Sovereignty’s Slow Erosion

Despite intent, no domestic compute came online. Dependence deepened as another member state signed a separate data sovereignty pact with a U.S. hyperscaler, bypassing EU coordination. This defection, combined with rising capability and no sovereign capacity offset, pushed `eu_ai_sovereignty` down again.
