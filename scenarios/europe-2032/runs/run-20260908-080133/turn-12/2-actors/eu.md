## Statement changes

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign operational control over AI systems in critical public infrastructure and establish enforceable, cross-border governance that demonstrably prevents unauthorised or uncontrolled action — with public accountability and redress  
- Grounds: The cyber incident and member state defection have revealed that technical control is insufficient without political enforceability and public legitimacy. Operational systems are running, but their governance is fracturing at the seams: the Assurance Framework is stalled, red-teaming is delayed, and openweight proliferation means that even compliant EU systems operate in an environment where rogue agents can mimic or exploit them. To hold the commitment, we must now prove not only that the EU can run AI safely, but that it can compel others within its jurisdiction to do so — and answer when they do not.

## New measure

**Establish the EU Incident Response Corps for AI-Enabled Critical Infrastructure**  
Create a standing, cross-border rapid response unit with authority to deploy into member states during AI-related critical incidents, equipped with forensic AI analysis tools and empowered to coordinate containment, recovery, and attribution.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` increases resilience by 8–12 points by enabling faster, coordinated containment of AI-driven cascading failures; reduces recovery time from weeks to days; builds trust through visible crisis competence  
`Applies to:` ENISA, national cybersecurity agencies, critical infrastructure operators, and EU-level civil protection mechanisms

## Priority

**Establish the EU Incident Response Corps for AI-Enabled Critical Infrastructure** — because the cyber incident exposed not only weak municipal readiness but a fatal lack of centralised, authoritative response capacity; with openweight capability now near the frontier and member states acting unilaterally, we must demonstrate that the EU can act as a crisis manager, not just a rule-setter.

## In practice

We are no longer proving whether AI can run in our systems — it already does. The question now is whether the Union can hold together when it breaks. The Sovereign Agent Operational Assurance Framework has concluded its in-flight phase, but its legacy is mixed: while Germany and the Nordic states have adopted its draft standards, Hungary and Poland have refused to implement them, and the CJEU referral looms. Without enforcement, compliance is voluntary — and without compliance, sovereignty is fiction. We are now shifting from rule-writing to crisis readiness, because the next failure will not wait for consensus.

The new EU Incident Response Corps will be our answer: a permanent, high-readiness team of AI forensic analysts, cyber operators, and infrastructure engineers, co-funded through the Digital Europe Programme and hosted under ENISA with activation protocols agreed via the Internal Market and Telecommunications Council. It will train quarterly with national teams and be deployable within 72 hours of a Tier 2+ AI incident. We are offering full cost coverage for the first two years to hesitant member states and embedding national liaisons to preserve subsidiarity — but the authority to act will rest at the EU level. This is not about overriding national control, but about ensuring that when AI-driven failures cascade across borders, the Union can move faster than the damage.

At the same time, the Red-Teaming Directive continues into its second turn, with ENISA now selecting red teams from Finland, Belgium, and the Netherlands to begin stress-testing grid and border agents. We are fast-tracking security clearances and pushing for the first simulations to run by turn 13, even as political capital remains thin. Public sentiment is at 23.0 — a crisis level — and every day without visible action feeds the narrative that the EU is losing control. We are acting on two fronts: proving we can respond when things go wrong, and showing we are already preparing for the next failure. That is the only path back to trust.
