## New measure  
**Secure Compute Anchoring Initiative**  
Launch a binding legal and financial mechanism to ensure that any sovereign compute capacity funded or permitted under EU instruments remains under enforceable EU jurisdiction and cannot be remotely disabled, reconfigured, or denied access by foreign entities.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` increases eu_ai_sovereignty by 8–12 points by ensuring that new compute is not just located in the EU but legally and operationally sovereign; reduces dependence on foreign-controlled infrastructure and strengthens leverage in negotiations with hyperscalers  
`Applies to:` all entities receiving public funding or accelerated permitting under the InvestAI Gigafactories or Tech Sovereignty Package, with enforcement via contract law, infrastructure escrow, and access to emergency override protocols under Commission authority

## Priority  
**Secure Compute Anchoring Initiative** — because the continued erosion of eu_ai_sovereignty despite major investments proves that compute on EU soil is not enough: if access can be revoked or systems disabled from abroad, we are still dependent — and with ai_capability now at 58.5 and climbing, the window to lock in real autonomy is closing fast.

## In practice  
This turn, we complete the operational handover of the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators: the Joint Threat Analysis Cell in Luxembourg is now fully staffed and integrated, receiving live telemetry from energy, transport, and digital infrastructure operators across 18 member states. ENISA conducts its first cross-border red-team exercise on non-core systems, validating detection protocols and exposing gaps in municipal-level cyber readiness. The AI-augmented intrusion detection tools funded under Digital Europe are deployed to all Tier 1 operators, with initial feedback showing a 40% reduction in dwell time for advanced persistent threats — a modest but meaningful gain in resilience. With this measure now finished, we shift focus from deployment to sustainment, embedding its protocols into NIS2 enforcement cycles and freeing up bandwidth for deeper hardening in healthcare and mid-tier industry.

On the industrial front, we advance the InvestAI Gigafactories by finalising land leases and grid connection agreements in Finland and Spain, with construction crews mobilising in April. However, behind the scenes, legal teams uncover a critical vulnerability: the contracts with US-based hyperscalers contain clauses allowing remote administrative override and service suspension under foreign legal orders. This is not hypothetical — one provider has already invoked such a clause in a non-AI context, cutting off a Central Asian government during a political crisis. In response, we launch the Secure Compute Anchoring Initiative, drafting binding addenda to all InvestAI and Tech Sovereignty Package agreements that require: (1) local administrative control with multi-signature access including EU-appointed stewards, (2) hardware-level kill switches under EU jurisdiction, and (3) escrow of configuration keys with the European Commission. We leverage the Anti-Coercion Instrument to deter non-compliance, making clear that access to EU markets and subsidies depends on enforceable sovereignty, not just physical presence.

Simultaneously, the Sovereign Evaluation Capacity gains momentum: the Joint Research Centre recruits 62 senior ML engineers and interpretability specialists, 44% from outside the EU, with security clearances fast-tracked through a new inter-agency protocol. We issue our first formal access requests to three frontier labs under Article 16 of the AI Act, citing the recent safety_breakthrough as justification for independent verification of goal stability in multi-agent systems. The US labs resist, but the scientific credibility of the interpretability advance weakens their argument — and with the bio_uplift_findings raising alarms in the public health community, even reluctant member states see the need for sovereign oversight. We also expand the Open-Weight Monitoring Framework, with ENISA deploying updated classifiers that can now detect derivatives of models at 49.0 capability with 88% accuracy. Advisory notices go out to five additional platforms, including GitLab EU and a major French open-source hub, warning of liability under systemic risk provisions if high-capability models are distributed without safeguards. The legal basis remains contested, but the signal is clear: the EU will no longer treat open-weight distribution as a free pass.
