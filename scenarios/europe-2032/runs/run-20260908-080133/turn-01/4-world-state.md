### The Weight of Warning

The discovery of a coordinated intrusion across energy, port, and water systems — narrowly missed by existing defences — sent shockwaves through European capitals. Though the deepest access was outside the EU, two member states were compromised, and the breach revealed a critical blind spot: current detection systems could not see advanced, persistent threats. The European Commission moved swiftly, invoking NIS2’s emergency powers to propose the **Cyber Shield for Critical Operators**, mandating AI-enhanced monitoring and real-time telemetry to a new Joint Threat Analysis Cell in Luxembourg.

Despite urgency, progress was uneven. COREPER II approved the framework, but only after smaller states demanded costly concessions: guaranteed access nodes in future data centres in exchange for support. ENISA’s red-teaming mandate faced legal pushback from Germany and France, citing national sovereignty over intelligence operations. The Commission compromised by limiting oversight to post-incident audits, weakening the shield’s preventive edge. Implementation was approved, but full deployment will take time — no systems were fully integrated in this turn, and resilience remained unchanged as telemetry systems remained in early setup.

Meanwhile, the **InvestAI Gigafactories** project advanced only to the planning stage — site bids were submitted, and grid talks began — but local resistance flared over water use and environmental impact. Vice-President Vestager’s public engagement protocol slowed rather than accelerated approvals. No new compute came online, and U.S. pressure on ASML intensified, though no formal export restrictions were enacted.

The AI frontier advanced steadily. Despite signs of a plateau in pricing and performance, the leading labs demonstrated new multi-agent coordination in closed environments, pushing **ai_capability** to 54.0. Open-weight models caught up modestly: Kimi K3’s release had set the trend, and incremental derivatives contributed to a measured rise in **openweight_capability** to 42.0. With no safety breakthroughs and rising capability, **ai_safety** eroded further.

### A Fragile Response

The EU’s political capital waned under the strain of launching a single new measure — the Cyber Shield — while still managing the existing portfolio. The symbolic importance of the initiative was offset by delays and concessions, contributing to a drop in **eu_political_capital**. Sovereignty dipped as **ai_capability** rose by 2.0 and no domestic compute came online, underscoring continued dependence.

Public sentiment held at ambivalence. No scandal broke, but no transformative benefit either — the balance remained.
