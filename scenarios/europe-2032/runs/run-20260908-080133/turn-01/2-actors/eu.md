Establish mandatory intrusion detection and response protocols for all operators of essential services, with shared monitoring infrastructure and red-teaming rights granted to ENISA.

## In practice
This turn, we activate the legal machinery behind the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators through Article 12a of the NIS2 Directive, leveraging the recent incident to justify expanded obligations on essential service operators. The Commission drafts implementing acts to mandate deployment of AI-augmented intrusion detection systems, co-developed by ENISA and national CERTs, with real-time telemetry feeds routed to a new Joint Threat Analysis Cell hosted in Luxembourg. We will push for adoption in COREPER II this month, offering member states cost-sharing at 70% from the Digital Europe Programme and invoking the solidarity clause if resistance emerges.

At the same time, we advance the InvestAI Gigafactories by convening the first site selection council—Greece, Poland, Finland and two regions in Spain have submitted bids—and fast-track grid connection negotiations via the TEN-E framework. Early tensions arise over water cooling access and local opposition in candidate regions; we respond by assigning Vice-President Vestager to lead a public engagement protocol tied to regional development funds, conditioning final approval on demonstrated community consultation outcomes. The Tech sovereignty package moves through trilogue, where we offer smaller member states guaranteed access nodes in exchange for expediting the accelerated-permitting zones legislation, knowing that without it, even funded projects stall.

We do not yet invoke export controls on lithography equipment, though ASML faces heavier US pressure—our leverage remains latent, not deployed. Public sentiment holds at ambivalence, and we resist calls for symbolic bans on open-weight models, aware that capability is already loose beyond recall, and that credibility now depends on what we can shield and build, not what we pretend to contain.
