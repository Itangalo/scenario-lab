## In practice  
We are operating on two fronts: one technical, one human. On the technical side, the Emergency Resilience Surge is now law in principle, but implementation is fracturing along familiar lines. Germany insists on Class A systems meeting zero-trust architecture benchmarks by Q3, while Greece and Bulgaria warn that even the extended timelines could strain national budgets. To hold cohesion, we are activating the Margins of the MFF to increase grant coverage to 80% for states below the median in cyber readiness, and we are fast-tracking the revised Tech Sovereignty Package through the Competitiveness Council, linking compliance with grid hardening to eligibility for accelerated permitting in designated AI zones. ENISA has begun its first unannounced red-team operations, and we are declassifying key elements of the intrusion playbook — not to alarm, but to end the fiction that AI-enabled attacks are still theoretical.

At the same time, the emergent displacement wave cannot be ignored. Employment agencies in France, the Netherlands, and Poland are reporting early but accelerating attrition in paralegal, accounting, and mid-tier administrative roles. We are not yet at systemic unemployment, but we are at inflection. The Labour Transition Readiness Initiative will deploy €12 billion from the European Social Fund Plus and the Just Transition Mechanism to fund real-time labour market monitoring via Eurostat and national agencies, with automated alerts when displacement exceeds 5% in a sector-region cluster. Where thresholds are crossed, pre-negotiated retraining consortia — led by vocational networks like Cedefop and backed by sectoral social partners — will activate within 30 days. This is not a welfare stopgap; it is a circuit breaker to prevent localized disruption from becoming political rupture. We are presenting it in the Social Affairs Council as a test of European solidarity under technological stress — not redistribution, but anticipation. The Dutch are cautiously supportive, especially if it includes safeguards on data use in retraining; Hungary and Poland remain sceptical, but we are offering co-design rights to secure buy-in. This is how we honour both mandates: by securing the system, and by ensuring the people within it are not left behind.
