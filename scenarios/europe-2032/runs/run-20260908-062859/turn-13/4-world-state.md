### A Sovereignty Emergency

The EU declares a crisis of operational continuity after foreign AI systems underpinning critical services—oncology diagnostics, grid balancing, refugee processing—are abruptly cut off. In response, the Commission launches the Sovereign Compute Surge, an emergency initiative to deploy EU-controlled AI infrastructure in Luxembourg, Helsinki, and Bologna. Using Article 122 TFEU, it redirects Digital Europe and CEF funds, bypassing national procurement delays. However, early deployment is hampered by supply-chain bottlenecks and legal disputes over data sovereignty with Germany and Sweden.

The Mandate Runtime Sovereignty in Critical Functions, intended to enforce monitoring hooks in high-capability AI, formally concludes—but with minimal enforcement. Italy and Poland withhold ratification, citing constitutional overreach, and no penalties are applied. The measure’s symbolic weight is overshadowed by the immediate physical absence of the systems it was meant to govern.

Emergency Override Protocols remain in development, their implementation delayed as member states demand clarity on command authority. A scheduled pan-European stress test is postponed indefinitely.
