Global AI capability reaches 67.5, with open-weight models advancing to 62.0 due to rapid diffusion of frontier techniques, though replication bottlenecks prevent full parity. A major AI-powered ransomware attack disrupts critical infrastructure across three EU states, exposing institutional fragility and accelerating calls for stronger oversight. In response, the EU proposes the EU-Controlled AI Evaluation and Intervention Nodes—a Category 5 measure for real-time monitoring and override of high-risk systems—but faces significant opposition from the Open Source AI Resistance Network and resistance from Poland and Hungary, delaying adoption. Public backlash and legal challenges loom, while site preparation for the European Sovereign Compute & Control Grid is delayed by protests in Finland. The InvestAI Gigafactories and Secure the Open Frontier initiatives conclude without delivering new compute or security gains, the latter leaving a legacy of open-source community distrust. Political capital declines under the strain of sustained large-scale measures and public anxiety; public sentiment drops sharply as fear of disruption eclipses earlier trust from healthcare AI successes. Despite strategic ambition, no sovereignty gain is achieved this turn due to insufficient capability growth, lack of completed high-category measures, and absence of access events. EU momentum stalls, with institutional cohesion failing to keep pace with escalating threats and policy ambitions.
