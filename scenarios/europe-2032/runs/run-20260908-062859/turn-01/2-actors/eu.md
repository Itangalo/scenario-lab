## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_test_shot as a live fire alarm, not a drill. Under Article 114 TFEU, the Commission will table a temporary Crisis Resilience Directive by January 2027, mandating AI-resistant segmentation, air-gapped backups, and continuous red-teaming for all critical energy, water, and logistics operators. Funding of €45bn will be drawn from the InvestEU pot and the Margins of the MFF, with 70% grants for operators in member states below the 75th percentile of current cyber readiness. ENISA will be empowered to conduct unannounced penetration tests using red teams drawn from national agencies and academic partners under Article 5a of the NIS2 Directive.

The resistance will be cost and sovereignty: Germany and the Nordic states want higher standards but fear Brussels overreach; Southern and Eastern members fear the fiscal burden. We are offering differentiated timelines — Class A systems (transmission, ports) hardened by turn 4, Class B (distribution, municipal) by turn 6 — and pairing the directive with a fast-tracked revision of the Tech sovereignty package to include grid resilience as a criterion for accelerated permitting. We are also quietly coordinating with CERT-EU and national intelligence services to declassify and share a sanitized version of the intrusion playbook, to break the complacency that still treats AI-enabled attacks as theoretical. This is not about catching up — it is about not being knocked down.
