A covert cyber intrusion across EU energy grids and port authorities, though non-disruptive, exposed critical vulnerabilities and triggered a major security response. The Commission launched an Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 114 TFEU, allocating €45 billion to enforce AI-resistant network segmentation, air-gapped backups, and red-teaming for critical infrastructure, with flexible timelines for member states. Germany pushed for stricter standards, while Eastern and Southern members resisted fiscal burdens, framing the directive as inverted solidarity. To secure support, the Commission linked grid resilience to faster data centre permitting in a revised Tech Sovereignty Package.

Despite political maneuvering, the measures yield no immediate gains in resilience. Safety declines to 32.0 amid control erosion and lack of new safeguards. EU sovereignty drops to 21.0, undermined by lagging compute access, while political capital falls to 40.0 under strain from overlapping initiatives.
