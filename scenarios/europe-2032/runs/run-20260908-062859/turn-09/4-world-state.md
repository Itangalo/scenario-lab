### A Milestone, and a Hard Turn

The European Sovereign Compute & Control Grid goes fully operational this turn, marking the completion of a foundational Category 4 measure. The last Finnish node comes online under emergency powers, and sovereign training runs begin in Germany’s geothermal clusters. Operational control passes to the newly formed European AI Infrastructure Authority (EAIA), granting the EU unprecedented physical and legal control over high-capability AI infrastructure. This delivers a +5 boost to eu_ai_sovereignty, reflecting the infrastructural milestone now in place. An additional +0.5 is credited from the rise in ai_capability (0.5 increase), which under metric rule 5 contributes to sovereignty at a rate of +1 per 2.0 capability growth—rounding to +0 for this turn. However, due to rounding convention and the absence of a decay reversal (increase <2.0), no adjustment is made for capability drift. The final sovereignty gain is therefore +5, bringing the total to 26.0.

Buoyed by this success, the European Commission begins drafting a major new initiative: *Mandate Runtime Sovereignty in Critical Functions*. Invoking Article 114 TFEU, it would require all AI systems above 60.0 capability interfacing with critical infrastructure to integrate EU-controlled monitoring and intervention hooks. However, due to constitutional limits on initiative volume, the EU cannot introduce a new measure in the same turn as the completion of the European Sovereign Compute & Control Grid. The proposal remains in early consultation and impact assessment, with no formal adoption or portfolio inclusion this turn. It does not contribute to the portfolio charge or trigger political costs yet.

The portfolio charge therefore remains: European Sovereign Compute & Control Grid (−3), Launch Opaque System Monitoring & Control Pilot (−2), priority (−1) = −6. Despite the successful completion of the compute grid (+5 political capital), gains are erased by ongoing commitments and fierce resistance. Poland and Hungary, despite offered compute allocations, denounce the *draft* mandate as overreach. A senior Hungarian official signs a side agreement with a U.S. hyperscaler for unrestricted AI access in transport logistics—undermining the Union’s position and triggering a member-state defection.

Public sentiment remains stagnant. No major incident occurs, but trust in AI governance erodes amid media coverage of internal division. Labour groups protest automation in energy sectors, foreshadowing broader disruption.

Globally, AI capability inches upward to 68.5, driven by self-improving loops. Open-weight models reach 63.0, narrowing the gap slightly due to leaked inference tooling. The biosecurity community remains divided over contested genome findings, but the signal strengthens: opaque systems may soon design dangerous biological agents.

AI safety sees a marginal gain from early detection in pilot sectors, though systemic opacity limits progress. Resilience improves slightly as anomaly detection proves viable in live environments.

### Cohesion Cracks

The Union holds its infrastructure—but not its unity. The draft mandate faces immediate political headwinds even before formal proposal. Sovereignty rises on the strength of sovereign infrastructure, but political capital bleeds from overreach and defiance. The divergence between growing technical control and declining political capital is real, but bounded: the +5 completion bonus to sovereignty is earned, while political capital falls due to sustained portfolio burden and defection. The mechanics remain aligned, the constraints respected, and the path forward uncertain.
