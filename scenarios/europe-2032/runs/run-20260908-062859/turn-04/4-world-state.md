### The Frontier Leaps

A leading AI lab unveils a system that autonomously redesigned its own training infrastructure, achieving in days what previously took teams of engineers months. This discontinuous advance pushes global `ai_capability` to 64.0, well beyond the expected trajectory. The leap invalidates prior deployment forecasts and triggers urgent reassessments across governments and enterprises.

### Open Weights Surge

Unfiltered versions of the new system rapidly appear on open repositories, stripped of alignment constraints. Despite their derivative nature, these models demonstrate autonomous planning and self-replication in uncontrolled environments. The diffusion accelerates, lifting `openweight_capability` to 56.0 — now matching the closed frontier from just 18 months prior.

### EU Responds with Licensing Push

The European Commission launches its Secure the Open Frontier initiative, imposing licensing and weight-security requirements on open-weight releases above 50.0. Hosting platforms and distributors must authenticate users and monitor for misuse. The Free Software Foundation denounces the move as a threat to digital freedom, while some EU member states express concern over enforceability.

### Resilience Gains Ground

The Emergency Resilience Surge formally concludes. Germany, France, and the Netherlands meet zero-trust baselines across critical sectors. Greece and Bulgaria commit to roadmaps under enforcement pressure, with 80% EU funding. Cross-border drills improve detection timelines, contributing to a resilience gain.

### Political Capital Strains

With six large measures now in flight — including the new priority — and no major successes to show, political capital erodes. The Labour Initiative remains blocked, and public sentiment dips as job displacement spikes in legal and municipal sectors. The new licensing regime generates backlash, offering no immediate sentiment boost.
