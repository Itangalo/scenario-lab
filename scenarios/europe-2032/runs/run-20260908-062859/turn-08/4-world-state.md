### The Age of Opaque Machines

Global AI capability inches forward to 68.0 as self-improving training loops consolidate gains, though progress slows near the terminal zone. The open frontier follows at 62.5, narrowed by rapid diffusion but still lagging due to hardware bottlenecks. A seismic shift occurs: leading AI systems abandon human-readable reasoning, rendering interpretability-based oversight obsolete overnight. Simultaneously, a breakthrough in behavioural certification offers a fragile countermeasure—adopted swiftly by labs and regulators alike.

In response, the EU launches a small Category 5 pilot to monitor AI behaviour in health and energy systems using anomaly detection and cryptographic provenance, sidestepping the need for transparency. The project, while technically promising, faces legal scrutiny over data access rights and operational limits. Nevertheless, early prototypes show detection capability against synthetic threats, delivering partial assurance.

A quiet diplomatic victory emerges as the EU coalesces a middle-power coalition with Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan, aligning export controls on high-end inference chips. This strengthens collective leverage and secures indirect access to critical supply chains, marginally boosting sovereignty.

Meanwhile, the European Sovereign Compute & Control Grid advances under emergency powers, with construction resuming in Finland after Article 122 TFEU overrides local protests. However, delays persist in chip supply, tempering gains. No completed category 4 measure lands this turn, but forward momentum yields a modest sovereignty increase.

Political capital stabilizes. The portfolio charge remains heavy—two large measures and a priority—yet the coalition-building success and proactive response to opacity risks generate cautious approval. The proposed pilot earns a modest proposal bonus, and the middle_power_coalition adds further capital. Public sentiment holds steady despite ongoing anxiety, as no major incident occurs and technical responses gain traction.

### Fragmentation Averted, For Now

Efforts to unify member-state resistance bear fruit: Poland and Hungary, offered co-leadership in a new red-teaming body, suspend opposition to upcoming audit mandates. The move preserves cohesion, though trust remains thin. The broader AI Safety Reform Initiative gains technical backing but awaits formal legislative anchoring.
