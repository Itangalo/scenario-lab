`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`

## Priority
**European Sovereign Compute & Control Grid** — because without physically and legally anchored infrastructure, every monitoring, safety, and intervention capability we build can be disabled by a foreign provider’s decision, a sanctions regime, or a severed supply chain. The grid is the foundation: if it fails, nothing else stands.

## In practice
We are executing under fire. The European Sovereign Compute & Control Grid remains our spine, and we are accelerating site development in Finland and southern Germany despite protests. We have invoked Article 122 TFEU to override local objections on national security grounds, and we are redirecting €40bn from the MFF to fast-track energy integration and secure chip supply via the Chips Act framework. This is not just about compute — it is about ensuring that when we deploy the Evaluation and Intervention Nodes, they run on hardware and software we control, not on foreign stacks that can be remotely disabled.

At the same time, we are adapting to the collapse of interpretability. The opaque_reasoning event is a system shock: overnight, our primary control mechanism became obsolete. But the safety_breakthrough — a method to certify behavioural invariants in deployed models — gives us a foothold. We are tasking a joint team from the European AI Office, ENISA, and the Max Planck Institute to operationalise it within the new Opaque System Monitoring & Control Pilot. This will not wait for consensus: we are deploying in health and energy sectors where anomaly detection can be trialled without full override authority, using data flow signatures and cryptographic provenance to flag deviations. The middle_power_coalition gives us leverage — we are coordinating with Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan to align export controls on AI inference hardware, ensuring that even if open weights spread, the most capable runtimes remain subject to joint oversight.

We are also responding to the emergent_ai_safety_reform_push not as a threat, but as a signal. The public and technical communities are demanding accountability, and we will channel that into the Council regulation under Article 114 TFEU, which will mandate pre-deployment audits and independent red-teaming for all systems above 60.0 capability. We know Poland and Hungary will resist, so we are offering them co-leadership in the red-teaming body and access to the Compute & Control Grid’s secondary tier. This is not appeasement — it is cohesion by design. We cannot afford fragmentation when the core is under pressure from both outside and within.
