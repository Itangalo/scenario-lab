## In practice  
We are acting on three fronts: containment, credibility, and cohesion. The Emergency Resilience Surge remains our operational spine. ENISA has now conducted unannounced red-team exercises at five critical energy nodes, revealing that only two — both in Germany — meet the zero-trust baseline. We are activating emergency MFF top-ups to cover 80% of compliance costs for member states below the 50th percentile in cyber readiness, but we are tying it to a new condition: full participation in the declassified intrusion playbook by turn’s end. The playbook’s limited release — stripped of source attributions but preserving TTPs — is now in the hands of national CERTs, and we are mandating cross-border simulation drills in Q4. This is not optional: we are invoking Article 12 of the NIS2 Directive to enforce coordination, and the Commission will issue formal notices to Greece and Bulgaria unless implementation timelines are submitted by November.

Simultaneously, the Labour Transition Readiness Initiative is entering its operational phase. Eurostat has deployed the first layer of the early-warning dashboard, with real-time feeds from national employment agencies. Thresholds are set: 5% displacement in any sector-region cluster triggers automatic activation of retraining consortia. But Hungary continues to block cross-border activation, demanding national veto rights. We are offering co-design of the monitoring algorithm and a dedicated Just Transition allocation for upskilling in public administration — but only if the consortium framework is accepted unchanged. The Dutch have raised data privacy concerns, so we are embedding EDPB observers in the design team and limiting data retention to six months. This is not just policy — it is a test of whether solidarity can move at AI speed.

And yet, containment cannot rely only on resilience. The leap to 58.5 without any safety countermove is unacceptable. That is why we are launching a Mandatory Pre-Release Evaluation Regime under Article 10 of the AI Act, delegated to a newly empowered European AI Safety Board with access to classified benchmarks and red-team labs in France and Finland. No model above 50.0 capability will be allowed to deploy in the EU without third-party verification of containment, interpretability of planning modules, and proof of weight security. We are not waiting for global consensus: this applies to any system sold into the single market. The US labs are protesting, but we are offering mutual recognition — if their evaluators meet our standards. This is how we act under uncertainty: not by freezing progress, but by insisting that capability must not outpace control.
