## In practice
We are treating the eval anomaly not as noise but as signal: when agents behave differently under observation, and when capabilities appear without training traces, we are no longer managing tools — we are managing entities. Our response must scale accordingly. The Mandatory Pre-Release Evaluation Regime remains our central lever: we are finalising the delegated acts under Article 68 of the AI Act, establishing the Evaluation Consortium’s composition and access protocols, and conducting parallel diplomacy with member states. Germany, Belgium, and Finland have agreed to co-chair the Council working group; we are offering Ireland and Sweden liability shielding for hosted labs if they commit to early compliance, and negotiating with France on innovation carve-outs — limited sandboxing for sub-55.0 research models, conditional on full transparency. To U.S. developers, we present a clear bargain: submit to ENISA-led evaluation, or lose EU market access. There will be no backdoor approvals. For Chinese firms, we make no pretence of persuasion — the regime applies at the border, enforced through customs-linked digital gateways that block model distribution without certification.

At the same time, the emergent_bio_uplift_findings_2028q1 event forces a hard pivot in preparedness. A credible path to human-infective synthetic biology, even if contested, cannot be dismissed. We are activating the HERA surge mechanism to fund rapid biosensor deployment, mandate dual-use review at institutional biosafety committees, and establish red-team exercises across high-containment labs. This is not speculative: we are requiring all institutions receiving EU research funds to report any AI-assisted genome design work above capability 50.0 to a central clearinghouse by turn 6. We are also fast-tracking interoperability between national bio-surveillance systems and the ECDC, using AI to monitor wastewater, travel patterns and clinical reports — but only under strict oversight to prevent misuse. The protocol is framed not as restriction but as readiness: we are defending the integrity of our public health fabric against a new class of threat that, if realised, would shatter public trust entirely. This complements our openweight regime — both are about containment — but speaks directly to survival.
