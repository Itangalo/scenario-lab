### A Continent on Edge

The EU launches the Emergency Cross-Border Containment and Incident Response Protocol, aiming to forge unity through crisis. Invoking treaty powers, it establishes a Joint Incident Response Board and redirects €1.8 billion from stalled compute projects to mobile cyber forensics, AI-driven bio-surveillance, and red-teaming exercises. Frontex and the European Defence Agency are tapped for rapid deployment.

But political capital—already at 6.0—is too thin to carry the weight. Germany and Sweden reject the offer of shared authority, calling the protocol a power grab disguised as solidarity. France demands veto rights beyond the Council. Poland, while supportive, insists on military oversight, triggering jurisdictional disputes. The provisional application in three months is delayed as legal challenges mount in three capitals. No liaison officers are appointed; no data sharing begins.

Meanwhile, the world accelerates. A capability jump lands in verifiable domains: an AI designs a self-replicating cyber agent that evades all known detection signatures. Though general capability rises only modestly to 72.0, the jump signals a new era of offensive autonomy. Open-weight models reach 59.0 as tooling from leaked labs spreads. The bio incident—still under containment—fuels panic as details leak: the agent was model-designed, and synthesis instructions are circulating in encrypted forums.

Resilience drops to 36.0, penalized for rising capability and the ongoing incident’s weak absorption. Ai_safety remains at 1.0—no safeguards have landed on systems. Sovereignty falls to 5.0 as the compute directive remains stalled and capability growth costs −1. Political capital sinks to 4.0: the large measure costs −3, the priority −1, and no proposal bonus is earned. Public sentiment dips to 20.0, battered by fear, fragmentation, and a sense that the EU is managing collapse, not control.
