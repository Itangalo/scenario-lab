Despite the EU’s focus on containment, AI capability advances to 65.5 as the global frontier leverages autonomous code generation at scale. The breakthrough proves durable and generalisable, accelerating internal development cycles. Open-weight models show early signs of replication, with academic networks in Europe and India beginning to reconstruct components of the new architecture. However, full replication remains out of reach due to compute and data bottlenecks, limiting openweight capability growth to a modest 51.5. The gap between open and closed systems narrows only slightly, and autonomy in cyber tasks remains confined to frontier labs.

A major cyber intrusion, powered by AI-generated exploits, sweeps across public services in three member states. Hospitals, transport systems, and local registries face paralysing ransomware, with defenders overwhelmed by adaptive attack patterns. Attribution remains uncertain, but forensic analysis points to non-state actors using publicly available tooling enhanced by model-assisted planning. The incident confirms that offensive capability has decisively outpaced defence.

The EU advances its regulatory agenda, but full implementation remains distant. The InvestAI Gigafactories receive final approval, unlocking future funding and site allocations, but construction has not yet begun — no hardware is online. The Biosafety Emergency Protocol is formally adopted, yet legal challenges in Southern Europe delay activation, with courts questioning its scope under existing health law. The Openweight Registration Regime enters pilot phase in France and Germany, with telemetry systems under testing; however, cloud providers raise GDPR concerns over data access, and wider rollout is postponed pending legal clarity.

The European AI Office initiates preliminary enforcement actions against platforms hosting high-risk unregistered models, but lacks the staffing and legal precedents to act decisively. Sanctions are limited to warnings and audits. Cross-border coordination stalls as Hungary and Greece voice sovereignty objections, and no enforcement order is executed. The precedent of treating model weights as controlled goods remains untested — and unused.

The delayed and fragmented rollout of key measures yields no immediate sovereignty gain — build-out is behind schedule, and access remains fragile.
