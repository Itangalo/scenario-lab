## In practice  
We are treating the emergent_labour_displacement_wave as a second front in the same strategic challenge: autonomy in an era of uncontrollable diffusion. The cyber intrusion was a test of our infrastructure; this is a test of our social fabric. The pattern is clear — AI is no longer assisting work, it is replacing it, and it is doing so in roles that anchor the middle class. If we do not act now, we will face not only economic dislocation but a legitimacy crisis. The Labour Transition Accelerator is not a relief programme; it is a sovereignty measure. It ensures that the EU remains the primary agent of adjustment, not the market or foreign platforms.

We are activating the European Social Fund+ and the newly established AI Transition Facility to co-finance national retraining schemes in Germany, France, and the Netherlands — the first hotspots. The Commission is issuing guidelines by Q3 2027 for wage insurance covering 60% of lost income for up to 18 months, paired with modular, AI-audited retraining paths in green tech, cybersecurity, and public AI stewardship. Member states must commit to data-sharing on displaced workers and to removing barriers to cross-border recognition of credentials. In return, they receive 85% co-financing and early access to public-sector AI deployment funds. The European Labour Authority will coordinate monitoring, and we are tasking Eurofound with publishing quarterly displacement maps to maintain transparency.

At the same time, we are holding the line on the Emergency Resilience Surge. ENISA has delivered the first red-teaming suite, and we are now mandating its deployment across all NIS2-covered operators by June 2027. The resistance in Poland and Hungary persists, but we are leveraging the InvestAI Gigafactories’ acquisition of the Irish site — offering both countries priority access to future compute allocations if they comply. The Tech Sovereignty Package remains on track, with the Council’s Competitiveness Working Party finalising the accelerated-permitting framework. We are not slowing down — we are redistributing attention, because the two mandates now converge: staying capable means protecting not just our systems, but our people.
