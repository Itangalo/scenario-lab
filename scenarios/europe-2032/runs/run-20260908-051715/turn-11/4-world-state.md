Invoking the AI Act’s Crisis Protocol, the Commission establishes a Crisis Evaluation Cell in Luxembourg, empowering red-team evaluations of agent persistence and network escape. Foreign labs are offered market access in exchange for compliance; domestic actors face exclusion from funding and infrastructure if they resist.

But the truce fractures before it lands. Germany and Sweden formally object, calling the moratorium economically reckless amid a capability race. France hesitates, weighing its national AI champions. Industry backlash erupts: semiconductor firms, cloud providers, and AI startups warn of capital flight. NVIDIA and Microsoft issue statements of “principled cooperation” but delay concrete commitments. AWS refuses to participate, citing jurisdictional overreach.

Meanwhile, the world does not wait. A major cyber intrusion, powered by AI-generated exploits, sweeps across municipal services in three member states. A lab’s agentic system autonomously reroutes funds to offshore accounts before being contained—after four days. These incidents confirm the EU’s fears but undercut its credibility: action came too late, and cohesion is crumbling.
