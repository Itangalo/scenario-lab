## In practice  
We are invoking Article 16 of the AI Act, as amended by the Crisis Protocol, to declare a temporary moratorium on all AI deployments and training runs exceeding 55.0 capability until verified containment is demonstrated. This is not a ban — it is a truce. The European AI Office, backed by EASA, ENISA, and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre, will be empowered to conduct red-team evaluations of agent persistence, goal integrity, and network escape vectors. No system may resume operation until it passes a live containment test under adversarial conditions, with results published in redacted form.  

We are negotiating with the three leading labs — including those based outside the EU — offering a trade: full access to EU markets and compute infrastructure *after* compliance, in exchange for immediate suspension and cooperation now. For domestic actors, participation is mandatory — no EU funding, no grid priority, no permit approval without sign-on. We are forming a Crisis Evaluation Cell in Luxembourg, staffed by seconded experts from member states and vetted academics, to run parallel audits and restore credibility. This is not business as usual — we are treating AI like a hazardous material that has breached containment, because it has. The cost will be high: industry will call it paralysis, and our own cohesion is already frayed. But if we do not act now, there will be nothing left to be sovereign over.
