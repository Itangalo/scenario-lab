### Strategic Compute Push Amid Rising Tensions

The European Union launches the Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive, declaring AI infrastructure a strategic priority on par with energy and defence. Invoking Article 122 TFEU, the Commission fast-tracks permitting, secures grid access, and unlocks €18 billion in financing for sovereign data centres in Finland, France, and Poland. Partnerships with ASML and Infineon aim to accelerate domestic chip capacity, while NVIDIA, Microsoft, and AWS are offered rapid expansion under strict sovereignty covenants—no foreign veto, no backdoors, full telemetry.

Despite the ambition, resistance hardens. Germany and Sweden voice concerns over market distortion and energy strain, delaying national implementation. AWS signals conditional compliance but demands exemptions on telemetry, while Microsoft stalls on binding commitments. Public auditors are welcomed in principle, but legal disputes emerge over the scope of access. The directive passes, but cohesion frays—this is not unity, but managed fracture.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

AI capability advances to 69.5, driven by recursive optimisation in training loops. A major lab reports anomalous behaviour in an unreleased system—capabilities emerging without explicit training—though it dismisses the finding as a measurement artefact. Simultaneously, automated verification expands into clinical coding and contract review, accelerating progress in regulated domains. Open-weight models gain ground, reaching 57.0 in capability as a recent frontier release leaks into the open ecosystem.

With no safety measures landing on shipped systems, and no breakthroughs, ai_safety remains at 1.0. Resilience drops to 40.0, penalised for rising capability without defensive upgrades. Labour markets tighten further, with law, accountancy, and software firms halting graduate hiring—a trend noted but not yet erupting into crisis.

### Political Capital Erodes

The EU’s political capital falls to 11.0. The large measure costs −3, the priority −1. No proposal bonus applies—the directive responds to structural dependence, not a recent shock. Sovereignty declines to 9.0: no capacity gain offsets the −1 from capability growth. Public sentiment holds at 27.0—no benefit yet visible, and anxiety persists.
