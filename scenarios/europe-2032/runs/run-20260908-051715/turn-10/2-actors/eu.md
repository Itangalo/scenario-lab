## In practice  
We are declaring AI compute a strategic infrastructure class, equivalent to energy or defence. Under Article 122 TFEU and the existing framework of the Chips Act, we are activating emergency powers to override national permitting delays, prioritise grid connections for certified AI facilities, and unlock €18 billion in blended financing from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, InvestEU, and the European Defence Fund (reclassified under dual-use AI). Three sites — in Finland, France, and Poland — are designated as priority zones for gigafactory-scale deployment, with modular nuclear and hydro partnerships to secure clean baseload. Control is non-negotiable: any operator benefiting from this regime must sign a sovereignty covenant — no foreign veto rights, no cloud backdoors, no inference without EU-registered telemetry.

We are negotiating directly with ASML, Infineon, and STMicroelectronics to fast-track lithography and packaging capacity, and with NVIDIA, Microsoft, and AWS to bind their EU expansions to these terms. The offer is clear: you can scale here, faster than anywhere, but only if the machines stay under European law. Resistance is expected — particularly from member states reliant on US cloud providers and from privacy advocates wary of state-controlled compute. We are countering with transparency: every facility will host public auditors from the European AI Office and independent researchers under vetted access. This is not nationalisation — it is sovereignty through partnership, and it is the only way to break our 10.0 dependence before capability outpaces us entirely. We are acting under the commitment to autonomy, because autonomy without infrastructure is a slogan, not a strategy.
