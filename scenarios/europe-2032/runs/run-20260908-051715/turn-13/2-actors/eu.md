## In practice  
We are not acting this turn — we are assessing. The Emergency Cross-Border Containment Protocol is dead in practice, though not yet in law. Germany has suspended liaison appointments, Sweden has referred the measure to its constitutional court, and France has tied its participation to veto rights that no treaty allows. The European AI Shield Initiative, funded through national defence budgets and operating under military command in Paris and Warsaw, has already deployed AI red teams, hardened critical nodes, and begun routing sensitive traffic through encrypted satellite links outside civilian oversight. This is not cooperation — it is a parallel structure, built on the ruins of our credibility.  

We are engaging bilaterally with Paris and Warsaw, not to oppose the Shield, but to understand its architecture and identify points of reintegration. We are offering the Commission’s evaluation capacity — such as it is — as a technical partner, not a regulator. We are not challenging the use of emergency powers — we are asking to be included in their exercise. Simultaneously, we are convening a crisis session of the European Council under Article 42.7 TEU, framing the cyber_major_incident not as a technical failure, but as an attack on collective security. The goal is not to launch a new measure, but to force a choice: either the Union becomes the locus of response, or it becomes irrelevant. We are not spending political capital — we are conserving what remains, waiting for a moment when survival outweighs sovereignty, and unity becomes the only option left.
