### A Regime Proposed, But Not Yet Enforced

The European Commission formally proposed the Mandatory Pre-Release Evaluation Regime, aiming to assert control over high-capability AI systems before deployment. Framed as a response to the lab anomalies detected earlier in the year, the proposal mandates adversarial testing of models above 55.0 capability by ENISA and an independent Evaluation Consortium. Despite diplomatic outreach, resistance hardened among member states hosting major AI labs. Ireland and Sweden questioned the legal basis under Article 68, warning it could drive investment offshore. France, while supportive of safety, argued the regime would delay innovation during a critical window.

Market access leverage proved insufficient to secure early alignment. U.S. developers signaled non-compliance unless bilateral concessions were negotiated, and Chinese firms dismissed the framework entirely. Within the EU, only partial consensus emerged: Germany, Belgium, and Finland backed the initiative, but Poland and Hungary refused to engage, citing sovereignty concerns. The red-teaming toolkit upgrades and Gigafactories co-location offers failed to shift their stance.

Meanwhile, AI capability advanced steadily to 58.0, driven by self-reinforcing gains in software engineering and scientific reasoning. Open-weight models rose to 46.0, as limited new releases filtered through governance bottlenecks. No safety improvements landed on deployed systems — the new regime remains in flight and yields no benefit yet. Resilience dipped slightly as the Emergency Resilience Surge failed to achieve full implementation ahead of its completion turn. Labour displacement continued to erode public trust, with sentiment falling to 43.0 despite the Labour Transition Accelerator’s rollout.

Political capital declined sharply under the weight of four large concurrent measures and a named priority. The proposal bonus for addressing recent disruptions did not materialize, as the lab anomaly was deemed insufficiently public to trigger one-off gains. Sovereignty weakened further as global advances outpaced domestic build-out, with no capacity event to offset the −1 decay from capability growth.
