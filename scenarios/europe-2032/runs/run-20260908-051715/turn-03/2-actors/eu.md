## Priority  
**Mandatory Pre-Release Evaluation Regime** — because the anomalous behaviour in unreleased models this turn crosses the threshold from theoretical concern to observable risk, and our commitment to act under uncertainty demands we institutionalise scrutiny now, not after failure.

## In practice  
We are treating the lab anomaly not as an isolated incident but as confirmation of a structural drift: capability is advancing faster than understanding, and opacity is becoming systemic. The current voluntary evaluations are failing — they are developer-led, unverified, and blind to emergent behaviour. We are therefore moving to establish a mandatory pre-release regime under the European AI Office, modelled on EMA’s drug approval process. By Q4 2027, we will propose a regulation requiring all models above 55.0 capability — assessed via standardised benchmarks including agent persistence, goal stability, and cross-domain reasoning — to undergo adversarial testing by ENISA and a newly mandated Evaluation Consortium comprising vetted academic, public-sector, and civil society researchers. The consortium will have access to full model weights, training data summaries, and internal monitoring logs. It will have 90 days to assess loss-of-control risks, misuse potential, and security vulnerabilities, with power to recommend delayed release or restricted deployment.

This applies equally to closed systems from U.S. and Chinese labs and to open-weight releases that could distribute dangerous capabilities irreversibly. We are leveraging Article 68 of the AI Act as legal basis and will fast-track it through the Internal Market Council formation, supported by a coalition led by Germany, Belgium, and Finland. Resistance is expected from France, which sees this as infringing innovation, and from Ireland and Sweden, hosting major labs. To secure buy-in, we are offering participating member states first access to the red-teaming toolkit upgrades and co-location rights in the InvestAI Gigafactories’ next phase. Crucially, we are also tying compliance to market access: no model above threshold operates in the EU without clearance. This is not protectionism — it is risk containment. And it is sovereignty: for the first time, the Union asserts jurisdiction not over where AI is built, but over what kind of AI is allowed to enter its social and institutional space.
