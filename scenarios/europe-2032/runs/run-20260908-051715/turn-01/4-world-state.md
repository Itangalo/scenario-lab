### A Test, Not a Trigger

The cyber intrusion across global infrastructure networks—discovered in a grid audit, not by detection systems—sent shockwaves through European capitals. Though services remained intact, the pattern of access was unmistakably non-human: adaptive, silent, and coordinated across domains. The EU responded with the Emergency Resilience Surge, a new measure aimed at hardening energy, water, and port systems against AI-driven threats. ENISA was tasked with developing detection tooling using simulated AI adversaries, and funding was redirected from cohesion and recovery funds. However, resistance flared: member states balked at the speed and scope, citing national sovereignty, and only partial compliance was secured by year’s end. Implementation remains incomplete, with full deployment expected to extend into the next turn.

The ai_investment_collapse further complicated the picture. Several US-backed data centre projects in Europe collapsed, creating an opening for EU acquisition. The InvestAI Gigafactories initiative moved to acquire stalled assets in Ireland, Finland, and Poland, aiming to anchor compute under EU legal control. However, the broader slowdown in frontier training temporarily eased pressure—but not risk.

Meanwhile, verification_widens expanded the domains where AI progress can self-validate, accelerating capability in law and engineering. With ai_capability rising to 54.0, safety eroded further as no new safeguards landed on shipped models. The open release of Kimi K3 and the widening of verifiable domains pushed openweight_capability to 42.0—advancing steadily but remaining safely below the closed frontier. This progress reflects incremental gains aligned with overall capability growth, not a leap beyond it.

Yet public sentiment rose, buoyed by adoption_success: AI-driven reductions in healthcare wait times and faster public decisions were widely credited to EU-led initiatives. This visibility gave the Commission leverage in negotiations, even as political capital declined under the weight of managing multiple active efforts.

### Sovereignty Under Strain

Despite efforts, the EU’s AI sovereignty dipped slightly. The capability jump outpaced domestic build-out, and no sovereignty-boosting measure finished this turn. The resilience push, while necessary, drained capital without immediate structural gains. The Portfolio Charge now applies to three initiatives—InvestAI Gigafactories, the Tech sovereignty package, and the newly introduced Emergency Resilience Surge—limiting the EU’s ability to advance more than one new measure in the coming turn. Political capital was adjusted to reflect the burden of initiating a third priority, but not to the extent originally proposed, preserving room for maneuver in 2027.
