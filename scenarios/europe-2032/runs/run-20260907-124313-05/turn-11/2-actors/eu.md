## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: one defensive, one foundational. The **Critical Function Sentinel Deployment** is now live in Germany, France, and Poland, with technical teams from the AI Office embedded in national cybersecurity agencies to accelerate integration. We are using the Digital Europe Programme to fund interoperability bridges between sentinel agents and existing SOC systems, prioritising hospital networks and grid operators where the risk of cascading failure is highest. Resistance from AI developers remains sharp — particularly from U.S.-based labs whose models are being monitored — but we are holding firm on Article 17 of the AI Act as our legal basis, while offering a new concession: any model that passes twelve consecutive months of sentinel monitoring will be granted a "trusted deployment" status under the AI Liability Directive, reducing its operator’s liability exposure by 40%. This is not a bribe; it is a signal that safety, when demonstrated, will be rewarded.

Simultaneously, the **Joint Threat Verification Framework** responds to the synthetic crisis spillover that rattled financial markets. We are convening a crisis council of central bank CTOs, Europol, and ENISA to build a unified verification layer that cryptographically signs all official communications and cross-checks media against a distributed ledger of verified source material. This will be piloted first with the ECB and national finance ministries, using €380 million from the Crisis Response Instrument. The framework will integrate AI-driven deepfake detection, but crucially, it will not rely on it — final verification will require multi-party attestation, ensuring that no single system can be spoofed. We are also negotiating with major news platforms to adopt the standard voluntarily, offering regulatory leniency in return. This is not just about stopping the next fake speech — it is about rebuilding the substrate of trust that AI has eroded.
