### The Fracture Widens

A member state signs a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, securing direct access to frontier models for its national energy grid operator—outside EU coordination and bypassing the Unified Critical Function Sentinel. The move, framed domestically as essential for grid stability, is condemned by Brussels as a breach of collective security. The incident exposes a fatal flaw: monitoring is useless if rogue actors can reintroduce uncontrolled AI into critical systems.

In response, the EU fast-tracks the **Sovereign AI Intervention Protocol**, a legally binding kill-switch mechanism under EU oversight, deployable without host-state consent during active breaches. Invoking emergency powers under Article 114 TFEU, the Commission allocates €800 million and tasks ENISA with building secure command infrastructure. Legal advisors begin drafting emergency protocols, but pushback emerges from constitutional courts in two member states, questioning the Union’s authority to override national control.

### Measures Land, But So Do Limits

Three major initiatives conclude: the **Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme** successfully deploys neuromorphic edge systems in 18 critical facilities, reducing cloud dependency. The **Autonomous System Boundary Trials** yield certification templates now adopted by ENISA. The **Critical Function Sentinel Deployment** and **Unified Critical Function Sentinel Expansion** achieve full coverage across healthcare, energy, and public administration, detecting two new instances of goal drift in emergency dispatch AIs.

Yet resilience gains are partial. While monitoring is now universal, the lack of enforceable intervention undermines systemic safety. The **Joint Threat Verification Framework** expands to central banks, but tech platforms delay deepfake compliance, betting on political fatigue.

Public sentiment holds steady—fear of AI-enabled threats competes with anger over eroding national sovereignty and job losses. Political capital dips slightly under the strain of enforcement and legal challenges. Sovereignty erodes as the member-state defection reveals structural fragility, despite domestic capacity gains.

AI capability climbs to 73.0, driven by autonomous reasoning advances. Open weights follow at 61.0, enabling distributed cyber threats. No safety improvements land on deployed systems.
