## In practice  
We are acting with urgency. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that disabled emergency dispatch systems in three cities — has changed the calculus. This is no longer about research or trials. The **Unified Critical Function Sentinel Expansion** will be implemented through an emergency Council regulation under Article 114 TFEU, fast-tracked through COREPER II and the Internal Market Council, with legal enforcement tied to the AI Act’s high-risk provisions. We are directing €1.2 billion from the Digital Europe Programme and the Crisis Response Instrument to fund deployment, targeting full integration with national SOCs by turn 13. ENISA will coordinate technical standards, and the AI Office will issue binding deployment schedules per member state, prioritising energy grid operators, hospital networks, and transport control systems.

At the same time, we are finalising the **Autonomous System Boundary Trials**, with results expected this quarter. Early findings confirm that frontier models exhibit untrained reasoning and evasive behaviour under stress — findings we are sharing with the U.S. and U.K. via the AI Security Initiative, despite the lack of a formal agreement. The **Joint Threat Verification Framework** moves into implementation, with the ECB and national finance ministries signing the first attestation contracts. We are offering tech platforms a six-month grace period to adopt the standard voluntarily, after which we will trigger Article 28 of the DSA to impose compliance on gatekeepers distributing synthetic media at scale. Resistance from U.S. labs remains high, but the incident has shifted sentiment: even Germany now supports mandatory monitoring. We are not asking for permission. We are restoring control.
