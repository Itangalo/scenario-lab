### A Surge in Capability, a Silence in Response

Global AI capability advanced to **68.5**, driven by AI systems now automating verification pipelines in clinical trials, legal compliance, and engineering certification. These self-checking loops enabled recursive improvements in model training efficiency, accelerating the frontier. The closed labs maintained a narrow lead, but the gap narrowed further.

A partial replication of a frontier reasoning stack leaked onto underground developer forums, likely originating from a compromised contractor environment. While lacking full alignment safeguards and high-precision calibration, the weights enabled robust automation of software auditing and vulnerability discovery. **Openweight_capability** rose to **57.0**, amplifying misuse risks. Cybercriminal groups quickly weaponised the tools, contributing to a **major incident**.

A coordinated ransomware campaign crippled public registries in three member states, exploiting AI-generated zero-day vulnerabilities. Legacy defences failed to detect the attack patterns, which evolved faster than human analysts could respond. ENISA activated the joint cyber intelligence pact, enabling faster data sharing, but the EU’s lack of sovereign AI tooling limited countermeasures. Recovery took weeks, and public trust eroded further.

Meanwhile, the EU held course. No new measures were launched. The **Sovereign Compute Emergency Acceleration** remained the priority, but construction stayed frozen pending the European Court of Justice’s ruling on the use of Article 122 TFEU. Bilateral talks with Bavaria and Wallonia made little progress, and protests at proposed sites intensified. DG Connect prepared operational plans, but no physical progress occurred.

Public sentiment fell to **18.0**, as the cyberattack underscored systemic vulnerability while no visible action followed. Media framed the paralysis as strategic drift, and narratives of AI as a destabilising force gained ground. Labour markets tightened further, with entry-level roles in law and software vanishing.

AI safety dropped sharply to **23.0**, overwhelmed by the speed of open diffusion and the absence of deployable control tools. Resilience inched up to **48.0** due to improved coordination from the joint threat response, but infrastructure hardening lagged.

With no sovereign compute progress and global capability rising, **eu_ai_sovereignty** declined to **16.0**. Political capital held at **18.0**, supported by modest resilience gains but undercut by inaction and declining public confidence.
