## New measure
**Cyber Resilience Emergency Programme**  
Launch a time-bound, high-intensity effort to harden critical public infrastructure against AI-generated cyber attacks, starting with energy, transport, and health systems.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 10–15 points through rapid deployment of AI-driven intrusion detection, automated patching systems, and mandatory red-teaming of critical nodes; slow the erosion of public trust after the cyber_major_incident.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with binding requirements for national operators of essential services and direct funding and technical support from the EU.

## Priority  
**Cyber Resilience Emergency Programme** — because the cyber_major_incident proves that offensive capability has already outpaced defence, and without immediate action to contain the damage and restore confidence, public sentiment will collapse and political capital will evaporate, undermining all other measures including our two-year commitment.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that the attack was not just serious—it was *generated*. The tooling used in the ransomware sweep across public services was not human-authored; it was model-synthesised, adaptive, and targeted. This is not a breach of security but a breach of the operating assumption: we can no longer assume that defences scale linearly against human-limited threats. The cyber_major_incident has exposed a gap not just in systems but in imagination. Our resilience metric was already low at 38.0, and without urgent intervention, it will fall further—dragging down public sentiment and, with it, our ability to act at all.

This turn, the Commission activates the Civil Protection Mechanism and redirects €12bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Recovery and Resilience Facility to launch the Cyber Resilience Emergency Programme. We issue a draft directive under Article 114 TFEU, mandating AI-powered intrusion detection and automated response systems in all designated essential services by the end of turn 5. ENISA is empowered to conduct unannounced red-teaming exercises, and a new Joint Cyber Defence Taskforce—staffed by national CERTs, Europol, and vetted private experts—will coordinate patching and response. Member states that comply by turn 4 will receive fast-tracked cohesion funding; those that resist will face infringement procedures. We are also negotiating emergency access to defensive AI tools from compliant developers under the Secure the open frontier framework—offering temporary liability shields in exchange for real-time threat sharing. This is not business as usual: we are treating this as a systemic emergency, because if we do not, the next incident may not be recoverable.
