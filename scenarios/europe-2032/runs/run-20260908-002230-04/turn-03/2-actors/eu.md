## New measure  
**Emergency Access to Defensive AI via Public Evaluation Pool**  
We activate emergency access rights to deploy defensive AI tools from compliant developers through the emergent EU eval pool, in exchange for temporary liability shields and real-time threat sharing.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by 5–8 points by enabling rapid evaluation and deployment of defensive models against AI-synthesised threats; strengthen oversight of open-weight models via the emergent eval pool.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, compliant AI developers, and public research institutions participating in the emergent EU eval pool.

## Priority  
**Cyber Resilience Emergency Programme** — because the systemic ransomware incident has already breached our defences, and without immediate hardening of critical infrastructure, all other measures — including our two-year commitment — will be overtaken by cascading failures and collapsing public trust.

## In practice  
We are acting on two realities: the attack was AI-generated, and our defences were human-paced. The Cyber Resilience Emergency Programme is now operational under Article 114 TFEU, with draft directives circulating in COREPER and fast-tracked through the Internal Market Council. ENISA has launched its first unannounced red-teaming cycle across ten critical energy nodes, and the Joint Cyber Defence Taskforce is coordinating patching in real time, drawing on expertise from Germany’s BSI, France’s ANSSI, and Europol’s EC3. €12bn has been redirected from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with disbursement tied to compliance milestones: member states implementing AI-driven intrusion detection by turn 4 will unlock cohesion funding tranches.

At the same time, we are leveraging the emergent EU eval pool — a coalition of public cloud providers and research institutions — to fast-track evaluation of defensive AI tools. Under a new emergency protocol, developers who submit models to the pool gain temporary liability protection and access to anonymised threat data from recent attacks, in exchange for real-time monitoring and containment guarantees. This is not a permanent waiver: it is a time-bound, condition-limited mechanism to close the gap between offensive capability and defensive readiness. The Commission is negotiating with three major developers under this framework, focusing on adaptive patch-generation and zero-day detection models. We are not waiting for full sovereignty to act — we are using the collective technical capacity we *do* have to stabilise the present, while our industrial measures build the future.
