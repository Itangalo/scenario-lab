## New measure  
None this turn.  
We are waiting for the European Court of Justice’s ruling on the use of Article 122 TFEU to enable emergency sovereign compute development, which will determine whether the Union can override member-state objections in strategic infrastructure projects — a threshold question for both sovereignty and political agency.

## Priority  
Sovereign Compute Emergency Acceleration — because without a legal pathway to deploy sovereign compute at speed, no other measure in AI safety, resilience or control can be sustained independently, and because the Court’s imminent judgment will define the outer boundary of what the Union is still capable of deciding for itself.

## In practice  
We are not advancing physically, but we are not idle. DG Connect, with ENISA and EIT Digital, continues to refine operational readiness for the Soria and Silesia sites: grid integration plans are stress-tested, physical security protocols are aligned with NATO CIIP standards, and workforce pipelines are mapped through regional innovation partnerships. The €10bn cohesion top-up remains ring-fenced, but disbursement is legally contingent on the ECJ’s judgment. To move now would invite annulment and deepen fragmentation — a risk we cannot bear at 16.0 political capital and 14.0 public sentiment.

Behind the scenes, the Presidency maintains quiet bilateral channels with Bavaria and Wallonia. We are not negotiating concessions, but outlining compliance pathways: conditional hosting agreements with co-governance on security, guaranteed regional energy rebates, and digital job quotas tied to site operations. These are not rewards for obstruction, but scaffolding for unity — should the Court affirm our legal authority. If it does not, we will have to confront a new reality: that the Union cannot build what it must defend on its own soil, and that sovereignty must be reconstituted through supply chains, talent, and access — not infrastructure. But that is not now. We wait. We prepare. We do not act blindly — not when the cost of failure is the end of credible action.
