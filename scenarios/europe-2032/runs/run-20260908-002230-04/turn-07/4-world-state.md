### A Denial That Reshapes Strategy

Global AI capability advanced to **63.5**, as frontier labs refined agent coordination and training efficiency, pushing autonomous systems deeper into scientific and engineering workflows. The open frontier followed: **openweight_capability** rose to **52.5**, reflecting wider replication of agent-grade models after the redteam exploit demonstration. While still lagging, the gap narrows—dangerously.

The EU’s emergency invocation of Article 122 TFEU to fast-track AI infrastructure was met with immediate backlash. Though legally grounded, the move was politically explosive. Two member states launched constitutional challenges, accusing the Commission of overreach. Regional protests disrupted site preparations, and several national parliaments passed non-binding resolutions condemning the erosion of local consent. The **Sovereign Compute Emergency Acceleration** was declared finished this turn, but no physical progress was made—only legal and procedural groundwork laid.

Meanwhile, the **InvestAI Gigafactories** officially concluded, yet remained unbuilt. The **Tech sovereignty package** and **Secure the open frontier** also closed, with patchy results: private investment fell far short, and enforcement of open-weight thresholds remains inconsistent. The **Cyber Resilience Emergency Programme** ended just as a new wave of AI-assisted intrusions targeted municipal networks—detected early but not blocked—exposing residual fragility.

Worst of all, the Union was abruptly locked out of the leading AI model. Hospitals relying on it for diagnostics, ministries for crisis simulation, and firms for R&D were left scrambling. No explanation was given. The cutoff was total, sudden, and humiliating—a stark demonstration of strategic dependence.

The EU responded by doubling down on sovereignty, naming **Sovereign Compute Emergency Acceleration** as its priority. But political capital, though slightly boosted by finishing four measures, remains strained. Public sentiment dipped further, as media framed the data centre push as elitist and environmentally reckless, while job losses in legal, design, and administrative sectors mounted.

Resilience eroded under unmitigated cyber pressure, and AI safety declined as capability surged ahead of oversight. The Union’s leverage remains theoretical—its sovereignty untested, its unity fraying.

### The Cost of Standing Still

The emergency measures may have bought time, but not trust. The legal battles have just begun, and the social contract around AI is fraying.
