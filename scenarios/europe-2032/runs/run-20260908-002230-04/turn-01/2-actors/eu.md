New measure
**Secure the open frontier**  
Establish a binding EU-wide threshold for open-weight model releases, requiring pre-release evaluation and capability containment measures for models above a defined capability level.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Reduce the rate at which openweight_capability converges on ai_capability; slow uncontrolled diffusion of offensive capabilities; strengthen EU leverage in developer negotiations.  
`Applies to:` The frontier developers directly, with enforcement through EU market access and export controls.

Priority  
**Secure the open frontier** — because the openweight_frontier_release event proves the window to govern what is loose is closing fast, and without binding limits on open releases, sovereignty and resilience investments will be outpaced by distributed risk.

In practice  
We are acting on the fact that capability is now permanently distributed: Kimi K3’s release means offensive cyber tools are downloadable and beyond recall. The United States has responded with unilateral control, but we cannot replicate that without our own industrial base and political cohesion. Our leverage lies not in imitation but in conditionality — access to the EU market, its talent, and its upstream supply chain — which we now bind to responsible release practices.  

This turn, the Commission drafts a regulation under the AI Act’s general-purpose AI provisions, defining a capability threshold (aligned with 45.0 on the index) above which open-weight releases must undergo third-party evaluation and demonstrate containment of dangerous capabilities. The legal base is Article 28b as amended, leveraging the high-risk framework but applying it upstream. We coordinate with ENISA and the EU AI Office to build evaluation capacity, while engaging developers early to avoid deadlock — offering fast-track certification for compliance, and using ASML-related leverage to ensure cooperation from US and Chinese firms dependent on EU lithography access. Resistance will come from open science advocates and cloud providers, but we offer transparency-for-access: compliant developers gain preferential procurement status and faster permitting under the Tech Sovereignty Package. The measure is large, costly, and urgent — but without it, every other investment risks being overtaken by what is already loose.
