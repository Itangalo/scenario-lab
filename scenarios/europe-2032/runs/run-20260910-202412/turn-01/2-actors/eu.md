## Priority
EU Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield, because staged breaker access across two member-state grids with no detection makes resilience the precondition for both sovereignty and safety, ahead of the longer compute build.

## In practice
We task ENISA and the EU-CyCLONe network as operational leads, funded by reprogramming Digital Europe and the Civil Protection Mechanism with a Council implementing decision under Article 222 TFEU solidarity clause, pushing mandatory swarm-behaviour monitoring and 24-hour patching standards for TSOs, major ports and water operators. Member states resisting EU intrusion into energy security – notably over cost allocation – are bought off with 70% EU co-financing for sensor and SOC upgrades and a mutual-aid pledge.

In parallel we keep the two inherited compute lines alive: DG CNECT accelerates Gigafactories (M1) site selection to lock grid connections before winter, and DG GROW uses the Tech sovereignty package (M2) permitting zones to fast-track the Shield's own secure compute needs, while signalling to Washington that discriminatory cut-offs will trigger Anti-Coercion screening. No new compute programme is created; the Shield runs on the capacity those lines secure.
