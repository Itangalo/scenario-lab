### The sweep
Autumn brought the attack everyone had warned about in abstract. A largely automated ransomware sweep, built with freely available tooling, moved through municipal systems, hospital administration and local public services in a matter of days. Screens went dark in town halls, appointment systems locked, backup generators and radios suddenly mattered more than dashboards.

Attribution was absent. Recovery was uneven. Where segmentation and kill-switch drills had been rehearsed, networks stayed clean. Where paper systems were already in use, the damage was less digital than operational: exhausted staff, closed wards, ambulances diverted again.

Days later, a new openly downloadable model family appeared, within touching distance of the best closed systems. It was mirrored hundreds of thousands of times before any regulator commented. Whatever had powered the sweep was now permanently out of reach.

### Corps on invitation
Brussels answered with field teams rather than new law. The newly created Restoration Corps — joint civil protection, health emergency and cybersecurity crews with air-gapped restore kits, backup power and clean triage partitions — deployed on invitation to the hardest-hit municipalities.

The rule of no forced reconnection held. Teams patched in place, restored local backups, kept wards running off-grid. In a dozen pact networks and two showcase cities, waiting lists briefly moved again and daily ward-level dashboards showed clean networks holding through the wave.

It was not enough to change the story. Clinician-led lawsuits over forced central triage and unpatched vendor software advanced in three states, vendors kept co-financing frozen, and most municipal networks waited for court rulings. Rumours of a coordinated judicial moratorium on reconnection and talk of cross-border walkouts circulated in staff rooms and single-source press reports, unconfirmed but persistent.

Visible help bought narrow credit amid broad anger at failing care and machine-made disruption. Trust did not return; collapse was contained where crews stood.
