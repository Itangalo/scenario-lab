### Isolation gates hold, wards stay empty
The isolation and lockdown programme finished this half-year. On paper it delivered: segmented hospital networks, enforced kill-switch audits, rogue-agent containment playbooks adopted by ENISA teams. Where crews were present, municipal systems stopped re-seeding infection and stayed clean through the spring.

Few patients saw it. Municipal hospital networks in three states stayed on paper and local systems, citing court protection. Their near-miss dossiers — delayed triage, diverted ambulances, night shifts run by hand — became coordinated lawsuits led by clinicians against vendors and health ministries. The suits named forced central triage and unpatched vendor software as the cause. Vendors froze co-financing; mayors refused reconnection until gates were proven in someone else's ward.

### Two funds, one refusal
Brussels pushed consent and proof at once. Mediation teams offered the Continuity Pact ward by ward: funded air-gapped triage bundles on reserved EuroHPC capacity, clinician override in writing, recovery money tied to telemetry fixes, no forced reconnection.

Uptake inched from a handful to a dozen networks, but the majority waited for court outcomes. Patch teams reported gates technically met; clinicians reported trust not met.

At the same time the new Replication Fund tried to copy what did work — two cities where EU-hosted models cut waiting lists and permit times, with dashboards branding the gains as European delivery. Local press covered the wins, then cut to the lawsuits on the same bulletin. The benefit story was real but isolated, and drowned by the care-failure narrative.

With coffers thin and legitimacy thinner, the priority on consent changed little on the ground. Money moved slowly, conditions multiplied, and private partners stayed out.
