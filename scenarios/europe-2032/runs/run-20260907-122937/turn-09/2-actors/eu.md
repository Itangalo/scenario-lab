## In practice
This turn, we close the Emergency Resilience Reinforcement as planned, with ENISA certifying 78% compliance across Tier 1 grid operators. The remaining gaps in Hungary and Poland are now tied to Cohesion Fund disbursements, making non-compliance costly. The hardening has already proven its worth: during the emergent_grid_instability_surge, manual overrides prevented cascades, and the red-team drills conducted in France and the Netherlands provided the muscle memory for rapid response. We are now treating resilience not as a technical checklist but as a continuous operational posture — and we are extending that logic to democracy itself.

The annulment of an election on grounds of AI-amplified disinformation — even if the causal link is contested — changes the game. We can no longer treat democratic processes as outside the attack surface. Under Article 7a of the European Democracy Action Plan and the newly activated crisis clause in the Digital Services Act, we are launching the Emergency Democratic Integrity Protocol. This means real-time fusion cells linking ENISA, EEAS and national electoral bodies to detect coordinated manipulation patterns; mandatory rapid disclosure of synthetic media used in political ads; and a standing communication protocol for public broadcasters to issue joint alerts during election periods. Funding of €220 million is drawn from the Internal Security Fund and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with a new rapid-response envelope under the European Peace Facility for cross-border coordination. We are also fast-tracking the establishment of the European Centre for Disease Prevention of Democratic Resilience — a deliberately provocative name, to signal that we now treat democratic erosion as a systemic threat on par with pandemics.

At the same time, we press forward with the Algorithmic Sovereignty Initiative, but now under greater strain. The U.S. silence on our co-stewardship proposal confirms that we cannot outsource our security. We double down on the sparse model roadmap: the first prototypes from ASML and Infineon show 40% efficiency gains over dense architectures at equivalent performance, and we are now testing them in closed simulations for grid control and election monitoring. To counter member_state_defection, we offer Poland and Hungary early access — not as a concession, but as proof of concept: EU-built, EU-controlled, EU-secured. This is not just about compute. It is about trust. And trust must be earned, not decreed.
