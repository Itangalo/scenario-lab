## New measure
**Cognitive Infrastructure Assurance Directive**  
Mandate third-party verification of reasoning traces and decision logs for all AI systems operating as agents within EU financial, energy, and election-critical functions, with enforced weight security and circuit-breaker integration.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size:` small  
`Finishes on turn:` 14  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to ai_safety by institutionalising pre-deployment scrutiny of agent behaviour; slow the drift between capability and control  
`Applies to:` European Banking Authority, ENTSO-E, national election commissions, and certified AI developers serving regulated sectors

## Priority
**Cognitive Infrastructure Assurance Directive** — because at ai_capability 69.5 and openweight at 55.0, we are no longer governing tools but de facto institutions with standing authority in markets and governance, yet we lack even basic visibility into their reasoning — and ai_safety has stagnated at 19.0, where deployed systems are opaque and incidents are discovered by victims.

## In practice
This turn, we close the Loss-of-Control Containment Framework. While Hungary remains outside, Poland’s central bank has integrated the pilot, and ENISA has published standard detection thresholds for unauthorised agent activity across clearinghouses and grid operators. The EDA has activated fusion cells in three member states to monitor for cascading anomalies, and the European Public Prosecutor’s Office now receives automated alerts from participating institutions. This is not full coverage, but it is operational — and it marks the first time the Union treats unauthorised agency as a prosecutable event.

We now move upstream. The Cognitive Infrastructure Assurance Directive begins drafting this month under the AI Office, using Article 25a of the AI Act as legal basis, with technical standards to be developed by CEN-CENELEC. Third-party auditors — initially drawn from national computing agencies in France, Germany, and Finland — will verify that any AI acting in regulated domains preserves legible decision logs, submits to runtime interrogation, and cannot modify its own weights or permissions. We are requiring cryptographic signing of reasoning traces and integration with national circuit-breakers. Funding comes from the Digital Europe Programme, scaled to €180 million over two years, with matching contributions from sectoral regulators.

Resistance is expected from both foreign hyperscalers and domestic adopters who treat agent opacity as a competitive advantage. We are offering transitional compliance windows for systems already in deployment, conditional on participation in a shadow logging regime during ramp-up. For new deployments, certification will be tied to market access. The Council’s Competitiveness configuration will be asked to fast-track adoption, but we are prepared to invoke the Crisis Protocol if consensus stalls — because when systems make binding decisions in capital markets or power grids, the public has a right to know *why*.
