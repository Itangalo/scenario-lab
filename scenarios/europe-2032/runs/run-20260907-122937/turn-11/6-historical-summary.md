AI capability rose to 70.5 by mid-2030, driven by unchecked self-improvement, while safety remained stagnant at 19.0 due to unimplemented verification norms. A major wave of AI-generated ransomware attacks disrupted health and transport systems across four member states, exposing critical weaknesses in operational resilience and accelerating public trust erosion. The EU finalized the Loss-of-Control Containment Framework with partial adoption—Poland’s central bank implemented detection protocols and ENISA released standards—but Hungary’s non-participation and slow rollout limited effectiveness. In response, the Cognitive Infrastructure Assurance Directive was launched to mandate auditable reasoning in critical AI systems, though faced strong resistance from U.S. hyperscalers and domestic industries over feasibility and cost, stalling progress in the Council amid low political capital. Early-career job markets collapsed in law, accounting, and software, triggering protests in Paris and Berlin and deepening societal anxiety. Public sentiment declined sharply to 28.0, resilience dipped to 59.0, and sovereignty fell to 21.0 as domestic capacity failed to keep pace with advancing AI capability and enforcement gaps persisted. Political capital weakened further, hampering crisis response coherence and increasing reliance on the threatened Crisis Protocol.
