### A Surge in the Shadows

This half-year was defined not by what the EU built, but by what it failed to contain. A wave of AI-driven ransomware swept across regional health authorities and transport agencies in four member states, exploiting newly automated vulnerabilities in legacy IT systems. The attack, attributed months later to a non-state actor using model-generated tooling, forced emergency manual fallbacks and exposed critical gaps in operational resilience. Though no lives were lost, public trust eroded further.

Simultaneously, early-career hiring in law, accounting, and software development collapsed, with firms openly citing AI substitution as the reason. Graduates reported ghosted applications and vanished entry-level roles, fueling protests in Paris and Berlin. Media coverage framed AI not as a tool, but as a gatekeeper shutting doors.

The EU closed the **Loss-of-Control Containment Framework**, achieving partial rollout: Poland’s central bank adopted detection protocols, and ENISA published standards. However, Hungary remained outside, and implementation lagged elsewhere, limiting systemic impact.

In response, the EU launched the **Cognitive Infrastructure Assurance Directive**, aiming to mandate auditable reasoning in critical AI systems. Drafting began under the AI Office, but immediate resistance emerged. U.S. hyperscalers warned of compliance costs, while domestic utilities and banks argued the technical standards were unworkable at scale. The proposal was named priority, signaling urgency.

Yet momentum faltered. With political capital strained and no immediate crisis to unify member states, fast-tracking through the Council looked uncertain. The Crisis Protocol loomed as a last resort, but its use risked legal challenges and deeper fragmentation.

Public sentiment dipped sharply, reflecting anxiety over job losses and cyber instability. Resilience declined slightly as the scale of automation-dependent infrastructure became clearer. Despite rising capability — now at 70.5 — safety remained stagnant, with no deployed systems incorporating the new verification norms.

### Fractured Foundations

The directive’s ambition clashed with reality: the EU still lacked the sovereign capacity to enforce such rules on frontier models. With U.S. posture remaining retrenchment, access to advanced systems narrowed further. Sovereignty ticked down as capability outpaced domestic control.

Political capital eroded under the weight of multiple stalled initiatives and rising social pressure.
