The European Commission declared an emergency in democratic integrity after an election was annulled due to AI-driven manipulation, triggering the AI Act’s Crisis Protocol and launching an Implementing Regulation requiring real-time logging, verification, and watermarking for political AI systems. €90 million was allocated to deploy monitoring cells across member states. However, enforcement weakened under resistance: U.S. tech giants challenged jurisdiction, and Germany, Netherlands, and Sweden blocked coercive measures pending legal review. Major platforms adopted only partial transparency, while unverified content persisted. The Cognitive Infrastructure Assurance Directive remained stalled, and no progress was made on securing critical sectors. Open-weight models with offensive capabilities continued spreading, fueling disinformation, cyberattacks, and grid disruptions. Public trust eroded further as protests linked the democratic crisis to broader algorithmic harms and job losses. AI capabilities advanced toward autonomous multi-domain operations, widening the gap between technological power and regulatory control, with open models progressing incrementally through leaked architectures but not surpassing frontier systems.
