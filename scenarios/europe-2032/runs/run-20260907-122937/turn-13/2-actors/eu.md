## New measure  
### Invoke Crisis Protocol for Democratic Integrity Safeguards  
Emergency binding rules on AI in elections and political communication to prevent cascading democratic collapse.  

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` Raise ai_safety by forcing transparency and control in politically sensitive systems; slow unauthorised agent deployment in election infrastructure; begin stabilising public_sentiment by demonstrating decisive action.  
`Applies to:` All AI systems used in election administration, political advertising, or public information distribution within EU member states, including foreign-hosted systems with domestic impact.

## Priority  
**Invoke Crisis Protocol for Democratic Integrity Safeguards** — because the annulment of an election due to AI-facilitated manipulation is not just a security failure, it is an existential crisis for the Union’s legitimacy, and we cannot wait for the Council to agree while democratic foundations erode.

## In practice  
This turn, we act. The annulment of an election on grounds of AI-driven manipulation is the line we said we would not cross — and now it has been crossed. We invoke the Crisis Protocol under Article 28 of the AI Act, citing “imminent and systemic risk to democratic integrity” as justification for emergency measures. The AI Office, in coordination with the European Democracy Action Plan and the European Centre for Electoral Support, drafts an Implementing Regulation within 72 hours, mandating: real-time logging and third-party verification of all AI systems used in voter registration, ballot counting, and election result transmission; a moratorium on unverified generative AI in political advertising; and mandatory watermarking and provenance tracking for all political content distributed via major platforms operating in the EU.

We deploy emergency funding from the Contingency Margin — €90 million — to stand up rapid monitoring cells in all member states, staffed by ENISA, the European Digital Media Observatory, and national electoral commissions, with authority to issue takedown orders and public alerts. We activate the Anti-Coercion Instrument in parallel, notifying U.S. hyperscalers that non-compliance with the new rules — including systems hosted abroad but targeting EU electorates — will trigger graduated countermeasures, starting with data access restrictions and escalating to market restrictions. Germany’s finance ministry resists, warning of economic fallout, but we frame this as non-negotiable: if the market fears democratic instability, it should support the remedy, not block it. We offer a six-week compliance window, not six months. This is not negotiation. It is emergency defence.
