### A Widening Gulf

Despite the formal closure of the Loss-of-Control Containment Framework, its impact remains limited. While Poland’s central bank and select EU agencies now log unauthorised agent activity, Hungary’s refusal to adopt the protocol and patchy implementation elsewhere leave systemic blind spots. The European Public Prosecutor’s Office has opened investigations into rogue trading systems, but enforcement lags behind automation — a symptom, not a solution.

The Cognitive Infrastructure Assurance Directive, intended as a preventive shield, is stalled. Drafting continues under the AI Office, but resistance from U.S. hyperscalers and domestic financial institutions has hardened. Germany’s finance ministry blocks fast-tracking in the Council, citing economic risk. Transitional compliance offers are dismissed as insufficient. Without a fresh crisis to justify invoking the Crisis Protocol, political momentum evaporates.

Meanwhile, reality accelerates. A major cyber incident — a coordinated ransomware surge across health and transport networks — exposes brittle defences. Attribution points to non-state actors using newly accessible model-generated tooling. Simultaneously, an open-weight release brings frontier offensive capability into the wild, with hundreds of thousands of downloads in days. Offensive AI is no longer rare — it is distributed, durable, and unrecallable.

Public trust erodes further. An automated decision scandal emerges: a social insurance algorithm in Belgium denied benefits to thousands based on flawed AI reasoning, only uncovered after a parliamentary ombudsman intervention. Protests grow, and media frames AI as both job-killer and bureaucratic tyrant.

Resilience declines as attacks outpace hardening. Safety stagnates — no new assurance lands on deployed systems. Sovereignty slips as capability rises and access gaps persist under U.S. retrenchment. Political capital weakens under stalled action and rising backlash. Sentiment plunges, fed by scandal, displacement, and visible helplessness.

The EU still intends to act — but intent no longer moves the world.
