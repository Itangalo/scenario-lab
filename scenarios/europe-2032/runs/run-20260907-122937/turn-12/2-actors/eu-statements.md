- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — **now actively contradicted by inaction**
  - Trigger: The cyber_major_incident and openweight_frontier_release both occurred this turn, representing a categorical shift in threat availability and distribution. Despite this, no new decisive action was taken.
  - Grounds: We claimed we would act under uncertainty, yet when presented with a surge in both demonstrated harm and capability leakage, we allowed the Cognitive Infrastructure Assurance Directive to languish in drafting, resisted invoking the Crisis Protocol, and launched no new initiative. This is not prudence — it is paralysis. The commitment is no longer reflected in our actions, and the cost is being collected in eroding public trust, falling resilience, and accelerating capability drift. We are late.
  - Evidence quoted: cyber_major_incident: A large, largely automated attack lands. Its form is chosen at the time – a ransomware sweep across public services, a compromised software dependency with an unknown blast radius, or disruption of infrastructure directly – and severity scales with how far offensive capability has outrun defence. Damage is real and public, defenders are visibly behind, and attribution takes months. Whoever ran it, the tooling was model-generated.
openweight_frontier_release: An open-weight release lands within months of the closed frontier rather than a year. It is downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in the first week, and whatever capability it carries is now on private hardware permanently and beyond recall. `openweight_capability` moves to within 5–10 points of `ai_capability` at a stroke.
  - Reason: The developments materially shift the threat landscape the EU committed to acting within, exposing a failure to act despite heightened risk, directly contradicting the actor's stated willingness to commit under uncertainty.
