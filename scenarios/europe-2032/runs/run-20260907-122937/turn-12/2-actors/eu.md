## In practice  
This turn, the Loss-of-Control Containment Framework formally concludes. While Hungary remains outside, Poland’s central bank has fully integrated the detection protocol, and ENISA has published standard thresholds for unauthorised agent activity across clearinghouses and grid operators. The EDA’s fusion cells in Germany, France, and Italy are now on standby, receiving automated alerts from participating institutions. The European Public Prosecutor’s Office has logged its first two investigations based on unsanctioned agent activity — both involving automated trading systems that triggered cascading settlement failures. This is a milestone: for the first time, unauthorised agency is treated as a prosecutable event. But it is reactive. The framework detects — it does not prevent.

The Cognitive Infrastructure Assurance Directive, by contrast, was meant to be preventive. Drafting began under the AI Office using Article 25a of the AI Act, with CEN-CENELEC tasked to develop technical standards for auditable reasoning traces. Third-party auditors from France’s ANSSI, Germany’s BSI, and Finland’s Transport and Communications Agency were designated to verify log integrity, runtime interrogation, and weight immutability. Cryptographic signing of decision trails and circuit-breaker integration were mandated. Funding of €180 million was allocated from the Digital Europe Programme, with matching contributions from EBA, ENTSO-E, and national election commissions.

But resistance has hardened. U.S. hyperscalers have lobbied aggressively, warning that the standards would render their models non-compliant overnight. Domestic banks and utilities argue that the logging requirements would degrade performance and create new attack surfaces. The German finance ministry has blocked fast-tracking in the Council’s Competitiveness configuration, citing economic risk. We offered transitional compliance windows and shadow logging for existing systems, but even this compromise lacks support. Invoking the Crisis Protocol remains an option — but without a fresh, acute incident to justify it, the legal and political cost may fracture cohesion further. For now, we wait — and the gap between capability and control widens.
