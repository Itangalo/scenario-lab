## New measure  
**Measure:** Strengthen the Middle-Power AI Coalition (MPAC) with binding evaluation reciprocity and joint compute access guarantees  
This measure institutionalises the emergent coordination framework among middle powers by locking in mutual recognition of safety evaluations and co-governance of shared compute reserves, reducing dependency on unilateral frontier access.

`Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `eu_ai_sovereignty` by 8–12 points by securing guaranteed access to evaluated foreign compute; strengthen `eu_political_capital` by demonstrating strategic cohesion and delivering tangible leverage; lay groundwork for future restrictions by creating a collective enforcement base  
`Applies to:` The EU and coalition partners (Canada, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and select Nordic and Benelux-aligned states) through a binding intergovernmental agreement outside the single market legal order

## Priority  
**Priority:** Strengthen the Middle-Power AI Coalition (MPAC)  
This is no longer about catching up on compute — it is about ensuring that even if the frontier surges ahead, the EU and its partners can still act as a bloc with collective access and shared standards, turning fragmented sovereignty into coordinated power; and with the openweight frontier now closing in, coalition leverage is our best tool to shape what cannot be built alone.

## In practice  
We are treating the middle-power coalition not as a forum but as a functional lever. The European External Action Service, supported by DG TRADE and ENISA, is drafting a binding intergovernmental agreement that establishes mutual recognition of pre-deployment evaluations conducted by national assurance bodies — including EAAD — so that no member of the coalition can be blackmailed by a hyperscaler threatening to withdraw access. We are coupling this with a joint reserve of 10,000 high-end GPU clusters, co-financed and co-located across neutral or allied jurisdictions, accessible only to coalition members who comply with shared safety and reporting standards. This reserve will be governed by a rotating council with veto power only for collective decisions, ensuring no single member can block access but also preventing unilateral exploitation.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on the InvestAI Gigafactories, shifting from negotiation to enforcement. The European Commission has triggered Article 260 TFEU proceedings against Spain for non-compliance with the Net-Zero Industry Act override in Andalusia, while offering a revised package of regional benefits: 15% of construction jobs reserved for local workers, a €500m innovation hub for green hydrogen applications, and a legally binding cap on residential energy prices for the province for ten years. In Brittany and Norrland, co-location agreements with ASML, BE Semiconductor, and Northvolt are now finalised, with construction beginning this quarter under the strategic designation of the Chips Act, enabling fast-tracked customs and security clearances.

The EAAD is entering its final phase, with its first three evaluations completed on models above 50.0 capability. Legal teams are preparing for the expected challenge to its authority by aligning precedent from DMA enforcement and digital tax rulings, while building a public record of near-misses in agent persistence. CIARS remains fragmented: we are now conditioning 2024–2027 Cohesion Fund disbursements to Poland and Hungary on minimal telemetry sharing, backed by an offer of ENISA-led technical audits to address data sovereignty concerns. The ALT-Fund is operational in France, Germany, and Sweden, with sectoral councils now designing retraining pipelines in radiology and legal tech; we are using the European Social Fund+ to front-load payments to employers who commit early, creating visible momentum ahead of the European Parliament’s mid-term review.
