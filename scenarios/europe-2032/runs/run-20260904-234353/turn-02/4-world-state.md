### Frontier Creeps Forward
AI capability advances to 55.0 as labs extend autonomous agent performance in research and engineering domains. Systems now routinely execute multi-day projects in software and materials science with only intermittent oversight. No safety breakthroughs occur, and the gap between capability and assurance widens. The biosecurity community remains divided over a recent genome model’s implications, while evidence of a capability plateau emerges in pricing data—though leading firms dispute the interpretation.

### Cyber Response Begins
In response to the recent intrusion, the EU launches the Critical Infrastructure AI Resilience Shield (CIARS), mandating AI-powered detection upgrades and cross-border red-teaming for energy, water, and transport operators. The programme leverages NIS2 enforcement and ties funding to Cohesion Fund disbursements. Early adoption is uneven: Germany and the Netherlands move quickly, but Poland and Hungary resist binding telemetry sharing, citing sovereignty concerns.

### Sovereignty Efforts Stumble
The InvestAI Gigafactories, prioritised and fast-tracked, see progress in Brittany and Norrland with grid reservations and local reinvestment deals. However, delays in permitting and semiconductor co-location persist. The Tech Sovereignty Package faces legal pushback over Article 114 TFEU use, with Italy and Austria warning of regulatory overreach. No sovereignty gains materialise this turn.

### Political Capital Erodes
With three large measures in flight—including the priority—political capital declines. The EAAD begins interim operations but faces legal uncertainty over its levy on non-EU firms. Public sentiment holds steady, but sectoral job displacement in legal tech and diagnostics fuels quiet unrest. No major incident occurs, but the resilience gain from CIARS is partial and forward-loaded.
