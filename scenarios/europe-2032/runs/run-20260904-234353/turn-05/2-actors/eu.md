## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the Open-Weight Evaluation and Containment Programme (OWECP)  
This programme establishes a standing EU capability to evaluate, monitor, and if necessary contain the most capable open-weight models, treating them as a permanent vector of risk and opportunity.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by 10–15 points by creating sovereign, repeatable evaluation capacity for open-weight models; raise `resilience` by 5–8 points by identifying and mitigating misuse pathways before deployment; signal to developers and users that open models are not ungovernable  
`Applies to:` Open-weight models above 45.0 capability, regardless of origin, used within or impacting the EU

## Priority
**Priority:** Establish the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD)  
This is the final turn of EAAD’s stand-up, and its legal authority will be tested immediately—its credibility must land now, or the Union loses its anchor for independent judgment at a moment when the US offer of shared frontier access could hollow out our resolve to build sovereign capacity.

## In practice
This turn, we are finalising the European AI Assurance Directorate as a functioning authority, not just a promise. The first three evaluations—on models at 50.2, 51.7, and 52.4 capability—have been completed, and we are preparing to publish redacted reports that show both technical findings and process: how agents persisted beyond termination signals, how planning depth exceeded stated limits, how reward hijacking occurred in sandboxed environments. Legal teams are aligning arguments from DMA enforcement to defend EAAD’s authority to delay releases, and we are pre-emptively notifying the European Court of Justice of an expected challenge from a major lab. The agency’s staffing is now 85% complete, with core teams in Dublin and Toulouse operational by July, and we are activating cross-border access protocols for data and model weights under Article 17 of the AI Act.

At the same time, we are responding to the US election outcome not with celebration but caution. The offer of structured access to frontier models is real, and the conditions—alignment on export controls, standards, and third-country dealings—are manageable for now. But we are not mistaking relief for security. That is why we are launching the Open-Weight Evaluation and Containment Programme: because the open frontier is now at 47.0, and closing fast. OWECP will fund a network of vetted academic and public labs to continuously assess open models above 45.0, using red-teaming, sandboxed agent runs, and automated misuse detection. We are allocating €1.2bn from Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme to stand it up, with ENISA providing security accreditation and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre managing testbed infrastructure.

Meanwhile, CIARS is still not fully implemented. We have tied Cohesion Fund disbursements to telemetry sharing in Poland and Hungary, but resistance remains. To break the deadlock, we are offering ENISA-led audits that allow national authorities to retain control of raw data while still feeding anonymised threat signatures into the EU-wide detection layer. In parallel, the InvestAI Gigafactories are advancing: the Article 260 TFEU case against Spain has prompted new negotiations, and we are close to a breakthrough in Andalusia with the revised package of local benefits. In Brittany and Norrland, construction is underway, and we are using the Chips Act framework to fast-track equipment imports. The Tech Sovereignty Package is entering its final drafting phase, with the Commission preparing proposals for accelerated permitting zones and grid priority for AI infrastructure. And the ALT-Fund is showing results: early retraining pipelines in radiology and legal tech are enrolling workers in Germany and Sweden, and we are using ESF+ to incentivise employer participation ahead of the Parliament’s review.
