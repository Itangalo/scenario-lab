## In practice
This turn, we are bringing the European AI Emergency Response Corps to operational readiness under extreme constraints. ENISA hosts the cyber division in Brussels, now staffed with 180 technical operators drawn from national CERTs and vetted private contractors, equipped with pre-authorised access to sovereign compute from the two InvestAI Gigafactories now online. The biosecurity division, embedded within ECDC and co-located with high-containment labs in Solna, Lyon, and Hamburg, has 60 analysts trained in dual-use AI monitoring and pathogen synthesis detection. A standing protocol under Article 122a TFEU has been agreed by the Council for declaring AI-driven bio-incidents, triggering automatic data access and technical intervention rights — but only in the seven member states that have signed the liability opt-in: Belgium, France, Italy, Finland, the Netherlands, Ireland, and Portugal. Germany remains outside, pending a constitutional court decision expected in May 2030 on whether Union-level intervention in federally governed research institutions violates state sovereignty. We are preparing fallback activation via the Union Civil Protection Mechanism, treating AI-driven bio-risk as a cross-border health threat, and have secured informal commitments from German Länder-level biosecurity directors to share alerts and allow remote audits during emergencies.

Simultaneously, the Emergency Biosecurity Evaluation Compact is being activated through the European Commission’s Directorate-General for Research. EMBO, SciLifeLab, and the Paul Ehrlich Institute have accepted mandates to conduct rapid evaluations, funded by €750 million reallocated from the unused Resilience Bond. The first target is the U.S. lab’s viral vector design system, which exceeds capability 80.0 and was released without pre-deployment review. We are offering developers early mitigation support and a 14-day grace period before public disclosure in exchange for cooperation, but the framework remains voluntary — and the leading U.S. labs have so far declined to engage. ENISA stands ready to recommend market suspension based on Compact findings, but without binding enforcement, our leverage rests on market access threats and public pressure. This is not control — but it is the closest we can get, for now, to a functioning safety floor.
