Establish a binding, time-limited coalition with neutral scientific institutions (EMBO, SciLifeLab, Paul Ehrlich Institute) and vetted private red teams to conduct rapid, adversarial evaluations of all models above capability 70.0 within 30 days of release notification, with findings reported directly to the Commission and ENISA for immediate action.

## In practice
We are acting where the ground has already shifted: the Resilience Surge Protocol and InvestAI Gigafactories have formally concluded. Their effects are now embedded — or not. The Gigafactories delivered two sites operational under EU legal control, with sovereign compute now powering essential public services and red-team simulations, but fell short of the five-site vision due to permitting bottlenecks in Poland and Spain. The Resilience Surge Protocol led to modest upgrades in cyber defences at energy and transport operators, but biosecurity detection remains patchy, and labour transition schemes were underutilised, held back by national discretion and low awareness. These are now background conditions, not active levers.

This turn, we double down on crisis response. The European AI Emergency Response Corps moves from planning to partial operation: 320 technical operators have been recruited, with ENISA hosting the cyber division and ECDC embedding biosecurity analysts in three high-risk labs across Germany, France, and Sweden. The Council’s legal working group has agreed on a deployment protocol under Article 122a TFEU, and seven member states — including Belgium, Italy, and Finland — have signed the opt-in liability agreement, granting the Corps binding authority during declared incidents. Germany remains outside, pending its constitutional court ruling, but we are preparing contingency protocols to ensure cross-border access in emergencies through mutual aid channels under the Union Civil Protection Mechanism.

Simultaneously, we launch the Emergency Biosecurity Evaluation Compact to close the gap between release and review. With the Mandatory Pre-Deployment Biosecurity Audit Regime still stalled in legal drafting and facing resistance from both industry and member states, we cannot wait. This compact bypasses the slow path of legislation by activating existing scientific networks under a temporary mandate. Within 30 days of a model’s release, if it exceeds capability 70.0, the Compact will run adversarial probes focused on pathogen design, synthesis planning, and lab protocol generation. Results will be published within 72 hours of completion, and ENISA will have standing authority to recommend suspension of market access pending Commission review. We are offering participating labs early feedback and mitigation support in exchange for cooperation, and we are funding the Compact through a €750m reallocation from the unused portion of the Resilience Bond. This is not full sovereignty — but it is action where we still have room to move.
