### The Surge That Wasn’t

The EU doubles down on crisis response. The European AI Emergency Response Corps begins recruitment and is embedded across ENISA and the ECDC, but legal gridlock stalls deployment authority. Only five member states sign the opt-in liability agreement, and Germany demands judicial review of Article 122a TFEU’s applicability—delaying operational readiness. The Resilience Surge Protocol remains fragmented, its impact capped by uneven national buy-in.

The Commission announces the Mandatory Pre-Deployment Biosecurity Audit Regime, aiming to assert control through market power. Frontier labs are notified: no model above capability 65.0 may enter the EU without adversarial bio-risk testing. However, this is a proposal only—no implementation occurs this turn. Legal drafting continues, and the Paul Ehrlich Institute has not yet been mandated or resourced to conduct audits. OpenAI calls the threshold arbitrary and withdraws its latest model from EU consideration. Meta states it will comply but delays submission pending formal adoption of the rule.

Meanwhile, the AI frontier leaps forward. A leading lab releases a system that autonomously designs and simulates functional viral vectors, marking a discontinuous advance. Capability jumps to 78.0. With no safety system keeping pace—and the new audit regime not yet functional—assurance plummets. Internal opacity deepens, and the leaked biosecurity assessment confirms private fears: the blueprint gap is real, and widening.

Openweights follow the frontier, but not all at once. A derivative model with attenuated bio-risk features is released publicly, lifting openweight capability to 60.0—closing part of the gap but remaining below the new frontier. The rise reflects catch-up from earlier suppression, not a leap beyond current closed capabilities.

Resilience holds steady, but only because no major incident occurs—existing defences are untested, not proven.

Public sentiment stagnates at 34.0, caught between fear and frustration. The political cost mounts: with four large measures in flight, including a stalled priority, political capital erodes further.

### America’s Shadow

The new U.S. administration takes office, and its posture is clear: strategic consolidation. Access to frontier AI will be tiered, and the EU is not in the top tier. The message is quiet but firm—cooperation is conditional.
