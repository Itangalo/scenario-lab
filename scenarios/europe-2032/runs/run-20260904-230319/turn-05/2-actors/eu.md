## New measure
**Measure:** Launch Sovereign Compute Backbone Programme  
Establish a unified, EU-operated high-performance computing network, integrating existing national and private clusters under enforceable operational control, with priority access for public safety, resilience, and sovereign AI development.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–20 points by ensuring EU-controlled access to at least 30% of projected domestic compute demand for frontier-aligned workloads; reduce dependence on foreign providers and hyperscalers; create a legal and technical backbone for future sovereign model development  
Applies to: EU member states, national HPC operators, private data centre owners receiving EU funding or grid support, and foreign-owned facilities operating within EU jurisdiction

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign Compute Backbone Programme  
We are pushing hardest on this measure because the election_retrenchment event has abruptly weakened our primary external compute partner, while the ai_investment_collapse disrupts the very foundation of our industrial scaling assumptions—this is not just a delay, but a structural break. If we do not now consolidate control over existing and near-term compute, we will lose sovereignty not from lack of investment, but from lack of jurisdictional grip. The Cyber Shield and Incident Corps finish this turn, but without sovereign infrastructure to run on, their operational independence is illusory.

## In practice
We are activating the Sovereign Compute Backbone Programme through a new legal instrument under Article 114 TFEU, framed as a digital single market necessity for critical service continuity. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) will be rechartered to integrate national facilities—starting with Germany’s Gauss Centre, France’s GENCI, and Finland’s CSC—into a federated network with binding service-level agreements for EU institutions and authorised public actors. We are negotiating with private operators, including those in the InvestAI network, to accept EU operational control in exchange for priority access to energy and grid reinforcement under the revised Tech Sovereignty Package. The European Commission, through DG CONNECT and DG ENERGY, is fast-tracking a common technical standard for remote access auditing and fail-safe isolation, to be enforced via the Cyber Resilience Act and NIS2.

To secure cohesion, we are convening a special Council meeting under the Competitiveness formation, offering differentiated participation models: smaller states gain guaranteed access without full operational burden; larger ones retain hosting rights but cede veto power over cross-border activation. Funding of €35bn will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme, Recovery and Resilience Facility, and EIB loans backed by the Multiannual Financial Framework, with disbursement tied to integration milestones. The first backbone nodes will be operational by turn 6, ensuring that the EU AI Incident Response Corps and future evaluation bodies can operate on infrastructure no external actor can disable. This is not just about compute—it is about command.
