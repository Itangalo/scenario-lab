## New measure
**Measure:** Launch Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative  
A rapid-hardening programme for energy, water, and transport operators, combining mandatory AI-driven intrusion detection upgrades, live red-teaming, and cross-border incident response coordination.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 5  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 10–12 points; reduce systemic vulnerability to AI-assisted intrusions; ensure detection, not chance discovery, catches future test shots  
Applies to: EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, with enforcement through revised NIS2 directives and conditional access to EU recovery funds

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative  
This measure is now our highest priority because the emergent cyber test shot has exposed a critical gap: we are building sovereign compute and oversight capacity, but our most vital systems remain blind and brittle — and no amount of future capability matters if the foundation is compromised today.

## In practice  
We are activating the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative through the NIS2 framework, with binding requirements issued by the European Commission in coordination with ENISA and the CSIRT network. All operators of essential services must deploy AI-augmented intrusion detection systems by turn 4, with real-time telemetry shared through a new EU-level fusion cell. We are allocating €18bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility to cover 70% of upgrade costs for mid-sized and regional operators, particularly in healthcare, water, and municipal energy grids where defences are weakest. The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre will lead red-team deployments using controlled AI agents modelled on known adversarial tactics, simulating multi-vector attacks to expose hidden vulnerabilities before they are exploited.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by finalising grid interconnection agreements with ENTSO-E and unlocking €40bn in initial disbursements via the European Investment Bank. To overcome local resistance in Brittany and northern Sweden, we are formalising regional benefit-sharing pacts: 15% of operational revenue will fund local green hydrogen projects, and 40% of technical jobs will be reserved for residents, with training pipelines established through EU4Skills. Legal challenges to the Tech sovereignty package are being addressed through a revised draft directive under Article 114 TFEU, now decoupling the hyperscaler levy from the permitting reform to isolate the WTO-sensitive element. The package will instead tie accelerated permitting to enforceable commitments on EU-based operational control and energy efficiency benchmarks aligned with the EU Green Deal, making it more defensible and politically palatable.
