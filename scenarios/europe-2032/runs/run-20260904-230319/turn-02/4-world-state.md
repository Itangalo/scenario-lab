### Accelerated Frontier, Widening Gap  
Global AI capability advances by +3.0 to 58.0, driven by compressed release cycles and deeper agent autonomy in research and cyber tooling. The jump brings systems closer to continuous operation under standing goals, though human oversight remains essential. With no safety assurance improvements landing on shipped models, and the gap between capability and safety widening, confidence in control erodes.

### A Wake-Up Call Ignored Too Long  
The discovery of a sophisticated, AI-assisted intrusion into critical infrastructure across three continents—including two EU grid operators—lands as a systemic warning. The attackers used AI to map vulnerabilities and evade detection for weeks; their exposure was accidental, not the result of active monitoring. The breach triggers urgent calls for hardening, but the damage to institutional credibility is immediate.

### EU Responds with Focus and Friction  
The EU launches the **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative** as its new priority, a large category 6 measure aiming to mandate AI-driven detection upgrades and cross-border response coordination by turn 5. €18bn is allocated to support deployment, especially for weaker regional operators. However, legal resistance emerges in Germany and Poland, which challenge the Commission’s authority to impose technical mandates under NIS2, slowing rollout.

Meanwhile, **InvestAI Gigafactories** progress with grid agreements and €40bn disbursed, though local opposition in Brittany delays construction. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** sees revised drafting to isolate the hyperscaler levy, but investor confidence remains shaky without clarity on permitting timelines.

Public sentiment dips to 40.0 amid anxiety over dependency and vulnerability. Political capital falls to 40.0: the portfolio charge of −8 (two large measures, one priority) outweighs no finishing benefits, and the proposal bonus is absent—despite the intrusion, the Cyber Shield was not deemed a direct enough response to trigger it under rule 6.

Open-weight model capability rises to 46.0, reflecting accelerated fine-tuning and deployment of last cycle’s frontier weights into open ecosystems. While diffusion is faster than usual due to permissive licensing and tooling advances, it remains behind the current frontier, which has again pulled ahead. No breakthrough in open training efficiency or architecture parity has occurred, preserving the capability lead of closed frontier labs.
