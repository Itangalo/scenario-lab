## New measure
**Measure:** Launch EU AI Incident Response Corps  
A standing, cross-border unit of cyber-physical incident responders, trained and equipped to deploy within hours to AI-compromised critical systems, with authority to isolate, assess, and stabilise affected infrastructure.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 5  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 5–7 points; reduce incident escalation and recovery time; ensure human-in-the-loop response capability is present before systems cross autonomy thresholds  
Applies to: EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, with deployment authority granted through revised NIS2 enforcement and mutual aid protocols

## Priority
**Priority:** Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative  
This remains our highest priority because the eval anomaly reports confirm that capability is accelerating beyond oversight, and the recent intrusion proves our foundations are vulnerable — we must harden our systems now, not after the first catastrophic failure.

## In practice
We are operationalising the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative by issuing binding implementing acts under NIS2, co-signed by the Commission and the High Representative, to mandate AI-augmented intrusion detection systems across all essential service operators by turn 4. ENISA is deploying technical teams to support integration, particularly in under-resourced municipalities and regional grids, backed by €18bn in conditional funding from the Digital Europe Programme and Recovery and Resilience Facility. To break the legal deadlock in Germany and Poland, we are reframing the mandate as a mutual recognition of national security measures under Article 36 TFEU, allowing for differentiated implementation paths while preserving EU-wide detection standards. The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre has begun live red-teaming with AI agents modelled on adversarial tactics observed in the recent breach, simulating multi-vector attacks on energy and water systems to expose hidden vulnerabilities.

Simultaneously, we are launching the EU AI Incident Response Corps as a surge capacity to contain and stabilise AI-compromised systems before cascading failures occur. Hosted under the Union Civil Protection Mechanism and co-funded with €9bn from the Internal Security Fund, the Corps will integrate 300 specialists from national CSIRTs, ENISA, and vetted private-sector partners, trained in AI behaviour triage, system isolation, and fail-safe reversion. Deployment protocols are being finalised with the Council’s Permanent Security Committee, ensuring rapid activation under a unified command. This complements the Cyber Shield by ensuring that when detection triggers — or when anomalies like the eval reports presage deeper instability — we have human teams on the ground who can act faster than bureaucratic chains allow.

On the industrial front, we are advancing InvestAI Gigafactories by resolving the Brittany siting dispute through a binding regional pact: 15% of revenue will fund local green hydrogen, and 40% of technical roles will go to residents via EU4Skills pipelines. Grid interconnections are now secured with ENTSO-E, unlocking €40bn in EIB disbursements. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being restructured to decouple the hyperscaler levy from permitting reform, instead linking accelerated permitting to enforceable commitments on EU-based operational control and energy efficiency benchmarks, making it WTO-compliant and politically viable ahead of the Council vote in turn 4.
