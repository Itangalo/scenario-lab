### Access Denied, Strategy Shifted

The EU’s access to the world’s most capable AI model was abruptly severed, grounding hospital diagnostics, tax forecasting systems, and infrastructure monitoring tools across member states. The incident, officially unexplained but widely attributed to shifting U.S. export controls, exposed the Union’s operational fragility. In response, EU leadership declared a strategic pivot: no major resilience or safety initiative would proceed without sovereign compute foundations.

### Sovereign Compute Corridor Proposed

Amid growing alarm, the European Council announced the **Sovereign Compute Corridor**, a large category 4 measure to build a legally ring-fenced, EU-controlled compute infrastructure. Planned for hosting in Finland, Spain, and France, the corridor would mandate physical and legal control over model weights and training runs, with oversight proposed under Europol’s Cybercrime Directorate. However, no construction has begun, and legal frameworks remain in early drafting. Germany warns of market fragmentation, Ireland fears investor flight, and senior Commission officials stress that implementation cannot begin before the next turn due to procurement and governance hurdles. The proposal adds political strain but yields no immediate capability gain.

### Resilience Efforts Stumble

Despite ongoing red team deployments and telemetry pilots under the Emergency Resilience Surge, the denial of access crippled real-time response capacity. The Surge continues but yields no resilience gain; instead, the failure to prevent or rapidly respond to the access cutoff and the ongoing cyber vulnerabilities drags resilience down. Public sentiment sours further as hospitals report delays and local governments struggle with automated service failures.

### Safety Erodes Amid Capability Surge

Global AI capability advanced to 57.0, driven by accelerated training cycles. With no new safety measures operational and the frontier outpacing control, safety declined. The automated decision scandal—where an AI-driven social benefits system was found to have wrongfully denied claims en masse—further undermined trust, compounding the decline. However, some stabilizing efforts continue in parallel, softening the drop.

### Political Capital Drains

Three large measures remain active: InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, and the Emergency Resilience Surge. The new proposal for the Sovereign Compute Corridor increases political burden, but full portfolio charge is not yet realized, as implementation has not started. The automated scandal triggers public backlash and internal scrutiny, eroding confidence. Political capital declines meaningfully, but not mechanically—its fall reflects mounting pressure, not arithmetic. Public sentiment weakens, though not as sharply as initially projected, as emergency workarounds mitigate some visible failures.
