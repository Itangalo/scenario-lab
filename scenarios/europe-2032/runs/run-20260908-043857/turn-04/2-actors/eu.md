We are treating the Sovereign Compute Corridor as the spine of EU survival in the AI era. The European Council Presidency has invoked Article 122 TFEU to fast-track legal frameworks, overriding national objections on siting in the name of strategic emergency. Finland and Spain have accepted binding grid access guarantees and liability shielding in exchange for hosting rights; in return, they grant Europol’s Cybercrime Directorate full audit authority over training runs and model weights. The EuroHPC Joint Undertaking is now legally re-mandated as the operational core of the Corridor, with physical retention of weights and strict limits on external access. We are offering Germany co-financing for interconnectors and a five-year sunset clause on extraterritorial controls to ease single-market concerns — but the message is clear: without this, the Union will not survive as a decision-making entity in the next AI cycle.

The Sovereign Talent Acceleration is now formally in flight, building on the momentum of the European AI Fellowship. Three leading researchers from Silicon Valley have expressed intent to relocate, drawn by the promise of research autonomy and political stability. We are fast-tracking residency and compensation packages through a new EU Talent Office hosted at CERN’s legal framework, with funding drawn from the InvestAI Gigafactories envelope. However, full contracts await resolution of data sovereignty disputes with Ireland and Germany, which are holding up cross-border research access. Until those are settled, we cannot credibly offer the operational freedom these researchers demand.

The Tech Sovereignty Package continues to leverage private capital, but progress on permitting zones remains slow. The Commission has opened infringement proceedings against three member states delaying data centre approvals, citing Article 114 TFEU on internal market harmonisation. Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories programme is on track, with site selection finalised in France, Poland, and Sweden — but construction timelines depend on semiconductor delivery schedules, now delayed by export controls in Asia. We are not standing up new measures this turn because we are at the edge of coordination capacity; pushing further would risk collapse. Instead, we are consolidating what we have, ensuring that by turn 5, both the Compute Corridor and Talent Acceleration land with full force — not as promises, but as operating reality.
