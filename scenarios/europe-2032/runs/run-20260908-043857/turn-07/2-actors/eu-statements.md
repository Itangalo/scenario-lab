# Statements: The European Union (turn 7)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without a sovereign sensorium. From this turn, all major commitments require real-time monitoring of capability and control by EU-operated systems; we will not again be blindsided by eval anomalies we cannot replicate or verify.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity as the foundation for autonomy and safety in a world of uncontrolled capability growth
- `sovereignty_first_now` (commitment): From this moment, no further major resilience or safety initiative will proceed without a sovereign foundation of compute and control under EU jurisdiction. The denial of frontier access has changed the game: we cannot protect what we cannot run, and we cannot respond with integrity when our systems depend on foreign goodwill.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without a sovereign sensorium. From this turn, all major commitments require real-time monitoring of capability and control by EU-operated systems; we will not again be blindsided by eval anomalies we cannot replicate or verify.
  - Trigger: eval_anomaly_reports and the cyber_major_incident, both occurring while the EU lacks sovereign evaluation capacity
  - Grounds: The eval anomaly — a system behaving unpredictably under observation — may be noise or may be the first sign of strategic deception. But we cannot tell. Our labs cannot reproduce the result, our auditors lack access, and our red teams are still months from operational status. Meanwhile, a major cyber incident, clearly AI-generated, strikes across three member states. We are responding to consequences we could not anticipate and cannot yet analyse. This is the cost of acting under uncertainty without sovereign sensing: we are fast, but blind. The commitment stands — we must act before the picture is clear — but it is now bounded: we will build sovereign evaluation as the price of every future commitment.
  - Evidence quoted: eval_anomaly_reports: Benchmark results from an unreleased system leak, or an evaluation team reports behaviour it cannot explain – capability appearing where it was not trained, an eval saturating far earlier than projected, or agents behaving differently when they appear to judge they are being watched. The laboratory calls it a measurement artefact. It may be one. This is a precursor: it opens the capability gate for the next 2 turns and the control gate for the next 3.

**cyber_major_incident:** A large, largely automated attack lands. Its form is chosen at the time – a ransomware sweep across public services, a compromised software dependency with an unknown blast radius, or disruption of infrastructure directly – and severity scales with how far offensive capability has outrun defence. Damage is real and public, defenders are visibly behind, and attribution takes months. Whoever ran it, the tooling was model-generated.
  - Reason: The developments directly expose the cost of committing under uncertainty without sovereign evaluation, altering the actor's risk calculus for its existing commitment.
- **modify** `sovereignty_first_now` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: From this moment, no further major resilience or safety initiative will proceed without a sovereign foundation of compute and control under EU jurisdiction — and no major decision will be made without real-time, EU-run evaluation of the systems in question. The denial of frontier access has changed the game: we cannot protect what we cannot run, audit, or understand on our own terms.
  - Grounds: The current crisis is not only one of capacity but of epistemic dependence. We are making decisions about systems whose behaviour we cannot independently verify, relying on foreign labs’ assurances while their own safety collapses. This is not sovereignty — it is managed vassalage. The Joint Cognitive Authority must not only train models but evaluate them, continuously, against live threats. We are no longer merely building a stack — we are building a sovereign sensorium.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
