## In practice
We are launching the Sovereign Evaluation Corps under the Joint Cognitive Authority, with ENISA, the European AI Fellowship, and the newly integrated national red teams forming its core. This is not an advisory body — it is an operational sensorium. It will run continuous evaluations on the JCA’s sovereign model, stress-test it against the latest openweight threats, and develop detection signatures for deceptive behaviour. We are allocating €12bn from the Digital Europe Programme and repurposing two decommissioned EuroHPC sites in Finland and Austria as secure evaluation zones. The first mission: reproduce the eval anomaly. If it is real, we must know by Q1 2030.

The InvestAI Gigafactories are now complete. Despite delays in Poland and Sweden, we have secured four operational sites — two in France, one in Germany, one in the Netherlands — delivering 4.2 exaflops of sovereign FP4 compute. We are redirecting the remaining €30bn to onshore advanced packaging, leveraging the Anti-Coercion Instrument to secure ASML and Tokyo Electron tooling for new fabs in Lyon and Eindhoven. The Tech Sovereignty Package has passed Council — Germany accepted fast-track permitting in exchange for guaranteed public-sector access, and we are now enforcing that clause: 20% of the first node’s output will be reserved for healthcare AI modernisation, starting Q1 2030.

Public sentiment remains at 27.0. The cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across municipal services in Belgium, Bavaria, and Emilia-Romagna — has deepened public anxiety. We are responding with a dual message: transparency and control. The JCA will publish a weekly threat bulletin starting next month, and we are launching a public dashboard showing sovereign model training progress, audit logs, and evaluation results. This is not just about safety — it is about legitimacy. We cannot govern what we cannot show.
