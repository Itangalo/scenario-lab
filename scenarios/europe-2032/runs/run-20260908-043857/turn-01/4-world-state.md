### Accelerating Frontier, Fragile Defences

The global AI frontier advances steadily, with capability rising to 54.5 as leading labs compress release cycles and scale training runs. Offensive cyber capabilities, now open and widely distributed through Kimi K3, enable a sophisticated intrusion detected across critical infrastructure in multiple regions. Though found by chance, the breach reveals systemic detection failures—especially in energy and water systems—prompting alarm across EU capitals.

### Emergency Resilience Surge Proposed

In response, the EU proposes the Emergency Resilience Surge, invoking Article 122 TFEU to treat the incident as a cross-border crisis. The Commission drafts mandates for telemetry sharing and red team deployments via ENISA, with €12bn in fast-tracked funding under discussion. However, legal challenges emerge: Hungary and Slovakia contest the use of Article 122, while several member states express concern over operational burdens. Negotiations delay full adoption, and no binding implementation begins this turn. The measure is introduced but not yet operational.

### Sovereignty Efforts Stumble

The InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package proceed slowly. Site selection advances in Finland and Spain, but grid interconnection delays loom. The permitting zones face legal reservations, and private investment remains cautious. With no immediate capacity gains, and rising frontier dependence, eu_ai_sovereignty declines.

### Safety Erodes, Capital Erodes

Despite the proposed surge, ai_safety drops as the gap between capability and control widens. The cyber intrusion—occurring without prior detection—underscores systemic fragility. The EU’s political capital erodes by 3 points due to the ongoing burden of two active measures (InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package) and the initiation of crisis deliberations. A portfolio charge of −3 applies; no priority is named, so no bonus is earned. The Emergency Resilience Surge does not yet contribute to the charge, as it was not adopted this turn. Openweight capability increases only modestly to 41.0, reflecting limited diffusion of Kimi K3’s release within existing infrastructure constraints.
