## In practice  
We are launching the Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 122 TFEU, treating the intrusion as a cross-border crisis that threatens systemic stability. The Commission will work through ENISA and the Crisis Management Directorate to deploy red teams, mandate real-time telemetry from grid operators and water utilities, and pre-position backup control systems in secure facilities. Member states will be required to adopt minimum detection thresholds and reporting timelines by February 2027, backed by emergency funding from the Cohesion Fund and the Digital Europe Programme — up to €12bn redirected on a fast-track basis.

The resistance is predictable: some capitals see this as an infringement on national security sovereignty, and mid-tier utilities fear the cost and complexity. We are offering full EU co-financing for the first 18 months, technical support from the newly expanded Joint Cyber Unit, and a fast-track exemption from certain state-aid rules for infrastructure upgrades. The Council Presidency will convene an extraordinary meeting of the Internal Market and Competitiveness formation to secure endorsement, but we are prepared to act under urgency procedures if consensus stalls.

Meanwhile, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by finalising site selections in Finland, Spain and Poland, leveraging CEF Energy funds to accelerate grid upgrades. The Tech sovereignty package is facing delays in the Council over the accelerated-permitting zones — some member states fear land-use precedents — so we are offering a sunset clause and independent environmental review to secure buy-in. Both remain on track, but their long timelines underscore why resilience cannot wait.
