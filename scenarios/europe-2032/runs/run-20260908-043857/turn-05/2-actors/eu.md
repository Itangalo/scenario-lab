## In practice
We are treating the completion of the Sovereign Compute Corridor and Sovereign Talent Acceleration as a turning point: the spine is built, the people are in motion, and now we must give the body a brain. The Sovereign Cognitive Infrastructure Initiative launches under Article 172 TFEU, with the Commission empowered to designate and fund a series of sovereign model training runs on Corridor infrastructure, starting with a replication and hardening of the best openweight models at capability 47.5 and progressing toward EU-originated systems at 60.0+. These models will be developed under strict audit protocols by a new Joint Cognitive Authority — a hybrid of EuroHPC, ENISA, and the AI Office — with interpretability baked in from the start and weights legally sequestered under EU jurisdiction.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, though semiconductor delays from the Taiwan blockade threaten construction in Poland and Sweden. We are reallocating 15% of the envelope to onshore advanced packaging and test facilities in France and the Netherlands, using the Anti-Coercion Instrument to negotiate preferential access to Japanese and Korean tooling. The Tech Sovereignty Package continues to face resistance on permitting, but the cyber incident has shifted sentiment in the Council: we are now invoking Article 114 TFEU to override national delays, backed by a new emergency clause in the Digital Markets Act. Germany, initially resistant, has agreed to fast-track approvals in exchange for hosting the first Sovereign Cognitive node and a guaranteed 20% allocation of its output for public-sector modernisation. The emergent regional aid pact is not being folded into central structures — yet — but we are offering it formal observer status and access to Sovereign Cognitive models in development, turning fragmentation into a testing network. This turn, we consolidate. Next turn, we scale.
