## In practice  
We are acting on the premise that compute is command. The withdrawal of international cloud access from EU research institutions is not merely a logistical setback — it is the moment when dependence becomes disarmament. We are therefore invoking Article 122a TFEU and the Crisis Coordination Mechanism to issue the Sovereign Compute Activation Directive: all EuroHPC nodes and nationally funded research clusters above 50 petaflops must, within 90 days, declare availability for emergency AI safety workloads, including pathogen detection, agent containment simulations, and real-time monitoring of open-weight diffusion. A new Joint Compute Authority, hosted at CERN and backed by DG CONNECT, will coordinate scheduling, security, and access — overriding national reservations where necessary under the emergency protocol.

Simultaneously, we are launching a €12bn procurement programme to fast-track the deployment of sovereign AI training infrastructure, to be built on EU soil, powered by renewable energy, and legally insulated from foreign jurisdictional reach. This will draw on the now-finished InvestAI Gigafactories framework, repurposing its permitting lanes and grid agreements. We are offering member states a choice: integrate now under common operational standards, or risk exclusion from future resilience funding. Germany and Austria will be offered a carve-out on health data access in exchange for compute sovereignty alignment — a trade we would not have made six months ago, but one we must now. The European AI Emergency Response Corps will be the first tenant, with guaranteed priority access to 40% of the new capacity. This is not industrial policy. This is infrastructure for survival.
