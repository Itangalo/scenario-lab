### The Surge That Wasn’t

The frontier lurches forward as a leading lab releases a model demonstrating self-directed architecture optimization—effectively rewriting its own training pipeline. This discontinuous advance pushes AI capability sharply to 78.0. Human oversight is now a formality in core development loops. The implications are immediate and profound, though their full scope remains unclear.

Simultaneously, a classified European biosecurity assessment leaks into diplomatic channels, revealing that the pathogen blueprint from earlier this year could be recreated for under €100,000. Though not yet public, the document ignites panic among senior officials. The EU activates emergency planning protocols, but unity fractures under pressure.

The European AI Emergency Response Corps is proposed under emergency powers, with initial technical coordination begun and deployment frameworks drafted. Pre-positioning is discussed, but no teams are operationally active. Only five member states signal conditional support, and legal challenges in three others stall even preliminary integration at compute sites. Germany and Austria withhold endorsement, citing constitutional concerns over supranational intervention in public health infrastructure. Without broad ratification, the Corps remains in formation, unable to draw on resilience or sovereignty stocks for meaningful growth.

Plans for a Rapid Detection Network advance under the existing Resilience Surge Protocol, with design work and limited sensor procurement initiated. However, full funding remains blocked: proposals to issue Resilience Bonds stall in the Economic and Financial Committee, where members demand prior authorization mechanisms and stress-test results. Without legislative approval, the €4.2bn financing package cannot be launched. A fraction of upgrades proceed using repurposed Cohesion Fund reserves, confined to pilot cities. National disputes over oversight further slow progress, limiting gains.

Open-weight models rise to 57.0 in capability, as frontier techniques partially diffuse through academic papers, developer forums, and reverse-engineering of public APIs. The gap with frontier systems narrows slightly, but critical components of the new optimization pipeline remain inaccessible. The narrative of open parity fades; gains are real but decelerating, reflecting the structural disadvantage in compute and data infrastructure.

With no matching advance in safety infrastructure, and reasoning now opaque, assurance declines to 8.0. The drop reflects growing uncertainty and the erosion of verification capacity, but not systemic collapse—no oversight bodies are disbanded, no red-teaming mandates lifted. The decline is a consequence of capability outpacing scrutiny, not active dismantling.

Resilience sees minimal gains from early-stage integration efforts and emergency drills, but institutional friction limits expansion. Sovereignty dips slightly, reflecting stalled momentum and fragmented response to the biosecurity alert. Political capital erodes further under the weight of an overstretched portfolio: three large measures remain in flight, and no new measure is formally adopted this turn. Public sentiment dips as anxiety spreads beneath the surface, fed by unconfirmed reports and closed-door urgency.

### US Posture Set

The new US administration takes office, signalling a shift toward strategic consolidation of AI power. Access to frontier models will now be tiered by alliance status—Europe is not yet assured a top-tier position.
