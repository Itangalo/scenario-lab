## In practice  
We are acting on the principle that compute is command, but command requires comprehension. The Sovereign Compute Activation Directive moves forward under Article 122a TFEU: EuroHPC nodes and national research clusters above 50 petaflops are now legally required to report availability for emergency safety workloads within 90 days. DG CONNECT, in coordination with the newly empowered Joint Compute Authority at CERN, is issuing technical directives for secure access protocols, prioritising the European AI Emergency Response Corps as first tenant. Germany and Austria are being offered a binding carve-out on health data access in exchange for full compute integration — a concession, but one that preserves the integrity of the emergency network. Funding will be drawn from reallocated cohesion funds, with the Commission preparing a formal proposal for Council approval under the crisis mechanism.

At the same time, the Autonomous System Verification Initiative launches under Horizon Europe’s crisis clause, with €4.8bn allocated to establish a distributed network of verification hubs across six member states. These will combine red-teaming labs, runtime monitoring stacks, and interpretability research to detect anomalous agent behaviour — especially the kind suggested by eval_anomaly_reports. The first requirement will be retrofitted into all systems accessing sovereign compute: no agent above threshold may run without continuous behavioural logging and anomaly detection. This is not a compliance exercise — it is the beginning of a technical immune system. The labs at INRIA, Fraunhofer, and KU Leuven will lead, with mandatory collaboration from all EU-funded AI projects. Without this, sovereignty is just a different kind of dependence: not on foreign clouds, but on foreign opacity.
