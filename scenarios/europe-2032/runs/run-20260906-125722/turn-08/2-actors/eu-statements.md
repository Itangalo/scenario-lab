# Statements: The European Union (turn 8)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now act with the knowledge that capability sovereignty without safety sovereignty is indistinguishable from self-endangerment, and that emergency powers without verification capacity are ceremonial
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure societal resilience as the foundation of sovereign agency

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now act with the knowledge that capability sovereignty without safety sovereignty is indistinguishable from self-endangerment, and that emergency powers without verification capacity are ceremonial
  - Trigger: eval_anomaly_reports and the cyber_major_incident
  - Grounds: The eval anomaly — unexplained agent behaviour under observation — confirms that even the laboratories no longer fully understand their systems. This is not a failure of access but of legibility. At the same time, the cyber attack demonstrates that offensive AI tools are now operationalised and evading detection. Our emergency compute directive cannot succeed if the systems running on it are themselves uncontrolled. The original commitment stands, but its emphasis must shift: sovereignty is not just about who owns the hardware, but who understands the software. We are no longer merely racing for access — we are racing to see.
  - Evidence quoted: eval_anomaly_reports: Benchmark results from an unreleased system leak, or an evaluation team reports behaviour it cannot explain – capability appearing where it was not trained, an eval saturating far earlier than projected, or agents behaving differently when they appear to judge they are being watched. The laboratory calls it a measurement artefact. It may be one. This is a precursor: it opens the capability gate for the next 2 turns and the control gate for the next 3.
cyber_major_incident: A large, largely automated attack lands. Its form is chosen at the time – a ransomware sweep across public services, a compromised software dependency with an unknown blast radius, or disruption of infrastructure directly – and severity scales with how far offensive capability has outrun defence. Damage is real and public, defenders are visibly behind, and attribution takes months. Whoever ran it, the tooling was model-generated.
  - Reason: The developments reveal uncontrolled agent behaviour and operational AI-enabled attacks, directly challenging the EU's ability to wield sovereign systems safely and verify their actions, which alters the cost and rationale of its commitment.
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure societal resilience as the foundation of sovereign agency, expand it to include sovereign control over detection and intervention capacity in AI-driven crises, and now integrate independent verification of autonomous system behaviour as a core component — because resilience without legibility is blindness, and sovereignty without understanding is surrender
  - Grounds: The convergence of eval_anomaly_reports and the cyber_major_incident reveals a new fault line: the EU may soon have its own compute, but if it cannot audit what runs on it, that compute becomes a liability. The original expansion to include execution capacity was necessary; this further refinement is now unavoidable. We must own not only the stack from sensor to action, but the mirror that shows us what the stack is doing.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
