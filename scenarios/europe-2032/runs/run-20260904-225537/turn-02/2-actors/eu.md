## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge  
A time-bound, cross-sectoral programme to harden energy, water, and transport control systems against AI-enabled intrusion, with mandatory red-teaming and automated detection upgrades.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 6  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 8–12 points; reduce systemic fragility in cyber-physical systems within 18 months  
Applies to: EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, with co-funding and binding technical standards enforced through CER Directive upgrades

## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
This remains the cornerstone of our two-year commitment: without sovereign compute, no evaluation, no resilience, and no credible industrial base — and delays in Brittany and Sweden threaten both timeline and credibility, requiring full political weight to overcome.

## In practice  
We are accelerating the InvestAI Gigafactories by invoking Article 122 TFEU and the Net-Zero Industry Act to override permitting bottlenecks in Brittany and northern Sweden. The Commission is finalising grid reservation agreements with ENTSO-E for 15GW of dedicated capacity, while the European Investment Bank is releasing the first €40bn tranche through the Recovery and Resilience Facility, conditioned on national co-financing from France, Germany, and Sweden. To counter local resistance, we are formalising binding reinvestment packages: 15% of each site’s operational revenue will fund regional green hydrogen projects, and 30% of skilled jobs will be reserved for local hires, verified by independent auditors. This is not negotiation — it is the price of inclusion in the Union’s strategic infrastructure.

The Tech sovereignty package advances through the Digital Markets and Digital Services Council, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable commitments on EU-based operational control and energy efficiency. The legal draft under Article 114 TFEU will harmonise permitting timelines to 12 months, with penalties for non-compliance. We are also structuring the private capital mobilisation: 40% tied to ASML supply chain resilience, 30% to Blue Card+ talent incentives, and 30% to long-term PPAs with nuclear and offshore wind providers. However, the lack of a unified legal base for the EAAD levy remains a vulnerability — we are now preparing fallback funding models in case the 0.5% levy on non-EU hyperscalers is struck down by the ECJ or WTO.

Meanwhile, the European AI Assurance Directorate begins operations under ENISA with an interim mandate: to audit the AI Act’s high-risk provisions ahead of their 2027 enforcement. We are recruiting its first cohort — specialists in agent behaviour, cyber-physical threats, and interpretability — with secondments from national nuclear safety and intelligence agencies. The first red-team evaluation will target models above 50.0 capability index, with access demands based on training logs and internal monitoring data. The bio_uplift_findings incident sharpens our urgency: while the debate rages in journals, we are treating the bio risk as live. The new Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge will mandate AI-driven intrusion detection upgrades across all critical operators, funded at €12bn from the Civil Protection Mechanism and the Digital Europe Programme, with binding red-teaming requirements and automated anomaly detection systems to be deployed by turn 6. This is not optional adaptation — it is the minimum credible response to a world where the next intrusion may not be discovered by chance.
