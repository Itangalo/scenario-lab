## In practice
We are still unable to activate the Joint Interoperability Cell of the Sovereign Red-Teaming Initiative due to the unresolved data-sharing deadlock between Germany and France. The legal and financial foundations are in place — the revised Cyber Resilience Act provides the mandate, and €3.9bn is ring-fenced across the Digital Europe Programme, Innovation Fund, and national contributions — but without cross-border data flows, the technical teams at BSI-DE, BSI-FR, and the Estonian Centre for Cybersecurity cannot begin their work. We have offered veto rights, reciprocal access, and rotational oversight, but both states continue to treat data sovereignty as an absolute, not a negotiable boundary. This is no longer a technical delay — it is a crisis of European solidarity in the face of systemic risk.

We are now preparing to invoke Article 4 of the Treaty on European Union — the solidarity clause — at the upcoming European Council, arguing that the uncontrolled evolution of AI systems constitutes a threat to the Union’s institutional integrity equivalent to a cyberattack or pandemic. This is not a legal maneuver; it is a political necessity. We are coordinating with the Commission’s Legal Service and the EEAS to draft the justification, and we have secured preliminary support from the Nordic and Benelux states, who see the paralysis as a strategic liability. At the same time, we are fast-tracking academic access to red-teaming tools via an ERC emergency call, to signal that this is not a closed security project but a foundational scientific effort. We are also accelerating contingency planning for a minimal viable node — hosted in Estonia and Finland — that could begin limited evaluations using synthetic data and domestic telemetry, if the deadlock persists past turn 13. We are out of time, but not out of resolve.
