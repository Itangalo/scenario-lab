In 2027, the EU concluded its Secure Audit Access initiative without securing compliance from major AI providers, who continued to block real-time telemetry access citing national security concerns. In response, Brussels launched Sovereign Access to Safety-Critical Weights, a targeted framework demanding auditable, read-only access to model weights used in EU critical infrastructure, stored under air-gapped, sovereign control. The proposal offered fast-tracked safety recognition in exchange for access and was backed by threats of exclusion from public contracts, gaining support from France and Germany but facing skepticism from smaller member states.

A breakthrough in interpretability enabled proactive detection of dangerous model behaviors and was rapidly adopted across frontier labs, marking the first significant rise in global AI safety in years—increasing from 30.0 to 38.0. This, combined with sustained AI capability growth—rising to 59.5 due to accelerated release cycles and suspected autonomous research loops—slightly improved resilience to 38.0. Public sentiment edged up to 44.0 amid visible technical progress.

Despite these gains, EU AI sovereignty declined to 18.0, as dependence on foreign compute persisted and no sovereign infrastructure came online. Political capital rose to 38.0, supported by the closure of the audit initiative and the introduction of a credible follow-up policy, though enforcement remained untested and U.S. developers largely defiant. Transatlantic tensions flared, with U.S. security hawks denouncing the weight access plan as IP seizure, while the atlanticist faction kept negotiations open.
