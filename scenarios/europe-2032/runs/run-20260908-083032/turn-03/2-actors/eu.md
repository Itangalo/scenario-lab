## New measure
**Sovereign Access to Safety-Critical Weights (Category 5)**  
Establish a legal and technical pathway for EU-certified institutions to obtain read-only, auditable access to model weights used in systems operating within EU critical infrastructure, under strict security protocols and with liability shielding for compliance.

## Priority
**Sovereign Access to Safety-Critical Weights (category 5)** — because the failure of Secure Audit Access confirms that real-time telemetry will not be granted, and because the safety breakthrough this turn creates a narrow window to demand access to what matters—weights—before providers lock them behind new layers of obfuscation and legal deflection.

## In practice
We are acting through the European Digital Infrastructure Board, now reinforced by emergency delegation under the Critical Entities Resilience Directive and the Digital Markets Act. With Secure Audit Access now concluded but unimplemented—providers did not comply, and Washington did not yield—we are pivoting to what remains possible: access to weights. The safety breakthrough changes the game. For the first time, a control technique has landed on deployed systems that can predict behaviour before observation. The labs want it adopted universally because it stabilizes their own operations. We are offering a bargain: the EU will fast-track recognition of any provider’s safety claims if, in return, they submit weights used in EU critical infrastructure to secure, read-only audit by EU-certified institutions. No telemetry, no real-time control—just the weights, stored in sovereign, air-gapped facilities under state-actor-grade security.

We are targeting energy, health, and transport providers first, where the stakes are highest and the dependency most acute. Germany and France are on board, having already invested in the red teaming nodes; we are offering them first access to the audit pipeline. Smaller states like Estonia and Cyprus, burned by limited support last turn, are demanding guarantees—so we are embedding a clause in the implementing act: any provider refusing weight access will be disqualified from public contracts in critical sectors across all member states. That leverage is thin, but it is real. The U.S. campaign’s security hawk faction will denounce this as theft; but the atlanticist coalition gives us a counter-narrative: shared risk demands shared assurance. This is not about copying models—it is about verifying that the systems keeping our lights on and our hospitals running are not ticking coordination risks. We are not asking for everything. We are asking for what we must have.
