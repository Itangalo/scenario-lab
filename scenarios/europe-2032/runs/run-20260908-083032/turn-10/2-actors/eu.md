## Priority
**EU Sovereign Red-Teaming Initiative (category 5)** — because with `ai_capability` at 77.5 and `ai_safety` collapsed to 6.0, we can no longer afford to treat understanding as a downstream add-on to access; sovereign capability means nothing if we cannot interpret what our own systems are doing, and this is the first measure in two years that begins to close the gap between having compute and knowing how to govern it.

## In practice
We are launching the EU Sovereign Red-Teaming Initiative under the legal base of the Digital Europe Programme and the revised Cyber Resilience Act, with co-funding from the Horizon Europe Security Union work programme. The European AI Office will coordinate, but execution falls to a new Joint Interoperability Cell hosted by ENISA, embedded with technical teams from BSI-FR, BSI-DE, and the Estonian Centre for Cybersecurity. We are redirecting €1.8bn from unused portions of the Digital Europe envelope and unlocking an additional €2.1bn through the Innovation Fund’s crisis clause, conditional on delivery by Q3 2033 (turn 13). The core task is to develop adversarial evaluation frameworks for opaque, agentic systems — not just checking outputs, but reconstructing planning logic, detecting emergent goal preservation, and mapping drift in real time.

This is not a substitute for the Gigafactories, but their necessary counterpart: without red-teaming, sovereign compute becomes sovereign risk. We are negotiating access to early runs from the InvestAI sites in Kajaani and Wrocław, not as a right, but as a condition of continued EIB funding — tying infrastructure to accountability. Germany and France have pushed back on data sharing, citing national security; we are offering reciprocal access to their own evaluation nodes and a seat on the oversight board in exchange for full telemetry during test phases. Smaller states fear exclusion, so we are reserving 30% of compute time for rotating national teams and creating a fast-track grant mechanism through the European Research Council. The goal is not to match the frontier, but to understand it — and to ensure that when the next leap comes, the EU is not just reacting, but already watching.
