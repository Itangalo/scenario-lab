## New measure
**Secure Audit Access for EU-Certified Evaluators (Category 5)**  
Establish binding access rights for EU-vetted evaluators to conduct real-time model inspections and agent-behaviour monitoring in high-risk systems operated by foreign providers within EU critical infrastructure.

## Priority
**Secure Audit Access for EU-Certified Evaluators (Category 5)** — because the emergent_unified_eu_ai_audit initiative gives us a narrow window to institutionalize real-time scrutiny before US providers consolidate opacity, and because without technical visibility, no restriction or resilience measure can land.

## In practice
We are acting through the European Digital Infrastructure Board, convened under Article 28 of the Digital Markets Act, to operationalize the audit framework launched by the coalition of member states. The Board is drafting a Implementing Regulation that will require any provider of high-risk AI systems in energy, water, transport or health infrastructure to grant secure, monitored access to EU-certified evaluation teams — not just post-hoc reports, but live telemetry under controlled conditions. This is being framed as a condition of market access, not a request.

Resistance is coming from Washington and the largest US-based AI labs, who call it industrial espionage. But smaller EU states — especially those without domestic compute — see this as their only leverage. We are offering reciprocity: EU evaluators will publish methodology in advance, use sealed environments, and share findings first with the provider. The German and French cybersecurity agencies are standing up the first two evaluation nodes, funded through the Civil Protection Mechanism, with talent drawn from Fraunhofer and INRIA.

The cyber_test_shot event has made it impossible to argue that detection-by-accident is sufficient. This measure does not stop attacks, but it makes hiding in plain sight harder — and it builds the technical muscle we lack. It is small, but it is ours.
