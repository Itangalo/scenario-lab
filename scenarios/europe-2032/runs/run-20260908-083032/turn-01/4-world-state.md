### The Audit Gambit

In late 2026, the European Union proposed a new measure—Secure Audit Access—granting EU-certified evaluators binding rights to inspect high-risk AI systems operated by foreign providers in critical infrastructure. Framed as a condition of market access, the initiative drew support from a newly formed coalition of member states and was accelerated by the recent cyber intrusion affecting energy and water systems. Germany and France began laying the groundwork for evaluation nodes under strict protocols, emphasizing reciprocity and methodological transparency.

However, resistance emerged swiftly. U.S. officials criticized the plan as overreach, and major AI laboratories signaled reluctance to submit to real-time monitoring, warning of service withdrawals from EU critical sectors. Behind closed doors, leverage was tested: American providers underscored their irreplaceable compute advantage, while smaller EU states called for safeguards they could not independently provide.

The proposal was formally introduced this turn but did not advance to implementation. No provider complied, and no enforcement mechanism was triggered—consistent with the minimum timeline for effect. The measure remains legally assertive but unenforced, a signal of intent without immediate consequence.
