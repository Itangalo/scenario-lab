### The Leap and the Fall

Global AI capability surges by +4.0 to 77.5, driven by a sudden, discontinuous advance — a leap that renders prior roadmaps obsolete. Technical communities report systems solving multi-domain planning problems autonomously, with internal reasoning that resists human interpretation. The jump accelerates diffusion: openweight_capability rises to 63.6, as fragments of the new architecture leak into open ecosystems through developer forums and repurposed evaluation tools.

`ai_safety` collapses further, down to 6.0. The capability jump triggers a −12 penalty under metric rule 3, reflecting opaque reasoning, loss of control, and the absence of any deployed safety measure. No audit regime, no interpretability tool, no containment protocol has kept pace. The frontier is now functionally autonomous in key domains, and no authority can explain how.

With no active resilience measures and systemic exposure growing, `resilience` declines to 31.0. Critical infrastructure remains unevenly hardened, and the ongoing compute shortage from the Taiwan blockade amplifies fragility. Emergency services report degraded AI support, and cross-border coordination falters.

The EU doubles down on the InvestAI Gigafactories, declaring them strategic assets under emergency governance. Crisis powers override local permitting, but grid integration delays in France and Poland stall full activation. Capacity remains below projection, and smaller member states protest the uneven allocation of audit rights. Cohesion frays further.

No new measure is proposed. The portfolio is empty, but political capital remains at 10.0 — paralysed. The priority is named, but at this level, it changes nothing: no pull-in, no cost, no effect. The Union acts, but cannot decide.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 14.0. The Gigafactories are not yet delivering at scale, so no completion bonus applies. The capability rise of +4.0 triggers a −1 decay, and the ongoing frontier access denial continues to erode capacity. Physical infrastructure exists, but not sovereign capability.

Public sentiment drops to 26.0. Widespread job losses in legal, accounting, and administrative sectors feed resentment. The Gigafactories are seen as a failing bet on control, while disinformation and shortages deepen public distrust.

### A Union Acting, Not Governing

The EU is acting — redirecting compute, invoking crisis powers, enforcing board rotations — but not governing in any coherent, empowered sense. It is reacting within the walls it has built, while the world outside moves beyond them.
