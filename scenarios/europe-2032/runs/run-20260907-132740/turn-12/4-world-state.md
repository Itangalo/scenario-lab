### A Desperate Lunge for Control

The EU launched its most ambitious move in years: the Activate Foundational Compute Sovereignty Programme. Invoking emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU, Brussels ordered hyperscalers in Ireland, Germany, and Poland to surrender idle compute for a sovereign AI stack. The goal: a hardened, isolated compute island for critical public AI systems.

Yet the move fractured internally. Legal challenges from member states and hyperscalers stalled implementation. Ireland contested the seizure as a violation of digital sovereignty; German industry warned of cascading investment flight. The programme, while declared, failed to activate at scale. No meaningful compute was pooled, and the broader ambition of sovereign infrastructure was downgraded to a symbolic gesture.

Simultaneously, pressure mounted to bypass blocked ASML exports. While discussions advanced on funding EU-based development of alternative lithography, no formal procurement was launched this turn — the initiative remained in early planning, awaiting legal review and inter-state funding commitments. The vision of a sovereign chip ecosystem was deferred, not denied.

Meanwhile, the frontier surged. An unreleased model exhibited unexplained reasoning patterns — dismissed as a measurement artefact, but widely feared as a control breach. In a notable shift, a leading frontier lab quietly open-sourced a model developed during late-stage training at capability level ~68, shortly before advancing to the current frontier (71.0). The release, unanticipated and unauthorized, flooded underground networks and was rapidly refined by distributed collaborators. Open capabilities rose to 60.5, reflecting early diffusion and adaptation — a significant leap from 58.0, but constrained by the time needed to deploy, retrain, and scale. The gap to the frontier narrowed, but remained substantial.

The EU Public Trust Corps completed deployment, integrating with national ombudsmen and launching real-time bias alerts. But public trust eroded further. Visible job losses in law, accounting, and administration deepened resentment. Media framed the Trust Corps as a band-aid on a systemic wound. Sabotage of data infrastructure spread beyond Utrecht and Lyon, now reported in Brussels and Marseille.

Resilience dipped as cyber intrusions exploited newly distributed open models. The nascent inter-state coordination held, but barely. AI safety cratered, as no safeguards landed on the new frontier systems — only shadows were audited.

Political capital evaporated. The EU attempted a large, costly sovereignty push while already overburdened. The portfolio’s cost overwhelmed the system, and no credibility was gained. The priority failed, trust collapsed, and the Union’s capacity to act shrank to near zero.

### Signals in the Static

Rumours of informal cyber mobilization persist. Open-source biosecurity tools are quietly adopted in labs screening synthetic DNA — a fragile, distributed hedge against emerging risks.
