## Priority
**Priority:** Establish EU Public Trust Corps for AI Incident Response and Transparency  
This remains our dominant effort because the convergence of domestic failure, public fury, and physical backlash demands a visible, operational response to harm — not just promises of future oversight. The Trust Corps is the only instrument already scaling that can deliver both technical redress and public communication in real time, especially now that it includes systemic bias alerts and the Redress Coordination Unit. Without its credibility, no new measure — not even sovereignty or accountability boards — will survive public scrutiny.

## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: containment and reconstruction. On containment, the Public Trust Corps is now fully reoriented. The Redress Coordination Unit is operational, embedded with national ombudsmen in France, Finland, and the Netherlands, and has begun fast-tracking compensation claims from the Dutch social insurance case. The AI Incident Dashboard’s new systemic bias module went live last week, pulling data from pilot audits in Lyon and Marseilles. This is no longer about managing perception — it is about proving that when AI harms, there is a path to repair.

Meanwhile, the Algorithmic Accountability Boards are moving from proposal to practice. The first board, mandated to investigate the Dutch case, will be convened by the European Ombudsman with members drawn from civil society, legal experts, and affected citizens. It will have subpoena power over model logs and internal audits, and its findings will bind the Dutch government to reform within six months. This is not symbolic: we are using Article 226 TFEU enforcement mechanisms to back its authority, and non-compliance will trigger infringement proceedings.

But legitimacy requires more than justice — it requires autonomy. With ASML cut off and open-weight models spreading cyber capabilities beyond control, we cannot rebuild trust on foreign dependency. That is why we are activating emergency powers under the Digital Markets Act and the Crisis Regulation Framework to repurpose idle data centre capacity in Ireland, Germany, and Poland. National governments are being asked to identify underused compute in cloud providers and research institutes; we will redirect it to sovereign AI operations under EU supervision. Simultaneously, we are fast-tracking procurement of alternative lithography tools through the Chips Act emergency clause, with funding unlocked from the European Defence Fund and the Recovery Instrument. This is not a long-term solution — but it is a bridge to survival.
