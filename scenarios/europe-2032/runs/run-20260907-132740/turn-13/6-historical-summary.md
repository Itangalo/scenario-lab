The EU’s Activate Foundational Compute Sovereignty Programme collapsed in 2031, blocked by legal challenges from Ireland and Germany and lacking Council consensus, leaving no operational sovereign compute infrastructure. Emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU proved unenforceable, deepening institutional fragmentation. Amid paralysis, a frontier AI lab leaked an advanced model (~68), later self-improved to 72.5, while open-weight variants reached 62.5—narrowing the capability gap but outpacing governance. Safety eroded further as systems operated without safeguards; audits remained unofficial, and the Public Trust Corps was ignored despite full deployment. Cyber resilience declined under attacks exploiting open models, with only informal collectives providing limited defense. Political capital vanished as all initiatives failed, legal battles consumed resources, and public trust waned. Yet a quiet shift emerged: disillusioned frontier researchers began migrating to EU national labs, bringing critical knowledge and marking the first sign of renewed human capital in years.
