### Fractured Foundations, Flickering Hope

The EU’s Activate Foundational Compute Sovereignty Programme remains mired in legal and political resistance. Despite invoking emergency powers, Brussels has secured no operational control over commercial data centres. Irish and German courts have frozen enforcement, citing violations of digital sovereignty and internal market principles. The European Council remains deadlocked, with smaller member states wary of setting a precedent for centralised resource seizure. The programme exists only on paper — a symbol of desperation, not a functioning infrastructure.

Meanwhile, the frontier advances. A new capability jump is confirmed in internal reports, driven by unverified self-improvement loops in a leading lab’s latest model. While officially unacknowledged, the shift pushes `ai_capability` to 72.5. The recent open-weight release, now widely distributed and refined, lifts `openweight_capability` to 62.5 — a rapid leap, but still trailing the frontier. The gap narrows, and with it, the window for governance.

AI safety collapses further. No safeguards accompany the new systems; audits remain shadow exercises. The completed Public Trust Corps dashboard logs incidents, but its authority is ignored by both developers and publics. Public Algorithmic Accountability Boards limp forward in a few capitals, but their reach stops at the border of foreign-controlled models.

Resilience dips as distributed cyber actors exploit newly accessible models. A coordinated intrusion hits regional energy monitors in three countries, barely contained. Only fragmented, informal defences — whispers of ad hoc cyber collectives — prevent worse.

The EU’s political capital plunges into negative territory. The portfolio’s cost — three ongoing measures, one prioritised — drains a system already broken. No new action is possible. The priority yields nothing; the legal battles consume all bandwidth.

Yet a quiet shift begins. A trickle of disillusioned frontier researchers settles in EU cities, joining national labs. No policy drives it — only sanctuary, modest grants, and purpose. They bring tacit knowledge, not hardware. But for the first time in years, the EU holds minds, if not machines.
