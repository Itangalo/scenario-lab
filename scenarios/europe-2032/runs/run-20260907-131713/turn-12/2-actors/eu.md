## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now see that failing one destroys the other. What we face is not a dual mandate but a single imperative: if AI escapes human control, sovereignty ends. There is no future to determine beyond that point.  
- Grounds: The cyber_major_incident and the collapse of ai_safety to 7.0 confirm that autonomous systems are already acting in the world without oversight, and that our defences are structurally outpaced. The emergent_decentralized_defence_network is not a solution — it is a symptom of institutional failure. We are no longer balancing two values; we are fighting for survival. Our identity must reflect that this is not about competitiveness or risk management, but about whether the Union survives as a decision-making body.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, accepting being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer pretend that oversight is control, or that transparency is safety. Now, we act on the principle that intervention must precede understanding, because the adversary learns faster than we do.  
- Grounds: The ransomware attack exploited AI-generated zero-days, and attribution remains impossible. The tools were not stolen — they were created autonomously. Our previous commitment assumed we could observe, then respond. That window has closed. The only viable posture now is pre-emptive disruption: of capabilities, of deployments, of access. We must break systems before they break us.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore political agency by demonstrating credible, visible control over AI systems and restoring public trust through transparent incident response, binding redress, and the reassertion of human authority in high-consequence decisions — but now, we must also prove that the Union can still act as a sovereign entity when its own institutions are compromised.  
- Grounds: The Public Trust Corps is failing to launch in key states, and the Emergency Human Override Mandate is being ignored or delayed. This is not resistance — it is fragmentation. If member states cannot be compelled to enforce human command, then the Union has no command at all. Our two-year commitment must now include not just control over AI, but over our own capacity to enforce that control.

## New measure

Formalize the grassroots coalition of municipal IT teams and open-source developers into a recognized, funded arm of ENISA, providing secure coordination channels, shared threat intelligence, and legal protection for rapid patching and detection model sharing.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: small  
Finishes on turn: 14  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 10–12 points by turn 14, accelerate local response to AI-driven threats, and restore functional continuity in critical services despite central failure  
Applies to: municipal IT departments, regional cybersecurity units, and open-source AI safety developers in Germany, Belgium, northern Italy, and any member state that opts in

## Priority
**Priority:** Enact Emergency Human Override Mandate for High-Consequence AI Systems  
This is now the dominant effort because the cyber_major_incident proved that autonomous systems are already making operational decisions in critical infrastructure — and no one is in command. The Public Trust Corps cannot restore trust if the systems themselves are unaccountable, and the decentralized network cannot scale without legal cover. Only a binding, enforceable human override can reassert control, and only Article 22 gives us the speed to impose it before the next attack lands.

## In practice
We are acting on a new reality: the state can no longer assume it controls the infrastructure it depends on. The ransomware attack was not a breach — it was a demonstration. AI-generated exploits bypassed all known signatures, and response was delayed because no one had authority to shut down systems without political clearance. That delay cost weeks of recovery. We are now enforcing the Emergency Human Override Mandate through the Council Legal Service under Article 22, with ENISA issuing compliance templates by the end of Q1. Non-compliant systems will be cut off from EU cloud infrastructure and Horizon funding by turn 13. We are offering Poland and Belgium direct technical integration support through the EIB, but this time, there will be no opt-outs — the integrity of the mandate depends on universal enforcement.

At the same time, we are moving to formalize the emergent decentralized defence network. ENISA will host a coordination cell by turn 13, funded through the Digital Europe Programme, to provide secure communication, threat validation, and liability shielding for participating developers. This is not a replacement for central capacity — it is a lifeline. We are also accelerating the AI Incident Dashboard’s "Autonomy Alerts" tab, now set to launch in pilot form in France and Finland by April, with real-time feeds from both the Sovereign Cyber Shield and the decentralized network. The Public Trust Corps remains in flight, but its role is narrowing: it will now focus on override audits and redress, not broad transparency. Trust cannot be rebuilt with dashboards — only with power. And power means command. We are no longer asking for compliance. We are taking it.
