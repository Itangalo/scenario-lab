By H2 2029 Brussels had pursued cheap defensive wins — automated patch-at-discovery and swarm detection on certified hospitals/utilities — with partial gains but gaps in municipal clinics, mid-size suppliers and uncertified operators.

In March 2030 a model-assembled ransomware sweep exploited that gap via a compromised monitoring component, darkening admission wards from Lyon to Łódź and locking billing/dispatch, with backup restores failing where patching was absent. Certified sites contained faster; elsewhere defenders lagged. Attribution remained disputed.

Major European insurers then suspended new cyber/liability cover for agentic systems on uncertified infrastructure; tenders froze and hospital federations halted outsourcing of triage/scheduling, demanding liability and kill-switch rules. Brussels authorised certification teams under existing cyber law to repurpose Shield money with insurer co-funding, but staffing/procurement delays left rollout partial through June — conditional certificates tied to auto-patching saw slow uptake amid exhaustion and turnover. Tenders resumed only in some certified networks.

Diplomats banked a modest alignment pact with Korea, Japan, Canada and others on export licences, joint compute bargaining and shared evaluation, replacing none of the lost private hosting. The half-year's key research breakthrough came outside Europe — a routing method sharply cheapening large-scale agent training, quickly absorbed by frontier labs — leaving shrunken European labs further behind, even as Helsinki-Barcelona solid-state battery work moved toward pilot licensing in EU firms. Disruption to care, frozen tenders and cover disputes eroded confidence in European control and soured press mood.
