### The attack that found the gap
The ransomware sweep came in March, model-assembled and fast-moving. It rode a compromised monitoring component used by regional hospitals, city utilities and two logistics software providers. Screens went dark in admission wards from Lyon to Łódź, billing and dispatch systems locked, and backup restores failed where automated patching had never been installed.

Where continuity certificates and swarm-detection were already in place, teams reported faster containment. Elsewhere — municipal clinics, mid-size suppliers, uncertified operators — defenders were visibly behind. Attribution dragged on for months, with conflicting claims circulating in the press.

### Insurers walk away
Within weeks, major European insurers jointly suspended new cyber and liability cover for any operator running agentic systems on uncertified infrastructure. Tenders froze. Hospital federations halted outsourcing of triage and scheduling tools, demanding liability and kill-switch rules before reconnecting anything.

Brussels authorised certification teams under existing cyber law, seeking to repurpose Shield money with insurer co-funding. Staffing and procurement delays meant deployment was partial through June: conditional certificates were offered to clinics that accepted automatic patching, but uptake was slow amid exhausted staff, turnover, and suspicion of temporary papers. By June tenders were moving again in some certified networks, still stuck outside them. Full rollout will require further turns.

### A coalition and a breakthrough elsewhere
Diplomats banked a modest win: an alignment pact with Korea, Japan, Canada and others on export licences, joint compute bargaining and shared evaluation. It replaced none of the evaporated private hosting, but gave the Union something to bargain with.

The half-year's notable research result came from outside Europe — a new routing method that made large-scale agent training markedly cheaper, quickly absorbed by frontier labs. European labs, still shrunken by the valuation reset, fell further behind in raw capability even as the Helsinki-Barcelona battery work moved toward pilot licensing.

The continent-wide disruption to care and continuity eroded confidence in European control, and press mood turned sour amid delayed care, frozen tenders, and disputes over cover and certificates.
