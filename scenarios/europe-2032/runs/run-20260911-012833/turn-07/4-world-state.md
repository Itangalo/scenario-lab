### A cheap win in a thin half-year
With money gone and frontier access still cut, Brussels shelved big builds and chased the one tailwind it had: defensive tooling that finally worked.

ENISA, with national response teams, pushed automated patch-at-discovery and swarm-behaviour detection onto the hospitals and utilities that already held continuity certificates. Where teams arrived, unpatched estates shrank in days, and insurers offered modest premium relief for certified operators. It was visible, cheap, and popular with exhausted administrators.

It was also partial. Municipal clinics, mid-size suppliers and uncertified operators stayed outside the rollout, and staff turnover undid some gains as fast as they landed. The lights stayed on more reliably, but performance gaps with the lost American models remained.

### Capital flees, labs shrink, robots stall
Global AI valuations reset hard in the autumn. Announced data-centre expansions were cancelled, not delayed, and two private hosting arrangements European planners had counted on evaporated. Frontier labs everywhere talked of smaller next training runs.

Commercial humanoids disappointed in parallel. Vendors sold fixed-task machines for caged warehouses against janitorial wages, and stopped at the cage edge. Defence carrying trials stayed trials. Manufacturers who had promised flexible labour spent the half-year explaining delays to works councils.

The backup gigafactory site ground through permits amid fresh protests; disbursement stayed frozen. The interim hosting pact signed nothing new, and the defecting capital kept its separate deal.

One bright spot was sited in Europe: a Helsinki-Barcelona consortium, using agentic assistants, reported a verified solid-state electrolyte doubling cycle life in the lab, with licensing anchored to EU firms. Battery makers took notice; effects on factories will take years.

Press paired relief at fewer intrusions with gloom about dependence and cancelled futures.
