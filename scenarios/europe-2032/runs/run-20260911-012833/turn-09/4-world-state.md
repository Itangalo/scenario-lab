### Cut off
In early autumn, administrators in hospitals, ministries and logistics firms found their calls to the leading American model returning refusals. No explanation, no appeal channel, only a short notice about revised access conditions. For wards that had rebuilt triage prompts on top of that service after the spring ransomware sweep, the timing was brutal: tenders still frozen, insurer cover still conditional, now the model itself gone.

Press coverage split between outrage at Washington and anger at Brussels for having let critical care rest on a foreign switch. Opposition parties called it a failure of foresight made visible. Health ministers spoke of humiliation in emergency meetings.

### Plugging the gap
Brussels pushed what it could without new money. Certification teams under the existing cyber law kept going clinic by clinic, converting temporary papers into full continuity certificates where automatic patching and swarm detection could be proven. Insurers quietly resumed cover on those sites, and a few frozen tenders moved.

At the same time, health and interior ministers ordered a fallback pool: an EU-hosted openly available model image plus paper and manual procedures for certified operators, bought through existing cloud contracts. Defence and foreign ministers accepted an invitation to join a joint cyber command with real-time telemetry sharing and a linked biosample pact, seconding liaison officers rather than building a parallel headquarters.

Delivery was partial. Procurement frameworks sped orders but understaffed hospitals struggled to install the fallback, and liaison arrangements took months to clear. Containment improved where both pieces landed; elsewhere staff improvised with offline forms.

### A breakthrough elsewhere
The half-year's notable science came from laboratories outside Europe — a physics result resolving a long-standing dispute on exotic materials, with clear implications for batteries and power electronics. Frontier labs absorbed the method within weeks. European firms licensed at the margins, but built nothing frontier-level from it.

By December essential services were degrading rather than stopping, yet public mood had soured further amid delayed care and open dependence.
