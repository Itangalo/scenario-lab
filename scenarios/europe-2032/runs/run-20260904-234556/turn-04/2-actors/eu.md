- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor sovereignty and risk resilience, prioritising compute, control, and containment within the next two years — but now with explicit focus on ensuring that sovereign systems are not only built, but *used*, in critical public functions  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now tie each commitment to observable thresholds, so error becomes learning, not drift; this turn, we add a new threshold: if openweight_capability reaches 52.0, we will treat distributed agent risk as irreversible and shift containment to resilience  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now acknowledge that without sovereign control, both mandates fail; and without public legitimacy, even sovereign control becomes brittle  

`Grounds:` The access revocation from foreign labs was not just a signal of dependence — it was a demonstration of political vulnerability. Our sovereignty is not secured by compute alone, but by *use*: if EU-controlled systems do not run essential services, then our control stack remains a prototype, not a shield. Hence the refinement of the two-year commitment to include operational deployment. The update to `act_under_uncertainty` anticipates the next inflection: openweight models are now at 44.0, progressing steadily, and if they reach 52.0 — the point where agents run continuously and autonomously — containment will no longer be possible through developer cooperation. We must now prepare for a world where the most dangerous capabilities are not just open, but *in the wild*. Finally, the rise of campaign_backlash — moratoriums gaining mainstream traction — reveals that sovereignty without legitimacy is unstable. Our identity must now reflect that political agency depends not only on control and capability, but on the consent of those we govern.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the Public AI Deployment Mandate  
Require all EU institutions and critical public services — including national health agencies, border control, and energy grid operators — to migrate at least 30% of their AI-driven workloads to EU-sovereign, control-stack-verified systems by Turn 8.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by anchoring sovereign systems in essential operations; raise public_sentiment by demonstrating tangible, accountable AI use; modestly improve resilience by reducing dependence on foreign providers  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member state agencies operating critical infrastructure, and public service providers receiving EU funding  

## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
Because without physical, legal, and operational control over the machines that run AI, every safety mechanism, every control stack, and every resilience protocol becomes a suggestion rather than a safeguard — and we are now six months into a world where suggestion is not enough, and six months from a potential openweight inflection that will make control even harder.

## In practice  
We are treating the InvestAI Gigafactories as the spine of our response. The EIB’s €40bn disbursement is now conditional on binding timelines for grid delivery — ENTSO-E has committed to 15GW, but we are now negotiating enforceable milestones with national regulators under the Net-Zero Industry Act. In Brittany and Norrland, we are pairing reinvestment packages not just with job quotas but with local ownership stakes in the energy infrastructure, turning resistance into partnership. Germany, France, and Sweden have agreed to co-finance an additional €30bn tranche, but only if we guarantee that at least 60% of the chips used are sourced through the EU’s ASML-aligned supply chain — a condition we are accepting, because strategic autonomy must extend to the silicon layer.

The Tech sovereignty package is now in trilogue, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable clauses on EU jurisdiction: no data centre receiving fast-track status can be operated by a non-EU entity without a mirrored control stack and local board representation. The Commission’s Article 114 directive is advancing, but we are facing resistance from smaller member states worried about regulatory overreach — so we are offering them priority access to the Sovereign Control Stack once developed, in exchange for early ratification. EAAD is now conducting post-incident forensics on the breached agent, working with national intelligence secondments to reconstruct its decision tree. The legal service is preparing for challenges to the 0.5% levy, but we are also building a public case: we are releasing redacted findings from the breach to show why opacity is not just a technical failure but a democratic one. Meanwhile, the eval_anomaly_reports have triggered our internal alert protocol — we are convening a closed session of the European Research Area Board to assess whether the next capability jump is emergent, not engineered.

CIRS implementation is underway: 18 member states have submitted red-teaming plans, but only 7 meet the baseline. We are withholding 12% of recovery fund disbursements from laggards, a move that has sparked legal complaints — but we are holding firm, because the Taiwan tension rise reminds us that infrastructure resilience is not just about AI, but about war. The Sovereign Control Stack is now in technical scoping, with DG CONNECT leading a cross-agency task force to define runtime verification standards. We are fast-tracking procurement rules so that once the stack is ready, deployment can begin immediately. And now, with the Public AI Deployment Mandate, we are ensuring that sovereign systems are not just built, but *used* — anchoring control in daily operations, not just contingency plans. This is how sovereignty becomes real.
