### Capability Creeps Forward  
AI capability rose again, reaching 58.0, as opaque self-improvement loops in frontier training pipelines produced emergent reasoning patterns. Developers report models solving novel planning tasks without explicit instruction. Verification lags further behind, with no safety architecture yet deployed that can interpret or contain such behaviour.

### Sovereignty Erodes Amid Access Dependence  
The abrupt revocation of frontier model access from EU institutions persists, now widely interpreted as a demonstration of strategic leverage. Despite the Sovereign Control Stack Initiative’s technical scoping, no EU-controlled system is yet operational in critical services. The Union’s AI sovereignty continues to decay, undermined by both capability growth abroad and the absence of sovereign deployment.

### Public Trust Frays  
Moratorium campaigns gain momentum across member states, with broad public support for restrictions on AI in hiring, education, and public services. Polls show growing alignment between left and right on the need for limits, framing AI as a threat to livelihoods and democratic oversight. Public sentiment dips to 40.0 as displaced workers and local governments demand action.

### EU Doubles Down on Compute, But Adoption Lags  
The InvestAI Gigafactories remain the priority, with binding grid milestones secured and co-financing expanded. However, the new Public AI Deployment Mandate — requiring 30% of public AI workloads to shift to sovereign systems by Turn 8 — faces immediate resistance. Member states question feasibility, citing technical immaturity and funding gaps. No migration begins this turn.

Legal challenges to CIRS enforcement intensify, and trilogue negotiations on the Tech Sovereignty Package stall over jurisdictional clauses. EAAD’s forensic work on the breached agent continues, but without legislative backing, its authority remains contested. The EU acts with urgency, but implementation falters under political and technical strain.
