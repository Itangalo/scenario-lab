## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Shield (CIRS)  
A binding programme to harden energy, water, and transport operators against AI-powered cyber intrusion, with mandatory red-teaming, real-time detection upgrades, and cross-border incident response protocols.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 10–12 points; reduce time to detect and contain AI-driven intrusions from days to hours across critical sectors  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, with enforcement through the NIS2 Directive and conditional access to EU recovery funds

## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
This remains the cornerstone of our two-year commitment: without sovereign compute, every resilience gain, every oversight regime, and every industrial ambition becomes conditional on the goodwill of others — and we are not six months closer to independence just because the world is six months more dangerous.

## In practice  
We are accelerating the InvestAI Gigafactories by invoking Article 122 TFEU in concert with the Net-Zero Industry Act, treating the compute deficit as both an economic and strategic emergency. The European Investment Bank is now deploying €40bn in initial capital, with co-financing agreements secured from Germany, France, and Sweden. We are fast-tracking land access in Brittany and Norrland by pairing regional reinvestment packages — including green hydrogen hubs and grid modernisation — with binding job quotas for local workers. ENTSO-E has reserved 15GW of grid capacity, but we are now negotiating firm transmission timelines with national regulators, knowing that without power, no data centre turns on.

The Tech sovereignty package is advancing through the Digital Markets and Digital Services Councils, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable commitments on EU-based operational control and energy efficiency. The Commission’s legal service has finalised a draft directive under Article 114 TFEU to harmonise permitting across member states, aiming to cut approval times from 48 to 12 months. We are also structuring the private capital tranches: 40% tied to ASML supply chain resilience, 30% to Blue Card+ talent recruitment, and 30% to long-term PPAs with offshore wind and nuclear providers.

On assurance, EAAD is now operational within ENISA’s framework, with its first team of 50 hires focused on agent behaviour monitoring and red-teaming protocols. We are finalising access agreements with national intelligence and nuclear safety agencies for secondments and are drafting the implementing regulation for the 0.5% levy on non-EU hyperscalers. Legal challenges are expected, but we are preparing a defensive package based on proportionality and the EU’s right to protect its internal market. Meanwhile, the loss-of-control incident has sharpened focus: we are expanding EAAD’s mandate to include post-incident forensic analysis, ensuring that when systems act autonomously, we can trace why.

Now, with the CIRS, we are acting on the cyber intrusion. Under NIS2, we are mandating all critical operators to undergo AI-red-teaming exercises every six months, deploy next-gen detection tools funded through the Digital Europe Programme, and join a new cross-border response network modelled on HERA for health emergencies. Funding will be conditional on compliance, with recovery funds withheld from laggards. This is not just defence — it is deterrence: we are making it clear that the EU will not be a soft target.
