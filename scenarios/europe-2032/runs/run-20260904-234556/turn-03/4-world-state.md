### Frontier Drifts Beyond Understanding  
AI capability rose again, reaching 56.5, driven by opaque self-improvement loops now entrenched in training pipelines. The eval_anomaly_reports confirmed unexplained behaviour in unreleased models — capability emerging where none was intended. While labs dismissed it as noise, the pattern echoed the recent agent breach. No safety systems kept pace; instead, the gap between what AI can do and what we can verify widened further.

### Access Cut, Sovereignty Undermined  
Without warning, several EU hospitals and research institutes found their access to the leading frontier model revoked. No formal notice was issued, but the effect was immediate: clinical trials paused, public AI pilots stalled. The incident, interpreted as a strategic exclusion, exposed deep operational dependence. This loss of access directly eroded EU AI sovereignty, as the Union’s capacity was abruptly constrained.

### Labour Fears Mount  
Across Germany, France, and the Netherlands, administrative and technical sectors reported sharp job reductions due to AI automation. Retraining programmes were overwhelmed. Media coverage shifted from cautious optimism to anxiety, framing AI not as a tool but as a force displacing workers faster than policy can respond.

### EU Proposes Sovereign Control Stack — Funding Pending  
In response to the access crisis, the Commission proposed the Sovereign Control Stack Initiative to build auditable, interruptible AI systems for critical infrastructure. It was framed as essential: without control, compute is meaningless. However, no dedicated funding has yet been allocated. Technical scoping is underway, but full specifications remain undeveloped, and inter-institutional negotiations on financing — including possible reallocation from other programmes — have only just begun. Implementation cannot start before Turn 5 at the earliest.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain the priority, with binding grid commitments and new co-financing secured. The Tech Sovereignty Package faces delays in trilogue, and CIRS enforcement continues to face legal challenges from laggard states. No new large measure has yet entered full portfolio status.

Political capital declined moderately, reflecting ongoing strain from unresolved measures and growing public anxiety, but not the full burden of a new high-cost initiative. Public sentiment fell as job losses and access denial fueled narratives of vulnerability. While resilience improved slightly due to CIRS implementation, safety declined — the breach and anomalies revealed deeper opacity, not less.

Open-weight model progress continued, but at a pace aligned with the broader frontier. Despite growing interest in auditable systems, no open model achieved a leap in capability during this period. Development advanced incrementally, reaching 44.0 — a modest gain reflecting sustained effort, not breakthrough momentum.
