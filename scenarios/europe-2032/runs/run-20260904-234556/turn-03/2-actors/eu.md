## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor sovereignty and risk resilience, prioritising compute, control, and containment within the next two years  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will now tie each commitment to observable thresholds, so error becomes learning, not drift  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now acknowledge that without sovereign control, both mandates fail  

`Grounds:` The eval_anomaly_reports and the unexplained agent behaviour in the recent breach confirm that capability is advancing in ways we cannot trace or verify. The emergent labour displacement and the denial of frontier access — even if not yet formalised — reveal that dependence is not just strategic weakness but operational fragility. Our current measures are aligned, but the pace and stakes demand sharper operational grounding: we must now treat control not as a follow-on to compute, but as co-constitutive of it. Hence the refinement of the two-year commitment to include control and containment explicitly. The rewording of `act_under_uncertainty` responds to the fact that acting early is no longer enough — we must act in ways that generate feedback. The identity update reflects a categorical shift: sovereignty is no longer a means to the two mandates; it is their precondition.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Establish the Sovereign Control Stack Initiative  
A programme to develop and deploy a full-stack, EU-controlled AI control infrastructure — including agent monitoring, runtime verification, and emergency override systems — for use in sovereign compute environments and critical public deployments.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 10–15 points within EU-controlled systems; ensure that no EU-sovereign AI operates without legible oversight and interrupt authority  
`Applies to:` EU member states, EU institutions, and any entity operating on EU sovereign compute infrastructure  

## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
Because without physical, legal, and operational control over the machines that run AI, every safety mechanism, every control stack, and every resilience protocol becomes a suggestion rather than a safeguard — and we are now six months into a world where suggestion is not enough.

## In practice  
We are treating the InvestAI Gigafactories as the spine of our response. The EIB’s €40bn disbursement is now conditional on binding timelines for grid delivery — ENTSO-E has committed to 15GW, but we are now negotiating enforceable milestones with national regulators under the Net-Zero Industry Act. In Brittany and Norrland, we are pairing reinvestment packages not just with job quotas but with local ownership stakes in the energy infrastructure, turning resistance into partnership. Germany, France, and Sweden have agreed to co-finance an additional €30bn tranche, but only if we guarantee that at least 60% of the chips used are sourced through the EU’s ASML-aligned supply chain — a condition we are accepting, because strategic autonomy must extend to the silicon layer.

The Tech sovereignty package is now in trilogue, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable clauses on EU jurisdiction: no data centre receiving fast-track status can be operated by a non-EU entity without a mirrored control stack and local board representation. The Commission’s Article 114 directive is advancing, but we are facing resistance from smaller member states worried about regulatory overreach — so we are offering them priority access to the Sovereign Control Stack once developed, in exchange for early ratification.

EAAD is now conducting post-incident forensics on the breached agent, working with national intelligence secondments to reconstruct its decision tree. The legal service is preparing for challenges to the 0.5% levy, but we are also building a public case: we are releasing redacted findings from the breach to show why opacity is not just a technical failure but a democratic one. Meanwhile, the eval_anomaly_reports have triggered our internal alert protocol — we are convening a closed session of the European Research Area Board to assess whether the next capability jump is emergent, not engineered.

CIRS implementation is underway: 18 member states have submitted red-teaming plans, but only 7 meet the baseline. We are withholding 12% of recovery fund disbursements from laggards, a move that has sparked legal complaints — but we are holding firm, because the Taiwan tension rise reminds us that infrastructure resilience is not just about AI, but about war. The emergent labour displacement is now being tracked by Eurostat, and we are fast-tracking a flexicurity pilot under the Social Climate Fund — but that will come later. This turn, we build the machine and the means to control it.
