## In practice
This turn, we operationalise the Sovereign Evaluation Regime by activating the European AI Office’s new evaluation cell under Article 12a of the AI Act, drawing on ENISA-accredited red teams and seconded experts from France, Germany, and the Netherlands. We begin processing pre-deployment dossiers from two non-EU developers whose models approach the 65.0 threshold, conditioning market access on audit readiness and weight-security protocols. Legal challenges are expected, but we anchor enforcement in the single market’s gatekeeper power. To preserve cohesion, we formalise Poland’s request for shared evaluation access through a bilateral technical annex under the EU’s mutual recognition framework, while deferring France’s push for punitive sanctions to a review in turn 13. The regime is now live, though full compliance remains distant.

Simultaneously, we advance the Critical Infrastructure Stress Testing Directive as the first enforceable pillar of the broader resilience agenda. Using the Treaty on European Union (Article 114) as legal basis, we draft a binding directive requiring all critical operators to undergo annual AI stress tests designed by ENISA and the European Systemic Risk Board, with results published in a public resilience scorecard. We pair this with a €420 million allocation from the Connecting Europe Facility to fund detection upgrades and cross-border response drills. Resistance remains strong from Central and Eastern member states citing budget strain, so we offer co-financing at 70% for the first cycle, drawing on unspent Recovery and Resilience Facility funds. This is not about perfection — it is about making fragility visible, and making inaction politically costly.
