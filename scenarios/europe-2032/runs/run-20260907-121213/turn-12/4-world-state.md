### A Resilience Push Amid Mounting Pressure

The EU launches the **Critical Infrastructure Stress Testing Directive**, marking its first binding move to expose AI-driven fragility in essential services. Drafted under Article 114, the directive mandates annual stress tests for energy, healthcare, and financial operators, with results publicly scored. ENISA and the European Systemic Risk Board design scenarios focused on autonomous cyber intrusions and cascading grid failures. A €420 million fund supports detection upgrades, co-financed at 70% for struggling member states. Yet resistance persists — Hungary and Slovakia warn of “regulatory overreach,” and Bulgaria delays implementation planning, citing budget constraints.

Meanwhile, the **Sovereign Evaluation Regime** becomes operational as the European AI Office activates its evaluation cell. Red teams from France, Germany, and the Netherlands begin reviewing dossiers from two non-EU developers. Market access is formally conditioned on audit readiness, reinforcing the Union’s gatekeeper leverage. However, no model yet complies, and legal challenges gain momentum in national courts, questioning the regime’s enforcement teeth. Poland’s bilateral annex grants limited shared access but underscores fragmentation.

AI capability advances to 72.0, driven by self-improving training loops at frontier labs. Open-weight models reach 59.5, enabling more robust underground cyber toolkits. With no safety breakthroughs and no new safeguards on deployed systems, **ai_safety** remains stagnant at 22.0. The gap between capability and control widens.

**Resilience** rises slightly to 59.0, reflecting early momentum from the new directive, though full effects await implementation. **EU AI sovereignty** declines to 19.0: capability rose by 1.5, triggering −1, and Poland’s partial disengagement from enforcement counts as ongoing **member_state_defection**, adding another −1. No category 4 measure completes, and no access is secured.

Political capital falls to 28.0. The portfolio charge is steep: −3 for the Sovereign Evaluation Regime, −3 for the new Critical Infrastructure Directive, and −1 for prioritising the former. No proposal bonus applies — the directive responds to long-building risks, not a recent incident. Public sentiment holds at 32.0, as healthcare AI benefits are offset by rising anxiety over disinformation and infrastructure vulnerability.
