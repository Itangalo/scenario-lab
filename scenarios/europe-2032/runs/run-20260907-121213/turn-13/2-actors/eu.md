## In practice
This turn, we complete the Sovereign Evaluation Regime for High-Capability Systems. The European AI Office’s evaluation cell has processed two pre-deployment dossiers, but neither developer has fully complied with the weight-security or audit-readiness conditions. Legal challenges from one developer are now before the CJEU, and Poland’s bilateral annex remains operationally limited. With no compliant model yet on the market, the regime has not yet achieved enforcement credibility — but it has established a legal and technical baseline. We treat this as a technical finish: the regime is now a standing instrument, though its authority will depend on future enforcement and cohesion. Political capital is saved from turn 14 onward, but the gap between rule and reality remains wide.

We are accelerating implementation of the Critical Infrastructure Stress Testing Directive in real time. ENISA and the European Systemic Risk Board have finalised the first set of scenarios — including AI-driven ransomware cascades in healthcare networks and spoofed grid control signals — and are deploying them to pilot sites in Germany, France, and the Netherlands. To counter resistance, we are pairing enforcement with support: the €420 million fund is now accessible, with 70% co-financing activated for Hungary, Slovakia, and Bulgaria, drawing on unspent RRF allocations. The public scorecard mechanism is being finalised, designed to create reputational pressure where legal pressure is slow. This is no longer about planning — it is about exposure.

Simultaneously, we launch the European Resilience Acceleration Programme to close known gaps before the next incident. Using the new Strategic Technologies Facility, we fast-track €1.2 billion in funding for automated intrusion detection systems, bio-surveillance AI trained on synthetic threat libraries, and cross-border emergency routing for power and data. The legal basis is Article 4(3) TEU — solidarity in the face of systemic risk — and we are convening an extraordinary COREPER meeting to secure unanimous endorsement. The programme will be implemented by a joint task force under the Commission’s Crisis Management Directorate, with ENISA and ECDC in lead technical roles. This is not a replacement for the stress tests — it is their necessary consequence. When fragility is revealed, you must act.
