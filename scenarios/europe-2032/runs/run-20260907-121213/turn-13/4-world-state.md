### A Capability Surge Amid Fragile Defenses

A sudden leap in AI capability reshapes the frontier: systems demonstrate autonomous code generation and cyber exploitation at a level that bypasses conventional defenses. This advance, while narrow, marks a qualitative shift in offensive potential. Simultaneously, the most advanced models cease producing human-readable reasoning, rendering existing oversight mechanisms blind. Interpretability tools used by regulators fail overnight, and no alternative is ready.

The EU completes the Sovereign Evaluation Regime, establishing legal authority to audit high-capability systems. However, no model yet complies with its requirements. A major developer’s legal challenge before the CJEU casts doubt on enforcement, and Poland’s bilateral access deal further fragments cohesion. The regime stands as law—but not yet as leverage.

The Critical Infrastructure Stress Testing Directive moves into live deployment. Pilot tests in Germany, France, and the Netherlands uncover critical vulnerabilities: AI-driven ransomware simulations cripple hospital networks, and spoofed grid signals trigger emergency manual overrides. A public scorecard system is finalized, aiming to use transparency to drive compliance. Financial and energy sectors respond, but Hungary and Slovakia continue to resist, citing sovereignty concerns.

In response, the EU launches the **European Resilience Acceleration Programme**, a €1.2 billion push to harden cyber and bio-defenses. Funding flows to automated intrusion detection, synthetic-threat-trained bio-surveillance AI, and cross-border emergency routing. Legal basis under Article 4(3) TEU is accepted at an emergency COREPER meeting, though debate over centralization intensifies.

Despite setbacks, resilience improves as coordinated action begins. Public sentiment rises slightly, buoyed by visible crisis response and medical AI advances elsewhere—though the EU’s limited access tempers enthusiasm. Sovereignty declines further as capability outpaces control and supply-chain pressure mounts.

### US Leverages Its Chokehold

Washington forces the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s export and servicing of mature lithography tools, expanding controls beyond cutting-edge machines to those vital for ordinary chip production. The move, justified by national security, effectively weaponizes the EU’s dependence on foreign-controlled supply chains. Brussels protests, but leverage is thin.
