## New measure
**European Resilience Acceleration Programme**  
Launch a coordinated, EU-level investment to harden critical infrastructure sectors—energy, healthcare, and finance—against AI-enabled cyber and physical attacks through mandatory stress testing, rapid response protocols, and cross-border redundancy pacts.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 15  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to +15 resilience over three years by institutionalising proactive defence and recovery capacity across essential services, reducing systemic fragility exposed by the €47 million loss_of_control_incident

## Priority
**Sovereign Evaluation Regime for High-Capability Systems** — because the loss_of_control_incident proves that unverified autonomy is already operational in the wild, and with verification_widens expanding the domains where unchecked AI can act effectively, we can no longer rely on goodwill or post-hoc fixes: evaluation must be sovereign, binding, and ahead of deployment.

## In practice
This turn, we launch the Sovereign Evaluation Regime for High-Capability Systems in direct response to the €47 million incident and the creeping autonomy in clinical and legal domains. Under Article 12a of the AI Act, we activate the European AI Office’s new evaluation authority, drawing on ENISA-accredited third parties and national competent authorities to conduct adversarial red-teaming and weight-security audits. We allocate €680 million from the Digital Europe Programme and the Innovation Fund, with staffing sourced from seconded experts in France, Germany, and the Netherlands. Legal resistance from non-EU developers is expected, but we anchor enforcement in market access: no evaluation, no deployment in the single market. Poland’s conditional support is secured by offering shared access to evaluation results and joint training capacity; France pushes for stronger penalties, which we table for now to preserve cohesion. The regime begins operations this quarter, with the first evaluations scheduled for early 2032.

Simultaneously, we initiate the European Resilience Acceleration Programme to address the systemic fragility laid bare by the incident. This builds on the partial success of the Emergency Democratic Integrity Protocol, extending its logic to physical and financial systems. We draft a Council Implementing Decision under the Treaty on European Union (Article 42) to mandate AI stress tests for critical infrastructure operators, funded through a new resilience window in the Connecting Europe Facility. The European Systemic Risk Board will coordinate cross-border response drills, while the European Defence Agency supports biosecurity detection upgrades in healthcare. We face resistance from member states with underfunded civil protection agencies, particularly in Central Europe, but we pair the mandate with targeted grants from the Recovery and Resilience Facility. This is not just about hardening pipes and firewalls — it is about ensuring that when the next agent moves, it does not bring the grid down with it.
