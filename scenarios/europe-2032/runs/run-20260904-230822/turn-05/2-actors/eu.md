## In practice
We are executing the Cyber Shield Expansion under Article 185 TFEU, treating it as a crisis response with strategic permanence. ENISA is now coordinating with national CSIRTs in Germany, France, and the Netherlands to deploy the first wave of defensive agents by December, using EU-trained models hosted on compute reserved under the now-finished Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive. The €3.8bn allocation from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility is being fast-tracked through the European Commission’s emergency procurement channel, with hardware delivery guaranteed by Q1 2029. We are requiring integration from all critical operators in energy, health, and transport by mid-2029, with phased compliance starting in high-exposure nodes—regional hospitals and rail signalling hubs—this December.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on sovereignty: the Sovereign Model Development Initiative is now governed by the European AI Research Consortium (EAIRC), with EuroHPC, CEA, Forschungszentrum Jülich, and INRIA leading training and red-teaming. The 30% sovereign compute floor is being allocated as planned—60% to training, 30% to safety evaluation, 10% to public-interest applications—with hardware access secured from non-US-aligned providers. We are also leveraging the Tech Sovereignty Package to tie data centre expansion to national security: in Poland and Finland, new AI infrastructure must meet 70% local employment targets *and* integrate into the Cyber Shield network to receive grid priority. Resistance from private hyperscalers on data sharing persists, but we are framing cooperation not as a commercial concession but as a condition of market access—sovereignty means no free ride on EU stability.
