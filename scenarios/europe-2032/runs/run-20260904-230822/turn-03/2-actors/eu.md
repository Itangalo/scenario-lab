## In practice
We are activating the sovereign compute base secured through the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive by launching the Sovereign Model Development Initiative under a new Article 185 programme. This is not a research pilot — it is a deployment-grade effort to build, train, and operate a full AI stack under EU legal and physical control, using the 30% compute carve-out now legally reserved for public-interest use. The European AI Research Consortium (EAIRC) will be convened by the Commission with EuroHPC, national HPC centres, and selected academic and industrial partners — including those outside the US cloud orbit — to co-develop the model architecture, training regime, and safety instrumentation. We are allocating €4.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and redirected cohesion funds to cover the first two turns of development, with procurement fast-tracked under emergency innovation clauses.

At the same time, we are pressing forward with the Tech Sovereignty Package, using the momentum from the emergent grid resilience initiative and the cyber defence breakthrough to secure buy-in for accelerated permitting zones in three member states — France, Poland, and Finland — where local opposition has been strongest. We are offering grid priority and tax rebates to developers who commit to 70% local employment and open-weight threat model sharing with ENISA. This is not just about building faster — it is about building differently: with resilience and sovereignty co-designed from the start. The bilateral deal by the major member state with the US hyperscaler remains a wound, but we are containing it by making EU-controlled infrastructure not just available, but superior in reliability, legal certainty, and operational autonomy. The world is accelerating — we will not chase it. We will anchor it.
