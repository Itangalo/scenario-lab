### Accelerating Capability, Widening Gaps  
AI capability advances to 57.5 as recursive training loops and agent coordination mature, enabling systems to execute complex, multi-day research and cyber operations with minimal oversight. The leap widens the gap between performance and control: no safety breakthrough occurs, and `ai_safety` drops to 29.0 as shipped systems remain opaque and vulnerable to emergent behaviour.

### Sovereignty Gains, Political Strain  
The **Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** formally completes, legally reserving 30% of frontier compute in the EU for public-interest use. This marks a symbolic and material shift, enabling the launch of the **Sovereign Model Development Initiative**—a large category 4 measure aiming to build a full AI stack under EU control by mid-2029. Initial funding is secured, and the European AI Research Consortium begins formation, though technical coordination across member states proves slow.

Simultaneously, the **Tech Sovereignty Package** gains traction in France, Poland, and Finland, where grid priority incentives unlock fast-tracking for two new data centres. However, local unions protest the 70% employment target as unenforceable, and open-weight sharing with ENISA faces resistance from private partners wary of liability.

Public sentiment dips slightly to 43.0 as backlash grows against unchecked AI expansion. Campaigns calling for data centre moratoriums and worker protections gain mainstream support, framing the EU’s push as elite-driven and socially detached.

Despite these tensions, resilience improves marginally to 40.0, buoyed by the **emergent_grid_resilience_initiative**—pilot AI systems in energy networks demonstrate faster threat detection—and the **cyber_defence_breakthrough**, which shifts the offensive-defensive balance in favour of defenders.
