### Acceleration Amid Uncertainty  
AI capability climbs to 55.5 as frontier labs refine recursive optimisation in training loops, enabling faster iteration on agent-based systems. The advance widens the gap between performance and understanding: no safety breakthrough occurs, and the prior incident within a leading lab remains unaddressed in shipped systems. With no new safeguards implemented, `ai_safety` slips to 31.0.

### Fragmentation and Initiative  
A major member state signs a bilateral AI infrastructure deal with a US hyperscaler, granting preferential access to domestic compute in exchange for joint R&D and cloud migration. The move undermines the EU’s unified stance on strategic autonomy, triggering quiet recriminations in Brussels. While not a full withdrawal, the act signals weakening cohesion — a political cost without immediate sovereignty loss, as the bloc retains partial access.

In response to the stealthy grid intrusions, a coalition of member states and regulators launches an emergent initiative to deploy AI-driven anomaly detection across energy networks. Pilots in three countries show reduced detection lag, but funding is patchy and coordination thin.

### EU Action: Scaling Resilience  
The European Union responds by proposing the **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Acceleration Fund**, a large category 6 measure backed by €15bn and tied to cohesion transfers. The proposal mandates deployment of locally hosted detection models across critical sectors by Q1 2029, with early planning now underway. ENISA is tasked with drafting technical standards, and a liability shield is under discussion to encourage adoption. However, formal approval and implementation will take time — no deployment has begun, consistent with the minimum two-turn timeline for high-cost measures.

Meanwhile, the **Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** advances through COREPER II. A voluntary carve-out offered to US providers — 35% capacity commitment for regulatory benefits — draws interest but no firm pledges. Legal challenges loom, and full adoption remains uncertain.

Public sentiment rises slightly as visible action counters narratives of paralysis. The resilience push gains early credibility, though tangible results are still pending.
