## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that resilience is not built in peacetime planning cycles when attacks are already in motion. This turn, we are converting the emergent grid resilience initiative into a binding Union-wide programme under Article 172 TFEU, backed by an emergency allocation from the Cohesion Fund and the newly established Critical Infrastructure Resilience Acceleration Fund. The system will deploy lightweight, locally hosted models trained to detect anomalous grid behaviour using synthetic threat patterns derived from Kimi K3-level capabilities — not chasing the frontier, but mirroring the tools already loose. These models will run on secure edge infrastructure within national operators’ own networks, minimising cloud dependency and ensuring continuity even if external providers degrade service or suffer compromise.

ENISA will be tasked with certification and baseline configuration, while the European Energy Security Agency (EESA) coordinates rollout timelines. We are attaching disbursement of cohesion transfers to compliance deadlines: no later than Q1 2028 for Tier 1 operators. To overcome resistance in capitals that still treat cyber as a back-office concern, we are pairing the mandate with a liability shield for operators who adopt the certified system in good faith — harm caused by undetected anomalies will be eligible for compensation from a new Union Mutualisation Pool. At the same time, we are advancing the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive through COREPER II, offering US hyperscalers a carve-out: if they voluntarily commit 35% of their EU-based capacity to public interest uses ahead of the directive’s enforcement, they gain fast-track access to both the InvestAI Gigafactories pipeline and priority dispute resolution under the Digital Markets Act. This is not concession — it is pre-emption. We are building autonomy not by expelling others, but by making dependence uncompetitive.
