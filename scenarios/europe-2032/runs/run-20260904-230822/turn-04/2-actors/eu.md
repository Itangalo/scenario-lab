## In practice
We are treating the cyber_major_incident not as an outlier but as a signal: the offensive use of AI is no longer theoretical. The attack — a coordinated ransomware sweep across regional health systems and transport hubs, exploiting model-generated zero-days — bypassed legacy detection systems and cascaded faster than human teams could respond. Attribution remains uncertain, but the tooling bears the hallmarks of agent-assisted development. In response, we are activating emergency clauses under Article 185 TFEU to expand the Cyber Shield programme into a fully integrated, AI-driven incident response framework. This will link national CSIRTs through a secure ENISA-operated backbone, deploying EU-trained defensive agents capable of real-time anomaly correlation, automatic isolation of compromised nodes, and verified countermeasure deployment. We are allocating €3.8bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, fast-tracking procurement to ensure deployment by mid-2029 — but with minimum viable capabilities in place by the end of this turn in the highest-risk sectors.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the Sovereign Model Development Initiative by finalising the European AI Research Consortium (EAIRC) governance structure, with EuroHPC, CEA, Forschungszentrum Jülich, and INRIA as core nodes, and binding agreements with three non-US-aligned hardware providers to ensure stack-level control. The 30% sovereign compute floor, now legally secured, will be allocated 60% to training, 30% to red-teaming, and 10% to public-interest applications like climate modelling. We are also pressing the Tech Sovereignty Package forward, using the cyber incident to reframe data centre expansion not as a commercial priority but as a security imperative. In France, Poland, and Finland, we are offering conditional grid priority: developers must meet the 70% local employment target *and* integrate their infrastructure into the new Cyber Shield network. Resistance from private partners on open-weight sharing with ENISA remains, but we are making clear: access to EU markets and incentives now depends on verifiable cooperation in threat defence. Sovereignty is not just about compute — it is about control, continuity, and collective survival.
