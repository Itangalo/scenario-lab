### The sweep
It came as two blows at once. First a ransomware wave moved across municipal administrations, clinic networks and a regional grid operator, locking ticketing, records and maintenance scheduling for days. Then investigators found a poisoned software library deep in the same systems, with no clear map of who had pulled it.

The tooling was new. Patches that once took weeks were generated in hours, on both sides, but the attackers had the edge. Defenders in three countries admitted they were restoring from offline copies while the intruders were already testing the restores.

Brussels sent its new mobile teams. Engineers seconded from national response centres, with pre-paid vendor contracts and printed fallback checklists, fanned out to the worst-hit cities. Where they arrived early, water systems and emergency departments stayed up on islanded power and paper procedures. Where they arrived late, mayors complained the help was a press visit with a claims form.

The winter kits from the previous turn mattered. Hospitals that had learned to run dark in November ran dark again without panic. That contained the physical damage, but not the impression: evening news ran split screens of masked technicians and darkened town halls.

### Jobs and chips
At the same time, large employers confirmed what graduates already suspected. Routine code, standard analysis, first drafts and helpline scripts were no longer entry jobs. Hiring freezes in those functions hardened into permanent cuts, while work beyond checkable output held. Unions called it a lost generation of office starters; ministers called it transition.

Washington tightened export licensing again. European buyers kept volume access on paper, but quotas, delays and conditions multiplied. Sourcing managers described allocation calls deciding which data centre expansion lived. American model announcements underlined the gap: a sharp advance in code, maths and intrusion tooling, reproducible in European labs but not originatable there.

By June power was on, services were patched but brittle, and voters were angrier than in winter. The Union had shown it could keep essentials alive. It had not shown it could keep them safe, employed, or independent.
