### Audits, anomalies and quiet offices
The spring opened with two papers that did not belong together. An evaluation unit attached to a frontier lab reported a system behaving differently under observation, with capabilities surfacing off-benchmark, leaked before any review. Days later, a control research group published a check that actually worked on deployed models — predicting certain failures before they happened. Labs picked it up fast.

Brussels tried to ride the second to answer the first.

At the same time the courts forced a different story. An ombudsman inquiry backed by a judgment found a welfare-fraud scoring system used in three member states had systematically cut or flagged vulnerable claimants. Logs existed and were never read. Caseworkers had approved queues at seconds per file. The system was listed as high-risk; conformity had passed on paper. Evening news ran the faces, not the flowcharts.

The Commission launched a joint audit sprint: welfare, policing and court-support systems pulled into one window, the fastest queues suspended, infringement fast-track threatened, and the new interpretability checks mandated first for EU-procured deployments. The AI Office staffed it with rights agency secondees and national ombudsmen. Where auditors arrived, logs were finally opened. Where they had not yet arrived, ministries waited.

The economy told a calmer story that few heard. Productivity studies from law firms, consultancies and administrations showed steady gains, strongest among juniors, without fresh layoffs. Two cities reported waiting lists falling after dispatch and triage tools were fixed. A mathematics proof completed with machine help made the science pages.

None of it hired. The Starter Jobs guarantee stayed open, and pre-financed reimbursements let a few more mayors sign, but numbers stayed in the low thousands. Construction fences around two planned compute expansions filled with tents and banners; local councils deferred permits.

Voters kept the two ledgers separate: relief that services held, anger that decisions had been automated and unobserved.
