### Patching holds, hiring stalls
The second half of 2031 was quieter on the screens, harder in the town halls.

The Recovery Corps finished its tour. Mobile teams closed their last tickets in October, leaving behind certified clean backups, islanding binders and a patched dependency list that municipalities were told to maintain. Where the work had landed early, autumn audits passed without incident. Water utilities ran drills on paper fallback. Clinics restored records. The physical recovery was real, and local press noted it.

It did not feel like safety. A new round of American code and intrusion models circulated in European labs — reproducible, still not originatable — and allocation calls for accelerators continued to decide which expansions lived. Sourcing managers spoke openly of quotas and delays.

Brussels tried to answer with jobs. The Starter Jobs and Municipal Repair Guarantee promised EU reimbursement for twelve-month starter contracts: young administrators and technicians hired to do cleanup, backup hygiene and fallback staffing, with union-signed retraining. Mayors welcomed the single claims form. Public employment services opened matching lists.

Delivery lagged the announcement. With budgets exhausted and cohesion funds repurposed, reimbursements arrived slowly. Some municipalities hired; many waited for written guarantees before signing contracts. Unions praised the curricula while warning the posts were temporary. Graduates queued, but hiring numbers by December were in the hundreds, not thousands.

Voters noticed the repair more than the hiring. Evening news no longer showed darkened town halls, but neither did it show full offices. The Union had kept essentials running and begun to pay for visible work. Trust stopped falling. It did not rebound.
