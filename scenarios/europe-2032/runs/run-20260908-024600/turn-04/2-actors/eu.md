## In practice  
This turn, we are executing on the EAIA mandate by finalizing the legal framework under Article 114 TFEU and convening a special COREPER session to break the deadlock on site selection. The compromise—national veto on location, binding standards on connectivity and security—will go to the Council for adoption. We are pairing it with the first tranche of the €30bn sovereign compute bond, tied to milestones in grid readiness and security certification, to lock in private investment. ENISA is already drafting the technical annexes for weight custody and training run logging, ensuring that any system trained on EU soil falls under enforceable jurisdiction. This is not about building faster than the frontier—we cannot—but about ensuring that what *is* built here cannot be weaponized elsewhere.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the Mandatory Pre-Release Red Teaming measure through the Commission’s delegated act process, with input from a newly convened panel of red teams: Fraunhofer, CNRS, and a civil society consortium led by AlgorithmWatch. The first test protocols are being piloted on a model at 54.0 capability, and we are preparing public summaries to build credibility. U.S. developers are lobbying hard against it, warning of fragmentation, but we are holding firm: market access requires accountability. The EP’s Internal Market and Civil Liberties committees are aligned, though we expect pushback on the transparency provisions—some want more, some want less. We are offering a carve-out for startups below €500M valuation, conditional on third-party sponsorship, to ease the burden without sacrificing oversight.

But the decisive move this turn is the export control measure. We have convened the Member States under the Dual-Use Regulation framework, leveraging ASML, Infineon, and STMicroelectronics as co-designers of the technical thresholds. The controls target not just chips, but the full stack: NVIDIA-class GPUs, EDA tools from Synopsys and Cadence, and open-source distributed training frameworks when bundled with commercial support. We are working with the U.S. Department of Commerce bilaterally to align on baseline thresholds, but we will act unilaterally if coordination stalls. This is not a trade barrier—it is a containment line. If we do not freeze openweight_capability here, at 45.0, we lose the ability to govern what happens next. The cost will be high: German industry fears retaliation, French startups worry about access, and the Commission’s digital economy directorate warns of innovation flight. But we would rather be accused of overreach today than preside over a cascade tomorrow.
