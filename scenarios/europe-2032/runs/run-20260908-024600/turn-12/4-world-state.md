### Resilience Gains from Pact Activation  
The EU formally joins a joint cyber and bio threat response pact, gaining real-time telemetry and coordinated protocols. This delivers tangible improvements in detection speed and response coordination, pushing `resilience` to 41.0. However, uneven implementation persists: smaller utilities and regional grids remain outside the zero-trust framework, and bio-detection hubs are still in pilot phase.

### Sovereignty Fractures Further  
A member state signs a bilateral deal with a foreign hyperscaler, bypassing EU data sovereignty and compute-sharing rules. The move undermines the Union’s unified front, exposing strategic dependency. Though the *Enforce Binding Grid Integration Deadlines* measure finished this turn, its impact is nullified by non-compliance and legal challenges. With `ai_capability` rising and no sovereign compute delivered, `eu_ai_sovereignty` drops to 24.5.

However, the new large category 4 measure costs −3, and the portfolio now includes two large and two small measures, with a priority, totaling −8 per turn. The proposal bonus is denied—it responds to a structural flaw, not a recent shock. Political capital falls to 31.0.
