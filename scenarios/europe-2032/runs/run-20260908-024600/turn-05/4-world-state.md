### US Election Sets Stage for Alliance

The United States elects a new president committed to technological cooperation with allied democracies. The administration signals structured access to frontier AI systems for vetted institutions, joint safety evaluations, and aligned export controls. While the posture does not take effect until next turn, early coordination begins, offering the EU breathing room after years of unilateral pressure.

### Three Measures Land, But Momentum Falters

The European AI Infrastructure Agency’s Fast-Track Mandate clears its final legislative hurdle, granting the Commission authority to enforce connectivity and security standards—though national vetoes on siting remain. Simultaneously, Mandatory Pre-Release Red Teaming becomes operational, with the first pilot completed and protocols scaled by EU research institutes. The Open-Weight Capability Freeze also finalizes under the Dual-Use Regulation, halting exports of key training components.

Despite these legal wins, physical deployment lags. Grid commitments for InvestAI Gigafactories remain unsigned, and no new sovereign compute comes online. The priority push yields only preliminary site discussions, slowed by regional opposition and energy concerns.

### Capability Creeps Forward

AI capability advances to 59.5, driven by improved agent coordination and training efficiency. Open-weight models reach 47.0, aided by community distillation of leaked tooling—just below the threshold for unsupervised operation, but the freeze holds for now.

### Safety Edges Up, Resilience Dips

With red-teaming now binding, ai_safety rises slightly to 30.0. Yet resilience declines to 43.0 as systemic fragility grows amid rising automation in public services and no active cyber-hardening program.
