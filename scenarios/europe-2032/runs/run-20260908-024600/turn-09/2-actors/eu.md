- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The decisive bottleneck is no longer legal or financial but physical and political—resistance in member states, grid inertia, and legal challenges have delayed deployment despite completed Gigafactories and available capital. The openweight frontier is closing in, and with global capability now at 61.0 and openweight at 49.0, the window to establish EU-controlled systems before the capability becomes unmanageable is narrowing. The commitment must now reflect that sovereignty is not built by funding or law alone, but by forcing deployment through resistance. This means overriding, binding, and conditioning access to EU funds on compliance—because without operational systems under EU jurisdiction, all other ambitions—safety, resilience, influence—rest on air.

## In practice
This turn, we are converting promise into compulsion. The Deploy Grid-Ready Compute Corridors initiative ends with a legal override under Article 114 TFEU already asserted, ENISA security certification protocols in place, and €40bn in funding ready—but no wires energized. We are now acting on the authority we claimed: the EAIA, backed by the Commission, will issue binding grid integration deadlines to Belgium, Poland, and Finland by Q4, requiring certified grid readiness plans with firm timelines for physical deployment. Failure to submit or meet them triggers automatic initiation of funding clawback procedures and revocation of ENISA weight custody certification, blocking access to sovereign models. This is not a threat—it is the terms of participation.

Simultaneously, we are holding the line on social stability. The Pilot Transitional AI Adjustment Fund is now operational, with €8bn drawn from the European Social Fund and matched by national co-financing. We have secured a temporary pause in strikes from the ETUC in exchange for union access to sovereign AI tools for workforce transition planning. The first retraining cohorts in legal tech oversight and compliance engineering are launching in Germany and France, with mandatory redeployment quotas now law for firms automating above 20% of their junior roles. This is not retreat—it is parallel enforcement: we are building the future while containing its cost.
