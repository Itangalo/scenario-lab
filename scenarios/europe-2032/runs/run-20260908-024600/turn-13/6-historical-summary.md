Global AI capability rises to 67.0, driven by autonomous research and open-weight models (59.5) that enable exploit generation and protein simulation, exacerbating dual-use risks amid absent safety enforcement. A wave of AI-generated ransomware disrupts health systems across three EU states, exposing response gaps despite early warnings from the Joint Cyber & Bio Threat Response Pact. Simultaneously, EU access to a critical frontier model is cut without explanation, paralyzing key institutions. In response, the EU invokes emergency powers to fast-track sovereign compute via EuroHPC-JU, redirecting €8.3bn and overriding national permits for data centres in the Rhineland and near Lyon. The move triggers legal threats from two member states and deepens resentment over cohesion fund linkages. Public sentiment collapses further as crises reinforce perceptions of dependency, elite detachment, and systemic failure, compounded by the unresolved welfare scandal and expired retraining programs. Resilience sees minimal gains from pact activation, but uneven implementation and two major incidents erode assurance, while EU AI sovereignty and political capital remain under pressure.
