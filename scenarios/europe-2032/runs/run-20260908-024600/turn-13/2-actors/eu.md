## In practice
This turn, we are acting on two fronts: containment and foundation. On containment, the cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep paralyzing regional health systems in three member states — has confirmed that offensive AI capability now outpaces defence, even where detection protocols exist. The joint threat pact is live, but its value is capped by access: our analysts can see the signatures, but cannot run countermeasures at speed because the models required are hosted abroad and now inaccessible. That is the second blow: eu_frontier_access_denied lands not as an anomaly, but as the logical end of outsourcing our cognitive infrastructure. Hospitals, research institutes, and critical agencies are locked out of the very tools they relied on — not due to misuse, but because access is discretionary, revocable, and unappealable.

We are therefore doubling down on sovereign compute not as a long-term ambition, but as an emergency necessity. The Commission has invoked Article 114 TFEU and the Crisis Protocol for Strategic Digital Infrastructure to fast-track two EuroHPC-JU sites — one in the Rhineland, one near Lyon — granting them emergency powers to override national permitting, secure grid priority, and bypass procurement delays. €8.3bn in redirected Digital Europe and Recovery funds are now ringfenced, with binding conditions: full EU legal jurisdiction, no foreign ownership of operational control, and mandatory integration with the joint threat pact’s detection layer. We are negotiating with national governments under duress: support will be tied to cohesion fund disbursements, and non-compliance will trigger automatic clawbacks. The message is clear — if you will not build with us, you will not benefit from what we build. This is not business as usual. This is the re-grounding of European agency. We will have compute, or we will have nothing.
