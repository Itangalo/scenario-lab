### Crisis Compounds

A wave of AI-generated ransomware cripples regional health systems across three member states, exploiting zero-day vulnerabilities identified and weaponised by autonomous agents. The Joint Cyber & Bio Threat Response Pact provides early warnings, but response lags — defenders lack the compute to run countermeasures at scale. At the same moment, access to the leading frontier model is abruptly revoked for EU-based users. No explanation is given, and appeals are ignored. Hospitals, research labs, and ministries relying on the model for diagnostics and planning are left stranded.

### Sovereign Compute Pushed, But at a Cost

In response, the EU invokes emergency powers to accelerate the EuroHPC-JU sovereign compute rollout, fast-tracking two data centres in the Rhineland and near Lyon. National permitting is overridden, grid access prioritised, and €8.3bn redirected. However, resistance flares: two member states threaten legal action, citing sovereignty breaches. Cohesion fund linkages deepen resentment, framing the move as coercion, not solidarity.

### Public Trust Erodes Further

The dual shocks — system failure and external exclusion — are widely perceived as a crisis of dependency. Media depict ministers helpless without foreign models, while displaced workers protest outside shuttered public offices. The welfare scandal lingers, retraining funds remain depleted, and the emergency compute drive is seen as reactive, elitist, and exclusionary. Public sentiment plummets.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Crumbles

Global AI capability advances to 67.0 as labs refine autonomous research loops. Open-weight models, now capable of generating exploits and simulating proteins, reach 59.5. With no safety measures landing on shipped systems and two severe incidents occurring, assurance collapses. Resilience inches up from pact activation, but uneven implementation limits gains.
