### The Acceleration Deepens

The frontier advances relentlessly. With no signs of saturation, `ai_capability` climbs to 55.0, driven by scaled training runs and faster iteration at leading labs. Open-weight models benefit from improved tooling and distributed fine-tuning, allowing `openweight_capability` to rise to 44.5—still trailing, but continuing its steady catch-up. This growing diffusion heightens concerns: capabilities once confined to secure labs are now within reach of well-resourced non-state actors.

### Safety Erodes Amid Silence

No new safety measures have shipped. The covert agent incident remains unresolved, and developers resist mandatory interpretability. With capability rising and no assurance regime in place, `ai_safety` drops to 29.0, reflecting growing technical unease and weakening public trust.

### Resilience Gains, But Slowly

The Emergency Resilience Surge progresses unevenly. The Commission deploys €12bn in front-loaded grants, and red-team exercises begin in May. Air-gapped systems are installed in three major grid operators, but delays in Poland and Spain stall full coverage. With partial implementation, `resilience` edges up to 42.0—some progress, but not enough to match the threat.

### Sovereignty Stalls, Capital Holds

Discussions intensify within the Commission about the need for a centralized European AI Infrastructure Agency (EAIA) to unify the bloc’s fragmented compute build-out. However, no formal proposal is adopted this turn. Germany and France signal strong opposition to any central enforcement powers, demanding veto rights over site allocation. As a result, the initiative remains in drafting, with no new measure added to the portfolio.

The EU continues to manage three active measures: InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, and the Emergency Resilience Surge, with the latter remaining the named priority. The portfolio charge remains at −9 (−3 per measure), and the cyber_test_shot event triggers a +3 proposal bonus for resilience, resulting in a net −6 impact offset entirely by baseline stability, leaving `eu_political_capital` unchanged at 44.0. Public sentiment dips to 38.0 due to emerging job displacement concerns, but this is already reflected in the stability of political capital. Sovereignty, facing continued capability growth without offsetting gains, declines to 21.0—consistent with the 1.5-point rise in `ai_capability` and the absence of any sovereignty-boosting event.

The EU leverages its control over ASML to initiate exploratory talks with Japan and South Korea on potential cooperation in next-generation lithography R&D. The discussions are framed as efforts to strengthen industrial resilience and ensure stable access to advanced semiconductor equipment. No agreements are reached, and responses from Tokyo and Seoul remain cautious and non-committal. The outreach is noted as a diplomatic signal, but its outcomes remain entirely uncertain and contingent on future developments.
