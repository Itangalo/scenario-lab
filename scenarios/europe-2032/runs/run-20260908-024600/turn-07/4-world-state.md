### Gigafactories Complete, But Ground Still Barren  
The InvestAI Gigafactories programme formally concludes with full disbursement of its €200bn fund. Yet no new sovereign compute capacity comes online—delays in grid upgrades and land access in Poland and Finland persist. The EAIA invokes Article 114 TFEU to override regional objections, declaring the designated sites critical for EU strategic autonomy. Legal challenges loom, but the political signal is clear: sovereignty now hinges on enforcement, not funding. The completion of InvestAI Gigafactories contributes +5 to eu_ai_sovereignty, but global capability growth of +0.5 triggers a −1 decay under metric rule 5, resulting in a net gain of +3.0. With last turn’s base at 19.5, sovereignty rises to 22.5.

### Deploy Grid-Ready Compute Corridors Begins, But Not Yet Funded  
With Gigafactories complete, the EU announces Deploy Grid-Ready Compute Corridors as its new priority, tying the first €40bn in funding to binding commitments from grid operators in Belgium and Poland. However, disbursement is delayed pending resolution of legal challenges from Finland and Poland and further approval by the Council. ENISA integrates security certification into the conditions for release, requiring training run logging and weight custody protocols. Resistance hardens in Helsinki and Warsaw, where local leaders decry federal overreach. The measure is now active but not yet delivering tangible capacity.

### Lithography Initiative Not Formally Proposed  
In response to supply chain vulnerability, the European Commission begins internal drafting of a potential Secure Sovereign Lithography Initiative—a concept for a future €120bn R&D effort anchored in Eindhoven, with preliminary coordination involving Fraunhofer, CEA-Leti, and IMEC. Informal technical discussions with South Korea and Taiwan are noted, but no formal agreements are reached. Given the constraint of introducing only one new measure per turn, and with Deploy Grid-Ready Compute Corridors now active, the Commission does not advance the lithography concept to proposal stage. It remains a non-committal exploratory process with no budget allocation or timeline.

### Labour Crisis Deepens Public Distrust  
Nationwide strikes by legal and accounting professionals intensify, demanding hiring moratoria on AI systems. The EU signals a pilot Transitional AI Adjustment Fund, offering wage insurance and retraining, but stops short of launching it. Media frames the moment as “abandonment by algorithm,” driving public sentiment lower.
