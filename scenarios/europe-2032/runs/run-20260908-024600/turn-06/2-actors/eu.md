## In practice
This turn, we are converting legal authority into physical momentum. The EAIA Fast-Track Mandate is now law, but national vetoes on siting remain a choke point. We are deploying the first €30bn tranche of the sovereign compute bond, tied strictly to milestones in grid readiness and physical security certification. ENISA’s technical annexes on weight custody and training run logging are now binding conditions for disbursement—no jurisdiction, no funding. We are targeting Belgium, Poland, and Finland with pre-approved corridor designs, offering co-financing for grid upgrades in exchange for binding commitments by Q3. The Council presidency has agreed to fast-track negotiations under Article 114 TFEU, treating this as a matter of strategic infrastructure.

Meanwhile, the Mandatory Pre-Release Red Teaming framework is operational. The first pilot, conducted on a 54.0-capability model, has produced test protocols now being scaled by Fraunhofer and CNRS. Public summaries will be released next month to build credibility, though full transparency is delayed pending IP compromise with industry. The startup carve-out below €500M, conditional on third-party sponsorship, has been accepted by the EP, easing innovation concerns.

The Open-Weight Capability Freeze is now in force under the Dual-Use Regulation, with ASML, Infineon, and STMicroelectronics helping define the thresholds. The freeze has held openweight_capability at 47.0, just below unsupervised operation, but backlash from German industry and French tech leaders persists. We are offering compliance support and reciprocal access pathways to maintain cohesion. The real test comes next turn: if we do not have shovels in the ground by then, the investment collapse and middle-power coalition may be our only leverage left.
