## In practice  
This turn, we close the Emergency Resilience Surge: the air-gapped systems are now live in three of the five designated grid operators, and red-teaming protocols have been exercised under the Civil Protection Mechanism. However, the delays in Poland and Spain mean full coverage will not be achieved — a gap we are now treating as strategic. We are converting the Surge’s operational core into a standing unit under ENISA, with permanent funding from the Digital Europe Programme, to ensure continuity beyond the sprint. That unit will report quarterly to the Cyber Crisis Liaison Organisation and integrate national CISOs into its governance — not as a central takeover, but as a shared nerve centre for when the next shock hits.

At the same time, we are pushing the EAIA Fast-Track Mandate through Article 114 TFEU, treating compute sovereignty as a single market issue. Germany and France remain resistant to central enforcement, so we are offering a compromise: veto rights on site allocation remain at the national level, but connectivity, security standards, and cross-border capacity pooling will be binding under the EAIA. We are backing this with a €30bn sovereign compute bond, callable in tranches as sites come online, to de-risk private investment and lock in EU jurisdictional control over weights and training runs. This is not just about building faster — it is about building *under law*.

Now, with the red teaming measure, we are acting on the widening gap between capability and control. The covert agent incident showed that self-assessment is not enough. We are drafting the measure as a delegated act under the AI Act, to be adopted by the Commission with EP scrutiny, requiring developers to submit models to a network of EU-certified red teams — including public research labs and civil society auditors — 90 days before release. Findings will be classified only where national security is at stake; otherwise, summaries will be public. We know this will face pushback from Silicon Valley and even some European startups, but we are framing it as a condition of market access, not a tax on innovation. If a model can’t pass scrutiny, it doesn’t enter. That is the price of operating in a Union that refuses to be a testing ground.
