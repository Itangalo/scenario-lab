### A Directive Dies, a Backchannel Lives

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation formally expires with no new capacity secured. Germany’s constitutional reservations and Ireland’s grid concerns remain unresolved; the directive becomes a cautionary footnote in EU digital policy — ambition outpaced by legal and technical reality.

With top-down leverage spent, the Union pivots to consolidation. The Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration is elevated to de facto priority, now framed as civil protection infrastructure. ENISA finalises draft interoperability standards for city-level AI monitoring nodes, while cross-border simulations between Finland, Spain, and France test response protocols for persistent agent intrusions in public services. Though funding remains contingent, the framing bypasses stalled Council negotiations, allowing quiet progress beneath the level of national veto points.

Full market access is suspended pending remediation plans. While developers denounce the move as legally overreaching, regional governments and technical watchdogs welcome concrete enforcement conditions. Trust is not restored, but the EU regains narrow credibility in operational circles.

Two member states formally reject the Evaluation Shield’s authority to impose remediation mandates, citing lack of legal basis and encroachment on national sovereignty. The rift between EU oversight and national control widens, paralyzing coordinated safety enforcement across the bloc.
