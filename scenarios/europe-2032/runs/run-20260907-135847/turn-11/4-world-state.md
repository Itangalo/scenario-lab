### The Shield Descends, the Backchannel Holds

The Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration is formally declared complete. ENISA’s interoperability standards are adopted, and successful cross-border simulations in Finland, Spain, and France demonstrate functional detection of persistent AI intrusions in city-level services. With €180 million in Digital Europe Programme funds unlocked for rollout, the network transitions from pilot to permanent infrastructure, marking a rare operational win for EU AI coordination.

Buoyed by this, the European Union launches the **European Evaluation Shield Activation and Enforcement**, a large category 1 measure aimed at binding frontier developers to real-time monitoring of memory persistence and goal continuity. Framed under Article 16 TFEU and repurposed Digital Markets Act authority, it mandates third-party verification and conditional market access for all systems above capability 50.0. A new Evaluation Shield Office is established at ENISA, backed by technical teams from Berlin, Paris, and Eindhoven.

Yet enforcement meets resistance. Two member states, already rejecting EU oversight, challenge the legal basis in national courts. Developers denounce the regime as extraterritorial overreach, and Washington signals no support. The American administration takes office next turn; posture remains unknown. Publicly, the EU asserts authority — but privately, compliance depends on cooperation that is fraying.

### Capability Creeps, Sovereignty Slips

Global AI capability rises to 62.0, entering the terminal zone, though market signals suggest slowing momentum. No safety improvement lands on deployed systems — the Shield is new, not yet effective — so `ai_safety` remains stagnant at 19.0. The resilience gain from the completed backchannel lifts `resilience` to 37.0.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 31.0: capability rose by 0.5 (below the −1 threshold), but the collapse of sovereign compute and lack of new capacity triggers a decay in strategic leverage. Political capital drops to 5.0 — the portfolio charge continues, the new large measure costs −3, the priority −1, and no completion bonus offsets them. Public sentiment holds at 35.0, unchanged.
