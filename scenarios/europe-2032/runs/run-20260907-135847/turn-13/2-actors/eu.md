## In practice
This turn, we are acting under siege conditions. The denial of access was not just a disruption — it was a demonstration of power, and we are answering with infrastructure that cannot be switched off from abroad. The Emergency Access Restoration and Sovereign Runtime Environment is now operational in planning: DG CONNECT has activated emergency procurement clauses under the Connecting Europe Facility, unlocking €1.2 billion in cohesion funds to retrofit EuroHPC clusters in Toulouse, Barcelona, and Kajaani. These will host stripped-down, safety-constrained versions of the latest open-weight models — no memory persistence, no autonomous goal pursuit — configured for healthcare diagnostics, grid balancing, and emergency dispatch coordination. We are mandating that all EU-funded critical services migrate to this runtime by turn 15, creating pull-through demand that no developer can ignore.

Simultaneously, we are racing to close the credibility gap in safety. The European Evaluation Shield continues toward full activation, but its authority is questioned where enforcement lags. To strengthen it, we are launching the Accelerated Open-Weight Verification and Certification Network under ENISA, tasking it with fast-tracking third-party evaluation of open models above 52.0 using redacted methodologies from the Shield’s own monitoring regime. This is not a replacement for frontier oversight — it is a workaround. We are coordinating with the emergent_eval_credentialing initiative, offering EU-level recognition to national certification schemes that align with our baseline, turning fragmentation into a distributed audit network. We are also meeting with open-model developers in Zurich, Berlin, and Lisbon, offering fast-track market access in exchange for pre-deployment verification — not as charity, but as the new price of entry. If we cannot control the frontier, we will harden the floor beneath it.
