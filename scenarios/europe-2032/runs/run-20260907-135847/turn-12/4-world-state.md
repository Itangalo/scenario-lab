### The Denial and the Response

In early January, access to the leading AI model was abruptly revoked for EU institutions, hospitals, and critical infrastructure operators without explanation. The cutoff — sudden, total, and unappealable — exposed a raw nerve: dependence on foreign frontier systems with no fallback. Simultaneously, open-weight models continue to advance steadily in line with the broader pace of capability growth, reaching `openweight_capability` of 52.5 as they incorporate recent architectural improvements. While these models are increasingly capable, they remain behind the frontier and cannot yet execute the full scope of multi-day professional projects end to end without significant human oversight.

The European Union responds with urgency. The **European Evaluation Shield Activation and Enforcement**, already designated as the priority measure, is expanded in scope to include emergency provisions for critical infrastructure access restoration. This expansion leverages existing legal frameworks and repurposes EuroHPC resources in France, Spain, and Finland to host safety-constrained open-weight models for healthcare, energy, and emergency response. Work begins immediately, but deployment is expected to take multiple turns due to technical and coordination challenges.

Public sentiment sours. Media coverage frames the access denial as a national humiliation, with headlines questioning Europe’s technological sovereignty. Labour groups protest outside data centres, and local governments delay AI adoption plans. Sentiment drops to 30.0.

The expanded scope of the Evaluation Shield faces growing resistance. Legal challenges from two member states gain traction, and developers push back against monitoring mandates in the absence of U.S. alignment. The Shield remains legally established but only partially implemented.

Sovereignty erodes further: despite new efforts, `eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 29.0 due to strategic exposure from the access denial. Political capital, already at 5.0, dips to 4.0 under the weight of the priority measure (−1) and the expanded scope of a large category 4 initiative (−3), with no completion bonus. A narrow escape from proposal failure occurs only because emergency funding is fast-tracked.

### Fracture Lines Deepen

Behind the scenes, the refusal of member states to fully align with EU enforcement widens into formal divergence. National certification initiatives emerge, citing technical rigor over supranational mandate. Coordination frays just as external pressure mounts.
