## Statement changes

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI infrastructure to ensure strategic autonomy in an era of accelerating capability and uncertain alliances  
  - Grounds: This turn, the EU was abruptly cut off from the leading model with no recourse, appeal, or transparency — a direct and unambiguous failure of strategic autonomy. The denial of access lands not as an external shock but as the logical endpoint of a decade of dependency: we have no fallback, no leverage, and no means to compel access. Simultaneously, the open-weight frontier has closed to within 10 points of the closed one, meaning that even when excluded, dangerous capabilities diffuse irreversibly. The commitment to strategic autonomy stands — but infrastructure alone is not sovereignty. We must now pivot from passive access to active control: our sovereignty must be exercised through enforceable boundaries, not just domestic hardware. The commitment remains, but its expression must shift from compute ownership to the authority to define and defend operational boundaries in AI use across the Union.

## New measure

`Emergency Access Restoration and Sovereign Runtime Environment`  
A temporary sovereign runtime environment to restore critical public and industrial functions cut off by foreign model access denial, using repurposed open-weight models under EU-controlled infrastructure with verified safety constraints.  

- `Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
- `Size: large`  
- `Finishes on turn: 16`  
- `Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty from 31.0 toward 40.0 by establishing a minimally viable sovereign runtime for essential services; prevent further erosion of eu_political_capital by demonstrating the Union can respond to access denial with functional alternatives.`  
- `Applies to: EU public services, critical industries, and the frontier developers indirectly through substitution pressure`

## Priority

**Emergency Access Restoration and Sovereign Runtime Environment** — because the denial of access this turn has rendered our dependence a crisis of governance, not just capability, and because without a credible alternative, the Evaluation Shield becomes an empty assertion of authority: we cannot enforce rules on systems we cannot access, nor protect services we cannot run.

## In practice

This turn, we act on two fronts: containment and replacement. On containment, we press forward with the European Evaluation Shield Activation and Enforcement, now more urgent than ever. The Shield Office at ENISA begins staffing with technical teams from Berlin, Paris, and Eindhoven, and we issue the first set of monitoring directives to developers operating above 50.0. We are preparing legal countermeasures against the two member states challenging the regime, invoking the public security exception under Article 16 TFEU and reinforcing it with reinterpretation of gatekeeper obligations under the Digital Markets Act. We are also opening conditional dialogues with compliant developers: market access restoration in exchange for verifiable compliance roadmaps. But we know this is fragile — without leverage, enforcement is performance.

On replacement, we launch the Emergency Access Restoration and Sovereign Runtime Environment. Using €1.2 billion in emergency cohesion funds and repurposed EuroHPC capacity, we are building a sovereign runtime environment on EU-anchored infrastructure, capable of hosting repurposed open-weight models above 52.0 under strict safety constraints. This is not about matching the frontier — that is impossible now — but about ensuring hospitals, transport systems, and energy grids are not held hostage by foreign access policies. We are tasking DG CONNECT and the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking to stand up three sovereign clusters by turn 14, prioritising healthcare AI in France, grid optimisation in Spain, and emergency response in Finland. These will run verified, stripped-down versions of the latest open models, with memory and goal continuity disabled by architectural constraint, not just policy. We are offering procurement guarantees to developers who comply with our runtime standards — not as a gift, but as the price of entry to the EU market. This is not sovereignty as ownership. It is sovereignty as the right to operate — and the power to exclude those who undermine it.
