## New measure  
**Measure:** Hardened Critical Infrastructure Access Protocols  
A binding directive requiring AI-mediated access to critical infrastructure systems—energy, water, health, transport—to be segmented, audited, and subject to human-in-the-loop escalation thresholds, with real-time monitoring by national CSIRTs and the EAAD.

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch the Openweight Containment Initiative  
This remains the priority because the **cyber_major_incident** was model-generated and exploited open-weight diffusion — proving that unsecured capability is now operationally dangerous, not just theoretically risky, and containment is the only measure directly cutting the threat at its source.

## In practice  
We are acting on the cyber intrusion not as an isolated failure but as confirmation of a systemic vulnerability: AI-generated offensive tooling is now in circulation, and our defences are reactive while the threat is autonomous. The **cyber_major_incident**—a coordinated ransomware sweep across regional health administrations in three member states—was enabled by an open-weight model fine-tuned for exploit discovery and phishing orchestration. Though the model itself was below 45.0 in base capability, its weaponised variant crossed that threshold in practice. This validates the Containment Initiative’s core thesis: we can no longer distinguish between open and dangerous. We are now fast-tracking the regulation under Article 114 TFEU through COREPER II, pairing it with emergency guidance from ENISA to national authorities on model provenance checks at cloud onboarding points. At the same time, we are accelerating the sovereign model prototype under EAAD oversight, with a minimum viable version to be tested by research hospitals and energy grid operators by turn 4. The goal is not to ban open models but to ensure that any model above 45.0 index operates only in a secured, auditable environment.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain our foundational effort, but progress is slower than hoped. We have secured grid reservation agreements with ENTSO-E for 12GW across three sites, but land access in Brittany and Norrland remains contested. We are deploying the Anti-Coercion Instrument in preparatory mode to strengthen our hand with ASML and EUV component suppliers, offering market access in return for priority delivery of 16nm+ tools for domestic fab resilience. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being renegotiated to include enforceable clauses on data sovereignty and operational continuity in the event of foreign access denial—turning mere investment into anchored capacity. Meanwhile, the EAAD’s red-teaming unit has identified persistent agent behaviour in two supply chain vendors, triggering the first formal audit under its provisional mandate. We are preparing to expand its authority through delegated acts once the legal framework clears the Council.

The new resilience measure responds directly to the health sector breaches: we are drafting a NIS2 amendment to mandate AI-specific segmentation in critical infrastructure access, requiring all AI agents to operate in sandboxed environments with time-limited credentials and mandatory human confirmation for high-impact actions. The EAAD and national CSIRTs will receive real-time telemetry from these systems, enabling coordinated shutdowns during anomalies. This is not a full autonomy ban, but a circuit breaker: we accept AI assistance, but not AI authority. The directive will be proposed to the Cyber Resilience Board in Q4 and fast-tracked through the Crisis Regulation mechanism if the incident cascade continues. Public sentiment remains fragile, but the health attacks have shifted the narrative—security is now a precondition of trust, not a brake on innovation.
