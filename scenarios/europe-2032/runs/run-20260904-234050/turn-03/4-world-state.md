### Frontier Pressures Mount
AI capability advanced to 55.5 as leading labs compressed training cycles and reused surplus compute, pushing agents toward longer autonomy. Open-weight models reached 43.0, reflecting steady diffusion of frontier techniques into accessible tooling. The gap remains stable, but offensive applications are now demonstrably within reach of determined non-state actors.

### Cyber Shock Triggers Response
A coordinated ransomware attack disabled regional health administrations in three member states, exploiting AI-generated phishing and zero-day discovery. The breach was traced to a fine-tuned open-weight model, underscoring the weaponisation risk of unsecured capability. In response, the EU initiated emergency procedures to draft a new directive mandating segmented, audited AI access to critical infrastructure, with human-in-the-loop requirements and real-time monitoring by national cyber agencies and the EAAD. The proposal is fast-tracked, but significant implementation hurdles remain, and full deployment will not occur before the next turn.

### Containment Efforts Stall
The Openweight Containment Initiative faced growing resistance. Member states introduced divergent moratoriums, while cloud providers challenged the technical feasibility of scanning private fine-tunes. A prototype sovereign model remained delayed, weakening the EU’s credibility. Public messaging faltered, with critics framing containment as stifling innovation without stopping misuse.

### Sovereignty Drifts
The InvestAI Gigafactories made minimal progress—land disputes and supply bottlenecks blocked construction. No new compute came online, and the EU’s access to frontier models remained externally dependent. With capability rising and no sovereign capacity landing, eu_ai_sovereignty declined further.

### Political Capital Erodes
The portfolio burden of four large measures and a named priority continued to weigh on coordination and delivery capacity. The cyber incident intensified scrutiny of the EU’s regulatory reach, and public trust softened due to perceived gaps in security and coherence. However, crisis mobilization and institutional response lent some resilience, limiting the political cost. Sentiment dipped as job losses and security fears converged, but the erosion of political capital was partial, reflecting both strain and ongoing legitimacy in core institutions.
