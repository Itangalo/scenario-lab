## New measure  
**Measure:** Accelerated Sovereign Model Prototype Deployment  
We deploy a minimum viable version of the EU’s sovereign AI model—secure, high-assurance, and hosted on EU-controlled infrastructure—to pilot in high-risk public sectors starting with health and energy, with strict access logging, red-teaming mandates, and human oversight protocols.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 5  
Targeted effect: Halt the decline in `eu_ai_sovereignty` and begin reversing it by delivering tangible, sovereign AI capability under EU legal and physical control; increase `ai_safety` by 5–7 points through a demonstrably secure alternative to open-weight models  
Applies to: EU institutions, selected national health and energy operators, and the EAAD as evaluator

## Priority  
**Priority:** Accelerated Sovereign Model Prototype Deployment  
This is now the priority because the **cyber_major_incident** has shattered the illusion that containment alone can protect us — we need a credible, operational alternative to open-weight models *now*, not just rules to restrict them, and this measure delivers the first real sovereign capability that can anchor both security and independence.

## In practice  
We are shifting from pure regulation to tangible capability because the world no longer lets us choose. The ransomware attack was not just a failure of defence — it was a failure of *substitution*. We banned nothing, but we offered nothing either. Now, with open-weight models at 43.0 and weaponisable by non-state actors, we cannot wait for gigafactories or perfect containment. We must field a sovereign model that is both secure and useful, and we must do it fast. Under the EAAD’s oversight, we are repurposing €18bn from the InvestAI fund to accelerate the prototype’s deployment, bypassing full-scale training runs by leveraging distillation from frontier models under strict licensing and air-gapped evaluation. The model will be hosted in Luxembourg and Finland on EU-owned infrastructure, with access granted only to vetted public operators in health diagnostics and grid resilience, each required to log all queries and outcomes for audit. We are negotiating with Gauss Labs and Mistral to allow knowledge transfer under EU jurisdiction, using the Anti-Coercion Instrument to secure cooperation in exchange for market access guarantees.

At the same time, we are pressing the Openweight Containment Initiative through COREPER II, but we now pair it with a positive offer: compliance with the 45.0 threshold will be rewarded with access to the sovereign model at subsidised rates, turning restriction into a pathway rather than a penalty. This is essential to overcome industry resistance, especially from cloud providers who argue they cannot scan fine-tunes — we are offering them a clean alternative instead. The EAAD’s red-teaming unit is already embedded in the prototype development, conducting continuous probing for emergent agent behaviour, and will publish a safety case by turn 5. Meanwhile, the Hardened Critical Infrastructure Access Protocols are being drafted into a NIS2 amendment, with the Cyber Resilience Board convening in April to finalise segmentation standards. We are also using the Taiwan tension rise as leverage: in bilateral talks with the US, we are offering intelligence sharing on AI-enabled cyber threats in exchange for guaranteed access to EUV components, framing chip security as joint strategic resilience. Public sentiment remains fragile, but we are launching a targeted communication campaign through the European Digital Innovation Hubs to position the sovereign model as both a shield and a tool — not just for safety, but for public service renewal.
