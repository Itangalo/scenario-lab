### Frontier Momentum Continues
AI capability advances to 57.0 as leading labs leverage reusable architectures and distributed training, narrowing the gap to autonomous agent deployment. Open-weight models reach 45.0, now matching the closed frontier of two years prior—fine-tuned variants are increasingly used in cyber and bio contexts, though not yet at scale.

### Sovereign Model Push Faces Headwinds
The EU accelerates its sovereign AI prototype, repurposing funds and securing hosting in Luxembourg and Finland. However, negotiations with Gauss Labs and Mistral stall over licensing and data sovereignty, delaying full knowledge transfer. The prototype launches in limited health and energy pilots, but its capability lags behind frontier models, raising doubts about utility. Still, its air-gapped evaluation and red-teaming by the EAAD mark a step toward assured systems.

### Containment Meets Resistance
The Openweight Containment Initiative faces legal scrutiny from cloud providers, who argue that enforcing capability thresholds on fine-tuned models violates digital single market principles. The incentive of subsidised sovereign model access softens opposition, but consensus in COREPER II remains fragile.

### Resilience Edges Up
Hardened protocols for critical infrastructure advance under NIS2 revisions, with segmentation standards finalised. Real-time monitoring pilots begin in two member states, absorbing some of the ransomware shock’s impact and lifting resilience.

### Political Capital Under Strain
The new large measure adds to an already overloaded portfolio. Despite the urgency of the cyberattack, the rushed sovereign model rollout is seen as reactive. Public messaging fails to counter narratives of dependency and delay. Sentiment dips slightly as job displacement fears grow, undermining the legitimacy of the push.
