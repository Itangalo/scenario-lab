## Priority
**Priority:** Unified Access Framework for Partnered Frontier AI  
This is now the priority because the **election_alliance** event fundamentally alters the strategic landscape: the US is offering structured access to frontier AI, but only under alignment conditions that could lock the EU into dependency if not governed firmly — we must lock in access *on our own terms* before the window closes and the balance of leverage shifts.

## In practice
We are closing the EAAD and Hardened Access Protocols as completed: the EAAD has stood up in Luxembourg with full audit authority and has already delayed one model release pending red-team findings; the NIS2 amendment on AI access to critical infrastructure is now law, with real-time monitoring operational in Germany and Finland. These deliver real gains in safety and resilience, but they do not close the capability gap. Meanwhile, the sovereign prototype is live in three hospitals and two grid operators, but its performance is pegged at 48.0 — useful, but not competitive with the frontier now at 57.0. Gauss Labs and Mistral remain resistant to full knowledge transfer, citing IP and export controls, and the Openweight Initiative is stuck in COREPER II, where cloud providers argue that fine-tuned models evade detection thresholds.

Now, the US offer changes everything. We are not being excluded — we are being invited in — but on terms that demand alignment on export controls, standards, and third-country dealings. We cannot refuse, but we cannot accept as written: doing so would make our sovereignty contingent on American policy. So we are negotiating a Unified Access Framework under Article 207 TFEU, to be adopted by the Council under the Foreign Affairs configuration. The framework will grant EU entities access to frontier models only if: (1) inference occurs on hardware under EU jurisdiction or allied sovereign cloud with data sovereignty guarantees; (2) the EAAD has pre-deployment evaluation rights and post-incident audit access; (3) misuse incidents are reported to a joint EU-US board; and (4) the EU retains veto power over downstream re-export to third countries. We are offering in return intelligence sharing on AI-enabled bio threats and support for multilateral export controls on advanced GPUs.

This is not a surrender to dependency — it is a managed bridge. We are using the US opening to buy time and capability while we scale our own compute and talent. The InvestAI Gigafactories are now breaking ground in Spain and Poland, and we are redirecting €30bn from the Tech Sovereignty Package to fast-track grid connections and chip assembly lines. But we cannot wait seven turns for sovereignty — we need capability *now*, and this deal, if shaped right, gives us both. Public sentiment remains fragile, so we are framing this not as reliance but as leverage: Europe is not taking a seat at the table — we are insisting on co-owning the table.
