## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the Openweight Containment Initiative  
A binding regulatory framework to restrict the distribution and deployment of open-weight models above 45.0 capability index within the EU, paired with a public alternative: a sovereign, secure, high-capability model hosted on EU-controlled infrastructure and available under strict access controls.

## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
This remains the cornerstone of our two-year commitment: without sovereign compute, every restriction we impose can be circumvented, every evaluation delayed, and every resilience plan undermined by dependence on external infrastructure that can be withdrawn or weaponised.

## In practice  
We are accelerating the InvestAI Gigafactories through binding agreements with EIB and national development banks to front-load €60bn in capital by Q3 2027, leveraging Article 122 TFEU and the Net-Zero Industry Act to override permitting delays in Brittany and Norrland. We are finalising grid reservations with ENTSO-E for 15GW across the five designated zones, and negotiating with local governments on enforceable community benefit packages — including regional reinvestment in grid modernisation, green hydrogen production, and guaranteed employment pathways. The goal is to break ground on all five sites by the end of this turn, despite the investment collapse, by redirecting public capital to fill the private gap.

The Tech sovereignty package is being advanced through the Digital Markets and Digital Services Council, where we are trading accelerated permitting for binding commitments on energy efficiency, EU-based operational control, and data sovereignty. The Commission’s legal service has completed the Article 114 TFEU directive harmonising permitting timelines, now under review by COREPER. We are also renegotiating with ASML and EUV supply chain partners to redirect 40% of the private capital tranche toward domestic lithography resilience, even as global investment retreats.

Meanwhile, the EAAD is now operational under ENISA’s temporary mandate, with its first team of 40 experts — including secondments from national cyber agencies and nuclear safety bodies — focused on developing red-team protocols for agent persistence and cross-system coordination. We are preparing the levy regulation on non-EU hyperscalers, though legal services warn of a WTO challenge; in parallel, we are building bilateral data access agreements with leading labs to secure weight access ahead of formal authority. The cyber intrusion has sharpened focus: our first evaluation will target models used in critical infrastructure supply chains, regardless of origin.  

The new Openweight Containment Initiative responds directly to the frontier release: we can no longer assume that what is open is weak. We are drafting a regulation under Article 114 TFEU to prohibit the distribution of models above 45.0 capability index unless they meet EU standards for weight security, misuse monitoring, and deployment logging. Enforcement will fall to the Digital Services Act gatekeepers and national digital authorities. Simultaneously, we are tasking the EAAD with standing up a sovereign alternative — a high-capability, tightly controlled model hosted on EU compute, available to researchers and public institutions under strict access protocols. This is not a ban on open science, but a containment of unsecured capability: we will protect the space for innovation, but not at the cost of leaving superhuman tools in unsecured hands.
