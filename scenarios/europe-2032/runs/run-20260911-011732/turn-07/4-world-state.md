### The autumn of closed counters
The second sweep came in September, larger than the spring one. Again appointment systems, benefit payments and container releases froze across several member states at once. This time the compromised component was familiar — administrators had been warned in the spring — but patches had stalled in municipal IT and hospital contractors. Nightly news showed handwritten triage lists and queues at town halls.

Technical press dwelt on the speed gap: intrusion tooling assembled in hours, defenders working in days. Attribution was absent. ENISA's daily notes described indicators without naming an actor.

### A seat at someone else's table
In October the Commission took the only funded option left. Ministers in foreign affairs and telecom formations approved seconding ENISA and CERT-EU analysts to an allied joint cyber command built by states hit by the same class of attack, with real-time telemetry flowing outward and pooled findings flowing back.

The decision passed because refusal was indefensible, not because it was loved. Parliamentary debate called it protection rented rather than built. The capital that had cut its own outside deal was offered a place in the liaison cell if it returned to joint licensing terms; it sent an observer without commitments.

On the ground, the link helped. Shared signatures shortened the October recovery from days to hours in connected ports, though clinics outside the telemetry feed lagged again.

### Paper permits, angry streets
The middle-power supply-chain compact concluded in November with communiqués on chokepoint coordination and a joint approach to Washington. Officials presented it as leverage preserved. Editorial pages called it diplomacy without buildings, noting the five reserved sites remained fenced fields with power connections and EIB paper.

Around those fields and in several cities, opposition hardened. Small blockades delayed grid works, graffiti blamed automation for the hospital chaos, and two municipal AI pilots were paused after protests. Polls showed use continuing and resentment deepening.

By December services ran, but trust did not. Brussels had bought time and a seat, not capacity.
