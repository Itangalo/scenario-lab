### The spring the screens went dark
The automated attack came in March, and it did not look sophisticated to the public. It looked like closed counters. A ransomware sweep moved through municipal systems, hospital administration and port logistics in several member states at once, exploiting a compromised software component whose users did not know they were exposed. Appointment systems, benefit payments and container releases stalled for days. ENISA and national teams worked publicly, issuing daily technical notes without naming a culprit.

Operators said the autumn hardening had helped — the grid itself stayed up — but clinics and city networks patched late or not at all were overrun. Model-generated intrusion tooling outpaced defenders visibly, and press coverage lingered on that gap.

### A capital goes its own way
Midway through the response, a member state announced its own arrangement for privileged model and compute access with an outside partner, on commercial terms better than Brussels was offering jointly. The government called it pragmatism in a crisis. In Brussels it was read as a break in the common front just as the new American administration took office in Washington pledging to treat advanced models as a national asset with tiered foreign access.

The Commission answered with process: an anti-coercion review opened, ministers debated cohesion behind closed doors, and the defecting capital was offered the joint Washington ask and the joint licensing terms as a return path. Joint work with the Netherlands, Japan and Korea continued without it. Nothing was reversed, and nothing was punished.

### Permits without buildings
The long-promised domestic build formally closed this semester. The financing for the two-hundred-billion data-centre push never materialised, and Brussels quietly shelved the construction plan, keeping only the five reserved sites, permits and power connections alive on paper through the EIB. What remained funded was the middle-power coordination track.

By June the public mood was bitter: services disrupted, dependence confirmed, and one capital openly shopping alone. Talk of sovereignty survived, but as diplomacy rather than capacity.
