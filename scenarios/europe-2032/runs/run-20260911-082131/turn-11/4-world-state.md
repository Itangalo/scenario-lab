### The wave and the shield
Autumn brought the attack everyone had rehearsed for and still was not ready for. A largely automated ransomware sweep, built with model-generated tooling, moved through public services in several member states — clinics postponing non-urgent care, municipalities reverting to paper, two grid operators islanding parts of their networks. Attribution lagged; officials admitted privately it would take months.

Where locked backups and isolation drills had been adopted, they held. Cooperating hospitals restored from clean copies within days and shared indicators through the pooled command. Where councils had refused telemetry sharing the year before, screens stayed dark for weeks. The split was visible on the evening news, and bitter.

In the middle of the wave, defenders got their first real win in years. Partner laboratories circulated automated patching that moved at the speed vulnerabilities were found, plus detection tuned to swarm behaviour rather than signatures. ENISA pushed the package hard to cooperating essential operators — short mandated patch windows, new sensors, extended sampling where joint feeds flagged risk. Technicians called it competent; grid engineers said whole classes of follow-on probes simply died.

It was not enough to prevent harm, and the other shock of the semester compounded it. A near-frontier open release was downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in its first week, putting advanced offensive assistance permanently on private hardware. Security services warned the patch race would now be permanent.

Brussels held its pooled accession together, trading playbooks for continued early-warning feeds while still denied raw intelligence. The chips-for-compute offer stayed warm without signatures. The civic trust programme formally closed, with workload limits and the redress desk kept in cooperating islands, but frozen elsewhere.

The Union ended the year more tooled, less shocked than it might have been — and still dependent, still divided between the plugged-in and the dark.
