### A seat at someone else's table
Brussels could not afford to build, so it joined. With chip slots still cancelled, the gigafactory fence still up, and private finance gone from data-centre plans, the Commission signed into the pooled cyber and bio-surveillance command that had been offered to capitals hit by the same winter malware wave.

On paper it was a good fit. ENISA brought the one thing that had worked — the locked-backup playbooks and cross-border isolation drills that kept cooperating clinics online in the autumn. In return, European hospitals and grid operators got early warning feeds and pooled attribution they could never have funded alone. Technicians called the first joint exercises competent and fast.

Politics made it thinner. The Council mandate insisted on European terms, data safeguards and a seat at the operational table. Partners welcomed the telemetry but balked at conditions, keeping the Union as a contributor with limited access to raw intelligence. Inside the Union, refusenik municipalities that had stayed dark last year saw another telemetry bargain and refused again.

The other pact of the semester — chips for compute plus a civic liability compromise — was declared finished. Trade officials kept the licensing offer to The Hague, Tokyo and Seoul warm, and lawyers published clarified liability language meant to coax insurers back to civic AI. Neither moved. Partners still demanded binding controls first, insurers still waited for law, and the co-location options lost in the finance rout did not return.

At home the trust repair effort ground on. Workload limits and human-override rules were imposed on benefits queues, scandal logs were published with a redress desk, and a handful of audited triage assistants expanded waiting-list cuts. Nurses praised them; national press kept calling the original harm lawful. Deployments outside the cooperating islands stayed frozen.

Brussels ended June more protected, no less dependent — plugged into shared shields while its own hardware base stayed idle.
