### The patch holds, the law breaks
The automated patching and swarm detection pushed through ENISA kept working through the spring. Cooperating hospitals and grid operators stayed up through a rolling series of probes built with openly available tooling. Where sensors were installed and patch windows enforced, follow-on attacks died quickly. Where councils had stayed outside telemetry sharing, outages dragged on. Technicians shared indicators through the pooled command, and Brussels finally had a visible defensive success to point to.

It was drowned out.

An ombudsman inquiry into automated triage in social insurance and policing support found thousands systematically denied, flagged or downgraded by systems that were rubber-stamped at speed. Logs existed and were never read. Oversight meant a caseworker clearing a queue. Hearings played nightly: lawful on paper, indefensible in practice. Whether the statute was breached or badly drawn became the argument in every talk show, and both readings left Brussels damaged. Trust in public AI collapsed further.

At the same time Washington ordered deeper cuts to lithography exports and servicing, extending beyond leading-edge tools to older machines. The Dutch government complied; the company warned it had no survivable alternative. Brussels logged the move for its anti-coercion instrument but did not contest it, preserving the pooled early-warning link and the chips offer. In the press it read as humiliation: the Union's one chokepoint used by someone else.

Job losses stayed narrow but real. Routine coding, standardised analysis, first-draft work and tier-one support stopped hiring juniors. Retraining vouchers and wage insurance helped at the margins, but forecasts of recovery were quietly pushed back.

Brussels answered the scandal with audits, a ban on queue-stamp approvals, mandatory log review and redress paid from old civic lines. The programme started, slowly. Faint reports of blockades at substations and data-centre sites grew louder in several states, still rumour, still single-source, but persistent.

### Shield without standing
By June essential services were more resilient where they cooperated, and politically the Union was weaker than ever.
