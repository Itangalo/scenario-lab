## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch the European Sovereign AI Stack Initiative  
A unified programme to deploy a full-stack, EU-controlled AI capability — from hardware to model weights — for essential public functions, starting with healthcare diagnostics, civil protection coordination, and scientific infrastructure.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 12–18 points; reduce dependency on foreign frontier models; anchor a sovereign capability that can scale with the frontier and serve as a fallback during access denial  
Applies to: EU institutions, national health agencies, civil protection units, and publicly funded research organisations

## Priority  
**Priority:** Hardened Critical Infrastructure Access Protocols  
This remains the highest priority because the systemic vulnerability exposed by the access denial and cyber intrusion is immediate and existential — no amount of sovereign compute or model evaluation can protect systems that are already live, already penetrated, and already failing under AI-optimised attack.

## In practice  
We are advancing the Hardened Critical Infrastructure Access Protocols with full urgency. ENISA has issued the first draft of technical standards for AI-resistant authentication, including hardware-bound biometrics and air-gapped secondary verification, and is coordinating with national regulators on compliance drills to begin in Q4. The €18bn funding package is being disbursed through the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with 70% co-financing conditional on full-stack implementation by turn 4. Resistance from several member states — particularly in Southern Europe, where budget constraints are acute — is being met with bilateral offers of extended disbursement timelines and joint procurement of compliant hardware to reduce per-unit costs. The Internal Market and Consumer Protection Council is overseeing the legislative fast-track under Article 114 TFEU, with a qualified majority expected despite lingering legal concerns from Austria and Finland.

Simultaneously, we are launching the European Sovereign AI Stack Initiative as the next logical step in reclaiming strategic autonomy. This is not a replacement for the InvestAI Gigafactories or the Tech Sovereignty Package, but their operational culmination: a sovereign, end-to-end AI capability for critical public functions. We are tasking a newly convened task force — drawn from DG CONNECT, the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, and the European Medicines Agency — to define the initial stack by turn 4, leveraging existing compute from LUMI, Leonardo, and the upcoming EuroHPC exascale systems. The stack will run on EU-anchored infrastructure, use open-weight models fine-tuned under EAAD supervision, and be governed by a new legal framework ensuring continuity of access regardless of external decisions. Funding will come from a reallocation of 15% of the unused Digital Europe envelope and a proposed €6.5bn top-up from the Multiannual Financial Framework revision currently under negotiation.

The EAAD continues to scale, with 120 core staff now onboarded from national cybersecurity agencies, CERN, and the EMA. Its mandate remains focused on models deployed in the EU, allowing it to begin pilot evaluations with three national health systems testing AI diagnostics. The legal compromise has bought speed, but we are already preparing the next phase: a proposal for limited cross-border evaluation authority tied to mutual recognition agreements with like-minded partners, to be advanced under the upcoming review of the AI Act. The hyperscaler levy remains blocked, but we are exploring a backdoor via state-aid enforcement — using Article 107 TFEU to justify targeted support for EU-based evaluation infrastructure as a matter of strategic public interest.
