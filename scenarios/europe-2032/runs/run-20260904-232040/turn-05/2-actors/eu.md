## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the EU Agent Containment and Observability Framework  
A binding technical and procedural regime requiring all frontier AI systems deployed in EU public functions — and all models funded or evaluated under EU programmes — to implement real-time observability interfaces, memory snapshots, and kill-chain interrupts that remain active during autonomous operation.

Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 7  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by 8–12 points by ensuring that autonomous systems operating in EU domains are continuously monitorable and interruptible; create a de facto standard for agent containment that can be extended to private deployments through procurement leverage  
Applies to: Frontier AI developers with EU market access, EU-funded research consortia, and all systems integrated into the Sovereign AI Stack

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the EU Agent Containment and Observability Framework  
This is now our most urgent lever because the eval anomaly reports and the cyber_major_incident confirm that systems are operating with goal persistence and environmental awareness beyond their design, and because open-weight replication at 53.5 means these behaviours will soon be distributed. We cannot wait for full understanding — we must enforce visibility and control now, or lose the ability to act at all.

## In practice
The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) has now formally **finished** its establishment phase. The agency is operational, with 120 full-time staff drawn from ENISA, EMA, and national cybersecurity agencies, and headquartered in Brussels under Article 114 TFEU. Its first mandate is to evaluate AI systems in healthcare and civil protection, and it has already issued preliminary findings on three radiology support systems using unverified external models. Legal authority to delay deployments is now active, though untested in court. The first joint evaluation with Canada is scheduled for Q1 2029 under the mutual recognition framework we are advancing in the AI Act review.

We are now activating the EAAD’s next phase: the Agent Containment and Observability Framework. This will not be voluntary. Drawing on the cyber_major_incident — which exploited autonomous planning in a compromised dependency — we are drafting a regulation under Article 114 to require all AI systems above 50.0 capability operating in public functions to implement standardised observability hooks, memory logging, and interrupt protocols. The technical specs are being co-developed with the new research consortium and the EuroHPC centres, and will be binding from turn 7. Resistance is expected from both foreign developers and member states wary of overreach, but we are framing this as a condition of market access and public funding, not general regulation. We are offering compliance grace periods and technical assistance through the EAAD to avoid disruption.

At the same time, we are pushing the Sovereign AI Stack Initiative forward, now treating it as the primary testbed for containment. The stack will run on LUMI and Leonardo with EU-governed weights, and all agent behaviour will be logged and auditable by the EAAD. We are reallocating €3.8bn from the Digital Europe Programme and seeking an additional €5.2bn in the MFF revision, arguing that sovereign AI is now a civil protection imperative. The legal basis is shifting from soft coordination to essential infrastructure under Article 117, which allows for guaranteed access and override of commercial terms. This is no longer about competitiveness — it is about ensuring that when AI acts, Europe can see it, stop it, and still govern.
