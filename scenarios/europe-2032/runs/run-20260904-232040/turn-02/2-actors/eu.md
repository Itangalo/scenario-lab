## New measure  
**Measure:** Hardened Critical Infrastructure Access Protocols  
A binding directive requiring AI-hardened authentication, continuous adversarial monitoring, and automatic degradation modes for all systems controlling energy, water, rail, and medical infrastructure above a defined scale.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 4  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 8–12 points; reduce systemic vulnerability to AI-optimised cyber intrusions; ensure fail-operational capacity in core services  
Applies to: All member states’ critical infrastructure operators, with enforcement through national regulators and co-funding from the Digital Europe Programme and Connecting Europe Facility

## Priority  
**Priority:** Hardened Critical Infrastructure Access Protocols  
This measure is now the highest priority because the cyber intrusion exposed a catastrophic vulnerability that no amount of sovereign compute or model evaluation can fix in time — we are defending systems that are already live, already penetrated, and already failing, and the next attack may come before our Gigafactories break ground.

## In practice  
We are activating the Hardened Critical Infrastructure Access Protocols through a crisis-prioritised legislative track under Article 114 TFEU, fast-tracked via the Internal Market and Consumer Protection Council. The directive mandates multi-factor AI-resistant authentication (including hardware-bound biometrics and air-gapped secondary verification), real-time adversarial probing of access controls, and automatic fallback to manual or degraded operation when anomaly thresholds are breached. Funding of €18bn will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with 70% co-financing for member states that implement the full stack by turn 4. ENISA will issue technical standards within six months, and national regulators will conduct unannounced compliance drills every quarter starting turn 3.

At the same time, we are re-engaging on the stalled EAAD initiative by decoupling its funding from the contested hyperscaler levy. Instead, we are redirecting €2.1bn from unused cohesion funds and proposing a temporary emergency budget reallocation under Article 322 TFEU to cover its first three years of operation. This allows recruitment to begin at competitive salaries, starting with 150 core staff seconded from national cybersecurity agencies, CERN’s safety culture unit, and the European Medicines Agency’s audit divisions. We are also scaling back EAAD’s initial extraterritorial ambitions to focus solely on models deployed in the EU, accepting legal compromise to gain operational speed.

The InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track, though delayed. We are bypassing local opposition in Brittany by offering a legally binding energy dividend — 12% of site output reserved for regional green hydrogen and desalination — and in northern Sweden by guaranteeing 40% local hiring and joint ownership of cooling infrastructure with municipal utilities. ENTSO-E has agreed to pre-reserve 10GW of grid capacity contingent on national energy plans being submitted by Q3 2027. The Commission is preparing a legal opinion on whether Article 122 TFEU allows temporary override of national permitting bottlenecks in cases of strategic infrastructure urgency — a step we will take if site finalisation slips further.
