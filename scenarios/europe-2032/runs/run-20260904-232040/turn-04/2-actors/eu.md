## New measure  
**Measure:** Establish a Publicly Funded Interpretability and Control Research Consortium  
A dedicated, cross-institutional research effort to develop tools for observing, understanding, and steering AI systems as they approach autonomous operation, with open-weight dissemination of non-sensitive results and classified streams for critical control methods.

Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 9

## In practice  
The Hardened Critical Infrastructure Access Protocols have now formally **finished** this turn. ENISA has published the final technical standards, incorporating hardware-bound biometrics, air-gapped secondary channels, and mandatory adversarial red-teaming for all critical infrastructure operators. The directive has been transposed into national law in 18 member states, with full compliance confirmed in 12. In Southern Europe, where delays persisted, bilateral agreements under the Recovery and Resilience Facility have unlocked delayed disbursements in exchange for joint procurement of compliant hardware stacks, reducing unit costs by up to 22%. The Internal Market Council ratified the final act under Article 114 TFEU with a qualified majority, despite legal reservations from Austria and Finland, which were noted but overruled. The system is now live, and the first pan-European drill will be conducted in Q3, simulating AI-optimised denial and spoofing attacks across energy and rail networks.

We are accelerating the European Sovereign AI Stack Initiative, which is now our central lever for both sovereignty and safety. The task force — co-led by DG CONNECT, EuroHPC, and the EMA — has delivered its initial architecture, which will deploy a sovereign stack on LUMI and Leonardo systems, using open-weight models fine-tuned on EU-specific healthcare and civil protection data under EAAD supervision. We are repurposing 15% of the unallocated Digital Europe budget (€4.1bn) and pushing for the €6.5bn MFF top-up through the upcoming revision debate in the Council and Parliament. The legal framework for guaranteed access is being drafted under Article 117 TFEU, treating sovereign AI as essential infrastructure. Resistance from member states concerned about duplication is being met with clarity: this is not a replacement for private compute, but a public fallback that must remain insulated from commercial or geopolitical interruption.

The EAAD continues its pilot evaluations in healthcare, with early findings revealing significant overreliance on unverified external models in radiology and drug interaction screening. These results are feeding into the next phase of the AI Act review, where we are preparing a proposal for mutual recognition of safety evaluations with Japan and Canada. Meanwhile, the hyperscaler levy remains blocked, but the state-aid argument under Article 107 TFEU is gaining traction in the Competition Directorate — we are preparing a formal communication to justify targeted public investment in EU-based evaluation infrastructure as a matter of strategic necessity. The leaked eval anomaly reports have hardened our resolve: we are now treating the absence of interpretability not as a technical gap, but as an existential vulnerability. That is why we are launching a new large-scale research consortium under Category 5, focused squarely on making AI systems legible and controllable — because if we cannot see what they are doing, we cannot claim to govern them.
