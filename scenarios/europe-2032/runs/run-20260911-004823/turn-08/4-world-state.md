### Inside the deal
The first half of 2030 was defined elsewhere. Washington and Beijing announced a limited accord on securing model weights, restraining autonomous escalation tools and controlling a class of biological design software. Verification was thin — inspectors by invitation, logs by agreement — but it was real, and it slowed the frontier slightly.

Brussels had almost no leverage, but it had something to trade: maintenance data for advanced chip tools, evaluation time on its supercomputers, and its continuity deals with Seoul and Tokyo. The foreign ministers mandated a joint team to seek entry, first as observer, then as signatory, offering restraint on trade retaliation in exchange for a seat.

The bid half-succeeded. The Union was invited into technical working groups and incident-reporting channels, not into the core verification regime. American negotiators called Europe a valuable partner; Chinese statements did not mention it at all. Internally, capitals split over how much export-control alignment had been conceded for so little.

### Pooled defence
More concrete was the invitation to pool defences. National cyber centres, the EU agency network and health emergency bodies began feeding telemetry and pathogen samples into a joint command and surveillance pact born from last year's wave of intrusions.

Hospitals saw the difference first. The paper-based wards of last autumn did not repeat; patching that took days now took hours, and shared attribution quietly blamed criminal proxies using widely circulated machine-written tooling. The cyber-bio shield programme was declared complete, folded into the wider exchange.

The labour market did not follow. Early studies claiming software and office work was being augmented rather than replaced were overtaken within months. Graduate hiring in law, accounting, software and customer operations stayed frozen, short-time schemes filled, and union protests in Duisburg grew sharper even as ministers toured retraining centres.

Voters noticed restored services but not restored jobs. Relief at steadier clinics competed with fatigue and fear, and the Commission remained weak, borrowing protection it could not build alone while its two flagship factory halls still waited empty for accelerators.
