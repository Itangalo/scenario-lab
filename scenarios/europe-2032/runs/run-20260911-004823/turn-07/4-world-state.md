### The sweep
It started with municipal helpdesks. By mid-July, hospitals in three member states were reverting to paper, a widely used ticketing library was found compromised, and grid operators were islanding control systems as a precaution. The tooling was new, fast, and clearly machine-written. Attribution statements promised months.

Brussels activated joint incident teams. Emergency patching money flowed quickly, offline backups saved several regions from the worst, but others queued for days. The continuity cell kept clinics in Lyon and Rotterdam running on rationed compute, degraded. Headlines called defenders visibly behind.

Paradoxically, the same automation tooling aided diffusion: lightweight fine-tunes and leaked weights of last year's frontier runs circulated widely during the chaos, and open-weight derivatives caught up toward the prior frontier even as defenders struggled. No new EU training run was needed for that catch-up to register.

### The paper no one wanted to publish
In September, a genome-model study circulated: a viable design step toward a human-infecting organism, reachable with assistance by non-experts. Authors and critics accused each other of alarmism and of saying too much. Health authorities stood up sequencing triage and bought detection reagents quietly, without publishing methods. The argument stayed technical, but emergency planners took notice.

### Empty desks
By autumn, graduate hiring data hardened. Law firms, accountants, software houses and customer-operations centres simply stopped replacing entry roles. Ministers toured retraining centres funded by reprogrammed social funds, promising short-time work and rapid courses. Unions filmed both the job boards and the pallet-stacking machines in Duisburg.

The two long-delayed factory projects did not finish this winter: concrete preserved, permits granted, capital pledged — but still no accelerators to fill them. Opposition called them empty halls; the Commission called them ready when hardware returns. With no new compute online, they confer no sovereignty gain this turn, and no new measure was proposed to add a bonus. The standing portfolio costs remain, and the capability gap weighs on standing capacity, leaving sovereignty slightly down. A supply-continuity pact with overseas partners also closed, keeping the trickle of Japanese lots and maintenance slots moving.

Voters noticed the patching more than the pledges. Fear rose, fatigue deepened, and criticism of the delayed, fragmented response eroded the Commission's standing rather than rallying support.
