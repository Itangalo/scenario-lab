### The machines learn to learn
Autumn brought no announcement, only a cadence no one had planned. Frontier labs pushed updates weeks apart where quarters had been normal. Researchers in Brussels pieced it together from papers and hiring patterns: training loops were now rewriting themselves, with human teams reviewing rather than directing. Capability jumped visibly — agents that sustained multi-day engineering work, results in protein design and materials that arrived without a press release.

The second power gained ground in the telling, its systems suddenly closer to the American frontier. In Europe, the two empty factory halls outside Paris and Warsaw became a shorthand for what had not happened.

### Patching at machine speed
Against that, one genuine reprieve. A new generation of defensive tooling — patching pushed as fast as flaws were found, detectors that caught coordinated swarms rather than single signatures — was shared through the pooled command built over the spring. Hospitals and grid operators felt it first: the autumn intrusion wave that crippled ticketing and forced paper wards a year earlier fizzled into overnight tickets closed before morning.

The Commission moved to ride it, ordering the joint cyber and health agencies to push the new stacks into clinics, municipal networks and biosurveillance feeds, folding the old shield operations into a wider automatic deployment. Engineers called it the first time in years the defence had gained.

It did not save jobs. Graduate hiring stayed frozen, short-time schemes rolled over in back offices, and retraining centres toured by ministers sat half-empty in Duisburg.

### The lawful wrong
Then the ombudsman report. An automated scoring system used in benefits and fraud referrals in three member states had systematically cut off disabled claimants, single parents and migrants — thousands of cases, a caseworker clearing a queue at under a minute each, logs generated and never read.

The Commission's own review conceded the worst reading: the deployment had sat outside the high-risk categories as written in 2024, and everything done had been lawful. Enforcement was intact; the law itself looked badly drawn. Talk shows ran the faces of those wronged beside the text of the statute. Ministers announced a gap-closing review, an audit of logging, and a freeze on similar deployments until real human review times could be proven. Adoption stalled overnight, and restriction became cheap.

Voters held two facts at once: clinics stayed open, and the state had wronged people by formula. Trust collapsed further, leaving Brussels with a working defence, a runaway frontier, and almost no room to act.
