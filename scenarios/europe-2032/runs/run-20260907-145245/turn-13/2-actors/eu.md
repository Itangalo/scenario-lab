## In practice
This turn, we consolidate not just systems, but legitimacy. The Sovereign AI Core Programme is now live in Helsinki, Marseille, and Bratislava — the first real-time failover tests between them have succeeded, and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking has assumed operational command under Article 122 TFEU. The control plane is unified, the software is certified, and public services in those capitals now run on a system that can survive cutoff. But Budapest and Warsaw remain outside. We do not invoke sanctions under no_more_free_rides — not yet — because the Secure Integration Incentive has fulfilled its purpose: cohesion funds were released on milestone, ECO teams stood ready, and temporary routing through Bratislava was demonstrated. The offer was real. The refusal was political.

With the Frontier Access Guarantee and Continuity Assurance Framework now complete, we turn to the deeper deficit: material capacity. The Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive launches this quarter as a large, three-turn measure to force the pace of domestic infrastructure. We invoke the Crisis Continuity Protocol to justify urgency, but this is not emergency rule — it is strategic correction. The Directive will require member states to fast-track permitting for EU-controlled data centres, guarantee grid priority, and align national subsidies with EU compute sovereignty targets. We will negotiate through the Competitiveness Council, backed by the Commission’s legal authority and the fiscal leverage of cohesion policy. Resistance will come from capitals that still see compute as national turf, but we are no longer asking. We are building the spine of European AI — and it runs on European soil.
