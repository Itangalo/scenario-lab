## In practice  
We are acting on a truth the last six months forced upon us: power without control is not strength — it is a time bomb. The Cyber Resilience Emergency Directive has now landed. ENISA’s Threat Fusion Cell is operational, receiving live telemetry from energy grids, transport networks, and financial clearinghouses. Red-teaming revealed three critical software dependencies already compromised by AI-generated exploits — not yet weaponised, but waiting. We have the eyes, but not yet the teeth. That changes now.

This turn, we are shifting from reactive hardening to proactive prevention. We are proposing a binding requirement: no high-risk AI system may be deployed without a pre-submitted, independently verified safety case. This is not a checklist. It is a living document, audited by a new unit within the European AI Oversight Authority (still in negotiation, but now gaining urgency), requiring proof of containment, alignment verification under stress conditions, and a plan for cascading failure. We are anchoring this in Article 114 TFEU, just like the Oversight Authority, and we are leveraging the current weakness of the private sector — the ai_investment_collapse has left developers dependent on public cloud access, procurement, and market stability. We will tie safety case compliance to eligibility for EU procurement contracts, access to testbeds, and, crucially, to the energy allocations we now control through the stalled Gigafactory permitting process. The Dutch, French, and Polish governments may resist supranational enforcement, but they cannot afford to be seen blocking basic safety after the ransomware sweep crippled hospitals in Lyon and Rotterdam.

We are not abandoning compute sovereignty — the Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track, and we are quietly renegotiating the collapsed investment deals around public-private co-investment with clawbacks. But this moment is not about scale. It is about leverage. The frontier is not growing — it is exposed. And we will use that exposure to build the one thing that lasts: the right to say no.
