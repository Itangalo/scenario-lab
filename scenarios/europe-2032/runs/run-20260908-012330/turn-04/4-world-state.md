### A Window of Control

The EU pivoted sharply from infrastructure to governance, declaring that the collapse in private AI investment and the recent cyberattack created a rare opening to impose safety requirements on frontier developers. The Cyber Resilience Emergency Directive formally concluded, with ENISA’s Threat Fusion Cell now receiving live telemetry from critical sectors. Red-teaming uncovered AI-compromised software dependencies, validating the need for deeper oversight.

The Commission launched its flagship measure: mandatory pre-deployment safety cases for high-risk AI systems. Anchored in internal market law and tied to procurement and energy access, it targeted developers weakened by the investment downturn. The proposal gained urgency after a major ransomware attack—powered by AI-generated exploits—disrupted hospitals and transport systems. Yet resistance hardened. France and Poland rejected supranational enforcement, while Germany questioned the legal basis, fearing regulatory overreach. The Netherlands, though supportive of safety, demanded strict limits on data sharing.

Public sentiment deteriorated further. The backlash against AI infrastructure intensified, with protests turning physical in Bavaria and Wallonia. A coordinated wave of non-binding national resolutions—Germany, Sweden, Belgium—called for moratoriums on large-scale training runs, citing energy strain and job losses. The Commission faced growing pressure not just from the streets, but from member states acting unilaterally.

Meanwhile, the American posture remained unconfirmed, with the 2028 election still undecided. No breakthrough in AI safety emerged, but the offensive leap had plateaued. The frontier’s growth slowed, not from policy, but from capital retreat.

### Fractures Deepen

A member state quietly struck a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, bypassing EU data rules in exchange for priority AI access—widely seen as a defection. This, combined with stalled oversight talks, eroded confidence in EU cohesion. The sovereignty metric dipped as dependence on external capacity deepened, despite ongoing compute investments.
