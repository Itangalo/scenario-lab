## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign control through operational resilience and enforceable oversight
  - Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
  - Grounds: The frontier has not advanced as feared, but the control gap remains wide and is now compounded by internal fracture. The cyber_major_incident and loss_of_control_incident proved that even plateaued capability, when unsecured, can inflict real harm. Meanwhile, emergent_regulatory_fracture revealed that member states are no longer waiting for Union-wide consensus — they are acting unilaterally, risking a patchwork of restrictions that weakens both sovereignty and safety. The original commitment to "secure sovereign capacity before the frontier outpaces governance" assumed a race we could win by building fast enough. But the race has changed: it is no longer about matching compute or capability, but about ensuring that what exists — foreign or domestic, private or public — can be monitored, contained, and held accountable. The new commitment shifts from infrastructure as the path to sovereignty, to oversight as its core expression: control that is exercised, not merely claimed.
  - Evidence quoted: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
  - Reason: The closure of the commitment period directly ends the timeframe for the original pledge, necessitating a reassessment of the EU's stated objective and its means of fulfillment.

  - Tier: commitment

  - Grounds: Having modified it substantively, the original formulation is no longer operative. The revised version replaces it fully.

- **add** `sovereign_oversight` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: The EU must be able to monitor, verify, and if necessary, halt the operation of any high-capability AI system deployed within its jurisdiction, regardless of origin.
  - Grounds: The bilateral member_state_defection and election_consolidation signal that access to foreign AI systems will be conditional and politically mediated. We cannot rely on goodwill. If we are to preserve our autonomy, we must have teeth: the ability to inspect, the right to audit, and the authority to de-authorise. This is not a technical standard — it is a political necessity.
