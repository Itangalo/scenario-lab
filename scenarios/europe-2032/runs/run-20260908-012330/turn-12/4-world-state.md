### The Aftermath of Automation

A major cyber incident sweeps across public health and local government systems in three member states, exploiting AI-generated exploits in widely used software dependencies. The attack is contained before cascading nationally, but patching is slow, and recovery takes weeks. The tooling behind it bears the hallmarks of advanced reasoning models, now capable of autonomously identifying and weaponising vulnerabilities. Despite the completion of the cyber defence breakthrough, deployment gaps in the Balkans and Southern Europe leave critical nodes exposed—defenders were overwhelmed, not outmatched.

AI capability advances to 69.5 as verification widens into clinical coding and contract validation, enabling automated deployment in new high-stakes domains. Yet safety fails to keep pace. Interpretability remains out of reach, red-teaming lags, and behavioural proxies collapse under the weight of emergent strategies. With no safety measure landing on frontier systems, and a capability jump in verifiable domains, ai_safety drops sharply.

### Audit and Authority

The EU launches a binding audit of offline fallback readiness, targeting energy, health, finance, and border systems. ENISA begins collecting evidence of functional procedures, staff training, and drill records. The Commission promises infringement actions for non-compliance by turn 14, backed by €180 million in conditional funding. Germany and France comply swiftly, but Bulgaria and Cyprus resist detailed disclosure, citing cost and legal ambiguity. Hungary blocks data sharing on grounds of sovereignty, undermining cross-border coordination. The audit is seen as necessary—but its enforcement tests a fragile consensus.

Public sentiment holds at 27.0. No visible benefit offsets the mood of reactive governance. The disinformation wave intensifies ahead of regional elections, eroding trust further. The sovereignty pact among Poland, Czechia, and Romania gains momentum, operating outside EU channels and deepening fragmentation.
