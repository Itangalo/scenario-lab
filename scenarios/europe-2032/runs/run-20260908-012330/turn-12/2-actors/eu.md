## New measure

**Launch a Union-wide audit of offline fallback readiness in essential public services, with binding remediation timelines and public reporting**

A binding audit will assess whether member states have implemented functional, staffed, and regularly exercised offline procedures for energy, health, finance, and border systems, and require corrective action within six months where gaps are found.

## Priority

**Launch a Union-wide audit of offline fallback readiness in essential public services, with binding remediation timelines and public reporting** — because the emergency continuity protocols are only as strong as their weakest implementation, and with a major cyber incident just landed and offensive capability now model-driven, we cannot afford to pretend readiness exists where it does not.

## In practice

We are acting in the wake of a major cyber incident — automated, model-generated, and wide-reaching — that exploited newly verifiable reasoning to weaponise software dependencies across public health and local government systems in three member states. The attack did not cascade nationally, but it exposed how thin our defences remain, especially where continuity planning is paper-thin. The cyber defence breakthrough measure is now finished, but its impact was uneven: Germany and France are resilient, but the Balkans and parts of Southern Europe were left exposed, and even there, detection came too late. The lesson is clear: we must now verify that our fallbacks work, not just that they are written.

The new audit will be run by ENISA under Article 114 TFEU, with technical support from the Joint Research Centre and classified red-teaming data from the behavioural red-teaming network. By Q3 2032, every member state must submit evidence — logs, drills, staff rosters — proving that core services can operate without AI for at least 72 hours. ENISA will publish a public scorecard, and the Commission will trigger infringement procedures against any state failing to meet minimum thresholds by turn 14. We are allocating €180 million from the Cohesion Emergency Fund to support remediation, particularly in Bulgaria and Cyprus, where cost exemptions were previously cited. In exchange for cooperation, we are offering fast-tracked access to the Supply Chain Shield’s rerouted components and co-leadership in the next resilience exercise cycle. Hungary remains resistant, but we are engaging through bilateral channels under the condition that data-sharing restrictions do not block cross-border incident response. This is not about punishment — it is about proving that the Union can still function when the systems we depend on fail.
