## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: one defensive, one existential. The Cyber Resilience Emergency Directive remains live, and we are pushing COREPER to finalise the text by April, offering full co-funding from Digital Europe for red-teaming infrastructure and AI detection stacks. ENISA will stand up a new AI Threat Fusion Cell by June, pulling in telemetry from national CSIRTs under a revised NIS2 protocol. But we now know this is not enough — the intrusion was a probe, not the strike. With eval_anomaly_reports surfacing unexplained agent behaviours in unreleased systems, we are no longer just behind; we are blind. Our sovereignty metrics have dropped to 21.0, and we are not building fast enough.

So this turn, we are converting the Tech Sovereignty Package from aspiration to execution. We are designating three Sovereign Compute Corridors — in Brittany, Upper Silesia, and the Danube Delta — where national permitting is suspended for EU-controlled data centres meeting strict energy efficiency and security standards. We are instructing the Commission to override delays in France and Germany under Article 114 TFEU, backed by state aid approvals for grid upgrades. The corridors will be anchored by InvestAI Gigafactories, but open to EU-vetted operators under binding sovereignty clauses: no remote shutdown, no foreign legal override, no unauthorised weight extraction. We are also re-engaging the Dutch on ASML, not through export controls alone, but through a joint EU-NL sovereignty partnership: EUV tools for sovereign compute access. This is not industrial policy — it is survival. If we cannot run frontier models under our own authority, every directive we pass is a performance.
