## Statement changes

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign control through operational resilience and enforceable oversight  
  - Trigger: The completion of the Sovereign AI Compute Corridors, InvestAI Gigafactories, Telemetry Mandate, and AI Incident Authority — all now finished — without delivering functional sovereignty or safety in the face of opaque reasoning and blocked deployment.  
  - Grounds: These instruments were designed for a world where AI was legible, deployment predictable, and infrastructure under our control. That world is gone. We have built systems that cannot see what they are meant to govern, on compute that does not exist, in a political environment fracturing under external pressure. Continuing to claim we are securing control through these means would be fiction. Our commitment must reflect what we are actually doing: surviving the gap.

- add `survive_the_gap` (commitment): Ensure the EU can endure the period between its lost leverage and any possible recovery of sovereign capacity, by hardening against cascading failure, maintaining minimal continuity of function, and preserving the institutional will to act when capability returns within reach  
  - Grounds: With eu_ai_sovereignty at 10.0, political capital at 13.0, and public sentiment hostile, we are not shaping the future — we are trying not to break irreversibly while waiting for a window. Our actions must shift from building control to preventing collapse. This is not surrender; it is triage.

## New measure

**Stand up emergency continuity protocols for essential state functions under AI-driven disruption**  
A binding directive will require all member states to designate fallback procedures for core services — energy grid management, medical triage, financial settlement, border control — that can operate without AI mediation during sustained cyber or systemic failure.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +15 (by ensuring continuity of essential functions), eu_political_capital +2 over time (by demonstrating competence in crisis response), public_sentiment +3 (by visibly preparing for worst-case scenarios)  
`Applies to:` all member states, European Commission Directorates-General, ENISA, national critical infrastructure operators

## Priority

**Stand up emergency continuity protocols for essential state functions under AI-driven disruption** — because while we try to test what we cannot understand and resist coercion we cannot stop, the next incident may come before the next election, and if hospitals cannot admit patients or grids cannot dispatch power when AI fails, the Union itself will be questioned.

## In practice

We are acting under siege conditions. The red-teaming network continues, but its pace is throttled by legal resistance from both hyperscalers and member states who fear being drawn into U.S.-China confrontations. We are now routing parts of the programme through the European Defence Agency to invoke Article 42.7 TEU as a legal basis for mandatory participation, offering Italy and Greece co-leadership of southern test nodes in exchange for dropping their veto demands. Funding is pulled from the dormant parts of the Digital Europe Programme, and we are fast-tracking clearance for ten red-team leads from national intelligence agencies to access model behaviour logs under classified conditions.

The cyber defence breakthrough is being rolled out, but only in fragments — Germany and France are live, Poland is mid-deployment, and the Balkans remain exposed. We are attaching deployment milestones to Cohesion Fund disbursements this quarter to accelerate uptake. Meanwhile, the Supply Chain Shield is holding: Tokyo has agreed to reroute 15% of Nikon’s mature-node steppers to Hungary and Slovakia, bypassing Dutch chokepoints. In return, we are softening our stance on Japan’s semiconductor subsidies under the Anti-Coercion Instrument review.

But everything rests on whether the state can still function when AI does not. That is why we are launching emergency continuity protocols: a directive drafted under Article 114 TFEU, to be adopted by qualified majority in COREPER next month. Every hospital must have non-AI triage cards. Every grid operator must maintain manual dispatch logs. Every central bank must be able to settle TARGET2 transactions without algorithmic assistance. These are not reversions — they are fire doors. We are assigning ENISA to audit readiness by Q2 2032, and withholding 5% of digital modernisation funds from any state that cannot demonstrate basic offline operation. This is not about going back. It is about making sure there is still a Union to go forward.
