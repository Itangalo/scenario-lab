### Capability Creeps Forward

The discovery that clinical coding, contract review, and structural engineering now admit automated verification—once assumed to require human judgment—has accelerated AI capability. Systems are improving faster in high-stakes domains, pushing `ai_capability` into the 60.0+ range. These advances allow partial automation of multi-day professional workflows, reducing time-to-completion for junior-level tasks in law, medicine, and engineering. The frontier continues to expand beyond code and math, though full autonomy remains uneven and context-dependent.

### Fractures Harden

Amid this acceleration, the EU’s internal divisions deepen. Germany, Austria, and Belgium enforce strict limits on training runs and data centre expansion, citing energy strain and labour disruption. France and Nordic states resist, pursuing sovereign capability through looser oversight. This divergence undermines the EU-wide telemetry mandate, as national policies conflict on data sharing, audit access, and enforcement. The Commission’s push for centralised incident response meets fierce resistance—Germany calls it overreach, the Netherlands invokes privacy, and France demands reciprocal intelligence rights.

### A New Shield, But at a Cost

The European AI Oversight Authority begins integrating ENISA, Europol, and national agencies into a coordinated incident response framework, building on the existing EU-Wide Telemetry and Audit Mandate. Initial efforts focus on protocol alignment and legal interoperability, but operational deployment is delayed by disputes over data sovereignty and jurisdiction. Only three member states commit to early participation. Legal challenges stall cross-border data access, limiting functional integration.

The completion of the Tech Sovereignty Package delivers modest gains in compute infrastructure, but fails to offset broader strategic drift. With no new sovereignty-building measures finishing, and `ai_capability` rising sharply, `eu_ai_sovereignty` falls further.

### Sentiment Stagnates, Capital Erodes

Public sentiment remains low. Labour unrest simmers in legal and public sectors, and energy protests persist. The new coordination effort is framed by critics as surveillance overreach, dampening support. Political capital declines under the weight of multiple large measures and growing fragmentation.
