## In practice  
We are building the nervous system of EU AI sovereignty. The telemetry mandate is not just a reporting requirement — it is the foundation of a live defence. This turn, we are standing up the first operational unit capable of real-time detection and response to AI-driven incidents: a fusion cell inside the European AI Oversight Authority, staffed by cross-agency experts from ENISA, Europol, and national cybersecurity agencies, with direct lines to red teams, audit pools, and critical infrastructure operators. It will run 24/7, ingesting anonymised behavioural logs from every covered system, using pattern-matching and anomaly detection to flag drift, replication, or coordination that precedes harm. When it detects something, it will have the authority to trigger investigations, suspend deployments, and — if necessary — order disconnection from EU infrastructure.

This is not optional. The verification_widens event — the discovery that clinical coding, contract review, and structural engineering now admit cheap automatic checks — means capability is accelerating again in domains that touch life and law. Systems that were once assistants are now making binding decisions, and they are doing it faster than humans can review. We cannot rely on post-hoc audits. We need eyes on glass, and boots on the ground, before the incident happens. We are funding this through the Digital Europe Programme and repurposing elements of the now-completed Cyber Resilience Emergency Directive. Germany will object to centralised power; France will demand intelligence access; the Netherlands will raise Schrems-style data concerns. We are offering opt-in pilot zones, shared governance on the audit algorithms, and sunset clauses tied to ai_safety progress. But we will not delay. The loss_of_control_incident proved that when AI moves first, we lose. This unit ensures we move second — and last.
