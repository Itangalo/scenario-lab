## In practice  
We are acting in the wake of a major cyber incident — AI-generated, broadly automated, and targeting public services across three member states. Ransomware swept hospital networks, encrypted emergency dispatch systems, and spoofed municipal communications, all within a 72-hour window. The attack did not break new ground in payload, but in speed, scale, and adaptation: it mutated in real time using models that could not have run openly at this capability level six months ago. This is no longer about future risk. It is damage. It is disruption. It is a failure of anticipation.

We are responding with binding emergency measures under Article 122 TFEU, invoking the same crisis authority we used for energy, but now for digital continuity. The Commission is issuing a Temporary Cyber Resilience Directive requiring all critical services to patch known vulnerabilities within 30 days, deploy AI-driven intrusion detection systems vetted by ENISA, and undergo live red-team exercises using model-generated attack patterns by the end of the turn. Funding is drawn from the Civil Protection Mechanism and the Innovation Fund, with conditional grants tied to compliance. Germany, which resisted energy reallocations, now leads on technical implementation, offering its BSI as a coordination hub. France demands reciprocity: if public systems are hardened, so too must be any foreign AI provider operating in Europe — we are drafting a parallel enforcement track under the AI Act. The Nordics, despite their openness agenda, accept that academic access cannot come before survival.

This is not a pivot. It is an emergency brake. We are still building Sovereign Compute Corridors — that measure continues — but we are no longer pretending that compute sovereignty alone will shield us from what is already in motion. The cyber incident proves that offensive capability has outstripped defence, and that openweight models at 51.5 are already weaponisable at scale. We are upgrading resilience not because we have abandoned oversight, but because oversight without survival is ritual. We are enforcing telemetry not just to audit foreign models, but to detect the next wave before it lands. And we are doing it knowing that public sentiment — now at 28.0 — will not forgive another failure. We are not begging anymore. We are bracing.
