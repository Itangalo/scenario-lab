### The Digital Onslaught

A wave of AI-synthesised ransomware cripples hospital networks across Belgium, Finland, and Slovakia. Emergency dispatch systems go dark, and forged municipal alerts sow confusion. The attack adapts in real time, exploiting zero-day-like vectors at machine speed. Though no lives are lost, recovery takes weeks. Attribution remains unclear, but forensic analysis confirms the tooling was generated by models far beyond open-source reach.

### Emergency Response and Fractured Unity

The EU invokes crisis powers under Article 122 TFEU, mandating immediate cyber hardening across critical sectors. ENISA certifies AI-driven intrusion detection systems, and red-team exercises using model-generated attacks are rolled out. Germany’s BSI becomes the operational backbone, but resistance flares: Hungary and Poland cite sovereignty concerns, delaying compliance. France pushes to extend the mandate to foreign AI providers, but legal challenges stall enforcement beyond public infrastructure.

The Sovereign AI Compute Corridors initiative advances in planning, but no new compute comes online. The InvestAI Gigafactories were expected to complete this turn, but final integration delays and unresolved energy supply negotiations push full operational launch into the next period. No completion bonus is applied. The Telemetry Mandate is not yet fully implemented, limiting data access for oversight. Meanwhile, the denial of frontier model access — abruptly cut off for EU research institutions — undercuts open capability development. U.S. firms cite compliance with export controls under the ongoing CONSOLIDATION posture, offering no appeal. In response, European open-weight developers accelerate collaboration and model sharing, improving coordination without increasing total compute. This allows more efficient use of existing resources, enabling a modest rise in openweight capability that tracks behind overall AI capability growth.

Public-sector AI successes in Estonia and Finland ease healthcare backlogs, but the cyberattack dominates headlines. Trust in digital continuity erodes. The Commission’s emergency measures are seen as necessary but reactive, underscoring the gap between ambition and resilience. Ongoing regulatory fragmentation, institutionalized legislative resistance to large-scale AI training, and spreading labour unrest in public services collectively weaken the coherence of the EU’s AI posture. With the InvestAI Gigafactories not yet delivering, and frontier access denied, eu_ai_sovereignty declines significantly, reflecting stalled progress and growing internal divergence. Political capital also falls, though less sharply, as member states accept emergency coordination even amid disputes over scope. Public confidence dips further, as the gap between systemic risk and institutional delivery widens.
