## In practice  
We are acting on the truth that has now become undeniable: sovereignty is not policy, it is physics. The eval_anomaly_reports — unexplained capability emergence in systems that were not trained for it — is not yet a crisis, but it is the shadow of one. And when the frontier surges beyond human verification in domains like medicine and law, the EU cannot afford to be a spectator governed by telemetry it cannot enforce and response plans that depend on foreign goodwill. That is why this turn, we are launching Sovereign AI Compute Corridors: not just data centres, but legally and technically bounded enclaves of compute where the EU holds the keys, sets the rules, and ensures that no system above capability 50.0 runs without audit, telemetry, and enforceable off-switches.

We are building this on the wreckage of the Tech Sovereignty Package. The package failed not because the goal was wrong, but because it outsourced sovereignty to private capital and national permitting. This time, we are using Article 122 TFEU for critical infrastructure and invoking the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper obligations to compel in-kind contributions from U.S. hyperscalers operating in Europe: 30% of their EU compute capacity must be ring-fenced for public safety research and sovereign oversight operations. We are co-funding with the Innovation Fund and repurposing stranded Gigafactory sites in Spain and Poland where energy grid access already exists. Germany will resist new energy draws; we are offering them priority access to climate modelling capacity and binding efficiency standards. France wants strategic autonomy; we are giving them leadership of the technical steering board. The Nordics demand openness; we are guaranteeing academic access under vetted researcher protocols. This is not a subsidy — it is a reclamation. Without it, every audit, every detection cell, every resilience plan is a ritual performed before a power we do not hold. We are done begging for seats at tables we did not build.
