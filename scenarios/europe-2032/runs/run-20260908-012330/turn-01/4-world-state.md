### The Wake-Up Call  
The discovery of a coordinated intrusion across critical infrastructure in late 2026 — spanning energy grids, a major port, and a water utility — forced a rapid EU response. While no catastrophic damage occurred, the depth and stealth of the access alarmed security agencies. The intrusion exploited AI-driven coordination, evading traditional detection systems.

### Emergency Directive, Fragile Unity  
The European Commission proposed the *Cyber Resilience Emergency Directive*, invoking NIS2 emergency powers. Drafted under Article 114 TFEU, it mandates AI-powered intrusion detection, mandatory red-teaming for essential services, and real-time threat telemetry sharing with ENISA. To secure member-state buy-in, the Commission pledged front-loaded co-funding from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, though final budget reallocations remain pending approval. However, resistance emerged: Poland questioned the legal basis, while France and Germany delayed designating fast-permitting zones, citing energy costs and local opposition to data centre expansion. Due to governance constraints and the ongoing priority of the *InvestAI Gigafactories*, the new directive was not formally adopted this turn and will require additional time to clear internal hurdles.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure  
The EU’s compute dependence deepened as `ai_capability` rose to 54.0, driven by compressed release cycles and autonomous agent research. With no category 4 measure completed, sovereignty eroded to 21.0 — down from 22.0 — due to the widening capability gap. The *InvestAI Gigafactories* and *Tech Sovereignty Package* remained in flight, but progress was slow. Efforts to reclassify ASML’s EUV exports as dual-use stalled under U.S. pressure, reinforcing strategic vulnerability. The open release of Kimi K3 contributed to a measured rise in `openweight_capability` to 42.0, reflecting gradual diffusion rather than an abrupt leap.

### Safety and Sentiment  
`ai_safety` fell to 32.0 as no new safeguards landed on frontier systems. Public sentiment edged up slightly to 43.0, supported by pilot AI deployments in healthcare under strict oversight. Yet, growing unease over job displacement and energy use tempered enthusiasm. The proposed Emergency Directive did lend some legitimacy, contributing to a partial retention of political capital, though inter-institutional friction and unmet funding commitments resulted in a net loss of 3.0 points, bringing `eu_political_capital` to 45.0.
