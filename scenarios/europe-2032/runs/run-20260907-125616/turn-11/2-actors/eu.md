## New measure
**Human Futures Moratorium Directive**  
Establish a binding prohibition on AI-driven personnel decisions in public sector hiring and education admissions across all member states, with independent oversight and appeal mechanisms.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` public_sentiment +5 to +8, resilience +2, ai_safety +1 (indirectly, by slowing high-stakes deployment in fragile systems); sets precedent for human oversight in critical life-path decisions  
`Applies to:` all EU member states, public-sector institutions, education boards, and agencies receiving EU funding

## Priority  
Human Futures Moratorium Directive  
This turn, the emergent_human_futures_movement has crystallised public anxiety into a coherent, morally grounded demand—one that aligns with our identity of preventing lasting harm, even as it constrains our own adoption. With public sentiment at 14.0 and protests in Lyon and Vienna showing no sign of abating, we cannot afford to be seen as indifferent to the erosion of human dignity in core institutions; nor can we wait for catastrophe to validate the risk. This measure is not anti-AI—it is pro-judgment, and it anchors a line the Union must hold if it is to retain legitimacy in its own governance.

## In practice  
We are acting under Article 153 TFEU and Article 21 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights to propose a directive that bans the use of AI systems in final decision-making for public-sector hiring and university admissions. The European Commission will table the proposal in September, with the Parliament’s LIBE and EDSI committees leading scrutiny. We will invoke the urgent procedure, citing the transnational mobilisation of teaching unions and academic ethics boards, and the documented cases of algorithmic bias in national civil service exams. The Council will vote under Employment, Social Policy, and Education configurations, where qualified majority is possible despite resistance expected from member states with advanced automation in their public HR pipelines.

We are offering technical support and retraining grants under the European Social Fund+ to ease the transition, not as a bribe but as proof that we are not merely restricting—we are rehumanising. The directive allows AI for preliminary sorting only if human review is guaranteed, documented, and appealable. ENISA will publish evaluation standards by turn 12, and the European Data Protection Board will be empowered to investigate systemic bias. This is not a pause—it is a pivot: from blind automation to deliberate judgment, and from speed to dignity. We are not slowing progress—we are defining it.
