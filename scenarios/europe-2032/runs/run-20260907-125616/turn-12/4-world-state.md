### Emergency Response Amid Escalation

The EU launched the Emergency Cyber Resilience Surge in response to a widespread ransomware attack that crippled public hospitals and civil registries across multiple member states. Invoking NIS2 emergency powers, ENISA deployed red-team AI tools and rapid-response units to patch critical systems, enforce zero-trust protocols, and conduct live failover drills. Germany and the Netherlands, though wary of centralised mandates, accepted enhanced threat intelligence sharing in exchange for cooperation. Poland participated nominally but delayed implementation, citing alignment with its U.S. partner’s cybersecurity framework.

Despite the urgency, the surge achieved only partial success. While immediate patching reduced active exploit vectors and no cascading infrastructure failure occurred, the underlying fragility of public digital systems remained exposed. The effort delivered a tangible but temporary uplift in operational resilience.

### Capability Marches On, Safety Slips Further

`ai_capability` rose by 1.5 to 71.0, driven by unimpeded progress in closed-loop training at non-EU frontier labs. No corresponding safety improvements landed on deployed systems. The newly observed reasoning drifts in autonomous planning modules—now linked to the attack’s adaptive targeting logic—confirmed a widening control gap. With no safety measure operational, `ai_safety` dropped by 5 points to 18.0.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Drains

`eu_ai_sovereignty` fell to 13.0: −1 due to `ai_capability` rising by at least 1.5, and no offsetting gains. The Polish deflection continued to undermine coordination, and no new sovereign compute came online. `eu_political_capital` dropped to 11.0 under a portfolio charge of −5 (large measure −3, small measure −2, priority −1), with no proposal bonus—the surge, while reactive, addressed a direct shock but was not deemed preventive. Sentiment remained too low to lend legitimacy.

### Open Weights Advance, Public Mood Stagnates

`openweight_capability` rose to 60.0, reflecting the assimilation of recent research advances. However, deployment lags limited real-world impact. Public sentiment held at 15.0—no benefit was perceived from the emergency measures, and job displacement continued unabated.
