## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: one to stop harm in motion, the other to prevent what is coming. The European AI Emergency Response Corps reaches operational status this turn, and we are treating its completion not as an endpoint but as a threshold. With Germany and France fully onboard, we are activating cross-border intervention protocols between ENISA, the BfArI, and the RKI, granting pre-emptive access to high-risk AI systems in healthcare, biotech, and critical infrastructure under declared bio-risk alerts. Liability shields are in place, and we are fast-tracking mutual recognition of emergency powers among participating states. But we know this is reactive — and the denial of frontier access via **eu_frontier_access_denied** proves we cannot rely on external systems even for defence.

So we are moving forward unilaterally on prevention. The Commission’s Legal Service has issued the Notice of Intended Enforcement under Article 114 TFEU: any model above 65.0 deployed in the EU without adversarial bio-risk testing will be treated as a non-compliant product under the AI Act. We are not waiting for unanimity. The EU AI Evaluation Agency is being reinforced with red team leads from Fraunhofer, SciLifeLab, and the Paul Ehrlich Institute, and we are mandating full adversarial testing of GPT-7, Claude 4, and Llama 4 within 60 days. Results will be public. If developers refuse, their models will be suspended. This is not a negotiation — it is enforcement of a red line. We are also preparing contingency plans to mirror-test open-weight models independently, because once a capability is loose, the only thing standing between it and misuse is detection, deterrence, and the certainty of intervention. We are building all three.
