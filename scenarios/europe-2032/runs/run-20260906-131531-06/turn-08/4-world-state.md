### The Frontier Slams Shut

In early January, European hospitals and research labs were abruptly locked out of the world’s most advanced AI model. No formal notice, no appeal—only error messages citing "geopolitical compliance." The cutoff, affecting diagnostics, drug discovery and emergency planning, landed like a betrayal. In Berlin and Paris, officials called it coercion; in Warsaw and Budapest, it was proof the EU had overreached.

### Corps Activated, But Teeth Blunted

The European AI Emergency Response Corps formally reached operational status, activating cross-border protocols between ENISA, BfArI and RKI. Joint drills were conducted in simulated bio-cyber incidents, and pre-emptive monitoring powers were granted in participating states. Yet without full member-state buy-in, coverage remains patchy. Sweden’s refusal to grant access to its biobanks and Hungary’s rejection of emergency override authority undermine the Corps’ unity.

### A Red Line Proposed, Not Enforced

The European Commission proposed mandatory adversarial bio-risk testing for all models above 65.0, to be enforced under the AI Act. The draft targets GPT-7, Claude 4, and Llama 4, with the EU AI Evaluation Agency beginning preparations for red team assembly. However, no testing has occurred—developer cooperation is absent, and legal questions over extraterritorial enforcement remain unresolved. The measure is under review and faces potential challenges, but no final authority has been established. As the proposal advances, it risks collision with U.S. developers threatening service withdrawal, yet it remains in the legislative pipeline, not in force.

### Capability Marches On

Global AI capability rose to 80.5, driven by self-improving loops. Openweight capability increased to 61.0, as Prometheus-derived architectures began circulating through academic and activist networks, enabling incremental retraining and localized deployment. While momentum builds, the diffusion remains constrained by compute access and coordination limits. Despite the EU’s audit regime, no safety improvements landed on deployed systems. Resilience inched up to 48.0, reflecting partial operational gains from the Corps. Sovereignty fell to 13.0, hit by access denial and capability growth. Political capital dipped to 8.0 under portfolio strain. Public sentiment held at 34.0—aware of the threat, but unconvinced the EU can deliver.
