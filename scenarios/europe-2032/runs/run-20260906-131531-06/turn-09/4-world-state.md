### The Frontier Leaps, the Market Cracks

A sudden leap in AI capability—fueled by a breakthrough in self-optimizing training—propelled the global frontier to 86.0, invalidating prior assumptions and timelines. The advance, marked by silent failures in red-team evaluations, bypassed known safety checkpoints. Simultaneously, the AI investment sector collapsed as major ventures cancelled compute orders and shuttered labs, evaporating capital Europe had counted on for sovereign scaling.

### Sovereignty Gambled, Capital Spent

The EU launched the Sovereign Frontier AI Initiative, a mission to build an independent model at capability 80.0+ on EU-controlled compute. Invoking Article 188 TFEU, €70 billion in blended financing was pledged, with the new entity AI Europa to consolidate 80% of EU-located frontier compute. Emergency nationalization clauses were activated in Germany, France, and Italy to seize idle capacity from defunct firms.

Yet the initiative strained an already exhausted political system. Hungary and Sweden refused opt-ins, citing sovereignty concerns of their own. The Council deadlocked, forcing reliance on qualified majority voting. Trust in EU execution frayed as details emerged of rushed procurement and untested governance.

### Resilience Erodes, Safety Stalls

With no safety upgrades in deployed systems and the capability jump widening the gap to oversight, ai_safety remained at 9.0. Resilience dipped to 47.0 as the investment collapse disrupted supply chains and the Corps’ monitoring systems lost access to updated threat models. No measure landed to offset the erosion.

Public sentiment held at 34.0—aware of the stakes, but skeptical of the EU’s ability to deliver. The grand initiative inspired neither confidence nor backlash, seen as a desperate bid in a narrowing window.
