## Priority

## In practice
We are acting on the conviction that the EU cannot regulate, defend, or respond to what it does not understand, control, or possess. The denial of access to GPT-7 and equivalent systems has not only disrupted healthcare and research—it has exposed a strategic dependency that no audit regime, no emergency corps, no red line can compensate for. We are therefore launching the EU Sovereign Frontier AI Initiative as a mission-scale project under Article 188 TFEU, co-funded by the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the European Defence Fund, with €70 billion in blended public financing over four years.

The initiative will be hosted by a new entity—AI Europa—governed by the Commission with representation from member states and independent technical oversight. It will consolidate access to 80% of EU-located frontier-class compute, repurposing idle capacity from the collapsed private ventures under emergency nationalisation clauses activated in Germany, France, and Italy. We are fast-tracking legal agreements to secure model weights, training data, and inference infrastructure under EU jurisdiction, with export controls and intrusion-resistant architecture from day one. Talent will be recruited through a pan-European fellowship with binding service obligations, drawing from the diaspora of researchers displaced by lab closures.

We are not waiting for unanimity. The Council will be convened under the special procedure for strategic infrastructure, and we are offering Hungary and Sweden targeted opt-ins on biosecurity and data governance to secure qualified majority support. The EIB is preparing bridge financing, and we are instructing ENISA to begin red-teaming the first training run by turn 10. This is not a substitute for regulation—it is the precondition of it. Without our own capability, every rule we pass is subject to veto by those who hold the keys. With it, we regain the power to act.
