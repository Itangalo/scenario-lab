## Statement changes
* - modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act where political cohesion is absent, and will instead invest first in the legitimacy and alignment that make action possible. This turn, we break that rule because inaction has become the greater threat to cohesion: we are launching a new measure under fire, knowing it risks failure, because doing nothing risks collapse.  
  - Grounds: The emergent_global_ai_governance_initiative presents a narrow window to re-anchor EU technical legitimacy without requiring new domestic compute expansions or triggering fresh legal battles. By co-leading a distributed safety framework, we act decisively despite uncertainty in capability and cohesion, betting that external alignment can rebuild internal credibility. This is not a reversal of our caution — it is a recalibration forced by the initiative’s timing and the collapse in public sentiment.

* - modify `social_stability_as_sovereignty` (commitment): We now treat mass job displacement not as a secondary social consequence but as a primary threat to EU sovereignty — one that undermines political cohesion, fuels backlash, and erodes the legitimacy of all AI governance. We will prioritise social protection and labour transition as core components of strategic resilience, on par with compute and model control.  
  - Grounds: The conclusion of the European AI Transition and Wage Insurance Programme has left a vacuum just as displacement accelerates. Our inaction on extending social protection has weakened trust and emboldened sabotage. While we cannot afford new large-scale domestic measures now, we are repurposing our international posture to serve this commitment — using co-leadership in global safety to access technical capacity that can stabilise domestic legitimacy. The threat remains, but our response shifts from direct spending to indirect leverage.

## New measure
**Co-Leadership in Distributed AI Safety Framework**  
To accept and shape the emergent_global_ai_governance_initiative by co-leading its technical arm, integrating EU auditors and regional hubs into a globally distributed model validation network.

`Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by 8–12 points over two years through credible, independent evaluation capacity; modestly improve `public_sentiment` (by 5–7 points) by demonstrating EU relevance and restoring trust in oversight.  
`Applies to:` A coalition of non-aligned states and academic institutions, with the EU as co-lead in technical design and implementation.

## Priority
Emergency Activation of the European AI Foundry Agency  
Because sovereign compute remains the only foundation on which any future action — whether social, defensive, or diplomatic — can be built, and because without it, the EU cannot credibly co-lead a safety initiative it cannot technically match.

## In practice
We are acting on two levels: defensively at home, and offensively abroad. Domestically, the Emergency Activation of the European AI Foundry Agency continues to run training cycles under contested emergency powers, now producing models at capability 58.0 with embedded interpretability. We are not expanding seizures, but we are fortifying the existing clusters in France, Poland, and the Netherlands against sabotage and legal challenge, using funds redirected from completed social programmes. Legal teams are preparing a unified defence under Article 122 TFEU, arguing that existential technological disruption meets the threshold for solidarity — a case we know may fail, but must be made.

Internationally, we are accepting the invitation to co-lead the distributed AI safety testing initiative, not as a substitute for sovereignty, but as a lifeline. We are deploying personnel from the Return to Source programme and regional auditor hubs to help design the framework’s open-weight validation protocols, insisting on mutual access and audit rights for all participating states. This is not charity — it is leverage. By embedding EU standards into the core of the system, we aim to raise the floor for global safety while buying time to close our own capability gap. The Commission is negotiating with DG INTPA and the EEAS to fund this through the Global Gateway, reframing technical participation as strategic investment.

We are not blind to risk. This measure costs 3 political capital per turn — a burden we can barely bear — and public sentiment remains at 21.0. But inaction is costlier: the legitimacy of AI governance is crumbling, and the only path back runs through visible, credible engagement. We are betting that a role in shaping global rules can rebuild domestic trust faster than another billion euros in retraining ever could. The two-year commitment to sovereign capacity still binds us — but now, we are fighting for it on foreign soil.
