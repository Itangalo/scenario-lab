### The Fracture Deepens

The EU responded to escalating physical attacks on data centres with the launch of the EU-Wide Physical Security Corridor, a sweeping directive to protect critical AI infrastructure through militarised zones, rapid response units, and mandatory community engagement. Framed as a defence of territorial integrity, it marked a shift from technological sovereignty to existential governance. Yet resistance flared immediately: constitutional councils in Poland and Hungary declared aspects of the directive ultra vires, and local protests in Bavaria turned violent when federal police clashed with demonstrators near a new InvestAI site.

The **backlash_physical** wave intensified, now spanning 14 incidents across seven member states, including arson at a substation powering a major data hub in eastern France. While the new security protocols prevented total system failure, they failed to prevent damage or restore public trust. Media coverage focused on the use of Frontex units in domestic operations, framing the Union as prioritising machines over people.

Meanwhile, the **Tech sovereignty package** and **Secure sovereign access to frontier model weights** formally concluded, but their legacy was one of erosion. Sweden and Ireland maintained their bilateral audit agreements with U.S. firms, refusing to integrate with the EU’s oversight framework. The Inter-State Compute Consortium expanded, yet without full participation, its cohesion remained fragile.

The U.S. election outcome — a victory for the **election_alliance** — took effect, and the new administration announced structured access to frontier models for allied nations under shared safety standards. While this offered a path to improved cooperation, it also formalised a tiered system that left the EU negotiating from weakness.

Public sentiment dipped further as job displacement in legal and administrative sectors accelerated, and the perception of an "AI police state" took hold in several regions. The Commission’s emergency funding, though fast-tracked, arrived too late to prevent political fallout.

### Sovereignty Under Siege

Despite the urgency, the EU’s political capital continued to drain. The cost of maintaining four large measures, including the new priority, overwhelmed its already strained capacity. Legal challenges mounted, and the legitimacy of centralised action crumbled in the face of member-state defiance.
