The EU launched the EU-Wide Physical Security Corridor in response to escalating attacks on data centres, deploying militarised zones and rapid response units to protect AI infrastructure, but faced immediate backlash. Constitutional challenges from Poland and Hungary, violent protests in Bavaria, and 14 cross-border incidents—including arson in eastern France—undermined the initiative, while the use of Frontex in domestic operations fueled perceptions of an "AI police state." Public trust eroded further amid accelerating job displacement and delayed emergency funding. The Tech Sovereignty Package and sovereign access to model weights formally concluded with limited success, as Sweden and Ireland retained bilateral U.S. audit deals, weakening EU cohesion. The Inter-State Compute Consortium expanded but remained fragmented. The U.S. election_alliance victory introduced structured access to frontier models for allies, establishing a tiered global system that placed the EU at a disadvantage. Overburdened by four major initiatives, the EU struggled with depleted political capital, mounting legal challenges, and declining legitimacy as member states increasingly defied central authority.
