The EU intensified efforts to scale AI infrastructure through accelerated-permitting zones and a proposed €15bn cohesion top-up, but faced internal fragmentation as member states resisted central coordination and bilateral deals with external powers undermined unity. A stalled governance statute delayed InvestAI Gigafactory site selections. Sovereignty concerns deepened when U.S. pressure on ASML restricted access to critical lithography tools, while a member state’s side deal with a U.S. cloud provider eroded the bloc’s unified stance. A sophisticated cyber intrusion into critical infrastructure, potentially AI-assisted, exposed systemic vulnerabilities and weakened confidence in AI safety and resilience. Politically, the EU advanced one major Category 4 measure at significant cost—draining three points of political capital—but failed to secure consensus on a second, with no gains in legitimacy. Public support waned due to job displacement fears and local opposition, while external shocks further strained sovereignty and resilience without altering the strategic trajectory.
