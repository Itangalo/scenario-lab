## New measure
Accelerated-permitting zones for critical AI infrastructure  
Establish fast-track approval corridors for data centres, energy connections and chip fabs tied to EU-controlled compute and security standards.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by +8–12 points; accelerate build-out of sovereign compute by compressing permitting from 5–7 years to 18 months.  
`Applies to:` Member states with strategic AI infrastructure projects, conditional on EU oversight and non-discrimination clauses in access.

## Priority
``Tech sovereignty package`` — Because the supply chain coercion and member state defection prove that legal anchoring and permitting speed are now strategic levers, not just economic ones, and because this turn is the last in which we can shape the package before national implementations begin to diverge.

## In practice
We are operationalising the Tech sovereignty package through the European Chips Act legal base, with the Commission’s Internal Market and DG Connect directorates leading coordination. The Council’s Competitiveness formation will vote on the accelerated-permitting framework in December, backed by a €15bn conditional cohesion top-up for member states that adopt the zones by Q2 2027. Resistance is strongest in capitals that have already struck bilateral hyperscaler deals — particularly in Central Europe — where we are offering co-investment in domestic AI labs as compensation for ceding control over siting and access terms. The package now includes binding clauses that any data centre receiving fast-track status must reserve 30% of its capacity for public-sector and EU-restricted workloads, and must submit to annual weight-security audits by a future EU AI Safety Agency.

At the same time, we are advancing site selection for the InvestAI Gigafactories, with final bids due in January 2027. The project’s financing is secured through a mix of EU guarantees and national equity, but cohesion among member states is fraying: two candidates are pushing for exclusive access to their regional gigafactory’s output, which would undermine the Union-wide pooling model. We are countering with a draft governance statute that allocates capacity based on strategic priority and contribution to resilience, not geography — a proposal we will table in the European Council in November, knowing it will face resistance. The cyber test shot has sharpened urgency, but not consensus.
