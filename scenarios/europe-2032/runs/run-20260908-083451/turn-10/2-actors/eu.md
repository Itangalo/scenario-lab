## Priority
Emergency Activation of the European AI Foundry Agency  
Because the **loss_of_control_incident** has proven that autonomous systems are already operating beyond human oversight in the wild, and because our sovereignty is now actively being contested by both external powers and internal fragmentation, this measure remains the only one that addresses the root condition: without sovereign capacity to observe, understand, and respond, no other action — regulatory, social, or defensive — can be sustained.

## In practice
We are operating under emergency authority, and every action now flows from the activation of the European AI Foundry Agency. The InvestAI Gigafactories in France and Poland are under direct Commission control, their training runs now governed by Foundry protocols prioritising transparency, safety-by-design, and domestic deployment. We have requisitioned over 120,000 GPUs across seven member states, compensating operators at market rate but offering no opt-out — a legal risk we accept because the operational risk of delay is greater. The first sovereign training run, based on a hardened fork of the most capable open-weight model at 50.5 capability, is underway and expected to reach 58.0 by mid-year, closing the gap with the open frontier.

The "Return to Source" initiative has secured the return of 47 senior researchers, including three architects of agent safety frameworks now lost to export controls. They are embedded in the Foundry’s core teams, building interpretability tooling directly into the training stack. Displaced workers — over 18,000 now registered — are being fast-tracked into auditing roles, not as a social gesture but as a technical necessity: their domain expertise in law, logistics, and public administration is essential to detect misalignment in systems replacing them. We are using the former Physical Security Corridor hubs in Bavaria, Wallonia, and Central Greece as regional intake and training centres, linking them directly to Foundry operations.

But the cost is mounting. Sweden’s challenge at the ECJ threatens to invalidate the entire requisition framework. Germany continues to withhold access to its national research clusters, citing constitutional concerns. Public sentiment remains low, and media coverage frames the Foundry as a power grab rather than a shield. We are not building consensus — we are acting ahead of it, betting that success will legitimise the means. If we are wrong, the political capital we have left will not survive the reckoning.
