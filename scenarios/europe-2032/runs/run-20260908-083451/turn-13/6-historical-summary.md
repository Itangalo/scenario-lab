A global shift to post-linguistic AI models collapses existing interpretability-based safety frameworks, rendering the EU’s oversight regime obsolete. In response, the EU launches the Sovereign Post-Linguistic Interpretability Initiative under crisis powers, redirecting Horizon Europe funds and assembling research teams in Paris, Berlin, and Warsaw, but progress stalls due to lack of access to high-capability frontier models. The Emergency Activation of the European AI Foundry Agency remains restricted to France, Poland, and the Netherlands, blocked by ongoing legal opposition from Sweden, Finland, Luxembourg, and Germany, while the Article 122 TFEU justification for expanded powers faces legal scepticism. The EU’s co-leadership in the global AI safety framework weakens as leading labs cease providing interpretable outputs, undermining mutual audits and diminishing the Union’s leverage. Public trust stagnates at 21.0 as protests spread in northern Italy and Belgium over AI-driven job losses in legal and administrative sectors, with blockades emerging at two new infrastructure sites. Resilience declines further as cascading failures hit industrial networks, exposing the absence of effective defenses against AI-driven disruptions. Despite multiple parallel initiatives, the EU lacks the cohesion, capacity, and capital to respond effectively, missing critical opportunities to influence post-linguistic AI governance. EU AI sovereignty remains at 10.0, with global leadership continuing to shift toward non-EU actors.
