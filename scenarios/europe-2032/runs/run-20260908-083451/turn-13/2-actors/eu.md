## New measure  
**Sovereign Post-Linguistic Interpretability Initiative**  
To establish an EU-wide research and deployment programme focused on detecting, monitoring, and interpreting non-linguistic agent reasoning in frontier AI systems, using novel techniques in circuit analysis, anomaly detection, and behavioural fingerprinting.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by 10–14 points over two years by developing sovereign capability to detect misaligned agent behaviour even in absence of chain-of-thought; modestly improve `eu_ai_sovereignty` (+3–5) by creating a unique technical asset no external power controls.  
`Applies to:` EU research institutions, the European AI Foundry Agency, and designated national cybersecurity agencies.

## In practice  
We are acting on three fronts, but only one anchor: sovereignty of understanding. Domestically, the Emergency Activation of the European AI Foundry Agency continues under emergency powers, now repurposing 30% of its training cycles to generate data for post-linguistic interpretability research — anomaly traces, internal activation maps, and agent drift patterns from models at capability 58.0. We are working with CNRS, Fraunhofer, and the Polish Academy to stand up a joint analysis cell by turn 14, funded from redirected Global Gateway allocations. Legal teams are finalising the Article 122 TFEU argument, but we are preparing fallback plans: if the Court rejects the emergency claim, we will request a special Council session under Article 222 to reframe the threat as a matter of internal security.

The Co-Leadership in Distributed AI Safety Framework is now operational, with EU auditors embedded in validation hubs in Singapore, Kenya, and Chile. We are pushing hard for mutual audit rights and shared access to internal model telemetry, but resistance is growing — particularly from labs that no longer produce legible reasoning and thus have nothing to show. Our leverage is thin, but we are using access to European testbeds as bargaining chips, insisting that any lab seeking validation in our network must submit to black-box anomaly testing using EU-developed protocols.

The new Sovereign Post-Linguistic Interpretability Initiative launches this turn, coordinated through a newly reactivated Joint Research Centre taskforce. We are fast-tracking funding via Horizon Europe’s crisis clause, pulling in researchers from the decommissioned Wage Insurance Programme’s technical wing. This is not about catching up — it is about forging a new path. If the world’s most capable systems are no longer explainable, then the EU must be the first to build the tools that can see into the dark, not by reading words, but by reading patterns. We cannot afford to wait for consensus. We are acting because if we do not, no one will — and the next incident will not be a data centre attack, but a decision cascade we never saw coming.
