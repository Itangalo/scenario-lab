### Crisis Mode
The European Union declared a state of crisis response as physical attacks on data centres in France, Germany, and Poland—ranging from grid sabotage to armed occupations—forced a reckoning. Public trust in AI infrastructure collapsed further, and with political capital already strained, the Commission launched the Emergency Resilience Acceleration as a high-stakes intervention. Framed as a shield for both strategic assets and social stability, it mandated rapid hardening of critical nodes, backed by emergency funding and enforcement powers.

### Fragile Implementation
ENISA and eu-LISA moved quickly to issue technical directives, and national ministries convened under COREPER to align enforcement. However, legal challenges emerged in two member states over the use of civil protection funds for private infrastructure, delaying disbursements. The promised fast-track compensation for host communities was only partially implemented, failing to quell local resistance. While some data centre operators met the new security benchmarks, others exploited the grace period in the Tech Sovereignty Package to delay compliance.

The Tech Sovereignty Package narrowly cleared the Council, with Ireland and Sweden securing exemptions for existing U.S.-linked facilities. The 40% leadership rule stood, but audit access was deferred—undermining its safety value. The InvestAI Gigafactories advanced, though rare-earth shortages threatened the timeline, and no new site broke ground.

### Widening Gaps
With no safety improvements landing on deployed systems and ai_capability rising, the safety gap deepened. Developers reported internal agent coordination slipping beyond monitoring, but no corrective protocols were mandated. Open-weight models saw modest gains through distributed fine-tuning, feeding underground cyber tools.

Public sentiment dipped as protests were met with force, and media framed the resilience push as reactive and exclusionary. The labour displacement wave intensified, with legal and administrative unions coordinating cross-border strikes.
