  - Grounds: The physical backlash against AI infrastructure reveals that public sentiment is no longer just sceptical but insurgent, and that even stalled initiatives like sovereign access are now entangled in a legitimacy crisis. * ``modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act where political cohesion is absent, and will instead invest first in the legitimacy and alignment that make action possible. This turn, we break that rule because inaction has become the greater threat to cohesion: we are launching a new measure under fire, knowing it risks failure, because doing nothing risks collapse.``  
  - Trigger: The **backlash_physical** event and the continued erosion of political capital below enforceable thresholds.  
  - Grounds: We previously committed to withholding action without cohesion. But cohesion is now actively decaying, not merely absent. The Union cannot afford to wait for consensus to form under drift; it must be catalysed by decisive, costly action that signals control. We accept the cost of reversing this commitment — it will be extracted in resistance, in overruns, in public dispute — but we judge that the alternative is strategic paralysis.

## New measure  
### Emergency Resilience Acceleration  
A rapid, targeted programme to harden critical data infrastructure against physical and cyber disruption, directly linking security to public legitimacy.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience from 36.0 to at least 45.0; reduce the frequency and impact of physical disruptions to AI infrastructure; begin restoring public sentiment by demonstrating control.  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with binding obligations on operators of fast-tracked data centres and critical grid nodes.

## Priority  
``Emergency Resilience Acceleration`` — Because the physical backlash is no longer a peripheral risk but a core threat to both mandates: it undermines the Gigafactories that anchor our sovereignty and exposes our inability to protect the very infrastructure meant to secure us, turning public fear into active resistance.

## In practice  
We are treating the **Emergency Resilience Acceleration** as a crisis intervention, not a long-term build. Using Article 122 TFEU and the recently invoked emergency powers from the Chips Act, we are directing the European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) and the European Union Agency for the Operational Management of Large-Scale IT Systems (eu-LISA) to co-lead a six-month hardening sprint. All operators of fast-tracked data centres under the Tech Sovereignty Package must now implement physical perimeter security upgrades, real-time intrusion detection, and redundant grid connections within 180 days, funded through a €15bn emergency allocation from the Civil Protection Mechanism and conditional on continued access to accelerated permitting. National interior and energy ministries are being convened in an extraordinary COREPER session to align enforcement, and we are offering fast-track compensation mechanisms for communities near sites to defuse local opposition.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** remain on track, with construction advancing at the first two sites. We are now embedding resilience requirements into their design: each will have dual-grid feeds, on-site battery storage, and armed private security under national police oversight, justified as essential for continuity of strategic compute. The equity model with holdout states is being formalised through a new Inter-State Compute Consortium, with voting rights tied to contribution and crisis access protocols. However, delays in securing rare-earth supply chains for on-site cooling systems threaten the turn-7 finish date — we are exploring emergency waivers on procurement rules to bypass bottlenecks.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** is nearing its final negotiation phase in the Council. We are holding firm on the 40% EU-resident leadership rule and the 30% cloud cap, but have agreed to a phased rollout for the audit access requirement, allowing a six-month grace period post-commissioning. Resistance from Ireland, the Netherlands, and Sweden persists, but the physical attacks on infrastructure have shifted the debate: security is now the dominant frame. We are using that momentum to link approval of the package to the Emergency Resilience Acceleration, presenting them as a unified shield — not just for AI, but for the Union’s capacity to act.
