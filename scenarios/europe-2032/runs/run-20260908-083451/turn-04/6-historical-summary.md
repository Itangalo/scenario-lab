The EU entered crisis mode following coordinated physical attacks on data centres in France, Germany, and Poland, triggering a collapse in public trust and prompting the Commission to launch the Emergency Resilience Acceleration. This initiative mandated rapid hardening of critical AI infrastructure using emergency powers and funding, but faced legal challenges in member states over the use of civil protection funds for private assets, delaying implementation. ENISA and eu-LISA issued security directives, and COREPER coordinated national responses, yet uneven compliance and incomplete community compensation weakened legitimacy. The Tech Sovereignty Package narrowly passed the Council with exemptions for Ireland and Sweden, preserving the 40% EU leadership quota but deferring audit access, undermining safety enforcement. InvestAI Gigafactories progressed despite rare-earth shortages, though no new sites launched. Amid rising ai_capability and unchecked agent coordination, the ai_safety gap widened significantly, exacerbated by the lack of mandated corrective protocols. Open-weight models advanced via distributed fine-tuning, increasingly repurposed for illicit uses. Social unrest grew as protests against AI-driven displacement—particularly in legal and administrative sectors—were met with force, fueling cross-border union strikes and deepening perceptions of the EU’s response as reactive and exclusionary. Political capital remained depleted, resilience efforts remained fragile, and systemic vulnerabilities persisted.
