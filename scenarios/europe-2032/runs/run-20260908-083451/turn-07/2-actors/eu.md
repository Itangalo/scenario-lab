## New measure  
### European AI Transition and Wage Insurance Programme  
A pan-EU framework to provide immediate income support and retraining pathways for workers displaced by AI in legal, administrative, and mid-level technical roles, funded through a temporary solidarity levy on AI-intensive firms.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience from 44.0 to 52.0 by turn 9 by reducing social shock from displacement; stabilise public sentiment from 28.0 to 40.0 by turn 9 through visible support mechanisms; reduce protest intensity in high-displacement regions by 50% within 12 months.  
`Applies to:` All member states, with mandatory participation for firms automating roles above 30.0 capability index tasks, funded by a 7% levy on AI-driven productivity gains reported in annual audits.

## In practice  
We are shifting from a posture of containment to one of reconstruction. The **emergent_labour_displacement_wave** has made it clear: AI is not just disrupting markets — it is dissolving the employment basis of entire professional strata. We are launching the European AI Transition and Wage Insurance Programme under Article 122 TFEU, invoking the economic crisis clause to fast-track approval through the ECOFIN Council. The programme will provide 75% wage insurance for displaced workers for up to 18 months, paired with modular retraining in AI-augmented public services, green infrastructure, and digital governance. Funding will come from a temporary 7% levy on firms reporting AI-driven productivity gains above 15% year-on-year, verified through national statistical offices and audited by Eurostat.

The InvestAI Gigafactories are completing this turn. While rare-earth bottlenecks delayed full deployment, three sites are now operational in France, Poland, and Romania, with grid integration underway. We are redirecting surplus power from decommissioned fossil plants under the Just Transition Mechanism to ensure uninterrupted supply. The Inter-State Compute Consortium will absorb these sites into its mutualisation framework by turn 8, though we acknowledge Sweden and Ireland remain outside full audit integration — a gap we are addressing through bilateral pressure and offers of shared safety research under EUCSIR.

The EU-Wide Physical Security Corridor remains in flight, but its legitimacy is under strain. We are scaling back the use of Frontex in domestic operations following the European Parliament’s emergency resolution, replacing it with a new EU Critical Infrastructure Guard under the authority of the Commission and coordinated through the Civil Protection Mechanism. Community liaison officers are now mandatory at all InvestAI sites, with local benefit-sharing agreements requiring 15% of security contracts to go to regional firms. These are not concessions — they are the new operational baseline for sovereignty in a contested society.
