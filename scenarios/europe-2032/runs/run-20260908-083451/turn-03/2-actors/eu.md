## In practice  
We are treating the InvestAI Gigafactories as the anchor of strategic survival. Site selection has been unblocked via qualified majority voting under the European Chips Act, and we are now finalising energy and grid-reserve pacts with national operators to guarantee uninterrupted power during disruptions. The two holdout member states have accepted equity stakes in the Union-wide operating consortium — 15% collectively, with governance rights and priority access during crises — in exchange for dropping legal challenges. Construction at the first two sites is beginning under emergency permitting, with the Commission invoking Article 122 TFEU to redirect cohesion funds toward grid upgrades and on-site renewable microgrids.

The Tech sovereignty package is being renegotiated to embed enforceable conditions: any entity benefiting from accelerated permitting must commit to 40% EU-resident technical leadership and allow real-time audit access to the EU Digital Reserves Agency for any model trained on public compute. We are also advancing a cap — no single non-EU cloud provider may control more than 30% of fast-tracked data centre capacity — framed as a resilience requirement, not a trade barrier. Resistance persists, particularly in capitals with deep U.S. investment ties, but the recent cyber intrusion has shifted the tone: security is no longer abstract.

The sovereign access measure remains in legal drafting at DG Connect, but we have paused its formal proposal. Without stronger alignment among member states — particularly on the role of U.S.-linked labs — a premature launch risks a high-profile defeat that would undermine all sovereignty efforts. Instead, we are using bilateral Council working groups and the upcoming ECOFIN meeting to build quiet consensus, linking access guarantees to continued market access and R&D collaboration. We are also offering labs a co-governance role in the Digital Reserves Agency, with observer seats and joint protocol design, to transform resistance into partnership. This is not delay — it is preparation.
