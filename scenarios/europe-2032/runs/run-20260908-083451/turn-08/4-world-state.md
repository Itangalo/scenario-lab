### A State of Emergency

In early 2030, the European Commission announced plans to establish a Sovereign AI Foundry, proposing a €45bn multi-year programme to build a fully autonomous AI development pipeline within EU borders. Invoking the strategic urgency of Article 173 TFEU, the Commission initiated preparatory work to mobilise Horizon Europe funds and explore the feasibility of a sovereign AI bond. The initiative is under urgent review by ECOFIN and requires unanimous Council approval, with significant debate expected over fiscal sustainability and allocation priorities. No funds have been disbursed, and no operational capacity has yet been created.

The proposal, still in its drafting phase, aims to leverage the recently completed InvestAI Gigafactories in France and Poland—assets originally built under a prior measure—but their integration into a sovereign training pipeline remains contingent on resolving legal, technical, and member-state consent issues. Sweden and Ireland have already signalled reservations about centralised control of national compute assets, citing bilateral agreements and security concerns. The Commission has not formally prioritised this initiative over existing measures, and no reprioritisation decision has been adopted. Given the scale and complexity of the proposal, implementation cannot begin before Turn 10 at the earliest, in accordance with minimum lag requirements for high-cost measures.

### Cascading Failures

A major AI-generated ransomware attack crippled emergency services in Belgium, Greece, and Slovakia, exposing critical gaps in cyber resilience. While the EU-Wide Physical Security Corridor officially completed its rollout, its focus on physical protection proved insufficient against digital threats. Response coordination was slow, and attribution remains unclear.

Simultaneously, the United States tightened chip and model export controls, now rationing even allied access. This *de facto* exclusion confirmed the EU’s strategic vulnerability. Public sentiment remains under pressure as media highlight job losses in legal and administrative sectors, though no new protest wave has emerged in this half-year. The European AI Transition and Wage Insurance Programme continues to face delays in ECOFIN, where concerns persist over the funding model and scope.
