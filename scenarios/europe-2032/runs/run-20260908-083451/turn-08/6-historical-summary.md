The European Commission proposed a €45bn Sovereign AI Foundry under Article 173 TFEU, aiming to create an autonomous AI development pipeline using Horizon Europe funds and potential sovereign AI bonds, with integration planned for InvestAI Gigafactories in France and Poland; the initiative remains in drafting, pending ECOFIN and unanimous Council approval, facing resistance from Sweden and Ireland over control of national compute assets and fiscal concerns, and cannot launch before Turn 10. A major AI-generated ransomware attack disrupted emergency services in Belgium, Greece, and Slovakia, exposing critical cyber vulnerabilities unaddressed by the newly completed EU-Wide Physical Security Corridor, which focuses on physical threats. U.S. export controls further tightened, restricting even allied access to chips and models, reinforcing the EU’s strategic dependency. The European AI Transition and Wage Insurance Programme remains stalled in ECOFIN over funding and scope disputes, while public anxiety persists due to job displacement in legal and administrative sectors. Institutional capacity is strained by three major ongoing initiatives—the Security Corridor, Wage Insurance Programme, and InvestAI Gigafactories—with no new measure activated; political capital remains stable but under pressure. Global AI capability advanced to 61.5 due to external progress, while open-weight model development within the EU saw no gains. Legal challenges to emergency powers grow, and trust in EU-level responses continues to erode amid perceived fragmentation and delayed action.
