Secure sovereign access to frontier model weights  
Establish a legal and technical framework to ensure the EU can access, audit, and if necessary assume control over model weights developed under EU-funded or EU-hosted training runs.

## In practice
We are now treating the InvestAI Gigafactories as a strategic emergency. The Taiwan blockade has frozen access to advanced packaging and mature-node chips alike, making domestic production not just an industrial policy goal but a survival imperative. We are fast-tracking site selection by bypassing national veto threats through qualified majority voting under the European Chips Act’s crisis clause, and we are offering the two holdout member states a 15% equity stake in the Union-wide operating consortium — not exclusive access, but a share of governance and first call on resilience-critical capacity during disruptions. The Commission is preparing to invoke Article 122 TFEU to justify emergency funding reallocations, and we are securing energy commitments through binding grid-reserve agreements with national operators.

Simultaneously, the Tech sovereignty package is being renegotiated to include hard caps on foreign cloud providers’ market share in sovereign AI workloads — no single non-EU entity may control more than 30% of fast-tracked data centre capacity. This directly counters the recent side deal, and we are framing it not as retaliation but as risk diversification. Resistance remains high in capitals dependent on U.S. investment, but the cyber intrusion and job displacement wave have shifted the debate: we are now linking permitting speed to enforceable social and security conditions. The cohesion top-up is being rebranded as a “resilience premium” — paid only if member states adopt both the fast-track zones and the new cyber hardening standards from the upcoming Council directive.

The new measure — sovereign access to model weights — is being drafted under DG Connect with legal grounding in the AI Act’s high-risk provisions and the Chips Act’s security clauses. We are requiring that any model trained at >50 petaflop-s under EU-subsidised compute must deposit encrypted weights with a new EU Digital Reserves Agency, with decryption keys held jointly by the Commission and a rotating trio of member states. This is not nationalisation — it is insurance. Developers retain IP, but the Union gains a last-resort option to restart or audit a model if its parent entity collapses, defects, or is compromised. We know this will face legal challenges, especially from U.S.-linked labs, but the openweight release has changed the game: if the frontier is going open anyway, our leverage is in controlling where and how it is trained — not just what is released.
