### Emergency Powers in a Collapsing System

The EU activates emergency protocols in response to a cascading ransomware attack that exploited AI-generated zero-days to cripple hospital appointment systems across Belgium, Hungary, and Portugal. With ai_safety at 0.0 and no capacity to verify or control frontier systems, the Commission invokes Article 12 of the AI Act to establish the Emergency Sovereign Control Cell under the European AI Office. The Cell is granted temporary authority to mandate disengagement of high-impact AI systems in health, energy, and civil protection during declared incidents.

Despite legal grounding, implementation falters. Resistance emerges from national digital ministries, particularly in Poland and Sweden, who denounce the move as institutional overreach. Legal challenges are filed within days, though the Cell proceeds under delegated powers. Initial operations are limited to coordination and manual override drills; no live disengagement occurs due to political hesitation and unclear thresholds for action. However, the mere activation of the Cell—widely reported as a "digital triage unit"—stabilises some public anxiety.

The broader sovereign compute initiative collapses. With eu_political_capital at 0.0 and no consensus on energy-sharing, Finland and Sweden withdraw conditional support. The project is suspended indefinitely, symbolising the EU’s eroded capacity to execute long-term infrastructure.

Resilience sees a modest uptick as the new Cell integrates with ENISA and HERA, improving crisis coordination. Public sentiment inches upward—not due to trust in AI, but because the EU is finally acting visibly in a crisis. Yet structural fragility remains. The US consolidation posture continues to restrict access, and openweight models at 65.0 enable further automation of mid-level jobs, feeding discontent.

### Triage, Not Transformation

The EU is no longer shaping the future. It is managing collapse. The Control Cell may buy time, but it does not restore control. The system is moving faster than institutions can adapt.
