**Launch emergency sovereign control cell for critical public services**  
Stand up a temporary, high-authority unit under the European AI Office to take direct control of AI systems in health, civil protection, and energy infrastructure, using emergency powers to disable or override autonomous functions during incidents.

## In practice  
This turn, we are treating the cyber_major_incident — a ransomware cascade that exploited AI-generated zero-days to disrupt hospital appointment systems in Belgium, Hungary, and Portugal — as a system failure, not an anomaly. The attack was not just automated; it was adaptive, evading signature-based detection and reconfiguring its payload in real time. With ai_safety at 0.0, we have no way to verify the integrity of any AI-driven system in public service. Our only remaining instrument is emergency power. We are invoking Article 12 of the AI Act, as amended during the 2027 crisis, to establish a temporary Sovereign Control Cell under the European AI Office, with direct line authority over all high-impact AI systems in critical infrastructure. This is not a new regulatory framework — there is no time — but a triage mechanism: during declared incidents, the Cell can mandate system disengagement, enforce manual override, and coordinate emergency patching through pre-positioned human teams. We are drawing on HERA’s crisis logistics network and ENISA’s incident response capacity to staff it, and we are fast-tracking access through delegated acts under the Digital Services Act. Resistance from national digital ministries is expected — particularly from those who see this as EU overreach — but we are offering a trade: this is time-limited, sunset after 12 months, and tied to a commitment to rebuild political capital through visible crisis resolution. If we cannot stop the bleeding, we cannot rebuild. This is not the future we wanted — it is the one we have.
