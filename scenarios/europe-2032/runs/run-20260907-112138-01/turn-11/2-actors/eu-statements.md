# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign technical agency through auditable AI

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now act with the knowledge that uncertainty is no longer a temporary condition, but the operating environment.
  - Grounds: The terminal phase is now structurally irreversible: ai_capability at 92.0, with ai_safety at 0.0, means that no future decision will be made under conditions of full understanding. The automated_decision_scandal and labour_displacement events confirm that harm is already materialising at scale, not as speculation but as lived injustice. Waiting for perfect clarity is not prudence — it is abdication. Our commitment must evolve: not merely to act despite uncertainty, but to institutionalise action *within* it, accepting that every intervention will be partial, contested, and subject to revision. This turn, we shift from treating uncertainty as a barrier to cross, to treating it as the ground on which legitimacy is now built.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Anchor sovereign capability in auditable AI by making it the mandatory standard for all high-consequence public-sector deployments, and by establishing an EU-wide verification infrastructure to enforce it
  - Grounds: The Open Science Resilience Initiative is on track, but its impact is bottlenecked by slow adoption and legal resistance. The automated_decision_scandal proves that unverifiable systems are politically toxic and institutionally unsustainable. Meanwhile, the rise of openweight_capability to 60.0 shows that distributed systems are now capable enough to displace junior professionals — meaning the EU can no longer wait for a sovereign frontier model to act. Our focus must shift from *proving* auditable AI works to *mandating* it where it matters. This is not a pivot, but a necessary deepening: sovereignty now means enforceable standards, not just technical alternatives.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
