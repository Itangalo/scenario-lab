## New measure  
**Mandate auditable AI in high-consequence public systems and build the verification infrastructure to enforce it**  
Establish a binding EU regulation requiring all AI systems used in social protection, justice, policing, and critical infrastructure to be fully auditable, with open reasoning traces, third-party verification rights, and public accountability mechanisms.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +15 (by forcing transparency where harm is most likely), eu_ai_sovereignty +8 (by creating EU-controlled verification stack), public_sentiment +5 (by responding to scandal with clear accountability)  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, all member states, with enforcement via national digital regulators and the European AI Office

## Priority  
**Mandate auditable AI in high-consequence public systems and build the verification infrastructure to enforce it** — because the automated_decision_scandal has made clear that unverifiable systems are now politically untenable, and because labour_displacement confirms that AI is reshaping the social contract; this measure turns defensive exposure into sovereign leverage by making trustworthiness a binding standard, not an aspiration, and does so in a way that directly advances our two-year commitment.

## In practice  
This turn, we are finalising the integration of the joint cyber-biosurveillance pact, with ENISA and ECDC now operationally linked through secure telemetry pipelines and co-located analysis cells in Brussels and Stockholm. The system is live, and while data sovereignty disputes have left gaps in coverage, the core architecture is functional — a rare win in a period of depletion. We are treating this as a proof of concept: if real-time cross-domain threat detection can work here, it must become the norm, not the exception. At the same time, we are accelerating the Open Science Resilience Initiative, with €4.2bn now disbursed and the three national hubs fully staffed and co-located with biosecurity agencies. DG CONNECT and DG SANTE are finalising deployment pathways, ensuring that any model developed under the initiative can be rapidly integrated into public health and infrastructure operations.

But the political ground has shifted. The automated_decision_scandal — a ruling that an AI-supported welfare eligibility system in a major member state wrongfully denied thousands of claims — has shattered the legitimacy of opaque public AI. Combined with visible labour displacement in law, accountancy, and software, public trust is fraying. We cannot afford to wait for the next crisis. That is why we are launching a binding regulation to mandate auditable AI across all high-consequence domains. The Commission will draft the act under Article 114 TFEU, with input from the European Data Protection Board and the AI Office, and propose it to the Council and Parliament by Q1 2032. We will require not just explainability, but verifiability: third-party auditors must be able to reproduce decisions, inspect reasoning chains, and trigger emergency overrides. National regulators will be empowered to halt deployments that fail audit. This is not just a technical standard — it is a political reclamation of control. We are offering member states opt-in support from the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking to build verification clusters, and tying cohesion funds to compliance. Resistance will come from ministries reliant on foreign black-box systems, but we will frame this not as restriction, but as the price of legitimacy in a terminal phase: if you cannot show how a decision was made, you cannot make it at all.
