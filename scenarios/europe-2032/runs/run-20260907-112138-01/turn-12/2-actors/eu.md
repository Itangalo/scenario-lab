## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that in the terminal phase, these mandates converge: without preventing harm, there is no future to determine, and without sovereignty, no harm can be reliably prevented.  
- Grounds: The collapse of public trust following the automated_decision_scandal and the irreversible momentum of ai_capability to 94.5 — far beyond any capacity for oversight — has erased the illusion of a trade-off. The system is not just failing; it is illegitimate. At the same time, emergent_local_grid_pressure and export_control_escalation confirm that dependence is not just strategic but operational: the EU cannot enforce standards if it cannot power or access the systems it seeks to govern. Our identity must reflect this synthesis — not balancing two mandates, but fusing them into a single imperative: to build authority where none can be assumed.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor sovereign capability in auditable AI by making it the mandatory standard for all high-consequence public-sector deployments, and by establishing an EU-wide verification infrastructure to enforce it — now extended to include energy sovereignty as a precondition of technical sovereignty  
- Grounds: emergent_local_grid_pressure — the coordinated curtailment of data centres by five member states — proves that without control over the physical substrate of AI, no regulatory standard can be enforced. The verification infrastructure we are building under the current measure is meaningless if it cannot run continuously. Our two-year commitment must now encompass not just the logic of decisions, but the lights that power them. This is not a dilution, but a deepening: auditable AI must rest on sovereign, resilient compute, or it will not rest at all.

## New measure  
**Establish EU sovereign verification compute with energy-resilient infrastructure**  
Build a dedicated, physically secured, and energy-hardened compute cluster under EU legal jurisdiction, powered by prioritised grid access and on-site renewable generation, to host the verification infrastructure for auditable AI and to serve as the foundation for future sovereign models.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +12 (by anchoring verification and future capability on EU soil), resilience +5 (through hardened energy access), eu_political_capital -2 (initial cost, but stabilises long-term trust), public_sentiment 0 (neutral in short term, but removes energy as a flashpoint)  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with primary siting in member states with surplus geothermal and hydro capacity (Finland, Sweden, Austria), managed by EuroHPC JU with oversight from the European AI Office

## Priority  
**Establish EU sovereign verification compute with energy-resilient infrastructure** — because without control over energy and infrastructure, no standard of auditability can be enforced, and because emergent_local_grid_pressure has revealed that the EU’s regulatory authority is now physically constrained: we cannot verify what we cannot power.

## In practice  
This turn, we are treating the completion of the Open Science Resilience Initiative as a technical success but a political warning. The three national hubs are operational, and models are being tested in biosecurity workflows — but their deployment is bottlenecked by reliance on foreign-hosted inference and unstable grid access. We are now converting that momentum into infrastructure: the Commission is drafting a delegated act under the Digital Europe Programme to redirect €6.8bn from unused cohesion funds into a new class of sovereign compute facilities. These will not be general-purpose data centres, but verification enclaves — legally bound to host only auditable systems, with physical access restricted to EU-certified evaluators, and powered by hybrid microgrids combining on-site geothermal and battery storage. We are negotiating with Finland and Sweden to fast-track permitting under the EU Strategic Projects framework, invoking Article 122 TFEU for energy security. Resistance from energy ministers remains, but we are offering binding commitments: these facilities will operate under dynamic load capping, and excess capacity will be reserved for public health and civil protection computing. The European AI Office is drafting the verification stack’s architecture now, ensuring it can be deployed the moment hardware comes online. This is not a retreat from auditable AI — it is its material foundation. Without it, every standard we write is a promise we cannot keep.
