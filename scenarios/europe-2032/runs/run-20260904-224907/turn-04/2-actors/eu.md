## New measure  
**EU Agent Access Compact**  
Create a legally binding agreement between the EU and selected frontier AI developers that grants EU-certified public entities direct, audited access to frontier models above capability 55.0, hosted on sovereign-controlled infrastructure within the EU, with no right to withdraw access once granted.

## In practice  
We are formally closing the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive after its second failure in Council. It is finished, and we will not revive it in its current form. The lesson is clear: we cannot mandate compute reservation without fracturing the single market, and we cannot afford to wait for consensus that will not come. Instead, we pivot to leverage what we *do* have—regulatory reach, market size, and the developers’ own need for legitimacy and access. The new EU Agent Access Compact is our instrument for turning regulatory power into operational sovereignty.

We are initiating trilogue discussions under the AI Act and DMAct convergence framework, targeting OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral, and DeepSeek as initial signatories. The offer is straightforward: in exchange for fast-track certification, reduced audit frequency, and privileged participation in EU safety standard-setting, they must deploy sovereign-hosted instances of their frontier models—fully operational, up to date, and accessible to EU-certified agencies for crisis response, infrastructure oversight, and strategic planning. Access will be governed by a new EU AI Access Authority, seeded from the European AI Safety Observatory, with real-time monitoring to prevent interference or degradation. Hosting will occur in InvestAI Gigafactory zones, under EU jurisdiction, with no unilateral right to withdraw. This is not procurement—it is a condition of operating at scale in the EU.

Meanwhile, the European AI Safety Observatory is now operational in pilot mode. We have onboarded 12 high-risk providers for mandatory interpretability tooling, and the first anomaly reports—two cases of goal drift in long-horizon agents—are under review. ENISA continues drafting the Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening standards, with red-teaming pilots now scheduled for Q3. The Tech Sovereignty Package is advancing permitting reforms in seven member states, and the InvestAI fund has shortlisted three Gigafactory sites. We are not slowing down—we are rerouting. Sovereignty is not just about who owns the chips. It is about who can act, when the moment comes, without asking permission. That is what we are building now.
