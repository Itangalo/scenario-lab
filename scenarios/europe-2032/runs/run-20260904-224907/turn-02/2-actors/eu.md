## New measure  
**Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative**  
Launch a binding programme to secure essential services — energy, water, health, transport — against AI-driven cyber threats through mandatory intrusion detection upgrades, red-teaming, and failover automation.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to +18 to resilience by turn 5; begins closing the gap between advancing AI capability and societal robustness, especially in cross-border infrastructure  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, targeting operators of essential services under NIS2, with enforcement tied to EU funding and certification

## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because despite rising costs and resistance, binding control over compute on EU soil remains the linchpin of sovereignty, and without it, even hardened infrastructure depends on foreign permission to defend itself.

## In practice  
We are advancing the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive through co-legislation under Article 114 TFEU, with the Commission’s AI Office leading negotiations in the Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formation. The text is now in trilogue preparation, and we are offering a binding side-letter to the Competitiveness Council: compliance with the 30% reservation will unlock fast-track access to both the InvestAI Gigafactories fund and preferential grid allocation in AI Acceleration Zones. We are also finalising the governance model for the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC), which will operate the capacity auction, ensuring transparency and insulating allocations from political interference. The goal is a first reading agreement by June, before summer recess.

Meanwhile, the Tech sovereignty package continues to face uneven uptake. Only three member states — France, Poland, and Finland — have formally committed to hosting AI Acceleration Zones, while Germany and Sweden remain观望 due to energy cost concerns. We are responding with a revised fiscal incentive: a temporary 15% EU co-financing top-up for zones that deliver grid connection in under 24 months. This is not a concession — it is a calibration. We cannot wait for perfect consensus when the capability frontier does not.

The new Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative is our answer to the recent intrusion. It will be proposed under the NIS2 Directive, with binding technical standards developed by ENISA in collaboration with national CERTs. The first phase targets 120 operators of essential services across energy and health, requiring deployment of AI-augmented intrusion detection systems, quarterly adversarial simulations, and automated failover protocols for core control systems. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with non-compliant operators losing access to EU resilience grants. This is not about chasing the frontier — it is about making sure the ground beneath us does not give way.
