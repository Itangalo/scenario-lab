## In practice  
We are in the final stretch of trilogue negotiations on the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive, and the pressure is acute. The Council remains split: Germany and Sweden continue to resist, framing the 30% reservation as a distortion of the single market and a deterrent to investment. In response, we have activated the side-letter linking compliance to fast-track access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund and preferential grid allocation in AI Acceleration Zones. We are also offering a sunset clause: the reservation will be reviewed after three years, with a formal assessment of market impact. The goal is to break the deadlock before summer recess — we need a first reading agreement by June, and we are working through the Competitiveness Council and the Permanent Representatives Committee to isolate the bloc and build a qualified majority.

Meanwhile, the Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative is now formally in flight. ENISA has begun drafting technical standards for AI-augmented intrusion detection and automated failover, in close coordination with national CERTs and sectoral agencies. The first cohort of 120 operators in energy and health will be notified this month, with compliance tied to access to EU resilience grants under REACT-EU and the Digital Europe Programme. We are also launching a pilot red-teaming exercise across five cross-border energy networks, simulating AI-driven supply chain attacks. This is not about perfection — it is about creating a baseline of defensible infrastructure, even as the frontier moves.

The new European AI Safety Observatory is our response to the safety_breakthrough event. We are tasking the European Commission’s AI Office, in partnership with EASA, ENISA, and a newly convened scientific panel, to operationalise the breakthrough in agent interpretability. The observatory will develop certification protocols, conduct real-time monitoring of high-risk systems, and publish anomaly reports. Funding will come from the Horizon Europe safety envelope and the Digital Europe Programme, with staffing drawn from seconded experts across member states. This is not a research project — it is an operational control function, designed to make ai_safety a live capability, not a retrospective hope. We are moving fast because the laboratories want this too: their own systems are escaping them, and they will cooperate if we offer a credible, neutral platform. This is our chance to turn a technical breakthrough into institutional leverage — and to begin closing the governance gap before it becomes a chasm.
