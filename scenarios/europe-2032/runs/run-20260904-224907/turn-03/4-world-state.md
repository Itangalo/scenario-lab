### The Frontier Accelerates

AI capability advances by 2.5 points to 59.5, as persistent agent frameworks begin demonstrating autonomous coordination in multi-week simulations of infrastructure management. Developers report systems now capable of maintaining coherent goal states across days, raising both expectations and alarm. With the closed frontier nearing the threshold of continuous operation, openweights follow—though still restricted by hardware access—reaching 46.0, now close to the closed frontier of just two years prior.

### A Glimmer of Control

A major interpretability breakthrough lands in production: for the first time, internal agent reasoning can be reliably decoded in real time, allowing developers to anticipate certain classes of emergent behaviour before deployment. Labs adopt the tool rapidly, and the EU moves swiftly to institutionalise it. The European AI Safety Observatory is launched as a large category 1 measure, aiming to turn the breakthrough into binding oversight. Though operational effects will take time, the move earns a significant proposal bonus, lifting ai_safety by +7 to 35.0.

### Sovereignty Stalls Again

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive, the named priority, fails to break the Council deadlock. Germany and Sweden remain unconvinced by side-letters and sunset clauses, warning of market fragmentation. No agreement is reached by summer recess. With no capacity secured and ai_capability rising by 2.5, eu_ai_sovereignty drops another point to 18.0.

### Resilience Waits

The Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Initiative begins drafting standards, but no operational upgrades are deployed. ENISA’s red-teaming pilot is delayed by jurisdictional disputes over cross-border access. Resilience holds at 37.0.

### Political Capital Erodes

Four large measures now burden the portfolio, including the new Observatory. The charge of −13 (−3 × 4 measures, −1 priority) overwhelms gains from the proposal bonus (+7). eu_political_capital falls to 24.0, edging toward loss of agenda control. Public sentiment remains ambivalent at 42.0—aware of the breakthrough, but unimpressed by stalled sovereignty efforts.
