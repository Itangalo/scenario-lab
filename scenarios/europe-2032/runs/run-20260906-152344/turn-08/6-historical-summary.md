AI capability stabilizes at 69.0 as private investment wanes, leaving opaque systems unregulated and advancement stalled. The EU completes the Sovereign AI Core Programme and Resilience Reinforcement Initiative, but both falter: the SACP’s infrastructure remains unstaffed due to legal disputes over hiring, while resilience measures see uneven adoption, with mandatory patching enforced only in larger states and incident protocols revealing coordination gaps. Gigafactories operate below capacity amid incomplete funding redirection. Public trust hits a nadir, fueled by media depictions of bureaucratic failure and the lingering welfare scandal. Behind closed doors, France, Germany, the Netherlands, and Poland deepen bilateral alignment through a de facto sovereignty pact—establishing joint compute vetoes and red-teaming funding—undermining EU unity and drawing accusations of a “directorate within the Union.” The European Red-Teaming Network launches without immediate impact, as informal cross-border tests begin in parallel to nascent efforts on synthetic biology risk assessment, signaling early steps toward an allied assurance framework despite absent formal structures. Political capital continues to erode under the strain of seven overlapping initiatives, most delivering limited results.
