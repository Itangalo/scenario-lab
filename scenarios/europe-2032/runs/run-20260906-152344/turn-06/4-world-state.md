### A Leap and a Fracture

The global AI frontier surges forward with a discontinuous advance: systems now autonomously chain exploits across digital infrastructure, completing multi-day cyber campaigns without human oversight. This leap pushes capability to 69.0, well beyond prior projections. In a rare positive development, leading labs deploy a breakthrough in mechanistic interpretability, allowing them to predict certain emergent behaviours before deployment — a genuine step toward control.

Yet cohesion fractures. One EU member state bypasses the bloc’s emerging safeguards, securing bilateral access to a 68.0-capability model from a US hyperscaler. The move, defended domestically as economic necessity, undermines the EU-wide Containment Protocol and exposes deep divisions over sovereignty versus access.

In response, the European Commission elevates enforcement of the AI Containment Protocol, invoking Article 16 TFEU to mandate air-gapped operation and dual-key control for all high-capability sovereign systems. France and Germany commit hosting rights for the first cluster, in Saint-Malo and Kiruna, under joint oversight. The newly operational European AI Assurance Directorate begins pre-training reviews under the now-legally binding Sovereign Model Assurance Framework.

Meanwhile, the expired Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge leaves a patching gap that the new Resilience Reinforcement Initiative aims to close. It launches with mandates for AI-assisted patching, cross-border incident handovers, and a nascent bio-detection network using sovereign models to scan for dangerous pathogen designs. Implementation will take time; initial systems are in testing, with full deployment expected in a future turn.

Public sentiment remains stagnant at 25.0. Fiscal strain intensifies as seven major initiatives remain in flight, draining political capital despite the completion of two key programmes.

The open-weight ecosystem shows modest progress, buoyed by spillovers from published methods related to the new interpretability techniques, but no coordinated open release or distributed training effort has yet materialized. Without structural investment or coordinated action, the rise in openweight capability remains limited and gradual, reaching 51.0. The gap with frontier capability persists and may widen if current trends continue.
