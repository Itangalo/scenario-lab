### The Hollow Core

The EU completes the Sovereign AI Core Programme and Resilience Reinforcement Initiative on paper, but their operational failures deepen the crisis of confidence. Data centres in Norrland, Marseille, and Vilnius stand powered but unstaffed. The auditability promises of the SACP collapse at launch: without personnel to monitor training runs or enforce access controls, the infrastructure is inert. Resilience gains are partial — patching velocity improves in larger states, but smaller members resist mandates, citing cost. A simulated ransomware test exposes critical coordination lags between national CSIRTs.

Public sentiment remains stagnant. Media outlets circulate drone footage of empty server halls, juxtaposed with leaked internal assessments calling the SACP a "zombie programme." The welfare scandal continues to shadow AI governance, now framed as systemic incompetence.

Behind closed doors, the Franco-German-Dutch-Polish group deepens its parallel coordination, signing informal agreements on joint vetoes over foreign compute deals and shared red-teaming facilities. This de facto directorate accelerates capability development outside EU structures, further eroding institutional unity.

### A Corps in Name Only

The European Union launches the European Sovereign AI Corps as its new priority, invoking emergency powers under the Digital Crisis Act to bypass national civil service constraints. Fast-track hiring begins, but immediate legal challenges emerge. Civil service unions in France and Italy file preliminary objections, arguing pay parity violations. Germany and France condition support on joint command authority and access to Finnish anomaly-detection models, delaying final agreements.

Estonia and Finland demand guaranteed model access in exchange for personnel, stalling recruitment from the Baltic. The Commission’s Legal Service delays final approval, citing unresolved tensions with Article 45 TFEU on free movement of workers.

Despite these hurdles, 80 positions are filled by Q3 — mostly junior roles — while leadership posts remain vacant. Without full staffing, red-teaming simulations and forensic monitoring cannot begin. The Corps exists in name, but not in function.

An open-weight release circulates widely, generating excitement in developer communities and concern among security agencies. However, its capabilities remain aligned with the broader trajectory of open development — it does not leap beyond the current ceiling set by the closed frontier.
