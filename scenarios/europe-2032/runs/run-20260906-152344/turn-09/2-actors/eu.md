## In practice
This turn marks the collapse of credibility in EU-level delivery. The Sovereign AI Core Programme delivered steel but not soul — its racks sit idle, its audit promises broken before first use. The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative achieved patching mandates in half the bloc and launched detection nodes in two cities, but coordination gaps remain wide and smaller states opt out under cost pressure. Public sentiment remains near historic lows, with nightly news showing empty server halls and leaked memos about “zombie programmes.” Behind the scenes, the Franco-German-Dutch-Polish directorate moves faster than Brussels, signing side agreements on compute vetoes and red-teaming funding, fracturing cohesion under the guise of necessity.

We are now betting everything on people. The European Sovereign AI Corps is not another procurement line — it is an institutional transplant. We are invoking Article 336 TFEU to create a new category of EU civil servant with binding technical competence requirements, paid above national scales with RePowerEU contingency funds redirected from stalled grid projects. Fast-track secondments will pull top talent from national cyber commands and research institutes, starting with 200 positions in Q3: 80 embedded in SACP operations, 60 in ENISA red teams, 40 in national bio-risk units, and 20 rotating through cross-border incident command. Germany and France have signaled conditional support — they want joint command authority and reciprocal access to Finnish anomaly-detection models. We are offering them seats on the Corps’ governing board and priority rotation slots in return for dropping bilateral exclusivity clauses. Estonia and Finland, meanwhile, demand guaranteed model access as a condition of sending coders — we are drafting side letters under the Tech Sovereignty Package to deliver it.

Our legal basis is contested. National civil service unions argue that the Corps undermines pay parity and collective agreements. We are countering with emergency provisions under the Digital Crisis Act, arguing that unstaffed sovereign infrastructure constitutes a systemic risk to Union continuity. The Commission’s Legal Service is reviewing, but time is short: if we do not staff the stack by turn 10, the next openweight leap could land inside hardened systems with no one watching. This is not about innovation — it is about occupancy. The servers are on; the lights are on; but the building is empty. We are hiring the guards before the vault is robbed.
