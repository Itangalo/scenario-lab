AI capability remains stagnant at 69.0 as public and private efforts falter. The EU’s Sovereign AI Core Programme and Resilience Reinforcement Initiative are operationally ineffective: data centres sit unstaffed, auditability fails, and patching mandates are unevenly adopted, exposing coordination gaps in cybersecurity response. Public trust erodes further amid media portrayals of systemic failure and the unresolved welfare scandal. A de facto directorate of France, Germany, the Netherlands, and Poland consolidates power through informal vetoes on foreign compute and shared red-teaming, weakening EU institutional cohesion.

The European Sovereign AI Corps is launched via emergency powers but struggles with legal and political barriers—civil service disputes, unresolved command arrangements, and tensions over worker mobility delay staffing. Only 80 junior roles are filled by Q3; leadership and operational capacity remain absent. Meanwhile, a widely disseminated open-weight model spreads rapidly and is weaponized against legacy systems, yet does not surpass closed-model capabilities, reflecting continued incremental progress without breakthrough. The EU lacks personnel to monitor or respond, leaving sovereign systems vulnerable and governance hollow.
