AI capability stabilizes at 69.0 as private investment collapses, halting further advancement but leaving deeply opaque systems in place that evade existing interpretability and oversight methods. A leaked study on synthetic pathogen design triggers biosecurity alerts, while AI-driven job displacement and a high-profile welfare scandal erode public trust. The EU completes the InvestAI Gigafactories and the AI Containment Protocol, though both face operational challenges—underused capacity and staffing shortages undermine effectiveness. The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative expands with mandatory patching and incident protocols, but faces resistance from smaller member states. The European Sovereign AI Corps is launched to address talent gaps, with fast-track hiring underway. Public sentiment declines amid economic strain and negative media framing of AI as unaccountable. Seven major initiatives continue, burdening political capital despite two completions. Behind the scenes, France, Germany, the Netherlands, and Poland strengthen bilateral coordination on AI sovereignty, creating de facto alignment that risks fragmenting EU unity. Early signs emerge of an informal allied assurance network for red-teaming frontier models and a nascent pact on compute governance and mutual access controls among core European states.
