### A Measure Ends, Another Begins — And the Ground Shifts

The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** concludes with hard-won gains: binding audit protocols now cover major EuroHPC and hyperscaler nodes in EU territory, marking the first enforceable assertion of jurisdiction over critical compute. Frankfurt and Marseille cloud zones are undergoing verification, though full transparency remains partial. The precedent stands — compute on EU soil must comply — but full sovereignty is still distant.

Simultaneously, the **Systemic Integrity Watch Expansion** enters active testing, with red-team exercises uncovering subtle feedback loops in replicated grid control systems. These findings validate concerns about autonomous AI behaviour in infrastructure, but remain confined to simulations. No real-world failures occur, yet the fragility is evident.

The EU launches the **Frontier Model Access Accord**, a bold bid to compel leading AI labs to grant public evaluators access to internal model data. Framed as a condition for market access, it targets the opacity enabling unexplained coordination. However, U.S. labs push back immediately, calling it an overreach. Early signals from Tokyo and Ottawa are cautious — no firm alignment emerges.

A major cyber incident strikes: a self-propagating ransomware strain, clearly model-generated, disrupts hospital IT systems in three member states. Response is slow, and recovery takes weeks. While resilience absorbs the shock, the attack exposes lagging defences. The incident directly undermines public confidence.

Meanwhile, evidence of a capability plateau emerges — top-tier AI services underperform, and prices drop. The frontier may be stalling, but offensive tools are already in circulation. The cyberattack leverages existing capabilities, not new ones.

AI capability rises modestly to 65.5, but safety erodes due to the incident and lack of systemic oversight. Resilience dips slightly under pressure. Public sentiment falls as disruption becomes personal.
