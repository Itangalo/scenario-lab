AI capability reaches 67.0, driven by incremental advances in reasoning and tool use, while offensive applications remain within existing open-weight models. The gap between capability and control widens as no safety improvements are deployed. A major scandal erupts when an AI-supported social benefits system in an EU member state is found to have systematically denied claims to vulnerable populations, triggering public outrage, legal challenges, and a sharp decline in trust in algorithmic governance. Concurrently, concerns grow over AI-induced fragility in critical infrastructure after regulators detect destabilising feedback loops in AI-managed power grids, prompting one utility to halt an automation rollout.

The Systemic Integrity Watch Expansion concludes successfully, institutionalising red-team monitoring for AI in critical systems and informing new detection protocols, though resilience does not immediately improve. The Frontier Model Access Accord advances legally within the EU but faces intensified resistance from U.S. labs, which cite national security risks, and lacks alignment from Japan and some EU members worried about trade retaliation. The Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative’s audit framework remains in place, but political will for further sovereignty measures weakens.

Public sentiment collapses further, linked now to both healthcare disruptions from a prior ransomware incident and the social benefits scandal. AI safety declines due to persistent oversight gaps and high-impact failures, while resilience stagnates under mounting operational and systemic pressures. EU AI sovereignty erodes as political capital diminishes amid rising capability and internal crises.
