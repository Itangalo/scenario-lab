### Scandal and Systemic Doubt

The fallout from the automated decision scandal dominates EU politics: an ombudsman report confirms that an AI-supported social benefits system in a major member state systematically denied claims to vulnerable populations due to flawed risk scoring. The ruling triggers national outrage and a wave of legal challenges, reinforcing public perception that AI governance is broken. Trust in algorithmic decision-making plummets.

Simultaneously, energy regulators intensify scrutiny of AI-driven grid systems after detecting feedback loops that could destabilise load balancing under stress. One utility halts an automation upgrade, citing precaution. These findings, though contained, amplify fears that AI is embedding fragility into core infrastructure.

The **Systemic Integrity Watch Expansion** concludes successfully, institutionalising red-team monitoring for AI in critical systems. Its insights feed into new detection protocols, marking a quiet but significant shift in operational doctrine. However, no immediate resilience boost follows — the work remains preparatory.

The **Frontier Model Access Accord** pushes forward, but resistance hardens. U.S. labs label the data access mandate as a national security risk, and Tokyo signals it will not mirror the EU’s stance. Behind closed doors, some EU member states express concern over retaliation in tech trade. The priority designation accelerates legal drafting, but no breakthrough in international alignment occurs.

AI capability advances to 67.0, driven by incremental gains in reasoning and tool integration, though offensive applications remain within existing openweight reach. No safety improvements land on deployed systems, and the gap between capability and control widens. Public sentiment collapses further as AI is now linked to both healthcare disruption and social injustice.

With no new compute sovereignty measure finishing and capability rising by 1.5, EU AI sovereignty declines. Political capital erodes under the weight of ongoing measures and the internal political cost of the scandal.
