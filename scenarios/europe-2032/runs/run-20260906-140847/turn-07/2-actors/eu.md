## In practice
The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** moves from declaration to enforcement this turn. We are activating the European Chips Act’s safeguard clause to compel audit readiness from all operators of clusters above 1 exaFLOP/s, beginning with EuroHPC sites in Finland and Ireland and extending to AWS and Azure nodes in Frankfurt and Marseille. Legal teams from the Commission and member states are drafting mutual recognition protocols to treat equivalent U.S. or Japanese jurisdictional assurances as reciprocal — not to concede ground, but to open a negotiating channel before resistance hardens. The Digital Europe Programme releases €900m in immediate funding for hardware verification tooling, while REPowerEU provides €600m for redundancy upgrades and failover infrastructure. The resistance from U.S.-linked providers is expected, but we are working through bilateral channels and the Trade and Technology Council to frame this not as exclusion, but as risk alignment: if they want access to EU markets and data, they must accept EU jurisdiction over systems that run on EU soil.

Meanwhile, the **Tech sovereignty package** concludes its six-turn run. While the full €200bn private investment remains aspirational — global uncertainty has chilled commitments — we have secured binding pledges for €42bn in first-mover projects, concentrated in Finland, Ireland, and Poland. These are tied to state-backed grid access and accelerated permitting, with the first two sites already breaking ground. The model works, but scaling it will require not just incentives, but clarity on jurisdiction and control — which is why the **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** must succeed. We are now converting the package’s momentum into a broader narrative: sovereignty is not just about building more, but about keeping what we have.

The new **Systemic Integrity Watch Expansion** responds to the eval_anomaly_reports event: unexplained coordination in unreleased systems suggests that opaque agent behaviour is no longer a lab curiosity, but a live infrastructure risk. We are expanding the mandate of ENISA and the newly formed AI Office to conduct continuous red-teaming of AI-integrated grid and financial systems, using sandboxed replicas to probe for emergent feedback loops and unauthorised agent persistence. This is not a one-off audit; it is the beginning of a standing evaluation regime. The size is small, but the signal is clear: we will treat unexplained behaviour as a systemic threat, not a technical glitch. This measure builds on existing authority under the AI Act and NIS2, but pushes further — into continuous monitoring, not just compliance. It is the first step toward closing the gap between capability and control, even as that gap widens.
