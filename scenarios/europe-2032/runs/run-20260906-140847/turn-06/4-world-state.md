### A Geopolitical Shock to the Core

The blockade around Taiwan triggers an immediate crisis in global semiconductor supply, freezing shipments of advanced chips and throwing AI development into uncertainty. With compute now a frontline strategic asset, the EU scrambles to secure its existing infrastructure against coercion or sabotage.

The **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** concludes this turn, delivering binding cyber mandates across energy, transport, and health sectors. Fallback systems are now operational in over 40% of Tier 1 operators, red-teaming has been institutionalised in major grids, and ENISA enforces compliance through new NIS2-derived thresholds. Resilience jumps to 51.0, reflecting full implementation (+6 under rule 4) of this category 6 measure.

The EU initiates the **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative**, a category 4 measure aimed at verifying and legally anchoring critical compute clusters under enforceable jurisdiction by turn 8. Funded via emergency clauses in Digital Europe and REPowerEU, it targets hyperscalers, EuroHPC nodes, and private AI labs. Legal anchoring begins in Finland and Ireland, but U.S.-linked cloud providers do not yet recognise the EU’s jurisdictional claims, citing undefined federal obligations. No bilateral agreement or leverage mechanism is in place to compel compliance, so progress remains limited to jurisdictions where national law aligns with EU enforcement. The initiative is formally proposed but faces early implementation delays due to unresolved cross-border authority questions.

Despite the high-stakes environment, no safety breakthrough occurs. While audits detect more feedback loops in grid systems, opaque coordination risks remain unaddressed in deployed agents. Ai_safety holds at 23.0. Openweight capability increases to 50.0 — accelerated replication and partial access to verification frameworks from closed labs allow modest gains, but growth remains bounded by underlying compute trends and the ongoing dependency on frontier models. The increase is consistent with gradual accumulation and stays below ai_capability, preserving the structural relationship required by Invariant 2.

Ai_capability advances by 1.5 to 62.5, within the expected range for post-60 growth. The U.S. retrenchment slows investment, but internal automation gains sustain progress.

Sovereignty sees a narrow gain: last turn’s −1 decay from capability growth is offset by early momentum on the new assurance initiative (+2 under rule 10). No capacity event removes access — the safeguard clause prevents loss — so no negative term applies.

Political capital improves to 45.0. The portfolio charge is −6 (Emergency Compute Assurance −3, Systemic Integrity Watch −2, priority −1). The completed Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint yields +5, and the new measure earns a proposal bonus of +4 — it directly answers the sudden supply shock. Public sentiment remains flat at 34.0; no visible public benefit emerges, but neither does a scandal.
