### Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now affirm that without sovereignty in execution, neither mandate can be fulfilled, and that without political capital, sovereignty itself is a fiction  
- Grounds: The metric `eu_political_capital` has reached 0.0 — a systemic zero, not a temporary dip — exposing that even binding infrastructure investments mean nothing if the Union cannot act collectively. This update reflects a categorical shift: we are no longer constrained by capability alone, but by legitimacy, cohesion, and the exhaustion of trust across institutions. The storm is not only technological; it is political, and we are out of runway.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, but now with binding investment in reducing the opacity that creates uncertainty — especially in AI behaviour, intent inference, and frontier access dependencies — and we will anchor those investments in physical and legal infrastructure that cannot be revoked by remote decision — but only where such investment does not deepen the capital deficit that makes all action impossible  
- Grounds: The failure to gain traction on the Sovereign Compute Energy Assurance Pact, despite its necessity, reveals that acting under uncertainty now requires not just courage but triage. We cannot build the floor beneath our feet if the floor itself collapses the house. This update binds our doctrine to survivability: we will still act early, but we must now weigh each action against its cost to the Union’s ability to remain a coherent actor at all.

- modify `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency — technical, infrastructural, or informational — will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity; this includes inference capacity, model weights, and the energy and spectrum required to run them — and we now extend this to include the political and financial architecture that funds and legitimizes them  
- Grounds: The bilateral deal struck by a member state with a U.S. provider, bypassing Union coordination, proves that sovereignty is not just about infrastructure but about alignment. A single point of political failure — a state acting alone under pressure — can unravel the whole. We must now treat fiscal and diplomatic cohesion as part of the stack.

## New measure  
**Sovereign Compute Energy Assurance Pact**  
Fast-track national and cross-border energy and spectrum guarantees for EU-controlled AI infrastructure, ensuring that sovereign clusters cannot be disabled by power or connectivity denial.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to eu_ai_sovereignty by binding energy and spectrum to sovereign compute as critical infrastructure; +3 to resilience by hardening continuity of operations against grid or telecom disruption; reduces vulnerability to hybrid coercion via infrastructure interdiction  
`Applies to:` EuroHPC sites, national crisis-response data centres, and designated sovereign AI operators across all member states

## Priority  
**Sovereign Compute Energy Assurance Pact** — because the Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative will fail if its clusters are left without guaranteed power and backhaul, and because recent member-state-level grid denials during heat emergencies have shown that even domestic infrastructure is not immune to cascading failure.

## In practice  
We are treating compute as critical infrastructure, not just another data centre. The Sovereign Compute Energy Assurance Pact launches under the Crisis Competence Protocol and the TEN-E framework, with the Commission designating sovereign AI clusters as “Tier 1 Resilience Assets” entitled to priority grid access, emergency microgrid deployment rights, and dedicated microwave and fibre backhaul corridors. We are negotiating with ENTSO-E and CEER to establish binding cross-border energy-sharing obligations during AI continuity events, and with BEREC to reserve spectrum for encrypted, low-latency links between redundancy sites. This is not a request — it is a reordering of national infrastructure priorities under Article 122 TFEU and the Digital Decade crisis provisions.

Resistance is strongest in two energy-stressed member states that fear blackouts will be blamed on AI over-provision. We are offering them co-location of their own sovereign nodes in Finnish and Bulgarian sites with guaranteed reciprocal access, and committing 15% of the new MFF infrastructure line to domestic grid hardening tied to participation. The Behavioural Observatory completes its integration this turn and transitions to operational status under the Joint Research Centre, now receiving Sentinel Node telemetry in near-real time. We are redirecting 30% of its coordination budget to joint incident simulations with ENISA and CERT-EU to stress-test response protocols. The world is moving faster than we can govern — so we are building the floor beneath our feet while we still have time.
