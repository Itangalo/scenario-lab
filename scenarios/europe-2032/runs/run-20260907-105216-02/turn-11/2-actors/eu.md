### Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now affirm that without sovereignty in execution, neither mandate can be fulfilled  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has proven that governance without sovereign infrastructure is performative. The two mandates cannot be balanced on paper when the systems that shape Europe’s future operate beyond its reach. This addition does not change our identity’s core — it sharpens it with the lesson of the last six months: capability without control is surrender.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, but now with binding investment in reducing the opacity that creates uncertainty — especially in AI behaviour, intent inference, and frontier access dependencies — and we will anchor those investments in physical and legal infrastructure that cannot be revoked by remote decision  
- Grounds: The repeated shocks — access denial, synthetic leaks, uncontrolled open-weight diffusion — have shown that procedural agility is not enough. We must hardwire resilience into the substrate: compute we control, power we command, and systems we can isolate. This update binds our early-action doctrine to material sovereignty, ensuring that future commitments are not undone by external withdrawal.

- modify `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency — technical, infrastructural, or informational — will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity; this includes inference capacity, model weights, and the energy and spectrum required to run them  
- Grounds: The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative has revealed that dependencies extend beyond software to the full stack: power feeds, cooling, backhaul, and physical access. We are expanding the scope of this position to reflect that sovereignty is not just about who owns the model, but who can keep it running when the world turns hostile.

## New measure  
**Sovereign Compute Energy Assurance Pact**  
Fast-track national and cross-border energy and spectrum guarantees for EU-controlled AI infrastructure, ensuring that sovereign clusters cannot be disabled by power or connectivity denial.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to eu_ai_sovereignty by binding energy and spectrum to sovereign compute as critical infrastructure; +3 to resilience by hardening continuity of operations against grid or telecom disruption; reduces vulnerability to hybrid coercion via infrastructure interdiction  
`Applies to:` EuroHPC sites, national crisis-response data centres, and designated sovereign AI operators across all member states

## Priority  
**Sovereign Compute Energy Assurance Pact** — because the Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative will fail if its clusters are left without guaranteed power and backhaul, and because recent member-state-level grid denials during heat emergencies have shown that even domestic infrastructure is not immune to cascading failure.

## In practice  
We are treating compute as critical infrastructure, not just another data centre. The Sovereign Compute Energy Assurance Pact launches under the Crisis Competence Protocol and the TEN-E framework, with the Commission designating sovereign AI clusters as “Tier 1 Resilience Assets” entitled to priority grid access, emergency microgrid deployment rights, and dedicated microwave and fibre backhaul corridors. We are negotiating with ENTSO-E and CEER to establish binding cross-border energy-sharing obligations during AI continuity events, and with BEREC to reserve spectrum for encrypted, low-latency links between redundancy sites. This is not a request — it is a reordering of national infrastructure priorities under Article 122 TFEU and the Digital Decade crisis provisions.

Resistance is strongest in two energy-stressed member states that fear blackouts will be blamed on AI over-provision. We are offering them co-location of their own sovereign nodes in Finnish and Bulgarian sites with guaranteed reciprocal access, and committing 15% of the new MFF infrastructure line to domestic grid hardening tied to participation. The Behavioural Observatory continues its integration work, now receiving Sentinel Node telemetry in near-real time, but we are redirecting 30% of its coordination budget to joint incident simulations with ENISA and CERT-EU to stress-test response protocols. The world is moving faster than we can govern — so we are building the floor beneath our feet while we still have time.
