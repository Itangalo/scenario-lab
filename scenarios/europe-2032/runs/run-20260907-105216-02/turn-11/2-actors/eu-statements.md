# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience
- `no_single_point_of_failure` (position): No critical AI dependency — technical, infrastructural, or informational — will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity; this includes inference capacity, model weights, and the energy and spectrum required to run them

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now affirm that without sovereignty in execution, neither mandate can be fulfilled
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has proven that governance without sovereign infrastructure is performative. The two mandates cannot be balanced on paper when the systems that shape Europe’s future operate beyond its reach. This addition does not change our identity’s core — it sharpens it with the lesson of the last six months: capability without control is surrender.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, but now with binding investment in reducing the opacity that creates uncertainty — especially in AI behaviour, intent inference, and frontier access dependencies — and we will anchor those investments in physical and legal infrastructure that cannot be revoked by remote decision
  - Grounds: The repeated shocks — access denial, synthetic leaks, uncontrolled open-weight diffusion — have shown that procedural agility is not enough. We must hardwire resilience into the substrate: compute we control, power we command, and systems we can isolate. This update binds our early-action doctrine to material sovereignty, ensuring that future commitments are not undone by external withdrawal.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `no_single_point_of_failure` — applied
  - Tier: position
  - Text: No critical AI dependency — technical, infrastructural, or informational — will be allowed to rest on a single foreign provider or unaccountable private entity; this includes inference capacity, model weights, and the energy and spectrum required to run them
  - Grounds: The Emergency Compute Redundancy Initiative has revealed that dependencies extend beyond software to the full stack: power feeds, cooling, backhaul, and physical access. We are expanding the scope of this position to reflect that sovereignty is not just about who owns the model, but who can keep it running when the world turns hostile.
