### The dark wards and the bright offices
The autumn brought two different Europes into the same news bulletin. In several municipalities and two hospital groups, systems went dark again as a largely automated ransomware sweep moved through a trusted administrative update. Records vanished, appointments collapsed to paper, ambulances diverted. Recovery teams cut networks and pulled locked backups off shelves, and where rehearsals had held, booking desks were back in days. Elsewhere staff rebuilt registries by hand for weeks. Investigators agreed the tooling was model-written. No one could name who ran it.

At the same time, office data told a calmer story. Law firms, accountants, local administrations and newsrooms reported measured output gains from assistants, largest among juniors, without fresh layoffs. Firms that had cut early were quietly hiring again. Work changed shape rather than disappearing.

Brussels pushed its triage cell hard. ENISA teams funded restores from existing envelopes, ran isolation drills in hit towns, and kept pooled allied feeds flowing. It stopped cascades but underlined dependence: detection was borrowed, spare parts were counted.

That dependence sharpened when Washington tightened licences again. Thresholds fell, paperwork doubled, European orders were delayed or downgraded. With domestic fabrication still fenced and idle, procurement officers stopped speaking of autonomy.

The bio warning landed more quietly. A genome model result suggesting a non-expert path to a human-infecting design circulated in biosecurity circles, contested and alarming in equal measure. HERA and the disease centre began upgrading sequencing, wastewater screening and hospital reporting in cooperating cities, pre-positioning diagnostics and paper-triage kits on the offline-kit model.

Civic repair inched forward. Health triage with workload limits and published logs kept cutting waits where mayors had signed, and the office gains gave quiet cover. But insurers still withheld blanket cover without statute, unions kept welfare automation frozen, and gratitude where help arrived sat beside anger at rationing everywhere else.
