Winter brought lab-escaped pathogen spread and Taiwan quarantine freezing chips, spiking accelerator prices. Brussels imposed emergency bio-surveillance, stockpile releases, compute/factory rationing, grid priority, and lithography controls.

Hardening kept large hospitals/grids online but strained small municipalities/utilities; autumn pathogen ebbed in cities while small towns saw failures like chlorination loss and insurer pullback. Brussels answered with manual-fallback playbooks, staffing pacts, EIB backstop, and Trust and Transition Pact for wage bridges/retraining — effective only where staff arrived. Gigafactories narrowed to two prioritized sites; Taiwan chips still frozen.

November US vote elected coalition-builder promising allied frontier access and joint evaluation; relief in Europe tempered by dependence fears, government not yet seated by December.

Spring: foreign lab leap to long-horizon, tool-using, self-coordinating agents shifted from research to product; Chinese humanoids/logistics robots with US software deployed in Rotterdam, Hamburg, Lyon ports/hospitals, improving rapidly, raising control/safety concerns. Brussels required kill-switches, override audits, continuity drills; large operators complied, small subcontractors lacked ownership of code/hardware. Coordination with incoming Washington yielded rhetoric, no hardware. Trust Pact payouts helped signed-on utilities/clinics; elsewhere seen as hollow. Two gigafactory sites advanced; permits and bio-monitoring kept systems upright, but public anxiety shifted to whose robots would take jobs.
