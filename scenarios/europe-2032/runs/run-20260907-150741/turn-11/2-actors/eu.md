## In practice
The Continuity Assurance Framework has formally concluded. Its central requirement — air-gapped fallback nodes for critical AI systems — was adopted in principle by 14 member states, but only six have completed certification. In Thessaloniki, the unannounced failover drill failed: the backup node lacked updated routing tables, and local operators had not been trained. The framework’s authority was procedural; it did not generate belief. Without trust, continuity is a fiction. We are not abandoning the idea — we are changing how we deliver it.

The Frontier Access Guarantee remains in flight, but its foundation is cracking. Washington’s export_control_escalation review has not yet cut access, but it has introduced delay and uncertainty. Our partners in Tokyo and Toronto now require pre-approval for any software update involving model weights, citing compliance risk. This is not denial — it is attrition. We are still receiving compute, but not on the terms we bargained for. The middle_power_coalition is now our only leverage: we are pushing for joint evaluation protocols and mutual recognition of safety standards at the upcoming OECD Digital Ministers meeting, to reduce our dependence on unilateral U.S. goodwill.

So we are acting where we can still move: in public services, with systems we control. The Sovereign AI Pilot Network will scale the Marseille, Ljubljana, and Athens pilots into a cross-border network, adding deployments in Hamburg (port logistics), Dublin (cyber incident triage), and Bucharest (public health surveillance). Each node will run the same hardened SACP stack (capability 62.0), monitored by the EAAD and audited quarterly by a new joint EU-national review panel. Performance data — uptime, incident response time, public satisfaction — will be published in real time on a public dashboard. We are not hiding failures. We are showing how we recover.

We are funding this through a €2.1bn reallocation from the stalled AI4PublicGood programme and additional contributions from participating member states, matched 1:1 by the Digital Europe Programme. The strike by public-sector AI staff is being addressed through a new consultation protocol: weekly technical councils with EAAD, national agencies, and union reps to co-develop safety procedures. We are not forcing deployment — we are inviting partnership. The lights went out. Now we are building a grid that doesn’t fail when one city burns.
