## In practice
The Frontier Access Guarantee is limping toward completion. We have secured SLAs with partners in Canada and Japan, but compliance overhead has turned what was meant to be seamless access into a bottleneck. Every model update now requires weeks of legal review. The middle_power_coalition has helped — we’ve locked in mutual recognition of safety evaluations at the OECD — but it does not override U.S. export controls. We are still dependent, and dependency is failing us. As capability climbs to 75.0 with safety at 5.0, the risk is not just disruption — it is that we will be locked out of the very tools needed to respond.

So we are acting on the ground. The Secure Core Compute Buildout will repurpose three state-owned industrial zones — in Oulu, Bilbao, and Wrocław — into AI gigafactories with dedicated nuclear and renewable microgrids. We are invoking Article 122 TFEU for emergency infrastructure to bypass national permitting delays, backed by €9.8bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and matched private investment. The European Chips Act will be amended to include AI-specific fabrication lines, and we are offering tax-free zones and fast-track residency to frontier engineers willing to relocate. National resistance is expected — especially in Finland, where local councils oppose the grid draw — so we are pairing the buildout with a Just Compute Transition Fund: 15% of each site’s output reserved for regional public services, and 10% of jobs guaranteed for local hires.

The Sovereign AI Pilot Network continues in flight, but its credibility depends on what runs beneath it. We are upgrading its stack to leverage early capacity from the Bilbao site, even if provisional. Trust must be earned, but it cannot be built on borrowed power. We are no longer patching the grid — we are laying new lines, deep in the bedrock, so that when the next storm hits, the lights stay on.
