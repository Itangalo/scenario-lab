## New measure
**Measure:** Emergency Sovereign Stack Integration  
Integrate early sovereign compute capacity from the Bilbao site into a minimal viable sovereign AI stack—training, fine-tuning, and inference—for critical public services, using open-weight models hardened through EU-led red-teaming and secured deployment protocols.

## Priority
**Priority:** Emergency Sovereign Stack Integration  
We are prioritizing this over further expansion of foreign access or new resilience directives because the Frontier Access Guarantee is now functionally degraded—bottlenecks in legal compliance and export controls mean we cannot rely on third-country systems during a crisis—and because the Secure Core Compute Buildout, while essential, will not deliver physical capacity until turn 15; we must use the first usable output from Bilbao now to prove sovereign continuity is possible, or lose credibility entirely.

## In practice
The Frontier Access Guarantee is reaching its final turn, but it lands not as a triumph but as a cautionary tale: access, when mediated through foreign legal and export regimes, is not control. We are finalizing the last SLAs with Canadian and Japanese partners, but we are already shifting focus. The legal teams at DG TRADE and DG CONNECT are drafting a sunset clause—by turn 13, all non-emergency frontier access routed through third countries will require revalidation against a new EU operational resilience standard. This is not a termination, but a recalibration: we will no longer treat external access as sovereign capacity.

Meanwhile, the Secure Core Compute Buildout stumbles forward. In Finland, the Oulu site faces a constitutional challenge over grid allocation under Article 122 TFEU. We are negotiating with the Finnish government to co-fund local grid upgrades in exchange for permitting acceleration, using funds from the Just Compute Transition Fund. In Spain, the Bilbao site—though delayed—is now producing early power and cooling capacity. This changes everything. We are instructing EuroHPC JU to divert 10% of Bilbao’s initial operational yield to a sovereign inference cluster, to be governed by EDIC and secured under the AI Act’s high-risk provisions. This cluster will run a hardened version of the best available open-weight model—currently at 57.0 capability—subjected to red-teaming by the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) and the newly established AI Safety Lab in Seville.

We are not waiting for perfection. The Emergency Sovereign Stack Integration will deploy this capability to three pilot sectors: cross-border civil protection coordination, automated legal aid in under-resourced jurisdictions, and AI-assisted diagnostics in EU reference laboratories for emerging biothreats. These are not luxury applications—they are stress tests of continuity. If the stack fails under real load, we will know before a crisis forces the question. And if it holds, we will have something no foreign provider can take away: a chain of trust from silicon to service, under EU law, answerable to EU institutions. That is the foundation of sovereignty—not just compute, but control.
