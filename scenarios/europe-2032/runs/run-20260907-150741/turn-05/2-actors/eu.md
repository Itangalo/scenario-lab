## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty.
  - Grounds: The convergence of *capability_jump*, *cyber_major_incident*, and *election_consolidation* reveals that raw access to frontier models is meaningless if those models are opaque, insecure, or subject to foreign veto. The Union’s survival as a decision-making entity depends not on matching capability alone, but on ensuring that any system we rely on cannot be turned against us — whether by design, compromise, or coercion.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability.
  - Grounds: The *bio_uplift_findings* and the unresolved attribution of the cyberattack prove that acting fast without sufficient safeguards risks enabling the very harms we are mandated to prevent. We remain committed to decisive action, but redefine the cost of delay: it is no longer only strategic irrelevance, but also catastrophic failure. Henceforward, acting under uncertainty means building brakes, not just engines.

## New measure
**Measure:** Enact the AI Containment Protocol  
A binding directive requiring all sovereign AI systems above 55.0 capability to operate under air-gapped, multi-party control with real-time anomaly detection and human-in-the-loop escalation.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 15–20 points for sovereign systems; reduce the risk of catastrophic misuse from both internal failure and external compromise; establish a precedent for enforceable containment thresholds  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, SACP participants, and any member-state entity operating a model above 55.0 capability on EU soil

## Priority
**Priority:** Establish the Sovereign Model Assurance Framework (SMAF)  
We are pushing SMAF hardest this turn because the *election_consolidation* event confirms that foreign access to frontier models will be conditional and revocable, making sovereign systems not just desirable but existential — and SMAF ensures those systems are not just powerful, but trustworthy and defensible against both internal failure and external coercion.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the hinge between capability and control. The *bio_uplift_findings* have set off alarm bells in the College: a model capable of designing a viable human-infective organism, even if contested, cannot be dismissed as theoretical. Combined with the *election_consolidation* in the US, which now treats frontier AI as a national strategic asset to be rationed, we face a new reality: the world’s most capable systems are not just inaccessible — they are weaponized by design, and their knowledge could leak through open channels or be repurposed by bad actors. Our response must shift from catching up to locking down.

SMAF is now the linchpin of our entire sovereignty strategy. The EAAD, which finishes its establishment this turn, is being staffed with emergency hiring powers under Article 336 TFEU, and we are fast-tracking security clearances for its core audit teams. The Commission is issuing delegated acts to ensure SMAF applies retroactively to all SACP development, including pre-training architecture reviews. We are negotiating with France and Germany to host the first air-gapped sovereign cluster under SMAF, with physical access limited to dual-key control between national and EU authorities. At the same time, the InvestAI Gigafactories are moving at pace: Sweden has accepted the Article 122 override, and construction on the first exaflop site begins next month. The solidarity payments to Brittany and Norrland have bought temporary peace, but we are preparing for legal challenges from Finland and Austria over grid allocation — we will meet them with binding energy solidarity clauses under the revised European Semester.

CIRS finishes this turn, but its legacy is mixed: only 40% of targeted systems have completed mandatory upgrades, and red-teaming exposed critical gaps in patch deployment velocity. We are converting CIRS into a standing programme under ENISA, with a new mandate to monitor AI-driven cyber threats in real time. Meanwhile, public sentiment remains at 25.0 — dangerously low. We are launching a targeted communication campaign through the Digital Europe Programme, focusing on how SMAF and the Containment Protocol ensure that AI built in Europe serves Europeans, not foreign powers or rogue systems. The narrative must shift: we are not just building AI, we are building a firewall around it.
