The EU, facing institutional fragility and public disengagement, launches the Emergency Labour Absorption and Civic Stabilisation Programme to stabilize collapsing sentiment, repurposing cohesion and social funds for wage insurance, retraining, and 10,000 civic oversight roles. Implementation falters due to member state resistance, delayed commitments, and lack of political capital, with the measure narrowly passing after threats from Germany and Poland. AI capability rises to 70.5 through foreign training cycles, but safety remains unimproved, widening the governance gap despite no major incidents. Grassroots cyber pooling grows informally but lacks coordination. The EU attempts to reconnect governance with public experience by involving displaced professionals in oversight, yet sentiment stays at 15.0, protests persist, and no gains in resilience (37.0) or sovereignty (9.0) emerge, underscoring the Union’s weakened authority and fragile cohesion.
