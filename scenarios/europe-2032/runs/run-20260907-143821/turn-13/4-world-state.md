### Fractured Foundations

The EU doubles down on survival, launching the Emergency Labour Absorption and Civic Stabilisation Programme as its sole priority. Framed as triage, the initiative aims to counter collapsing public sentiment by repurposing cohesion funds and the European Social Fund to provide wage insurance, retraining, and 10,000 new civic roles in AI oversight. Yet the effort begins under near-zero political capital and fractured legitimacy. Member states demand excessive flexibility, and several delay implementation, citing fiscal strain and lack of clear EU leadership.

### Silent Systems, Rising Tensions

AI capability inches forward to 70.5, driven by unrelenting training cycles abroad. No safety improvements land: opaque reasoning persists, and no audit regime has teeth. The gap between capability and safety widens, but no incident occurs—resilience, though low, prevents cascading failure. The emergent grassroots cyber pooling continues informally, with national CERTs exchanging defensive models, but without EU coordination, its impact remains marginal.

### A Bid for Reconnection

The new programme is the EU’s clearest attempt to re-anchor AI governance in public experience. By embedding displaced professionals in redress panels, it seeks to transform resentment into participation. Yet the narrative struggle is immense. Public sentiment remains at 15.0, with protests still targeting data centres and AI-driven public services. The programme is too new to shift perception, and its scale is questioned from the start.

### Institutional Freefall

With eu_political_capital at 0.0, the EU operates at the edge of institutional collapse. The priority mechanism is nullified—no pull-in effect, no cost, no benefit. The measure starts, but only barely, surviving a narrow vote in the Council after Germany and Poland threaten to withhold assent. No immediate boost to resilience or sovereignty follows. The Union acts, but barely holds.
