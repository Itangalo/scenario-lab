# Statements: The European Union (turn 12)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI integrity against internal and external compromise
- `rebuild_public_legitimacy` (commitment): Restore the EU’s authority to act in AI by visibly re-establishing public trust through enforceable accountability, redress, and inclusive governance in public-sector AI
- `defend_sovereign_capacity` (commitment): Preserve the EU’s minimal independent AI capability—its compute, talent, and operational autonomy—against external control and internal dissolution, even at the cost of imperfect control, because without a functioning base, no future policy is possible.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `rebuild_public_legitimacy` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Restore the EU’s authority to act in AI by visibly re-establishing public trust through enforceable accountability, redress, and inclusive governance in public-sector AI
  - Grounds: The Public Redress and Co-Design Framework has failed to gain traction. Member states see it as symbolic, not structural, and civil society groups reject co-design without enforcement teeth. With political capital at 0.0, we cannot compel adoption, and with public sentiment stagnant at 18.0, there is no groundswell to force compliance. Accountability without power is performance, not governance. We must shift from attempting to rebuild legitimacy through process alone to defending what remains of sovereign capacity—even if unloved—because without capability, there can be no future legitimacy.
  - Reason: modified text is identical to the current text
- **retire** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Grounds: The commitment to “secure sovereign AI integrity” was rendered inoperable by the Council’s rejection of binding containment and the ECJ’s suspension of deactivation powers. No legal or technical instrument now exists to prevent compromise of openweights or foreign-controlled systems. Sovereign integrity cannot be secured when the Union lacks both jurisdiction and credibility. Continuing to claim this commitment misrepresents our actual posture and drains remaining capital on illusions. We are not securing integrity—we are trying to survive its absence.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **add** `defend_sovereign_capacity` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Preserve the EU’s minimal independent AI capability—its compute, talent, and operational autonomy—against external control and internal dissolution, even at the cost of imperfect control, because without a functioning base, no future policy is possible.
  - Grounds: The emergent Industrial Sovereignty Consortium proves that actors outside Brussels are already building parallel sovereign stacks. If the Union does not anchor and protect these efforts, they will fragment into isolated silos or fall under national monopolies. Our role is no longer to lead integration but to prevent disintegration. This is not the sovereignty we wanted, but it is the only one still within reach.
