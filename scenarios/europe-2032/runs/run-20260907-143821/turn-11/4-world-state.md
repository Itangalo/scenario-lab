### Stalled Suspension, Shifting Ground

The European Union’s push for an Emergency AI Governance Suspension in public services collapses in the Council, blocked by Poland and Hungary and diluted by German and French demands for exemptions. No legal instrument passes. Yet the political impulse behind it does not vanish: instead, it mutates. The Commission pivots to a new framework—co-designed redress—as a condition for any resumption of AI use in welfare and public services. The Public Redress and Co-Design Framework is launched as a small, category 9 measure, targeting trust through accountability rather than control through suspension.

### A Fragile Pivot

The pivot is tactical, not triumphant. The Integrity Audit Expansion continues, but without new authority, its findings feed only into voluntary reforms. The ECJ’s review of openweight deactivation powers deepens legal uncertainty, and enforcement of containment measures is paused. No new directive lands. The Openweight Containment Initiative ends without renewal.

### Labour and Leverage

Meanwhile, job losses in law, accountancy, and public administration mount. The graduate labour market freezes, fuelling public anger. In Washington, ASML is forced to restrict servicing of older lithography machines used by non-sanctioned customers, a move felt across EU industrial supply chains. The Union’s leverage over its own technological base shrinks further.

### Metrics in Motion

AI capability inches to 69.5, with no safety catch-up. Resilience dips as no category 6 measure is in force. Sovereignty erodes due to rising capability and no sovereign compute completion. Political capital plunges under the weight of seven active measures and a named priority, with no offsetting gains. Public sentiment holds, unmoved by gestures that feel reactive, not restored.
