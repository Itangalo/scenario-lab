The Emergency AI Governance Suspension fails in the Council due to opposition from Poland and Hungary and compromises demanded by Germany and France, resulting in no binding legislation. The Commission shifts course, launching the Public Redress and Co-Design Framework—a minor, symbolic measure—aimed at rebuilding trust through accountability rather than control. The pivot yields no immediate gains: the Integrity Audit Expansion continues without enforcement power, the ECJ’s review stalls containment efforts, and the Openweight Containment Initiative lapses. Job losses grow in professional sectors, deepening public discontent, while external tech restrictions from Washington strain EU industrial capacity. AI capability rises to 69.5, outpacing safety efforts, as sovereignty and resilience decline amid stalled governance, eroding political capital and public confidence. No significant reform takes hold; the bloc drifts without strategic coherence.
