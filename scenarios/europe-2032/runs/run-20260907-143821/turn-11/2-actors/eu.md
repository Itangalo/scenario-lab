## Statement changes

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI integrity against internal and external compromise  
- Grounds: The political foundation for sovereign AI has collapsed: public sentiment is at 18.0, member states are fracturing on enforcement, and the ECJ has suspended key containment powers. Integrity cannot be secured through technical control alone when the legitimacy to govern AI use has been lost. Continuing to frame the commitment this way would bind us to a failing strategy without addressing the root cause—public consent. We must pivot from asserting control to rebuilding permission.

- add `rebuild_public_legitimacy` (commitment): Restore the EU’s authority to act in AI by visibly re-establishing public trust through enforceable accountability, redress, and inclusive governance in public-sector AI  
- Grounds: The automated decision scandal has transformed AI into a symbol of systemic injustice. Without a credible path back to public buy-in, no technical or industrial measure will survive political backlash. This turn, we act to halt the erosion of legitimacy—not by building capacity, but by proving we can govern it accountably. The Emergency AI Governance Suspension is a first step, but it must be followed by binding redress and co-design with affected communities.

## New measure

**Measure:** Launch the Public Redress and Co-Design Framework  
A binding requirement for all public-sector AI systems above 50.0 capability to implement legally enforceable redress mechanisms and to co-design audit and oversight procedures with civil society representatives and affected communities.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `public_sentiment` by at least 10 points by turn 14 through visible accountability and inclusion; strengthen legitimacy of sovereign AI by grounding it in participatory governance rather than top-down assurance  
`Applies to:` All EU member states deploying AI in public services, particularly welfare, employment, and social benefits

## Priority

**Priority:** Launch the Public Redress and Co-Design Framework  
We are pushing this hardest this turn because trust cannot be restored by suspension alone—without a credible, visible path to justice and inclusion, the Emergency AI Governance Suspension will be seen as another delay tactic, not a turning point.

## In practice

This turn, we are governing from the ruins of assumed legitimacy. The automated decision scandal has left us with no choice but to rebuild from the ground up. We are advancing the Emergency AI Governance Suspension under Article 114 TFEU, but we now know it will not pass in its current form. Germany and France demand carve-outs; Poland and Hungary reject supranational interference. So we are shifting strategy: instead of demanding a uniform pause, we are offering a new framework—co-designed redress—as the condition for resuming any public AI use. The Integrity Audit Expansion continues, but its findings will now feed not just into technical corrections, but into binding redress pathways: every audited system must publish how individuals can appeal, challenge, and be compensated for harm. We are tasking the EAAD, in coordination with national data protection authorities and civil society networks, to draft model redress protocols by turn 12, to be piloted in three member states by turn 13. Funding will come from the Social Cohesion Instrument, repurposed under emergency provisions. The Openweight Containment Initiative has ended, and with the ECJ review pending, we are not enforcing deactivation clauses—instead, we are redirecting that energy into proving we can govern our own systems before claiming authority over others. This is not the sovereignty we wanted, but it is the one we can still earn.
