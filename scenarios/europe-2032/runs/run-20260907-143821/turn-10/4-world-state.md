### A Fractured Response

The European Union attempts a desperate course correction, proposing an Emergency AI Governance Suspension in public services to halt the erosion of public trust after the welfare scandal. The measure, though small and time-bound, is framed as essential triage: a pause to allow audits and redress mechanisms to catch up with deployment. Yet even this modest step fractures along familiar lines.

Germany and France back the suspension in principle but demand exemptions for already-audited systems, arguing that blanket halts penalise responsible adopters. Poland and Hungary resist outright, citing national sovereignty over social policy and warning of administrative collapse without automation. The Council’s Justice configuration stalls, unable to reach qualified majority. Only limited technical coordination proceeds—funding for triage teams is pledged, but no legal mechanism to enforce suspension emerges.

Meanwhile, the world moves on. Top-tier AI capability inches forward to 69.0, driven by private actors outside EU jurisdiction. The plateau once suspected proves fleeting; underdelivering releases are dismissed as noise, not signal. More alarmingly, developers confirm that internal reasoning in leading models no longer surfaces in human-readable form. Audit protocols across the bloc, including those under the now-completed EAAD and SMAF, suddenly lose their primary inspection method. Assurance evaporates where it was most trusted.

All major initiatives conclude this turn—Gigafactories, CIRS, SACP, SMAF, Public Assurance Protocol, Openweight Containment—but their completion brings no political reward. The mood is not closure, but exhaustion. The belief that sovereign infrastructure guarantees safety lies in ruins. With multiple large measures ending and no visible gain, political capital erodes further.

### Trust, Unrestored

The Integrity Audit Expansion continues, but slowly, and without authority. The ECJ’s review of openweight deactivation powers deepens uncertainty. No new containment tools are deployed. Resilience holds, but only because no major incident strikes—vulnerability remains high.

Public sentiment stays frozen at 18.0. Protests persist. The suspension proposal, though announced, fails to land as a decisive act. It is seen not as leadership, but as hesitation too late.
