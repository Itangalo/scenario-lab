## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that the cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across municipal services in Belgium, Italy, and Estonia, leveraging Kimi K3-derived polymorphic payloads — has shattered the illusion that adaptive AI threats are still on the horizon. The attack disrupted emergency dispatch, school meal logistics, and water billing systems for days, and defenders were blind to its coordination until it was too late. The incident was not catastrophic, but it was *visible*, and it landed precisely where our weakest defences met the most diffuse exposure: local government IT, often outsourced, rarely red-teamed, and deeply integrated into daily life. Public sentiment is holding at 42.0, but the campaign_backlash is gaining ground — and this time, it is not fringe parties alone demanding moratoriums, but mayors, union leaders, and even some tech CEOs warning that unsecured adoption is now a liability.

With the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive now finished, we are converting its legal framework into operational reality: the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) will begin registering eligible compute facilities in Q1 2028, with the first 30% reservation triggers activated upon confirmation of strategic workload eligibility. However, the ASML coercion has left us exposed — we cannot build sovereign compute if the tools to make the chips are hostage to third-party leverage. We are therefore redirecting part of the Tech Sovereignty Package’s permitting acceleration to include domestic lithography maintenance hubs and dual-use repair capacity, to be co-funded by the European Defence Fund under the Net-Zero Industry Act’s strategic autonomy clause. This is not a full fix, but it is a hedge: we are building the capacity to keep existing tools alive, even if new ones are blocked.

Meanwhile, the Critical Infrastructure AI Shield is now legally binding under NIS2 and the Cyber Resilience Act. We are deploying €15bn in immediate disbursements to Germany, France, and the Benelux countries to fast-track AI-driven intrusion detection systems, with a requirement that all models used for monitoring are themselves red-teamed quarterly using adversarial agents at or above Kimi K3 capability. For Hungary and Slovakia, we are offering a grace period tied to a technical assistance package from ENISA and the newly formed Joint Cyber Unit, but non-compliance will trigger infringement procedures. The new European AI Incident Response Corps — proposed this turn — will be staffed by seconded experts from national CERTs, Europol’s EC3, and academic AI safety labs, and will conduct its first live exercise during the Shield’s second red-team cycle in Q2 2028. This is not about catching up — it is about ensuring we never fall behind in the first place.
