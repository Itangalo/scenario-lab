## New measure  
**Critical Infrastructure AI Shield**  
A rapid deployment programme to harden energy, water, and transport control systems against AI-enabled intrusion, using real-time anomaly detection, air-gapped fail-safes, and mandatory red-teaming.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to +18 to resilience by turn 5; begins closing the gap exposed by the grid intrusion; reduces systemic fragility to offensive cyber agents now embedded in open models  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, with co-funding and enforcement via the NIS2 framework and the European Cyber Resilience Act

## Priority  
**Critical Infrastructure AI Shield** — because the grid intrusion and the rise of offensive AI agents in open models prove that resilience is no longer a background condition but an urgent front-line defence, and without it, sovereignty collapses the moment it is tested.

## In practice  
We are acting on the understanding that compute sovereignty without systemic resilience is a castle built on sand. This turn, the Commission will launch the Critical Infrastructure AI Shield under the legal authority of the NIS2 Directive and the Cyber Resilience Act, mobilising €45bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme. The funding will be allocated to member states on a matching basis — 60% EU, 40% national — for the deployment of AI-powered intrusion detection systems trained to recognise novel, adaptive attack patterns, particularly those consistent with the stealthy coordination seen in the recent grid incident. Each critical operator in energy, water, rail, and air traffic management must implement air-gapped emergency protocols and undergo biannual red-team exercises using AI adversaries modelled on Kimi K3-level capabilities.

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive moves into its final negotiation phase this turn. We are engaging the Council’s Legal Affairs and Internal Market formations to finalise the text under Article 114 TFEU, while offering a revised compliance pathway: operators who pre-commit capacity to the EDIC allocation system will gain fast-track access to the Tech Sovereignty Package’s AI Acceleration Zones and liability protection for any performance degradation caused by reserved workloads. The US cloud providers remain hostile, but the campaign_atlanticist gives us leverage — we are quietly circulating a joint EU-US resilience task force proposal to frame the directive not as exclusion, but as burden-sharing.

Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package advances with five member states now signed on to AI Acceleration Zones, including Spain, Poland, and Finland. We are streamlining the permitting model and linking it directly to grid investment under the Net-Zero Industry Act. But we are clear-eyed: without the Shield, every data centre we build could become a vector, not a victory. This turn, resilience is not secondary to sovereignty — it is its price.
