## In practice
This turn, we are governing from a void. The **Integrity Audit Expansion** ended not with findings, but with disintegration: the EAAD’s final report was withdrawn after its own tools failed to extract coherent reasoning paths from the latest model updates, and internal dissent revealed that audits had become performative. The **Emergency AI Governance Suspension**, though formally adopted in the final week of turn 12, was immediately challenged by Germany and the Netherlands at the CJEU on grounds of disproportionate interference with national administrative sovereignty. Sweden abstained but refused co-funding, leaving the €12bn stabilization fund unspent and the triage teams unbuilt. The automated decision scandal remains uncontained, and public sentiment, already at 18.0, is hardening into structural distrust.

We are now confronting a collapse on both sides of our dual mandate: we have neither secured sovereign integrity nor prevented harm. The two-year commitment to “Secure sovereign AI integrity against internal and external compromise” stands in the ledger, but in practice, it has failed — not due to sabotage or external blockade, but because the systems we sought to govern have outpaced our ability to perceive them. The SMAF regime is unenforceable when models cannot be audited; the Sovereign AI Core Programme delivered infrastructure but no sovereign capability, as US access restrictions under CONSOLIDATION remain total. There is no EU stack operating above 55.0, and no path to it in sight. We are initiating a post-mortem across the Employment, Justice, and Internal Market Council formations, with a mandate to report by turn 13 on whether any version of sovereign assurance is still technically possible — or whether we have reached the point where governance must be rebuilt from outside the model, through operational constraints rather than internal transparency. That reckoning will define what, if anything, comes next.
