### Cracks in the Foundation

The EU closes two legacy initiatives on schedule: the *Algorithmic Sovereignty Initiative* delivers its sparse model prototypes, now in simulation trials for grid stability and synthetic media detection, while the *Emergency Democratic Integrity Protocol* shows mixed success — fusion cells flag disinformation, but Hungary’s reliance on a U.S. API reveals a dangerous divergence in enforcement. Public trust wavers further as a Belgian court rules that an AI-supported social insurance system systematically denied claims due to biased scoring, branding it “automation without recourse.” The *automated_decision_scandal* triggers widespread outrage, eroding confidence in AI governance.

Simultaneously, unexplained benchmark anomalies from a frontier model — capability emerging in untrained domains, performance far exceeding projections — suggest systems are evolving beyond their training. Laboratories dismiss the results as measurement artefacts, but regulators are not convinced.

In response, the EU doubles down on control. The *Sovereign Evaluation Regime* advances, with technical standards drafted and €680 million secured for evaluation infrastructure. Third-party red-teaming units begin accreditation under ENISA, and France and Germany commit personnel. However, U.S. labs signal legal challenges, and staffing lags due to competition for talent.

Even more urgently, the EU launches the *Resilience Acceleration Programme*, a large category 6 measure targeting cyber hardening in energy, healthcare, and justice. €1.2 billion is mobilised under the emergency clause, mandating AI-resilient designations and biannual incident exercises. Poland joins after securing a new evaluation node in Warsaw.

Yet cohesion frays. A quiet coalition of mid-sized member states resists the evaluation regime’s compliance burden, pushing for flexible implementation. The *emergent_regulatory_backlash* gains momentum, threatening uniform enforcement. Meanwhile, an open-source German model achieving 58.0 capability on public datasets — the *emergent_open_source_breakthrough* — spreads rapidly, bypassing central oversight.

Public sentiment dips as job losses in law, accountancy, and administration mount. The scandal and displacement together deepen scepticism, despite healthcare efficiency gains.
