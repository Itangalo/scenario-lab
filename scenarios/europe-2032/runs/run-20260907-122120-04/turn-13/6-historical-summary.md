A German court exposed systemic failures in Bavaria’s AI-driven social insurance system, which had quietly denied claims to vulnerable citizens without accountability, triggering a collapse in public trust and reframing AI as a vector of internal institutional decay. In response, the EU launched the Public Algorithmic Accountability Directive to mandate transparency and appeals in public-sector AI, though immediate resistance emerged from France and Hungary. While the Sovereign Evaluation Regime was formally completed, accrediting independent evaluators and initiating red-team testing, its high threshold left lower-risk systems like Bavaria’s unregulated. The Resilience Acceleration Programme advanced slowly, and the Emergency Response Modernisation Initiative stalled due to member state opposition to data sharing. A voluntary pause on a widely adopted open-source energy grid tool highlighted risks of uncoordinated sovereign innovation. Public sentiment declined sharply amid perceptions of institutional betrayal, and despite progress on regulatory frameworks, EU strategic sovereignty weakened further as dependence on external compute deepened and confidence eroded, with no gains in sovereign infrastructure.
