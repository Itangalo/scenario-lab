### The Scandal Within

A German court delivered a damning verdict: an AI-supported social insurance system in Bavaria had systematically denied claims to vulnerable citizens for over a year, with no effective appeal. The system, built from a repurposed mid-tier model and deemed low-risk, operated in opacity — its logic unexplained, its errors uncorrected. The ruling exposed not a cyberattack, but a quiet erosion of democratic accountability. Public trust, already strained, collapsed further.

The EU completed the *Sovereign Evaluation Regime*, a landmark in principle — independent evaluators are now accredited, and red-team testing has begun. But its mandate begins at 65.0 capability, leaving the Bavarian system untouched. The regime guards the frontier, not the foundation.

In response, the Commission launched the *Public Algorithmic Accountability Directive*, a large category 2 measure requiring transparency, impact assessments, and appeals for all public-sector AI. It is a direct answer to the scandal, aiming to restore legitimacy. France immediately demanded exemptions for national security systems; Hungary questioned its proportionality. Negotiations begin under pressure.

Meanwhile, the *Resilience Acceleration Programme* continued, but the *Emergency Response Modernisation Initiative* stalled as member states balked at data-sharing protocols. A voluntary pause in three countries on an open-source grid tool — praised as sovereign innovation — revealed that distributed adoption without assurance risks systemic instability.

Public sentiment plunged as media framed the scandal as institutional betrayal. The narrative shifted: AI is not just a tool of foreign adversaries, but a vector of internal decay.

### Sovereignty’s Slow Erosion

Despite finishing a major measure, the EU’s strategic position weakened. Capability advanced, but no sovereign compute gains offset the growing gap. Dependence deepened, confidence frayed.
