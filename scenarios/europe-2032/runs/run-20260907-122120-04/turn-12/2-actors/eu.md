## Priority  
**Resilience Acceleration Programme** — because the cyber_major_incident this turn exposed not just vulnerabilities in critical systems, but in our collective capacity to respond, and because Medica Europa’s emergence proves that public alternatives are possible only if we can protect them from collapse under attack.

## In practice  
This turn, the ground shifted beneath us. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that encrypted hospital scheduling systems in three member states and disrupted emergency dispatch — landed with speed and precision no human team could match. Attribution remains unclear, but the tooling was adaptive, polymorphic, and clearly AI-synthesised. The attack did not seek ransom; it sought paralysis. We were slow to coordinate, slower to communicate, and unprepared for the psychological toll. Resilience, we now see, is not just redundancy — it is speed, clarity, and trust.

We are advancing the Sovereign Evaluation Regime despite legal threats from U.S. labs and staffing shortages. The European AI Office, under DG CNECT, has finalised the first red-teaming protocols focused on agentic persistence and stealthy data exfiltration. ENISA is accrediting third-party evaluators, with France and Germany contributing technical teams. But we cannot evaluate what is already loose — and the emergent_open_source_breakthrough, a German open model now at 58.0, is spreading faster than any enforcement can follow. It is not weaponised — yet — but it is unsecured, and it is everywhere.

Meanwhile, Medica Europa — the emergent_medical_data_pool — offers a glimpse of what we could be: a public, sovereign, cooperative AI effort, built for care, not control. But it is vulnerable. Without protection, it will be co-opted, compromised, or crushed by the next incident. So we act. The Resilience Acceleration Programme moves forward: all member states must designate AI-resilient critical services by turn 13, with hardened detection and redundancy plans. Poland has joined, secured by the promise of a new evaluation node. And now we launch the Emergency Response Modernisation Initiative — not as a replacement, but as its nervous system. Funded from the same emergency clause, it will integrate automated triage into civil protection agencies, establish a standing EU-wide incident response cell, and mandate real-time public updates during crises. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that when the next attack comes — and it will — we do not lose the capacity to answer.
