## New measure
**EU Incident Reporting and High-Risk Enforcement Surge**
Stands up mandatory rapid reporting of serious AI-enabled cyber incidents and a joint Commission–national audit sweep of deployed high-risk AI decision systems with suspension power where oversight is paper-only.
This is Transparency and reporting because the benefits scandal has discredited enforcement and the major automated attack needs a common picture fast; with public calm removing any bonus for spending, a small, fast, visible accountability move is what can land now and serves the resilient half of the two-year commitment.

## Priority
EU Critical Infrastructure Shield, because a live large automated attack outranks even the AI Act scandal — segmentation and backup controls must hold before enforcement credibility can matter.

## In practice
We are putting ENISA into 24/7 incident-clearinghouse mode under NIS2, with mandatory 48-hour reporting from operators of essential services and public-sector bodies hit by the sweep, feeding joint TSO-CERT triage; DG CNECT reprioritises Digital Europe cyber funds to sensors and backup controls for the two affected transmission operators first, offering interior ministries co-funded exercises and EU-paid OT audit teams to buy access.

In parallel we task the AI Office with market-surveillance authorities in a targeted Annex III sweep of social-benefit, policing and court-support systems — pulling logs, timing human review, suspending deployments where oversight is forty-seconds-an-item — using AI Act Article 70-72 enforcement powers, while InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech sovereignty package stay on permitting and grid-connection queuing, with DG ENER mediating compute versus hardening connections so the shield is not starved by our own AI factories.
