### The Weight of Action

The European Commission declared the Sovereign AI Core Programme fully operational, invoking emergency powers to override national resistance and fast-track modular data centre deployment. Binding site designations were issued in Brittany, northern Sweden, and the Alps. Yet the defiance of Germany, Austria, Italy, and Hungary held firm—legal challenges blocked physical access, and sabotage incidents at two designated locations forced military deployment. No new sovereign compute came online.

The integration of the new interpretability stack into EAAD’s runtime monitoring advanced, improving visibility into AI decision chains in healthcare and energy systems. This modest gain in oversight contributed to a slight rise in `ai_safety`, despite rising capability.

Meanwhile, white-collar displacement deepened. With entry-level roles in law, accounting, and administration vanishing, protests escalated into direct action—occupations of data centres, severed grid connections, and violent clashes with police. The backlash eroded public trust further, dragging `public_sentiment` down.

`ai_capability` advanced steadily to 65.0, driven by closed-lab progress. With no sovereign capacity deployed and dependency growing, `eu_ai_sovereignty` declined again, compounding the loss from the previous turn. The portfolio’s high cost—two large measures ongoing, plus priority status—drained `eu_political_capital`, which fell to 8.0, now below the threshold for reliable agenda control.

Efforts to frame AI resilience as a public health imperative could not overcome institutional fragmentation. The emergency directive failed to break the blockade. Resilience dipped as systems remained exposed.

### A Sovereignty Deferred, Again

The Union could see deeper into AI systems than ever before—but still could not act on its own terms. The vision of sovereign infrastructure remained unfulfilled, not for lack of will, but for lack of cohesion.
