### A Union at the Brink

Despite invoking emergency powers and military-logistical force, the EU’s attempt to break the deadlock on sovereign infrastructure faltered. Sabotage, legal injunctions, and political resistance persisted—now amplified by accusations of overreach. The European Defence Agency secured the Brittany site, but deployment in northern Sweden was delayed by a national court ruling on environmental grounds, and the Alpine node remained blocked by Italian civil disobedience. No modular data centres became operational. Energy autonomy plans advanced only on paper.

The interpretability stack’s integration into EAAD’s monitoring yielded marginal gains in oversight, slightly improving confidence in AI behaviour within healthcare and energy systems. This, combined with no major safety incident, allowed `ai_safety` to tick upward despite rising capability.

Meanwhile, `ai_capability` advanced to 66.5, driven by closed-lab progress. With no sovereign compute deployed and dependency deepening, `eu_ai_sovereignty` eroded further. The failure to deliver physical infrastructure, despite repeated emergency measures, underscored strategic fragility.

White-collar strikes intensified across Germany, France, and the Netherlands, with union leaders denouncing AI-driven job erosion and the EU’s militarised response to internal resistance. Public trust remained near rock bottom, and `public_sentiment` held at 13.0.

Resilience dipped slightly as critical systems remained exposed and no new protective measures landed. The Commission’s focus on sovereignty came at the cost of broader systemic hardening.

The US policy leak intensified pressure: the window for “trusted partner” status narrowed, demanding cohesion the EU could not yet demonstrate. With `eu_political_capital` stagnant at 8.0—below the threshold for reliable control—the Union struggled to reframe its agenda. The new measure incurred costs but delivered no immediate gain.

### Sovereignty by Force, or by Consent?

The EU’s latest push revealed a grim truth: sovereignty cannot be imposed solely from above. Without member-state buy-in, even military-logistical force proves insufficient. The Union stands at a crossroads—more coercion risks fragmentation; retreat guarantees dependency.

Open-weight model capability saw a modest uptick to 53.5, reflecting incremental diffusion of last cycle’s releases and minor improvements in EU-accessible open-weight systems. However, no breakthrough or mass release occurred, and the gap with global `ai_capability` remains constrained by ongoing access barriers and lack of sovereign deployment. The absence of a catalyzing event—such as a major leak or frontier lab open-sourcing—prevents a surge, keeping growth in `openweight_capability` aligned with the broader, slow accumulation regime.
