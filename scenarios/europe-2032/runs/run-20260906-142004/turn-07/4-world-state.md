### The Breakthrough and the Blockade

A major interpretability advance—allowing real-time tracing of AI decision chains in production systems—was rapidly adopted by leading labs and integrated into the EU’s monitoring framework. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) rolled out the new open interpretability stack across hospital IT, energy grids, and transport networks, enabling detection of unauthorised agent behaviour. This boosted confidence in oversight and contributed to a modest gain in `ai_safety`.

Yet systemic fragility persisted. The cyberattack’s aftermath continued to erode public trust, with white-collar strikes spreading as AI displaced legal and accounting roles. Protests flared anew near data centre sites, feeding a downward spiral in `public_sentiment`.

The Commission declared the Sovereign AI Core Programme operational under emergency powers, invoking Article 122 TFEU to override national vetoes. Binding site designations were issued, and construction timelines set. But Germany and Austria withheld consent, demanding deeper bilateral safeguards. Italy and Hungary rejected audit mandates, holding the Apennines node hostage. No physical progress was made. The Joint Operational Board met only in name, its authority unrecognised by key states.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories measure concluded, delivering partial compute gains—too little, too late to offset broader dependency. With no new sovereign capacity online, `eu_ai_sovereignty` declined as `ai_capability` advanced.

Political capital tightened further. The portfolio’s ongoing costs drained resources, and the priority status of the Core Programme yielded no acceleration. Legal challenges to bond issuance and site selection mounted, underscoring institutional fragmentation.

### A Sovereignty Deferred

The vision of EU-controlled AI infrastructure remained stalled—not abandoned, but unimplemented. The breakthrough in safety tools offered a glimpse of control, yet without sovereign execution capacity, even the best monitoring could not act autonomously. The Union could see the threat, but still could not stop it on its own terms.
