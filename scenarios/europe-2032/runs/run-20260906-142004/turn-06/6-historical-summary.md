The EU's response to a major AI-driven cyberattack on hospital systems exposed critical vulnerabilities in its digital infrastructure, prompting the Commission to launch the Sovereign AI Core Programme with €80bn in redirected funds and plans for air-gapped data centres in Brittany, Sweden, and the Alps. However, implementation stalled due to member state opposition: Germany and Austria rejected overrides of unanimity requirements, while Italy and Hungary blocked audit access and logging mandates, preventing any new nodes from being established. Legal challenges halted EIB bond issuance, and the Joint Operational Board failed to convene. The EAAD expanded monitoring to systems above 55.0 capability, but enforcement remained inconsistent. Public trust deteriorated further as job losses in professional sectors fueled protests, including a violent incident in Brussels. Amid growing fragmentation, the new US administration took office, with anticipated AI restrictions pending clarification of its policy direction.
