### The shield and the repair cell
January brought a rare piece of good news for defenders. Research groups on both sides of the Atlantic demonstrated automated patching that kept pace with newly found flaws, and detection that flagged coordinated swarms of agents by behaviour rather than by signature. For Brussels, exhausted by two years of clinic lockouts and port backlogs, the timing was providential.

The Commission pushed the new tooling through the existing emergency repair operation. ENISA teams carried clean images and the new detectors back to the worst-hit clinics, permit offices and the two ports, replacing temporary servers with hardened builds. Mayors finally received timetables for permanent replacement. The work was visible, competent, and welcomed — but it was repair, not construction. The five fenced gigafactory plots stayed empty, and the solo computing deal with an American provider remained outside the common line.

Two cross-currents complicated the reception. In journals, a genome modelling paper claimed a non-expert could be guided toward a viable human-infecting design. Methodologists attacked the claim, editors were accused of both hype and irresponsibility, and health ministries quietly asked whether the new cyber detectors could be extended to lab screening. At the same time, business pages filled with studies from law firms, consultancies and newsrooms: output per person up, junior staff augmented rather than replaced, early cutters re-hiring. Power-price pickets outside the empty sites thinned; job-fear rhetoric lost its edge.

The result was paradoxical. Clinics stayed online longer, offices reported steadier weeks, and the public mood lifted slightly from despair. Yet with no displacement crisis to point at, ministers found it harder to justify emergency spending. The new patch shield was approved as a small, fast deployment on reprogrammed funds, its mandate stretched only to December. Talk of blockades and sabotage around substations remained rumour and single-source claims, not confirmed attacks. Dependence endured, managed a little better than before.
