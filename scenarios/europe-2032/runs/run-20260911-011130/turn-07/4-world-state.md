### Patching in the dark
Through autumn the repair crews kept working. In the worst-hit towns, clinic records came back online and one of the two blocked ports cleared its backlog with clean servers and offline backups. Mayors who had welcomed the crews now asked when the temporary systems would be replaced. Elsewhere the same legacy controllers failed again, and paper fallback returned for days at a time.

Brussels had no second front to offer. The Commission told ministers plainly that the emergency cell was the only funded operation until December, and no new fund was tabled. Talk of future gigafactory capacity remained talk: no sites were built out, no new measure was launched, and the InvestAI Gigafactories build remained unfinished with its five fenced sites still empty — any large build would take years, not months, and no completion can be credited this turn. The joint licensing talks with Japan and Korea continued in The Hague and Paris, and the capital that had struck its own computing deal was offered shared terms to return. It did not return.

Then the labs announced what engineers had whispered for months: the best models no longer reasoned in words at all. The intermediate steps that auditors, regulators and safety teams had relied on to read were gone, replaced by representations no human could follow. Vendors presented it as a performance gain. In European newsrooms it landed as a loss of oversight at the exact moment machine-assisted intrusions were still unattributed, eroding safety capacity.

The technology package formally closed with its permit simplifications on paper. Power-price protests outside two of the empty sites grew, and eastern commentators described a Union managing dependence while its access was tiered elsewhere. Restoration, partial and visible, was the only success the Union could point to — and it barely held the line.
