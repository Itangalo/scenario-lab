### Repair holds, then breaks
Autumn began with the repair operation finally looking like a system. ENISA crews pushed automated patching and behaviour-based swarm detection beyond the original clinics and ports to a wider ring of hospitals and municipal offices, and at health ministries' urging linked feeds to laboratory screening. Mayors quoted timetables. For a few weeks outages shortened.

Then a large automated assault landed. In some cities it arrived as ransomware sweeping appointment systems and permit desks; elsewhere as a poisoned software component whose blast radius took weeks to map. Screens went dark in clinics that had just reopened, backlogs returned, and attribution slipped away into months of forensics. Defenders were visibly behind. The tooling, analysts agreed, had been largely machine-generated.

The Commission's answer was to hold the line with what it had. Crisis funds were reprogrammed to keep the shield running to December, crews returned to the worst-hit sites with clean images, and no new sovereignty vote was tabled. The five fenced plots for future compute plants stayed empty under bank reservation.

### A visible win, claimed as European
In the middle of the disruption, administrators could point to something else: waiting lists that had actually fallen where assistance systems triaged cases, permit decisions arriving in days, tutoring pilots with measurable learning gains. Commission departments moved quickly to document the results and brand them as European decisions on vetted European-run models, preparing dozens of clinics, town halls and schools as expansion sites.

That claim helped contain the political damage. Public anger over the outages was real, but the sight of a working service blunted calls to abandon the programme.

Abroad, the Union joined a new coordination framework with other middle powers holding supply-chain chokepoints — aligning export licences, pooling bargaining over compute access and sharing evaluation work. No sovereignty was ceded, but joint forensics help and a backstop on parts arrived. Around substations feeding the empty sites, rumours of blockades and sabotage talk persisted in local press and single-source police notes, never confirmed as a campaign, adding to the sense of a grid under watch.
