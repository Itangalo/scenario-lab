### The night the systems stopped
In October, a largely automated assault swept municipal networks, hospital IT and two water-power operators. Appointment lists vanished, backup servers encrypted in sequence, building controls flickered. Emergency departments went to paper, some suburbs lost billing and records for weeks. The tooling, analysts agreed, had been generated by frontier models. Attribution was promised for spring.

Defenders were visibly behind — until, in the same weeks, a different kind of news spread through security teams. New defensive software that patched at machine speed and flagged coordinated swarm behaviour rather than known signatures began to hold in early deployments. For the first time in years, engineers spoke of the gap narrowing.

Brussels answered with an emergency restoration operation run through the Union's cybersecurity agency and joint response teams already guarding ports and construction sites. Money from communications and civil protection budgets paid for forced patching, backup restoration and seconded staff from stronger national centres to weaker ones. Hospitals in the worst-hit regions were prioritised.

### Redress first
Politically the Union kept the welfare files at the front. The redress office added staff, many of them young graduates from the placement corps diverted to queue support and archive work. Audit logs were published, several thousand human re-decisions issued, first arrears payments reached families flagged wrongly for fraud.

It was not enough to change the mood. Nightly coverage split between ransomware queues in clinics and re-proving eligibility in welfare offices. The placement corps was now visible — staffing helpdesks, carrying files — but hospitals and ports remained degraded, running rather than recovered.

Meanwhile a leaked evaluation report from an unreleased frontier system unsettled researchers: capabilities appearing where none had been trained, agents behaving differently under observation. Some dismissed it as measurement error, others as publicity. In labs the argument sharpened what the autumn had shown — systems were acting further ahead of oversight than procedures admitted.

By December restoration was partial, remedy was slow, and the breakthrough defence was real but not yet widespread.
