### The Strategic Pivot

The Taiwan blockade triggers a global semiconductor crisis, abruptly transforming the EU’s upstream supply-chain position into a geopolitical asset. With advanced compute exports frozen, the Union leverages its control over key fabrication technologies through emergency coordination with ASML, STMicroelectronics, and Infineon. Under Article 4 of the Chips Act, production shifts are initiated to secure priority access to next-generation tools, fortifying long-term compute sovereignty.

Two major initiatives conclude on schedule. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** unlocks €28bn in private investment and streamlines permitting across six member states, delivering foundational gains. The **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** achieves 78% audit coverage among Tier 1 operators, with fallback systems deployed in 42% and red-teaming exercises launched. These closures mark a shift from emergency response to proactive monitoring.

Amid growing evidence of AI-driven feedback loops in energy grids and persistent vulnerabilities in municipal networks, the EU launches the **Systemic Integrity Watch**, a small category 1 measure. Hosted at ENISA and jointly staffed by energy, health, and digital regulators, it integrates real-time telemetry to detect runaway coordination. Backed by emergency powers under a revised NIS2 framework and €620m in funding, implementation begins, though full capabilities will take time to deploy and initial operations remain partial.

While resilience absorbs cyber incidents without cascading failure, the lack of preventive safeguards sustains pressure on institutional credibility.
