## New measure  
**Emergency Real-Time Detection and Response Framework for Synthetic Interference**  
Establish a temporary but legally empowered EU-level taskforce to detect, verify, and counter AI-generated synthetic media campaigns targeting electoral processes, with direct access to platform data, rapid response authority, and coordination with national election commissions and fact-checking networks.

## Priority  
**Emergency Real-Time Detection and Response Framework for Synthetic Interference** — because the emergent synthetic election interference event has exposed a critical vulnerability in democratic infrastructure, and with regional elections underway in three member states, the integrity of institutions is under immediate attack: this is not a future risk but a present breach of the very systems we committed to defend.

## In practice  
This turn, we accelerate the **Openweight Safety Assurance Programme**, now fully staffed and operational across the three research hubs in Paris, Berlin, and Helsinki. DG CNECT has issued the first call for open-weight submissions above 45.0 capability, and ENISA is adapting the new interpretability tools from the safety breakthrough into standardised evaluation suites. We are negotiating access to key open repositories and working with community maintainers to encourage voluntary submission — not to control, but to illuminate. The first safety profiles will be published by turn 9, giving public and private operators a clear signal of which models carry unpatched agent risks. This is not regulation, but visibility — and in the distributed frontier, visibility is the first line of defence.

At the same time, we respond to the **emergent synthetic election interference** that overwhelmed fact-checking in two member states. This was not mere disinformation — it was coordinated, multimodal, and adaptive, using synthetic voices, deepfake video, and AI-generated social media personas to mimic grassroots movements. The current DSA framework is too slow; verification happens after amplification. So we are activating emergency provisions under Article 23 of the DSA and Article 5 of the Crisis Regulation to stand up a temporary **Real-Time Detection and Response Framework**, hosted by ENISA in coordination with Europol and the European Digital Media Observatory. It will have direct API access to high-risk platforms, deploy AI-powered detection models trained on the latest synthetic patterns, and issue verified alerts to national election commissions within two hours of detection. We are fast-tracking legal approval through the COREPER II formation, offering sunset clauses and strict oversight to secure Council support. The cost in political capital is real, but the cost of inaction — eroded legitimacy, contested results, public distrust — would be irreversible. This is resilience in motion: not just hardening systems, but defending the perception of truth itself.
