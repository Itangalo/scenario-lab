**Openweight Safety Assurance Programme**  
Launch a coordinated EU-wide effort to evaluate, harden, and monitor the safety of openly released model weights, leveraging the recent safety breakthrough to apply new interpretability tools to open models and establish a baseline of assurance for distributed AI.

## Priority  
**Openweight Safety Assurance Programme** — because the safety breakthrough and widening verification now allow meaningful assurance on systems that are already loose, and with openweight_capability at 49.0 and rising, we can no longer treat the open frontier as a secondary concern: the next dangerous agent will not come from a lab with compliance officers, but from a distributed network running unsecured weights on consumer hardware.

## In practice  
This turn, we complete the **Systemic Integrity Watch**, transitioning it from initial deployment to full operational status. ENISA now receives live telemetry from 61% of designated Tier 1 operators in energy, health, and digital infrastructure, with automated anomaly detection running in pilot mode across three national grids. The first alerts — not yet public — flagged AI-driven load-balancing feedback loops in two regional energy markets, prompting technical interventions that prevented instability. The system is not yet comprehensive, but it is alive, and for the first time, the Union can see coordination before it cascades. We are now formalising its mandate under a permanent NIS3 framework, ensuring continuity beyond emergency powers.

At the same time, we act on two converging developments: the **safety breakthrough** in interpretability and the **widening of verification** into domains like legal and clinical coding. These are not incremental — they mean that for the first time, we can *predict* certain dangerous behaviours in models *before* deployment, and we can *automatically verify* outputs in high-risk applications. We are directing €410m from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe to fund a new **Openweight Safety Assurance Programme**, hosted jointly by DG CNECT and ENISA, with participating labs in France, Germany, and Finland. This is not about controlling open models — we cannot — but about understanding them. We will develop standardised evaluation suites using the new verification tools, apply them to all open models above 45.0 capability, and publish safety profiles that operators, developers, and public institutions can use to assess risk. Where possible, we will work with open communities to patch vulnerabilities and promote secure deployment patterns. The goal is not to stop open innovation, but to ensure it does not become the weakest link in our collective defence.
