## In practice
This turn, we conclude the **Openweight Safety Assurance Programme**, delivering the first EU-wide safety profiles for open models above 45.0 capability. The hubs in Paris, Berlin, and Helsinki have published their initial assessments, identifying three widely used agent-capable models with unpatched coordination risks. We are distributing mitigation advisories through repository maintainers and alerting public-sector users via the EDPS. While no bans are imposed, the transparency shift is real: developers now expect scrutiny, and users can make informed choices. This closes a critical visibility gap in the distributed frontier — and with the programme complete, we retire it from our portfolio, its effect now embedded in ongoing evaluation practice.

We continue to operate the **Emergency Real-Time Detection and Response Framework**, which has already intercepted two new synthetic campaigns — one targeting regional elections in Spain, another impersonating a national health authority in Belgium. Legal challenges from Poland and Hungary persist, but the Council’s conditional extension holds, and we are now integrating Europol’s cybercrime intelligence feeds to improve signal fidelity. The system’s value is no longer theoretical: it has reduced response lag from days to hours, and public trust in electoral integrity has stabilised in affected states. We are working with the European Parliament’s LIBE committee to convert emergency powers into a standing capability under reinforced oversight, but that will require a new legal basis — a task for next turn.

Most critically, we launch the **EU Cognitive Sovereignty Initiative**, responding to the eval_anomaly_reports and the ASML export restrictions. We cannot rely on foreign goodwill for the compute and talent needed to evaluate systems that may already be behaving unpredictably. Under EuroHPC, we are fast-tracking two new sovereign data centres — one in Finland, one in Portugal — with hardened access controls and legal prohibitions on remote shutdown or backdoor access. We are pairing this with binding agreements for EU-certified safety researchers: multi-year contracts, mobility guarantees, and secure access to both open and licensed models for evaluation purposes. DG GROW is negotiating with member states to waive permitting delays for these sites, offering energy grid priority in return. The cost is high — 3 political capital per turn — but without this foundation, our resilience and oversight are performative. This is not about catching the frontier; it is about ensuring we can see it, test it, and act on it — independently.
