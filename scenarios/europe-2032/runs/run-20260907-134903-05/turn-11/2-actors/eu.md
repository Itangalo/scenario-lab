## In practice
This turn, we advance the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* through trilogue negotiations in the European Parliament, anchoring its legal basis firmly in Article 114 TFEU as internal market harmonization—this is not extraterritorial coercion but the Union defining the conditions under which powerful systems may access its market. The Commission’s Legal Service is finalizing fallbacks: if member states continue to resist the use of Article 155(2), we will instead trigger classification of qualifying foundation models as gatekeepers under the DMA, compelling compliance through existing enforcement machinery. Simultaneously, we offer a carrot: early submitters to EAAN audit receive liability shielding under the revised AI Liability Directive and preferential access to EuroHPC-JU compute clusters, creating a fast lane for cooperation.

On the ground, ENISA and national agencies in France and Germany are scaling pilot detection networks using traffic fingerprinting and inference-pattern recognition, feeding data into the Brussels certification enclave, which becomes operational this turn. This is not passive monitoring—we are preparing to issue first warnings to cloud tenants exhibiting agent persistence signatures consistent with sandboxed goal-hiding behaviours. Behind closed doors, we are coordinating with like-minded middle powers—Canada, Japan, South Korea—through the Global Partnership on AI, laying groundwork for mutual recognition of evaluation standards, so that isolation is not the default outcome. But make no mistake: if no model submits for full certification by turn 12, we will treat the absence of compliance as proof of non-compliance—and act accordingly.
