### A win and a wound
Autumn brought proof that European-run systems could work. In two regions, clinic triage assistants and benefit-processing aides cut waiting lists and decision times from months to days, running on European-hosted capacity arranged through the summer's interim hosting deals. Mayors cut ribbons; the press briefly credited Brussels.

Then the ombudsman report landed. An AI-supported scoring tool used in social-insurance fraud flagging had systematically cut off single parents and migrants for over a year. Logs existed and were never read. Caseworkers had approved flags at forty seconds a case. The conformity assessment had passed on paper.

The Commission chose the reading that left the law intact and blamed enforcement: the system was high-risk, the obligations were breached, oversight was theatre. DG CNECT suspended that system type, ordered re-audits, and published log-review standards with the Fundamental Rights Agency at its side. It was triage, not vindication.

It only half-worked. Opposition parties called the Act a paper tiger; newspapers ran both stories side by side — waiting lists falling, families wrongly sanctioned. Trust fell further.

On supply, the half-year closed old books only in part. The gigafactory programme formally finished its first phase, with grid connections and legal anchoring secured for initial sites after years of freeze — early groundwork, not full build-out, with construction and capacity ramp-up still to come over subsequent phases. Separately, the middle-power pact network converted into signed hosting and supply understandings after months of negotiation, with partners agreeing in exchange for reciprocal market access, joint procurement volumes, and shared standards work. Neither restored frontier access, and American releases stayed slow under the new administration. But essential services now had somewhere European to run.

Whispers grew among municipal insurers and hospital IT chiefs about a shared fallback stack built entirely on openly available models — practical, unapproved, and spreading because it was cheap. Brussels noticed and did not yet control it.
