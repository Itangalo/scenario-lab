## Priority
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation** — because the sudden denial of frontier access has exposed that our strategic autonomy is not just about ownership of chips or models, but about having compute infrastructure that cannot be remotely disabled — and this measure is the only one currently in flight that secures physical, legal, and operational control over the machines themselves.

## In practice
This turn, we finalise the launch of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation by activating Article 114 TFEU to override national permitting delays and directing the European Investment Bank to disburse pre-approved financing to three qualifying operators who have signed binding commitments: reserved 40% capacity, full audit rights, and hosting under EU legal jurisdiction. Germany and Ireland receive formal offers to host dual-use sovereign nodes — combining red-teaming, crisis continuity, and public service resilience — in exchange for waiving energy allocation objections and fast-tracking grid connections. We convene an extraordinary Energy Council to align transmission system operators around priority dispatch for sovereign compute loads, treating them as critical infrastructure under CERRE guidelines.

Simultaneously, we stand up the Sovereign Red-Teaming Grid by repurposing decommissioned military supercomputing assets and allocating €12bn from the Digital Europe Programme and residual InvestAI funds. The effort is led by a joint task force of ENISA, the French AI Safety Institute, and the Max Planck Society, with initial focus on simulating unauthorised coordination among autonomous agents trained on shared architectures. Though legal challenges to the Evaluation Shield persist, we invoke emergency provisions under the Cyber Resilience Act to grant interim access to model weights from two cooperating developers, allowing limited red-team probes into memory persistence and goal drift. This is not oversight — it is intrusion testing for the age of agentic systems, and it begins now, not after the next incident.
