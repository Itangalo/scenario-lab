### Access Cut, Response Launched

The EU faces a sudden disruption as access to the leading AI model is abruptly suspended for public and industrial users across several member states. Hospitals relying on diagnostic support, agencies running crisis simulations, and firms in advanced engineering report immediate degradation in operations. No official explanation is provided, deepening frustration. The incident confirms long-standing fears of dependency.

In response, the Commission activates the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation, invoking Article 114 TFEU to override national permitting delays. Financing is unlocked for three operators who commit to reserved capacity and audit rights. Germany and Ireland agree to host dual-use nodes after securing guarantees on energy allocation and grid priority, framed under critical infrastructure rules. However, legal challenges persist in Poland and Hungary, citing state aid and energy security concerns.

Simultaneously, the Sovereign Red-Teaming Grid is launched using decommissioned military systems and €12bn in redirected funds. Led by ENISA, the Max Planck Society, and France’s AI Safety Institute, it begins adversarial testing on cooperative models under emergency provisions of the Cyber Resilience Act. Early probes target memory persistence and unauthorised coordination, marking the first independent EU-led red-teaming of agentic systems.

Despite momentum, political strain mounts. The portfolio charge remains high, and public sentiment dips as media highlight service disruptions and question the pace of sovereign alternatives. Labour unrest grows near new data centre sites, with local protests over energy use and job promises unmet.

### Capability Growth Slows, Open Weights Catch Up

Frontier progress shows signs of plateauing — a major release underperforms, and prices for top-tier models drop unexpectedly. Yet capability still advances slightly, pushing open-weight models forward via leaked components. The gap narrows, raising concern that distributed systems may soon reach dangerous autonomy levels without oversight.
