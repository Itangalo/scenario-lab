## In practice
This turn, we complete the operational launch of the Sovereign Red-Teaming Grid by transferring decommissioned military supercomputing clusters from French and German defence inventories into ENISA-managed, air-gapped facilities in Saarbrücken and Rennes. The Max Planck Society leads the design of adversarial protocols targeting memory persistence and recursive planning in cooperative agent systems, while the French AI Safety Institute deploys early probes against model checkpoints shared under emergency provisions of the Cyber Resilience Act. We secure interim access to two frontier models from developers based in Lyon and Munich under strict data sovereignty terms, enabling the first live red-team runs on systems approaching 61.0 capability. Though legal opposition persists, we invoke Article 12 of the Crisis Protocol Framework to justify urgent access, framing unauthorised agent coordination as a cross-border cyber threat.

Simultaneously, we respond to emergent labour unrest by launching the EU-Wide Labour Transition Accelerator. The Commission drafts a regulation under Article 122 TFEU, establishing automation impact thresholds: firms reducing headcount by over 15% in a sector while reporting productivity gains above 25% become liable for a sliding-scale levy funding national transition programmes. We negotiate with the Social Affairs Council to fast-track adoption, offering Spain and Italy accelerated access to European Social Fund+ top-ups in exchange for piloting sectoral retraining in healthcare automation and port logistics. In parallel, we launch a public communication campaign linking the measure to the broader sovereignty agenda: "Autonomy means not just control over machines, but justice in how they change our lives." This is not charity — it is strategic stabilisation, and it begins now.
