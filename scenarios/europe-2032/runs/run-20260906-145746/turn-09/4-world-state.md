### Capability Creeps Forward, Open Weights Advance Cautiously

AI capability inches upward to 62.0, sustained by integration refinements and tighter deployment loops rather than breakthroughs. Open-weight models advance modestly to 49.5, supported by incremental distillation gains and limited access to older frontier checkpoints. The gap with frontier capability remains structurally intact, with no evidence of leakage or deliberate release from closed developers. Growth in open-weight performance is constrained by model depth and training compute, preventing faster convergence absent major policy shifts or leaks.

### EU Proposes Broader Oversight, Faces Immediate Limits

The Commission proposes a **Frontier Access Enforcement Directive**, aiming to establish binding audit rights and real-time monitoring for high-capability models operating in the EU. The proposal is formally logged as a new measure, entering early consultation. However, no enforcement mechanisms are active this turn, and implementation is delayed by inter-institutional debate and legal uncertainty. This counts as the EU’s one new measure for the turn, pausing further expansion of the policy portfolio.

The directive faces immediate skepticism. U.S. hyperscalers reject its extraterritorial claims, citing lack of reciprocity and jurisdictional overreach. Chinese developers similarly decline to engage, noting absence of trade leverage or mutual recognition. Without prior agreements or conditioning of market access, the EU cannot compel compliance. Resistance is not reactive—it is foundational. Legal scholars warn the framework may not survive challenge under WTO digital trade precedents.

### Scandal and Backlash Undermine Trust

Reports emerge of systemic errors in an AI-supported social insurance system, disproportionately affecting vulnerable groups. Initial findings from a national ombudsman spark debate and raise concerns about accountability, though final conclusions remain pending. Public discourse grows more anxious, and automation fears intensify amid visible labour unrest. The EU-Wide Labour Transition Accelerator continues to face funding delays and pilot-state disputes, limiting its impact.

Public sentiment declines to 33.0, reflecting mounting unease but not yet collapsing. Resilience erodes as AI-driven disruptions outpace institutional responses. Political capital declines to 24.0—moderately, due to partial portfolio strain and early costs of the new proposal, but not full activation, as implementation remains distant. The EU’s sovereignty, weakened by member-state defection and persistent dependency on external compute, falls to 29.0, consistent with ongoing decay from lack of unified access and no offsetting gains.
