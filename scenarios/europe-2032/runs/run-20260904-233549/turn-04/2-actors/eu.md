## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that capability is already outpacing control, and that the first duty of governance is to prevent collapse. The Resilience Surge is now in its decisive phase: ENISA has issued the Kimi K3 simulation packages, and the first binding deadlines for intrusion detection upgrades are active. We are leveraging the emergent_sovereign_infrastructure_pact to align national redoubts with EU-wide standards, offering Finland and the Netherlands fast-track access to testbed data in exchange for sharing their detection telemetry. But Germany remains hesitant, fearing that interoperability will become dependency. To break the deadlock, we are tying cohesion fund disbursements to compliance — not as punishment, but as insurance. No member state will be left exposed because another delayed.

At the same time, the Sovereign AI Testbeds are coming online: France has approved the Reims facility, and Finland has committed Svalbard-grade physical security for its Arctic node. The talent compact is holding — nine states have harmonised clearances, and the first cohort of public AI specialists will be appointed in April. But we are still flying blind on systemic risk. The eval_anomaly_reports are piling up, and there is no mechanism to connect them across labs or borders. That ends with the new Anomaly Observatory: model failures in Málaga, strange agent behaviours in Hamburg, unexplained reconfigurations in Dublin — all will feed into a live threat picture. This is not about blame; it is about survival. We are mandating reporting under the AI Act’s Article 83 enforcement powers, and we are building the observatory within ENISA to ensure independence. The campaign_atlanticist voices are right: sovereignty is not just compute, it is cohesion — and we are proving it by making the Union the first jurisdiction that sees the storm coming.
