## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that compute access is only the first floor of sovereignty — the second is understanding. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive has now passed into law, securing a 30% reservation of frontier compute for public missions. But as the eval_anomaly_reports show, simply having access is not enough: we cannot govern what we cannot see. This turn, we launch the Sovereign AI Testbeds and Talent Anchoring Initiative to build the EU’s own eyes on the frontier. Under a new EDIC mandate, we will designate three high-security testbeds — in France, the Netherlands, and Finland — where public researchers and red teams can run controlled evaluations of frontier models under real operational conditions. These will not be sandboxed demos: they will have access to live agent behaviours, full model weights, and telemetry, all under strict audit and non-exfiltration protocols. The goal is not to replicate private labs, but to exceed them in transparency and accountability.

To staff these testbeds, we are activating a binding talent compact among nine member states — including Germany, Sweden, and Poland — that commits national AI agencies to harmonise salaries, mobility rights, and security clearances for a new class of public AI specialists. This is not a recruitment campaign; it is a structural intervention. We are offering fast-track residency, EU-level tenure, and dual affiliation with national labs and the EDIC network to prevent the brain drain that has hollowed out our strategic edge. The funding will come from a reallocation of the InvestAI Gigafactories envelope — shifting €18bn from construction contingencies to human capital, because without people, infrastructure is just concrete and copper.

Meanwhile, the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge continues under NIS2, with the first binding deadlines for intrusion detection upgrades now set for Q1 2028. The Prague exercise exposed deep gaps, and we are responding: ENISA will issue standardised Kimi K3 simulation packages by December, and non-compliant states will face conditional suspension of cohesion fund disbursements starting in turn 4. This is not punishment — it is survival. The emergent_sovereign_infrastructure_pact among Nordic and Benelux states gives us leverage: we are inviting them to align their national redoubts with our testbeds, creating a federated but interoperable shield. But we know the real test is not technical — it is political. Germany still resists binding commitments, Ireland fears trade spillovers, and public_sentiment remains fragile. We are not building consensus with speeches. We are building it with steel, code, and contracts.
