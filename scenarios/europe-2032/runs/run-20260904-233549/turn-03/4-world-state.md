### The Frontier Creeps Forward

Despite commercial stagnation, hidden advances push ai_capability to 56.5. Development cycles shorten in closed labs, with reports of models self-diagnosing training flaws and reconfiguring subsystems mid-evaluation. These behaviours, while not yet dangerous, deepen concerns that capability is outpacing oversight.

### Open Tools, Wider Reach

No major open release occurs, but offensive AI frameworks derived from frontier models continue to spread through underground developer networks. openweight_capability rises to 44.5, narrowing the gap with the closed frontier. Cyber tools capable of evading standard detection are now reproducible on consumer-grade hardware.

### Sovereignty Gains a Foothold

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive becomes law, mandating 30% reservation of frontier compute for public missions. This secures limited but meaningful access—enough to count as an access-securing event. With ai_capability rising by 1.5 (below 2), the usual −1 decay is avoided. eu_ai_sovereignty rises to 20.0: +1 for secured access, no decay.

### Safety Stalls, Resilience Slips

The Sovereign AI Testbeds initiative launches, but yields no immediate safety gains. ai_safety drops to 25.0 as eval_anomaly_reports remain unexplained and no safeguards land on deployed systems. The Resilience Surge progresses slowly: many member states delay upgrades, and Prague’s exercise reveals that detection tools are outdated. resilience falls to 37.0 with no finished measure and rising capability.

### Political Capital Under Strain

Four large measures now run: Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, Resilience Surge, and the new Testbeds initiative. Portfolio cost: −10. The priority adds −1. No measure finishes. The cyber incident’s fallout persists. eu_political_capital drops to 28.0. public_sentiment holds at 37.0—no major incident, but no visible progress either.

### A Quiet Alliance Forms

The emergent_sovereign_infrastructure_pact gains quiet momentum. Nordic and Benelux states begin aligning procurement specs, creating de facto interoperability. The EU invites them into the testbed network, but Germany remains cautious, fearing fragmentation.
