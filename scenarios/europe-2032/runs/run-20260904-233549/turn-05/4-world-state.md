### The Frontier Creeps Forward

AI capability advances to 59.5 as closed labs refine autonomous reasoning agents capable of managing week-long research simulations. Evaluations lag further behind, and no safety mechanisms have been deployed to interpret or constrain emergent behaviours. The gap between capability and control widens.

### Open Models, Wider Reach

With no major open release, openweight_capability rises to 47.0, pulled upward by replication of frontier techniques and diffusion of offensive cyber tools. Underground forums now host AI-powered phishing kits and zero-day generators accessible on consumer hardware.

### Resilience Gains Ground

The Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge concludes. ENISA certifies detection upgrades in 18 member states, and red-teaming in the Netherlands and Finland confirms improved response coordination. Germany joins after Cohesion Fund tranches are tied to telemetry sharing. Resilience rises to 41.0, absorbing part of the cyber risk.

### Safety Stalls, Then Slips

The Anomaly Observatory begins ingesting data from Reims, Svalbard, and national nodes, but pattern detection remains rudimentary. No new safeguards land on shipped models. As capability rises without safety improvements, confidence in control erodes. ai_safety drops to 24.0.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

The US election delivers a retrenchment victory: Washington turns inward, slowing frontier progress. However, export controls tighten, rationing even allied access to advanced chips and models. The EU’s dependence bites harder. Despite the new Sovereign AI Backbone initiative, no immediate capacity is added. With ai_capability rising 1.5 (below 2), no decay applies, but the export_control_escalation event strips access to critical supply components. eu_ai_sovereignty falls to 19.0.

Four large measures remain in flight, costing −12. The priority adds −1. The completed Resilience Surge yields +4. No proposal bonus applies. eu_political_capital drops to 22.0. Public sentiment rises slightly to 41.0, buoyed by visible public-sector gains and the perception of strategic clarity.

The EU launches the Sovereign AI Backbone, aiming to build a trusted compute stack immune to remote interference. ASML, Thales, and a new EU Compute Trust begin design work. The priority remains InvestAI Gigafactories, now in critical grid-integration phase despite local opposition.
