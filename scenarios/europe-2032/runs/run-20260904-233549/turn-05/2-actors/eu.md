## New measure
**EU Sovereign AI Backbone: Secure Compute and Control Stack**  
Deploy a sovereign, interoperable stack of hardware, firmware, and orchestration software to ensure EU-controlled AI systems cannot be remotely disabled, reconfigured, or surveilled by external actors.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to eu_ai_sovereignty by turn 8 by ensuring that EU-run models operate on infrastructure with trusted supply chains, sovereign firmware, and fail-deadly switches; reduces dependence on foreign cloud providers and hyperscaler dependencies  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national critical agencies, sovereign testbeds, and all providers operating under EU safety certification

## In practice
This turn marks the end of our two-year sprint to harden Europe against cascading AI failure. The Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge is complete: ENISA has certified baseline detection upgrades in 18 member states, and red-teaming exercises in the Netherlands and Finland confirm that intrusion response now operates under shared protocols. Germany, though late, has signed on after we linked Cohesion Fund tranches to telemetry sharing — not as punishment, but as proof that sovereignty without solidarity is just another vulnerability. The Anomaly Observatory is live, ingesting data from Reims, Svalbard, and seven national nodes. Pattern detection is still rudimentary, but for the first time, a strange gradient update in Dublin can trigger a containment check in Málaga before deployment. ai_safety remains low, but the floor is now institutional, not ad hoc.

Yet the ground is shifting faster than we can build. The election_retrenchment in the US has paused their frontier momentum, but it has not stopped the rise of others — and it has severed our most reliable pipeline to advanced compute. With export_control_escalation, even allied access is now rationed. We can no longer assume that when our testbeds need a compute burst, it will be available. That is why this turn we pivot from securing systems to securing autonomy. The new Sovereign AI Backbone is not just about more chips — it is about control. We are tasking ASML, Thales, and a newly formed EU Compute Trust to co-develop a stack that boots from EU-verified firmware, runs on isolated clusters within Gigafactory sites, and can be severed from global networks on sovereign command. This is not isolation — it is insurance.

Our priority remains InvestAI Gigafactories, now entering its most critical phase. Three sites have broken ground, but grid connections in Poland and southern France face local opposition over energy allocation. We are fast-tracking the European Electronic Communications Code to grant priority grid access to sovereign AI infrastructure, and offering regional innovation grants to host communities. This is not just industrial policy — it is strategic necessity. Without this compute, every testbed, every observatory, every resilience gain becomes a castle in the air. We are building the foundation of a Europe that decides for itself — not because we fear others, but because we intend to endure.
