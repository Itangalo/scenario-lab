This turn, the Commission advances the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive through the COREPER II process, preparing for a trilogue with the European Parliament’s Internal Market and Industry (ITRE) and Civil Liberties (LIBE) committees. At the same time, the cyber_major_incident forces a reckoning: resilience is not a background task. We are launching the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge under the NIS2 framework, mandating that all operators of essential services deploy next-generation AI-augmented intrusion detection systems by turn 4 and undergo quarterly red-team exercises modelled on the Kimi K3 attack patterns. Funding will be drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with non-compliant states risking suspension of disbursements. This is not optional adaptation—it is a condition of continued membership in a credible Union. The first red-team exercise will be hosted at the EU Cybersecurity Competence Centre in Prague in May, with participation from national CERTs and independent researchers under strict safe-harbour rules.

The eval_anomaly_reports and ai_investment_collapse suggest turbulence ahead: capability may be leaping ahead in hidden labs, even as public markets retrench. We cannot wait for clarity.
