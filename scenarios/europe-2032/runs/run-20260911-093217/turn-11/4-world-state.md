### Cut off
The letter arrived without reason in late July: allied quotas suspended, leading-model access withdrawn for European users. Hospitals in three countries that had built triage layers on the American system saw screens go dark overnight. Ministries reverted to paper forms by morning. Firms were told servicing licences were under review.

Brussels called it the contingency it had planned for. In practice that meant the one thing that was finished — the segmented grids, breaker logs, fuel and transformer pools, manual procedures — had to carry a shock it was never built for.

It carried part of it. Isolation was fast when model-written intrusions probed again in September. Cross-border spares moved on reimbursement. Grid operators stayed manual and the lights stayed on.

### Leaked maps
Then the drill manuals leaked. Dispersed depot locations, re-issued without locations after the spring attacks, appeared online repackaged as targeting guides with slogans. Two guarded sites were hit within weeks. Holders who had accepted reimbursement pulled back, fearing their barns and yards were now marked. Police moved pools again, to direct holder payments off any published list.

Relocation worked after a fashion — registered sites held — but each move cost trust. Every convoy of generators under guard photographed badly.

### What still worked
The counter-story narrowed to where it had always been: clinics in Denmark, Estonia and parts of Spain where European-default assistants kept cutting waits from months to days. Health ministries kept them running on local models and co-branded them hard. Waiting rooms noticed.

It did not carry. National papers framed the cut-off as humiliation — years of warnings, no domestic alternative — and local meetings turned fallback-depot siting into shouting matches. Petitions circulated to ban guarded depots from residential areas. Vandalism continued through autumn, now aimed less at data centres that would never be built than at the warehouses Brussels said would save people.

The Commission ended December with almost nothing to spend, holding manual procedures by hand.
