### The sweep
It started as locked screens in town halls. By mid-February a ransomware wave built with automated tooling had crossed municipal billing, appointment systems and two regional grid service providers in five countries. Ransom notes were identical, decryption keys unreliable. Attribution statements hedged for months.

Defenders were visibly behind. The Essential Services Cyber Triage and Care Continuity Corps, proposed in response to the wave, remained half-staffed and still hiring through June. Its rotating triage teams arrived after the worst week. Where they arrived they helped: isolating networks, restoring from paper logs, keeping emergency departments on manual protocols. Where they had not yet arrived, mayors paid or rebuilt alone. No full effect could be claimed this turn; staffing and funding must build before continuity improves.

The lights largely stayed on. Segmentation and pooled spares again prevented cascade. But care backlogs lengthened and local press filmed guarded convoys moving transformers past graffiti calling the depots targets.

### A leap elsewhere
While Europe triaged by hand, an American lab demonstrated a sharp advance in autonomous research and long-horizon coding. Benchmarks from the week before were obsolete overnight. European firms under licence review watched via clips.

The timing sharpened the cut-off humiliation. Editorials paired the breakthrough with dark triage screens: the frontier moving on without Europe.

### Cures at a distance
Spring then brought tailored therapies for several hard cancers and a rare-disease treatment, developed on leading models abroad, offered to European hospitals under restrictive access terms and data conditions.

Health ministries in Denmark, Estonia and parts of Spain folded the news into their local-assistant story — waits still falling where European models ran. Nationally it landed differently: a cure Europeans could read about but not run themselves. Gratitude mixed with resentment, and depot referendums gathered signatures in three more cities.

Brussels ended June with a small corps half-staffed, holding continuity by hand while the frontier receded. Any political benefit from the new corps is deferred until staffing completes in a later turn.
