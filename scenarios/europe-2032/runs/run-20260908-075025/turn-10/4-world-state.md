### The Weight of Failure

Global AI capability advances to 68.5, as closed labs refine autonomous research agents capable of sustained hypothesis generation in materials science and networked cyber operations. The open frontier narrows the gap slightly—openweight_capability rises to 55.0—fueled by leaked fine-tuning data and distributed compute pooling, though still lagging behind the leading edge.

The EU formally completes the Transatlantic AI Safety Accord. A ceremonial signing takes place in Brussels, but the substance remains hollow: Washington grants no reciprocal evaluation access, citing national security protocols. Joint tabletop exercises occur, but data sharing is delayed. The accord delivers symbolic alignment, not operational trust.

Simultaneously, the automated decision scandal erupts in the Netherlands. A court rules that an AI-assisted welfare fraud system wrongfully denied benefits to over 2,000 people, citing opaque logic and broken appeal mechanisms. The judgment triggers outrage across northern Europe, reinforcing public perception of AI as a tool of administrative cruelty. Media coverage drowns out technical progress, and trust plummets.

In response, the EU doubles down on the AI Watch and Respond Framework, activating €420 million in conditional grants. ENISA begins integrating cyber, health, and border threat feeds into a prototype dashboard. However, only six member states appoint national coordinators by the deadline—others cite budget constraints or legal ambiguities. Progress is fragmented, and the system remains untested under real pressure.

The political cost is severe. The portfolio charge for the turn includes the completed Transatlantic Accord (−3 until finish), the new Watch and Respond Framework (−3), and the Open Safety Commons (−2), plus the priority cost (−1), totaling −9. The completed accord yields a +3 bonus. No proposal bonus applies—the scandal was internal, not external, and the new measure was already in flight. The scandal itself, of internal origin, costs −12 to political capital under rule 6. With public sentiment (23.0) below political capital (20.0), no legitimacy lending occurs. Net change: −8.

Ai_safety drops to 20.0 as capability outpaces safeguards and the scandal reveals systemic accountability failures. Resilience declines to 53.0—technical hardening is underway, but social absorption mechanisms are overwhelmed. Sovereignty falls to 24.0: capability rose by 1.0 (below the −1 threshold), but no category 4 completion and no access event occurred, leaving prior momentum flat.

Public sentiment collapses to 23.0, reflecting widespread anger and fear.
