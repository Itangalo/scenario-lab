## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: consolidating downward with ENISA’s municipal mandate and reaching outward through the joint threat response. On the first, we are finalising the delegated act under NIS3 to empower ENISA to set binding cybersecurity baselines for sub-national governments, with compliance tied to disbursement of Digital Europe Programme funds. Hungary, Slovakia, and Greece remain resistant, so we are pairing enforcement with incentive — fast-track access to InvestAI compute capacity and dedicated technical teams for early adopters. The legal base is Article 114 TFEU, justified by the internal market rationale of preventing cascading digital failures across borders. We are also integrating EIC-developed AI threat feeds into ENISA’s dashboard, piloting in Bologna and Wrocław, where the last wave hit hardest.

But the real shift this turn is external. The joint_threat_response pact — initiated by the Netherlands, Germany, Japan, and South Korea after a shared AI-driven supply chain intrusion — offers real-time telemetry sharing, pooled forensic analysis, and standing response protocols. We are fast-tracking Council Decision-making through COREPER II, using the cyber_defence_breakthrough in automated patching as justification for urgent adoption. Our added value is access to central European telemetry and InvestAI’s red-teaming capacity. This is not charity: we are negotiating binding reciprocity — full access only if all members commit to shared rules of engagement and data sovereignty standards. If we succeed, resilience jumps not through unilateral spending but through networked defence, and political capital rebounds because we are seen not just policing, but protecting.
