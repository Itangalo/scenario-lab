## In practice  
The cyber_major_incident — a self-propagating ransomware sweep through municipal services in three member states, exploiting an AI-generated zero-day in a widely used open-source dependency — has shifted the ground. Detection lagged by 36 hours; recovery will take weeks. This is not a test shot. We are invoking Article 122 TFEU to fast-track the core obligations of CS-CO: real-time telemetry, automated red-teaming, and deployment of EU-certified detection stacks in energy, water, and digital infrastructure. ENISA is being empowered to designate operators and enforce compliance within 90 days, with emergency funding of €8bn drawn from the RRF and the Digital Europe Programme. Germany, France, and the Nordic states have signalled support; we are offering fast-track access to the InvestAI Gigafactories grid commitments for southern member states that accept early adoption. This is not ideal — we wanted a negotiated directive — but the incident has made it necessary.

Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package advances through Competitiveness Council, with revised terms offering modular access to accelerated-permitting zones based on national contributions to cross-border grid resilience. We are also deepening talks with ASML and Intel Europa on co-location of chip assembly at two Gigafactory sites — Finland and Spain — to anchor not just compute but fabrication within EU jurisdiction. The export_control_escalation means we cannot rely on frontier access from abroad; every watt and wafer must be treated as strategic. The InvestAI sites are no longer just about scale — they are becoming the backbone of a sovereign stack, from silicon to software. But if we cannot protect what we have, no amount of compute will save us. That is why CS-CO, now emergency-activated, is our focus: resilience is the precondition of sovereignty.
