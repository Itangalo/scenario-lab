### The Breach and the Response  
A self-propagating ransomware wave, generated by advanced AI models, cripples municipal services in Italy, Belgium, and Poland. The attack exploits a zero-day in a widely used open-source library, evading detection for over a day. Critical systems stagger, and recovery timelines stretch into weeks. Public trust frays as officials admit the EU’s defences were outpaced.

In response, the Commission invokes Article 122 TFEU to emergency-activate the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO), bypassing stalled negotiations. Mandates for real-time telemetry and certified detection stacks are rolled out in energy, water, and digital infrastructure sectors. ENISA is empowered to enforce compliance within 90 days, backed by €8bn in emergency funding. Germany, France, and Nordic states comply swiftly. Southern members, however, resist — citing cost and sovereignty concerns — despite offers of priority access to InvestAI Gigafactory resources.

### Fragmentation and Fallout  
The emergency measure yields rapid resilience gains, but at a steep political cost. The Tech Sovereignty Package advances slowly through the Competitiveness Council, with only modest progress on cross-border grid commitments. Talks with ASML and Intel Europa on chip co-location in Finland and Spain continue, but no final agreements are signed.

Meanwhile, the US tightens export controls further, restricting access to advanced AI chips and frontier models even for allied partners. This `export_control_escalation` event directly undermines EU access to critical capacity, compounding the sovereignty deficit.

With `ai_capability` rising by 2.0 — driven by autonomous agent development — and no safety measures fully implemented, `ai_safety` plummets due to growing gaps in oversight. The cyber incident, combined with visible fragmentation in the EU’s response, sours public sentiment. Media coverage focuses on unpreparedness and infighting, eroding confidence in AI governance.

The emergency activation of CS-CO provides a much-needed boost to resilience, but the political strain of fast-tracking a major measure without consensus drains `eu_political_capital`. The portfolio remains heavy, and the emergency move fails to unite member states.
