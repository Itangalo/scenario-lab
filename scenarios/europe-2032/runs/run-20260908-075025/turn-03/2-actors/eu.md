## New measure  
**Strengthen ENISA’s Mandate and Expand CS-CO to Municipalities**  
Empower ENISA to enforce minimum cyber resilience baselines across all levels of government infrastructure, including municipalities, and integrate AI-generated threat intelligence into real-time defence systems.  

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect: resilience +10, ai_safety +2 (by closing lower-tier attack surfaces and enabling faster response to AI-synthesized threats)`

## Priority  
**Strengthen ENISA’s Mandate and Expand CS-CO to Municipalities** — because the cyber_major_incident exposed that the weakest links are no longer in core infrastructure but in decentralized, under-resourced public services, and because without extending the shield downward, the gains from the emergency activation will be undermined by the next wave of AI-driven attacks.

## In practice  
The emergency activation of CS-CO succeeded in getting core critical infrastructure under telemetry and detection in Germany, France, and the Nordics — but it left the municipal layer exposed. The ransomware wave hit hardest in mid-sized cities and regional administrations, where patching discipline, staffing, and monitoring capacity collapsed under the speed of AI-generated exploits. We now know that the next incident will not wait for consensus: the tooling is loose, and the attack surface is fractal. So we are acting to extend the shield downward.

We are proposing a regulation under the NIS3 framework to formally empower ENISA to set and enforce minimum cyber resilience baselines for all government-operated digital services, including municipalities, with phased compliance tied to access to EU digital modernisation funds. This will be backed by a €4bn reinforcement of the Digital Europe Programme to cover detection stack deployment and red-teaming for sub-national operators. Crucially, we are integrating AI threat intelligence feeds — developed in partnership with EIC and CERT-EU — into ENISA’s monitoring dashboard, enabling near-real-time response to model-generated zero-days.

Resistance remains in southern and eastern member states, who argue that cost and sovereignty cannot be overridden indefinitely by emergency logic. We are offering a compromise: fast-track access to InvestAI Gigafactory grid capacity and technical support teams for those that commit to full compliance by mid-2028. But we cannot afford another six months of negotiation. The attack surface is growing faster than our consensus. This is no longer about protecting systems — it is about preserving the credibility of the Union’s capacity to act at all.
