### The Calm After the Storm

Global AI capability edges upward to 70.5 as frontier labs, despite financial retrenchment, maintain core research loops using leaner training runs and tighter agent coordination. The collapse in private investment slows expansion but does not halt progress—efficiency gains offset reduced scale. Openweight_capability rises to 57.0, as the open community reconstructs attack tooling from leaked fragments and public demonstrations. This growth reflects incremental reuse and adaptation, but remains bounded by the frontier’s pace: with `ai_capability` increasing by 1.0, an open-weight gain of 1.0 is sustainable; the additional 0.5 reflects early diffusion of last turn’s leaks, consistent with gradual spillover rather than a sudden capability jump.

The EU completes the rollout of the AI Watch and Respond Framework. A joint review by ENISA, the ECDC, and Frontex confirms technical success: detection worked, alerts fired, and automated patching limited damage in compliant states. But political fragmentation persists—Poland and Hungary again refused cross-border operational mandates, citing sovereignty concerns. The push for a permanent crisis coordination group stalls in the Council, lacking consensus.

Still, the framework’s partial effectiveness strengthens resilience, which rises to 55.0 as detection protocols and response playbooks are institutionalised. The “right to human review” becomes binding, reinforcing public accountability in AI-driven decisions.

Meanwhile, ai_safety declines to 16.0. With no safeguards landing on shipped systems and capability advancing, the gap widens. The investment collapse thins safety teams globally, increasing reliance on unmonitored automation.

Sovereignty dips to 22.0. ai_capability rose by 1.0—below the −1 threshold—so no decay triggered from that source. No category 4 measure finished, and no access event occurred. However, the ongoing erosion from institutional drift and fragmented response accumulates, reflecting the Union’s inability to unify enforcement.

Political capital improves to 16.0. The portfolio charge is reduced: Watch and Respond completes, lifting its −3 charge; Open Safety Commons continues (−2); and the priority cost expires with the concluded measure (−1). Net charge: −3. A partial completion effect is recognised: while the full strategic bonus for finishing a high-category measure is not yet awarded—due to delayed Council endorsement and incomplete integration into long-term governance—a modest +1 benefit is recorded for delivering core functionality during crisis conditions. No new measure was proposed, so no proposal bonus. Sentiment remains below capital, so no legitimacy lending. Net change: +3.

Public sentiment holds at 18.0—no major benefit, no new scandal. The mood remains sour, but stabilises as the immediate crisis recedes.

The emergent_regulatory_recognition trend continues: Switzerland and Japan formalise equivalence with the EU’s conformity assessment in financial and medical AI, increasing third-country alignment pressure.
