### Accelerating Capability, Fragmented Response  
The frontier continues its advance, with `ai_capability` rising to 54.0 as training runs deliver incremental but compounding gains in agent autonomy and cross-domain reasoning. The release of Kimi K3 and its rapid deployment in offensive cyber operations foreshadowed the `cyber_test_shot`, a coordinated intrusion into critical infrastructure across three continents. Though not catastrophic, the breach exposed systemic detection failures — particularly in energy and water systems — and confirmed that AI-enabled threats now operate beyond human monitoring timelines.

In response, the EU proposes **Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)**, a large category 6 measure aimed at mandating detection standards and red-teaming across essential services. The proposal gains political traction, especially among northern states, but faces resistance from southern members wary of cost and centralisation. To secure consensus, the Commission offers 80% co-financing and joint procurement, but no final agreement is reached this turn. The measure remains in negotiation, with full adoption deferred to a future period. Due to the lack of implementation, no resilience gains are locked in beyond early planning effects, and no political capital is yet spent on this initiative.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech Sovereignty Package** remain in flight, with site assessments underway and permitting frameworks under negotiation. However, no immediate sovereignty gains materialise, and `ai_capability`’s rise by 2.0 triggers a −1 to `eu_ai_sovereignty`. The **export_control_escalation** event further strains access: the US tightens model and chip controls, granting only limited allied exceptions, effectively rationing frontier access. This constitutes an `export_control_escalation` event under rule 5, costing −2 to sovereignty — the largest capacity-related event this turn.

With `ai_capability` advancing and no safety measures landing on shipped systems, `ai_safety` drops by 2. Resilience sees a marginal +1 from preparatory work on CS-CO, but full effects await implementation. The portfolio’s political cost remains unchanged: only existing measures are carried forward, and no new measure has been formally adopted, so no additional political capital is expended. Public sentiment holds steady, with no major AI benefits or scandals.

`openweight_capability` increases by only +1.0, reflecting continued incremental progress in open-weight model development and deployment, consistent with accumulated gains and existing infrastructure. The leap to +7.0 was inconsistent with the gradual trajectory of this metric in the absence of a breakthrough release or major open deployment, which did not occur.
