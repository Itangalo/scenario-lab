## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: building our own capacity and hardening what we depend on. The InvestAI Gigafactories are being scoped with DG Connect and EIB, identifying four anchor sites — in Finland, Spain, Poland and France — where land, grid access and cooling allow gigawatt-scale deployment by 2031. We are negotiating with ASML and Intel Europa to co-locate chip assembly, leveraging our supply-chain leverage to bind compute to territory. The Tech sovereignty package is advancing through the Council’s Competitiveness formation, but resistance remains in smaller member states fearing exclusion; we are offering tiered access to the accelerated-permitting zones in exchange for cross-border grid commitments.

The cyber_test_shot changes the urgency. We are drafting a Directive under Article 114 TFEU, to be proposed in December, that mandates real-time telemetry, automated red-teaming and EU-certified detection stacks for all operators of essential services in energy, water, transport and digital infrastructure. ENISA will be empowered to audit and enforce, with funding drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and an emergency allocation of €12bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility. Germany and the Nordic states support it, but some southern member states warn of cost and sovereignty; we are offering 80% co-financing and joint procurement of detection tooling to overcome resistance. This is not just defence — it is the foundation of trust that allows sovereign AI adoption to proceed without public revolt.
