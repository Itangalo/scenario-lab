The commitment must now reflect that sovereignty means not just owning the stack, but being able to run it without blind spots — control that functions even when reasoning is not legible. This is not a reversal, but a necessary refinement under the same strategic imperative.

## Priority
**Launch EU Sovereign Agent Framework Initiative** — because despite the promising emergence of open safety tools, the core failure remains: we cannot see into the reasoning of the systems now driving attacks, and the only way to ensure continuity of control is to build our own stack from the metal up, with hardware-enforced sandboxing and goal stability verification that does not depend on legibility.

## In practice
We are treating the completion of the Sovereign Agent Framework Initiative’s launch as a milestone, not an endpoint. The €45bn reallocation from InvestAI is now legally ring-fenced under a delegated act of the Digital Europe Programme, with disbursement tied to quarterly TRL progression reviews by a joint board of DG Connect, EuroHPC JU, and national AI coordinators. The first contracts go to Atos, Siemens, and a consortium of Fraunhofer, INRIA, and CNR to co-develop the runtime monitoring layer, with strict clauses requiring hardware-level enforcement of sandboxing and third-party auditability of control mechanisms. Hungary, Slovakia, and Greece remain resistant, but we are offering them opt-in participation in the agent framework’s municipal pilot layer — with full access to threat feeds and red-teaming support — in exchange for dropping their CJEU references. This is not concession, but containment: we cannot afford fragmentation when the next attack may exploit agent persistence.

At the same time, we are acting on the emergent_open_safety_tools event not as a distraction, but as a signal. The grassroots coalition behind the open-source monitoring suite has demonstrated that public-interest engineering can move faster than bureaucracy. Through the new Open Safety Commons Programme, we will provide €1.2bn in grants and cloud compute access via GAIA-X to scale their tools into a distributed safety layer, with formal integration pathways into ENISA’s threat intelligence pipeline. This is not a substitute for sovereign capacity — it cannot stop a determined actor — but it is a force multiplier for resilience, especially in mid-tier institutions now exposed by the ransomware wave. We are embedding this within the existing public technical capacity framework, not as a standalone agency, to avoid duplication and ensure interoperability with the sovereign agent stack. The programme will be governed by a new Open Safety Board under CEN-CENELEC, with seats for civil society and independent researchers, ensuring transparency without compromising security. This is how we act under uncertainty: by reinforcing both the core and the edges of our defence.
