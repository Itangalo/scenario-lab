## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity to anchor European agency in a world of distributed superintelligence and begin operationalising it through public-sector integration and resilient adoption

- Grounds: The emergent labour adaptation pilot demonstrates that diffusion of AI into high-skill professions is not only feasible but accelerating outside EU coordination, creating both opportunity and urgency. To hold coherence, sovereignty must now extend beyond compute and access to include operational integration in trusted public functions. The original commitment remains valid but insufficient; it did not anticipate that sovereign capability would begin to leak through uncoordinated national adoption before central control was established. This turn, we pivot from securing capacity to ensuring it serves a unified standard of control, safety, and public legitimacy — or risk building a sovereign stack that no member state uses.

## New measure
**Launch the European AI Co-Pilot Adoption Programme**  
Scale the emergent German-Dutch-Danish vocational pilot into a formal EU-wide certification and deployment framework for AI co-pilots in regulated professions, integrating safety monitoring, audit rights, and interoperability standards.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` public_sentiment +6 (by reframing AI as augmentation in trusted roles), resilience +3 (through structured adoption in critical sectors), eu_ai_sovereignty +2 (by tying certified systems to EU-controlled stacks)  
`Applies to:` Member states, national vocational authorities, regulated professions (healthcare, legal, engineering), EU Digital Innovation Hubs, EURES, and national labour ministries

## In practice
We are treating the finalisation of the Sovereign Agent Framework as a make-or-break moment for EU agency. DG Connect envoys are in Budapest, Bratislava, and Athens with a binding offer: full opt-in to the municipal threat feed integration by the end of this quarter, in exchange for guaranteed access to GAIA-X subsidies, national recovery funds, and a permanent seat on the joint audit board. The runtime monitoring developed by Atos and Fraunhofer has cleared TRL6; we are mandating its deployment in all Tier 1 agents funded under EU programmes. Legal services confirm that while Article 12 of the DMA remains contested, its activation as a conditionality lever for state aid is defensible — and we are invoking it. This is not a threat but a threshold: sovereign capability means nothing if it is not uniformly enforced.

Simultaneously, we are responding to the openweight_frontier_release not with restriction but with direction. The new measure — the European AI Co-Pilot Adoption Programme — will be proposed to the Education, Youth, Culture and Sport Council in June, co-funded through Erasmus+ and the Social Climate Fund. It tasks Cedefop with scaling the German-Dutch-Danish pilot into a pan-European certification standard for AI co-pilots in regulated professions, requiring real-time logging, human-in-the-loop verification, and post-hoc auditability. We are offering member states fast-track access to EU retraining co-financing if they adopt the standard by turn 10. This is not about catching up — it is about shaping how AI enters the bloodstream of European labour before it becomes a foreign-controlled fait accompli.
