## In practice  
We are conducting a full after-action review of the AI Watch and Respond Framework’s performance during the bio_incident, co-led by ENISA, the ECDC, and Frontex, with observer status from the European Parliament’s LIBE and ENVI committees. The technical layer worked: anomaly detection flagged unusual genomic synthesis patterns in commercial lab orders within 48 hours; automated alerts triggered in six member states; and patching protocols were deployed to restrict access to high-risk sequences. But the political layer failed. Poland and Hungary again refused activation of cross-border enforcement mandates, citing national jurisdiction over health infrastructure and biosafety. As a result, containment lagged by over a week in key transit zones, and the agent spread undetected through secondary supply chains. We are now pushing for a binding Council decision under Article 114 TFEU, extended via the solidarity clause (Article 222), to establish a permanent AI Crisis Coordination Cell with standing authority to activate response protocols across all domains—cyber, bio, critical infrastructure—without requiring ad hoc consensus during an event.

Behind the scenes, we are leveraging the bio_incident to reframe the narrative around sovereignty and safety. The European Public Prosecutor’s Office has opened a formal investigation into the software supply chain that allowed the design tools to be repurposed, focusing on third-party validation failures and inadequate access controls at a Swiss-based cloud provider used by a now-defunct US lab. We are coordinating with Switzerland and Japan—both of whom recently recognised EU conformity assessments—to demand mutual accountability in frontier model deployment. At the same time, we are accelerating internal planning for a potential compute grab: early intelligence suggests that two major European-trained research leads at a collapsed Bay Area lab are open to relocation, and a mid-sized GPU cluster in Ireland, currently under US corporate control, may be available for restructuring under EU legal anchor. But we will not move until capital allows. With public_sentiment at 18.0 and political_capital at 16.0, any new structural measure risks overreach. We are instead consolidating the gains of Watch and Respond, ensuring its technical infrastructure becomes the backbone of future resilience, and preparing the legal and institutional ground for a sovereignty play—when the moment comes.
