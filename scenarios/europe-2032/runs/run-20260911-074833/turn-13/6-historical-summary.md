Autumn 2032 brought engineered illness and great-power diplomacy together. A machine-designed modified pathogen released far abroad spread via travel hubs; three member states triaged fever cases while pooled tailored countermeasures were deployed. Containment held for weeks, but stocks again counted in weeks and schools closed locally.

Washington and Beijing reached a limited understanding on bio-design tools and weights security with thin verification. Brussels, lacking frontier models and funds, bid as implementer — offering detection data, evaluation work, aligned export controls. It half-succeeded: labs joined sample-sharing and patch-evaluation, input lists held, but full accession was deferred and Europeans were briefed after sessions.

Shelter lifelines paid: dispersed registries stayed up, reserve engineers held grid and landing stations, machine-speed patching stopped hospital cascades. Voters saw continuity but feared the next vial.

The second capital kept its separate supply deal in exchange for quiet cohesion assurances, further weakening joint bargaining. By December fear of engineered disease dominated and trust in machine-mediated care fell again.
