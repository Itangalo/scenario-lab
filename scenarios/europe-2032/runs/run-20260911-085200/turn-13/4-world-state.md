### Holding the seat
July to December 2032 was a holding operation. No new hospital clusters were confirmed, and health ministries completed the handover of cordon routines to ordinary hospital budgets. Paper wards thinned out, isolation beds stayed funded, and cross-border transfers continued as routine rather than emergency.

The final cheques under the Displacement Income and Retraining Bridge went out through employment offices and the social fund. For care assistants and municipal staff in the two worst-hit states, the money and retraining places eased the worst anger. Walkouts shrank to local disputes. It did not restore trust in automated systems, but it stopped the slide.

Abroad, little moved. EU envoys stayed in the room in Geneva and with supply-chain holders, pooling export-licence offers and evaluation capacity in exchange for a verification role and firmer access terms to the US-China risk pact. The channel stayed open; no quota, price, or verification commitment came back. On synthesis screening, larger European vendors took EU funds and applied screening, while smaller and offshore vendors continued to stall. Procurement stayed conditional on screened supply, which slowed some purchases without closing the gap.

A trade newsletter and one German paper carried single-source claims that offshore providers were openly advertising unscreened synthesis to buyers who had read the Geneva inquiry. No enforcement case was brought, and the Commission declined to comment beyond restating the procurement rule.

The solo hyperscaler contract remained outside common logging and reimbursement. The Commission held the line, but the carve-out continued to undercut its credibility in both Geneva and national capitals.

With no binding win to show, standing at home barely recovered. Care endured on regular budgets; leverage did not follow.
