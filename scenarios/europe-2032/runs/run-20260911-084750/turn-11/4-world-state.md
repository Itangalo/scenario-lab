### Holding the line
Through autumn the Union's cyber recovery corps became the face of Europe in dozens of town halls and hospital basements. Three mobile teams shuttled between the worst-hit municipalities, carrying vendor patches bought in advance and dog-eared offline checklists. Daily isolation notes from the crisis network told operators when to disconnect and when to reconnect. Water and emergency wards largely stayed up. Everything else ran slow: bookings, payroll, building controls, grid-maintenance portals.

It was visible, and it was not enough. Intruders were gone but the bills remained, and attribution never arrived. Mayors queued for a single claims window covering both cyber cleanup and energy relief, and left with fractions.

### Borrowed cures, foreign hands
In October, tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions reached ordinary clinics — designed with American models, on American compute, licensed through tiered queues. Health ministries rushed to procure them, and patients were grateful. Editorials were less so: even the cure, they wrote, arrives on borrowed infrastructure.

At the same time warehouses and logistics yards changed shape. Cheap sorting, palletising and welding robots, most built in China and driven by American control software, spread through ports from Rotterdam to Gdansk. Jobs for pickers and junior logistics planners vanished in months. Repair, care and construction-site work stayed stubbornly human. The army took the same machines for resupply and perimeter patrol, not for targeting. For European manufacturers, the message was brutal: the automation lands on industries Europe still leads, and none of the stack is European.

Technicians kept using freely available models on local servers to triage logs and rewrite scripts — help no authority could recall. Graduate protests outside job fairs merged with anger over dark suburbs and curtailed computing. Faint reports spoke of coordinated blockades at data-centre fences and utility strikes discussed in municipal chats, still rumour, still single-source, but growing louder.

By December services stood, degraded. Trust did not.
