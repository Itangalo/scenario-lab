By mid-2031 a continent-wide ransomware wave built with automated tooling hit municipalities, regional hospitals and a widely used software component, locking booking, payroll, building and grid-maintenance systems to paper. Intruders had dwelled for weeks; attribution lagged. Union cyber teams with pre-paid patches and offline procedures kept early-arrival water plants and emergency departments islanded, with daily isolation notes.

During cleanup a US lab demonstrated a sharp advance in code, maths and intrusion automation, testable but not reproducible in Europe. Export licensing tightened further into country-tier rationing — smaller volumes, longer queues, higher prices, mid-negotiation cuts — while already-local open models filled gaps for log triage and scripts beyond recall.

Entry-level hiring in routine coding, analysis and support failed to recover after spring as firms used AI for first drafts with only senior review, sparking graduate protests and transition-fund talk. Prior winter kits softened impact: hospitals re-islanded, homes/hospitals protected with large compute curtailed first. By June 2031 services ran degraded-standing, but voters remembered bills, November dark suburbs and borrowed dependence on foreign models, chips and patches, deepening distrust.
