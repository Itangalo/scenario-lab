## Priority

## In practice
We are acting on two simultaneous failures: we did not see the last breach in time, and when it came, we could not stop it. The Mandatory Incident Reporting Network remains essential — without visibility, no defence holds — but this turn’s events proved it is not enough. A coordinated ransomware sweep, generated by fine-tuned offensive models and targeting hospital systems in three member states, took down emergency dispatches for hours. Backup systems were compromised through poisoned software dependencies. This was not just an outage; it was a demonstration of how fast AI-enabled attacks can exploit systemic interdependence. We cannot assume defenders will keep pace. We must assume, instead, that they will be overwhelmed — and build accordingly.

So we are launching **Cyber Shield for Critical Services**, a category 6 measure under Article 4 of the NIS2 Directive and funded through the Digital Europe Programme and urgent拨款 from the Crisis Response Instrument. Over the next four turns, we will mandate that all operators of essential services implement three layers: first, AI-powered intrusion detection trained specifically on synthetic attack patterns generated by the AI Assurance Agency; second, hardened fallback protocols — including manual overrides and physically isolated control channels — that activate automatically upon anomaly thresholds; third, dedicated rapid-response cells co-staffed by ENISA, national cyber units, and vetted private experts, placed on rotational alert with pre-authorised escalation paths. To overcome resistance from under-resourced municipalities and mid-tier providers, we are offering full cost coverage for implementation and liability shielding for good-faith participation. The US labs may restrict access, and openweight models may spread — but if the lights stay on, the Union holds. This is not about catching every threat. It is about ensuring that when we fail, the failure does not cascade.
