In 2032, a surge of AI-driven threats further destabilized European systems. AI-powered ransomware crippled hospital infrastructure across three member states, while synthetic media campaigns disrupted electoral processes in two others, exposing critical gaps in both cybersecurity and democratic resilience. In response, the EU accelerated **Cyber Shield for Critical Services**, aiming to deploy AI-driven detection and rapid-response units, but progress remained hampered by procurement delays, staffing shortages, and unresolved liability disputes. The **Mandatory Incident Reporting and Threat Sharing Network** concluded with high reporting compliance but no reduction in attack success, underscoring the limits of visibility without enforcement.

A new initiative, **Emergency Detection and Response for Synthetic Election Interference**, was launched to counter AI-generated disinformation, though full deployment is delayed until 2034 and faces resistance over civil liberties concerns. Public trust hit a nadir following the unemployment benefits scandal and ongoing fears of election manipulation, fueling protests against AI expansion.

Global AI capability rose to 73.5, with openweight models reaching 61.5, increasingly incorporating cyber-offensive functionalities. AI safety remained stagnant at 10.0, with no new safeguards on frontier systems. Resilience increased slightly to 43.0 due to partial planning gains, while sovereignty and political capital continued to decline amid widening capability gaps and eroding public confidence. Systemic risks from AI-driven disinformation and infrastructure attacks intensified, signaling deepening vulnerability.
