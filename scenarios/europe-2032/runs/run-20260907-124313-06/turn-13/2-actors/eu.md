## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Services** — because the cyber_major_incident this turn revealed that our critical systems are not just vulnerable, but structurally unprepared for AI-driven attacks that move faster than human response cycles, and because resilience is now the only buffer between accelerating capability and cascading societal failure.

## In practice
We are acting on two simultaneous failures: we did not see the last breach in time, and when it came, we could not stop it. The **Mandatory Incident Reporting and Threat Sharing Network** has now concluded its run. It delivered baseline visibility — over 80% of critical operators now report incidents within four hours — but this turn proved that visibility without resilience is ritual, not defence. The ransomware sweep through hospital systems exploited poisoned dependencies that had lain dormant for months, and backup systems were compromised because isolation was procedural, not physical. The attack was AI-generated, AI-coordinated, and moved faster than any human-led response could match.

So we are doubling down on **Cyber Shield for Critical Services**, our category 6 emergency programme. Under Article 4 of the NIS2 Directive and funded through the Digital Europe Programme and urgent拨款 from the Crisis Response Instrument, we are mandating three layers of protection across all essential services. First, AI-powered intrusion detection systems trained on synthetic attack patterns generated by the AI Assurance Agency — these will be deployed via ENISA-certified vendors with open evaluation logs. Second, hardened fallback protocols, including manual overrides and air-gapped control channels, that activate automatically when anomaly thresholds are breached. Third, dedicated rapid-response cells co-staffed by national CSIRTs, ENISA, and vetted private experts, placed on rotational alert with pre-authorised escalation paths to isolate compromised systems. To overcome resistance from under-resourced municipalities and mid-tier providers, we are offering full cost coverage and liability shielding for good-faith participation. The US labs may restrict access, and openweight models may spread — but if the lights stay on, the Union holds.

At the same time, the **emergent_synthetic_election_interference** event forces us to act beyond infrastructure. In two member states, deepfake videos and AI-generated smear campaigns overwhelmed local fact-checking capacity, distorting regional elections and triggering emergency debates. While Cyber Shield is our priority, we cannot allow democratic processes to become the next frontier of unmitigated AI harm. So we are launching a new measure: **Emergency Detection and Response for Synthetic Election Interference**. This will deploy AI detection tools trained to identify synthetic media at scale, integrate them into national electoral monitoring systems, and establish rapid-response teams with authority to issue public alerts and coordinate takedowns with platforms. It will be piloted in the two affected states and scaled to high-risk regions by turn 16. This is not about perfect detection — it is about ensuring that when democracy is attacked, the response is not months of investigation, but minutes of action.
