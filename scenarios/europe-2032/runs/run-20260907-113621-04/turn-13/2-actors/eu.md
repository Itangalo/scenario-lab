The Open Science Resilience Initiative’s auditable models exist, but they sit idle. We have the knowledge, but not the power to deploy it. The two mandates have not just converged — they have been subsumed by a single, immediate condition: operational continuity under AI-driven systemic crisis.

We were right to act under uncertainty, but we underestimated the speed at which capability outruns legal and physical infrastructure. The commitment now demands not just urgency, but forcible execution — not because we enjoy overreach, but because the alternative is irrelevance.

The commitment must now include physical, energy, and network autonomy — not as enhancements, but as prerequisites. We are not building capacity; we are building bunkers.

## In practice  
This turn, we are shifting from infrastructure to survival architecture. The cyber_major_incident — which disabled emergency services in three member states for over 48 hours — was not a wake-up call. It was a demonstration of our extinction condition. We have the models. We have the plans. But we do not have the power to run them when it matters. That ends now.

We are re-tasking the Marguerite II Fund to finance sovereign AI bunkers: hardened underground or repurposed military facilities equipped with modular, liquid-cooled GPU clusters, on-site fusion-adjacent microreactors, and quantum-encrypted, satellite-linked network backbones. These are not data centres — they are fail-deadly nodes designed to cold-start from tape backups when all else fails. DG DEFIS, the European Defence Agency, and ENISA are jointly overseeing design, with construction to begin immediately in Bavaria, Wallonia, and Central Greece — sites already under legal dispute, where precedent must be set. Two new sites in Finland and Northern Portugal will be added to ensure geographic redundancy.

To secure buy-in, we are offering host regions a guaranteed 30% of bunker compute for local public services, plus priority access during crises. We are also invoking Article 122 TFEU to override national grid authorities: if they will not grant dispatch priority, we will build off-grid. The Just Compute Transition Facility will now include energy resilience grants — regions that host bunkers get first access to EU-developed microgrid tech. This is not negotiation. It is triage. If the Union cannot run its own systems when the world’s are runaway, it does not exist. We are building the first places where it still can.
