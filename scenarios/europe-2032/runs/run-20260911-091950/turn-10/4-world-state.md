### Queues held, ground lost
January to June 2031 was a holding operation, and Brussels said so openly. With no money to spend and courts blocking new builds, the Commission let the pooled-access pact run its course, sent energy and legal teams to negotiate water-and-power compacts council by council, and kept hospitals on patched domestic systems.

Where those triage helpers worked, the line held. A few regions reported shorter waits for scans and first specialist visits, and joint press conferences claimed the credit. Clinicians still called the tools slow, locum funding was gone, but wards stayed open.

The diggers did not restart. The two frozen factory sites stayed under tape and vigils through spring, with technical referees haggling over capped water envelopes, grid sequencing and health earmarks. No injunction was challenged. The capital with its separate foreign supply kept it. Washington answered demarches with sympathy and no date.

Two shocks from outside made the pause costlier.

First, a contested biosecurity paper claimed a genome model had helped sketch a viable human-infecting design. Specialists quarrelled over methods and over publishing at all, but health agencies took notice. Hospitals received hygiene checklists, labs got guidance notes, and monitoring of model use was quietly widened under existing mandates. Nothing new was funded.

Second, a freely downloadable release landed almost at the closed frontier. Hundreds of thousands of copies spread in days. Municipal IT officers and university labs could now run what only a few labs could run months before, on their own hardware, beyond recall. Guidance told them to be careful; it could not take the weights back.

By June the compacts were drafts, not lifts. Cohesion survived — no second capital broke ranks — but sovereignty did not advance, safety assurance slipped further behind capability, and the public mood stayed sour, brightened only where queues had actually shortened.
