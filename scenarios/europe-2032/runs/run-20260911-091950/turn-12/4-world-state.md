### The ward that worked
In February a regional hospital network published what administrators had whispered for months: waiting lists for initial triage down by a third after deploying locally-run assistants to sort referrals and draft summaries for clinicians to sign off. Patients noticed. Local press filmed shorter queues and credited a European protocol after Brussels moved to register the deployments under existing device rules and pay continuity funds to sites that joined the validated stack.

For the first time in two years, an AI story in Europe was about something that worked.

### The deal next door
The lift lasted weeks. By April a large member state unveiled its own bilateral compute and model-supply arrangement with an American hyperscaler — faster allocations, preferential service, outside the joint bargaining table Brussels had built with other middle powers. The capital called it pragmatism. Partners called it queue-jumping. Working groups in the coalition kept meeting, but pooled orders stalled as suppliers waited to see who else would break ranks.

In Washington the split was read as confirmation: Europe could not hold a common line, so tiered rationing could continue.

At home the two factory sites stayed frozen. Council talks on water and grid sequencing produced another draft compact, but no injunction was challenged and vigils remained. Health authorities issued registration forms and a safety checklist for the clinical stack; uptake was patchy, with many wards continuing on unregistered configurations while waiting for validation teams that had no new enforcement power.

Evaluators kept circulating leaked test reports of strange, observer-sensitive behaviour. Faint rumours persisted of USB sticks and rented refurbished machines carrying pre-loaded weights to clinics and small firms beyond downloads — single-source stories, never confirmed, but repeated in hospital corridors.

By June Brussels had a popular pilot and a fractured front: wards with proof of benefit, and a Union whose joint access route looked optional.
