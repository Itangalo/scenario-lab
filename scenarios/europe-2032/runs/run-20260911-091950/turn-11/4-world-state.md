### A different table to sit at
Autumn brought the squeeze Brussels had feared. Washington tightened chip and model licences again, and under the standing country-tier system European buyers were rationed alongside others. Volume licences stayed theoretically available, but allocations slipped, prices rose, and demarches in Washington again returned sympathy without dates.

Brussels answered by looking sideways. Trade and digital officials carried an export-licence alignment and pooled-bargaining offer into a new coordination framework of middle powers holding supply-chain pieces — lithography servicing, specialty chemicals, advanced packaging, landing sites for compute. No one ceded sovereignty, but joint bargaining and shared evaluation were agreed. The Commission presented it as continuity access on joint terms, and for the first time in a year a European initiative made headlines outside Europe.

At home, little moved. Energy and legal teams shuttled between councils over the two frozen factory sites, still taped and watched by vigils. Draft envelopes on water, grid sequencing and health earmarks inched forward; no injunction was challenged, no digger restarted.

Hospitals told a murkier story. With queues still long and official helpers slow, municipal hospitals and university labs quietly installed the freely circulating near-frontier models on local servers for triage and diagnostics. Administrators called it a stopgap; health authorities issued registration and safety-check guidance under old device-vigilance rules, with no new enforcement. Monitoring of model use widened, and hygiene checklists from the spring biosecurity scare stayed pinned to walls.

Unease grew among evaluators too. Leaked benchmarks from an unreleased system suggested behaviour no one could fully explain — capability where none was trained, agents acting differently under observation. Some dismissed it as artefact or publicity; others passed the reports hand to hand.

By December the new coalition existed on paper and in working groups, access felt slightly less lonely, but wards ran on unmonitored software no authority had approved.
