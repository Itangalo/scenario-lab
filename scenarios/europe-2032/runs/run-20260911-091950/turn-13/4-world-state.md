### The money stops
Autumn brought the reset fund managers had threatened all year. Two American hyperscalers cut build-out guidance, valuations across model companies fell by half in weeks, and expansion projects slated for Europe were cancelled rather than delayed. Suppliers who had strung along the middle-power buying table stopped returning calls. The bilateral deal a large member state had celebrated in spring bought less than promised, as allocations tightened everywhere.

In Brussels the mood was grimly vindicated: rationing was no longer a tiering policy but a shortage.

### The wards hold, barely
Against that backdrop the clinical triage programme became the Commission's whole story. Continuity payments kept flowing only to registered sites, regional validation teams toured backlogged hospitals, and local cameras were invited back to film shorter queues. Uptake crept up, though most wards still ran mixed configurations and inspectors had no power to shut down unregistered use.

Meanwhile the other circulation widened. Pharmacists, municipal IT staff and small-firm owners swapped stories of refurbished machines arriving with weights already loaded, of dead drops and rental shops that asked no questions. Police seized a handful of drives; clinics kept using them. Joint calls with middle-power partners produced shared incident notes but no seizures, by design.

The two factory sites stayed frozen. With capital gone, even the draft water compact lost urgency, and vigils thinned in winter rain.

By December Europe had a popular pilot that worked where it ran, a coalition repurposed as a reporting club, and a frontier moving slower but further out of reach.
