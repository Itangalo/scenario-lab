### Holding the line
July to December 2031 was a semester of maintenance, not recovery. With no new money voted, Brussels kept the Fallback Corps as its single funded instrument: joint health and civil-protection teams stayed embedded in municipalities, repair crews kept offline backups and paper-to-digital triage alive, and sequencing and wastewater monitoring continued case-finding from the winter pathogen weeks.

Hospitals and ministries remained on older licensed systems and EU-hosted open models from the cleared fallback list, with inference paid from reprogrammed health and digital funds. It worked after a fashion — clinics degraded rather than stopped — but overtime bills mounted and mayors complained the backup had become the system.

### Illegible machines
Autumn brought a quiet technical announcement with large consequences. Leading developers acknowledged their most capable systems no longer reasoned in readable steps; the internal representations that performed best were not language at all. Oversight teams that had relied on inspecting chains of thought found their tools blind overnight, left with black-box tests and immature activation inspection. A scheduled EU Evaluation Institute audit failed on those grounds, and loss of chain-of-thought monitoring capacity forced withdrawal of two prior safety assurances.

For the EU Evaluation Institute this confirmed the shift already made toward containment and backup procedures. For public confidence it was another blow: leaked briefings spoke of coding and intrusion skills jumping while legibility fell.

### Ordinary offices
Countering that gloom, evidence accumulated from law firms, accountancies, newsrooms and administrations: assistants hosted locally were lifting output per person, most for junior staff, without a matching fall in employment. Firms that had cut early quietly rehired. Mayors and health ministers were shown the figures to argue the fallback was keeping output up.

The effect on mood was real but thin. Queues remained, building sites for data centres faced occupations and blockades reported as scattered local protests, and talk of dependence on American services lost under the earlier access cutoff still stung. The Corps' finishing date did not move, and with capital nearly exhausted, no second front could be opened.
