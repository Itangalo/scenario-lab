## Statement changes

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse — but we now acknowledge that control without cohesion is unenforceable, and cohesion without legitimacy is unsustainable. We are shifting from containment as a technical condition to sovereignty as a political project, anchored in social resilience and institutional credibility.  
  - Trigger: This turn, `eu_frontier_access_denied` lands with full force: the Union is cut off from the leading model at short notice, with no appeal, disrupting hospitals, ministries, and firms. Simultaneously, `public_sentiment` drops to 23.0 amid strikes and perceived governance failure, and `eu_political_capital` collapses to 9.0 — not from lack of action, but from action that lacks legitimacy and operational grounding.  
  - Grounds: The denial of access confirms that sovereignty cannot be outsourced, even temporarily. But the simultaneous erosion of public trust and political capacity reveals that sovereignty cannot be imposed either. The *Social Licence Framework* was a response to the legitimacy crisis, but its stalled implementation under Article 88 TFEU shows that legal authority is not enough — we must embed consent into the architecture of control. Our commitment stands, but its urgency is now existential: we are not merely building systems we can trust; we are rebuilding the Union’s capacity to act at all, before both capability and cohesion slip beyond recovery.

## New measure

**Measure:** Launch the Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive  
A binding directive to fast-track energy allocation, grid access, and permitting for AI compute infrastructure critical to the Sovereign AI Core Programme, overriding national delays in Sweden and Finland, and mandating reallocation of stranded capacity from stalled private projects.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `eu_ai_sovereignty` by 10–15 points by operationalising InvestAI Gigafactories; reduce dependency on foreign models by enabling domestic training runs above 60.0; signal that the EU can enforce its own priorities under strategic duress  
`Applies to:` EU member states with stalled Gigafactory sites (Sweden, Finland), national energy regulators, and private data centre operators with underutilised capacity in Northern Europe

## Priority

**Priority:** Launch the Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive  
We are pushing this measure hardest because `eu_frontier_access_denied` has exposed our dependency as a strategic vulnerability, not a market failure — and because `ai_capability` at 69.0, even if stagnant, is now beyond our reach, while `openweight_capability` at 51.0 means the next jump could distribute catastrophic risk before we can respond. We must act now to secure the physical means of AI production, or lose not just competitiveness, but the ability to govern at all.

## In practice

This turn, we are acting from emergency footing. The denial of access to the leading model — a system already in use across EU hospitals and research institutes — has triggered a crisis of capability and confidence. With `eu_political_capital` at 9.0 and public sentiment in freefall, we cannot afford prolonged negotiation. Our priority is to make the Sovereign AI Core Programme *real*, not just legal. That means power, permits, and hardware — now.

We are invoking Article 122 TFEU on grounds of critical infrastructure failure and invoking the Union’s right to ensure continuity of essential services. Under this legal base, the Commission is issuing the Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive, which overrides national permitting delays in Sweden and Finland and compels energy regulators to prioritise grid access for the Gigafactories. We are also mandating that any private data centre operating below 60% utilisation in Northern Europe must offer excess capacity to the SACP under emergency leasing terms, compensated at market rate but non-negotiable. This is not expropriation — it is activation of latent sovereignty.

The resistance is fierce. Sweden cites energy sovereignty; Finland fears Russian retaliation if its grids are seen as militarised. But we are offering: binding commitments on civilian-only use of SACP systems, shared access to any models developed, and €15bn in emergency grid reinforcement funding drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility. This is not coercion — it is cohesion under fire. We are not building a fortress; we are rebuilding a Union that can act.
