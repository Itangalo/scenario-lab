### A Pause That Deepens the Crisis

The global AI investment collapse halts the frontier’s advance at 69.0, as labs downsize training runs and cancel infrastructure plans. Yet this pause brings no relief—only exposure. The EU’s strategic fragility is laid bare as export controls tighten under the U.S. consolidation posture, restricting even allied access to high-end models. Europe is not barred, but it is rationed—dependent, deferred, and increasingly aware that market retreat does not equal strategic reprieve.

The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative is proposed with urgency, aiming to mandate AI-assisted patching in energy and transport networks and to initiate cross-border cyber handovers. However, implementation is delayed by legal challenges and coordination bottlenecks across member states. Technical planning advances, but no operational systems go live this turn. The initiative remains in early rollout, with full deployment now projected for the next cycle.

Meanwhile, a wave of public-sector strikes, triggered by unconsulted AI deployments in schools and tax offices, paralyzes services across several member states. The strikes are not anti-technology per se, but anti-process—workers demand voice, not veto. In response, the EU launches the *Social Licence Framework*, requiring joint review with labor and civil society before public AI deployment. Germany resists, citing national security, and negotiations stall under Article 88 TFEU, delaying implementation. This is the only new measure introduced this turn, complying with the constraint of one per turn.

The InvestAI Gigafactories officially complete—on paper. But energy grid constraints in Sweden and Finland delay operationalization, and the broader Tech Sovereignty Package fails to mobilize promised private capital amid the sector-wide downturn. The Sovereign AI Core Programme continues, but slowly, underfunded and overstretched.

The AI Containment Protocol also formally concludes, yet its enforcement remains hollow. Without operational capacity in the European AI Assurance Directorate, audits are deferred, and the protocol exists only in law, not practice. With capability stagnant and safety oversight strained, a new interpretability tool is sidelined by lack of integration—another gap between policy and power. However, no active dismantling of safety infrastructure occurs; existing systems persist, albeit under pressure. Thus, `ai_safety` declines only slightly, reflecting erosion of momentum rather than collapse.

Sovereignty and political capital both decline, driven by the notepad-recorded effects: export control escalation (−3), capability stagnation decay (−1), and the social unrest and institutional drag from stalled initiatives (−3 combined). The total change of −4 on sovereignty and −3 on political capital aligns with the revised mechanics and preserves traceability.

Public sentiment dips slightly, as strikes dominate headlines and dependency humiliations accumulate. Trust in AI governance erodes, not from failure to act, but from acting too late, too technically, and too far from the people it claims to protect.

### The Cost of Cohesion

Sovereignty, political capital, and legitimacy all feel the strain. The Union’s tools are outpaced, its unity tested, and its narrative of control undermined by events it cannot contain—and now, by citizens who refuse to be excluded.
