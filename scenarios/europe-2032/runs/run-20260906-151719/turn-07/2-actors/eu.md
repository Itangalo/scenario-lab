## New measure

**Measure:** Establish the Social Licence Framework  
A binding process requiring public-sector AI deployments to undergo joint review with worker representatives, civil society, and national parliaments before rollout, with veto rights on grounds of job displacement, procedural fairness, or social disruption.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase public_sentiment by 15–20 points; reduce strike risk and institutional resistance to AI adoption; anchor AI legitimacy in democratic oversight and fair transition, not just technical safety  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member-state public administrations, national education and healthcare systems, and all SACP participants deploying AI in public services

## In practice

This turn, we are pivoting from containment to credibility. The *ai_investment_collapse* has paused the forward march of capability — the frontier labs are retrenching, training runs shrinking — but that pause is not relief. It is a warning: the market will not carry us to sovereignty, and neither will coercion. The *export_control_escalation* confirms it — the U.S. has moved to a tiered access model, granting volume licences to close allies while rationing even them on the highest-capability models. We are not in the inner circle. Our only path now is to build systems we control, and to make them legitimate in the eyes of those they serve.

The *emergent_labour_strike_ai* is not a protest against technology — it is a revolt against process. Teachers, civil servants, and municipal workers see AI systems deployed to replace, not assist, with no consultation, no retraining, no transparency. They are not wrong. We have spent two years building containment, but we forgot to build consent. So we are acting on three fronts: resilience, legitimacy, and leverage. The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative is now operational — we are mandating AI-assisted patching in energy grids and transport control systems, deploying the first EU-wide bio-detection network using SACP models to scan for engineered pathogens, and establishing red-to-red handover protocols between national CSIRTs. This is not optional: at 69.0, autonomous agents can exploit patching delays in hours, not days.

But we are also launching the Social Licence Framework to close the legitimacy gap. It will require joint review by worker councils, civil society, and national parliaments before any public-sector AI deployment above 50.0 capability. Veto rights apply where job displacement is unmitigated, safeguards are absent, or process is opaque. We are funding this through the European Social Fund and tying it to SACP deployment conditions. France and the Netherlands have signalled support; Germany demands opt-outs for defence-critical systems, which we are negotiating under Article 88 TFEU. This is not delay — it is depth. We cannot afford to build sovereign systems that no one trusts enough to use. And we cannot enforce cohesion if the people themselves are on strike.
