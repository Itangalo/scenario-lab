### Holding the line
Through autumn, municipal IT teams worked off checklists: hardened configurations, offline backups, paper procedures in clinics that had learned not to trust the network. The ransomware wave built from public models did not repeat at its spring scale, but it did not go away either. Hospitals restored records slowly, town halls reopened counters in stages. Grids and payments stayed up. Mayors who had frozen permits for new computing sites said the near-miss proved them right.

Brussels ran the response through existing emergency networks, without new money or new law. Health ministries and administrations cut off from the leading American system made do with Union-hosted systems and partner models. Staff complained about slower answers and broken integrations, but workflows no longer collapsed.

### The chokepoint turned
The heavier blow came from The Hague. Under intense American pressure, the Dutch government extended curbs on lithography exports and servicing — beyond the most advanced machines to older equipment used for ordinary chips, and to more customers. For the company involved, with American components and software deep in its supply chain, refusal looked commercially impossible.

In Brussels the decision landed as humiliation. Europe's single strongest piece of leverage in the technology stack was being deployed, but by Washington. Trade officials kept a coercion review open, coordinated daily with Dutch counterparts, and repeated an offer of joint licensing and mutual recognition of evaluations as a way back. Nothing moved.

### Quiet gains, no dividend
Elsewhere the economy told a different story. Law firms, accountancies, newsrooms and consultancies reported the same pattern: assistants lifted output, most for juniors, without new rounds of layoffs. Some firms that had cut early rehired. The gains were real, widely noted in business pages, and politically useless. With no jobs crisis to answer, calls for a large rebuilding programme found no audience, and hostility to new infrastructure remained entrenched.

By December the Commission closed its two-year pledge to rebuild independent leverage with containment achieved and leverage smaller than when it began.
