### The weights get out
The release came without warning in late summer: a full open-weights package only a step behind the closed frontier, downloadable by anyone. Within days security researchers showed it could rebuild the automation seen in last winter's ransomware sweep, now without a command server to take down. Mirrors multiplied faster than takedown requests could be written.

ENISA and national response teams shifted to containment. Daily technical notes returned, this time with hardened configurations, offline-backup checklists and triage playbooks for town halls and clinics. Where spares and mutual-aid stocks existed, recovery drills were re-run. Where they did not, mayors improvised. No new money moved; the work ran on what was already in national hands.

### Holding the line without building it
Brussels deliberately started nothing new. With trust at rock bottom and the budget exhausted, the Commission kept the coercion review open against the defecting capital, kept the joint licensing offer and the new middle-power supply compact as the stated return path, and kept the five factory site permits legally alive with planning staff but no construction funds.

That husbanding avoided an open failure, but it did not stop erosion. Municipal councils in several countries froze new data-centre permits after petitions linked the clinic outages to new builds. Washington's tiered-access system hardened: European requests for top-tier model access were answered with conditions on re-export and onward training that few EU firms could meet.

By December the Union could claim it had prevented a cascade, but not that it had regained control. The most capable tools were now permanently outside any licensing regime, the common front remained broken, and the public mood settled into open hostility to further AI infrastructure.
