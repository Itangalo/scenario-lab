### Containment without control
The first weeks of the year brought a fast, largely automated assault on public services. A ransomware sweep rebuilt from openly available weights moved through municipal systems, clinics and contractors, encrypting records and forcing several hospitals back to paper. Response teams issued daily hardened configurations and backup checklists and restored from spares where they existed. Where they did not, town halls queued.

The Union prevented a cascading failure of grids and payments, but defenders were visibly behind. Forensic work stalled on attribution, and the tooling was quickly shown to be model-generated. Mayors who had frozen new data-centre permits cited the outages as vindication.

### Cut off at short notice
In the middle of the response, European hospitals, ministries and firms built on the leading American model found their access withdrawn without detailed reason or appeal. Workflows broke overnight. Health and industry officials shifted what they could to models hosted inside the Union and to partners under the middle-power supply compact, with degraded performance.

Trade officials kept a coercion review open but stressed joint licensing and evaluation recognition as a return path. In capitals the episode read less as outrage than as a failure of foresight long warned about.

### A distant floor, a quiet office
Washington and Beijing announced a limited agreement on weights security and escalation risks, with thin verification. Brussels welcomed it as a future floor for evaluation work, but was not at the table.

Meanwhile consultancies, law firms and newsrooms reported steady productivity gains from assistants, largest among juniors, without fresh layoffs. Firms that had cut early quietly rehired. The improvement did little to soften hostility to new infrastructure, and left the Commission with no crisis to spend against and no capital to spend with.
