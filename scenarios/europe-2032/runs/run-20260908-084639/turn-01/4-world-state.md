### The Edge of the Storm

The second half of 2026 ends under a darkening sky. A wave of AI-powered intrusions — first glimpsed in scattered grid audits — culminates in a coordinated cyber assault that cripples public services in two mid-sized EU cities, deploying polymorphic ransomware through a poisoned software update chain. The tooling was novel, adaptive, and clearly model-generated. Defenders were overwhelmed; recovery took weeks.

In response, the EU launches the Cyber Resilience Emergency Buildout, invoking emergency powers to fast-track hardening mandates for critical infrastructure. ENISA receives emergency funding and begins drafting binding standards. Yet resistance flares: Germany challenges the Commission’s authority under Article 4(2) TEU, while Poland delays implementation, citing budget constraints. Only preliminary planning occurs this turn; no infrastructure upgrades are completed. The measure is formally proposed and registered as the EU's sole new initiative and named priority for the period.

Meanwhile, offensive capability surges. Verification widens to structural engineering checks, enabling autonomous design validation — a domain once thought to require expert review.
