## New measure
**Cyber Resilience Emergency Buildout**

A crash programme to harden electricity transmission, water, and port operators against AI-generated cyber threats.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `resilience` by +15–20 points; reduce blast radius of `cyber_major_incident`-class events; signal EU capacity to absorb and respond to systemic shocks.  
`Applies to:` EU member states with critical infrastructure exposed to ongoing threats, coordinated through ENISA and the EU Cybersecurity Agency.

## Priority
**Cyber Resilience Emergency Buildout** — because the cyber_test_shot and cyber_major_incident prove that offensive AI capabilities are already in the wild, and resilience is the only layer of defence the EU can control unilaterally and at speed.

## In practice
This turn, we are treating cyber resilience as a matter of strategic survival. The intrusion discovered across grid operators and the port authority was not targeted at any single state — it was a test of systemic weakness. The fact that it was found by accident confirms that our current detection architecture is blind to AI-native attack patterns. We are activating the Union Civil Protection Mechanism to fast-track funding under the Recovery and Resilience Facility, repurposing 12% of unspent 2026–2027 allocations toward mandatory intrusion detection upgrades for Tier 1 operators. The legal base is Article 4(2) TEU, which obliges solidarity in security of critical infrastructure. We are bypassing the usual co-decision process for technical standards by invoking Regulation (EU) 2022/2558’s emergency provisions, allowing the Commission to issue binding hardening directives within 90 days.

The pushback is real: Germany resists centralised mandates on grid security, Poland questions the fiscal logic of front-loading cyber spend, and several member states are still using legacy SCADA systems with no vendor support. We are offering a 70/30 co-financing model from the Digital Europe Programme, conditioned on participation in quarterly cross-border red-teaming exercises led by ENISA. The Agency is being resourced with an additional €1.2bn from unused Connecting Europe Facility funds, redirected via delegated act. This is not about catching up — it’s about ensuring that when the next attack comes, it doesn’t cascade. The measure will be sustained beyond its finish turn through integration into the revised NIS2 directive, currently under trilogue.
