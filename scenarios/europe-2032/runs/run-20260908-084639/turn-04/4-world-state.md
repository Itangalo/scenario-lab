### A Shift in Strategy, But the Ground Keeps Moving

The EU closes out its **Cyber Resilience Emergency Buildout**, delivering detection tools and red-teaming across critical operators. The effort earns a modest +3 to `resilience`, lifting it to 45.0 — though physical infrastructure upgrades remain incomplete, leaving vulnerabilities at the edge.

Meanwhile, frontier AI capability advances rapidly. Autonomous research agents accelerate materials discovery and exploit generation, pushing `ai_capability` to 62.5. This growth enables broader diffusion of advanced models. A significant **openweight_frontier_release** occurs — a near-frontier model is published online and spreads rapidly — but the resulting leap in open-weight capability is constrained by inherent performance limits and deployment friction. `openweight_capability` rises to 50.0, a substantial +5.0, reflecting rapid adoption and optimization on distributed hardware, but remains clearly behind the closed frontier. The gap narrows from 14.5 to 12.5, consistent with gradual accumulation, not a catastrophic breach.

In response, the EU launches the **Sovereign Access Framework**, a bold attempt to reclaim leverage by conditioning market access on legal anchoring, operational control, and audit compliance. Framed as a sovereignty safeguard rather than a trade barrier, it leverages the Digital Markets Act to enforce licensing for foreign AI providers. However, early reactions are mixed: U.S. tech firms warn of fragmentation, and legal scholars question enforceability beyond data privacy grounds. No immediate compliance is observed, and implementation will take time.

The move signals intent but strains already thin political capital. Four large measures remain in flight, including the new priority, costing −13 total. The completion of the Cyber Resilience Buildout adds +3, and the proposal of a large category 4 measure in direct response to recent systemic shocks earns a +3 bonus. However, the net effect on `eu_political_capital` is a −3 drop, from 38.0 to 35.0, reflecting partial implementation costs and the burden of a growing portfolio without unaccounted capital inflows.

Public sentiment worsens. Visible job losses in law, finance, and software — particularly among graduates — dominate media coverage. The **labour_displacement** wave fuels resentment, dragging `public_sentiment` to 33.0. The EU’s pivot is seen as technocratic gatekeeping, not relief.

`ai_safety` holds at 30.0 — no new safeguards have landed on deployed systems, and the accountability framework remains in implementation. Sovereignty slips further: despite the new push, no capacity gain offsets the −1 from capability growth. Legal delays persist, and no event secures access. `eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 18.0.
