## New measure  
**Public Algorithmic Accountability Framework**  
A binding regulatory framework requiring independent audit, real-time monitoring, and redress mechanisms for all AI systems used in public-sector decision-making, with automatic suspension upon verified harm.  

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by +6–8 points by forcing deployment safeguards into public AI; reduce misuse incidents by 40% in social services, policing, and courts; stabilise `public_sentiment` by demonstrating institutional accountability.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member state administrations, and all public contractors using AI for decisions affecting rights, benefits, or legal status.

## Priority  
**Public Algorithmic Accountability Framework** — because the automated decision scandal has shattered public trust, and without legitimacy, no sovereignty or resilience measure can command the political capital needed to endure.

## In practice  
This turn, we are pivoting hard to repair the democratic breach. The automated decision scandal — now confirmed by the European Ombudsman — has exposed systemic failures in how AI is used in social insurance and policing. We cannot build sovereignty on broken trust. The **Public Algorithmic Accountability Framework** will be tabled in the Justice and Home Affairs Council by September, under Article 16 TFEU, with a delegated act prepared for urgent adoption. It mandates that all public AI systems undergo third-party audit before deployment, continuous monitoring via ENISA-certified tools, and automatic suspension if error rates exceed thresholds or harm is verified. Affected individuals will have a direct right to appeal and redress, funded through a new Digital Justice Facility. We are already coordinating with the EDPS and national data protection authorities to operationalise oversight, and will tie disbursement of cohesion funds to compliance — making adoption of the framework a condition for receiving EU money.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on **Cyber Resilience Emergency Buildout**, though with adjusted messaging. The physical upgrades remain delayed, but we are enforcing the quarterly red-teaming requirement: no exercise, no funding. ENISA is expanding its pilot to include municipal water systems in Marseille and Naples, and we are fast-tracking procurement for intrusion detection systems via the EU Cyber Shield initiative. The 70/30 co-financing model is now linked to audit readiness for the new accountability framework — bundling resilience with legitimacy. For the **Compute Sovereignty Accelerator Directive**, we are holding firm on legal anchoring but softening the rhetoric: this is not about exclusion, but about ensuring that when capability lands in Europe, it serves European law. Talks with hyperscalers continue, but now framed as a trade: compliance buys liability protection and research access, but only if they accept the new public accountability regime as a condition of operating in public-facing domains.
