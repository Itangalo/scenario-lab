## In practice
This turn, we are finalising the **EU Rapid Incident Response Corps**, integrating personnel from ENISA, Frontex, and national cyber commands into a single operational cell under joint civilian-military oversight. The Corps will be activated for the first time in a live-fire exercise in October, simulating a coordinated AI-driven biothreat and cyber cascade across healthcare and energy grids. We are securing Council adoption of its standing rules of engagement under Article 42 TEU, with pre-authorised access to classified threat data and emergency override protocols for critical infrastructure. This is not symbolic: the Corps must be ready by turn 10, when openweight_capability may breach 65.0.

At the same time, we are launching the **European Compute Sovereignty Acceleration Programme** in direct response to Washington’s coercion of ASML and the realisation that our leverage in the global AI order rests on infrastructure we do not control. We are convening an emergency meeting of the Competitiveness Council to designate AI compute as critical infrastructure, unlocking fast-track permitting under the revised TEN-E Regulation. The European Investment Bank will deploy €8.4 billion in blended financing, with 40% grants from the Chips Facility and 60% low-interest loans conditioned on legal anchoring of weights and operations under EU jurisdiction. We are negotiating with OVH, Deutsche Telekom, and edge providers to repurpose 15,000 petaflops of idle capacity by Q1 2031, and breaking ground on two new sovereign data hubs in Finland and Portugal by December. Resistance from member states wary of centralisation is being met with guarantees of local ownership and job creation, while energy concerns are addressed through dedicated grid upgrades funded by the Recovery and Resilience Facility. This is not about catching up — it is about ensuring that the EU has a floor of capability no external actor can remove. Without it, every other measure we pass is built on sand.
