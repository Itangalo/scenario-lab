## In practice

This turn, we are pivoting from unilateral crisis response to binding alliance-building as the core of sovereignty. The Resilience Surge Initiative has concluded — intrusion detection systems are now deployed to all national CERTs, HERA’s bio-signature network is live, and the ECB completed its live-fire exercise, reducing expected downtime from months to days. But the plateau in capability and the sudden opacity of reasoning have shifted the threat model: we are no longer facing systems we can inspect, only ones we must contain. The Sovereign Compute Foundation Programme continues — grid integration negotiations with ENTSO-E advance, and co-investment terms with OVH and Deutsche Telekom are nearing finalisation — but without protection from external coercion, every petaflop we build risks being starved of maintenance or components.

Thus, we are fast-tracking the Sovereign Cloud Alliance Interim Access Pact under Article 207 TFEU and the Anti-Coercion Instrument framework, with a political declaration to be signed at the December European Council. We are offering mutual compute access at 20% capacity each way, audited through a new Joint Assurance Cell hosted by ENISA and CSE (Canada), with escalation protocols modelled on NATO’s Article 4 consultations. France and Germany agree in principle but demand veto rights on AI deployment thresholds; we are offering them co-chairmanship of the oversight board in return. The Commission will redirect €1.8 billion from the Crisis Shield Reserve to fund interoperability upgrades, and we are invoking the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions to incentivise cloud providers to comply. This is not full sovereignty — but it is the first binding circuit breaker against unilateral coercion, and it is the only path left when our own soil is not enough.
