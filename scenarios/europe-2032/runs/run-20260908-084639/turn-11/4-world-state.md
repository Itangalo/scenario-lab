### The Illusion of Completion

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation Directive formally concluded its legislative journey this turn, but its failure to pass final adoption in ECOFIN and overcome legal challenges stripped it of force. National courts in Germany, Spain, and Finland issued preliminary injunctions, citing violations of energy sovereignty and environmental law. Without binding implementation, the directive collapsed into symbolism—its ambition exposed as politically unmoored.

### A New Foundation, But No Momentum

In response, the European Commission launched the Sovereign Compute Foundation Programme: a €24 billion, decade-long industrial project to build 100,000 petaflops of EU-controlled compute across six hardened, renewable-powered hubs. Designed to operate within legal frameworks rather than override them, it sought legitimacy through co-investment and geographic balance. Yet initial pledges fell short—only four member states committed co-funding, and private operators hesitated, wary of long-term liabilities. The programme started, but without the surge of confidence its architects hoped for.

Early legal anchoring of two hub sites and cross-border coordination on permitting provided partial momentum. While full implementation remains distant, these foundational steps were sufficient to offset the structural erosion from the capability gap and absence of safety progress. Sovereignty stabilised at 14.0.
