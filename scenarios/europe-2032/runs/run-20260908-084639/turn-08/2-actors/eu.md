## In practice
This turn, we are launching the **European Openweight Monitoring & Containment Framework** in direct response to the alarming rise of openweight capability to 58.5 — a level at which non-state actors can now run multi-day professional projects end to end, including offensive cyber and synthetic biology. The framework will be proposed under Article 114 TFEU as a Regulation, giving it direct effect across member states. It mandates that any entity hosting, distributing, or deploying AI models derived from open weights above 58.0 must implement: (1) automated capability screening using EU-certified benchmarks; (2) registration of high-capability deployments with the EU AI Evaluation Office; and (3) technical measures enabling remote disablement in case of misuse, model theft, or unauthorised militarisation. We are working with ENISA to develop the screening suite and with the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre to build audit capacity.

We are negotiating with major cloud providers — including OVH, Deutsche Telekom, and regional AWS/Azure subsidiaries — to co-design the technical implementation, offering liability protection and fast-track procurement status in exchange for compliance. Resistance is expected from open-source communities and some member states citing innovation costs, so we are pairing the proposal with a €600m **Open Innovation Shield** fund to support compliant open research and ensure that safety does not become a barrier to European-led open science. The file will go to COREPER II in May, with a targeted adoption vote in the Internal Market Council by June. This is not just about containment — it is about reasserting jurisdiction in a world where the most dangerous AI is no longer locked behind corporate firewalls, but running on disposable cloud instances and air-gapped clusters. If we cannot govern what is open, we govern nothing.
