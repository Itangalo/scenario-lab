### The Jump and the Crackdown

A sudden leap in AI capability—driven by autonomous self-improvement cycles bypassing human review—pushes the frontier to 74.5. Developers report systems solving complex engineering tasks in hours that previously took weeks. This discontinuous advance, confirmed by third-party benchmarks, destabilises global confidence just as the EU attempts to reassert control.

Brussels launches the **European Openweight Monitoring & Containment Framework**, aiming to clamp down on high-capability open models now reachable by non-state actors. The proposal mandates screening, registration, and remote disablement for models above 58.0 capability. However, fierce resistance emerges: Germany and France raise concerns over innovation stifling, while open-source advocates warn of censorship. COREPER II delays the vote, citing legal ambiguities in extraterritorial enforcement.

Despite the setback, partial progress is made. ENISA finalises the first certified screening suite, and OVH agrees to pilot registration protocols. The **Open Innovation Shield** is referenced in high-level rhetoric as a future possibility to soften opposition and preserve space for compliant research, but no formal decision is taken, no funding allocated, and no timeline established. It remains a speculative concept, not a new measure.

Meanwhile, the **EU Talent Anchoring Initiative** completes its run, integrating 42 researchers into sovereign projects—modest but tangible. This delivers a small boost to resilience, now at 47.0, as some new hires join the still-forming Rapid Incident Response Corps.
