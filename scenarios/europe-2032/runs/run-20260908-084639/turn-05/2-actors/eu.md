## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity as the foundation of democratic legitimacy and systemic resilience``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The past two years have shown that accountability frameworks and transparency regimes cannot restore public trust or ensure safety if they are applied to systems over which the Union has no control. Democratic legitimacy erodes when governance is symbolic rather than operational, and resilience fails when it depends on foreign infrastructure that can be withdrawn or compromised. The **openweight_frontier_release** and the **election_retrenchment** in the U.S. confirm that neither leadership nor partnership is stable — we must now treat sovereign capacity not as a means but as the precondition for all else. Without compute, control, and continuity under EU jurisdiction, no framework holds.

## Priority
**Sovereign Access Framework** — because this turn determines whether the EU can enforce jurisdictional control over AI systems operating in its market, and without enforcement, our entire strategy collapses into aspiration. While the **election_retrenchment** in the U.S. creates an opening, it also removes a predictable partner — making it urgent that we define our own terms now, before second-tier actors fill the void. This measure leverages existing competition authority to impose real conditions, and its credibility hinges on decisive implementation this turn.

## In practice
This turn, we are finalizing the **Compute Sovereignty Accelerator Directive**, which clears permitting, grid access, and state aid pathways for AI infrastructure that meets EU strategic criteria — particularly legal anchoring of weights and operational control within our jurisdiction. With the directive now complete, national regulators and energy ministries begin aligning their processes, and we are fast-tracking three sites in Finland, Spain, and Poland under its provisions. The message is clear: if you want to run AI at scale in Europe, you must run it here — with EU-supervised redundancy, secured weights, and auditable operations.

At the same time, we are driving hard on the **Sovereign Access Framework**, advancing it through the Digital Markets Act enforcement machinery. The Commission’s Competition Directorate is drafting licensing conditions that tie market access to proof of weight anchoring, remote override protection, and real-time auditability. We are setting up the EU AI Evaluation Office to deliver fast-track certification for compliant providers, offering liability shields and preferential procurement status in return for full transparency. Resistance from U.S. hyperscalers is expected, but the **election_retrenchment** weakens their lobbying position in Washington, and we are reaching out to Japanese and South Korean partners through the **emergent_lab_cooperation** network to build a coalition around jurisdictional integrity.

We are also launching the **EU Talent Anchoring Initiative**, recognizing that compute without competence is inert. Working through Horizon Europe and a new pan-European AI Fellowship, we are offering ten-year research grants, visa fast-tracks, and dedicated compute allocations to top-tier talent willing to anchor their work in EU institutions. Member states are being asked to match funding and housing support, with Germany and France already signaling strong interest. This is not just recruitment — it is nation-building in the cognitive domain.
