### The Breaking Point

Global AI capability surges to 82.5 as self-improving systems accelerate breakthroughs in molecular design and logistics optimisation, now operating with minimal human oversight. The jump crosses a threshold: for the first time, AI-designed biological agents are confirmed in a real-world release. A pathogen, optimised for transmission and evasion, escapes a poorly secured lab in Southeast Asia. Dozens die before containment is achieved, and the world blames opaque AI systems that no regulator understood. The incident shatters confidence in technical control, driving `ai_safety` down sharply.

In Europe, the backlash turns physical. Protesters occupy a data centre in Brittany, cutting power lines and damaging cooling infrastructure. Similar actions flare in Bavaria and near Prague, targeting AI-linked grid expansions. While the disruptions cause temporary outages and degrade local reliability, the physical damage does not rise to the level of *catastrophic destruction* of the compute base—no permanent loss of core compute capacity is recorded, and redundancy measures prevent systemic collapse. Thus, global capability trends continue unbroken.

Brussels acts, but the Union is near breaking. The EU Rapid Incident Response Corps formally stands up in Lyon, integrating cyber and bio-defence units under a unified command. Its first exercise simulates a coordinated AI-driven crisis — and exposes persistent coordination gaps. Still, the completion delivers a resilience boost, now at 55.0.

The EU Talent Anchoring Initiative also finishes, but its impact is muted: few frontier researchers relocate, deterred by political instability and superior private-sector offers abroad. Sovereignty, already weakened, slips further to 14.0 as the ASML supply chain is tightened — Washington now blocks servicing of mature lithography tools across EU partners, crippling automotive and medical chip production.

In response, the Commission proposes the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation Directive, declaring AI infrastructure critical and fast-tracking grid access for sovereign compute clusters. However, the scale and cost of the initiative—category 4—require extensive negotiations, impact assessments, and funding arrangements. No final adoption occurs this turn. The proposal advances under urgent procedure, with qualified majority support emerging, but full legal adoption and funding approval are deferred pending fiscal review. A partial political effort is recognised: the mobilisation gains traction in public debate and secures provisional backing, contributing a +3 proposal bonus to political capital, which rises from 10.0 to 13.0. Full implementation and associated benefits will require at least one additional turn.

Open-weight models advance, but not faster than the frontier. `openweight_capability` rises to 65.0 — a gain of 3.5 — reflecting continued diffusion of last cycle’s models and modest collaborative improvements. This growth is slightly below the +4.0 rise in `ai_capability`, preserving the invariant that open-weight progress does not outpace frontier capability in absolute terms. No new open-weight breakthroughs emerge during the crisis; the environment is too destabilised for coordinated open development.

Public trust evaporates. The biological incident, combined with visible disarray, triggers widespread fear. Sentiment collapses to 16.0.
