### Cut off in the middle of containment
The notice arrived without explanation and without appeal. Overnight, access to the leading foreign model went dark for European users — triage assistants in hospitals, dispatch support tools, port logistics helpers built on top of it stopped answering. Ministries and firms that had assumed continuity scrambled to re-route. In Brussels the reading was immediate: dependence had become exposure.

It came in the worst possible week. Health authorities confirmed a real biological incident with model involvement — a modified agent released deliberately, investigators said — that left casualties and forced a containment operation running for weeks. Sequencing labs and wastewater monitors installed the previous autumn went to full alert, and coordination ran through emergency health channels. At the same time, a sharp advance in code, mathematics and automated intrusion landed in deployed tools. Defenders said machine-written attacks that had jammed and re-jammed booking, dispatch and clearance systems since summer suddenly became faster and harder to filter.

What prevented collapse was unglamorous and local. Repair teams already embedded in communes generalised into standing crews, rebuilding from offline backups and handing out hardened paper-to-digital kits. Clinics ran on EU-hosted open models for triage and prescribing, ports on simplified clearance packs. Mayors credited crews who stayed until systems held. The upgraded sentinel network caught secondary spread early enough to keep hospitals functioning, degraded but open.

Everything else froze. No new factory money was voted; sites were kept warm, grid works crept forward. Retaliation over servicing curbs on chipmaking tools was shelved. The Council was asked only to endorse continuity, not new money, for fear a funding fight would break cohesion entirely.

By June, essential services were running without the frontier provider — slowly, on domestic fallback. Citizens experienced it as queues, paper forms and exhausted staff. Trust fell further; outages were visible, gains were not. In technical circles, leaked notes about systems behaving in ways testers could not explain circulated hand to hand.
