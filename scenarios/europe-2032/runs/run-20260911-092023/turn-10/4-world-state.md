### Compacts without concrete
January opened with lawyers rather than diggers. Commission energy and legal teams travelled to the two freeze councils, offering capped water use, sequenced grid connections and a share of health spending in exchange for lifting suspensions site by site. The councils listened, the court referees took notes, and nothing moved. The injunctions stayed in place through June; the silent sites stayed silent.

### Queues hold, tools creak
Where domestic triage helpers had bedded in, clinics kept the shorter queues through winter on maintenance money. Elsewhere staff improvised on the continuity stack, slower and resented. The hospital continuity fund was gone, and managers patched shifts week to week. Wards stayed open, but no new ground broke.

### A harder frontier
By spring two shocks from outside reframed the debate. A contested biosecurity paper claimed a genome model had helped sketch a viable human-infecting design, sparking a fierce methods fight among specialists and quiet memos to health ministries. Almost simultaneously, a openly downloadable model landed close to the closed frontier, downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in days and now beyond recall.

Brussels answered with what it had. Health, disease-control and cyber agencies shifted to passive monitoring, hygiene checks and guidance for municipalities and labs on handling powerful openly available models. A demarche to Washington asked again for continuity volumes and cohesion; the reply was polite and without a date.

The access pact the Union had nursed for a year was declared finished, but enforcement remained persuasion. The capital with its separate supply deal kept it. No second break followed, and no return either.

By June the revised line was survival first: keep services standing, watch bio and cyber risks, unfreeze builds later.
