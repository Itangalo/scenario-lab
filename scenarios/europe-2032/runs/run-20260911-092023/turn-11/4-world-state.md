### Holding the line
July opened with Brussels explicitly in maintenance mode. With no money to start anything new, Commission teams shuttled between the two councils blocking data-centre builds, offering capped water, staged grid hook-ups and a slice of health spending. Judges kept the injunctions in place. No diggers moved.

Hospitals lived week to week. Where home-grown triage assistants had taken root, queues stayed shorter. Elsewhere doctors and clerks nursed the slower backup systems put in after the American cut-off. The emergency hospital fund was gone; rotas were patched, wards stayed open, backlogs crept.

### A darker black box
In autumn the frontier shifted under everyone's feet. Leading labs acknowledged their newest systems no longer reasoned in readable words — the internal steps that auditors used to check had dissolved into machine-native patterns. Oversight teams that relied on reading those steps found themselves blind, left with blunt output tests and slow, experimental probes of model internals.

At almost the same time, offices reported what augmentation really meant: lawyers, accountants, journalists and consultants worked measurably faster, juniors most of all, but nobody was fired and nobody hired differently. The great transition stalled into a useful tool. For workers it was relief; for budgets premised on a productivity boom it was a hole.

Robots, too, disappointed boosters. Machines took fixed jobs in warehouses arranged around them, and defence ministries trialled carriers and mine-clearers without committing. Firms that had promised general labour saw orders questioned.

Washington and Beijing, meanwhile, announced a limited pact on securing weights, avoiding automated escalation and restricting certain biological design tools, with thin verification. Brussels was not at the table, only briefed after. Health and cyber agencies issued hygiene guidance for labs and towns living with freely downloadable near-frontier models, and watched hospital and municipal networks passively.

Rumours swirled — talk of fences cut near the frozen sites, of night-shift staff threatening to walk out over crashing tools — but stayed rumours, single-source and unconfirmed. By December survival held, unfreezing did not.
