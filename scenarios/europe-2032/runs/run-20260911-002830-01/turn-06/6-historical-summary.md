Winter shocks of lab-escaped pathogen and Taiwan chip freeze eased into strained stabilization: Brussels emergency bio-monitoring, stockpiles, compute rationing and grid priority kept large hospitals and grids online, while small utilities suffered staffing gaps, blind isolations, chlorination loss and insurer pullback, partly eased by manual-fallback playbooks, EIB backstop and Trust and Transition/Municipal Pact.

Late winter brought dual capability jumps: a US lab demonstrated long-horizon autonomous planning/coding/tool-use, and US and Chinese firms commercialized dexterous humanoid robots for warehouse/digging/clearance.

US November coalition-builder took office offering structured allied frontier access, joint evaluation and aligned controls, but envoys secured only process — tiers and harmonization still to negotiate — fueling fears of access as permission and loss of home-build urgency.

Brussels closed emergency bio-shield into standing procedure, poured concrete on two grid-prioritized gigafactory sites, but accelerators stayed at crisis prices with Taiwan allocations frozen and lithography coordination not delivering machines. Small towns trialed imported robots amid staffing gaps and union warnings; where pact crews arrived boil notices ended, where not resentment and reinsurer hesitation persisted.

By June mood was capability without assurance: more powerful systems and uncontrolled robot supply chains, thinner evaluations.
