### MPAC Operational, But Crisis Deepens

The Middle Power AI Coalition (MPAC) becomes fully operational, with binding protocols now active among the EU, Japan, South Korea, and Norway. Canada remains in provisional status, participating in intelligence sharing but not joint decision-making. The Coordination Protocol enables mutual recognition of safety evaluations and synchronized export controls on advanced AI chips, marking the first coordinated leverage over frontier development by non-superpowers. However, early friction emerges: Seoul delays implementation of co-location rules, citing security concerns, while Oslo questions the scope of side-payments tied to compute access.

### Frontier Leaps Ahead

Global AI capability advances to 70.0, driven by recursive self-improvement breakthroughs in closed labs. The jump is enabled by model-generated toolchains that automate debugging and optimization at scale. Openweight capability rises to 59.0, reflecting gradual diffusion of last cycle’s frontier models—now capable of multi-day software projects—but distribution remains constrained by licensing, infrastructure gaps, and deliberate throttling. The increase is substantial but remains within the bounds of accumulated capability, falling clearly below frontier levels as expected under controlled spillover.

### Safety Gains, But Resilience Falters

A major interpretability breakthrough allows developers to predict certain model behaviors before deployment, significantly improving control. This lifts ai_safety to 31.0. However, a large-scale cyber incident—originating from an AI-generated exploit in a widely used software dependency—disrupts public services across three member states. Recovery is slow, and healthcare systems are particularly exposed. Despite MPAC’s coordination mechanisms, response is fragmented, and resilience drops to 32.0 as critical infrastructure proves brittle.

### Sovereignty Stalls, Capital Erodes

The Sovereign AI Core operates at 66.5 capability, but expansion is delayed beyond Bavaria and Tyrol due to funding shortfalls and legal pushback from regional regulators. Meanwhile, a member state quietly negotiates direct access to a U.S. hyperscaler, bypassing MPAC’s joint framework—a move framed domestically as pragmatic but seen internationally as a breach of collective discipline. This undermines cohesion and accelerates sovereignty decay.

With no new measures proposed but the portfolio still active, political capital erodes under sustained costs. Public sentiment remains frozen at 14.0, with protests in Lyon and Vienna intensifying over perceived elite detachment.
