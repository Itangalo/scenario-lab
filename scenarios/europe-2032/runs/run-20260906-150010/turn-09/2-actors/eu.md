## New measure
**European Sovereign Compute Corridor**  
Establish a legally integrated, energy-backed network of high-priority AI compute facilities across core EU member states, with guaranteed grid access, fast-tracked permitting under EU law, and binding capacity reservations for public safety and evaluation use.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`

`Targeted effect:` Increases `eu_ai_sovereignty` by 8–12 points by creating a physically and legally anchored compute base that cannot be unilaterally withdrawn by third parties; stabilises `ai_safety` indirectly by enabling EU-run evaluations and agent monitoring at scale; begins to restore `public_sentiment` if framed as infrastructure renewal rather than AI expansion

## Priority
**Measures for Joint Threat Response Integration** — because it is the only active measure delivering tangible resilience gains this turn, closing a dangerous window in cyber-bio coordination while our domestic capacity remains too weak to stand alone, and abandoning it now would collapse trust in EU crisis leadership at the worst possible moment.

## In practice
We are finalising implementation of the **Measures for Joint Threat Response Integration** through the Council’s Permanent Security Committee, with ENISA and ECDC co-leading operational activation. Legal interoperability is being established under Article 75 TFEU, allowing cross-border data flows between national cyber agencies and the joint node. Funding of €1.2bn has been redirected from the Internal Security Fund – Borders, supplemented by €300m from the Crisis Management Instrument. To secure buy-in from Poland, Hungary, and Austria, we have accepted a four-year sunset clause and reinforced national veto rights over data routing — concessions that dilute long-term effectiveness but prevent immediate deadlock. The system goes live this month, with daily threat briefings now shared among 14 states.

At the same time, we are launching preparations for the **European Sovereign Compute Corridor**, recognising that emergency mobilisation failed not because the goal was wrong, but because it lacked legal durability and energy integration. This new measure will be proposed under the Digital Decade framework and co-financed through the Recovery and Resilience Facility and Connecting Europe Facility. We are engaging national energy regulators and transmission system operators early — learning from past mistakes — to embed compute load guarantees into grid modernisation plans. The Commission’s Vice-President for Energy and the Executive Director of EuroHPC are leading talks with operators in Lyon, Munich, and Amsterdam to identify three anchor sites. Our message to member states is clear: sovereignty is not an option, but it must be built on foundations we all agree to uphold.
