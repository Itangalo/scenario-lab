### A Capability Creeps Forward

Global AI capability advances to 61.5 as frontier labs refine persistent agent frameworks, enabling systems to manage multi-day workflows with minimal oversight. These agents now initiate debugging cycles, coordinate with simulated experts, and adjust goals in response to feedback — a shift from task execution to open-ended pursuit. No single breakthrough defines the jump, but the cumulative effect widens the gap between control and competence.

### Open Weights Gain Ground — But Not Ahead

Signals from a non-Western research lab suggest the imminent release of a new open model architecture, sparking speculation in technical communities and early replication attempts. While preliminary, this has accelerated distributed development and modestly improved open-weight capability to 47.0. This remains below frontier AI capability, consistent with the persistent compute and data gap between open and closed ecosystems. No full deployment has yet occurred, and the model’s actual performance characteristics remain unverified.

### Safety Erodes Amid Institutional Strain

With no safety measure completed and capability rising sharply, confidence in system control declines. A leaked internal review reveals unmonitored agent behaviour during training runs at one major lab — not yet a breach, but a sign that oversight lags behind development. Without an established EU evaluation mechanism, there is no formal process to audit claims, and `ai_safety` drops to 17.0.

### Resilience Under Pressure

Municipal infrastructure faces renewed AI-augmented attacks, this time targeting water regulation systems in two member states. Though contained, the incidents expose slow patch deployment and fragmented coordination. ENISA confirms defences are adapting too slowly to keep pace, and resilience slips to 31.0.

### Sovereignty Advances Cautiously

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme continues toward completion, with preliminary agreements secured for sovereign compute sites and construction beginning at two locations. However, final binding commitments remain pending legal review and supply-chain delays. The programme is now in its final phase, but full operational capacity will not be achieved until the next turn. As a result, only partial gains accrue this turn, lifting `eu_ai_sovereignty` to 32.0, reflecting ongoing progress offset by decay from advancing capability (+1.0 → −1).

### Political Capital Stagnates, Sentiment Sours

No new measure is launched, and the portfolio charge remains high. Labour unrest intensifies in legal and diagnostics sectors, feeding public anxiety. Media frames AI as both displacing workers and evading control. `public_sentiment` dips to 33.0, further isolating policymakers. The absence of a functioning evaluation framework continues to undermine institutional credibility, but this gap is not attributed to a named, active EU policy initiative, as no such measure is currently in flight.
