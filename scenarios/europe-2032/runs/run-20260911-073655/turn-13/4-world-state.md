### Holding the floor
July began with wards and control rooms still on the emergency protocol. Vetted open models ran intensive-care triage and southern grid balancing from European machines and partner capacity, screened through joint cyber and disease feeds. Rationing stayed explicit: emergencies first, everything else queued. It was slower than the cut-off American tools, but it did not switch off.

Brussels spent no new money. Reprogrammed health and digital lines paid for operations, mayors kept bridge payments for port and logistics households, and envoys stayed in the Washington licensing queue asking for telemetry in exchange for observer status at the US-China weights understanding. No answer came.

### The jump and the tightening
Mid-autumn the American labs showed another discontinuous leap — systems that planned and built across days with almost no legible trace. European oversight teams admitted they could only test the outputs, not audit the reasoning. The week-old deployment timelines were obsolete overnight.

At the same time export rules tightened again. Under the published allied terms, Europe kept volume licences but at rationed doses, with hospital and grid uses still queued behind domestic allocation. Procurement officers described approved quotas arriving late and short. The middle-power cable-protection talks produced language, not guarantees.

Single-source whispers grew that a checkpoint in the continuity stack carried altered weights via partner hardware. Nothing was confirmed; quarantine drills were run quietly in two hospital networks and one transmission operator.

By December the floor held. Lights stayed on, emergency care continued, and the joint threat feeds blunted a wave of probing intrusions. Graduate hiring did not return, cranes did not move, and evening news still paired idle youth with idle sites. Survival without recovery kept cohesion from snapping, but only just.
