US-China war deepened supply fracture: in February US frontier-model access cut off for EU hospitals, ministries and grid operators, replaced by refusal notices and a Washington licensing queue, forcing paper triage and phone balancing.

EU responded with a continuity protocol authorising vetted open-weight models on European and partner hardware for intensive care, dispatch and balancing, filtered through ENISA/CERT-EU cyber and biosurveillance feeds. It preserved a floor — lights on, wards open — but slower, less capable, with explicit rationing to emergency care.

Entry-post hiring in law, accountancy, software and admin did not return; bridge payments for port/logistics workers and mayors continued. Southern gigafactory grid works stayed idle.

Mid-spring US labs made an unreadable capability jump to black-box models as Washington and Beijing reached a limited understanding on securing weights and restraining autonomous escalation; Europe was only briefed. Export rules tightened further to rationed volume licences. Middle-power club continued pressing for cable/site non-targeting without guarantees, as public resentment grew over idle graduates and cranes.
