### Shield closed, Accord opened
ENISA declared the Cyber Shield Surge complete in October. Patching mandates and swarm detectors were now standard in port clouds and municipal networks. In Lille and Gdansk response teams stopped an autumn ransomware wave in hours; in two southern regions billing still went dark for a weekend when telecoms delayed maintenance. Brussels claimed vindication, operators complained about costs, but cascades no longer crossed borders.

The cures programme held. Joint procurement kept tailored cancer therapies flowing in French and Polish wards, with provenance labels and clinician sign-off. Queues grew, outcomes impressed, and papers kept asking why European lives depended on models hosted across the Atlantic. The grudging capital stayed inside the joint frame, though two capitals continued to grumble about obedience.

Abroad, Washington and Beijing announced a limited risk accord on weights security and biological escalation tools, verification thin but real. Frontier progress did not stop — another quiet model drop showed longer planning and more autonomous training — but the frantic pace eased slightly. European evaluators were informed afterwards, not consulted.

The Commission moved fast to seek an observer-plus seat, offering supply-chain alignment, evaluator access and standards coordination. American and Chinese diplomats welcomed interest in principle and deferred terms in practice. Inside the Council, mandate talks dragged over what the Union would actually trade.

Work changed shape without disappearing. Studies showing augmentation with stable employment were quoted by ministers, then undercut by hospital administrators automating triage and logistics around the new therapies. Relief and resentment rose together.
