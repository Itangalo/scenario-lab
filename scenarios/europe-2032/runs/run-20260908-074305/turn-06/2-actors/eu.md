## In practice
This turn, we finalise the activation of the **European Foundation Model Initiative** as the cornerstone of our sovereign capability build-up, now transitioning from planning to execution. JUPITER time is formally allocated under Union title, with 25% of annual compute reserved for the initiative and legal ownership of all model weights vested in the Union under Article 172 TFEU. France, Finland, and the Netherlands have signed implementation agreements, committing national researchers and datasets; Germany remains outside the core group but participates in audit design through CEN-CENELEC. The sovereign model training sprint concludes its final training run this month — while it did not yield a fully deployable model, it exposed critical gaps in real-time monitoring and control tooling, which are now being fed into the next phase of the Foundation Model Initiative.

Simultaneously, we launch the **European AI Incident Response Corps**, a direct response to the growing gap between capability and safety. The unit will be hosted under a newly reinforced European Resilience Directorate, drawing on ENISA for technical forensics, Europol for threat intelligence, and the JRC for systemic analysis. Pre-authorised playbooks will allow rapid deployment during fast-moving incidents, including AI-driven disinformation campaigns, automated cyber intrusions, and model hijacking. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme and the Internal Security Fund, scaled up through a mid-term review adjustment. Member states will be required to designate national liaison officers and to participate in biannual cross-border exercises starting in 2030. This is not a new army — it is a nervous system for a Union whose critical functions are now embedded in code that no one fully controls.

We are acting under the weight of two truths: that we must build our own capacity, and that we must survive long enough to use it. The sprint showed us what we can do; the Incident Response Corps is what we must do to remain.
