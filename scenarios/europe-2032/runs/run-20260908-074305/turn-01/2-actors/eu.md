## In practice
The cyber_test_shot changes everything. It was not an attack — it was a rehearsal no one detected. The fact that it was found by accident in a non-EU system is no comfort. Our own grids, water systems, and ports are not meaningfully harder to breach. The AI Act’s high-risk provisions are still two years from enforcement, and the Tech sovereignty package does not harden, only builds. We cannot wait.

This turn, the Commission drafts a Council directive under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market to impose minimum cyber resilience standards on designated operators of essential services. ENISA is tasked with drafting the technical annex, working with national CSIRTs and the European Defence Agency to model red-team scenarios based on the intrusion patterns observed. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme and REPowerEU’s grid modernisation envelope, repurposed — €12bn over three years, co-financed 70% by Brussels. The directive mandates automated intrusion detection, air-gapped backups, and quarterly cross-border penetration testing, with non-compliance triggering Article 260 enforcement.

Resistance is expected from member states with underfunded utilities and from industry groups warning of cost and overreach. We offer fast-track access to InvestAI Gigafactories for operators who comply early, and technical assistance from the newly established European Cyber Resilience Centre. The message is clear: sovereignty begins with survival. We are not building AI if we cannot protect what we already have.
