### The Rehearsal That Woke the Union

The discovery of a coordinated intrusion across critical infrastructure operators — including two EU grids — sent shockwaves through Brussels. Though no damage was done, the breach went undetected for weeks and was only uncovered by chance. The episode, widely described as a “dress rehearsal,” exposed a dangerous gap: even as AI systems grow more capable, the systems they could disrupt remain brittle.

In response, the European Commission proposes the **Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure**, a sweeping directive to harden electricity, water, and port networks. Leveraging internal market rules, it would mandate automated detection, air-gapped backups, and cross-border red-teaming. A funding package of €12bn is under discussion, to be repurposed from existing programmes, but no final allocation is approved this turn. ENISA is tasked with scoping technical standards, but drafting will not begin in earnest until the legal basis is secured and member states are consulted. The directive is adopted in principle by the College of Commissioners, but formal proposal to the Council and Parliament is pending, and full implementation cannot begin before turn 3. Early resistance from member states with underfunded utilities and industry groups warning of overreach signal a rocky path ahead.

Meanwhile, the global AI sector reels from the **ai_investment_collapse**. Frontier labs scale back training runs, and European compute partnerships dissolve overnight. This contraction slows global capability growth — but only slightly. AI capability still rises by 2.5 points, driven by existing momentum.

With no safety measure landing on shipped models, and the gap between capability and safety widening, confidence in control erodes. The earlier lab agent incident lingers in expert memory, and new whispers about AI-designed pathogens intensify unease. AI safety drops.

Open-weight models benefit modestly from frontier spill-over: early technical details from Kimi K3’s architecture circulate in research communities, enabling incremental improvements in fine-tuned variants. However, the full model weights remain closed, limiting replication. Openweight capability rises, but within bounds consistent with gradual diffusion, not a leap.

EU sovereignty dips slightly as compute ambitions falter amid capital flight. Political capital declines, but not as sharply as proposed — the cost of initiating Cyber Shield is real, but without immediate implementation or spending, the drain is partial. The measure remains in preparation, consuming attention but not yet resources at scale.

Public sentiment holds steady: no major scandal, no visible benefit.
