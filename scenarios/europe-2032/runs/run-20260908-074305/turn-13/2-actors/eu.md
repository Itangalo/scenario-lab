## In practice

This turn, we launch the Emergency Cyber-Physical Resilience Surge under Article 114 TFEU and the NIS2 Directive framework, mandating immediate hardening of control systems in energy grids, water treatment, and emergency medical dispatch across all member states. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will lead a six-month sprint to deploy air-gapped monitoring, algorithmic anomaly detection, and manual override protocols, with red-team exercises conducted by CERT-EU and national counterparts every four weeks. Funding of €1.2 billion will be reallocated from stalled Digital Europe Programme initiatives, including deferred components of the enforcement infrastructure for the stalled directive. Member states must report compliance biweekly; non-compliant operators will face temporary suspension of automated control privileges.

Germany, France, and the Nordic states have signalled support, but Spain and Hungary resist binding mandates, citing national competence. In exchange for their cooperation, we offer accelerated access to EU-wide AI threat intelligence feeds and co-funding for local retrofitting. The AI Office will integrate findings into a live Common Operational Picture for AI-enabled threats, shared with national crisis cells. This is not a substitute for sovereignty — but while we lack the power to audit or build, we retain the duty to shield. The surge begins now, because the next incident may not be contained.
