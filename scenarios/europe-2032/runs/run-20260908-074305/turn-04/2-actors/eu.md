## In practice  
This turn, we complete the **Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure** directive, which now enters force. ENISA finalises the technical annex, defining automated detection thresholds for zero-day exploits, mandating quarterly cross-border red-teaming involving national defence agencies, and setting minimum standards for air-gapped backups in energy and water systems. The €12bn co-financing mechanism is activated through Digital Europe, with 70% grants available to member states that ratify within six months. To break the deadlock, we offer Spain and Germany transitional compute allocations from EuroHPC’s non-JUPITER clusters as incentive — a temporary diversion, but one that preserves unity. Compliance is now formally tied to InvestAI eligibility and EuroHPC priority access, making adoption both attractive and enforceable.

At the same time, we accelerate the **European Foundation Model Initiative**, shifting from planning to execution. The Commission signs governance agreements with France, Finland, and the Netherlands to pool talent and secure initial training runs on JUPITER. Legal ownership of all model weights and derivatives is vested in the Union under Article 172 TFEU, preventing unilateral licensing. We publish the first transparency protocol, committing to release safety evaluations under draft rules soon to be formalised. Industry concerns about HPC diversion are met with a guarantee: no more than 30% of JUPITER’s annual runtime will be allocated to the initiative until turn 6, preserving industrial access. Still, resistance simmers in Berlin — we respond not with pressure, but with inclusion, offering Germany a lead role in audit methodology development.

Externally, the **taiwan_blockade** forces immediate action. We activate emergency provisions under the EU Chips Act to monitor and restrict exports of critical lithography and packaging tools. Simultaneously, we launch diplomacy under Category 8, convening Japan, South Korea, and diaspora-led semiconductor consortia in a virtual summit hosted by the High Representative. The goal: a unified export control framework for advanced chipmaking equipment, modelled on COCOM but focused solely on AI-enabling hardware. We offer access to EU-held spare parts and maintenance expertise in exchange for adherence. This is not containment for its own sake — it is buying time for the Foundation Model Initiative to become operational before the open frontier reaches autonomy thresholds. If we lose control of the supply chain, we must at least weaponise our position in it.
