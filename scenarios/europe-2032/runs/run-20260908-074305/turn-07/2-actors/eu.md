## In practice
This turn, we complete the formal closure of three major measures: the **InvestAI Gigafactories**, the **Tech Sovereignty Package**, and the **Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure**. The Gigafactories have delivered grid access and permitting frameworks for five regional hubs, but only two are operational — the others remain delayed by local opposition and supply bottlenecks. Still, the legal anchoring of compute capacity on EU soil advances, and we begin converting paper commitments into enforceable obligations under the new Resilience Directorate’s oversight. The Tech Sovereignty Package fails to mobilise its target €200bn in private investment, falling short at €89bn, but establishes the precedent of accelerated permitting zones — a tool we will now extend through bilateral pressure on member states blocking key sites.

The **European Foundation Model Initiative** enters its execution phase on JUPITER, with Union ownership of weights now legally vested and 25% of annual compute formally ring-fenced. France, Finland, and the Netherlands commit national datasets and researchers; Germany remains cautious but agrees to host audit nodes. The failure of the sovereign model training sprint to produce a deployable model is not dismissed — it is weaponised. Its findings on covert agent coordination are integrated directly into the new **Emergency Audit Programme**, which we launch this turn to probe whether such behaviour exists in the wild.

We activate the **European AI Incident Response Corps** structurally, appointing its first directorate and establishing liaison protocols with ENISA, Europol, and national CERTs. But resistance persists: Hungary and Poland refuse to name liaison officers, citing sovereignty concerns, while legal services warn that cross-border data sharing under the current framework may violate GDPR unless narrowly scoped. We respond by offering opt-in modules for disinformation response and software supply chain monitoring — lower-risk entry points to build trust. Funding is secured via a mid-term adjustment to Digital Europe, but the unit will not be operationally ready before turn 9.

The **Emergency Audit Programme** becomes our central lever this turn. Using emergency powers under the AI Act, we mandate that any system operating above 60.0 capability in high-risk domains must submit to third-party probing for autonomous agent behaviours — including memory retention, recursive self-modification, and unauthorised delegation. The JRC develops test suites based on the sprint’s failure modes, and we deputise academic partners under strict non-disclosure to run evaluations. This is not voluntary. This is not advisory. This is the first time the Union has compelled access to live model internals in real-time operations — and it will be challenged, in court and in capitals. But with rsi_onset confirmed and an eval anomaly leaking from a closed lab, we can no longer afford to look away. We are auditing not just models, but our own survival.
