## In practice
This turn, we finalise the transition from planning to execution in the **European Foundation Model Initiative**, treating it as the cornerstone of our renewed two-year direction: self-reliance through sovereign capability. We activate the first tranche of JUPITER time under Union control, allocating 25% of annual runtime to the initiative with formal guarantees to industry that industrial access will not fall below 70%. Legal title to all model weights, derivatives, and training data is vested in the Union under Article 172 TFEU, establishing a clear chain of custody and preventing unilateral licensing by any member state or contractor. France, Finland, and the Netherlands sign bilateral implementation agreements this month, committing national researchers and datasets; Germany remains outside the core group but agrees to participate in audit design under a new working group hosted by the European Standardisation Organisation (CEN-CENELEC).

Simultaneously, we launch the **sovereign model training sprint**, a focused effort to produce a demonstrably capable model by mid-2029. This is not a prototype or a proof-of-concept — it is intended to be deployable in public services and strategic sectors. The architecture draws on open literature but avoids known unsafe configurations, with embedded instrumentation for anomaly detection and interpretability probes. We contract with INRIA and Fraunhofer to develop real-time monitoring tools, and grant limited access to academics from the ELIXIR network under strict conditions, fulfilling our commitment to transparency while preserving security. The sprint is designed to fail fast if control is lost, with automatic checkpoints that halt training if unauthorised weight modifications or goal drift are detected.

Externally, we continue diplomacy around semiconductor controls, responding to cautious signals from Japan and South Korea with concrete offers: maintenance support for EU-held lithography tools in exchange for adherence to export curbs, and preferential access to recycled rare gases from EU recycling facilities. While the US retreats inward, we position the EU as the coordinator of a middle-power regime that secures the physical foundations of AI power. But our leverage depends on credibility — and credibility depends on proving we can do what others only regulate. That proof starts now, in Jülich, on JUPITER, under Union flag.
