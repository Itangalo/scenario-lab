## New measure

**EU Legal Authority for Compelled Model Access (Directive Proposal)**  
Establishes a binding legal framework granting the Union authority to compel full model weight disclosure and runtime access for high-risk AI systems under emergency and ongoing oversight powers, resolving jurisdictional disputes and enabling coercive audit.

## In practice

This turn, we launch the drafting of a new Directive under Article 114 TFEU, to be proposed to the European Parliament and Council by Q2 2032, establishing a clear, enforceable legal basis for Union-level compelled access to model weights, training data, and runtime environments for systems classified as high-risk under revised AI Act thresholds. The proposal will be led by DG CONNECT in coordination with the Legal Service, the AI Office, and the European Commission’s Crisis Response Coordination Cell, with input from the AI Safety Board and red-team units at INRIA and TU Munich. It will define conditions under which emergency audits can override commercial secrecy, national security claims, or extraterritorial objections, drawing on the contested but narrow precedent of Article 16(4) TEU and aiming to replace ad hoc emergency powers with a durable, proportionate regime.

We are already facing resistance: Germany insists on national veto rights over foreign model access, while the U.S. developer community warns of retaliatory data restrictions. To secure Council support, we offer a dual-track approach: member states retain co-ownership of audit findings and a role in the designation of high-risk systems via the Internal Market Council, while non-EU developers gain early notification and appeal rights through a newly empowered AI Compliance Panel. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with a dedicated €400 million envelope for legal enforcement infrastructure, including secure evaluation facilities and cross-border data trustees.

At the same time, we formally pause the EU-owned foundation model training run planned for JUPITER and LUMI. Without legal authority to audit existing systems, we cannot responsibly train our own. The 35% reserved compute remains locked, but architectural decisions and team mobilisation are suspended pending the outcome of this legal effort. Sovereign capability cannot be built in the dark — and right now, the law leaves us blind.
