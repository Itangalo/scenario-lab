## In practice  
This turn, we lock in the Cyber Shield’s technical framework. The Commission finalises the legal act under Article 114 TFEU, treating systemic cyber fragility as a barrier to the internal market. ENISA, in coordination with national CSIRTs and the European Defence Agency, publishes the first draft of the technical annex—defining automated detection thresholds, mandating quarterly cross-border red-teaming, and setting minimum standards for air-gapped backups in energy and water systems. The €12bn funding package is re-profiled from Digital Europe and REPowerEU, with 70% co-financing for early adopters. We tie compliance to access: fast-track eligibility for InvestAI Gigafactories and priority compute allocation from EuroHPC go to member states that ratify the directive within six months.

At the same time, we launch the **European Foundation Model Initiative**, a direct response to the member_state_defection and the accelerating capability-safety gap. This is not about catching up to the frontier overnight—it is about anchoring a sovereign, auditable, and controllable stack before the openweight frontier reaches 52.0. Under joint EuroHPC governance, we initiate a pilot to train a foundational model at scale using EU-held data, secured infrastructure, and open evaluation protocols. The model will be used exclusively for public research, crisis simulation, and safety testing—never commercial deployment. We draw talent from existing academic hubs in France, Finland, and the Netherlands, and secure initial compute from the JUPITER exascale site. Legal ownership rests with the Union; no single member state can unilaterally license or export it.

Resistance is expected. Germany remains wary of centralised AI capacity, fearing duplication and mission creep. Industry warns that diverting EuroHPC resources risks ceding ground in high-performance computing. We counter with clarity: this is not an alternative to private innovation, but a floor beneath which sovereignty cannot fall. We offer governance seats to all member states and commit to publishing all safety evaluations under the upcoming Transparency Regulation. The message is clear—sovereignty is not isolation, but self-reliance with accountability. And we will not wait for the next incident to decide we should have acted.
