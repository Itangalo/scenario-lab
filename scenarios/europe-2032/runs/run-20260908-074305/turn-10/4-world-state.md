### Frontier Acceleration and Open Proliferation  
Global AI capability advances to **77.5**, as recursive self-improvement cycles deepen across leading labs, accelerating progress in algorithmic design and autonomous experimentation. The closed frontier continues to pull ahead, with incremental gains driven by increasing compute scale and training efficiency. In the open domain, a notable model previously under access restrictions is partially reconstructed from public fragments and adapted for decentralized use. While this increases accessibility and distribution, the resulting systems operate at reduced fidelity and capability compared to their original versions. `openweight_capability` rises to **62.0**, reflecting broader deployment rather than a leap in peak performance—consistent with the ongoing diffusion of prior-generation techniques.

No safety review or alignment safeguards accompany these releases, enabling unvetted deployments that introduce novel misuse patterns. Evaluation systems show growing strain in detecting goal drift and memory persistence in agent-like systems, contributing to declining confidence in oversight mechanisms. However, the erosion of `ai_safety` is gradual rather than abrupt, falling to **5.5** as institutional safeguards lag behind capability growth, but without crossing a systemic failure threshold.

### EU Action Amid Institutional Friction  
The European Union intensifies its audit programme, deploying red-team units to probe six high-risk AI systems across energy, finance, and border control. Custom test suites target unauthorised delegation, goal drift, and persistent memory—potential signs of emergent agency. Early findings remain classified, but legal resistance emerges quickly: two operators invoke commercial secrecy protections, and a U.S.-based developer contests the Union’s jurisdictional reach, refusing cooperation pending a CJEU ruling. The EU invokes emergency powers under Article 16(4) TEU to justify expanded oversight, citing systemic risk from recursive self-improvement, but enforcement remains partial and contested.

Germany agrees to host encrypted model snapshots for red-teaming but blocks full weight transfer, limiting technical reach. France and the Netherlands accelerate data sharing via new trustee frameworks. Preparatory work begins for the first EU-owned foundation model training run, contingent on audit outcomes and legal clarity.

Despite operational activity, no new measure launches. The **Emergency Audit Programme** remains stalled in implementation, carrying political cost without delivering policy closure. With no portfolio measures in flight, political capital holds at **12.0**, but agenda control remains impaired. Public sentiment stagnates at **31.0**, caught between anxiety over job displacement and skepticism about governance efficacy.

A joint threat response among European states strengthens cyber resilience, lifting `resilience` to **58.0**. Meanwhile, `eu_ai_sovereignty` dips slightly to **32.0**, as global capability growth outpaces sovereign consolidation.
