### Lockdown on top of blackout
Autumn began with locked dispatch screens and ended with empty streets. A largely automated ransomware sweep rolled through municipal systems, clinics and care networks just as a new engineered pathogen spread globally. Hospitals triaged on paper while wards filled; power and water operators ran on clean rebuild kits and reserve licences rushed from joint stocks.

Where joint cyber playbooks were followed, essential services degraded rather than stopped. Where feeds were thin — above all in the capital running its own cheaper hosting deal — recovery stalled and returned data was unusable. Liaison officers stayed, but triage priority quietly shifted to interoperable cities.

### A bridge paused
The audited-hospital channel for tailored therapies held for weeks, with published logs and nurses explaining each dose. Then containment protocols froze wider rollout. Patient groups that had been cautiously hopeful turned angry as clinics closed to all but emergency and pandemic work. The benefits and policing system stayed frozen, insurers again refused cover.

### Machines and chokepoints
In ports and warehouses foreign-built robots kept running through night shifts with skeleton crews, while repair and care work stayed stubbornly manual. Works councils called it abandonment during a health emergency.

Washington at the same time forced further cuts to servicing of older lithography equipment, reaching deep into ordinary-chip supply. Brussels offered quiet legal shielding to the supplier but opened no new front, lacking money and room. The Union ended the year with a tested municipal recovery surge finally delivered, a new bio-containment surge only starting, and dependence visible in every clinic, substation and loading bay.
