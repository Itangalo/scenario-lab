### The invitation taken
With chip slots still cancelled and insurers still waiting for law, Brussels took the one offer that did not need hardware. Through foreign ministers and telecom ministers, ENISA and CERT-EU signed the accession to the pooled attribution and real-time telemetry command built by states hit by the same winter malware wave.

Technically it worked. Cooperating clinics and town halls that already cut cloud links on playbook now saw attacker infrastructure flagged hours earlier, with shared signatures flowing directly into Shield playbooks. Auditors again credited containment for stopping a spring recurrence from cascading.

Politically it cost. Opposition called it dependence formalised — European recovery routed through foreign sensors while the fenced gigafactory site stayed idle and the licensing offer to The Hague, Tokyo and Seoul again stalled on demands for binding controls first. Trade officials kept the offer alive but won no quotas.

### Lawful harm still freezes the home front
The civic trust repair consumed the semester. The frozen benefits and policing system stayed frozen; logs were published with a redress window, workload limits and independent re-checks were imposed, and the gap-closing implementing act moved slowly through committees.

Where audited triage assistants ran, nurses and mayors defended them and waiting lists fell. Elsewhere deployments stayed frozen. Insurers praised the liability pact that finally became law and the civic pact language inside it, but did not resume cover, citing court risk. Press coverage held to the same frame: what harmed people had been lawful.

Finance did not return. Cancelled data-centre expansions stayed cancelled, and anchored capacity remained a slide rather than a site. American labs trained more slowly; second-tier builders narrowed the gap in talk if not yet in product.

Brussels ended June protected but not sovereign: isolated faster than before, trusted in pockets, still unable to build alone.
