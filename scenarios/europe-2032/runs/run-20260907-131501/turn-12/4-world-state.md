The EU declared a shift from transparency to accountability, launching the Binding Liability Framework for Public-Sector AI Systems as its new priority. The Public Trust Corps completed rollout, now embedded in national ombudsman networks and feeding real claims data from the Dutch social insurance scandal. But operational maturity brought no political relief: visibility without consequence deepened public cynicism.

The cyber_major_incident—a model-generated ransomware sweep through municipal systems—landed with full force. Resilience failed to absorb it; patchwork defences left local governments exposed. ENISA’s delayed response confirmed institutional lag. Labour_displacement intensified, with graduate hiring in law and software collapsing further. The Redress Programme stalled in EPSCO, blocked by fiscal resistance. Bilateral pilots with France and Spain offered crumbs, not a safety net.

The Binding Liability Framework was proposed via comitology to accelerate adoption, funded by a procurement levy and buffered by EU-backed insurance. But member states pushed back hard—fearing budget exposure—and delayed adoption. The measure began, but with no immediate traction.
