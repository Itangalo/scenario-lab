# Statements: The European Union (turn 12)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore political agency by embedding enforceable redress into the core of AI governance — through binding liability for public-sector AI harms, operationalised Algorithmic Accountability Boards with investigative and sanctioning powers, and a Public Trust Corps that delivers not just transparency but material repair to affected individuals and communities.

The public sees no consequence for failure, no remedy for loss. Our commitment must now shift from visibility to accountability — not merely showing how systems fail, but ensuring someone pays when they do. This recalibration is not optional: without enforceable redress, the EU forfeits its claim to political agency in the AI era.
  - Evidence quoted: A large, largely automated attack lands. Its form is chosen at the time – a ransomware sweep across public services, a compromised software dependency with an unknown blast radius, or disruption of infrastructure directly – and severity scales with how far offensive capability has outrun defence. Damage is real and public, defenders are visibly behind, and attribution takes months. Whoever ran it, the tooling was model-generated.

Measurable job losses attributed to AI in named sectors, with the graduate market worst hit: entry-level positions in law, accountancy, software, customer operations and administration are not replaced. The numbers are argued about; the absence of hiring is not.
  - Reason: The cyber incident and job losses directly expose failure of current transparency measures, undermining trust and forcing shift to enforceable accountability.
