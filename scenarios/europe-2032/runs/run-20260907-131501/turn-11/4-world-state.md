### A Turn of Damage Control

The EU entered this half-year under fire. A major ransomware campaign, exploiting a model-generated vulnerability in a widely used open-source library, crippled municipal services across Belgium, southern Italy, and parts of Germany. Forensic analysis by ENISA was slow, underfunded, and inconclusive—public confidence in the Union’s ability to defend its own infrastructure eroded further. Simultaneously, labour markets in law, accountancy, and software development showed unmistakable signs of structural collapse at the entry level, with graduate hiring evaporating and automation of junior roles now the norm.

In response, the EU fast-tracked elements of the Public Trust Corps, integrating redress functions and bias alerts into its mandate. Pilot programmes launched in France and Finland, but implementation remained fragmented. The upgraded AI Incident Dashboard went live, yet its warnings arrived too late to prevent the cyberattack, reinforcing perceptions of reactive rather than preventive governance.

The new **EU-Wide Redress and Reform Programme** was formally launched, offering wage insurance and retraining for displaced graduates. However, negotiations with fiscally cautious member states stalled, and no major disbursements occurred this turn. The initiative provided symbolic relief but no immediate material impact.

Meanwhile, the **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Sovereign Cyber Shield** both completed their rollouts. The former delivered new domestic compute capacity, though not yet at scale to offset dependency. The latter hardened Tier 1 infrastructure, but uneven adoption left local governments exposed—exactly where the ransomware struck.

Political capital, already near collapse, dipped further due to portfolio strain. The Trust Corps remained the named priority, but with limited traction, its symbolic weight outweighed its operational success. Public sentiment fell as job losses mounted and high-profile systems failed, feeding a narrative of managed decline.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

AI capability advanced to 69.5, driven by continued investment in the U.S. and China. Open-weight models reached 57.5, nearing thresholds for generating functional cyber exploits autonomously. No safety breakthroughs occurred, and no new safeguards were embedded in deployed systems—leaving the gap between capability and control wider than ever.
