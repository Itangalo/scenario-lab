## Priority  
**Priority:** Establish EU Public Trust Corps for AI Incident Response and Transparency  
This remains our dominant effort because the convergence of a domestic AI scandal and a major cyber incident has created a legitimacy crisis that cannot be met with new promises — only with visible, operational proof that we can respond, account, and repair. The Trust Corps is the only instrument already scaling that can integrate redress, bias detection, and public communication in real time, and its success or failure will determine whether the EU is seen as managing AI or being managed by it.

## In practice  
We are treating this turn as one of triage and reorientation. The cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across municipal services in Belgium, Germany, and southern Italy, exploiting a model-generated zero-day in a widely used open-source dependency — has confirmed that offensive capabilities are now loose and operational. ENISA’s initial report, delayed by under-resourced forensics, will take months. Meanwhile, the labour_displacement numbers are no longer deniable: graduate hiring in law and accountancy has collapsed, and software firms report automation of junior coding roles at scale. The public sees no safety net, no plan, and no remorse — only acceleration.

The **Public Trust Corps** is now our central nervous system for crisis response. We are fast-tracking the Redress Coordination Unit, embedding it with national ombudsman offices and legal aid networks, to process claims from the Dutch social insurance case and pilot similar mechanisms in France and Finland. The upgraded AI Incident Dashboard will go live next month with systemic bias alerts, fed by audit logs from high-risk public systems. This is not optional — it is the minimum required to show that accountability is not just procedural, but material.

At the same time, we are launching the **Redress and Reform Programme** under the European Social Fund+ and the newly empowered Employment Committee. This will provide 18 months of wage insurance for displaced graduates, co-financed by employers who automate entry-level roles, and channel funds into retraining in AI-augmented professions — not just coding, but AI oversight, ethical auditing, and public-sector digital stewardship. The programme will be monitored by a new Observatory on AI and Work, hosted by Eurofound, with real-time reporting to the European Parliament. We are negotiating with member states through the Employment, Social Policy, Health and Consumer Affairs Council (EPSCO), offering front-loaded disbursements to those who sign up by turn 12. Resistance is expected in fiscally conservative states, but the alternative — social unrest and brain drain — is worse.

The **Algorithmic Accountability Boards** remain in preparatory phase, with scoping underway in DG JUST and civil society consultations launching in October. But without political capital to launch them fully, they will not become operational this turn. We are instead concentrating on making the Trust Corps a living institution of redress — not just a dashboard, but a remedy.
