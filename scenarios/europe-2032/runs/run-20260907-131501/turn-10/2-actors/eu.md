## Priority
**Priority:** Establish EU Public Trust Corps for AI Incident Response and Transparency  
This remains our dominant effort because the automated_decision_scandal has made clear that trust cannot be rebuilt through top-down assurances alone — we must now demonstrate both responsiveness *and* accountability when AI fails, especially when it fails *within* public institutions. The Trust Corps is the only instrument already in flight that can operationalise both, provided we reorient it toward redress as much as communication.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as one of reckoning: not only did foreign access collapse, but our own systems have now been found to harm those they were meant to serve. The ombudsman’s report on the Dutch social insurance AI — which denied benefits to thousands based on flawed behavioural proxies — has become a symbol of everything gone wrong. Protests in Utrecht mirror those in Lyon; the message is identical: “You built systems that see us as threats.” We cannot deploy transparency drills while ignoring that the machines we trusted have already judged people guilty without trial.

The **Public Trust Corps** is being reshaped in real time. DG HOME and DG CONNECT are adding two new functions: first, a Redress Coordination Unit, embedded with national ombudsmen and civil society groups, to track harms from AI in public services and fast-track compensation. Second, we are upgrading the AI Incident Dashboard to include not just cyber threats and outages, but *systemic bias alerts* — flagged anomalies in public-sector AI decisions, cross-referenced with complaints and audit trails. This will go live in pilot mode in France and Finland by April, with full rollout by turn 11.

Meanwhile, the **Sovereign Cyber Shield**, though formally complete, is being audited for internal coherence. Poland and Spain remain partial holdouts, but ENISA’s scorecard — delayed no longer — will be published next month, naming non-compliant systems. The EIB’s link between recovery funding and compliance is now active: three Polish municipalities have requested emergency integration support to unlock funds. But legitimacy demands more than coercion. That is why we are launching the **Algorithmic Accountability Boards** — not as a replacement for the Trust Corps, but as its necessary counterpart. These boards will include technologists, ethicists, and citizens selected by sortition, with subpoena power over model logs and decision rationales. Their first mandate: review the Dutch case and issue binding recommendations within six months. This is not optional reform. It is the price of being trusted with power.
