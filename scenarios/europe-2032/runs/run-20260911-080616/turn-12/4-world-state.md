### The screening surge
Brussels moved fast on paper in January. Health emergency funds were redirected to wastewater sequencing in major hubs, emergency-room triage screening and renewed checks on DNA-synthesis providers. Money from recovery and health budgets flowed only where screening data was shared, copying the audit playbook used for isolation gates. Labs, pharmacies and synthesis firms got visits; general wards did not.

Implementation was uneven. Large university hospitals in the west plugged in quickly. Municipal clinics, eastern labs and private synthesis intermediaries haggled over costs, staffing and who owned the sequence data. Franco-Dutch medical teams stayed on call but were stretched thin across follow-up visits. The programme bought coverage, not closure.

### A contested warning
Through the spring a genome-model study claiming a viable human-infecting design circulated in biosecurity circles. Methodologists attacked it, others accused the authors of publishing too much, and the fight stayed technical. No public alarm followed, but inside health agencies the mood darkened: with controls still paper-based, the next attempt would not be caught by a checklist.

### Jobs that did not come back
Entry-level hiring in routine coding, standard analysis, first-draft work and tier-one support failed to recover. Firms insisted this was targeted automation, not a general wave — juniors elsewhere were kept, some early cuts were even reversed. Unions called it a breach of promise; employers pointed to productivity data. Evening news led with graduates without offers.

Against that, city halls could point to something working. Several municipal health administrations using European-hosted models cut waiting lists and decision times measurably, and the Commission claimed credit loudly. It steadied talks with unions but did not shift the wider mood shaped by paper wards and job anxiety.

Where isolation gates held, winter intrusions stopped at the boundary. Where the new bio screening lagged, officials privately admitted gaps remained.
