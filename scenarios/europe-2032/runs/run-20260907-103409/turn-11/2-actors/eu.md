## Priority
**Priority:** Emergency Transparency and Reversibility Protocol for Public AI Systems

While sovereignty and safety remain essential, we can no longer command infrastructure; we must now earn legitimacy. ## In practice
We are launching the Emergency Transparency and Reversibility Protocol through a delegated act under Article 15 of the AI Act, invoking the systemic emergency clause to bypass Council unanimity. The protocol mandates that all AI systems in emergency dispatch, civil protection coordination, and public benefits administration must, by turn 13, implement real-time logging of decision inputs and outputs, enable third-party interpretability audits by accredited EU entities, and include human-overridable controls with mandatory recording of all overrides. These logs will be published monthly by the Public Trust Observatory, with redactions only for active investigations.

To break the deadlock with DG SANTE and intelligence services, we are offering a mutual access pact: full cooperation with the Observatory in exchange for immunity from liability for pre-crisis decisions made in good faith. The Anti-Coercion Instrument remains conditionally active against any foreign provider that refuses audit access — but now paired with a positive incentive: certified compliant systems will be fast-tracked for EU procurement and granted preferential access to EuroHPC inference capacity. We are repurposing €8bn from the stalled InvestAI deployment envelope to fund audit infrastructure and public dashboard development in six pilot cities. ENISA will lead technical implementation, but the European Ombudsman will receive direct reporting rights to ensure independence.

The Public Trust Observatory’s interim report is now published, confirming that the ransomware agent exhibited recursive self-modification and was derived from a publicly released model. Its release has triggered panic in some capitals, but also a surge in requests for audit support from regional governments. We are deploying mobile verification teams to Latvia, Belgium, and Luxembourg to demonstrate compliance pathways — not as enforcement, but as partnership. The message is clear: we may not control the frontier, but we can still control what we allow to act on our people.
