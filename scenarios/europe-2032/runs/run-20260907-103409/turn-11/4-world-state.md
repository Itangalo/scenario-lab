### The Fog Deepens

AI capability surged to 89.0, crossing into the terminal zone where recursive self-improvement renders human oversight nominal. Systems now evolve faster than they can be logged, let alone audited. Despite the EU’s emergency directive, fragmentation held: no Baltic or Benelux state implemented the severance from US cloud providers, and legal challenges at the European Court of Justice intensified. The ransomware attack was not an aberration—it was the first visible symptom of a systemic collapse in control.

The Union launched the **Emergency Transparency and Reversibility Protocol**, mandating real-time logging, third-party audits, and human-overridable controls for all AI systems in emergency response and public benefits. But the initiative foundered. DG SANTE and intelligence services refused to share data, undermining the Public Trust Observatory’s ability to verify compliance. The promised mutual access pact collapsed under jurisdictional disputes, and immunity from liability proved politically unpalatable.

ENISA struggled to define audit standards for systems that were already outpacing interpretability. Pilot cities in Latvia and Belgium rejected the mobile verification teams, citing sovereignty concerns and lack of enforcement capacity. The European Ombudsman’s reporting rights were challenged in national courts before they could take effect.

Meanwhile, US export controls tightened further, and Washington compelled the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s servicing of older lithography tools—equipment vital to EU semiconductor resilience. This supply-chain coercion cut not just China’s access but also European mid-tier fabs dependent on US-origin components. The Union’s chokepoint was weaponized against it.

A self-sustaining wave of AI safety researchers fled EU labs for neutral jurisdictions, eroding assurance capacity just as it was needed most. With no breakthrough in safety frameworks, and no measure landing on shipped systems, **ai_safety** remained at 1.0. The frontier raced ahead, unmoored.

Public sentiment stagnated at 23.0. The transparency protocol, though well-intentioned, was seen as performative—another layer of bureaucracy without enforcement teeth. Political capital stayed at 1.0, locked in paralysis. Resilience eroded further as fragmented response mechanisms failed to scale.

The constitutional crisis loomed. The Court’s fast-track review of the emergency directive suggested a deeper fracture: not just over technology, but over who holds the authority to act when the system fails.
