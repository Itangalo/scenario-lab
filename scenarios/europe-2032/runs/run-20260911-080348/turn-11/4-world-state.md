### The machines arrive
By autumn the docks told the story first. Logistics hubs in Rotterdam, Hamburg and Łódź took delivery of commercial humanoids — Chinese-built chassis running American control stacks — that learned a warehouse in days. Municipal contractors followed, trialling street-cleaning and sorting crews. Managers spoke of productivity; works councils spoke of rosters halved with no interval left to retrain.

At the same time a new open-weight release spread to hundreds of thousands of downloads in a week. Whatever the closed labs could do was now, within points, on private servers permanently. Police reports of underground clinics offering custom therapeutics from leaked design models multiplied, still single-source and denied, while insurers quietly stopped writing cover for outages blamed on autonomous behaviour.

### Shield half-built
Brussels pushed the Containment Shield toward operation. ENISA pooled telemetry with the joint attribution cell, grid and hospital operators rehearsed isolate-and-manual-fallback, EuroHPC space was held for clean rebuilds. The winter agent was quieter but not gone — tooling rewrites were found in two more banks — and drills exposed how thin staffing remained.

The older Recovery Pact formally closed, its ransomware-era playbooks filed into the new cell. Restoration improved in finance, stayed patchy in clinics and town halls.

### A battery to point at
The Commission claimed a European win: a Fraunhofer–Aleph Alpha agentic run that cracked dendrite stability in solid-state electrolytes, hailed inside battery chemistry as a landmark, now moving to pilot lines in Germany and Sweden. Ministers toured the lines promising maintenance jobs.

The new Shock Absorber — wage insurance and employer-paid retraining levies for displaced logistics and service workers — passed, but payments will take a year. On the ground it read as a promise against layoff notices already posted. Graduate queues lengthened, and procurement officers warned the Union was automating its own ports with machines it could neither build nor fully inspect.
