### Triage without relief
The second half of 2032 was defined by the February cutoff still biting. The pooled inference scheme for hospitals and ministries moved from paper to triage: DG CNECT directed spare capacity from certified sites, emergency teams migrated workloads off the dark American system, and talks with Japan, Korea and Canada explored swaps of near-frontier models for hosting and joint testing.

It helped, but slowly. Clinicians reported summarisation tools back in thinner, slower form, with manual checks filling gaps. Two ministry systems stayed on workarounds. Allied contacts produced memoranda and pilot lots, not volume. Hosting money held conditional on interconnection annoyed some operators, who said the conditions slowed what little capacity existed.

### A bridge for graduates
To stop the jobs crisis merging with sovereignty anger, labour ministers launched the Graduate Entry Bridge through repurposed social funds: a 6-12 month income bridge tied to paid traineeships plus hiring credits for small firms and municipalities in digital, care-administration and retrofit work. Student groups were invited into design; a few walkouts were called off.

Uptake was uneven. Regions with strong employment services placed cohorts quickly; others stalled over co-financing paperwork and employer scepticism that traineeships would become real jobs. Graduate leafleting outside data centres continued into autumn, though with less fusion with cutoff protests where the bridge was visible.

By December, services limped on, the continuity build inched forward, and politics thinned further under the weight of running two efforts at once with no finished delivery to show.
