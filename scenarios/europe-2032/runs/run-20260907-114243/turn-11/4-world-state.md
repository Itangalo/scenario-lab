### A Directive Unravels

The Emergency Sovereign Continuity Directive, declared complete this turn, collapses under the weight of its own implementation. Early simulation exercises reveal catastrophic flaws in the Sovereign Core’s design: conflicting access controls, broken isolation boundaries, and cascading failure modes that could paralyze emergency response during an actual crisis. What was meant to secure continuity becomes a liability. The European Commission suspends enforcement, halting migration from foreign cloud providers across critical systems.

### Audit Over Acceleration

In response, the EU signals intent to launch the *Independent Audit and Recovery of Sovereign Core Systems* as a future measure, with senior officials designating it a desired priority. However, governance delays and internal disputes over mandate scope prevent even formal proposal this turn. DG CONNECT and key contractors resist, warning that premature disclosure will expose vulnerabilities to adversaries. The Commission signals that full compliance will be tied to the release of €18bn in remaining InvestAI repurposing funds, but no decision is taken to advance it as a new measure. With the portfolio already carrying two active measures — Emergency Sovereign Continuity Directive and Public Trust Observatory — and no political capital to justify expansion, no new initiative can be introduced this turn.

Public sentiment falls to 18.0 as media frame the reversal as institutional incompetence. The recent annulment of a national election, officially linked to AI-driven information manipulation, deepens anxiety about governance in the age of opaque systems.
