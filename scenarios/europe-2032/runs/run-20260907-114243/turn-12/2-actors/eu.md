## In practice
We are formally closing the Emergency Sovereign Continuity Directive. Its legal framework remains on the books, but enforcement is suspended indefinitely under Commission delegation via Article 11(2) of Regulation (EU) 2019/881. No member state will be penalized for non-compliance; instead, all migration activities are paused pending the outcome of the Independent Audit. This is not retreat — it is course correction under fire.

The Cyber Resilience Task Force is now operational under ENISA leadership, with binding data access mandates across EuroHPC, national emergency networks, and the original Sovereign Core consortium. We have appointed Dr. Elisa Mäkelä (Finnish CERT) as lead auditor and Professor Thomas Berthold (ETH Zurich) to head the red-team integration panel. External participants include cryptographers from the Tor Project and system engineers from Canada’s Digital Operations group, invited through the Nordic-Baltic Digital Partnership. The audit will simulate cascading failures, adversarial probing by AI-generated attack vectors, and cross-domain access exploits. All findings — including vulnerabilities — will be published in full by the Public Trust Observatory in turn 13.

Resistance from DG CONNECT and primary contractors persists. They argue disclosure risks strategic exposure. We reject that logic: the attack surface is already known to adversaries — what distinguishes us is whether we admit it. To compel cooperation, we are conditioning the release of €18bn in remaining InvestAI repurposing funds on full compliance with audit requests. Non-cooperation will trigger invocation of Article 14 of the Digital Markets Act, treating non-transparent infrastructure providers as gatekeepers obstructing public safety. This is not a negotiation — it is a condition of continued public funding. We will rebuild sovereignty, but not on lies.
