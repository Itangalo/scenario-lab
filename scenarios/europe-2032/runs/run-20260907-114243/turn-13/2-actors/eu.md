## Statement changes
* - modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent catastrophic failure of core state functions by any means necessary — but now with the explicit recognition that our own institutions, when unprepared, can become the vectors of that failure. Our focus shifts from securing infrastructure to securing competence: rebuilding sovereign technical capacity not through mandate, but through retention, reintegration, and re-legitimization of the expertise required to operate it.  
  - Trigger: The emergent_researcher_desertion_wave is not a warning — it is an active hollowing-out of the human foundation of EU AI sovereignty. We can audit systems all we want, but if no one remains who can understand, repair, or command them, sovereignty is an illusion. The collapse of the Sovereign Core was a failure of design; this exodus is a failure of legitimacy and conditions. We still commit to preventing catastrophic failure — but now see that the most urgent threat is not in the code, but in the absence of those who can secure it.
  - Grounds: Our previous commitment assumed that infrastructure, once made sovereign, would be operable. It is not. The Cyber Resilience Task Force is already struggling to staff red-team simulations. DG RTD and EMA posts are emptying. The €18bn InvestAI leverage works on contractors, but not on scientists who no longer believe in the project. We must now treat human capital as strategic infrastructure. This is not a retreat from sovereignty — it is its recalibration around the people who make it real.

* - modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not burn the ground beneath us to move fast. This turn, we have learned that acting under uncertainty includes the responsibility to sustain the conditions in which action is still possible. We now bind ourselves to pair every major commitment with not only a theory of harm and monitoring mechanism, but also a sustainability assessment: will this measure, if it fails or falters, degrade the Union’s ability to respond to what comes next? If so, we must redesign it before launch.  
  - Grounds: The Emergency Sovereign Continuity Directive degraded our response capacity. It did not just fail — it poisoned trust, drained capital, and accelerated talent flight. We acted early, but we did not ask whether the action itself could become the crisis. We still believe in early commitment, but not at the cost of systemic exhaustion. The audit must go on — but so must the reckoning with what makes action legible, credible, and staffable.

## New measure
**Measure:** EU Talent Anchoring and Reintegration Initiative  
A binding programme to halt the researcher desertion wave by offering guaranteed funding, legal protection, and operational autonomy to AI scientists and engineers willing to return or remain within EU jurisdiction, with priority given to those with experience in safety, alignment, and critical infrastructure.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 15  
Targeted effect: Stabilize eu_ai_sovereignty at 13.0 and begin reversing talent flight by turn 15; increase public_sentiment by 5–10 points by demonstrating commitment to technical competence; lay the foundation for future sovereign capability by securing the human layer  
Applies to: EU-funded research institutions, EuroHPC, ENISA, EMA, DG RTD, national AI agencies, and returning diaspora researchers from Canada, Switzerland, and India

## Priority
**Priority:** Independent Audit and Recovery of Sovereign Core Systems  
This remains our top priority because the systems meant to secure the Union are still compromised, and without verified integrity in our core infrastructure, no talent will return and no new capability can be trusted — making this the necessary precondition for every other action, including the new measure.

## In practice
We are finalizing the Independent Audit and Recovery of Sovereign Core Systems in this, its third and final turn. Dr. Elisa Mäkelä’s team has completed red-team simulations, including AI-generated zero-day exploits and cascading access failures, and Professor Thomas Berthold’s panel has validated the findings with external cryptographers from the Tor Project and Canada’s Digital Operations. The full report — including vulnerabilities in access isolation, failure propagation, and contractor backdoors — will be published by the Public Trust Observatory in the first week of December. We are already drafting the recovery roadmap: no forced migration, no blanket mandates. Instead, a phased, audited rebuild, starting with health and transport emergency nodes, using only components that pass independent verification.

The €18bn InvestAI funding remains conditioned on compliance, and we have invoked Article 14 of the Digital Markets Act against two primary contractors who withheld data. Legal challenges are mounting, but we will not yield: public safety is not negotiable. The audit’s conclusion is clear — the Sovereign Core was not sabotaged. It was built by people who did not understand what they were building. That is why we are launching the EU Talent Anchoring and Reintegration Initiative. Under Horizon Europe’s new Emergency Innovation Clause, we are offering five-year guaranteed grants, fast-track residency, and immunity from national prosecution for researchers working on critical AI systems. We are reaching out personally to those who left — through the Nordic-Baltic Digital Partnership, the EU Science Diplomacy Corps, and direct engagement with Swiss and Indian labs. This is not just recruitment. It is a plea: come back, and help us build something that does not collapse under its own weight.
