### Holding on what exists
July to December 2032 was a half-year of making do. With no new fund or law in the pipeline, Brussels kept the lights on through what had already been built: municipal repair crews under existing social and resilience budgets, cyber recovery playbooks run by ENISA, and health ministries staying on locally stored open models after the February cutoff.

The youth compact formally closed this autumn. In cities where registries were finally working, the effect was tangible — shorter queues, clinics with folders that matched patients, local papers naming the trainees in vests. Elsewhere contracts lapsed as emergency money ran out, and crews thinned. Functioning but unloved remained the verdict: services held, trust did not return.

### Distant guns, near prices
From late summer, extended exercises around Taiwan and rising shipping-insurance premiums filtered into European procurement. No chip shipment stopped outright, but forward prices for accelerators, memory and freight edged up, and suppliers added cautious language to delivery dates. Paris and Warsaw, already budgeting without American supply, saw their caution vindicated.

The Council asked foreign-policy and transport staff to map exposure — which ports, which stockpiles, which energy purchases would hurt first if the strait worsened — and asked the secretariat set up with middle-power partners to quietly compare notes on export licences and back-up compute. It was preparation on paper, deliberately without a spending commitment. Officials were frank in private: with coffers empty and cohesion thin, a large build announced now would fail.

### Contamination rumours
The other pressure was domestic. In several cities, whispers spread that trainees rebuilding registries had pasted sensitive clinic data into unvetted local assistants, or reused scripts of uncertain origin. No ministry confirmed a breach, a regional paper cited a single leaked memo, staff denied it. Yet the rumour stuck because the set-up invited it: young workers, borrowed models, old servers, too little supervision.

By December, Europe entered winter stable and exposed — clinics running, warehouses automated, docks divided over robots, and silicon still not sailing in the volumes promised.
