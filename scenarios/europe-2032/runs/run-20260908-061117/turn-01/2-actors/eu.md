## Priority

## In practice
We are treating the cyber_test_shot as a wake-up call: the intrusion was not targeted, not detected, and could have cascaded. This turn, we activate the Crisis Coordination Mechanism under Article 14 of the Treaty on European Union to fast-track a resilience surge. The ENISA mandate is expanded by delegated act to issue binding hardening standards for all operators of essential services, starting with electricity transmission, water utilities, and major ports. We are releasing €18bn from the Civil Protection Mechanism and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, conditioned on rapid adoption of AI-driven intrusion detection systems, zero-trust architecture rollouts, and mandatory red-teaming using certified third-party agents. Member states resist the cost and the precedent of binding technical mandates, so we are pairing this with a political offer: full EU liability assumption for any incident during the transition if standards are met, and a seat on the new Joint Resilience Board for each participating state. The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre in Bonn is being scaled up to coordinate R&D on AI-aware detection, and we are fast-tracking procurement rules to allow emergency purchases outside normal tender cycles. This is not about catching up — it is about ensuring that when the next intrusion comes, it does not become a collapse.
