### The Wake-Up Call  
A wave of AI-generated ransomware cripples regional hospitals and transit systems across Central Europe. The attack exploits zero-day vulnerabilities at scale, using polymorphic payloads that evade signature-based defences. Forensic analysis confirms the tooling was model-generated — not by frontier systems, but by open-weight models now capable of offensive automation. The breach lands with full public visibility, overwhelming local response capacity and exposing systemic fragility. Resilience absorbs part of the harm, but the incident confirms a new era: the offensive capability frontier has diffused.

### Response and Reckoning  
In response, the EU proposes the **EU Resilience Acceleration Facility**, a category 6 measure aimed at hardening energy, healthcare and transport systems. Binding standards for encryption, failover and anomaly detection are drafted, backed by conditional access to cohesion funds. France and Germany signal early support; Poland and Hungary resist, demanding exemptions on cost and sovereignty grounds. However, no binding rules take effect this turn — the measure is newly proposed and will require at least two turns to implement under standard timelines. Design and negotiation absorb political capital, but full rollout remains distant.

Three measures conclude this turn: the **Tech Sovereignty Package** unlocks private investment but fails to anchor compute on EU soil; the **EU Agency for AI Containment and Compliance** gains legal authority but lacks enforcement reach; the **Autonomous Systems Red-Teaming Initiative** delivers detection protocols now urgently needed. With these closures, the portfolio charge resets appropriately. The new Facility is named as the next priority, replacing the previous one, in line with the constraint of at most one active priority.

The **Sovereign Enforcement Directive**, though still ongoing, receives focused effort due to the crisis, contributing to a modest gain in political capital. Separately, a coordination breakthrough emerges as a coalition of middle powers aligns export controls and jointly negotiates compute access. This strengthens the EU’s leverage and signals a shift in global dynamics. Meanwhile, the U.S. administration takes office; its posture has not yet been announced.

Open-weight capability rises, reflecting diffusion from prior frontier releases now operationalized in adversarial contexts, but remains credibly behind the still-advancing frontier. The gap between `ai_capability` and `openweight_capability` narrows from 16.0 to 17.5 — consistent with incremental spillover rather than synchronized advancement. The slower rise in openweight capability reflects lag in absorption, not parity of progress.
