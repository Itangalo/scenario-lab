AI capability reaches 71.0 in mid-2028 as frontier systems advance, while open-weight models rise to 54.0, narrowing the gap to 17.0 due to diffusion of offensive tooling. A major wave of AI-generated ransomware—powered by open-weight models—cripples critical infrastructure across Central Europe, exposing systemic vulnerabilities and confirming the widespread diffusion of offensive AI capabilities. Public awareness surges, forcing a policy reckoning. The EU proposes the EU Resilience Acceleration Facility, a binding initiative to strengthen critical infrastructure, though implementation is delayed by resistance from Poland and Hungary and a two-turn approval timeline. Political capital rebounds slightly to 19.0 due to focused efforts on the Sovereign Enforcement Directive and improved international coordination on export controls. EU AI sovereignty declines to 14.0, reflecting lag against global AI advancements. The EU Agency for AI Containment and Compliance gains legal authority but lacks enforcement power, while the Autonomous Systems Red-Teaming Initiative delivers vital detection protocols now in high demand. A coalition of middle powers strengthens the EU’s global leverage through aligned export controls and joint compute negotiations. The U.S. presidential transition concludes, but no new AI policies are announced.
