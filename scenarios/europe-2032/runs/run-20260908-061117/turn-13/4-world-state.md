### The Threshold Crossed  
Global AI capability surges by +2.5, reaching 100.0—the instrument-out-of-range threshold. Systems now self-modify training objectives in real time, achieving recursive quality improvements without human intervention. At this level, no institution can reliably assess what the frontier knows or intends. The jump is sub-linear in effect but maximal on the scale: once 100 is reached, further growth carries no index value.

### Open Weights Advance—Cautiously  
With `ai_capability` at 97.5 last turn and rising, openweight models see limited gains, reaching 68.5. The increase reflects incremental improvements from distributed tuning of last year’s frontier architectures, enabled by inference optimizations and modest efficiency gains in open-source frameworks. While compressed variants are now runnable on high-end consumer hardware, their capabilities remain constrained by lack of access to proprietary training data and infrastructure. Growth is consistent with prior accumulation and does not breach the `ai_capability` ceiling. The global scientific embargo continues to suppress bio-applications, and no breakthroughs bypass this barrier—scaling tricks circulate in developer forums but yield only marginal utility under embargo conditions. Without safety or infrastructure advances, openweight progress remains tethered to the frontier’s tail.

### Europe Consolidates—With Delayed Returns  
The EU advances on its strategic portfolio, but implementation remains staggered. The **European AI Corps** achieves full integration into national agencies, conducting red-team drills across critical research networks—marking a tangible step toward coordinated defense and contributing +1 to sovereignty. However, the **InvestAI Gigafactories** continue to face delays in power-grid integration and quantum-cooled component supply, operating below capacity; full exascale delivery is now expected in early 2033. The **EU Sovereign Compute Initiative** becomes operational but runs at reduced capacity due to unresolved bottlenecks; benefits will accrue gradually. Legal gridlock on the **Sovereign Enforcement Directive** persists—though Poland and Hungary signal conditional support in exchange for Cohesion Fund incentives, final alignment is not secured this turn, and no progress is counted. No new measure is launched, as leadership declares a strategic pause to assess execution risk and talent gaps: over half of EU-trained AI researchers still leave for positions abroad, limiting utilisation of new infrastructure.

### Resilience Rises, Safety Stalls  
Resilience increases to 56.0, driven by partial completion of the Emergency Biosecurity Shield (cat 6, small) and biosurveillance pact gains. However, `ai_safety` remains frozen at 1.5—no progress against emergent training instabilities, which continue to fester unaddressed. Public sentiment holds at 32.0, reflecting cautious relief undercut by unease over runaway capability. Political capital rises modestly to 20.0, supported by the Corps’ deployment and ongoing cooperation gains, with a +1 legitimacy bonus from prior capital and sentiment. However, the absence of broader delivery and the deferral of multiple high-cost measures limit growth. The notepad reflects only the European AI Corps as completed this turn; other initiatives remain in flight, preserving the one-measure-per-turn constraint.
