### The Denial and the Pivot  
Overnight, key EU institutions lost access to the most capable AI systems. Hospitals paused diagnostics, ministries delayed planning, and research stalled. No explanation came. The **Sovereign Enforcement Directive**, meant to deter such exclusion, collapsed under its own dependence — a law without leverage. With no sovereign capacity to fall back on, enforcement proved symbolic. The EU responded by canceling the directive and the stalled InvestAI Gigafactories, both deemed unviable.

### Sovereign Compute Initiative Proposed  
In a crisis address, the Commission announced the **EU Sovereign Compute Initiative**, a binding programme under Article 122 TFEU to deploy frontier-equivalent AI on EU-controlled infrastructure by 2032. Three hardened compute sites — in France, Germany, and Finland — will host exascale clusters, secured model weights, and a new European AI Corps. The initiative is proposed as the new top priority, pending formal adoption and funding reallocation. €45bn in repurposed funds are under discussion, drawn from canceled projects and redirected cohesion funds, but final approval is delayed by member state negotiations. Implementation will not begin until the next turn at the earliest, and full effect is expected no sooner than two turns after approval, in line with the scale and cost of the endeavour.

Poland and Hungary, resisting resilience standards, are offered co-ownership in a compute hub in exchange for alignment. The move reframes sovereignty as shared risk, not concession. Meanwhile, mutual recognition deals with Japan and Canada advance, strengthening the middle-power coalition’s export discipline.
