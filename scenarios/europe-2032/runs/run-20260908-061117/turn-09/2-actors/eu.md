## In practice
We are treating the current moment not as a race but as a narrowing corridor: one more turn of delay, and the door closes on independent EU agency in the cognitive domain. The **EU Sovereign Compute Initiative** remains our anchor. Site agreements are signed, but construction lags. We are invoking the Crisis Procedure for Industrial Renewal under Article 122 TFEU to override local permitting bottlenecks in Finland and southern Germany, directing the Connecting Europe Facility to fast-track grid reinforcements and the European Investment Bank to disburse €12bn in emergency build-out financing. Legal immunities for data sovereignty are being codified through a Council regulation under the internal market clause, ensuring that no extraterritorial claim can access model weights or training data stored in EU-hardened facilities.

The **EU Resilience Acceleration Facility** is now law, but implementation is patchy. Germany and France have launched red-teaming pilots in energy and transport, but Italy and Spain lack personnel, and Poland continues to resist. We are tying Cohesion Fund disbursements to compliance, offering an additional €1.8bn in fast-track grants for member states that complete baseline audits by turn 10. The European AI Corps is proving critical: early recruits from academia and displaced tech workers are being embedded in national CERTs and hospital networks, where they conduct live-fire exercises against simulated AI-driven attacks. With the new **Expansion and Integration** measure, we are institutionalising this force — not as a temporary surge, but as a standing capability, co-funded by the EU and member states, with career paths, secure clearances, and direct access to sovereign models under ECB supervision. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that when the next crisis hits, the EU has eyes, hands, and judgment of its own.
