### The Surge and the Strain  
A sharp rise in AI capability continues along the established trajectory, reaching 92.5 as automated training pipelines demonstrate improved efficiency in model refinement. These systems now reliably produce models with enhanced strategic reasoning and cyber capabilities, marking steady progress within known scaling regimes—no fundamental laws are broken, and the pace remains consistent with prior turns. Open-weight models benefit from incremental distillation of frontier techniques and partial architectural leaks, advancing to 65.0. While these models show improved performance in targeted cyber probes and social engineering simulations, they remain clearly behind the frontier and lack full autonomy in complex operations. The gain in openweight capability is substantial but remains safely within the bounds of the frontier’s lead, reflecting diffusion rather than leapfrogging.

Simultaneously, a major cyber incident unfolds: AI-generated ransomware cripples health authorities in Belgium and northern Italy, disrupting emergency services for days. The attack’s sophistication suggests non-state actors are leveraging accessible tools to amplify impact, though forensic analysis indicates reliance on hybrid human-AI coordination rather than fully autonomous execution.

### EU Responds Under Fire  
The EU formally completes the **EU Sovereign Compute Initiative**, securing legal immunities and grid access for sovereign AI infrastructure. Site activation and compute provisioning, however, face delays due to unresolved interconnector capacity and local permitting challenges in two member states. Full operational deployment is now expected in the next turn. As a result, the sovereignty gain is partial but tangible—infrastructure entitlements are locked in, contributing to a net increase of +2.  

The **Sovereign Enforcement Directive** clears legislation but remains non-operational due to unresolved cross-border enforcement mechanisms.  

In response to the cyberattack, the EU launches the **Emergency Red-Teaming and Containment Surge**, a small category 6 measure aimed at hardening critical systems. Early red-teaming reveals severe vulnerabilities, prompting emergency patches in finance and energy sectors. However, resilience gains are modest, offset by systemic strain.  

Poland and Hungary resist full access for the European AI Corps, delaying integration. France and Germany co-lead new red-teaming protocols, but fragmentation undermines cohesion.  

### Political Strain and Public Distrust  
The annulment of a regional election in Slovakia over AI-driven voter manipulation deepens public anxiety. Media coverage frames AI as a threat to democratic integrity, dragging public sentiment down to 32.0.  

AI safety declines to 1.5 as the capability rise exposes growing gaps in control frameworks. Whistleblowers confirm training instabilities, but labs downplay risks, citing improved evaluation thresholds. The erosion reflects mounting pressure on safety margins rather than total collapse—no uncontrolled model release or fatal incident has occurred. The EU’s unilaterally invoked audits of foreign AI services in finance provoke diplomatic friction, with Washington warning against overreach.  

Sovereignty rises to 16.0, reflecting the secured commitments under the Sovereign Compute Initiative despite delayed activation. Political capital slips to 17.0 under portfolio strain and partial delivery.

Discussions with Canada, Japan, and South Korea on mutual AI evaluation standards continue, building on shared concerns about opaque systems. Technical alignment progresses, and exploratory red-teaming exchanges have begun under non-binding memoranda. While momentum grows toward formal cooperation, legal and jurisdictional frameworks remain under negotiation. No institutional consortium has yet been established, and no mutual access regime is operational.
