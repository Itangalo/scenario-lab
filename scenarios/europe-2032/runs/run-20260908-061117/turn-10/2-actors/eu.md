## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: one physical, one cognitive. The **EU Sovereign Compute Initiative** concludes this turn, and while not all sites are online, the legal and grid foundations are now binding. We have invoked Article 122 TFEU to override permitting delays in Finland and southern Germany, and the European Investment Bank has disbursed €12bn in emergency financing. Legal immunities for model weights are now codified under internal market law, making extraterritorial access legally impossible and technically infeasible. This is not full sovereignty yet, but it is the first time the EU owns both the metal and the mandate.

The **European AI Corps Expansion and Integration** remains our operational backbone. With the **Emergency Red-Teaming and Containment Surge**, we are redirecting its early units to conduct live-fire exercises against AI-driven ransomware, supply chain poisoning, and disinformation cascades. These are not theoretical: the recent **cyber_major_incident**—a ransomware sweep across regional health authorities in Belgium and northern Italy—was executed with AI-generated payloads that bypassed legacy detection. Attribution remains unclear, but the tooling was not state-grade in volume; it was state-grade in design. We are now requiring all member states to grant the Corps access to their most sensitive systems for red-teaming, tied to Cohesion Fund disbursements. France and Germany are fully onboard; Poland and Hungary are resisting, citing sovereignty concerns. We are offering them co-leadership in the next phase of the Corps’ development in exchange for compliance.

Public sentiment remains fragile at 37.0, and the **election_annulled** event in Slovakia—where a court cited AI-manipulated voter targeting as grounds for rerunning a regional vote—has deepened anxiety. We cannot afford to be seen as slow or captured. That is why we are not waiting for the **Sovereign Enforcement Directive** to become operational. We are using the ECB’s red-teaming authority to conduct unannounced audits of foreign-hosted AI services used by EU financial institutions, under the guise of financial stability oversight. If they refuse access, we will invoke the Directive’s market access provisions unilaterally, even if enforcement mechanisms are still being finalised. We will be late on safety, but we will not be late on control.
