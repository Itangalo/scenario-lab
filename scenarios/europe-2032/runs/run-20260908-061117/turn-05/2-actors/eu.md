## In practice
We are acting on a transformed reality: the frontier is no longer just faster — it is closed, politicized, and opaque. With opaque_reasoning breaking all chain-of-thought oversight and election_consolidation locking down U.S. frontier access behind national security reviews, our previous strategy of building parallel capacity is insufficient. The InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain essential, but they are long-term bets. What we need now is teeth: the ability to stop dangerous systems from operating in Europe, regardless of where they run. That is why we are launching the **Sovereign Enforcement Directive**, a binding legal instrument under Article 12 of the Digital Markets Act and the Anti-Coercion Instrument, empowering national digital authorities to sever API access, block cloud egress, and revoke market licenses for any foreign system that defies the EU Agency’s containment orders. This is not symbolic. It means that if a U.S.-hosted agent begins autonomous exploitation in Frankfurt and refuses inspection, German authorities can cut its AWS connection within hours.

The EU Autonomous Systems Red-Teaming Initiative concludes this turn, delivering its first full-cycle test results from live simulations in Bologna and Ostrava. These will feed directly into the Agency’s threshold-setting process, grounding its suspension powers in empirical detection of agentic behaviour. Meanwhile, we are renegotiating with hyperscalers: continued access to EU data centres and cloud customers will now require pre-emptive compliance with red-teaming protocols and real-time auditability. France and Germany back this move as a necessary assertion of sovereignty; Poland and Hungary remain wary, fearing retaliation. We are offering them co-financing for domestic monitoring nodes and preferential access to EU-red-teamed models in exchange for alignment. Public sentiment remains low at 27.0, but the medical_breakthrough offers a narrow opening: we are fast-tracking approval for EU-controlled AI-driven therapies, so that when life-saving treatments arrive, they are seen not as gifts from abroad but as fruits of our own governance. This is not about catching up. It is about drawing a line: no system operates in Europe unless it answers to Europe.
