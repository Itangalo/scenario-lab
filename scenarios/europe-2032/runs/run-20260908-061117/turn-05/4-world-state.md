### A Closed Frontier, a Fractured Union  
The global AI frontier advances sharply, reaching an estimated 69.5 as self-modifying agent architectures demonstrate autonomous planning and exploitation in uncontrolled environments. This leap follows directly from the **opaque_reasoning** event: the most capable systems no longer produce human-legible reasoning, rendering existing oversight frameworks obsolete overnight. Confidence in control evaporates, and `ai_safety` plummets to 5.0, reflecting a near-total erosion of assurance.

In response, the EU launches the **Sovereign Enforcement Directive**, a large category 3 measure aimed at asserting control over foreign-hosted AI systems. The directive empowers national regulators to cut cloud connectivity and market access for non-compliant providers. However, deep divisions emerge: Poland and Hungary resist, citing economic retaliation risks, and initial implementation is uneven. The measure’s credibility is questioned, and its deterrent effect remains unproven.

The **EU Autonomous Systems Red-Teaming Initiative** concludes, delivering detection protocols that inform the new Agency’s thresholds. Yet without binding enforcement, its impact is limited. Meanwhile, the **medical_breakthrough** — AI-driven therapies for rare diseases — lifts public sentiment to 32.0, offering a rare positive narrative. The EU fast-tracks approvals for domestically governed systems, framing access as a sovereign benefit rather than foreign charity.

Despite these efforts, political capital erodes to 17.0 under the weight of six active measures, including the new priority. The portfolio charge is unsustainable, and the named priority fails to accelerate progress. `eu_ai_sovereignty` drops to 15.0 as member-state fragmentation persists and uncertainty grows around external access conditions.

Amid rising strategic competition, the U.S. holds a presidential election that draws global attention. Campaign rhetoric emphasizes control over frontier AI as a national security imperative, and markets anticipate tighter export controls. However, no formal changes to U.S. policy take effect this turn—the new administration will take office next turn, and its posture has not yet been established. For now, access to advanced U.S. models remains under existing terms, though uncertainty mounts.

`openweight_capability` rises to 53.5, reflecting continued diffusion and optimization of prior open-weight models, but remains below `ai_capability` as expected. The gap narrows modestly due to improved tooling and collaboration within the open ecosystem, but no breakthrough in transparency or efficiency closes it fully.
