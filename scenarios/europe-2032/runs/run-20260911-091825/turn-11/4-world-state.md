### Queues for jobs, queues for swabs
Autumn brought the hiring freeze into the open. Law firms, accountants, software houses and call-centre operators confirmed what graduates already knew: entry posts were not being refilled. Ministers argued over numbers; families did not. Evening news cut from empty graduate job fairs to nurses logging extra screening swabs under the new bio-shield.

Brussels tried to hold two lines at once. The sequencing shield stayed the operational centre — pooled screening, hospital lab triage, hardening of hospital and water-control networks — now with a visible police win as officers in two countries seized rented clusters of graphics processors bought with stolen identities and used to fine-tune the leaked frontier model. Investigators spoke of a rental black market; health officials said the seizure showed the shield was working.

On jobs, employment ministers repurposed social-fund money into wage top-ups, retraining vouchers and six-month re-entry contracts for young hires, asking automating employers to pay a levy to use pooled public helpers. The offer was modest, the queues long, and national agencies complained the guidance arrived late and thin.

### A side deal and silent sites
Cohesion frayed further when another capital confirmed its own supply arrangement outside the pooled offer, defending it as pragmatism for its hospitals and factories. In Brussels it read as undercutting; the Commission did not litigate, letting pooled-buyer benefits flow and offering re-entry through the new graduate fund.

Nothing moved on concrete. The two stalled factory sites remained taped off, courts upheld suspensions, and water-and-power compacts stayed in draft. American praise for European preparedness came again without a restoration date.

By December, showcase hospitals reported drills completed and kits stockpiled, but staff called it burden on burden. Fear of unrecallable recipes and anger over missing jobs fused into a broader resentment. Talk of packaged lab instructions circulating underground persisted in single-source reports, never confirmed, never dispelled.
