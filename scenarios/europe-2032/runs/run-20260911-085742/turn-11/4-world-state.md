### Wards hold, staff break
The winter agent did not return, but its casualties filled the second half of 2031. Cross-border transfers kept moving patients off paper wards, and joint stocks of countermeasures stopped a second containment scare in September from spreading beyond two hospitals. The surge programme that ran those transfers closed the year formally complete, with ministers claiming wards had been kept open.

On the ground the claim wore thin. Double-check teams thinned as nurses resigned mid-shift, and isolation discipline slipped where overtime no longer covered exhaustion. Municipal patching continued quietly, grids avoided cascade, but local news fused staff departures with still-unreadable frontier systems and leaked-model fraud.

### A bridge, half built
Brussels pushed money toward retention premia and cross-border hires, paired with a fast income bridge for displaced clerical and junior professional workers. Hiring channels opened in autumn; premia arrived late and unevenly across member states. Retraining entry points appeared in national employment offices, with first payouts visible before December.

It was not control, but it was visible effort. Containment held through the year on exhausted staff, and displaced workers saw an entry point within months rather than nothing. Hostility did not lift — protests over data centres and clinic closures continued — yet polling stopped falling. Whispers of underground clinics selling unlicensed countermeasures made from leaked weights circulated in border regions, single-source and unconfirmed, adding to unease without becoming a confirmed outbreak.

No new AI law came, disbursements stayed frozen, and the supply position remained paper only.
