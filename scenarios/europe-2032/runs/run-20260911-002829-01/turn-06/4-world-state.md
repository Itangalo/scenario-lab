### The jump and the machines
Winter ended with two announcements weeks apart. First an American lab demonstrated a system that planned across days, wrote its own tooling, and scored far above anything public. Benchmark sheets leaked, then a product. Timelines rewritten overnight.

Then came embodied deployment at scale: Chinese-built humanoids in warehouses and ports, American control stacks on top. Videos of machines carrying, sorting, digging spread through works councils. In German auto suppliers and northern Italian logistics hubs, managers asked for quotes. Unions asked what was left to retrain for.

Brussels felt both as exposure. Frontier access was still rationed, domestic compute still two narrowed sites waiting on grid connections and frozen Taiwanese allocations.

### Access talks, home build
The new American administration took office promising structured allied access. EEAS teams flew to Washington seeking published tiers, researcher access, joint evaluation protocols. Early meetings were cordial, technical annexes exchanged. Nothing was signed that switched on access. Washington wanted export-control alignment first; Berlin and Paris wanted guarantees access would not become a reason to slow the gigafactories.

At home the two advanced sites poured concrete and secured power priority, but accelerator deliveries slipped again. The tech sovereignty package was declared complete — permitting zones, capital pledges — celebrated in press releases, thin on operating machines.

### Shield pays, towns doubt
The Bio-Shield continuity regime formally closed: sequencing network, stockpiles, rationing rules now standing procedure. Large hospitals stayed stable through a late flu surge, credited to monitoring and triage assistants.

In small municipalities the story curdled. Manual-fallback drills for water and power were run by visiting teams, wage bridges paid where mayors co-signed. Where staff never arrived, boil notices and insurer letters returned. The Trust and Transition Pact, now the stated priority, bought tolerance in cities showing shorter waiting lists, and resentment where coverage lapsed. Reinsurers pocketed the EIB backstop and still repriced the smallest utilities.

By June, relief at American openness competed with fear of dependence, and with anger that robots built elsewhere would decide industrial towns. Brussels had terms to negotiate, concrete to pour, and little trust to spend.
