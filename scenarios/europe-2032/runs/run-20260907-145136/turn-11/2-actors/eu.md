## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Sovereign AI Core Readiness Campaign  
We are prioritizing this because the cyber_major_incident exposed that having rules is not the same as having readiness — and without proven resilience, sovereignty is a legal fiction. This campaign turns paper continuity into lived credibility, and that is the only foundation on which trust, and future action, can be built.

## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from emergency response to durable repair. The Emergency Continuity Response Initiative succeeded in stabilizing 17 critical sites — but stabilization is not assurance. We now know that nodes failed not because of malice, but because they were untested, untrained, and disconnected from real command. The Sovereign AI Core Programme exists in law and in infrastructure — but not yet in practice.

Under Article 122 TFEU and the newly activated Crisis Assurance Protocol, we are launching the Sovereign AI Core Readiness Campaign to close that gap. The European AI Assurance Directorate is deploying red teams to conduct unannounced attack simulations on the first 20 designated core nodes — including in capitals that resisted prior interventions. These are not compliance checks; they are war games. Each node must demonstrate autonomous failover, sustained operation for 72 hours, and secure reintegration — or be marked as non-operational.

We are offering full cost coverage and joint ownership to all participants — but no exemptions. The results will be published in full, including failures. This is not transparency for its own sake — it is the only way to rebuild trust. If a system cannot survive a drill, it cannot survive an attack. And if it cannot survive an attack, it does not belong in the sovereign core. We are not punishing failure — we are refusing to pretend it didn’t happen. The lights must not only stay on — they must stay on when we are not watching.
