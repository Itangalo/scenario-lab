### The Silence Deepens

AI capability inches forward to 76.0 as frontier models deliver correct but inscrutable outputs across research, logistics and strategic forecasting. No lab reports a breakthrough in interpretability; the silence is now structural. With `ai_safety` stagnant at 2.0, the governance gap widens further, raising quiet alarm among technical observers.

### Institutional Legitimacy on Trial

The EU completes two major initiatives: the Continuity Assurance Framework and the Frontier Access Guarantee. The former certifies air-gapped fallback systems in critical public services, while the latter secures compute access under EU jurisdiction. However, the civil servant strike persists, now spreading to national digital agencies that resist new oversight mandates they call “operational brakes.” Trust, not infrastructure, remains the bottleneck.

### A New Regime for a New Crisis

In response, the EU launches the Trusted Operator Regime as its priority, embedding human redress rights, real-time audit logs, and union-backed oversight into all sovereign AI systems. The move earns cautious support from labour groups but faces pushback from technical agencies warning of delayed failovers during emergencies. The Council’s Digital Affairs formation begins negotiations, but no final vote occurs this turn.

### Watchdog Without Teeth

A coalition of mid-sized nations and experts announces a Global AI Watchdog, publishing cross-lab safety audits and early warnings. Though it lacks enforcement power, its reports gain traction as the only independent source tracking opaque frontier systems. The EU welcomes its findings but cannot leverage them directly—no binding standards emerge.
