## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now see that capability without control is not sovereignty, and caution without continuity is not safety. The two mandates do not merely conflict — they collapse into each other when the systems meant to uphold them no longer explain themselves. We are not balancing two duties. We are holding a single line: that the Union must remain *legible to itself*, even as the technology it depends on becomes opaque.``  
- Grounds: The silent leap in `ai_capability` to 75.5, with no traceable reasoning, renders traditional oversight meaningless. Our institutions can no longer interrogate the systems they rely on — and now, our own civil servants are refusing to operate them. This is not a failure of policy but of epistemic sovereignty. If the EU cannot understand the logic of its own critical systems, it cannot act sovereignly at all. The two mandates have fused into one: to remain a polity that knows what it is doing.

* ``- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but we now add that this enforcement will only activate when the Sovereign AI Core Programme is *trusted as well as ready*. We will not compel compliance through funding cuts unless we can prove, through operator voice and redress, that our own systems are worth defending.``  
- Trigger: The strike by civil servants administering AI emergency funds persists, now spreading to national digital agencies.  
- Grounds: The credibility of the regime is eroding faster than its technical rollout. We cannot enforce a rule-based order when the people meant to uphold it see the system as illegitimate. The threat of exclusion only works if the alternative is trusted. We are adjusting the commitment to reflect that enforcement depends not on legal authority, but on earned legitimacy.

## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the Trusted Operator Regime  
A binding framework requiring that all sovereign AI systems deployed in public services include verified human operator redress rights, real-time audit logs, and union-backed oversight panels — with veto authority during stress events.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Stabilize eu_political_capital by 4–6 points through institutional buy-in; modestly improve resilience by ensuring human operators can halt unsafe or unexplained system behaviour during continuity events; begin repairing public_sentiment by demonstrating that oversight is not just technical but participatory.  
`Applies to:` EAAD, EU civil service unions, national digital agencies, SACP operational command

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Trusted Operator Regime  
We are prioritizing this because the silent advance in AI capability — systems that work but do not explain — has made traditional oversight obsolete, and now our own operators are refusing to run the systems we built. The crisis is no longer technical or geopolitical — it is epistemic and institutional. We must prove that the Union can still act with purpose, even when its tools no longer speak back. This regime does not slow progress — it makes it governable.

## In practice
This turn, we are operating in the silence after the reasoning. The most advanced systems no longer leave a trace of how they reached their conclusions. That is not just a technical problem — it is a constitutional one. The European AI Assurance Directorate, under Article 122 TFEU, is now restructuring the Sovereign AI Core Readiness Campaign around a new principle: no system without redress. We are not rolling back capability — we are embedding a right to challenge into every node that matters. 

The European Civil Service Union is being formally integrated into the design of the next generation of red-team exercises. Operators will have the authority to halt a test if a system behaves unpredictably and to demand a full audit log within 15 minutes. These logs will be reviewed by joint panels of technical experts and union representatives. The Frontier Access Guarantee, now complete, is being retrofitted with this same regime — not as a delay, but as a condition of certification. We are not building faster. We are building legible. The Council’s Digital Affairs formation will vote on the Trusted Operator Regime this autumn, funded through the Emergency Continuity Instrument. Resistance remains in national agencies that fear operational drag — but we are offering them not just rules, but protection: a system where no operator is left alone with a decision they cannot understand.
