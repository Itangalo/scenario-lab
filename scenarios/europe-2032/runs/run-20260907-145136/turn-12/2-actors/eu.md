## In practice
This turn, we are confronting a crisis not of systems, but of faith. The Sovereign AI Core Readiness Campaign exposed real fragilities — Athens and Bratislava failed not because of malice, but because readiness was paper-thin. But now, the strike by civil servants responsible for disbursing AI aid reveals a deeper truth: even where systems are being built, the people meant to operate them do not trust them. And if they do not trust them, they will not defend them.

Under Article 122 TFEU and the Crisis Assurance Protocol, we are now expanding the Readiness Campaign not by adding more drills, but by changing who runs them. The European AI Assurance Directorate will co-design the next phase of red-team exercises with representatives from the European Civil Service Union and national digital agencies. Operators will have the right to pause a test, demand an explanation, and appeal a result. We are not softening standards — we are making them survivable. The Frontier Access Guarantee will proceed to completion this turn, but we are redirecting its final phase to include mandatory operator feedback loops in all certified nodes. This is not a delay — it is a correction. We are not building a fortress against failure. We are building a system that learns from it.
