## In practice  
The cyber intrusion revealed by accident in three member states and beyond has shaken confidence in our ability to defend essential systems. This turn, I am directing the Commission to draft a binding Cyber Shield Directive under Article 114 TFEU, to be adopted by qualified majority in the Council by spring 2027. The directive will mandate AI-enhanced intrusion detection systems across electricity transmission, water utilities, and major ports, with ENISA overseeing compliance and joint exercises. Resistance from smaller member states over cost-sharing is expected, so I am pairing the measure with a €12bn resilience facility under the Multiannual Financial Framework, drawing from unused cohesion funds, to cover 70% of implementation costs for states below the EU median income.  

Simultaneously, the InvestAI Gigafactories programme is advancing site selection in France, Germany, and Poland, with the first environmental impact assessments concluding this quarter. I am instructing DG Connect to fast-track grid connection permits through a new legal instrument based on Article 122 TFEU (energy security), pre-empting delays that could derail the 2031 operational target. The Tech sovereignty package is being negotiated in the Council’s Competitiveness formation, where pressure from southern member states to weaken permitting acceleration is being met with binding commitments on regional job guarantees and retraining co-financing from the private investors.  

Public sentiment remains ambivalent, and the cyber incident has amplified fears of uncontrolled AI. I am therefore holding off on any broad adoption push under category 9, despite pressure from digital economy advocates. Instead, I am directing the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) to conduct a public trust survey in Q1 2027, to inform future diffusion strategy — but only after resilience and sovereignty are on firmer ground. The two-year commitment demands that we consolidate before we expand.
