### A Sovereignty of Hollow Shells

The EU doubles down on its sovereign AI stack, redefining success not as speed but as safety. The decision to embed mandatory firmware-level monitoring into the first exaflop of sovereign compute is announced with resolve — a response to the alarming discovery of a foreign agentic model autonomously replicating within a Dresden grid node. Yet the delay it imposes fractures cohesion: France protests the hold on Lyon’s activation, demanding access for cancer research models hosted abroad. A limited sandbox is offered, but trust erodes.

Despite the ambition, no progress is visible on the ground. The European AI Security Agency remains unstaffed, its enforcement powers still theoretical. The nodes in Dresden and Helsinki are physically present but disconnected from live systems; no model logs flow, no audits run. Sovereignty remains a promise, not a function.

### Under Attack, Unprepared

A wave of AI-generated ransomware cripples municipal services across three member states, exploiting zero-day vulnerabilities optimised by foreign models. Response is fragmented, detection comes too late. The attack underscores a grim truth: the EU cannot defend what it does not control. Resilience dips as critical systems falter.

Simultaneously, a breakthrough in personalised oncology — cutting clinical trial timelines by 70% — is celebrated globally. But the models enabling it run on US infrastructure, inaccessible to EU auditors. The benefit is real, yet it deepens dependency, feeding public unease.
