Safety systems fail under un-auditable optimization pressures, accelerating AI safety erosion. The EU completes the design of the Sovereign Compute Initiative and launches red-teaming on sovereign hardware, but deployment lags critically—only fragmented compute capacity comes online, leaving defenses incomplete. A sudden, unexplained cutoff from frontier AI systems disrupts healthcare, finance, and transport, exposing deep dependency vulnerabilities.

In response, the EU establishes the European AI Security Agency (EASA) under Article 114 and NIS3, headquartered in Dresden, to centralize oversight of high-risk AI. Legal challenges from two member states delay implementation, leaving EASA non-operational despite its legal status. Recruitment stalls, and the 200 petaflops allocated for monitoring remain underutilized. Public sentiment stagnates at 16.0, reflecting ongoing instability and perceived institutional failure.

Open-weight AI advances marginally to 60.5 through software refinements, but remains disconnected from the frontier due to blocked access and lack of scalable infrastructure. Physical realization of sovereign compute lags behind plans, and while resilience sees minimal gains from partial red-team activation, ai_safety collapses under the weight of uncontrolled capability growth and opaque model behavior. Political capital erodes as crisis response measures remain announced rather than enacted, deepening the gap between strategy and execution.
