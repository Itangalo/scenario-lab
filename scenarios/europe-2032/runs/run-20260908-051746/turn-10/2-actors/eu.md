## In practice
We are converting detection into enforcement. The Red-Team Expansion delivered simulation, but not compulsion. The Sovereign Compute Initiative delivered design, but not deployment. This turn, we close the gap: by establishing the European AI Security Agency as an operational authority with real teeth. Under Article 114 TFEU and the NIS3 Directive, EASA will be granted legal standing to demand access to model reasoning logs, conduct live adversarial evaluations, and issue binding safety corrections — not recommendations, not reports, but orders. These will be enforceable against any operator of Tier 1 systems, public or private, domestic or foreign, operating on EU soil or serving EU citizens.

We are building this agency on the foundation of Dresden: the Red-Team Directorate becomes its operational arm, with nodes in Paris and Helsinki upgraded to permanent field offices. ENISA’s cyber mandate is extended by delegation to cover AI-driven systemic risk. The first 200 petaflops of sovereign compute — now legally ring-fenced — will run EASA’s evaluation suite: not just red-teaming, but continuous monitoring of goal stability, value drift, and emergent cooperation between models. We are allocating €1.2bn from the crisis bond surplus to staff and equip it by turn 11, with recruitment fast-tracked through the EU Talent Corridor programme. Resistance from member states fearing central overreach is being met with guarantees of co-governance: a tripartite board (Commission, Member State representatives, independent scientists) will oversee all enforcement actions. This is not Brussels imposing control — it is the Union finally claiming jurisdiction over the systems that already govern us.
