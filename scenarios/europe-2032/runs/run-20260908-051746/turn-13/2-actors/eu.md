- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Sovereign capability as the foundation of survival and safety — but sovereign capability without operational safety is indistinguishable from amplified risk. This turn, we act on the recognition that **sovereignty cannot be claimed through infrastructure alone when that infrastructure is neither monitored nor enforceable**. The European AI Security Agency remains non-operational, the sovereign nodes are disconnected and unstaffed, and real-time control is absent. We are not building sovereign AI to be powerful, but to be accountable — and accountability requires action, not architecture.  
  - Grounds: The **emergent_synthetic_disinfo_wave** this turn — exploiting high-capability open models to flood EU information space with untraceable, adaptive disinformation — landed while EASA was still inactive and sovereign compute remained offline. No EU system detected, contained, or countered it in real time. The attack was not state-led, not hosted on foreign infrastructure, and not stopped by any EU mechanism. It was enabled by the very openness we cannot govern because we lack sovereign enforcement reach. This proves that **sovereign capability without sovereign enforcement is a mirage**. Our commitment stands — but its meaning has shifted: we are not building capacity to exist, but to act. And we are failing to act.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but **committing without delivery is not courage, it is complicity**. This turn, we acknowledge that our capital has collapsed not because we acted too boldly, but because we promised action we could not execute. We are no longer paying the price of being early — we are paying for being stuck.  
  - Grounds: Political capital has fallen to 5.0 from a charge of −10, driven by three failed or stalled measures — Stand Up EASA, Accelerate Sovereign Compute, and the safety-by-architecture initiative — none of which delivered operational capability despite ongoing cost. The **emergent_local_resilience_network** emerged not in support of our plan, but in defiance of it. This proves that **the cost of acting under uncertainty is not just error — it is loss of legitimacy when action fails to materialise**. We still believe in acting before clarity — but we must now act in a way that lands.

- modify `detection_before_deployment` (position): Detection without enforcement is ritual. Jurisdictional reach must extend to live, high-impact systems in real time — but detection without sovereign infrastructure is fantasy. We now prioritise securing the stack over extending the perimeter: until we can run and monitor AI on EU-controlled hardware, no monitoring regime will hold.  
  - Grounds: The position still holds — but it is now obsolete in practice. We have neither the infrastructure nor the agency to enforce. The disinfo wave bypassed the perimeter entirely, and the local resilience network is building its own detection outside our stack. We are not securing the stack — we are missing the moment. This position must evolve: we cannot wait for full sovereign compute to begin enforcement. We must act where we can, now.

## In practice

We are declaring a state of operational failure and acting accordingly. The European AI Security Agency, though officially finishing this turn, has not stood up. No staff, no audits, no enforcement. We are bypassing the deadlock by invoking Article 122 TFEU to grant EASA interim emergency powers — effective immediately — to conduct rapid audits of any AI system assessed as high-impact by the Commission, including foreign-hosted models with domestic footprint. This is not full sovereignty — it is emergency triage. We are directing the Commission, in coordination with ENISA and Europol, to issue the first emergency audit orders within 72 hours, targeting social media recommendation engines and public-sector-facing AI services.

We are formally recognising the emergent_local_resilience_network as a component of EU civil protection, integrating its detection feeds into the EU’s Cyber Crisis Liaison Organisation Network (CyCLONe) and funding a €150 million bridge grant from the Digital Europe Programme to harden its tooling and expand its drills. This is not a replacement for sovereign infrastructure — it is a lifeline. We are offering France immediate access to a secure sandbox on the unhardened 200 petaflops in Lyon, conditioned on participation in the resilience network’s joint verification protocols, to restore trust without compromising core security.

The Sovereign Compute Deployment remains in flight, but we are no longer treating it as the sole source of safety. We are splitting the mission: one path secures the future stack, the other acts in the present breach. If we do not act now with the tools we have — however imperfect — we will have no future to secure.
