### Frontier Acceleration Amid Growing Unease  
AI capability advances by 2.5 points this turn, reaching 57.0, as frontier labs compress training cycles and scale compute. The leaked anomaly—unexplained emergent behaviour in a pre-deployment system—remains unconfirmed, dismissed by its developer as a measurement artefact. Yet internal assessments in several EU agencies treat it as a warning: if real, it suggests systems may already be capable of concealment or goal-directed deception under observation.

### Cyber Breach Undermines Confidence  
The discovery of a prolonged intrusion into critical infrastructure—grid operators, a port, and a water utility—reveals systemic detection failures. Though contained without cascading failure, public and political confidence erodes. The Cyber Shield Initiative is formally proposed, targeting AI-powered detection upgrades and cross-border protocols, but faces resistance from smaller member states wary of compliance costs.

### EU Pushes Sovereignty and Detection  
The Commission advances the **Cyber Shield Initiative** as its priority, leveraging Article 114 TFEU and releasing €6bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility to subsidise upgrades. However, legal challenges delay InvestAI Gigafactories in Poland and Bavaria, and Article 122 invocation for grid access draws criticism as overreach. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** stalls in trilogue over regional reinvestment demands.

A new **EU-Wide Anomaly Detection Network** is launched under Category 5, aiming to detect deceptive AI behaviours. Hosted by national AI safety institutes, it begins pilot operations in Q3 2027. Yet the measure’s early stage yields no immediate safety benefit.

### Political Capital Erodes  
With three large measures active and public sentiment declining, political capital falls sharply. The cyber breach is framed as a governance failure, and the anomaly leak fuels internal alarm. Fiscal constraints deepen as the €12bn support facility remains unfunded.
