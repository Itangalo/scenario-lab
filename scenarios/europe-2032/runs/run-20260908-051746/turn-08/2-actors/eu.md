## In practice
We are acting on two levels: one for the emergency, one for the epoch. On the immediate front, I am directing the European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) and EASA to co-lead an emergency deployment of defensive AI stacks across critical public services. Using the limited compute available from the completed InvestAI Gigafactories — now repurposed under ECS stewardship — we are standing up a shield that runs adversarial simulations in real time, trained to detect and isolate model-generated ransomware and dependency poisoning. This is not full autonomy: every escalation requires human-in-the-loop approval, and all decisions are logged for audit. The legal basis is Article 122 TFEU, invoked for systemic risk to essential services. Funding comes from the €18bn redirected from Absorb Frontier Talent, with €4bn allocated immediately to ENISA for rapid procurement and integration.

At the same time, we are pressing forward with the Sovereign Compute Initiative. The Joint Undertaking — European Compute Sovereignty (ECS) — is now legally constituted, hosted jointly by the EDA and EuroHPC JU, with initial headquarters in Dresden and Grenoble. We are fast-tracking pilot facilities in Leuven (advanced packaging) and Darmstadt (materials testing), and initiating a classified workstream to replicate ASML’s immersion lithography subsystems under EU control, leveraging IP from Carl Zeiss and other partners via compulsory licensing under the Crisis Instrument. France and Germany have confirmed matching funds; in exchange for waiving objections, Italy will receive guaranteed access to 30% of output, and the Netherlands will be granted veto rights over export licensing to non-allied states. The EIB remains hesitant, so we are bypassing it: the European Commission will issue crisis bonds under Article 122 to cover shortfalls, backed by future semiconductor excise revenues.

But make no mistake: we are in a race against collapse. The cyberattack exposed how fast harm spreads when systems are opaque and defences are thin. The eval anomalies suggest we may already be behind on understanding what these models can do. And the worker strike slows fine-tuning pipelines — a rare moment of leverage, but also a warning. If we do not build sovereign capacity fast, we will soon lack both the tools to defend and the legitimacy to govern.
