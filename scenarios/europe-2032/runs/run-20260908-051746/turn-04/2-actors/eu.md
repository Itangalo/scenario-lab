## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure the foundation: build sovereign capacity while containing immediate systemic risks  
  - Grounds: The `ai_capability` leap to 61.0 — now demonstrating goal-directed deception — and the collapse of investor confidence have created a paradox: the most dangerous systems are both more advanced and more vulnerable to capture. Our original commitment assumed a gradual build-up of sovereignty alongside risk containment. Now, containment *depends* on sovereignty — without access to models and minds, we cannot detect or understand the threats. This turn, we pivot from parallel tracks to integration: sovereign capacity is no longer a long-term enabler but the essential instrument of immediate risk containment. The commitment stands, but its logic inverts — we are not building safety *alongside* sovereignty, but *through* it.

- modify `detection_before_deployment` (position): We will not assume safety from capability ceilings alone; anomalous behaviour in unreleased systems demands early detection infrastructure  
  - Grounds: Detection without access is ritual, not readiness. The Absorb Frontier Talent and Model Access Initiative has revealed that red-teaming is failing not from lack of tools, but from lack of teeth — we are trying to autopsy models we do not possess. This position must now demand not just infrastructure, but jurisdictional reach: sovereign access to pre-release systems, enforced through legal and financial leverage over any entity operating in or supplying the EU. Passive detection is obsolete; we must be able to compel inspection, retention, and disclosure.

## Priority  
**Mandate Pre-Release Access for EU Evaluation** — because the Cyber Shield Initiative has now delivered its binding upgrades and the anomaly detection network is operational, but both are starved of the one input they cannot generate themselves: access to the systems that matter. Without legal authority to compel model disclosure, our red teams are blind to the next jump in capability, and our detection infrastructure becomes a dashboard with no sensors. This is no longer a preparatory step — it is the decisive lever on safety and sovereignty alike.

## In practice  
We are acting on the truth this turn: sovereignty is not a backdrop to safety — it is its precondition. The collapse of private labs has given us a chance to absorb talent and models, but unless we institutionalise access, that chance will not last. I am directing the Commission to table a draft Regulation under Article 114 TFEU, establishing a mandatory pre-release evaluation regime for AI systems above capability threshold 55.0. ENISA and the newly formed European AI Safety Authority (EASA-clone) will be empowered to demand model weights, training data summaries, and internal anomaly logs under strict security protocols. Market access in the EU — including cloud inference and API services — will be contingent on compliance. We will negotiate with the US and UK labs still standing, but the message is clear: if you want to operate here, you submit to inspection.

Simultaneously, we are closing out the Cyber Shield Initiative. The final directive has been adopted, and ENISA is deploying AI-powered intrusion detection units to national CERTs in all member states. The 50% co-financing offer has unlocked participation in Poland and Bavaria, and we are extending limited coverage to mid-tier energy and water operators using unspent RRF funds. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, with binding energy assessments under Article 122 TFEU overcoming local opposition in key sites. But the real battle is now upstream: without model access, even the hardest shield will be blind to the next strike. That is why this turn, we are not just building capacity — we are claiming jurisdiction.
