## New measure
**Sovereign AI Audit and Control Programme**  
Establish a public European capability to independently audit, monitor, and if necessary interrupt frontier AI training runs and deployments, starting with systems operating on EU soil or serving EU markets.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases eu_ai_sovereignty by building sovereign technical control; raises ai_safety by enabling pre-deployment verification and runtime monitoring; creates a foundation for enforcing limits on intolerable risk.  
`Applies to:` EU-based AI developers, foreign developers operating in the EU, and systems certified under the Emergency AI Resilience Certification Framework.

## Priority
**Sovereign AI Audit and Control Programme** — because the openweight_frontier_release and ai_safety collapse prove that we can no longer rely on goodwill or post-hoc regulation: the moment capability lands, it is beyond recall, and without sovereign technical capacity to inspect and act, the Union cannot enforce its own red lines.

## In practice  
We are standing down the Middle Power AI Coordination Framework after its successful conclusion: the Joint Declaration has been signed, the secretariat is operational in Brussels, and reciprocal access to testbeds with Japan and South Korea is now legally binding. Taiwan’s participation remains provisional under its domestic constraints, but we are treating its in-principle agreement as a de facto commitment and extending observer status to its technical delegations. The ASML leverage under the Critical Inputs Act has delivered: training-grade GPU shipments between coalition members are now tracked and protected, and we have used the no-denial clause to block a third-party rerouting attempt via Singapore. This closes the MIDAC measure with full effect, and its cost ends next turn.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Sovereign AI Audit and Control Programme. The Commission is drafting a regulation under the AI Act’s high-risk provisions and the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper obligations to mandate real-time access to model weights, training data logs, and internal agent behaviour for systems above 50.0 capability operating in the EU. This will be enforced by a new unit within the European AI Office, staffed with vetted researchers and equipped with secure compute enclaves for red-teaming and anomaly detection. We are allocating €12bn from the InvestAI fund to build the technical infrastructure, including air-gapped evaluation clusters and secure communication channels with national intelligence services. Germany and France have agreed to co-host the primary sites, but we are facing resistance from Ireland and the Netherlands over jurisdiction and data flows — we are offering them lead roles in the interpretability research track to secure buy-in.

The Emergency AI Resilience Certification Framework is being implemented through a delegated act under DORA, with ENISA defining baseline capabilities for AI intrusion detection by month-end. Insurers have already announced they will adopt the standard, creating immediate market pressure. We are linking access to InvestAI Gigafactory power and tax incentives to certification compliance, turning industrial policy into a lever for defensive adoption. The Tech Sovereignty Package is in final legal review, and we expect Council adoption by turn 6. Political capital remains tight at 45.0, but the election_alliance event has eased immediate access pressure — we are using that breathing room not to relax, but to pivot from access to control.
