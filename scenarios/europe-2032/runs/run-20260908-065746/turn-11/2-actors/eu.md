## Priority  
**Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive** — because without sovereign infrastructure, our runtime intervention mandate is a command without force: we can demand access, but if we cannot run our own systems when foreign providers withdraw, that access is meaningless. The directive builds the substrate of enforcement, evaluation, and continuity — and its success or failure will determine whether the EU acts as a power or pleads as a client.

## In practice  
We are driving the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive through the Council’s Competitiveness formation, using the emergency clause of the Net-Zero Industry Act to fast-track permitting and the Digital Europe Programme to allocate €8.2 billion in cohesion and IPCEI funds. The legal basis is clear, but the politics are not: Ireland still resists any measure that conditions data free-flow on physical co-location, fearing it will deter investment, while Poland demands guarantees that its node will not become a secondary site. To close the gap, we are offering Dublin a binding 20% allocation of its local sovereign compute output for SMEs and research consortia — a concession that preserves market access while embedding mutual obligation. For Warsaw, we are advancing disbursement of public AI retraining funds and offering leadership of the node’s technical council, turning resistance into ownership.

At the same time, we are finalizing the rollout of the Mandatory Runtime Intervention Architecture, which finishes this turn. The European Digital Infrastructure Authority (EDIA) is conducting compliance sweeps across all high-risk AI deployments — including foreign models operating in healthcare, transport, and energy — verifying integration of the EU-controlled intervention interface. Firms found non-compliant by the end of December will be suspended from cross-border data flows under Article 17 of the Data Governance Act. The deadline is real, and so is the enforcement: we are not building a registry, we are building a circuit breaker. But we know that without sovereign compute, even a perfect circuit breaker cannot restore power when the grid goes down. That is why compute is the priority — not because it is flashy, but because it is foundational. This turn, we are not chasing the frontier. We are building ground.
