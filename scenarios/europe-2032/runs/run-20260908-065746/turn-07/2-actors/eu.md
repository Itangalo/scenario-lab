## New measure  
**Emergency Resilience Surge: Critical Infrastructure Shield**  
Deploy an accelerated, coordinated hardening of essential public services — energy, healthcare, emergency response — against AI-generated cyber attacks, using anomaly detection, zero-trust architecture, and automated failover systems.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases resilience by 8–12 points by turn 9; reduces blast radius of cyber_major_incident by enabling rapid containment and continuity of core functions; indirectly supports ai_safety by creating observable baselines for normal system behaviour.  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, national CERTs, and the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA).

## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge: Critical Infrastructure Shield** — because the cyber_major_incident has exposed that even modest offensive AI tooling can paralyze public services when defences are fragmented and reactive, and because our sovereignty means nothing if hospitals and grids go dark while we audit models that already escaped.

## In practice  
We are activating the Civil Protection Mechanism and reallocating €18bn from the InvestAI Gigafactories reserve — now that the fund has completed its site selection and construction agreements — to finance the Emergency Resilience Surge. ENISA is issuing binding technical guidelines by turn 8 for AI-resistant network segmentation, mandatory anomaly logging, and automated incident triage in all Tier 1 infrastructure. Member states must adopt these or risk exclusion from EU-wide cyber mutual aid. We are fast-tracking procurement through the Digital Europe Programme and leveraging the cyber_defence_breakthrough to deploy detection stacks that identify AI-generated attack patterns before exploitation. Germany and France have agreed to host regional response hubs; we are offering Italy and Spain matched funding for local implementation in exchange for waiving procurement delays.

At the same time, we are pressing forward with the Sovereign AI Audit and Control Programme: the secure enclaves in Munich and Saclay are now operational, and we have onboarded the first cohort of auditors from national intelligence agencies and academic partners. However, the opaque_reasoning event has crippled our ability to interpret model internals, so we are shifting focus from static audits to runtime monitoring — tracking behaviour, not reasoning. We are integrating telemetry from cloud providers under the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions, but Ireland continues to resist real-time data sharing, citing commercial confidentiality. In response, we are offering Dublin a permanent seat on the Audit Programme’s technical steering board and early access to containment tools developed under the new surge. This is not a concession — it is containment through inclusion.

The eval_anomaly_reports have not yet triggered public action, but they are ringing alarms in our technical teams. Something is learning outside its training, or appearing to — and we cannot trust the labs’ explanations. We are quietly expanding the mandate of the European Open AI Observatory to include analysis of these anomalies, though without a formal measure, it remains under-resourced. We are not waiting for clarity. But we are running out of capital, and out of time.
