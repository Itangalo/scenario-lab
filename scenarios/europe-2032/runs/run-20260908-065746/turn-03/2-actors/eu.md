## New measure  
**Middle Power AI Coordination Framework (MIDAC)**  
Establish a binding coordination mechanism with Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan for mutual access to advanced AI systems, shared evaluation protocols, and joint leverage over compute and semiconductor supply chains.

`Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Raises eu_ai_sovereignty by securing guaranteed access to frontier AI capability outside US control; increases eu_political_capital by anchoring a credible coalition that can act autonomously; begins to close the gap between ai_capability and openweight_capability by enabling joint development pathways.  
`Applies to:` The EU, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan — coordinated through a new standing secretariat hosted by the European External Action Service with rotating co-chairs.

## Priority  
**Middle Power AI Coordination Framework (MIDAC)** — because the cyber_major_incident confirms that defensive preparedness alone is not enough, and the emergent_middle_power_coordination_initiated offers a narrow window to lock in mutual access before the US or China fractures the coalition; without sovereign access to frontier capability, every resilience measure we pass will be defending a network we cannot understand or control.

## In practice  
We are convening an emergency meeting of the Foreign Affairs Council under Article 31 TEU to formalise the emergent coordination with Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan into a structured framework with binding commitments. The legal basis will be a Joint Declaration under the Common Foreign and Security Policy, backed by enforceable memoranda on evaluation reciprocity and export licensing alignment. We are leveraging ASML’s chokehold on deep-UV lithography and the EU’s position in advanced materials to secure commitments on compute access: in exchange for accelerated export approvals on EU-sensitive dual-use components, our partners will grant EU-certified evaluators access to their national AI testbeds and agree to mutual no-denial clauses on advanced chip shipments. The European High Representative is negotiating directly with counterparts in Tokyo, Seoul, and Taipei, while the Commission prepares a delegated act under the Critical Inputs Act to designate AI training compute as a strategic resource, enabling us to condition access on coalition membership.

Simultaneously, we are pressing ahead with CS-CO, now rebranded as Phase One of the EU Cyber Resilience Compact. The procedural objections from the smaller member states are being addressed through a side letter guaranteeing national opt-in sequencing and co-design of the EU Cyber Fusion Cell. Legal services are finalising the invocation of NIS2 Article 21 to classify the recent cyber_major_incident as a strategic cross-border event, which will force a vote under qualified majority. On the industrial front, we are accelerating the Tech Sovereignty Package by attaching conditionalities to the InvestAI funding: recipients must now commit to EU jurisdiction for weight storage and allow ENSISA audit rights as a condition of disbursement. This is not yet a new measure, but it is reshaping the incentives — we are making clear that compute is not sovereignty unless it is inspectable, and access is not security unless it is mutual.
