## In practice  
We are finalising the MIDAC Joint Declaration, with the European External Action Service hosting the first plenary of the standing secretariat in Brussels. The High Representative is pressing Tokyo, Seoul, and Taipei to sign memoranda on three pillars: reciprocal access to national AI testbeds for certified evaluators, alignment on export licensing for advanced semiconductor equipment, and a no-denial clause on shipments of training-grade GPUs between coalition members. We are backing this with a delegated act under the Critical Inputs Act, designating AI training compute as a strategic resource — this allows us to condition EU export approvals on coalition participation, leveraging ASML’s position without violating WTO commitments. Legal services are reconciling CFSP procedures with competition law to ensure that joint bargaining does not trigger internal market challenges.

At the same time, we are operationalising the finish of Cyber Shield for Critical Operators. The invocation of NIS2 Article 21 has cleared the way for qualified majority voting, and the Council is expected to adopt the standards this month. The EU Cyber Fusion Cell is being stood up under ENISA with initial staffing from Germany, France, and the Netherlands, and will begin live monitoring in Q3. We are also embedding the emergent_insurance_market_shift into policy by launching the Emergency AI Resilience Certification Framework: the Commission is drafting a delegated act under the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) to define minimum capabilities for AI intrusion detection systems, which insurers can then require as a condition of coverage. This creates a fast, market-enforced upgrade path for defensive AI without waiting for new legislation.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is entering its final drafting phase, with the Council Legal Service resolving disputes over audit rights and jurisdiction for model weights. We are linking disbursement of InvestAI funds to compliance with the certification framework and to MIDAC-aligned export practices, making clear that industrial support now flows through strategic coherence. Political capital remains tight, but we are holding the line — the cyberattack has made inaction costlier than overreach.
