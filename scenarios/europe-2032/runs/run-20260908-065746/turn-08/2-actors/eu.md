## In practice  
We are acting on the lesson of the last six months: audits before deployment are not enough when systems behave unpredictably in the wild and when their weights are already beyond recall. The Sovereign AI Audit Programme has delivered its core infrastructure — the secure enclaves in Munich and Saclay are live, and our auditors have begun runtime telemetry integration — but its mandate was too narrow. We are now converting that capability into enforcement: using the AI Act’s conformity assessment framework to mandate real-time behavioural logging and remote interruption access for all AI systems touching critical infrastructure. This applies equally to domestic operators and foreign providers — if your model runs hospital triage or grid balancing in the EU, it must have a monitored runtime and a verified off-switch under EU authority.

To make this credible, we are leveraging the joint_threat_response: the new cyber command structure offers shared telemetry standards that we will now adopt as regulatory baselines. ENISA, in coordination with national CERTs, will issue technical specifications by turn 9, and non-compliant operators will be barred from public contracts and essential service provider lists by turn 10. We are offering transition support through the Digital Europe Programme, but no carve-outs. Ireland’s resistance to telemetry will be met with a clear choice: comply with the new framework or lose access to the mutual aid protections it depends on. At the same time, we are integrating tools released by the fractured lab’s independent collective — their open-source anomaly detectors will be piloted in the German and French response hubs under the Emergency Resilience Surge. This is not a pause. It is a pivot: from trusting developers to watching systems, from controlling origins to containing consequences.
