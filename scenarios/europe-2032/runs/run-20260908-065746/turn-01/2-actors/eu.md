## New measure
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)**  
Establish mandatory intrusion detection and response standards for all critical infrastructure operators, with EU-level monitoring and rapid response teams.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +15, ai_safety +5 (indirectly, by reducing attack surface and enabling faster incident learning)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with enforcement through ENSISA and the NIS2 Directive framework

## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)** — because the cyber_test_shot event proves that existing detection is insufficient and that the window for voluntary compliance has closed; this measure directly addresses a demonstrated vulnerability in essential services, and failure to act now risks cascading failures in energy, water and transport.

## In practice
We are launching the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO) under the NIS2 Directive’s expanded scope, using Article 21 to mandate baseline detection capabilities — including memory-resident agent monitoring, behavioural anomaly tracking, and automated telemetry to a new EU Cyber Fusion Cell hosted at ENSISA. This requires Council adoption in the Digital Affairs formation, with qualified majority now possible due to recent alignment among core member states. Germany and France support the principle but resist centralised monitoring; we are offering them co-leadership of the Fusion Cell and a phased rollout that begins with cross-border operators in energy and ports. The €9.2bn cost will be split 70% from the Digital Europe Programme and 30% co-financed by member states, with flexibility for smaller economies.

At the same time, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by fast-tracking environmental assessments in three candidate zones — one in Spain, one in Finland, and one in Poland — using the proposed accelerated-permitting zones from the Tech sovereignty package. The Commission is negotiating with national regulators to pre-approve grid connections up to 200MW per site, conditional on binding commitments from consortiums led by Atos, Siemens, and a new EU AI Foundation. The main resistance comes from regional authorities concerned about water and energy use; we are countering with binding promises of local reinvestment and priority access to AI-powered grid optimisation tools.

Public sentiment remains ambivalent, but the cyber_test_shot has shifted media framing from abstract risk to concrete vulnerability. We are leveraging this to justify CS-CO as a necessary shield, not a restriction — a narrative coordinated through the Commission’s Digital Champions network. No new measure is proposed beyond CS-CO this turn, as political capital is already stretched across three major initiatives, and overreach risks fracturing cohesion.
