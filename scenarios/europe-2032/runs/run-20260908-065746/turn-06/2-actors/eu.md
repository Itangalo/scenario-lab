## In practice  
We are finalising the legal drafting of the Sovereign AI Audit and Control Programme under the high-risk provisions of the AI Act and the gatekeeper enforcement powers of the Digital Markets Act. The European AI Office is being expanded into a technical authority with secure compute enclaves in Germany and France, funded by a €12bn allocation from the InvestAI Gigafactories reserve. These sites will host red-teaming operations, runtime monitoring, and weight-security audits for all systems above 50.0 capability operating in the EU. To overcome resistance from Ireland and the Netherlands, we are offering them co-leadership of the interpretability research track and guaranteed access to anomaly-detection tools developed under the programme. The first technical specifications for real-time logging and model introspection are being drafted with ENISA and national intelligence services, with a pilot expected by turn 7.

The Tech Sovereignty Package has cleared legal review and is now before the Council for adoption. With private investment responding to the promise of accelerated permitting and grid access, we are on track to mobilise €180bn of the targeted €200bn by 2036. However, the lack of sovereign energy infrastructure remains a bottleneck: we are now pressing member states to designate AI compute as critical load in national grid planning. Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories remain on schedule, with site selection completed and construction underway at two locations; the third is delayed by local opposition in Spain, which we are addressing through regional development offsets.

The new Secure Open-Weight Monitoring and Containment Initiative responds to the silent escalation in distributed capability: leaked model fragments are now being stitched together into functional agents outside any oversight perimeter. Using the emergent_insurance_market_shift as leverage, we are mandating that any entity distributing models above 45.0 capability must register with the European AI Office and submit to random integrity checks. We are also funding a new European Open AI Observatory — hosted by the EU Agency for Cybersecurity — to monitor public repositories, detect dangerous adaptations, and coordinate containment with national CERTs. This is not about stopping open innovation, but about ensuring that the open frontier does not become a backdoor for uncontrolled risk. The cyber_defence_breakthrough gives us a narrow window: we must build detection and disruption capacity now, before the next offensive leap.
