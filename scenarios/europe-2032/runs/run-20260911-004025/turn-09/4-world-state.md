### Patchwork holds, ground shifts
The second half of 2030 was defined by holding degraded systems together. The bio containment regime matured into routine: pooled procurement, surge sequencing and protected wards kept hospitals open, and the cyber shield teams cleared backlogs in municipal and water networks. Power and water ran, appointments returned, but slowly. Officials called it continuity; mayors called it triage.

That fragile steadiness was overtaken from outside. Commercial humanoid robots, built on Chinese hardware and American control software, began appearing in logistics pilots and automotive suppliers. The price undercut European integrators within months. Works councils in automotive regions warned of lines retooled without workers, and retraining pilots under the new displacement buffer started too small to match the anxiety.

Diplomacy added humiliation. Washington and Beijing announced a limited risk pact on weights security and biological design tools, with thin verification. Brussels, offering incident data from its shield and sequencing network, was briefed afterwards, not consulted. The press read it as proof the Union defends but does not decide.

The wound deepened when a large member state signed its own compute and robotics arrangement with an American hyperscaler, breaking siting discipline for cheaper access. The Commission called it pragmatism within unity; every other capital read it as a separate deal.

There was real science. A Franco-Dutch lab, using AI-guided search, reported a solid-state electrolyte with industrial implications for batteries — genuine advance, celebrated, but years from factories. Patients still praised tailored therapies, yet nightly coverage paired cures with outages and robots. Trust sagged further.

### Friction
Co-funding for the buffer stalled as automating employers haggled, and the American hyperscaler deal froze Paris fab planning again.
