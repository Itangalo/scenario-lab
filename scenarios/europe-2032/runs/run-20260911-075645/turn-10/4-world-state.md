### A War No One Voted For
In February, American and Chinese forces exchanged strikes after months of warnings about a closing window for decisive advantage. By March, fabrication plants, undersea cables, satellite links and data centres were listed openly as military targets. Europe was not consulted. A cable cut in the Atlantic, debris warnings over low orbit, and a fire at a supplier fab in Dresden brought the war into council agendas within weeks.

The Council declared the Union non-belligerent, denied targeting use of its territory, cables and computing sites, and ordered dispersal of registries, hospital systems and emergency communications. Estonian, French and German reserve teams that had spent the autumn restoring town-hall records were redeployed to keep clean images moving between regions when networks degraded.

### Care Amid Blackouts
The same months brought a genuine therapeutic advance — tailored treatments for conditions long deemed untreatable, demonstrated in American and Chinese clinics. Brussels made access conditional on European hosting, regulatory verification and delivery through public clinics, tying retraining placements to triage, sequencing and repair crews.

Where it arrived, it helped. Waiting rooms in Denmark and Portugal saw new referrals; nurses credited the staffing guarantees. Elsewhere, patients watched news of cures they could not yet receive. Power brownouts, cancelled freight, frozen graduate hiring and fresh warehouse automation marches kept mood brittle.

The new shelter operation exists mostly as fuel stocks, pooled reagents, hardened logistics contracts and airspace orders. It kept lights and registries degrading rather than stopping, but did not prevent loss: missing accelerators in Paris and Warsaw now matter less than damaged cables and diverted supply, and European re-runs of multi-day self-tooling systems fell further behind as the frontier labs went under military control.

By June, blame had shifted outward. The Union was poorer, less connected, and still standing — a neutrality that held on paper, a health service that had not collapsed, and a public that saw survival as policy.
