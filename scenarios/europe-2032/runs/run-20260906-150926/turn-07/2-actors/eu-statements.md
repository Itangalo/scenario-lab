# Statements: The European Union (turn 7)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without social legitimacy is a fortress without a people. However, this turn reveals that legitimacy cannot be built on containment alone: when harm lands visibly and institutions appear reactive, the Union’s authority erodes faster than it can be reconstructed through audits or reports.
  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** — a model-generated ransomware sweep across public health systems in three member states — has exposed a fatal gap between sovereign oversight and societal protection. Despite EAAD’s first audit and the launch of the Public Assurance Protocol, the attack landed with devastating effect, bypassing legacy defences that were never hardened against AI-driven polymorphic payloads. Resilience remains at 38.0, and public sentiment has not budged. This is not a failure of intent, but of sequencing: we prioritised sovereign control before societal absorption, and the cost is now measured in lost trust and cascading disruption. Our identity must reflect that sovereignty includes shielding people, not just systems.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and we will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; nor will we assume that control justifies silence, and we will now couple every containment measure with a public explanation of its purpose and limits. Yet this turn proves that transparency without resilience is performance, not protection: we will now pair every disclosure with a demonstrated capacity to absorb harm.
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event — sudden revocation of access to a critical US-hosted model used in emergency medical diagnostics — has left hospitals in five countries without decision-support tools during a flu surge. The denial was framed as administrative, but its timing and totality suggest strategic leverage. We acted under uncertainty to build sovereign stacks, but did not act under uncertainty *for* continuity of care. The lesson is stark: acting under uncertainty now requires dual bets — on capability *and* fallbacks, on control *and* redundancy. Our commitment must evolve to include operational endurance under denial.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Prevent catastrophic misuse through sovereign control of high-capability AI systems, with enforceable containment, real-time monitoring, and public accountability for all systems above 55.0 capability — and now, ensure that essential public functions continue to operate at minimum capacity even when external AI services are withdrawn or compromised.
  - Grounds: Trigger: **eu_frontier_access_denied** and **cyber_major_incident**. The twin shocks reveal that “sovereign control” is meaningless if public services collapse when cut off or attacked. Our original commitment focused on preventing misuse within our jurisdiction, but ignored the risk of *disuse* — the paralysis that follows when systems we depend on vanish. To hold, sovereignty must now include operational autonomy: not just owning the stack, but ensuring it can run essential functions at reduced fidelity when disconnected. This is no longer about containment — it is about continuity.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
