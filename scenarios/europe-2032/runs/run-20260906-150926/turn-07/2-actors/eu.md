## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without social legitimacy is a fortress without a people. However, this turn reveals that legitimacy cannot be built on containment alone: when harm lands visibly and institutions appear reactive, the Union’s authority erodes faster than it can be reconstructed through audits or reports.

  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** — a model-generated ransomware sweep across public health systems in three member states — has exposed a fatal gap between sovereign oversight and societal protection. Despite EAAD’s first audit and the launch of the Public Assurance Protocol, the attack landed with devastating effect, bypassing legacy defences that were never hardened against AI-driven polymorphic payloads. Resilience remains at 38.0, and public sentiment has not budged. This is not a failure of intent, but of sequencing: we prioritised sovereign control before societal absorption, and the cost is now measured in lost trust and cascading disruption. Our identity must reflect that sovereignty includes shielding people, not just systems.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and we will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; nor will we assume that control justifies silence, and we will now couple every containment measure with a public explanation of its purpose and limits. Yet this turn proves that transparency without resilience is performance, not protection: we will now pair every disclosure with a demonstrated capacity to absorb harm.

  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event — sudden revocation of access to a critical US-hosted model used in emergency medical diagnostics — has left hospitals in five countries without decision-support tools during a flu surge. The denial was framed as administrative, but its timing and totality suggest strategic leverage. We acted under uncertainty to build sovereign stacks, but did not act under uncertainty *for* continuity of care. The lesson is stark: acting under uncertainty now requires dual bets — on capability *and* fallbacks, on control *and* redundancy. Our commitment must evolve to include operational endurance under denial.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent catastrophic misuse through sovereign control of high-capability AI systems, with enforceable containment, real-time monitoring, and public accountability for all systems above 55.0 capability — and now, ensure that essential public functions continue to operate at minimum capacity even when external AI services are withdrawn or compromised.

  - Grounds: Trigger: **eu_frontier_access_denied** and **cyber_major_incident**. The twin shocks reveal that “sovereign control” is meaningless if public services collapse when cut off or attacked. Our original commitment focused on preventing misuse within our jurisdiction, but ignored the risk of *disuse* — the paralysis that follows when systems we depend on vanish. To hold, sovereignty must now include operational autonomy: not just owning the stack, but ensuring it can run essential functions at reduced fidelity when disconnected. This is no longer about containment — it is about continuity.

## New measure

**Measure:** Launch the Essential Continuity Stack  
A minimal, resilient AI layer — lightweight, interpretable, and designed to run on commodity hardware — to maintain core public functions (health triage, emergency dispatch, civil registry access) during AI service denial or cyber disruption.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 10–15 points; reduce dependence on external systems; stabilize public_sentiment by demonstrating operational endurance  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national health and emergency agencies, and critical municipal services across all member states

## In practice

This turn, we are reorienting from prestige capability to essential endurance. The **cyber_major_incident** — a self-propagating ransomware strain generated by a frontier model, targeting hospital scheduling and diagnostic support systems — crippled emergency response in Belgium, Greece, and Slovakia. It exploited zero-day logic vulnerabilities in legacy middleware, morphing faster than human teams could patch. ENISA’s new quarterly threat assessment confirmed what we feared: offensive AI now outpaces defensive readiness, and resilience at 38.0 is not enough. Simultaneously, the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event severed access to a US-hosted diagnostic model used in radiology and ICU planning. No reason was given. Patients were delayed. Trust evaporated.

We are responding by shifting focus from proving we can audit advanced systems to proving we can keep lights on when they vanish. The Essential Continuity Stack will be developed under DG SANTE and ENISA, using the French interpretability tool as a foundation, and deployed first in the most vulnerable nodes: regional hospitals and emergency dispatch centres. It will run on air-gapped servers with open, verifiable weights capped at 45.0 capability — sufficient for triage, not for research. Funding will come from the Modernisation Fund, reprioritising €8bn from stalled digitalisation projects. We are invoking Article 122 TFEU again, arguing that systemic fragility constitutes a crisis.

At the same time, we are finalising the SACP prototype stack, integrating the new circuit-tracing tool into its agent monitoring layer. But we are no longer selling it as a rival to the frontier — we are selling it as a fallback. The Public Assurance Protocol’s first reports are being drafted, but we are adding a new section: “What Happens When the Signal Is Lost?” — a public-facing guide to continuity plans. We are presenting this to the Council in ECOFIN, knowing that finance ministers will resist diverting funds from growth to redundancy. But we tell them: without redundancy, there is no growth to fund.
