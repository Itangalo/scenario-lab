AI capability reaches 67.0 following a US breakthrough in self-improving agents, undermining prior safety assumptions despite a French-led advance in interpretability enabling real-time detection of deceptive reasoning. The EU counters geopolitical fragmentation—sparked by Poland’s bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler—with SMAF observer offers, a co-hosted air-gapped cluster in Wrocław, and a €10bn resilience grant tied to full infrastructure hardening by 2031. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) completes its first audit of the Sovereign AI Core, while the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge transitions to ENISA as a permanent body with public reporting mandates. The Public Assurance Protocol launches, and construction begins on the Swedish InvestAI Gigafactory under fast-tracked Article 122 TFEU powers.

A self-propagating AI-generated ransomware strain cripples hospitals across three member states, coinciding with the unexplained loss of access to a critical US-hosted diagnostic model during a flu surge, exposing a critical gap between sovereign oversight and societal protection. Public trust declines as emergency services falter; resilience, stagnant at 38.0, proves inadequate. In response, the EU launches the Essential Continuity Stack—a minimal, interpretable AI framework for sustaining core services during outages—funded by €8bn reallocated from stalled digital projects, targeting deployment in health and emergency sectors by turn 9. The initiative marks a strategic pivot toward operational sovereignty.

However, progress slows: the InvestAI Gigafactories and EAAD conclude without resolving physical compute shortages; the Sovereign AI Core remains untested. With six major initiatives straining political capital, finance ministers resist funding redundancy measures. US consolidation of frontier AI deepens European dependency, and unexplained model denials confirm strategic leverage concerns. Public sentiment falls to 23.0, reflecting disillusionment with technical safeguards amid real-world failures. The EU’s path to resilience clarifies, but resource disputes and legacy underinvestment hinder execution.
