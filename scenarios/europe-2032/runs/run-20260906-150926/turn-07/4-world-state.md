### The Cost of Continuity

A self-propagating ransomware strain, generated by a frontier AI, cripples hospital systems across three member states. Legacy middleware collapses under polymorphic attacks that evolve faster than human patching can respond. Simultaneously, access to a critical US-hosted diagnostic model vanishes without explanation, disrupting ICU planning and radiology workflows during a flu surge. The twin shocks expose a fatal gap: sovereign oversight exists, but societal protection does not.

Public trust erodes further as emergency services falter. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) publishes its first audit, and the Public Assurance Protocol launches, but both read as technical theatre amid real-world harm. Resilience, stagnant at 38.0, proves insufficient against coordinated AI-driven disruption.

In response, the EU pivots from prestige to survival. The Essential Continuity Stack is launched as a small, category 6 measure, aiming to sustain core public functions on minimal, interpretable AI during outages. Funded by reprioritising €8bn from stalled digital projects, it targets deployment in vulnerable health and emergency nodes by turn 9. The move is framed as operational sovereignty — not control over cutting-edge models, but the ability to keep essential services running when they fail.

Yet momentum falters. The InvestAI Gigafactories and EAAD formally conclude, but physical compute remains scarce. The Sovereign AI Core Programme nears completion, but its prototype stack is still untested under stress. With six major initiatives ongoing, political capital tightens. Finance ministers resist diverting funds to redundancy, calling it defeatism.

The US posture of consolidation deepens dependency. No new frontier access is granted, and the sudden model denial confirms fears of strategic leverage. Public sentiment dips to 23.0 as headlines highlight disruption over audits.

### A Narrow Path Forward

The EU’s shift is real, but narrow. It acts under uncertainty, yet pays the price of prior underinvestment. The path to resilience is now clear — but the resources to walk it remain in dispute.
