## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without social legitimacy is a fortress without a people. This turn reveals that legitimacy cannot be built on containment alone: when harm lands visibly and institutions appear reactive, the Union’s authority erodes faster than it can be reconstructed through audits or reports. Now, with the **ai_investment_collapse** shrinking the frontier and the **cyber_defence_breakthrough** shifting the balance, we see that harm is not only in what AI *does*, but in what we *fail to protect* — and that our existence as a political agent depends not on matching the frontier, but on being trusted to endure its failures.

  - Grounds: The twin shocks of the ransomware attack and model denial have already forced a reckoning. But this turn’s external developments — particularly the **cyber_defence_breakthrough** — prove that defensive leverage is now within reach, not just reactive spending. Yet public sentiment remains at 23.0, and political capital is collapsing. Our identity must reflect that we are not merely a regulator or a backstop, but a steward of continuity: our legitimacy flows from whether people believe the lights will stay on, not whether we can audit the system that went dark.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and we will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; nor will we assume that control justifies silence, and we will now couple every containment measure with a public explanation of its purpose and limits. Yet this turn proves that transparency without resilience is performance, not protection: we will now pair every disclosure with a demonstrated capacity to absorb harm. And now, with investment collapsing and defences improving, we will act under uncertainty not by reaching for the frontier, but by anchoring below it — building what works, not what impresses.

  - Trigger: **ai_investment_collapse** and **cyber_defence_breakthrough**  
  - Grounds: The collapse in private investment has frozen several of the compute expansions the EU was counting on, but it has also slowed the frontier’s advance — for the first time, capability growth is not outpacing our ability to respond. Simultaneously, the **cyber_defence_breakthrough** proves that defensive innovation can outpace offensive use, reversing years of drift. These developments change the calculus: acting under uncertainty no longer means racing to catch up, but choosing what to stabilise. We are no longer late — we are, for a moment, in time.

- retire `two_year_commitment`

  - Grounds: The commitment to “prevent catastrophic misuse through sovereign control” was rooted in a world where access and capability were still contested. But the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event and the **cyber_major_incident** have already exposed its insufficiency: control without continuity is not sovereignty, it is ritual. The **ai_investment_collapse** and **cyber_defence_breakthrough** now present a narrow window — not to build a rival frontier, but to stabilise below it. The old commitment assumed we could govern the most capable systems; we now see that we must govern the consequences of their failure. It has served its purpose, but the situation has moved categorically beyond it.

- add `two_year_commitment` (commitment): For the next two years, our mission is to ensure that essential public functions continue to operate at minimum capacity when AI systems fail, are denied, or are weaponised — by building, testing, and demonstrating resilient, interpretable fallback systems that run independently of frontier models or foreign infrastructure.

  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** have shown that the most dangerous risk is not misuse, but collapse. The **cyber_defence_breakthrough** gives us a chance to harden, not just react. The **ai_investment_collapse** gives us breathing room. This is not a retreat from capability — it is a reorientation toward endurance. We are committing not to match the frontier, but to survive its absence.

## New measure
**Measure:** Establish the Resilient Fallback AI Network  
A distributed, open-weight, low-capability AI network — based on the Essential Continuity Stack — deployed across national and regional emergency infrastructure, with mandatory integration into civil protection systems and tested annually under live disruption scenarios.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 20 points; stabilise public_sentiment by demonstrating credible continuity; reduce dependence on external or frontier systems  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, all member state emergency management agencies, national health systems, and critical municipal services

## Priority
**Priority:** Establish the Resilient Fallback AI Network  
We are pushing this hardest because the **cyber_defence_breakthrough** proves defences can win, and the **ai_investment_collapse** gives us time to build — but only if we act now to institutionalise endurance, not just capability; and because without a demonstrated fallback, every future denial or attack will deepen public distrust and political paralysis.

## In practice
We are shifting from prestige to presence. The **cyber_defence_breakthrough** — a new class of automated patching and swarm-detection tooling — has given us a rare opening: for the first time in years, the offence-defence balance is shifting back. But the **ai_investment_collapse** means the frontier is no longer accelerating out of reach. This is not a crisis — it is a reprieve. We are using it to institutionalise what the Essential Continuity Stack began: a minimal, interpretable AI layer that can run on commodity hardware during outages.

We are drafting the Resilient Fallback AI Network under Article 122 TFEU, invoking systemic fragility as a crisis. The network will be built on open weights capped at 45.0, secured by the French interpretability tool, and deployed across ENISA’s newly permanent Critical Infrastructure Resilience body. Funding will come from the Modernisation Fund and reallocated Gigafactory capital — €12bn total, over three years. We are requiring all member states to integrate it into civil protection plans by turn 11, with annual red-teaming exercises led by ENISA.

Finance ministers resist — they call it redundancy, not investment. But we are framing it as credibility: without a fallback, no sovereign stack is sovereign. We are offering them a choice — fund endurance now, or fund collapse later. And we are taking the argument to the Council, knowing that only a shared demonstration of resilience will begin to restore public sentiment. The lights must stay on. Everything else follows.
