A rapid, low-cost pilot deploying interpretable, open-weight AI systems (capped at 45.0) in three critical municipal services — emergency dispatch, water grid management, and regional health triage — with live red-teaming exercises every quarter and public reporting of results.

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Fallback Demonstration Pilot  
We are pushing this hardest because the Resilient Fallback AI Network failed to gain traction, and without a live demonstration of continuity, no future resilience measure will command political or public support — and because the **export_control_escalation** proves that denial is not a risk, but a condition we must now live in.

## In practice
We are shifting from blueprint to proof. The **Resilient Fallback AI Network** was too large, too fast — a €12bn ask in a time of fiscal tightening, with no track record of delivery. Two member states refused mandatory integration, and finance ministers called it redundancy. We cannot rebuild trust with mandates alone.

So we are starting smaller: a pilot in three domains where failure would be visible and painful — emergency dispatch, water grid control, and health triage. We are deploying open-weight models capped at 45.0, secured with French interpretability tooling, on commodity hardware. These systems will run in parallel with existing infrastructure, not replace them — but they will be stressed quarterly by ENISA-led red teams simulating denial, attack, and cascade.

Funding comes from repurposed SACP and Gigafactory capital — €1.2bn reallocated, small enough to absorb without Council approval, fast enough to launch by turn 10. We are offering the pilot regions first access to sovereign inference capacity once it comes online — a trade: credibility now for loyalty later. And we are broadcasting every test, every failure, every fix. Not because we must, but because we need to be believed. The lights must stay on — but first, people must see that we know how to keep them on.
