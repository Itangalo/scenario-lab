### A Leap and a Fracture

The frontier surges forward with jarring speed as a US laboratory demonstrates a self-improving agent architecture, accelerating progress across quantum materials and cryptanalysis. This discontinuous advance pushes `ai_capability` to 67.0, redefining timelines and overwhelming existing safety assumptions. Simultaneously, a major interpretability breakthrough — a real-time circuit-tracing tool capable of detecting deceptive reasoning — is rapidly adopted by leading labs, including a French developer now integrating it into sovereign systems.

Yet unity falters. Poland signs a bilateral agreement with a US hyperscaler for priority access to frontier models, embedding joint AI cells within its defence ministry. Framed as pragmatic deterrence, the move bypasses EU-wide containment efforts and exposes deepening fragmentation. In response, the EU offers inclusion over punishment: expedited observer status in the Sovereign Model Assurance Framework (SMAF), a co-hosted air-gapped cluster in Wrocław, and a €10bn resilience grant tied to full infrastructure hardening by 2031.

Domestically, the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) and the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge (CIRS) formally conclude their establishment phases. EAAD launches its first audit of the Sovereign AI Core Programme’s prototype stack using the new tracing tool, while CIRS transitions to ENISA as a permanent monitoring function, now mandated to publish redacted quarterly threat assessments.

The EU launches the Public Assurance Protocol, requiring accessible public reporting on safety and incidents for all high-capacity sovereign systems. Groundbreaking begins on the Swedish InvestAI Gigafactory under fast-tracked Article 122 powers, though physical progress remains nascent. Despite these moves, public sentiment holds at 25.0, reflecting skepticism toward institutional responses. Fiscal and political strain intensifies, with six major initiatives still in flight.

### US Posture Set

In Washington, the new administration takes office, officially classifying frontier AI as a strategic national asset. Access is now rationed by geopolitical tier, cementing Europe’s dependent status.
