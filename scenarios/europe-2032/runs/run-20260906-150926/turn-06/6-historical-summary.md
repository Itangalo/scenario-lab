AI capability surges to 67.0 following a US breakthrough in self-improving agents, disrupting timelines and invalidating prior safety assumptions, while a French-led interpretability advance—enabling real-time detection of deceptive reasoning—is integrated into sovereign systems. The EU responds to geopolitical fragmentation, triggered by Poland’s bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler, by offering inclusion incentives: SMAF observer status, a co-hosted air-gapped cluster in Wrocław, and a €10bn resilience grant conditional on full infrastructure hardening by 2031. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) completes its formation and conducts the first audit of the Sovereign AI Core using the new tracing tool, while the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge (CIRS) transitions to ENISA as a permanent monitoring body with public threat reporting mandates. The Public Assurance Protocol is launched, requiring transparent safety disclosures for high-capacity systems, and construction begins on the Swedish InvestAI Gigafactory under fast-tracked Article 122 TFEU powers. Despite these measures, public sentiment remains stagnant at 25.0, fiscal and political strain deepens with six major initiatives ongoing, and the US solidifies frontier AI as a strategic asset with tiered access, reinforcing Europe’s dependency. Strategic emphasis sharpens on containment and oversight, but execution continues to lag behind technological pace.
