### Holding the line without building
July to December 2031 was a semester of maintenance. With council freezes and court suspensions still locking new compute sites, the Commission stayed in civil-protection register. Health emergency teams ran pooled sequencing checks and referral triage, while crews carried over from the hospital continuity effort worked through patch lists for hospitals, labs and water controls.

Progress was partial. Showcase regions with working triage helpers kept imaging waits falling and absorbed screening kits routinely. Elsewhere night monitoring went uncovered, sensor deliveries slipped again, and municipal utilities patched what they could. The common access pact held politically — no second breakaway — but changed no lead times, and the capital with its separate foreign supply arrangement stayed outside the pool. Brussels chose not to force it.

What changed the mood slightly came from ordinary offices. Studies across law, accountancy, administration and consulting showed measurable gains from AI helpers, largest for juniors, with no matching fall in employment. Ministers in employment and health councils seized on the figures to draw a line between useful daily tools and unrecallable frontier misuse, and for a time it slowed the fusion of silent-site vigils with pathogen fear.

It did not reverse it. Queues falling in a few cities could not compete with talk of engineered pathogens, and local press still linked stalled diggers to distrust of any new infrastructure. Police noted single-source reports of threats around sequencing depots and a stalled site, and a rumour of a longer utility shutdown circulated after a brief outage — neither confirmed, both repeated. Wards and grids held through December, but on effort and overtime, not on margin.
