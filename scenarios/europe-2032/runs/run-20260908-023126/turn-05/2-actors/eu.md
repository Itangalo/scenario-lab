## New measure
**Secure the Open Frontier: Sovereign Runtime Assurance for Open-Weight Models**  
We mandate sovereign, tamper-proof execution environments for all open-weight models above 45.0 used in EU critical infrastructure, combining hardware-enforced isolation, real-time anomaly detection, and EU-controlled orchestration to prevent misuse while preserving access.

## Priority
Priority: Emergency Sovereign AI Access Programme  
Because the bilateral deal with the U.S. hyperscaler has exposed the fragility of our collective stance, we are doubling down on sovereign access—not just as a technical fallback but as the only remaining lever to restore credibility and cohesion, making this the anchor of both capability and trust.

## In practice
We are executing the Emergency Sovereign AI Access Programme under Article 122 TFEU, treating it as a non-discretionary response to systemic risk. The Commission has fast-tracked procurement, and EuroHPC JU is now operating exascale partitions in Lyon, Warsaw, and Milan under sovereign control, with model weights signed and backed up in air-gapped facilities managed by ENISA. The Joint Research Centre leads validation under the Temporary Assurance Protocol, restricting use to human-in-the-loop triage, logistics, and translation—no autonomous agents. Staffing remains a bottleneck, but emergency mobilisation of the EU Digital Corps and fast-tracked Schengen visas for AI engineers are beginning to close the gap. We are framing the use of the Solidarity Fund as crisis mitigation, not industrial policy, to bypass ECOFIN’s resistance.

Cyber Shield enters its final trilogue this turn, with Germany and Poland now committed due to embedded red-teaming rights. We are linking its detection mandates to the Emergency Programme by requiring that all sovereign AI hubs run on hardened infrastructure with real-time telemetry shared through the new joint threat response mechanism. The Gigafactories continue to strain grid capacity, but our provisional deal with ENTSO-E reserves 30% of new generation for AI, co-financed by commitments to black-start resilience. The Strengthened Open-Weight Evaluation Protocol is being implemented by the European AI Office with third-party audits rolling out in Q2—closing the loophole that allowed unsafe models into public services under the guise of availability.

The new measure, Secure the Open Frontier, responds to the fact that openweight capability has reached 46.0 and is now dual-use at scale: essential for red-teaming, but also weaponised for forgery and intrusion. We cannot ban it—our own safety research depends on it—but we can contain it. This measure mandates that all models above 45.0 used in critical functions run in sovereign, hardware-isolated environments with EU-controlled monitoring stacks. We will deploy through the EuroHPC infrastructure, using secure enclaves and runtime anomaly detection developed by ETSI and Fraunhofer. The directive will be proposed under Article 114 TFEU, with enforcement through national digital regulators. Resistance from member states reliant on foreign-hosted models will be high, so we are offering transition funding and access to the Emergency Programme’s fine-tuning pipelines as incentives. This is not about stopping open models—it is about ensuring they run where we can see them, and stop them.
