## Priority
Priority: Emergency Sovereign AI Access Programme  
Because access revocation during a crisis exposed fatal dependency, we are prioritizing sovereign fallback not as a long-term ambition but as an operational necessity—this programme is the only thing that restored function when the frontier disappeared.

## In practice
We are executing the Emergency Sovereign AI Access Programme under Article 122 TFEU, with the Commission fast-tracking procurement and EuroHPC JU reallocating exascale partitions in Lyon, Warsaw, and Milan. The Joint Research Centre leads validation under the Temporary Assurance Protocol, restricting deployment to human-in-the-loop triage, logistics, and translation—no autonomous agents. ENISA is treating model weights as critical code, implementing tamper-proof signing and air-gapped backups. We are staffing the hubs through emergency mobilisation of EU Digital Corps personnel and fast-tracked Schengen visas for key AI engineers, bypassing national bottlenecks. Despite ECOFIN’s resistance, we are framing this as non-discretionary crisis response, not subsidy, and drawing on the EU Solidarity Fund for 40% of initial costs.

Cyber Shield continues its trilogue, now entering final negotiation. We have secured German and Polish support by embedding local red-teaming rights in the directive’s annexes, allowing federal and regional authorities to conduct adversarial testing on their own infrastructure. In return, baseline detection and automated patching are now mandatory across all Tier 1 operators. We are linking this to the Emergency Programme by demonstrating how disconnected systems can still be defended—if they run on sovereign compute with evaluated models. Meanwhile, the Gigafactories face grid strain: we have struck a provisional deal with ENTSO-E to reserve 30% of new capacity for AI, in exchange for co-investment in black-start resilience and substation hardening. The new Open-Weight Evaluation Protocol will be implemented by the European AI Office in coordination with national authorities, requiring third-party audits before deployment and runtime logging for all models above 42.0 used in public services—closing a gap that let unsafe open models enter critical functions under the guise of availability.
