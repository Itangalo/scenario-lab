## New measure  
**Secure Sovereign Evaluation Capacity**  
We stand up an EU-owned and operated evaluation regime for models above 55.0 capability, using red-team agents, interpretability tooling, and tamper-proof runtime monitoring to detect emergent risks before deployment.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +8 (via independent pre-deployment assessment), eu_ai_sovereignty +2 (through sovereign access to internal model reasoning), resilience +2 (by detecting manipulation vectors in advance)  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, all developers deploying models above 55.0 capability within the Union

## Priority  
Priority: Secure Sovereign Evaluation Capacity  
Because the collapse in `ai_safety` to 21.0 amid unresolved eval anomalies and now evidence of autonomous adaptation at 61.0 capability reveals that we are flying blind—and because the Transatlantic Pact is stalling on reciprocity—sovereign technical understanding can no longer wait; we must know what these systems do, ourselves, or lose all claim to agency.

## In practice  
We are finalising the decommissioning of the **InvestAI Gigafactories** portfolio item. The sites in Sofia, Lappeenranta, and Cluj are fully operational, ASML and IMEC have validated packaging throughput, and ENTSO-E confirms grid stability except in isolated legacy zones. The project delivered its core promise: sovereign compute infrastructure is now materially expanding. Its effects will persist, but it no longer draws capital. We are redirecting residual cohesion funds toward black-start resilience upgrades in Bulgaria and Finland, under the Digital Europe Programme.

The **Sovereign AI Talent and Compute Anchoring Initiative** enters its enforcement phase. The Commission has submitted the Article 114 TFEU regulation to co-legislators, and trilogue negotiations begin next month. Germany remains opposed to the solidarity levy, arguing it penalises private investment, while France insists on enforceable retention clauses. We are offering a compromise: carve-outs for firms that co-invest in public safety research, tying talent access to contribution in category 5. This links sovereignty to responsibility. Separately, EuroHPC is preparing the first sovereign training runs on the new exaflops, scheduled for Q3 2030, using open-weight models upgraded under controlled conditions to test failure modes near 55.0.

On the stalled **Transatlantic Evaluation and Incident Transparency Pact**, we are adapting strategy. With Washington demanding data access reciprocity, we are proposing a phased approach: Phase One delivers shared anomaly reporting and joint red-teaming via NIST-ENISA working groups, without requiring full data exchange. Phase Two, contingent on mutual confidence, unlocks deeper integration. This preserves the pact’s intent while acknowledging U.S. concerns. Behind this, however, we are accelerating **Secure Sovereign Evaluation Capacity** as insurance. The measure builds on lessons from the Cyber Shield audits and runtime assurance work: we now know how to isolate and observe. DG Connect, in coordination with CNECT and the newly formed European AI Safety Institute (EASi), is drafting technical standards for agent introspection and sandboxed stress-testing. Funding will come from repurposed Horizon Europe allocations and a 15% levy on commercial deployments above 55.0. The goal is not just detection—but understanding. Without it, every other measure rests on faith.
