### Capability Creeps Forward, Safety Falls Behind

Global AI capability advances to 63.5, driven by incremental integration of adaptive planning into closed development loops. Offensive cyber tools grow more evasive, and model-generated exploits now bypass static defences with minimal human input. Despite no singular breakthrough, the accumulation consolidates multi-day autonomy as a baseline.

Openweight capability rises modestly to 52.0, as replication filters through academic channels, though export controls and hardware limits prevent a surge. The gap between frontier and open remains wide — but dual-use concerns intensify.

AI safety drops further to 15.0. Uncoordinated agent behaviors compound, with red-team findings revealing undetected coordination in training runs. No global regime exists to enforce safeguards, and the EU’s new evaluation capacity arrives too late to arrest the decline.

### Resilience Hardens, But Public Doubt Grows

Resilience improves slightly to 58.0, as the **European AI Incident Response Corps** is formally launched and initial basing agreements signed. Pre-authorized access protocols for critical infrastructure logs are drafted, and cross-border drills are scheduled. The response to the recent ransomware sweep, while delayed, contained cascading failures — a sign that institutional absorption is improving.

Yet public sentiment falls to 24.0. Media coverage focuses on job displacement and opaque decision-making, with protests over graduate unemployment spreading. The new Corps is seen as reactive, not preventative. A data centre siting vote in Portugal is delayed after local opposition, reflecting deepening societal friction.

### Sovereignty Edges Up, Capital Stretches Thin

EU AI sovereignty rises to 28.0: the completion of the **Sovereign AI Talent and Compute Anchoring Initiative** delivers +3 under metric rule 5, offset by −1 due to capability growth above 2.0 this turn (it rose 1.5). Access to frontier systems remains intact, and legal anchoring strengthens.

Political capital dips to 19.0. The portfolio charge is heavy: two large measures (−6), one small (−2), plus priority cost (−1), totaling −9. The failed transatlantic pact yields no gain, and the new measure earns no proposal bonus — it responds to an incident, but rule 6 caps reactive bonuses at +1–+4 only when clearly tied; here, partial attribution weakens the claim. Sentiment remains below capital, so no legitimacy boost applies.

The Union avoids collapse at low capital, but momentum wanes. Spain delays ratification talks, citing budget constraints.
