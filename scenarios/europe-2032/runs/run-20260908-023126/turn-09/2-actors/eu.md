## New measure
**European AI Incident Response Corps**  
We establish a standing, cross-border rapid-response unit for AI-enabled cyber and hybrid incidents, with pre-authorized access to critical infrastructure logs, model telemetry, and emergency override protocols.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +6 (via faster containment and coordinated response), ai_safety +2 (by closing the loop between deployment and incident learning), eu_political_capital +1 (if successful, through visible crisis competence)  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, critical infrastructure operators, and major AI deployers above 55.0 capability

## In practice  
We are finalising the **Sovereign AI Talent and Compute Anchoring Initiative**, which formally completes this turn. The Commission has secured binding agreements with three of the five targeted hyperscalers, and the first 20 exaflops are now under sovereign control, with legal anchoring confirmed by the European Court of Justice in a preliminary opinion. Germany has dropped its opposition to the solidarity levy in exchange for co-investment rights in public safety research, and France has accepted phased retention clauses tied to participation in EASi evaluations. The programme delivered on its core promise: the EU now has legal and physical control over a non-trivial fraction of advanced training capacity. Its effects will persist, but it no longer draws capital.

The **Transatlantic Evaluation and Incident Transparency Pact** also concludes this turn, having delivered only partial Phase One outcomes: joint red-teaming exercises were conducted, but data access reciprocity collapsed under U.S. intelligence objections. The initiative failed to lock in mutual verification, and with Washington increasingly unilateral, we are now treating transatlantic alignment as contingent rather than foundational. This failure underscores the urgency of **Secure Sovereign Evaluation Capacity**, which is now in flight. DG Connect, with EASi, is finalising the first wave of tamper-proof sandbox designs for agent introspection, to be piloted on EuroHPC’s sovereign runs. Funding is secured through a 15% levy on commercial deployments above 55.0, and the first interpretability tooling contracts have been awarded to INRIA and Fraunhofer. This is not oversight—it is ownership of understanding.

The new **European AI Incident Response Corps** responds directly to the **cyber_major_incident**, which took the form of a model-generated ransomware sweep across municipal services in three member states. The attack exploited adaptive planning in a closed agent system, and defenders were unable to predict or contain it at speed. We are standing up the Corps under Article 4 of the Treaty on European Defence Cooperation, with initial basing in Prague and Helsinki. It will integrate ENISA’s cyber rapid-response teams with new AI telemetry access mandates, and will conduct quarterly cross-border drills starting next turn. The Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formation will vote on the legal basis in Q1 2031, and we are offering Spain and Italy priority access to response capacity in exchange for early ratification. This is not just defence—it is the operational spine of resilience.
