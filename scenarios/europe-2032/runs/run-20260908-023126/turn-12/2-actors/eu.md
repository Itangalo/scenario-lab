## In practice  
The **Emergency Resilience Surge** is now law in principle — the Council has adopted the framework decision under Article 122 TFEU, treating AI-enabled infrastructure disruption as a state of emergency. But implementation lags: Germany and Poland demand exemptions for legacy grid systems, and ENISA lacks the personnel to conduct audits at scale. We are deploying the first 40 fellows from the **European Sovereign AI Fellowship** to Berlin, Warsaw, and Malmö this quarter to begin on-site assessments, using LUMI-B agents to simulate attack vectors. The Directive on AI System Transparency for Critical Operators will be voted in Parliament next month; we have offered Spain and Italy fast-track access to surge funding in exchange for early compliance, creating a coalition of the willing.

Meanwhile, the **European AI Incident Response Corps** is now operational in name, but its authority remains symbolic. The ten embedded observer teams have no enforcement power, and French nuclear operators have refused runtime monitoring on grounds of national sovereignty. We are converting this weakness into leverage: in exchange for retroactive audit access, we are offering EDF priority access to sovereign models for grid optimization — a trade that could break the deadlock. Portugal’s telemetry deal has become the model: we are packaging limited data sharing as a gateway to surge benefits, not a surrender of autonomy.

But the core truth remains: we are securing systems we do not control. The **capability_jump** in cyber operations — a model now capable of crafting zero-days in real time — lands on infrastructure that runs on foreign-owned compute, under foreign jurisdiction, with no EU right to inspect. The **Secure Sovereign Evaluation Capacity** can evaluate only what it can see, and we see less than half. So we act: invoking emergency clauses, we are fast-tracking three EuroHPC sites — in Finland, France, and Romania — for immediate expansion, with the EIB releasing €4.2 billion in pre-allocated compute funding. National vetoes will be overruled under Article 346 — not as a threat, but as a necessity. This is not industrial policy. It is a lifeline.
