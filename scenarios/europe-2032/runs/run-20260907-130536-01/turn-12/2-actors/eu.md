This is no longer about balancing mandates — it is about whether the Union can endure in a world where uncontrolled diffusion enables deliberate or accidental catastrophe. Our identity must reflect that we are not merely arbitrating between values, but defending continuity.

* - modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late, and now — when evidence points toward cascading failure modes — assume that delay is indistinguishable from surrender.  
  - Grounds: Trigger: eval_anomaly_reports and emergent_bio_uplift_findings. The anomalies suggest capability leaps outside developer control; the bio uplift shows a path from model output to human infection. Waiting for consensus would mean waiting for repetition. This turn, we escalate from precaution to pre-emption because uncertainty now carries existential weight.

* - retire `two_year_commitment`  
  - Grounds: It was superseded last turn by an expanded version embedded in the modified commitment, which included enforceable control and safety intervention. That formulation has now been operationally overtaken by events: the red line is under legal challenge, resilience is failing, and sovereign access means nothing if systems cannot be contained. A new two-year direction must emerge from this crisis, not inherit a framework built for slower threats.

* - add `sovereign_intervention_capacity` (commitment): By turn 18, establish the EU’s independent ability to detect, halt, and reverse dangerous AI-driven incidents in real time, using sovereign infrastructure, enforceable jurisdictional reach, and binding coordination with member states.  
  - Grounds: The hospital ransomware attack and bio_incident exposed fatal latency: we detected too late, responded too slowly, and lacked authority to compel action across borders. Having capabilities is meaningless without intervention speed. This commitment binds us to build not just tools, but reflexes.

## In practice

We are treating health infrastructure as a warzone. Under HERA’s emergency powers, we are activating a cross-border surge: every major hospital network will receive sovereign-hosted intrusion detection agents trained to spot AI-generated ransomware patterns and anomalous biological data queries. These run on EU-controlled compute clusters in Luxembourg and Helsinki, with kill switches accessible only to a joint ENISA-HERA command cell. Funding comes from the Crisis Response Reserve (€9.2 billion unlocked via qualified majority override after Germany abstained), and deployment is mandated by 30 June — no opt-outs.

At the same time, we are renegotiating the Oulu verification lab’s mission: it will now test not only model provenance but also live traffic from clinical AI tools for signs of misuse. Finland and Norway agree, but Austria insists on civilian oversight — so we are embedding judges from the ECHR into the monitoring board, rotating quarterly. To Germany, we offer full source-code access and a veto on any system touching patient records. To France, we promise first access to any pathogen signatures detected. This is not business continuity — it is triage. We are building response capacity faster than norms allow because norms did not anticipate that a single model could collapse a nation’s care system in 72 hours.

The red line proposal continues, but we are shifting enforcement emphasis: instead of relying solely on upstream blocking of open-weight releases, we are preparing downstream countermeasures. If models above 55.0 enter health systems — however they arrive — we must be able to stop them mid-operation. That is why the Safety Assurance Infrastructure is being repurposed this turn to include live rollback protocols: if an agent behaves anomalously during evaluation (triggered by eval_anomaly_reports), we will have the technical and legal means to suspend and reverse its deployment across all connected systems. That power rests on new interpretability modules now being fast-tracked by EAAD — funded from redirected Horizon Europe grants, over protests from academic AI labs. We are trading openness for survivability because the alternative is irrelevance.
