Though no immediate cutoff occurs, the shift tightens leverage over EU compute procurement and raises the cost of sovereignty-building measures.

Meanwhile, the Middle Power AI Coalition (MPAC) concludes its formation, establishing coordination channels for export controls and joint evaluation. Japan and South Korea commit to aligning licensing frameworks, and Norway integrates Arctic monitoring nodes. However, Canada holds back from binding commitments, and the pact remains operational in intent only.

With the Sovereign AI Core now live, the EU launches its new Safety Assurance Infrastructure, aiming to transform passive logging into active intervention. Yet pushback flares: Germany and Austria challenge the scope of central intervention under Article 122, delaying deployment of audit triggers. While red-teaming support arrives from Finland and Norway, operational authority remains contested.
