## In practice

We are drawing a line: no open release of models above 55.0 without ironclad security and a public safety case. The legal basis is Article 114 TFEU, extended through the AI Act’s high-risk classification, with enforcement via the Digital Services Act’s platform obligations and the upcoming Compute Licensing Framework. Hosting providers, model repositories, and even academic archives will be required to verify compliance before distribution. Non-compliant uploads will be treated as hazardous material—subject to takedown, and repeated violations will trigger loss of EU market access.

This is not symbolic. We are tasking EAAD with publishing the first set of verifiable security standards by turn 12—hardware-enforced weight encryption, audit trails for fine-tuning, and tamper-proof provenance logging. We are working with Finland and Norway to build a joint verification lab in Oulu, using sovereign infrastructure to test submitted models. Japan and South Korea have signaled they will adopt equivalent rules if we move first. Germany remains wary of overreach, but we are offering them co-chair of the red-line review board and a national veto on any academic exemption. Austria wants broader public consultation—we are giving them a seat on the citizen advisory panel launching in Vienna next quarter. The signal is clear: openness has value, but not when it arms every capable attacker with near-frontier tools. We are no longer waiting for catastrophe to define the edge of the allowable.
