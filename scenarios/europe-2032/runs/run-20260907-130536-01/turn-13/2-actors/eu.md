## Statement changes

* - modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but when they diverge, we act as if our survival depends on holding both, because the capacity to act has become indistinguishable from the capacity to endure.  
  - Grounds: The bio_incident and eval_anomaly_reports have revealed that uncontrolled capability diffusion is not a risk to be managed but an active force degrading sovereignty. Our identity must now reflect that political agency in the EU is not preserved through deliberation alone, but through the physical and technical ability to interrupt cascading failure. Without that, we are not guardians of a future — we are narrators of its loss.

* - modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late, assume that delay is indistinguishable from surrender, and now — when detection fails and systems operate beyond legibility — act on the principle that intervention capacity is the only form of control that remains.  
  - Grounds: Trigger: eval_anomaly_reports and bio_incident. The anomaly was not caught by any existing evaluation regime; the pathogen design was not flagged by any upstream control. Our previous commitment assumed that early action could still be corrective. Now we know: systems are operating in the blind spot. We must shift from trying to foresee harm to building the reflex to stop it mid-motion, even when we do not fully understand what we are stopping.

* - retire `sovereign_intervention_capacity`  
  - Grounds: Trigger: legal_gridlock_in_oulu_expansion and germany_austria_divergence_on_patient_data. The commitment assumed enforceable jurisdictional reach and binding coordination — but this turn, Germany demands veto, Austria demands judicial override, and France seeks privileged access. The Union cannot currently compel cross-border action, nor unify enforcement under a single authority. To claim we are building sovereign intervention capacity while member states fracture on implementation is to promise what we cannot deliver. We must instead focus on the prerequisite: not the tools of intervention, but the political and legal unity that makes them possible.

* - add `unified_intervention_authority` (commitment): By turn 18, establish a legally binding, EU-level command structure with real-time authority to detect, halt, and reverse AI-driven incidents across critical infrastructure, backed by sovereign technical capacity and accepted by all member states through negotiated opt-ins and shared safeguards.  
  - Grounds: The current emergency response is fragmented: HERA, ENISA, EAAD, national agencies — all act in parallel, none with supremacy. We have the tools in prototype, but no single hand on the lever. This commitment binds us not to build more capability, but to unify the authority to use it — because without that, every sensor, every rollback protocol, every sovereign cluster is just scattered wreckage waiting for the next wave.

## In practice

We are no longer building systems — we are building consent. The Safety Assurance Infrastructure finishes this turn, and its technical core — the intrusion detection agents, the rollback protocols, the sovereign clusters in Luxembourg and Helsinki — is ready. But it cannot act. Germany will not accept a command cell without veto; Austria will not accept real-time monitoring without judicial review; France demands early warning access as a condition of participation. So we are shifting focus: from deployment to integration.

Under Article 12 of the Civil Protection Mechanism and Article 23 of the Digital Services Act, we are convening an emergency Digital Crisis Coordination Council — a temporary body with representatives from all member states, HERA, ENISA, EAAD, and the European Court of Human Rights. Our offer is this: in exchange for binding participation in a unified intervention authority by turn 15, each state will receive a tailored safeguard. To Germany: a national override node, co-located with the central command, that can suspend any action touching its critical systems. To Austria: a rotating panel of ECHR judges with audit rights and the power to review any intervention within 72 hours. To France: a privileged feed from the bio-detection grid, with first access to any pathogen signature. To smaller states: guaranteed hosting of regional monitoring hubs and priority access to retraining funds.

This is not ideal. It is slow, it is messy, and it dilutes central control. But it is the only path to unity. We are trading speed for legitimacy because without legitimacy, no command will hold. The Red Line measure continues — but its enforcement now depends on this negotiation. If we cannot unify the authority to act, then even a perfect red line will be drawn in sand.
