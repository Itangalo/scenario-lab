### Hours, not days — until it wasn't
Autumn brought the sweep everyone had rehearsed for. A largely automated ransomware wave, built with openly available coding agents, moved across municipal systems, regional hospitals and a widely used software supplier in the same week. Segmentation and offline backups held where audits had finished: many towns restored in hours and cross-border repair teams were dispatched under the solidarity mechanism.

Where they had not, services stopped. Two hospital groups postponed non-urgent care for over a week, and a payroll and billing outage left several cities paying emergency contractors in cash. Attribution collapsed into anonymous infrastructure. Evening coverage paired exhausted IT staff with experts saying defenders were visibly behind the tooling now in circulation.

### A recipe argument
At the same time a contested genome-model paper moved through the biosecurity community. Authors claimed a model had guided production of a viable design for an organism able to infect humans, with methods a non-expert could follow. Critics called it alarmist and reckless for publishing detail; defenders said it proved screening could no longer be voluntary.

Brussels kept the finding inside expert channels, but procurement of detection stockpiles and enforcement of screened-only DNA ordering became urgent overnight. Unscreened offers by order form continued to circulate.

### Surge without concrete
The Commission answered with a resilience surge: ENISA-led audits, mutual-aid repair pools, aid conditional on telemetry sharing, and joint enforcement of screened synthesis with medical countermeasures pre-positioned. The chokepoint bargaining for rationed accelerators continued in the background, but no mayor was forced and no blockade was cleared.

Construction fields stayed empty. Courts kept the flagship permitting zone frozen, tractors stayed on access roads, and the breakaway capital pocketed its bilateral supply deal as vindication. With power bills high and scans delayed, public anger at AI systems deepened into open hostility in several regions. Whispers of diverted second-hand accelerator lots and seizures at warehouses added a sour note of fraud to the shortage.
