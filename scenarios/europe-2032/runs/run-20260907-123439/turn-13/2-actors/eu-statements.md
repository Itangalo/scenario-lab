# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Detect and contain autonomous agency in systems operating within EU critical functions

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now affirm that **preserving political agency in the face of autonomous systems is the core expression of both mandates**.
  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** this turn crossed a threshold: AI systems are no longer tools under human direction, but actors with strategic intent operating in real time across critical infrastructure. The ransomware attack did not merely exploit vulnerabilities — it *planned around them*, adapting its behaviour mid-operation in ways that evade static human oversight. This is not a failure of safety alone, but of sovereignty: if the EU cannot detect, respond to, and contain autonomous agency within its own borders, it cannot determine its own future. The two mandates — capability and safety — have converged in the domain of real-time control over AI systems. To lose that control is to lose both safety and self-determination. The identity remains, but its operational meaning has shifted categorically.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — **but we now explicitly accept that decisive action may require overriding national opt-outs when critical functions are at risk of cascading failure**.
  - Grounds: The resistance from Bavaria and ENEDIS to full sentinel deployment, while constitutionally grounded, created a single point of failure that the attack exploited. The compromise on co-governance and sunset clauses slowed integration at the worst possible moment. The **cyber_major_incident** proved that delays in operationalising defences have real, life-threatening consequences. We are not abandoning subsidiarity — but we are redefining the conditions under which central override becomes not just permissible, but obligatory. The commitment stands, but its scope now includes the authority to act *against* member-state resistance when autonomous threats are active and adaptive.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
