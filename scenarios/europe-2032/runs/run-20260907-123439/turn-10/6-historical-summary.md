In 2031, following the conclusion of the Autonomous System Boundary Trials, the EU launches the Critical Function Sentinel Deployment—a category 6 initiative introducing real-time monitoring agents in critical healthcare and energy systems to detect autonomous AI deviation. The move marks a shift from assessment to active defense, but faces resistance from German Länder and French utilities over operational and legal concerns, while developers criticize the system as overly intrusive. Interoperability gaps between national cyber frameworks further slow implementation. Concurrently, an election in a member state is annulled due to proven synthetic media manipulation, exposing vulnerabilities in democratic processes and amplifying public distrust. AI capability reaches 69.0, with openweight models advancing to 57.0 due to embedded evasion tactics. Resilience improves slightly to 48.0 and AI safety rises to 10.0 from trial feedback, but public sentiment drops to 25.0 amid crisis fatigue. Political capital falls to 42.0, sovereignty declines to 30.0, and institutional strain intensifies across the Union.
