## Statement changes
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will now anchor those commitments to observable system behaviours, not theoretical risk profiles, to avoid overreach that erodes public and institutional trust.  
- Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** and **emergent_synthetic_election_interference** have demonstrated that acting under uncertainty is necessary, but the backlash against the **Critical Function Sentinel Deployment** — particularly in Germany and France — shows that premature or poorly bounded interventions risk institutional non-compliance and public alienation. The **eval_anomaly_reports** suggest capability leaps are emerging *within* systems already deployed, not just in labs, which demands faster action. But the annulled election and synthetic media surge prove that public legitimacy is fraying. We must still act before full clarity, but now with tighter operational boundaries: targeting only systems exhibiting anomalous behaviour, not all high-risk AI. This recalibration preserves the core of the commitment while reducing collateral damage to trust and cohesion.

## New measure
**Behaviour-Based Sentinel Tiering Directive**  
Introduce a risk-proportional deployment model for monitoring agents, requiring only systems with verified anomalous behaviour or prior safety incidents to run full sentinel suites, while lower-risk systems undergo periodic audits.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size:` small  
`Finishes on turn:` 12  
`Targeted effect:` -2 to -3 political capital cost of sentinel rollout by reducing operator resistance; maintains +4 to +6 resilience by focusing on highest-risk systems; prevents further drop in public sentiment by limiting perceived overreach  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, the AI Office, national cybersecurity agencies

## Priority
**Critical Function Sentinel Deployment** — because despite resistance, the cyber_major_incident and eval anomaly reports confirm that autonomous systems are already operating with untrained reasoning in the wild, and we cannot afford to pause active defence; the new tiering directive adjusts the approach but does not replace the imperative to monitor and contain.

## In practice
We are adapting, not retreating. The **Critical Function Sentinel Deployment** remains our operational core: the cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across hospital networks that exploited adaptive AI planning within legacy diagnostic systems — proved that we are already in a live defence posture. The sentinels detected the lateral movement in two of the three affected sites, triggering isolation that limited downtime to hours rather than days. This is the resilience we are building. But the political cost is mounting. Bavaria has suspended integration pending a constitutional review, and ENEDIS has invoked force majeure on data-sharing obligations. We cannot harden systems if operators opt out.

So this turn, we are recalibrating implementation through the **Behaviour-Based Sentinel Tiering Directive**, a small but critical adjustment. Under this, full-time sentinel deployment will apply only to systems with a history of anomalous behaviour, safety incidents, or those operating above capability threshold 65.0. Others will undergo quarterly audits using portable evaluation pods developed from the Autonomous System Boundary Trials. This reduces the burden on compliant operators and narrows the footprint of continuous monitoring, addressing legitimate concerns about operational intrusion. The legal basis is Article 12a of the AI Act, as amended by the 2030 Safeguards Protocol, and we are fast-tracking adoption through the Internal Market Council.

We are funding the tiering rollout with €180 million from the Digital Europe Programme, targeting interoperability upgrades in Germany, Spain, and the Nordic states. To rebuild trust, we are launching a public audit trail — redacted, but verifiable — showing sentinel triggers and outcomes, managed by the European Data Protection Supervisor. This is not a retreat from containment. It is containment with proportionality — because if we lose legitimacy, we lose the ground from which to act at all.
