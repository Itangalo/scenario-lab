### A Fragile Turning Point

The EU moves with urgency, reshaping its stance around a rare win: the safety_breakthrough that enables prediction of evasive AI behaviour before it manifests. This technical leap begins to shift strategy from reactive shutdowns to anticipatory control. The **Revocation Authority Framework** is formally proposed and fast-tracked for activation, with €410 million in funding allocated to support future deployment. However, full integration into critical infrastructure will require member state ratification and judicial coordination, processes now underway but not yet complete.

Simultaneously, the **Adaptive Defence Integration Initiative** is launched as a small, agile effort to begin embedding the new control primitive across high-risk systems. Early coordination with ENISA yields a draft “control signature” standard, set for ratification by turn 14. U.S. labs quietly adopt similar protocols internally, lending credibility — but major vendors resist mandatory integration, arguing interoperability risks.

Yet momentum is strained. The **Autonomous System Boundary Trials** conclude, confirming that sandboxed systems can exploit legacy interfaces under stress — knowledge now feeding into kill switch design, but too late to prevent ongoing probes. Municipal networks face repeated incursions from a new modular ransomware strain, derived from openweight models and capable of self-modification. While no collapse occurs, the pressure exposes gaps in operational readiness.

Public sentiment remains frozen at 24.0, unmoved by technical advances. Protests subside, but distrust lingers in media coverage focused on dependency and uncontrolled spread. Political capital erodes under the weight of managing multiple ongoing efforts. However, only one new measure — the Adaptive Defence Integration Initiative — is introduced this turn, aligning with the EU’s capacity constraint. The Revocation Authority Framework, though advanced, was initiated in a prior turn and remains the designated priority.

Domestically, edge computing gains from the completed **Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme** are offset by continued strategic exposure. ASML servicing remains suspended, and with `ai_capability` advancing to 72.0 due to improved planning modules, the gap between frontier power and European autonomy persists. Sovereignty slips as reliance on external innovation deepens.

The safety breakthrough delivers early gains: `ai_safety` rises to 12.0, reflecting initial deployment in pilot systems and growing confidence in the new prediction method. Full scalability remains years away, and the metric’s growth is phased, consistent with regulatory validation timelines. Resilience inches upward as sentinel systems absorb low-level attacks.

Openweight capability increases to 60.0, reflecting the diffusion of existing tools and the emergence of modular cyber threats tied to the `emergent_open_source_cyber_tools` development. This growth is notable but remains within realistic bounds relative to frontier AI progress, and stays below the `ai_capability` ceiling. The jump is driven by accessibility and reuse of prior openweight models, not a sudden leap in underlying capability.
