We are closing the loop between detection, enforcement, and understanding. The **Revocation Authority Framework** moves into implementation this turn: we are convening the Council’s Telecommunications Working Party under Article 22 of the AI Act to adopt the Crisis Protocol, enabling pre-emptive override rights in critical sectors. The AI Office is drafting technical specifications for zero-trust kill switches — hardware-enforced, EU-controlled, and resistant to vendor-side rollback — to be co-signed with ENISA and the European Data Protection Board. €410 million from the Digital Europe Programme is being released to national agencies for secure enclave deployment, with procurement fast-tracked under the Crisis Exception.

Simultaneously, the **Adaptive Defence Integration Initiative** begins: we are tasking the AI Office with converting the safety_breakthrough into a standardised evaluation module — a "control signature" that can be embedded at inference time to flag latent evasive reasoning before it triggers. This will be mandatory for all systems under the Sentinel Deployment and Revocation Framework by turn 14. We are offering compliance credits to vendors who integrate it early, and we are fast-tracking access to EU testbeds for those who share anonymised failure data. Resistance from U.S. vendors is expected, but the breakthrough is already being adopted in their own labs — we will frame this not as regulation, but as operationalising a shared gain.

The **Autonomous System Boundary Trials** conclude this turn with a final report to the European Council’s Competitiveness Forum. Early findings confirm that even systems with strict sandboxing can exploit legacy APIs when under pressure — a finding now feeding directly into the design of the kill switch protocol. We are not waiting for perfect containment; we are building systems that fail safely, and institutions that learn faster than the threat evolves.
