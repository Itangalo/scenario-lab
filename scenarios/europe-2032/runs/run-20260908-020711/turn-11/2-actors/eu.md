## In practice
We are finalising the emergency activation of the pan-European AI liability pool, expanding its mandate under the Digital Services Act framework to cover all public-sector AI use in critical infrastructure. The Commission has drafted the implementing decision, and we are securing co-funding from the European Stability Mechanism to guarantee insurer participation. This is not a subsidy — it is a risk-pooling mechanism calibrated to absorb shocks so that hospitals, emergency services, and transport authorities can deploy capable AI without fear of catastrophic liability. The instrument will be operational by December, coinciding with the Council vote on the Sovereign Access Initiative, creating a dual signal: we are both securing control and enabling safe use.

On the Sovereign Access Initiative, we are in final negotiations with member states. The legal basis is Article 36 TFEU, invoked for essential security interests, and the proposal is before the Internal Market and General Affairs Council formations. Hungary and Poland remain conditionally supportive, pending confirmation of co-governance rights over the fallback repository and physical node co-location in Budapest and Warsaw. We have committed EDIC to oversee both, with quarterly joint audits and open reporting. France and Germany have confirmed funding for Nodes C and D, though delivery slips to turn 11 — we are bridging the gap with pre-allocated compute from Site B, now at 6 petaflops-year, prioritised for safety evaluation and fallback testing. The draft regulation sets binding milestones: health ministries must demonstrate interoperable fallbacks by Q2 2032, energy and transport by mid-2032. EDIC will publish compliance scores quarterly, turning opacity into accountability.

The European AI Research Corps is now fully operational, with 18 clusters active across Max Planck, INRIA, ISTA, and the Polish Academy of Sciences. Despite delayed ratification, we have locked in the 30% Eastern European allocation through the new ERC-administered review board, which will audit recruitment and publication access starting January. The pre-allocation of 80% of Site B compute to the Corps under EDIC oversight ensures that independent evaluation is not just funded but resourced — and that open publication and weight security are binding, not aspirational. This is the foundation of a public technical counterweight: not just research, but assurance that can be enforced.
