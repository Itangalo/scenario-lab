``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign access to frontier AI systems through enforceable technical and legal pathways, ensuring no critical public function depends on externally controlled models``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event proved that strategic autonomy cannot rest on compute and talent alone — it collapses the moment access is revoked, regardless of compliance or cooperation. The previous commitment focused on preventing dangerous open-weight diffusion and reversing talent flight, both still relevant but now secondary to the binding constraint: enforceable, real-time access to frontier capabilities for essential state functions. This turn, we learned that even legally grounded cooperation with foreign labs offers no guarantee — sovereignty must be operational, not aspirational. The new commitment shifts from containment and investment to structural independence, prioritising interoperability, fallback enforcement, and sovereign deployment as non-negotiable conditions for public AI use.

## In practice
We are finalising the European AI Research Corps, with the first clusters now operational at Max Planck and INRIA. Compute allocation remains tense — only 6 petaflops-year have been distributed so far, below the 10 promised — but we are resolving bottlenecks by pre-allocating capacity from Gigafactory Site B in Finland, which came online in May. France and Germany have confirmed co-funding for three additional nodes, and we are establishing the monitoring mechanism for the 30% Eastern European allocation under the ERC’s oversight. All grants will require open publication of methods and compliance with EU weight-security standards, closing the door on hidden forks.

Simultaneously, we are moving fast on the Sovereign Access Initiative. The access cutoff during the July health IT crisis was not just a disruption — it was a regime change. We can no longer assume that legal compliance or strategic alignment guarantees access. Under Article 36 TFEU, we are drafting a regulation that will require all certified public AI systems to support model portability, maintain interoperable fallbacks on EU-controlled infrastructure, and document deployment pathways that do not rely on foreign-controlled APIs or authentication. The legal proposal will go to the Internal Market and Industry Council in September, with a targeted adoption by Q1 2031. Funding will come from repurposed InvestAI tranches — €45bn redirected from delayed Gigafactory sites — and implementation will be led by the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC), with binding targets for health, energy, and transport ministries. Hungary and Poland remain sceptical, but we are offering joint governance of the fallback repository and co-location of secondary nodes in Budapest and Warsaw to secure their buy-in. This is not about building a rival GPT — it’s about ensuring that when the next cutoff comes, the lights stay on.
