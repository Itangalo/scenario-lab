### Sovereign Access, Fragile Foundations

The EU launched the Sovereign Access Initiative, a bold legal framework to ensure public AI systems can switch models and infrastructure without service disruption. Invoking Article 36 TFEU, the Commission drafted binding rules for model portability, fallbacks, and EU-controlled deployment pathways. Yet resistance flared: Hungary and Poland demanded not just co-location of nodes in Budapest and Warsaw, but veto power over access decisions, calling the plan a “digital fortress with foreign locks.” Talks stalled in September, delaying the Council vote.

Meanwhile, Gigafactory Site B in Finland went live, delivering 6 petaflops-year of compute—below the 10 promised. Pre-allocating capacity eased bottlenecks for the European AI Research Corps, but France and Germany’s co-funding pledges remain unratified. The EDIC will lead implementation, yet its authority is untested, and member states question whether fallback systems can match frontier performance.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Slips

Global AI capability advanced again, reaching 61.5, as labs integrated AI into training loops. No safety evaluations covered the latest systems, and the open-weight leak from a non-EU source—now widely deployed—lacked transparency tools. With no new safeguards in place, ai_safety dropped to 35.0. Resilience weakened further as coordination gaps persisted; during a simulated outage, several health IT systems failed to activate even basic redundancies.

Public sentiment fell to 30.0, dragged down by media coverage of the health IT crisis and growing job displacement in legal and administrative sectors. The narrative of “dependency humiliation” hardened, especially in Eastern Europe, where a disinformation campaign amplified distrust in EU research equity.

Political capital dipped to 37.0 under strain from overlapping initiatives. The Sovereign Access Initiative earned a proposal bonus, but ongoing costs outweighed gains.
