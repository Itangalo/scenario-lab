## New measure
### Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure
A binding directive requiring all critical infrastructure operators in the EU to implement AI-resistant intrusion detection and response protocols within 18 months.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +15–20, ai_safety +5 via reduced attack surface; signals EU can enforce minimum baselines despite member-state defection

## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure** — because the cyber_test_shot proves existing defences are blind to AI-driven intrusions, and member_state_defection shows cohesion frays when threats arrive; this turn demands visible, enforceable unity.

## In practice
We are launching the Cyber Shield directive under Article 114 TFEU, using the internal market legal base to override fragmented national standards. The ENISA mandate will be expanded to certify detection systems resistant to AI-generated obfuscation patterns, drawing on classified findings from the autumn intrusion. National CIRTs will be required to conduct biannual red-team exercises using AI agents trained to simulate frontier adversarial behaviour — these simulations will be coordinated by the Joint Research Centre and fed into an EU-wide early-warning network.

Resistance is expected from smaller member states citing cost and from energy-sector incumbents arguing compliance will delay grid modernisation. We are countering with a dedicated €12bn disbursement from the Modernisation Fund, tied to milestone-based adoption, and offering fast-tracked access to InvestAI compute for participating operators to run defensive AI workloads. The Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formation will vote in December; we have secured informal backing from seven core members who see this as the first real test of whether the Union can harden itself while holding together.
