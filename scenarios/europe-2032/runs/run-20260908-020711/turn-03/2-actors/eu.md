## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure** — because enforcement of minimum resilience standards across member states is now the only lever that keeps the Union collectively safer in the face of bilateral backsliding, rising openweight capability, and collapsing external investment.

## In practice
We are pressing the Cyber Shield directive through the Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formation, where ratification remains fragile. The €12bn Modernisation Fund disbursement is being structured as milestone-based grants tied to ENISA certification, with early payouts unlocked for red-team participation and defensive AI integration. We are deploying the Joint Research Centre to coordinate biannual AI-driven penetration tests across energy, water, and rail operators, using agentised simulations trained on adversarial patterns from the autumn intrusion. National CIRTs are being required to report detection latency and evasion rates into a centralised dashboard managed by ENISA, which will trigger enforcement actions if baselines are not met by mid-2028.

At the same time, we are advancing preparatory work on sovereign model access: drafting a new Article under the AI Act that would condition market access for foreign developers whose models exceed 50.0 capability on establishing a sovereign deployment enclave within the EU. These enclaves must allow EU-certified entities to run full inference workloads offline, with secured weights, audit logs, and interpretability tooling. Legal services are assessing compatibility with trade law, and early dialogues have begun with two U.S. labs and one French startup meeting the threshold. This is not framed as retaliation but as reciprocal assurance — they get market access, we get agency. However, no formal proposal will be launched this turn, given the current strain on political capital and the risk of provoking unilateral reactions during the U.S. campaign cycle.

The Tech sovereignty package continues its slow grind through national permitting offices. We are pushing member states to designate at least one accelerated-permitting zone per country by June, backed by fast-tracked grid connections and tax incentives for firms committing to local hiring. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, though land acquisition in two locations has been delayed by environmental reviews. Senior project managers have been assigned to unblock them, but no additional funding or redirection is warranted yet. All three measures are running at full political cost, and no new burden beyond the current portfolio can be sustained.
