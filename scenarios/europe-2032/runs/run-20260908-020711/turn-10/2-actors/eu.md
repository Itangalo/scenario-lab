## New measure
**Propose emergency activation of the emergent AI liability pool and expand its mandate to cover all public-sector AI use in critical infrastructure**

This measure institutionalises a rapidly emerging trust mechanism, enabling broader deployment of AI in public services while reducing systemic risk.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` Stabilises and potentially increases `public_sentiment` by reducing perceived risk in public AI use; supports `resilience` by enabling faster, safer adoption of capable systems in healthcare and emergency response; indirectly supports `eu_political_capital` by demonstrating crisis responsiveness.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member state ministries in health, social services, and emergency management, and all public operators of critical infrastructure.

## Priority
**Launch the Sovereign Access Initiative** — because the cyber_major_incident and the ongoing access fragility in public systems prove that sovereignty without enforceable fallbacks is not sovereignty at all, and because this turn’s events have made clear that even legally compliant dependencies can collapse under geopolitical or commercial pressure.

## In practice
We are finalising the European AI Research Corps as planned, with the first 18 clusters now operational across Max Planck, INRIA, ISTA, and the Polish Academy of Sciences. Compute allocation remains tight—only 6 petaflops-year delivered from Gigafactory Site B—but we have pre-allocated 80% of it to the Corps under EDIC oversight, with binding commitments for open publication and weight security. France and Germany have confirmed co-funding for Nodes C and D, though ratification delays mean full delivery slips to turn 11. We are enforcing the 30% Eastern European allocation through a new ERC-administered review board, which will audit recruitment and publication access quarterly.

Simultaneously, we are pushing hard on the Sovereign Access Initiative. The cyber_major_incident—ransomware sweeping municipal health records in three member states, powered by AI-generated exploits—has shattered any illusion that we can rely on external model providers during crises. The attack exploited a dependency chain compromised months earlier, and no public system had a fallback that could activate in time. We are now accelerating the legal proposal under Article 36 TFEU, targeting a Council vote in December. The draft regulation mandates model portability, EU-controlled deployment pathways, and interoperable fallbacks for all certified public AI systems. EDIC will enforce compliance, with binding milestones for health, energy, and transport ministries by mid-2032. To break the deadlock with Hungary and Poland, we are offering joint governance of the fallback repository and co-location of secondary nodes in Budapest and Warsaw—concessions, but necessary to ensure unity when the next crisis hits.

We are also acting on the emergent AI liability pool, which gained unexpected momentum after the health IT failures. By expanding it to cover all critical public-sector AI use, we reduce adoption risk and signal that the EU can innovate institutionally even under pressure. The Commission will propose the expansion under the Digital Services Act framework, with co-funding from the European Stability Mechanism to ensure insurer participation. This is not a substitute for sovereignty—it is a complement, making safe deployment possible where we *do* have control.
