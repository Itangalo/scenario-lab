## In practice  
We are finalising the legal and technical architecture for the European AI Research Corps. The Commission has circulated a draft decision under Article 185 TFEU, and we are fast-tracking the first call for proposals to launch in Q1 2030. The Max Planck Society and INRIA have agreed to host the first clusters, with the Netherlands and Poland co-funding regional nodes. We are offering 10 petaflops-year of guaranteed compute from the Gigafactories’ first wave, tied to five-year retention clauses. France and Germany support the structure; Hungary remains wary of equity, so we are reinforcing the 30% reservation with a joint monitoring mechanism under the European Research Council. The first disbursements will be conditional on public publication and compliance with EU weight-security standards — no closed forks.

Simultaneously, we are responding to the access crisis. The sudden cutoff of the leading model from EU hospitals and agencies — without explanation or recourse — has triggered emergency coordination under the Crisis Cell. We are now drafting the *Sovereign Access Initiative*, a new legal instrument that mandates fallback capabilities for all critical public AI dependencies. This will require certified operators to maintain model portability, ensure interoperable interfaces, and — where necessary — deploy EU-sovereign alternatives on Gigafactory infrastructure. The legal base will be Article 36 TFEU (security of essential services), and we are preparing a proposal for the Internal Market and Industry Council in March. The initiative will be funded from the €200bn InvestAI envelope, repurposing unused tranches from delayed Gigafactory sites. We are not building a new model from scratch — that would be reckless — but we are ensuring that no public function is ever again held hostage by a single provider’s access policy. This is not protectionism; it is insurance.
