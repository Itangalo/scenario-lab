## New measure
**Propose a new measure to strengthen sovereign evaluation and control over high-capability models deployed in the EU**

Establish an independent EU authority with legal power to audit, evaluate, and mandate modifications to any AI system above 50.0 capability operating in the Union, including foreign-owned models.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases `ai_safety` by enforcing transparency and accountability on deployed systems; indirectly supports `eu_ai_sovereignty` by asserting jurisdictional control over frontier models in use.  
`Applies to:` Frontier developers directly, within EU jurisdiction

## In practice
We are finalising the enforcement arc of the Cyber Shield directive. The holdout member state remains resistant to mandatory designation of critical operators, but our Article 4(2) TEU legal opinion has cleared internal review, allowing differentiated implementation without formal treaty breach. ENISA has begun baseline assessments at key energy and telecom providers, and the first tranche of the Modernisation Fund—€4.8bn—has been disbursed to the three most affected states for rapid-response upgrades. Full red-teaming will begin in Q3, coordinated through the newly activated Joint Crisis Cell for AI-Enabled Threats.

The Tech sovereignty package concludes this turn with mixed results: only two accelerated-permitting zones have been designated, falling short of the one-per-state goal. We are treating this as a partial failure, and the Vice-President for Values and Transparency will report to the College on systemic bottlenecks in national permitting regimes. No additional funding will be unlocked until a credible path to full rollout is presented by July. Meanwhile, InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track for completion next turn, though environmental remediation delays persist at two sites. Our senior envoys continue daily coordination with regional authorities, and we have ruled out further capital injections until market fragility stabilises.

Behind the scenes, we are drafting the founding regulation for the new Evaluation and Oversight Authority. It will be based in Vienna, co-located with the JRC’s AI Safety Lab, and granted powers under Article 114 TFEU with enhanced executive authority from the Commission. Its first mandate: require all providers of models above 50.0 capability to submit to pre-deployment evaluations and ongoing audits using the recently adopted interpretability framework. We have secured tacit support from France and Germany, and the middle_power_coalition will align evaluation standards with Japan, South Korea, and Canada by turn 7, creating a bloc that can credibly condition market access on compliance. Early dialogues with Meta and Anthropic suggest conditional acceptance—if we offer liability shielding and fast-track approvals for compliant models. We table the proposal in June, ahead of summer recess, to force engagement before the U.S. retrenchment fully isolates us.
