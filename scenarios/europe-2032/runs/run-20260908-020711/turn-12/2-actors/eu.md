## In practice
We are in the final stretch of the Sovereign Access Initiative, with the draft regulation now before COREPER and scheduled for a vote in the Internal Market Council in April. The legal basis under Article 36 TFEU holds, but we are under pressure to dilute enforcement — particularly from France and Germany, who argue that full compliance by mid-2032 is unrealistic. We are holding firm: the health ministry fallback demonstrations are now due 30 June, and EDIC has published the compliance scoring methodology, which will factor into cohesion funding allocations next year. The pre-allocated compute from Site B is being redirected to run interoperability stress tests on the fallback models in Warsaw and Budapest, with results to be published in May. This is not symbolic sovereignty — it is code, hardware, and audit logs.

At the same time, the schism in the AI Ethics Council has become a crisis of legitimacy. We cannot afford for EU oversight to be seen as a rubber stamp. That is why we are launching the Independent AI Safety Oversight Board: it will absorb the technical capacity of the Research Corps’ safety clusters, integrate threat intelligence from the Nordic-Benelux pact, and be empowered to mandate evaluations and block deployments above 55.0 capability unless safety claims are independently verified. The Board will be appointed by the Commission in consultation with the European Parliament, with a third of seats reserved for researchers from the Corps and civil society experts vetted for independence. Its first mandate will be to assess the leaked model now circulating in developer forums — a test of both credibility and speed. This is how we act under uncertainty: not by waiting for proof of harm, but by building institutions that can see it coming.
