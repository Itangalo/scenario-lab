### Fractured Foundations

The EU closed five major initiatives this turn — a technical triumph, but a political one only in fragments. The Sovereign Access Initiative became operational, with fallback systems stress-tested in Budapest and Warsaw, reinforcing `eu_ai_sovereignty`. The European AI Research Corps completed its rollout, anchoring talent and compute across the bloc. However, the momentum stalled: France and Germany delayed enforcement in critical sectors, citing integration burdens, and softened penalties weakened deterrence.

Globally, `ai_capability` rose to 64.0, driven by iterative training gains. With no safety evaluations completed on frontier models, and a leaked agentic system circulating at 52.5, the gap with `ai_safety` widened. The Independent AI Safety Oversight Board was launched, but early appointments faced delays, and public trust in its independence faltered — especially as it prepared to assess the very system that exposed systemic lab failures.

The annulment of a regional election in Lithuania over disinformation concerns triggered a legitimacy crisis. Courts could not prove manipulation altered results, but could not rule it out — and half the electorate rejected the annulment. Simultaneously, physical backlash intensified: data centre occupations in Ireland and sabotage in Sweden forced a re-evaluation of infrastructure security. Policing contained the damage, but local opposition hardened, and `public_sentiment` fell further into anxiety.

`Resilience` dipped as cyber drills yielded gains but were overshadowed by systemic fragility. The completed Cyber Shield directive had no measurable impact this turn, as adoption lagged. `Eu_political_capital` eroded under the weight of five ongoing measures, the priority cost, and the fallout from democratic strain and physical protest — both internal-origin negative events.

### Sovereignty Rebuilt, Trust Unraveling

Despite the completion of multiple category 4 measures, `eu_ai_sovereignty` rose only modestly, buoyed by operational gains but weighed down by persistent access fragility. The US posture remains unconfirmed, but no capacity event occurred this turn. The Board’s formation offered hope, but without swift, visible action, it risked being seen as another layer of bureaucracy — not a shield.
