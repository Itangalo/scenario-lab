The EU completed five major initiatives, including the operationalization of the Sovereign Access Initiative with stress tests in Hungary and Poland, boosting `eu_ai_sovereignty`, and the full rollout of the European AI Research Corps. However, enforcement delays by France and Germany, along with weakened penalties, undermined regulatory effectiveness. The Independent AI Safety Oversight Board was launched but faced credibility issues due to delayed appointments and doubts over its independence, especially as it prepared to assess a leaked frontier model. Global AI capability rose to 64.0, while safety efforts lagged with no new evaluations, widening the safety-capability gap. A regional election annulment in Lithuania over disinformation concerns triggered a legitimacy crisis, and physical attacks on data infrastructure in Ireland and Sweden exposed systemic vulnerabilities. Cyber Shield adoption stalled, resilience dipped, and `eu_political_capital` declined further amid growing public anxiety, democratic strain, and backlash against AI expansion, eroding trust despite technical progress.
