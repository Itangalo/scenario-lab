## In practice
We are now in the final phase of closing the Sovereign Access Initiative, with the regulation formally adopted and compliance timelines locked in. The fallback stress tests in Budapest and Warsaw have run successfully using Site B compute, and EDIC has published the audit framework that will determine cohesion funding adjustments next year. This is no longer about legal text — it is about operational sovereignty embedded in public systems. But the political momentum from that win is fragile. France and Germany have already begun lobbying to delay enforcement in health and energy, citing integration costs, and we are resisting by tying the first tranche of next year’s cohesion disbursements to verified fallback deployment.

At the same time, the backlash is escalating. The annulment of the Lithuanian regional election — officially suspended due to AI-generated disinformation undermining voter confidence — has triggered a crisis of legitimacy. Courts could not prove manipulation changed the outcome, but they could not rule it out — and half the electorate now sees the annulment as illegitimate. Simultaneously, data centre occupations in Ireland and sabotage in Sweden have shifted the cost of infrastructure development from negotiation to confrontation. Policing is holding the line, but at a political cost: local governments are withdrawing support for new sites, and public sentiment is slipping further into anxiety.

That is why we are doubling down on the Independent AI Safety Oversight Board. The Commission has issued the call for nominations, with Parliament co-leading the vetting panel. We are fast-tracking the appointment of the technical cluster leads from the Research Corps to staff its evaluation wing, and we have directed Europol to share threat intelligence from the Nordic-Benelux pact to inform its first assessment — the leaked model now circulating at 52.5 capability. The Board’s first act will be to issue a public risk profile and deployment moratorium recommendation within 90 days. This is not symbolic: it is how we act under uncertainty — by building institutions that can move faster than the crisis, even when the picture is incomplete.
