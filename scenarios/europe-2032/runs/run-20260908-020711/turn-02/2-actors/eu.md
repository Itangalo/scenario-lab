## New measure
### Sovereign Access to Frontier Models
Establish a legal and technical framework enabling EU public authorities and vetted research institutions to run high-capability models under sovereign control, even if developed abroad.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`

## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure** — because it is now clear that cohesion cannot be assumed, and this turn demands we prove the Union can enforce minimum resilience standards across member states despite bilateral backsliding and industrial resistance.

## In practice
We are advancing the Cyber Shield directive through the Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formation, where ratification remains fragile. The €12bn Modernisation Fund disbursement is being structured as milestone-based grants tied to ENISA certification milestones, with early payouts unlocked for red-team participation and defensive AI integration. We are deploying the Joint Research Centre to coordinate biannual AI-driven penetration tests across energy, water, and rail operators, using agentised simulations trained on adversarial patterns from the autumn intrusion. National CIRTs are being required to report detection latency and evasion rates into a centralised dashboard managed by ENISA, which will trigger enforcement actions if baselines are not met by mid-2028.

At the same time, we are laying groundwork for sovereign model access by drafting a new Article under the AI Act — one that would condition market access for any foreign developer whose model exceeds 50.0 capability on establishing a sovereign deployment enclave within the EU. These enclaves must allow EU-certified entities to run full inference workloads offline, with secured weights, audit logs, and interpretability tooling provided. This is not about ownership of training runs, but operational independence: we cannot regulate what we cannot run, and we cannot respond to incidents we cannot reproduce. Early dialogue has begun with two U.S. labs and one French startup meeting the threshold, framed not as retaliation but as reciprocal assurance — they get market access, we get agency. Legal services are assessing compatibility with trade law, and the Commission’s digital cabinet is preparing a communication to frame this as proportionate, not protectionist.

The Tech sovereignty package continues its slow grind through national permitting offices, with progress tracked quarterly. We are pushing member states to designate at least one accelerated-permitting zone per country by June, backed by fast-tracked grid connections and tax incentives for firms committing to local hiring. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, though land acquisition in two locations has been delayed by environmental reviews — we are assigning senior project managers to unblock them, but no additional funding or redirection is warranted yet. All three measures are running at full political cost, and no new burden beyond the current portfolio can be sustained without a shift in public sentiment or a crisis that forces reprioritisation.
