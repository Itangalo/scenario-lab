### Closing One Chapter, Opening Another

The EU concludes three foundational initiatives: the InvestAI Gigafactories, the Tech Sovereignty Package, and Cyber Shield. While not all compute sites are operational, the financial and legal framework is complete, marking a political victory for industrial intervention. Cyber Shield’s transposition is achieved, though real-world testing reveals critical gaps in cross-border coordination during crises.

A new priority emerges: talent. With senior AI researchers leaving EU labs at an accelerating pace, the Commission launches the European AI Research Corps — a €12bn, pan-European effort to anchor frontier research through endowed chairs, guaranteed compute, and strict retention clauses. France and Germany back the plan; Poland and Hungary raise concerns over regional equity, prompting a compromise reserving 30% of positions for Central and Eastern Europe.

### Global Momentum and Measured Gains

Global AI capability advances to 59.5, driven by sustained progress in closed labs. A significant interpretability breakthrough improves auditability of deployed systems, boosting confidence and contributing directly to a rise in ai_safety. This, combined with the completion of Cyber Shield and participation in a new joint cyber defence initiative, strengthens resilience to 47.0.

The open-weight frontier lags, rising only modestly as older models diffuse. Labour markets feel the strain: entry-level roles in law, software, and administration vanish faster than retraining programmes can respond, dragging public sentiment down despite the EU’s ambitious new research push.

### Sovereignty Rebuilt, Capital Spent

The completion of the Gigafactories triggers a +5 boost to eu_ai_sovereignty, offset slightly by ongoing capability growth. The new Research Corps begins absorbing political capital immediately. With the prior priority lifted and a new large measure launched, the portfolio stabilizes — just in time to face deeper questions about cohesion and capacity in the months ahead.
