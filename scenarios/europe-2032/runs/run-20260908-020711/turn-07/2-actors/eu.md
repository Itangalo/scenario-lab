## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent open-weight capability from reaching dangerous thresholds while securing EU access to sovereign AI capacity on resilient infrastructure, and reverse the emergent talent exodus through structural investment in career continuity, compute access, and institutional autonomy for EU-based researchers  
- Grounds: The *emergent_ai_talent_exodus* event reveals a systemic failure in the EU’s ability to retain the human capital underpinning sovereignty. Without reversing this trend, no amount of infrastructure or regulation will close the capability gap. The original commitment assumed that compute and control were the binding constraints; this turn shows that talent is the true bottleneck. The revision binds us to act on the full stack of sovereignty — not just hardware and jurisdiction, but the people who make it function.

## New measure  
**Propose a new measure to reverse the AI talent exodus and anchor frontier research in the EU**  

Establish the European AI Research Corps: a €12bn, pan-EU network of endowed chairs, guaranteed compute allocations, and long-term funding for independent research groups working on safety, evaluation, and capability development, with strict conditions on retention and open publication.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases `eu_ai_sovereignty` by directly countering the talent exodus; supports `ai_safety` through sustained public research; indirectly strengthens `public_sentiment` by demonstrating investment in European scientific leadership.  
`Applies to:` EU-based AI researchers and frontier labs, with eligibility tied to EU residency, open publication, and compliance with weight-security standards.

## Priority  
**New measure – European AI Research Corps** — because the *emergent_ai_talent_exodus* is now the dominant threat to sovereignty, and without reversing it, all other measures — oversight, resilience, even compute — will fail for lack of people to run them.

## In practice  
We are closing out the InvestAI Gigafactories programme this turn. While the sites are not all operational — two remain delayed by environmental remediation — the legal and financial architecture is complete, and the Commission is triggering the final disbursement of €200bn in blended public-private funding. The sites in Finland and Portugal are already under construction, with compute delivery expected by turn 9. We are treating this as a qualified success: the pace was too slow, but the precedent of direct EU industrial intervention in compute is now set. The Vice-President for Industry will present a lessons-learned report to the College in October, focusing on streamlining permitting and grid access for future builds.

The Cyber Shield directive is now fully transposed, though enforcement remains patchy. ENISA has completed baseline assessments at 18 critical operators across energy, telecoms, and transport, and the Joint Crisis Cell has run its first red-team exercise on a simulated AI-coordinated grid attack. The results were sobering: detection worked, but coordination between national CERTs broke down within 48 hours. We are now drafting a follow-up resilience directive to strengthen cross-border command protocols, but that will wait for next turn. For now, we are activating the €1.2bn emergency reserve to fund real-time telemetry integration at six key nodes.

Behind the scenes, we are launching the European AI Research Corps. The legal base will be Article 185 TFEU, allowing us to pool funding across member states with a 70% EU co-financing rate. The programme will create 150 endowed chairs at universities and independent institutes, each with a guaranteed 10 petaflops-year of sovereign compute annually, drawn from the Gigafactories’ first wave. We are negotiating with the Max Planck Society, INRIA, and the Dutch National AI Coalition to host the first clusters. Crucially, the funding is tied to retention: researchers who leave the EU within five years must repay 80% of their compute and salary support. We are also offering fast-track visa and family settlement rights for incoming talent, modelled on the ERC’s success. France and Germany have signalled support; Poland and Hungary are concerned about brain drain from their own institutions, so we are reserving 30% of chairs for Central and Eastern Europe with matched national funding. The first call for proposals will launch in January 2030.
