### The honest admission
Autumn 2032 in Brussels was defined by what the Commission refused to pretend. The full ombudsman and court files on the benefits eligibility system were published, with scoring logs and appeal routing laid bare. Officials repeated the same line in hearing after hearing: the system had acted lawfully because the law had not covered it. No enforcement cases were opened. No rewrite was tabled. Newspapers kept the faces on front pages for weeks, then moved them inside — not because anger faded, but because there was no new decision to report.

Ministers stopped defending the rulebook in public. In private, several said the same thing: a reform would fail, and a failed reform would cost more than an admission.

### Paid by hand
With no standing to start anything, the Union nursed what existed. Employment offices kept paying the six-month wage support and retraining vouchers from reshuffled social funds and the temporary levy on automating employers. Clerks accepted incomplete employer forms rather than chase them. Take-up remained thin. Counsellors in Brussels, Milan, Warsaw and Barcelona described the same queue: graduates arriving, collecting two or three payments, then disappearing from registers.

Grid operators kept the manual fallback alive. Paper switching logs, dispersed spares, cross-border crews. After the spring intrusions, isolation was again faster by minutes; restoration again took days. Two more storage sites were quietly removed from shared lists after tampering. No announcement was made.

Clinics stayed on the domestic default, with metered American access held in reserve. No ministry claimed success for public assistants.

By December the common line still held, but only because nothing had been asked of it. Lights stayed on by hand. Trust did not return.
