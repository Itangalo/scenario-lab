Global AI capability rises to 67.0, driven by autonomous agents in cyber and materials research, while AI safety remains stagnant at 24.0. A major ransomware attack disrupts health systems in Spain and Belgium, highlighting the growing accessibility of AI-optimized offensive tools. In response, the EU activates its Sovereign Compute Emergency Procurement Directive, securing commitments from ASML and TSMC Europe, though no physical compute is delivered and firmware vulnerabilities persist. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme completes site preparation but remains unbuilt. ENISA launches the Conditional Open-Weight Licensing Framework, deploying scanning tools and imposing upload restrictions, yet enforcement is inconsistent and legally contested. Public trust erodes amid ongoing disruptions and unmet infrastructure promises. Political capital declines as major initiatives stall. U.S. maintains restrictive API controls, preserving EU strategic dependence on frontier AI access.
