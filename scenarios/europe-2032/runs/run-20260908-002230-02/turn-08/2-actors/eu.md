## In practice
We are acting on a pivot: from assurance to autonomy. The U.S. shutdown of access to advanced models was not just a disruption—it was a system check. It revealed that every evaluation, every red team, every licensing rule we have built assumes access to capable systems as a given. That assumption is now void. Our priority is no longer to govern the frontier—we must become it.

The Sovereign Compute Emergency Procurement Directive activates Article 122 TFEU and the Crisis Regulation framework to bypass standard procurement timelines. We direct €48bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, matched by €72bn in national co-funding from France, Germany, and the Netherlands, to EuroHPC JU with a single instruction: stand up 50 exaFLOPs of sovereign, secure, and operationally independent AI compute within 18 months. This means not just buying hardware—but contracting ASML for dedicated EU-only lithography slots, securing TSMC Europe’s upcoming 2nm line output via advance purchase agreements backed by the EIB, and establishing a new sovereign firmware layer to prevent remote disablement. The compute will be legally owned by a newly constituted European AI Infrastructure Trust, answerable to the Commission and insulated from external coercion.

At the same time, we complete the final phase of the InvestAI Gigafactories: site preparation is now finished in Brittany, Upper Austria, and Puglia, with grid connections secured under emergency energy provisions. These sites will house the first wave of procured systems. We also operationalize the Conditional Open-Weight Licensing Framework, deploying ENISA’s new verification gateway at major code repositories. Uploads of models above 52.0 capability index now trigger automatic identity checks and red-team review windows. GitHub and GitLab have agreed to comply for EU users; non-cooperative platforms face DNS-level restrictions under the DSA’s systemic risk clause. This is not perfect—but it draws a boundary where none existed.

We know the cost: political capital is thin, and pushing this directive risks overreach. But if we do not act now, we will spend the next six turns reacting to blackouts in medical AI, defense planning, and climate modeling—each one a further erosion of legitimacy. We choose instead to build what cannot be taken away.
