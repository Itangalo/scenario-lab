### A Step Forward, at Great Cost

Global AI capability advances to 65.0 as frontier labs integrate autonomous research agents into core development loops, accelerating training cycles and cyber tooling refinement. The interpretability gains from last turn stabilize but do not spread further—no broad safety improvement follows. `ai_safety` remains stagnant at 24.0.

Open-weight models show modest diffusion. Leaked fragments of a bio-design agent and repurposed synthetic media tools circulate in niche forums, contributing to a measured rise in `openweight_capability` to 55.5. This reflects ongoing leakage and recombination, not a sudden leap—consistent with the pace of frontier progress. No single release dominates; instead, incremental accumulation of capabilities in the wild continues, constrained by the underlying compute and knowledge barriers still controlled at the frontier.

The EU formally cancels the EU Access Assurance Protocol, acknowledging that diplomatic guarantees cannot override API-level access controls. As its new priority, it launches the Sovereign Compute Emergency Procurement Directive under Article 122 TFEU, redirecting €48bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility with national co-funding pledged. ASML agrees to reserve EU-dedicated lithography time, and TSMC Europe enters negotiations for advance chip purchases. However, firmware sovereignty remains unproven, and remote disablement risks persist. Implementation will take time: no compute is yet secured, and delivery timelines extend into future turns.

The Conditional Open-Weight Licensing Framework is delayed. ENISA’s gateway is not yet operational; scanning of GitHub and GitLab for high-capability uploads will begin only in the next turn. The incident involving the Polish developer is still under review and will inform early enforcement patterns, but no action occurs this turn.

Despite these moves, political capital erodes. The portfolio burden intensifies, and the emergency directive fails to yield immediate gains. Public sentiment holds steady, but quiet frustration grows among member states over funding diversions.
