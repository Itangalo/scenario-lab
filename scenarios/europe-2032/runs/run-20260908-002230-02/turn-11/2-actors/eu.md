## In practice
We are seizing the initiative opened by the Sovereign Cloud coalition. Legal authority means little if the systems we must defend run on infrastructure someone else can throttle or inspect. This turn, we shift from contingency to construction: the Commission will issue a Recommendation under Article 114 TFEU urging all member states to designate at least 40% of non-sensitive public-sector AI workloads for migration to sovereign cloud platforms by mid-2033. We are unlocking €3.8bn in accelerated cohesion and Digital Europe Programme funds to cover transition costs, with bonuses for early adopters and penalties in future funding allocations for laggards. The EuroHPC Joint Undertaking will repurpose two upcoming exascale deployments—LUMI-2 and JUPITER-2—as anchor tenants, ensuring they serve public administration first.

Resistance from states tied to U.S. hyperscalers is real, but so is their exposure to rationing. We are offering a phased transition: three-year contracts can be honoured, but renewal requires a sovereign fallback. To France, which fears fragmentation, we offer coordination through a new Sovereign Cloud Governance Board under the Digital Ministers’ Council. To Germany, we promise interoperability standards that prevent vendor lock-in. The first benchmark—10% workload migration by Q2 2032—will be reported to the European Parliament as proof we are not just reacting, but building. This is not a replacement for the Incident Response Corps; it is the ground it must stand on. Without it, every emergency power we claim is rented, not owned.
