## In practice  
We are finalising the Emergency Resilience Surge with urgency: the cyber_major_incident has struck, a coordinated ransomware sweep across regional health authorities and municipal services in five member states, exploiting AI-generated exploits targeting legacy authentication systems. The attack did not cascade, thanks to the air-gapped monitoring stacks now live in 60% of priority nodes, but recovery remains slow. COREPER II has fast-tracked adoption of mutual aid protocols, and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre is deploying emergency response teams with pre-positioned decryption and failover tooling. The two holdout member states are now onboard, swayed by the co-financing model and the undeniable evidence of shared exposure. This measure will finish this turn as planned, delivering binding cross-border operational resilience and justifying its cost.

Meanwhile, we are advancing the EU Access Assurance Protocol through the Justice and Home Affairs Council, using Article 114 TFEU to frame runtime deposits as a condition of market access. Bilateral talks with U.S. hyperscalers continue under the Trade and Technology Council, where we are offering multi-year licensing certainty in exchange for compliance. The first pilot—AI-driven energy load forecasting—will go live in Q1 2028, with audited runtimes stored in Luxembourg and accessible only under Council-confirmed disruption. Simultaneously, the InvestAI Gigafactories are moving from site selection to construction: grid interconnections in Finland and Spain are under accelerated REPowerEU rules, and the European Investment Bank has released the first tranche, contingent on national security frameworks being enacted by December.

The new measure, Mandatory Pre-Deployment Cyber Red-Teaming, responds directly to the offensive capabilities now loose in the open frontier and demonstrated in the recent attack. We are drafting a regulation under the AI Act’s conformity assessment framework, mandating that any model above 50.0 undergo red-team evaluation by ENISA-accredited labs for autonomous cyber exploitation, self-preservation behaviours, and stealth deployment. The threshold ensures it targets only systems capable of sustained agency. We will face resistance from open-weight communities and frontier developers, so we are preparing a carve-out for research use under air-gapped conditions, and building support in the European Parliament’s LIBE and IMCO committees by framing this as basic product safety for the digital age. This is not a pause — it is a checkpoint, and it is the minimum credible response to a world where the tooling to break society is now open-source.
