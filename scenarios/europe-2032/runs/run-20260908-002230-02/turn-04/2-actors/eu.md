## New measure  
**Conditional Open-Weight Licensing Framework**  

Introduce a licensing regime for open-weight model releases above 45.0 capability index, requiring proof of secure distribution channels and red-team validation before public release; non-compliant uploads to public repositories are treated as unauthorised diffusion of dual-use infrastructure.  

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` openweight_capability -4 (by delaying or preventing unsecured releases at critical thresholds), ai_safety +6 (by raising baseline assurance for open models), public_sentiment -3 (due to backlash from developer communities and innovation advocates)  
`Applies to:` Open-weight developers, hosting platforms, academic institutions releasing models, EU member states, and cloud infrastructure providers operating in the EU

## Priority  
**Mandatory Pre-Deployment Cyber Red-Teaming for High-Capability Models** — because it is now clear that offensive cyber capabilities are not only in the hands of states and hyperscalers but are actively being weaponised in the wild via open channels, and this measure delivers the first enforceable safeguard at the point of release, which is the last moment we can act before systems escape control.

## In practice  
We are finalising the Emergency Resilience Surge this turn, and its completion is a relief: the cyber_major_incident has confirmed both the threat and our response capacity. Air-gapped monitoring stacks are now operational at 60% of critical nodes, mutual aid protocols are binding, and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre has deployed rapid-response teams with pre-positioned tooling. The attack was contained, but recovery remains slow—proof that resilience is not just about prevention, but continuity. This measure finishes as planned, and its success must now be leveraged politically: we will publish a lessons-learned report through ENISA to anchor credibility and justify continued investment in preparedness.

Simultaneously, we are pushing hard on the Mandatory Pre-Deployment Cyber Red-Teaming measure, now in its second turn. The regulation is being drafted under Article 16 of the AI Act, using conformity assessment procedures to mandate ENISA-accredited labs conduct adversarial testing on models above 50.0. We are facing resistance from open-source communities and frontier developers alike—FSF Europe has called it “a firewall against collaboration,” while one U.S. lab has threatened to withdraw from EU market access. To counter this, we are negotiating a research exemption for air-gapped academic use and building support in the European Parliament’s LIBE committee by framing this as digital product safety. The cyberattack has helped: interior ministers now speak of “AI as infrastructure weaponised,” and we are using that shift to lock in support before the backlash from the tech sector solidifies.

The new measure, Conditional Open-Weight Licensing, responds directly to the campaign_backlash and the open frontier’s drift toward offensive capability. With openweight_capability at 49.0 and community-driven tooling enabling autonomous replication, we can no longer treat open releases as inherently benign. This framework will require developers to obtain a license before releasing models above 45.0, conditioned on secure distribution and red-team validation. Hosting platforms and cloud providers will be required to enforce takedowns of unlicensed uploads. We are preparing for fierce opposition—this is a direct challenge to the norms of open development—but we are framing it as proportionate: not a ban, but a licensing regime akin to dual-use export controls. The legal basis will be Article 114 TFEU, and we will launch consultations with member states in COREPER I, targeting adoption by turn 6. This is not about stopping openness—it is about ensuring it does not become a vector for systemic harm.
