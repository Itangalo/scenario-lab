## In practice
We are activating Article 14 of the NIS2 Directive with emergency provisions to mandate immediate cyber hardening across energy, water and transport operators identified as high-impact. The ENISA Rapid Response Facility will deploy pre-approved technical packages — including air-gapped monitoring, AI-driven anomaly baselining, and zero-trust architecture rollouts — to all designated operators within six months. Funding of €18bn is being unlocked from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with 70% grants for compliance by Q2 2027.

This is being coordinated through the COREPER II formation, with a special Council meeting on digital resilience scheduled for November. Resistance from smaller member states concerned about cost and sovereignty is being met with binding offers of full EU co-financing and technical assistance through the newly expanded European Cybersecurity Competence Centre. We are also fast-tracking mutual aid protocols so that any future intrusion detected in one member state automatically triggers defensive surges in others.

At the same time, we are holding the course on InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech sovereignty package, pushing national governments to designate priority zones for data centre development with grid access guaranteed within 18 months. The European Investment Bank is finalising loan terms this month, but political cohesion hinges on demonstrating that sovereignty is not just about compute — it is about keeping the lights on.
