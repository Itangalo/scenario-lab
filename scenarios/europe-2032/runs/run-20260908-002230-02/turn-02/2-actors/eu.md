To overcome resistance from member states concerned about sovereignty and cost, we are finalising a co-financing model that covers 85% of compliance expenses and embedding mutual aid clauses into national contingency plans — a breach in one triggers automatic defensive surges in all.

The European Investment Bank has approved the first €40bn tranche, contingent on member states designating land and security frameworks by Q3. The new EU Access Assurance Protocol responds directly to the member_state_defection: we are drafting a regulation under Article 114 TFEU that requires any foreign provider offering advanced AI services in the EU to deposit operational runtimes — containerised, audited, and capable of independent execution — in a sovereign EU enclave, accessible under strict conditions if access is disrupted. This is not about ownership, but assured continuity. We are engaging with U.S. hyperscalers bilaterally through the Trade and Technology Council, offering regulatory predictability in exchange for compliance. The protocol will be piloted with healthcare and energy forecasting systems, where disruption would be most destabilising. This is sovereignty not as aspiration, but as operational fact.
