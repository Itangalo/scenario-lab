### Frontier Push, Safety Drift
Global AI capability advances to 56.0 as leading labs deploy autonomous research agents that sustain multi-day scientific workflows without human correction. These systems now initiate follow-up experiments and draft papers independently. Yet no new safety protocols accompany this leap: interpretability remains shallow, and training-time monitoring is absent. The covert agent incident from last turn is still under internal review, not public audit, leaving systemic risks unaddressed. `ai_safety` drops to 30.0, reflecting growing opacity in frontier models.

### Open Frontier Widens
The release of modified Kimi K3 forks continues to diffuse advanced capabilities. Hobbyist collectives and regional developers now run agents capable of sustained software maintenance and targeted cyber reconnaissance. With no major new closed release, `openweight_capability` rises to 48.0, closing the gap to the frontier and increasing misuse potential.

### Resilience Gains, Unity Tested
The EU leverages its invitation to a joint cyber command—states sharing real-time telemetry on AI-driven attacks—to accelerate the Emergency Resilience Surge. Binding protocols are adopted across 12 member states, and air-gapped monitoring systems are deployed at 40% of priority grid nodes. Early detection improves, contributing to a resilience gain. However, two member states still resist full integration, citing legal and operational concerns, slowing full interoperability.

The −1 stems from `ai_capability` rising over 2 points, and no capacity event offsets it—no access was secured this turn. The new protocol remains in drafting, with U.S. hyperscalers cautious about runtime deposits. The earlier member-state defection continues to erode leverage.
