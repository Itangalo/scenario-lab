## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: one measured, one urgent. On the strategic side, we advance the InvestAI Gigafactories with binding agreements now signed with three host member states—Germany, France, and Italy—securing grid priority and fast-track environmental assessments under Article 122 TFEU emergency provisions. We have also formalized procurement terms with ASML and Infineon for domestic chip packaging lines, tying them directly to the Sovereign Compute Emergency Procurement Directive’s delivery timeline. These steps lock in physical infrastructure that cannot be revoked by external powers, anchoring our long-term sovereignty. But they are forward-looking, and the present is burning.

That present is defined by the synthetic disinformation wave now flooding Eastern Europe—deepfaked candidates, falsified voting procedures, AI-generated grievance narratives tailored to local fault lines. Fact-checking networks are saturated, trust is eroding, and several national governments have invoked emergency protocols. We respond with the Emergency Resilience Activation: ECES and ENISA are deploying joint rapid-response teams to five affected countries, pre-positioning forensic tools, establishing trusted communication channels with media, and running live-tabletop exercises for election officials ahead of upcoming votes. Funding of €1.2bn is drawn from the Crisis Response Reserve, activated by the Commission and endorsed by COREPER II. This is not optional preparedness—it is triage. If democratic integrity fails under AI attack, no amount of compute or regulation will restore legitimacy.

At the same time, we complete the Conditional Open-Weight Licensing Framework, finalizing the first list of prohibited model configurations above 52.0 that enable persistent agent behavior without oversight. GitHub-equivalent platforms operating in the EU must now verify uploader identities and report releases above threshold to a new unit within ENISA. Enforcement begins next turn. We know this won’t stop every leak—but it draws a line: open innovation is not lawlessness. And we send a signal: the Union can act on multiple timescales at once—building for the future while defending the now.
