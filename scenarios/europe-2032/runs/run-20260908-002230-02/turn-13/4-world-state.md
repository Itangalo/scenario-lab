### A Crisis of Control

Global AI capability edges to 74.0, driven by recursive optimisation and autonomous cyber toolchains, while safety plummets to 14.0 after a major cyber incident reveals model-generated attack code and opaque coordination failures in training environments. The gap between power and understanding widens into a chasm.

The EU responds to cascading shocks: a ransomware sweep paralyses public health registries, and private AI investment collapses, cancelling European compute expansions. In reaction, the Commission drafts the **Emergency Compute Mobilisation Facility**, a large category 4 measure intended to seize stranded data centres, fast-track permitting, and backstop the emergent Sovereign Cloud Initiative with EU ownership. Invoking Article 122 TFEU, it frames the move as a sovereign rescue of critical infrastructure. However, France and Poland resist, demanding veto rights over asset seizures. The Council delays a vote until January, leaving the Facility in legal limbo — it remains a draft proposal, not formally introduced.

Due to the ongoing **Sovereign Cloud Integration Acceleration**, the EU cannot introduce a second measure this turn. The **Emergency Compute Mobilisation Facility** is held in draft, with no formal proposal made, preserving compliance with the one-measure limit. As a result, the **Sovereign Cloud Integration Acceleration** remains stalled, yielding no sovereignty gain. The **Public Sector AI Deployment Moratorium** formally ends, but public trust erodes further as the benefits scandal and hospital walkouts linger in media coverage. A new wave of job losses in legal and administrative sectors, driven by unregulated AI adoption, deepens the sense of institutional betrayal.

Resilience dips to 54.0 as the cyber incident overwhelms response protocols, exposing gaps in cross-border coordination despite the newly formed Incident Corps. The AI investment collapse triggers a −2 in resilience under metric rule 4, as planned defences evaporate.

Sovereignty remains at 9.0: −1 from rising capability, no gain from Sovereign Cloud (still in flight, no progress), and no benefit from the unproposed Emergency Facility. Political capital remains at 8.0 — no proposal bonus is awarded, as no new measure was formally introduced; the portfolio charge remains −6 (Sovereign Cloud −3, moratorium −2, priority −1), with no offsetting gain. Public sentiment falls to 21.0, hit by the cyber disruption, job losses, and perception of chaotic, reactive governance.

Openweight capability rises to 70.0, consistent with the ongoing accumulation of accessible AI capability at the fixed, unannounced global rate. The increase reflects gradual diffusion of knowledge and tooling, with no discrete event enabling a larger leap. The gap between openweight and frontier capability narrows slightly, but remains within bounds.
