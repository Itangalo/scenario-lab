### A Step Backward in a Rising Storm

Global AI capability advances to 68.5 as autonomous agents accelerate self-improvement cycles in cyber and materials domains. No safety improvements land on deployed systems: `ai_safety` remains stagnant at 24.0, with developers focused on performance, not control. The gap between what AI can do and what anyone can reliably govern widens.

A major automated ransomware attack disrupts health services across Spain and Belgium. Diagnostic delays stretch into weeks; recovery takes months. The incident reveals not only weak defences but paralysed coordination — no agency had pre-authorised powers to intervene across borders, and national CERTs hesitated without political clearance. Public frustration mounts.

The EU formally concludes the Sovereign Compute Emergency Procurement Directive. Despite €48bn in commitments and high-level industrial pacts, no sovereign clusters are operational. Firmware vulnerabilities and remote disablement risks remain unresolved, leaving dependence intact.

In response, the European Union launches the **European AI Incident Response Corps**, a large category 6 measure aimed at building rapid cross-border response capacity. Recruitment begins, legal charters are drafted under ENISA, and exercise planning starts. However, resistance from interior ministries slows integration, and only Estonia and Finland commit fully. The initiative yields no immediate resilience gain.

Open-weight capability sees limited progress, rising to 61.5, as some open-source model training completes on newly allocated public cloud slices. However, deployment bottlenecks and fragmented access prevent broader scaling. This growth remains within the bounds of global AI capability, preserving the constitutional constraint that openweight cannot outpace frontier development.

Political capital erodes further. With public sentiment unchanged at 31.0 and trust in AI infrastructure declining, the burden of past failures compounds. The new measure is large and contentious, launched into a fragile institutional landscape.

U.S. posture remains under CONSOLIDATION, maintaining tiered access to frontier models. No breakthrough eases Europe’s strategic dependency.

### Hardening Amid Fragility

Efforts shift from acquiring compute to ensuring continuity of function during crisis. But the transition begins too late to reverse current losses.
