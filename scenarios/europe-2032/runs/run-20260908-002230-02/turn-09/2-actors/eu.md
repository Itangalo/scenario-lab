## In practice
This turn marks the end of our push for sovereign compute under emergency powers — a directive launched with urgency, funded at scale, and politically sustained through national co-financing agreements with France, Germany, and the Netherlands. Yet despite binding commitments from ASML and TSMC Europe, no physical clusters are online. Firmware sovereignty remains unproven, remote disablement risks unresolved, and the promised 50 exaFLOPs exist only as contracts and credit lines. The directive has bought intent, not insulation. As it finishes, we face a new reality: even if compute arrives next year, it will be useless unless it can survive and operate during crisis.

We now pivot to operational endurance. The European AI Incident Response Corps begins drafting its charter under Article 14a of the ENISA Regulation, expanding the agency’s mandate beyond advisory functions to direct intervention. We allocate €9.2bn over three years — €3.5bn from the Digital Europe Programme, €4.7bn from national contributions coordinated via the Permanent Council on Cybersecurity, and €1bn from the Innovation Fund as surge capacity. Recruitment launches immediately for 1,200 specialists: red-team engineers, protocol auditors, crisis communicators, and legal liaisons trained in cross-border data access. Their first exercise — “Nightfall Cascade” — simulates coordinated AI-optimised attacks on power grid调度 systems in four member states, testing whether pre-approved mutual aid overrides can activate within 90 minutes.

Resistance comes from interior ministries wary of ceding control, and from some capitals questioning whether another centralised Brussels mechanism adds speed or bureaucracy. To win consent, we offer opt-in tiers: full integration for early adopters like Estonia and Finland, lightweight alert-sharing for others, with full membership requiring only that national CERTs accept common playbooks and joint training. The real leverage is recent memory — the images of hospital appointment logs frozen for weeks still dominate evening news. We are selling not more infrastructure, but the promise that next time, someone will already be on the scene.
