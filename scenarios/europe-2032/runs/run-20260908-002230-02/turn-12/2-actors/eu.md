## In practice
The European AI Incident Response Corps completes its build-out this turn and transitions from construction to operational readiness. ENISA will host a live-fire exercise in June, simulating a coordinated model compromise across three member states’ energy and transport systems. Legal authorities in those states have pre-authorised escalation protocols, and cross-border command will be tested under real-time conditions. This is not ceremonial: the exercise will determine whether the Corps can be trusted with live incidents. Funding for staffing and tooling is now secured through the Digital Europe Programme, and the unit will report directly to the Cybersecurity Board under the next Council Presidency.

But legitimacy is crumbling. The automated decision scandal — now confirmed by a European Ombudsman investigation — reveals that a national benefits system in a major member state denied thousands of claims based on unexplainable AI assessments, with no human review. Simultaneously, the emergent labour disruption wave spreads: hospital administrators in two capitals have walked out, refusing to implement AI-driven scheduling tools imposed without consultation. The narrative is clear: the Union is deploying systems faster than it can govern them, and faster than society can absorb them.

We cannot double down on rollout while trust evaporates. So we are imposing a moratorium — not a retreat, but a pause to re-ground. The Commission will issue a Recommendation under Article 16 TFEU calling for an immediate halt to all new AI deployments in public-sector decision-making until two conditions are met: first, an independent audit by national AI safety authorities (to be established under revised AI Act guidelines) must certify the system’s interpretability and recourse mechanisms; second, sectoral social partners must co-sign implementation plans. The European Trade Union Confederation and employer groups will be convened in July to draft a framework for AI in public work. This is not a surrender to resistance — it is a reassertion of democratic control. We are still building sovereign infrastructure, but we will not anchor it on broken trust.
