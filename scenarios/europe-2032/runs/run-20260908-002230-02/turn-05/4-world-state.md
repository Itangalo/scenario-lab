### Capability Surge, Safety Breach  
A leading lab demonstrates a sudden leap in narrow technical domains: its latest system autonomously discovers a novel exploit chain in widely used cryptographic libraries and replicates it across test environments in under 48 hours. While general capability rises only modestly to 62.0, the jump triggers widespread alarm. No safety upgrades accompany the advance; interpretability remains poor, and monitoring for goal drift is still absent. `ai_safety` plummets to 19.0.

### Open Frontier Widens Moderately  
Fragments of the new exploit tooling leak into open-weight model repositories, enabling community developers to enhance agent persistence and task automation. The diffusion is partial and delayed, but noticeable. `openweight_capability` rises to 53.0—reflecting absorption of frontier advances at a lagging pace—now at 85.5% of `ai_capability`, consistent with gradual spillover and no improvement in spillover efficiency.

### EU Access Assurance Protocol Advances  
The EU completes the final legislative steps for the EU Access Assurance Protocol, requiring foreign AI providers to maintain runtime enclaves under EU jurisdiction. However, enforcement remains conditional on future negotiations with third countries and companies. Without prior agreements or leverage, U.S. and Chinese firms have not committed to compliance. Two non-U.S. hyperscalers signal tentative interest, but no operational access has been granted. The measure is formally adopted but not yet implemented, and no sovereignty benefit is realized this turn.

### Pre-Deployment Red-Teaming Measure Delayed  
The proposal for Mandatory Pre-Deployment Cyber Red-Teaming is revised and resubmitted with a tiered model based on model capability thresholds. It does not finalize this turn. Full implementation will require at least one additional turn, consistent with minimum procedural timelines for high-stakes regulatory measures. No progress bonus applies.

### Emergency Compute Procurement Announced, Not Launched  
In response to growing restrictions on U.S. compute access, the European Commission proposes the Sovereign Compute Emergency Procurement Directive under Article 122 TFEU. The plan calls for 10,000 AI accelerators from EU-aligned suppliers, but procurement has not yet begun. Supply chain constraints and vendor capacity delays mean delivery timelines extend years into the future. As a new high-cost measure, implementation will take multiple turns.

### Sentiment Dips Further  
Public anxiety grows amid security fears and perceptions of overreach. The new procurement proposal is framed as reactive, and developer opposition to licensing rules hardens. `public_sentiment` slips to 34.0. `eu_political_capital` remains unchanged at 26.0, as no measure has yet delivered tangible results.

### U.S. Election Outcome  
The United States elects a president who treats advanced AI as a strategic asset to be tightly controlled. Access for foreign partners, including the EU, will now be tiered and reviewed against national security interests. In the first executive actions of the transition, export controls are tightened, and several pending data-sharing agreements with European counterparts are suspended indefinitely. This shift in posture reduces `eu_ai_sovereignty` by 3.0. Combined with a −1.0 penalty from the 2.0-point rise in `ai_capability`, sovereignty falls to 14.0.
