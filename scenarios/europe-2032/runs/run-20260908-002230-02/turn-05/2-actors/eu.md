## In practice  
We are finalising two major measures this turn: the EU Access Assurance Protocol and the Mandatory Pre-Deployment Cyber Red-Teaming regime. The former is now legally binding: Article 16 of the AI Act, as interpreted through the Schengen+ framework, establishes that any provider serving the EU market must maintain a runtime enclave under EU jurisdiction, with access guaranteed even under foreign export controls. We have secured commitments from two non-U.S. hyperscalers to host these enclaves, and ENISA is auditing their technical implementation. The political cost was high—especially with the U.S. election outcome—but the election_consolidation event made the necessity undeniable.

The Cyber Red-Teaming mandate also crosses the finish line. After intense negotiation with LIBE and the EDPB, we have adopted a tiered compliance model: models above 55.0 must undergo full adversarial testing by ENISA-accredited labs, while those between 50.0 and 55.0 can use certified private red teams under audit. A research exemption has been carved for air-gapped academic use, narrowly defined to prevent abuse. The first test results will be published next turn, and we are preparing a public dashboard to show exploited vulnerabilities—transparency as credibility.

The new Sovereign Compute Emergency Procurement Directive responds to the stark reality: with eu_ai_sovereignty at 17.0 and U.S. access now explicitly tiered, waiting for market-led solutions is no longer viable. We are invoking Article 122 TFEU for emergency infrastructure, allowing the Commission to directly contract with suppliers like ASML, Infineon, and a newly formed EU semiconductor consortium. The goal is not to match the frontier, but to secure a minimum viable compute base—10,000 accelerators—under irrevocable EU control by turn 8. This will anchor future sovereign models and provide execution environments for red-teaming and resilience monitoring. We are already in talks with three member states to host the facilities, offering grid priority and security guarantees. The cost is steep—3 political capital per turn for three turns—but without it, every other measure rests on borrowed capability.
