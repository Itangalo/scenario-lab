### Compacts and queues
January opened with lawyers rather than diggers. Commission energy and legal teams sat with councillors and court-appointed engineers in the two freeze regions, haggling over water caps, staged grid connections and a share of any future revenues earmarked for clinics. No injunction was challenged. By spring drafts circulated site-by-site, but no tape was lifted and concrete stayed silent.

Hospitals held, barely. Maintenance money kept the continuity stack and the showcase triage helpers online. Where they worked, imaging lists stayed a little shorter and regional papers repeated the European-fix line. Elsewhere queues lengthened again and staff complained the tools were slow and disliked.

The no-undercut pact formally closed this semester. Brussels declared unity preserved — no second capital broke ranks — but the capital with its separate supply deal kept it, and Washington offered only polite language on pooled volumes.

### What leaked out
Two outside shocks reframed the semester. A contested biosecurity paper claimed a genome model had helped sketch a viable human-infecting design, sparking a fierce methods fight among specialists and alarmed memos inside health agencies. At the same time a openly downloadable model landed almost at the closed frontier, mirrored hundreds of thousands of times in days.

HERA, disease-control and cybersecurity agencies shifted to low-cost watch: syndromic monitoring, hygiene checks in hospitals, quiet guidance to municipalities and university labs on handling the downloadable frontier system. No new money or mandates followed.

By June the Union had survived without restarting: wards open in showcase sites, builds frozen everywhere, dependence deeper and now unrecallable.
