### The floor built elsewhere
Washington and Beijing announced a limited understanding on securing model weights, restraining autonomous escalation, and controlling a class of biological design tools. Verification was thin but not empty, with inspection visits and shared incident channels promised. Brussels learned the details from press briefings and late-night calls, not from the negotiating table.

The Commission and the external service moved quickly to ask for observer status, offering European evaluation sites, hospital and cyber incident data, and cooperation on supply-chain checks in return for information-sharing and eventual adherence. The reply was courteous and non-committal: partners welcomed the interest, promised technical workshops in the new year, made no promise of a seat.

### Compacts and wards
At home the semester looked like the last one. Lawyers shuttled between regional councils and court-appointed engineers over water caps and staged grid links for the two frozen build sites. Draft compacts grew thicker. No injunction was lifted, no foundation poured.

Hospitals kept running on maintenance money. In showcase clinics the European triage helpers kept imaging waits a little shorter, and local papers still called it a European fix. Elsewhere queues lengthened and staff grumbled at slow tools. Locum funding ran to December with no successor agreed.

Health and cyber agencies stayed on watch-only routines: syndromic monitoring, hygiene rounds, quiet guidance to universities and municipalities on handling the freely mirrored near-frontier model. The great-power understanding gave them a reference to cite, but no new mandate and no new money.

By December the Union had preserved cohesion — no second separate supply deal emerged — but dependence deepened. The outside world had set a floor under the race, and Europe was standing on it without having helped pour it.
