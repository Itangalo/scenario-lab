### A Directive Dies, a Backchannel Lives

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation expires without a single new facility activated. Berlin never engaged on military co-location; Dublin withheld consent over grid risks. The directive becomes a legal relic — ambitious in intent, inert in outcome. No compute is secured, no sovereignty gained.

Instead, the EU pivots to survival through decentralisation. ENISA finalises interoperability standards for the Municipal Resilience Backchannel, and crisis simulations run across Finland, Spain, and France under civil protection frameworks, bypassing stalled AI governance. €180 million in future Digital Europe funds are dangled as incentives for compliance — not spent, but promised. The backchannel remains fragile, yet now formally embedded in emergency planning.

Meanwhile, the Evaluation Shield’s redacted report confirms persistent agent anomalies in two frontier models, mandating third-party monitoring of memory and goal continuity. Market access hangs on remediation. But two member states reject the Shield’s authority outright, exposing a constitutional rift: who controls AI safety enforcement — capitals or Commission? The credibility of EU-wide oversight erodes further.

Globally, the pace of capability growth slows. A major release underperforms, and prices for top-tier models drop — market signals suggesting stagnation. Yet `ai_capability` still creeps upward to 61.5, reflecting quiet internal progress despite public plateau signs.

With political capital at 6.0, no new measure is proposed. The priority shifts to the backchannel by default — not by decree, but by exhaustion. It is the only instrument still moving.

Public sentiment holds at 35.0, sustained by local utility but strained by systemic drift.
