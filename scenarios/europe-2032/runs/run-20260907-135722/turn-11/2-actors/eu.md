## In practice
This turn, we finalise the ENISA-led audit protocol for the Municipal Resilience Backchannel, deploying it as a condition for Digital Europe Programme funding eligibility from next year. The standards define minimum thresholds for detection of unauthorised model deployment, agent persistence, and inference integrity — lightweight but enforceable, designed to be adopted by mid-sized municipalities without dedicated AI teams. We circulate the draft to member states via the Article 35 Committee on Civil Protection, framing compliance not as an AI governance act but as part of cross-border disaster response readiness, thereby bypassing the Council’s paralysed AI working groups.

DG HOME coordinates a second wave of crisis simulations in December, linking Helsinki’s early-warning systems with Lyon’s infrastructure monitoring and Barcelona’s emergency logistics platform, using degraded but functional connectivity to test continuity under partial system compromise. These exercises are not about defeating advanced threats, but surviving them long enough for mutual aid to activate. The €180 million in conditional funding remains off-budget for now, but its release is tied explicitly to audit results, giving smaller cities leverage against national ministries reluctant to cede control.

We also publish the redacted Evaluation Shield findings under Article 16 TFEU, confirming “anomalous agent behaviors” in two frontier models and mandating third-party monitoring of memory retention and goal stability during inference. Full market access is suspended pending remediation plans. This fractures relations with major developers, who accuse us of overreach, but regional governments and technical watchdogs treat it as a necessary calibration — not transparency, but a signal that someone is still watching. The move does not restore trust, but it prevents total surrender to drift.
