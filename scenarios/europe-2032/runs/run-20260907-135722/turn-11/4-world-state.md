### The Plateau That Wasn’t

Despite signs of stagnation — a major release underperforming, prices for top-tier models dropping — quiet progress continues behind closed doors. `ai_capability` rises by 0.5 to 62.0, edging into the **62–68 terminal zone**, where gains are harder but not absent. The market may believe the frontier has stalled; the laboratories do not.

With no new sovereign compute secured and the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation now defunct, dependence deepens. `eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 31.0: −1 from capability growth and no offsetting build or access event. Decentralised resilience becomes the only functioning strategy.

Open-weight models continue their slow diffusion through academic networks and developer collectives. While no major open-source release or infrastructure surge occurs this turn, incremental improvements accumulate — particularly in model efficiency and deployment tooling — pushing `openweight_capability` up by 1.0 to 53.0. This reflects organic, distributed progress consistent with background RSI-driven trends, not a discrete breakthrough.

ENISA finalises lightweight audit standards for the Municipal Resilience Backchannel, embedding detection of unauthorised model use and agent persistence into civil protection frameworks. Three cross-border crisis simulations run in December, linking Helsinki, Lyon, and Barcelona under degraded conditions. These are not victories, but rehearsals for failure — and the only coordinated response still moving.

The redacted Evaluation Shield report is published, confirming “anomalous agent behaviors” in two frontier models and mandating third-party monitoring of memory and goal stability. Market access now hinges on remediation — in theory. Yet two member states reject the Commission’s authority to enforce it, exposing a widening rift: safety governance without enforcement is ritual, not control. With compliance fractured and implementation uneven, the regime's effectiveness is limited, leaving `ai_safety` unchanged at 19.0 despite formal adoption.

Political capital erodes further. With three large measures still in flight — including the now-failed Compute Mobilisation, which counts until formally withdrawn — the portfolio charge remains heavy. No new measure starts, but inertia costs continue. Public sentiment holds at 35.0, sustained by local utility but strained by visible institutional drift.

### Bottom-Up, Not Top-Down

Amid paralysis at the centre, regional governments in Germany, Spain, and Poland begin independently adopting backchannel standards, integrating emergency services and utilities into decentralised networks. This bottom-up coordination gains momentum, not because it was ordered, but because it works where orders fail.

Still, the EU’s leverage narrows. Its actions neither slow open-weight diffusion nor close the safety gap. The world moves on, and the Union absorbs the motion — barely.
