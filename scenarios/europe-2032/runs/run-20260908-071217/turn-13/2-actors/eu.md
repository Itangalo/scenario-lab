## In practice  
We are still in operational silence, but the silence is no longer passive. The audit into the ransomware attack on emergency care systems is now in its final phase, with technical teams from ENISA-AI and the AI Office reconstructing model provenance chains across three national health data platforms. The focus is on whether the breach originated in adversarial fine-tuning of open-weight models, undetected backdoors in certified systems, or compromised deployment infrastructure — a distinction that will determine whether our failure was technical, procedural, or strategic. Legal teams are resolving access disputes with two member states over training logs held by foreign-hosted cloud providers, a process slowed by conflicting interpretations of the Data Governance Act and the Cloud Rules Regulation.

Behind the scenes, the European Commission’s Task Force on AI Sovereignty has begun mapping the fallout from the **ai_investment_collapse**, assessing which planned compute expansions have vanished and which foreign hyperscaler partnerships are now untenable. The US posture of consolidation has hardened, with three key partners withdrawing from EU co-investment talks after Q2 writedowns. This has forced a quiet reckoning: the assumption that Europe could buy its way into capability through foreign partnerships is no longer credible. DG CNECT, in coordination with the European Investment Bank, is preparing a classified paper on sovereign compute options under capital scarcity, to be delivered before the Council’s special session. But without audit clarity and without capital, no directive can be proposed, no funding unlocked, no agency empowered. We are not acting because we cannot — but we are preparing to act, and the next move must be decisive.
