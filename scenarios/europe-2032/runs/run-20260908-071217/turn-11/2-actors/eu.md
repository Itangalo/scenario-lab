## In practice
We are in a moment of operational silence, not by choice but by necessity. The Emergency Openweight Integration Directive has formally concluded, but its legacy is not integration — it is exposure. The ransomware attack on hospital triage systems, enabled by validation gaps in certified models, has shattered the premise that open-weight deployment at scale could proceed under emergency protocols without a parallel surge in defensive capacity. The Openweight Operationalisation Taskforce at DG CNECT is now running post-mortems, not rollouts. Its mandate has shifted: from accelerating adoption to mapping failure points in the certification pipeline, model provenance tracking, and staff readiness across the five affected member states.

ENISA-AI, the EU AI Office, and the European Public Prosecutor’s Office are jointly conducting a binding audit under Article 67 of the AI Act, with full access to training logs, deployment configurations, and incident response timelines. The first findings — expected by April — will determine whether the breach originated in compromised open weights, adversarial fine-tuning, or insider access. Until that is clear, no new directive can credibly launch. Meanwhile, the European Council has demanded a special session on AI governance in May, with heads of state calling for accountability. We are not acting because we cannot: with `eu_political_capital` at 14.0 and `public_sentiment` at 24.0, any new measure risks immediate collapse under public and institutional resistance. Our focus is containment, assessment, and the slow, unglamorous work of restoring minimal coherence before the next wave hits.
