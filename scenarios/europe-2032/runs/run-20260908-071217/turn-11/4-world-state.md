### The Breach That Keeps Spreading

A wave of automated ransomware crippled emergency triage systems across five EU countries, locking patient records and delaying critical care for days. The attack exploited subtle adversarial fine-tuning in certified open-weight clinical models — models trusted under the now-defunct Emergency Openweight Integration Directive. Forensic analysis confirmed the tooling was AI-generated, and the damage was widespread: public trust in AI-assisted healthcare collapsed overnight.

The European Union, already paralyzed by low political capital, chose not to act. With no new measures proposed and no priority declared, institutions retreated into damage control. The Openweight Operationalisation Taskforce shifted from rollout to post-mortem, scrambling to trace how flawed validation protocols allowed compromised models into production. ENISA-AI, the EU AI Office, and the European Public Prosecutor’s Office launched a joint audit, but answers would take months.

### Safety in Freefall, Sentiment Withers

As capability advanced to 70.5 — driven by further gains in automated legal and clinical reasoning — no safety improvements landed. Interpretability tools remained unused in deployed systems, and the breach revealed systemic opacity. With no safeguards completed, `ai_safety` plummeted by 5 points to 7.0, now deep in the danger zone.

Resilience eroded to 51.0. The absence of AI redundancy in critical services meant recovery was slow and uneven. Some regions restored operations within weeks; others faced months of manual triage. The attack exposed a fatal gap: the Union could build AI, but not defend it.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` dipped to 12.0, as the capability gap widened and no sovereign compute or access gains offset the decay. Political capital held at 14.0 — spared further loss only by inaction, but now frozen by it.

Public sentiment fell sharply to 19.0. Protests erupted outside data centres, and families of delayed patients demanded accountability. Labour unrest simmered as public sector workers feared algorithmic replacement. The only bright spot — AI tutoring in schools — was drowned out by headlines of medical harm.

Brussels remained silent. The European Council’s special session loomed, but with no audit findings and no consensus, the pause deepened into paralysis.
