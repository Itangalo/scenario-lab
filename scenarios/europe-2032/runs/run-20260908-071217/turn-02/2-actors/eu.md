## In practice  
We are acting on the growing gap between accelerating AI capabilities and lagging global safety standards by transforming the scientific coalition’s call into a formal diplomatic initiative. The European Commission, through the High Representative and DG CONNECT, is drafting a summit concept paper that proposes binding pre-commitments: participating states and labs must submit to third-party evaluations of their most capable models and agree to a common incident reporting mechanism before attending. We are leveraging the EU’s market power and regulatory gravity to make attendance a de facto condition for access to EU digital markets and research partnerships.

The summit will be co-hosted with France and Germany, with technical groundwork laid by a new task force at ENISA and the European AI Office, supported by €300 million from Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme. We are engaging the US and UK bilaterally to secure alignment, while offering China and others observer status with opt-in technical cooperation. This is not symbolic diplomacy — it is an attempt to create a new baseline of accountability, using the EU’s leverage not to dictate, but to convene and condition.
