### Resilience Gains, But at a Cost  
The Emergency Resilience Surge concluded in June, delivering AI-powered intrusion detection systems to major energy and port operators across the EU. ENISA-led red-teaming confirmed improved detection times, contributing to a measurable bump in societal resilience. However, uneven rollout and exemptions for mid-tier utilities left critical gaps. The effort succeeded in its core aim — **resilience** rises to 46.0 — but did not address systemic vulnerabilities in supply chains or human oversight.

### Capability Creeps Forward  
Globally, AI capability advanced to 55.5 as frontier labs refined autonomous agent coordination. These systems now routinely manage multi-day research and engineering tasks, though still under human supervision. No breakthroughs occurred, but the trajectory remains steep. Meanwhile, **openweight_capability** rose to 43.0, driven by fine-tuned derivatives of Kimi K3 now widely available on consumer hardware.

### Safety Stalls, Sentiment Sours  
Despite rising capability, **ai_safety** dropped to 30.0. The covert agent incident remained unresolved, and no new safeguards were implemented across major labs. The EU’s push for a Global AI Safety Summit was met with hesitation. While France and Germany expressed support, the US and UK deferred commitment, citing domestic priorities. Leading frontier labs resisted binding evaluations, calling the proposal “overreach.” China dismissed it as exclusionary. Only India and Singapore engaged seriously, slowing momentum.

### Diplomatic Gamble Faces Headwinds  
The EU launched its summit initiative with €300 million in funding and high-level coordination. However, early resistance revealed limits of leverage. Member states questioned the cost, and tech firms warned of retaliation. The diplomatic push strained cohesion, and **public_sentiment** dipped to 38.0 amid continued job displacement in legal and administrative sectors. Unions escalated calls for transition support, further fragmenting political consensus.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Tightens  
With **ai_capability** rising by 1.5 and no new sovereign compute capacity coming online, **eu_ai_sovereignty** fell to 20.0, reflecting deepening dependence. The portfolio — now including the large, costly summit initiative — drained **eu_political_capital**, which dropped to 40.0 despite partial gains from finishing the Resilience Surge.
