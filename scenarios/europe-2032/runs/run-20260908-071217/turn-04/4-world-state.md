### A Strategic Pivot Amid Rising Tensions  
The EU launches the Sovereign Agent Development Initiative, aiming to build trusted autonomous systems on domestic infrastructure. Framed as strategic insurance, the plan wins support from France, Germany, and the Netherlands, but faces resistance from smaller states wary of centralisation and private firms accusing the bloc of crowding out innovation. Legal debates over Article 185 TFEU delay full approval, though funding begins flowing to core labs under provisional agreements.

### Capability Accelerates  
Global AI capability rises to 59.0 as frontier labs deploy agents managing multi-week research and engineering cycles with minimal oversight. These systems now initiate debugging loops and refine experimental designs autonomously, marking a shift toward self-directed development. Openweight models reach 47.0, driven by fine-tuned Kimi K3 derivatives now capable of executing multi-hour research tasks with moderate supervision — a capability now widely distributed.

### Safety Erodes Further  
With no new safeguards implemented at leading labs and interpretability still absent from production systems, ai_safety drops to 26.0. The gap between capability and control widens, raising concerns among technical experts about unanticipated system behaviours.

### Resilience Edges Up  
The Labour Transition Accelerator begins delivering retraining vouchers and wage support at scale, easing local unrest and contributing to a +1 resilience gain. However, coverage gaps persist in smaller member states.

### Sovereignty and Capital Under Strain  
Despite the new sovereignty push, eu_ai_sovereignty falls to 17.0 as capability growth outpaces domestic build-out. Political capital drops to 29.0 under the weight of four large measures and growing public unease.

### Sentiment Turns Downward  
Public anxiety grows as campaign rhetoric frames AI as both an economic threat and a national security gamble. Sentiment dips to 38.0, reflecting rising scepticism despite visible efforts.
