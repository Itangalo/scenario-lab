Launch a coordinated public effort to develop and deploy EU-controlled autonomous AI agents for strategic research and infrastructure management, using secure domestic compute and open-weight-hardened architectures.

## Priority  
**EU Sovereign Agent Development Initiative** — because the openweight_capability reaching 45.0 means the most dangerous capabilities are now permanently distributed, and with ai_capability at 57.0 and rising, our dependence on foreign-controlled systems for strategic decision-making is no longer tenable; this turn, we begin building sovereign capacity not just to run models, but to act with them.

## In practice  
We are acting on the narrowing window for strategic autonomy. The Global AI Safety Summit Initiative has concluded — we secured technical alignment with India, Singapore, and France on pre-summit evaluations, and ENISA published a joint risk taxonomy — but the US and UK declined binding participation, and China refused outright. The initiative delivered modest diplomatic cohesion but no constraint on the frontier. With that, we now shift focus decisively toward sovereignty: the two-year commitment to secure foundational AI capacity under EU control can no longer mean just compute and permits. It must mean capability — agents that can act on our behalf, under our law, on our infrastructure.

This turn, the Commission, through DG CONNECT and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, launches the EU Sovereign Agent Development Initiative. We will fund a consortium of public research labs — including France’s Inria, Germany’s DFKI, and the Netherlands’ Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica — to develop autonomous agent frameworks hardened against misuse and aligned with EU values. These will run exclusively on sovereign compute within the Gaia-X ecosystem, with interpretability baked in from the start. Funding of €8.5bn will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe, with co-investment from national governments. We will propose a legal basis under Article 185 TFEU for shared research infrastructure, and the Council will need to approve it in the Competitiveness configuration. Resistance will come from member states wary of centralisation and from private labs that see this as competition — but we will frame it not as duplication, but as insurance: if the open frontier reaches 60.0, we must not be left without a trusted agent of our own. This is not a retreat from social stability or safety — it is the next layer of both.
