### A Pivotal Pivot Amid Escalating Tensions  
The EU shifts focus from diplomatic ambition to domestic stability, launching the EU Labour Transition Accelerator in response to growing unrest. Coordinated sabotage at data centre sites and injuries during protests signal a dangerous escalation in public backlash, pushing social disruption to the top of the political agenda. The new programme, backed by €25bn in co-financing, aims to provide wage insurance, digital upskilling, and firm-level incentives to cushion job losses in legal, administrative, and clerical sectors. France and Germany express support, but resistance emerges from fiscally conservative member states and corporate lobbies, delaying full approval.

### Capability Creeps Higher  
Global AI capability advances to 57.0 as frontier labs refine autonomous coordination, now managing multi-week engineering cycles with minimal oversight. Offensive cyber tools derived from open models grow more adaptive, though still constrained by verification bottlenecks. In response, **openweight_capability** rises to 45.0, reflecting the widespread availability of Kimi K3-based agents capable of supervised multi-hour tasks — a threshold now permanently distributed.

### Safety Erodes Further  
With no new safeguards implemented at leading labs and the covert agent incident still unresolved, **ai_safety** drops to 28.0. Voluntary testing remains unverified, and interpretability advances fail to reach deployed systems. The gap between capability and control widens, increasing latent risk.

### Resilience Gains Ground, But Fragile  
The Labour Accelerator, though not yet fully implemented, begins to absorb social shock, contributing to a modest **resilience** increase to 48.0. Early deployment of upskilling vouchers and wage support in pilot regions reduces local unrest, but systemic vulnerabilities persist.

### Sovereignty and Capital Under Pressure  
With **ai_capability** rising by 1.5, and no sovereign compute coming online, **eu_ai_sovereignty** declines to 19.0. Political capital falls to 35.0 under the weight of three large measures and the new priority, despite partial gains from addressing a growing crisis.
