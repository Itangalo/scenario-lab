## In practice  
We are acting on the widening crisis of public legitimacy. The Global AI Safety Summit Initiative continues under the High Representative’s office, with ENISA and the European AI Office preparing evaluation templates and outreach to India and Singapore to solidify a coalition of middle powers. But the ground is shifting beneath us: the backlash_physical events — occupations, grid sabotage, injuries — are no longer isolated protests but coordinated disruptions targeting the very infrastructure we are trying to protect. Policing them drains resources and deepens resentment. We cannot harden ports against AI-enabled intrusion while failing to shield citizens from AI-enabled dislocation.

So this turn, we pivot to social resilience. The European Commission, through DG EMPL and DG REGIO, is drafting the EU Labour Transition Accelerator, a €25bn programme co-financed by member states and firms that automate at scale. It offers wage insurance for displaced workers for up to 18 months, fast-tracked access to digital upskilling vouchers, and incentives for firms to redeploy rather than replace. We will propose it under the Social Climate Fund and the European Social Fund Plus, with activation tied to national AI adoption reporting. The Council of Ministers will need to approve it in ECOFIN, but we are already engaging France and Germany to pre-clear the design, while offering Poland and Hungary targeted cohesion top-ups to secure buy-in. Resistance will come from fiscally hawkish states and from firms that see automation as a cost-saving tool without social cost — but we will frame this not as redistribution, but as risk mitigation: a stable transition is cheaper than a revolt. This is not a retreat from sovereignty or safety — it is the ground on which they must stand.
