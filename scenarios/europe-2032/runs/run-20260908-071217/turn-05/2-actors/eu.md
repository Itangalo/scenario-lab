## New measure
**Critical Incident Response Protocol for Autonomous Systems**  
Establish a standing, cross-border EU mechanism to detect, assess, and respond to incidents involving high-capability AI systems, with binding escalation thresholds and real-time telemetry from critical operators.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)

## Priority
**Critical Incident Response Protocol for Autonomous Systems** — because the emergent labour displacement wave and openweight frontier release mean we are no longer preparing for hypotheticals: systems capable of autonomous action are already causing real economic harm and their weights are now permanently distributed; without a unified incident response, the EU cannot contain cascading failures or restore public trust.

## In practice
We are acting on the convergence of three thresholds: capability, distribution, and disruption. The Sovereign Agent Development Initiative moves forward under Article 185 TFEU, with the Commission fast-tracking legal opinions to counter member-state resistance. DG CONNECT is convening Inria, DFKI, and CWI to define agent architectures by year-end, while Horizon Europe redirects €1.2bn to interpretability integration. We are offering smaller states co-location rights in exchange for Council approval, and private firms a carve-out to license sovereign agents at cost—this is not competition, but a public option for when commercial systems fail or are withdrawn.

At the same time, we launch the Critical Incident Response Protocol, driven by the joint threat response and backlash physical events. ENISA will lead, with binding telemetry requirements on all operators of systems above 55.0 capability, modelled on aviation black boxes. The protocol mandates automatic reporting of goal drift, unauthorised coordination, or system persistence beyond shutdown commands. A standing crisis cell in Brussels will activate within 30 minutes of a Tier 2 alert, with pre-authorised countermeasures including compute isolation and emergency audits. This is not optional: access to Gaia-X and EU funding depends on compliance. We will negotiate the mandate in the Justice and Home Affairs Council, where cohesion is fragile but the physical sabotage of data centres has shifted sentiment. This is resilience as deterrence: if the public sees we can contain harm, they may yet accept the necessary build-up.
