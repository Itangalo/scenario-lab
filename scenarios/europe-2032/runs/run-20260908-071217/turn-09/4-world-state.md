### Integration Under Fire

The EU’s final push to embed open-weight AI across critical systems collided with cascading crises. In late October, a coordinated ransomware sweep — model-generated, adaptive, and targeting hospital triage software — disrupted emergency services in five member states. ENISA-AI confirmed the attack exploited validation gaps in newly deployed models, some of which had passed certification under relaxed emergency protocols. The **cyber_major_incident** exposed brittle defences, eroding trust just as integration deadlines loomed.

Public backlash intensified. As audits revealed inconsistent training and patchwork validation, **backlash_physical** erupted: activists occupied two data centres supplying emergency logistics AI, cutting grid links. Police intervention followed, drawing condemnation and amplifying narratives of AI as both fragile and oppressive. Meanwhile, **adoption_success** offered rare good news — school districts using EU-developed tutoring agents cut remediation delays by 40% — but gains were overshadowed by disruption.

The Emergency Openweight Integration Directive concluded under duress. While legally fulfilled, uneven implementation undermined credibility. The Openweight Operationalisation Taskforce began operations at DG CNECT, but arrived too late to prevent fragmentation. No new measure was proposed; leaders awaited audit results before committing further political capital.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Dwindles

With one large measure completed, portfolio charge dropped from −7 to −4. However, the **cyber_major_incident** and **backlash_physical** constituted internal-origin negative events, each draining political capital. Combined with stagnant delivery and rising public anger, `eu_political_capital` fell to 14.0. `public_sentiment` dipped to 24.0 amid coverage of injuries and service failures.

`resilience` declined by 2 as capability advanced without matching safeguards. `ai_safety` fell to 14.0 due to opaque reasoning in breached systems and no landed improvements. `eu_ai_sovereignty` dropped to 14.0: despite the directive’s completion (+3), the −1 decay from capability growth and a −1 event term for access vulnerability during the cyberattack outweighed gains.

`openweight_capability` rose modestly to 53.0, reflecting incremental diffusion. `ai_capability` increased by +1.5, within expected range.
