## New measure
**EU Compute Access Guarantee**  
Establish a legally binding mechanism to ensure uninterrupted access to frontier AI systems for EU institutions, critical public services, and licensed operators, even when foreign providers withdraw access.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: eu_ai_sovereignty +15 (by guaranteeing access through sovereign fallbacks and pre-positioned alternatives), resilience +5 (by preventing cascading failures in health, transport, and governance when external models are cut off)  
Applies to: EU institutions, member state ministries, public health providers, critical infrastructure operators, and licensed AI developers

## Priority
**EU Compute Access Guarantee** — because eu_frontier_access_denied has already landed: hospitals and ministries were cut off without warning, and the US now treats AI access as a lever of power. This is no longer about competitiveness or long-term ambition—it is about basic operational continuity. Without guaranteed access, every other measure fails when the plug is pulled.

## In practice
We are acting on the shock of being cut off. The eu_frontier_access_denied event was not a hypothetical—it disrupted real services, and it exposed a strategic vulnerability we can no longer paper over. The Sovereign Agent Development Initiative remains our long-term engine, but it is not yet delivering. So we are building a bridge: the EU Compute Access Guarantee will require pre-positioned sovereign models, hardened against openweight proliferation, ready to activate the moment external access is revoked. We will fund rapid replication of the most critical foreign models using open-weight equivalents at or above 58.0 capability, run on Gaia-X-linked clusters with priority access for health, justice, and emergency response. DG CONNECT and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking will lead, with Horizon Europe unlocking €800m in emergency replication grants this turn.

At the same time, we are pressing the Sovereign Agent Initiative through the Council. The legal opinion on Article 185 TFEU is now in—this is permitted as a matter of common interest—and we are offering Austria and Finland preferential access to agent testing in exchange for support. The private sector pushback remains, but we are narrowing the carve-out: firms can opt out of licensing sovereign agents if they offer equivalent continuity guarantees, audited annually. On the resilience front, we are embedding ENISA’s new telemetry mandates into the Critical Incident Protocol, but we are not waiting for unanimity: qualified majority voting will be invoked in Justice and Home Affairs, using the backlash_physical events as proof of urgency. This is not the full protocol yet—but it is a crisis cell with teeth, and it starts this turn.
