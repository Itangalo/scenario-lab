## In practice  
We are acting on the intrusion findings by mobilising the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) and the Health Security Committee’s crisis architecture as a template. Under Article 114 TFEU for internal market harmonisation, we are drafting a temporary Crisis Resilience Directive that sets mandatory AI-assisted intrusion detection thresholds for electricity transmission operators, major ports, and water utilities above a defined scale. This will be backed by a €12bn emergency fund from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused allocations and new contributions from member states in proportion to GDP. The fund covers sensor deployment, red-teaming, and cross-border incident simulations every quarter.

Resistance is expected from member states with weaker cyber posture and from energy firms citing cost and operational burden. We are offering 70% co-financing for compliance upgrades and fast-tracking access to InvestAI Gigafactory power allocations for utilities that meet the new benchmarks by Q2 2027. The Council’s Competitiveness configuration will be the lead, but we are preparing a blocking minority and may need to activate the emergency brake under Article 122a if consensus fails. This is not about building new capacity — that takes years — but about forcing alignment and readiness now, before the next probe becomes a breach.
