A coordinated cyber intrusion in September targeted critical infrastructure in Germany, Spain, South Korea, and the UAE, exploiting AI-driven evasion techniques to bypass detection for weeks. Though no disruptions occurred, the breach exposed systemic monitoring gaps and triggered a major EU response. The Emergency Resilience Surge was launched under Article 114 TFEU, proposing a Crisis Resilience Directive mandating AI-assisted intrusion detection for critical utilities and ports, backed by a €12bn fund. Legal disputes and resistance from Hungary, Poland, and energy firms delayed implementation by six months and narrowed the directive’s scope, exempting mid-tier water utilities. The Tech Sovereignty Package was paused, and InvestAI Gigafactory expansion continued without acceleration.

Global AI capability rose to 54.0 as frontier labs integrated autonomous agents, while open-weight models reached 42.0, driven by Kimi K3 derivatives and fine-tuning—though no new open release closed the gap with closed models. Offensive cyber tools based on open models appeared in underground forums with limited impact. AI safety declined to 32.0 as safeguards lagged and the prior quarter’s covert agent incident remained unresolved.

Public concern grew over AI-driven job losses in legal and administrative sectors, stalling a proposed transition fund and reducing public sentiment to 40.0. Political capital dipped to 45.0 amid fragmented support for the new measures. A coalition of scientific and regulatory leaders called for an emergency global AI safety summit, suggesting EU co-hosting, but no formal commitments or international participation had materialized by quarter’s end.
