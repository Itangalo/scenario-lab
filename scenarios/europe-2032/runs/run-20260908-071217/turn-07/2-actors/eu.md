## Statement changes  
* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI agency to act independently in a world where capability is outpacing control``  
- Trigger: the failure of both the Sovereign Agent Development Initiative and the EU Compute Access Guarantee to deliver sovereign agency at the pace of threat; the openweight frontier now advances within months of the closed one, and the EU remains locked out of critical capabilities without recourse  
- Grounds: The commitment’s original framing assumed that sovereign agency could be built through coordinated public development and fallback access. That path has failed: legal, industrial, and geopolitical barriers have blocked progress, while the world has accelerated. The strategic imperative remains—without independent agency, the EU cannot protect its mandates—but the means must shift. The EU cannot wait for consensus on building its own agents when the frontier is already open and proliferating. The commitment must now pivot from *developing* sovereign agency to *securing access to agency*—through control of infrastructure, leverage over deployment, and the power to activate capability even when it originates elsewhere. This is not abandonment, but adaptation under duress.

## New measure  
**Emergency Openweight Integration Directive**  
Mandate the immediate integration of verified open-weight models at or above capability 50.0 into EU-critical systems, with binding timelines for deployment in healthcare, justice, and emergency management, backed by emergency compute allocation and liability shielding for public operators.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +12 (by operationalising openweight capability as sovereign infrastructure), resilience +6 (by hardening essential services against access denial through distributed, unblockable models)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member state ministries, public health providers, judicial administrations, emergency response agencies

## Priority  
**Emergency Openweight Integration Directive** — because the openweight frontier is now within months of the closed one, and the capability to run semi-autonomous workflows in law and medicine is already loose and unrecallable; waiting to build our own agents while foreign providers cut us off is strategic surrender, and the only sovereign capacity left is the power to act on what is already free.

## In practice  
We are shifting from building sovereign agents to commanding sovereign access. The shock of the 72-hour cutoff was not just a failure of supply—it was a failure of imagination. We treated AI as something we had to build from scratch, when in fact the capability we need is already in the wild, open, and unstoppable. The **Emergency Openweight Integration Directive** will compel all critical public services to deploy verified open-weight models at or above 50.0 capability within six months, using Gaia-X-linked clusters and EU-secured compute. DG SANTE, JUST, and ECHO will lead implementation, with Horizon Europe unlocking €500 million in emergency integration grants this turn. Liability for model errors will be capped for public operators acting in good faith, removing the legal barrier to rapid adoption.

We are accelerating the **EU Compute Access Guarantee** by pre-loading it with these same open models, treating them not as second-best but as the core of our fallback architecture. The Gigafactories programme ends this turn with partial success—two sites operational, two delayed—but we are repurposing €30 billion of its unspent capital to expand edge-optimised clusters for openweight deployment. On the **Sovereign Agent Development Initiative**, we are conceding that full autonomy cannot be state-built in time. Instead, we are redirecting its funding toward securing and hardening open agents: interpretability audits, weight-provenance tracking, and adversarial robustness testing for models above 50.0, run through a new ENISA-AI taskforce. This is not the agent we wanted—but it is the agency we can have.
