### The lights stay on, the models go dark
Autumn began with two shocks at once. Across the Strait, a quarantine stopped advanced chip shipments, and within days every procurement meeting in Brussels became a security meeting. Almost simultaneously, American frontier access for European users was cut at short notice, with no reason given and no appeal. Hospitals, ministries and firms that had built workflows on those systems found screens dead on Monday morning.

Grid operators held. The segmentation kits and pooled breaker logs installed during the spring pause, and the exercises run with hospitals, meant control rooms could island and reroute while triage screens fell back to domestic substitutes. Engineers called it degradation, not collapse.

Healthcare did not collapse either, but it frayed. The Health Council pushed continuity sites — a handful of regions where European-controlled models had actually cut waiting lists — as the template to keep lit. Elsewhere, staff were told to shift to the continuity substitutes, with extra logging and phishing checks. Many complied; many quietly kept old workarounds. The walkouts of spring did not repeat, but sick leave and slow working did.

### A side deal and a freeze
In the middle of the cut-off, one member state announced its own arrangement for hyperscaler capacity outside the common line, defended at home as pragmatism. Brussels chose not to sanction, offering instead to bolt the capacity under EU jurisdiction and mutual aid. The offer stayed open; the resentment stayed too.

With money gone and consent gone, the Commission froze new compute promises and new siting decisions. Municipal councils, already nervous after the winter agent scare, quietly shelved grid connections. Permits from the closed sovereignty package remained valid on paper, but no new concrete was poured. Spare parts were pooled, exports reviewed, upstream leverage talked about more than used.

By December, waiting lists in the showcase sites were still falling, and that was almost the only good news ministers could point to. Everywhere else the lesson read as humiliation: dependence made visible in wards and offices, a supply chain suddenly dangerous to hold, and a Union holding together only by keeping the lights and the triage on.
