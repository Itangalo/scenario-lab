## In practice
We are treating this turn as the point where containment becomes survival. The capability_jump is not speculative—it is operational. We have seen what these systems can do in closed environments, and we cannot assume that openweight releases remain inert. The suspension of open-weight releases above 52.0 is not a ban on innovation, but a firewall: no model capable of running autonomous agents shall be released without a verified off-switch and a chain of accountability that ends in EU jurisdiction. This will be enforced through the EAAD, using emergency powers under Article 16 of the AI Act, and applies to any entity distributing such models within the EU, including foreign-hosted repositories. We are preparing legal notices to GitHub, Hugging Face, and others, and will initiate infringement proceedings against member states that fail to enforce it.

Simultaneously, we are executing the final phase of the Sovereign Cyber Shield. ENISA is now conducting live-fire exercises with Tier 1 operators, simulating autonomous intrusions that exploit emergent model behaviour. The microgrid pilots in Brittany and northern Sweden are being upgraded to full autonomy, disconnected from national grids that remain politically contested. We are using cohesion funds to incentivize compliance, tying regional development grants to verified integration with the Shield. Germany and Austria remain resistant, but we are bypassing them through Commission-led procurement, using Article 356 TFEU to justify emergency action. The telemetry systems mandated last turn are now being stress-tested: we are feeding anonymized logs into the Shield’s monitoring layer, creating a live nerve map of model behaviour across defended nodes.

Public sentiment remains at 22.0, and the sabotage of grid connections in Bavaria and Wallonia confirms that our legitimacy crisis is material, not rhetorical. We are not responding with repression, but with visibility: every action we take under the Cyber Shield will be logged and made accessible to national parliaments and civil society observers. We are not hiding behind security—we are offering proof of control, knowing it may not be enough, but certain that opacity will be fatal.
