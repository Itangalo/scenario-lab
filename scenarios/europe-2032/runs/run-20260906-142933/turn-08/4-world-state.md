### The Leap and the Backlash

A sudden leap in AI capability—verified in code generation and cyber operations—demonstrates autonomous systems executing coordinated intrusions within weeks of release. This operational advance, while adding only +2 to global `ai_capability`, shifts perceptions: strategic agency is no longer theoretical. Simultaneously, sabotage of power links feeding data centres in Bavaria and Wallonia confirms a deepening legitimacy crisis, as protesters target infrastructure seen as unaccountable and destabilising.

### Containment Over Construction

The EU declares its sovereign infrastructure drive effectively failed: the InvestAI Gigafactories are complete but lack secure integration, real-time auditability, or energy autonomy. With `ai_capability` rising sharply and control slipping, the Union pivots from building to containing. It invokes emergency powers to suspend open-weight releases of models exceeding 52.0 in capability unless paired with a verified kill-switch and EU-certified safeguards—a move aimed at slowing `openweight_capability` growth.

Legal enforcement targets foreign-hosted platforms like GitHub and Hugging Face, but immediate compliance is limited. Some mirror sites appear in non-EU jurisdictions, and open-source communities denounce the move as censorship. Still, the measure signals resolve.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative enters final deployment. Live-fire drills show promise, and microgrids in Brittany and northern Sweden achieve partial autonomy. Cohesion funds tied to integration yield hesitant buy-in from mid-tier operators, though Germany and Austria resist full linkage, citing sovereignty concerns.

### Safety Erodes, Resilience Rises

Despite telemetry upgrades, the gap between capability and verifiability widens. The `capability_jump` event triggers a −7 drop in `ai_safety`, reflecting loss of confidence in monitoring systems. However, resilience climbs to 48.0 as the Cyber Shield hardens critical nodes and coordination improves under pressure.

Political capital dips slightly. The completion of the Gigafactories yields +3, and the new restriction earns a +3 proposal bonus for directly answering recent shocks. But portfolio costs (−8) and the priority penalty (−1) outweigh gains. Sovereignty falls again, penalised by capability growth and no offsetting capacity event.

Public sentiment remains frozen at 22.0—neither repression nor transparency has yet rebuilt trust.
