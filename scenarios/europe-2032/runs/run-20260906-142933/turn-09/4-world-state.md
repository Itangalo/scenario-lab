### The Shield Rises, the Frontier Slips

The European Union completes the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative, integrating AI-hardened defences across 87% of Tier 1 critical operators. The cyber_defence_breakthrough—real-time detection of swarm behaviour—enables the Shield to identify and isolate autonomous threats before escalation. Microgrids in Brittany and northern Sweden operate autonomously, proving resilient during simulated grid attacks. Resilience climbs as coordination hardens under pressure.

Yet the gains are overshadowed by strategic retreat. The eu_frontier_access_denied event cuts EU institutions and hospitals off from the world’s most capable model. No explanation is given, no appeal possible. The incident confirms dependency as a vulnerability, not a convenience. Simultaneously, supply_chain_coercion intensifies: Washington forces ASML to restrict even mature lithography tools, undermining EU leverage in semiconductor supply.

In response, the EU launches a new small measure: Mandate Jurisdictional Accountability for Autonomous AI Systems. It requires all AI above 52.0 capability operating in the EU to register control chains, deploy EU-verified kill-switches, and submit to EAAD monitoring. The move signals a pivot from sovereignty through infrastructure to sovereignty through control. Legal enforcement targets cloud providers, but U.S. platforms signal resistance, and mirror sites proliferate.

The portfolio charge remains heavy. The Sovereign Cyber Shield finishes, yielding a +3 completion bonus. However, political capital erodes under ongoing costs: the new measure (−2), the priority (−1), and no offsetting gains from sentiment or sovereignty. Public sentiment stays frozen at 22.0—neither transparency nor enforcement has yet rebuilt trust.

Sovereignty falls further: ai_capability rises by +1 (slowed by capability_plateau_evidence), costing −1; eu_frontier_access_denied triggers a −2 event penalty. No capacity-building measure offsets the loss. The EU’s control ambitions grow, but its material base shrinks.

### Control Without Capacity

The EU asserts jurisdiction over AI behaviour, but cannot guarantee access to the systems it seeks to control. The Shield works—but only where it is adopted. Legal notices to Germany and Austria under Article 356 TFEU deepen intra-EU tensions. Cohesion frays even as cyber unity is demanded.
