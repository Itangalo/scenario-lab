## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure containment of autonomous AI systems through enforceable control mechanisms and jurisdictional accountability``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The previous commitment to build sovereign infrastructure with full operational control, energy autonomy, and real-time auditability has failed in practice: the InvestAI Gigafactories are complete but lack integration, oversight, and trust. The capability_jump event confirmed that autonomous systems can now execute strategic cyber operations, while eu_frontier_access_denied exposed our dependence on foreign-controlled models. We no longer face a deficit of compute or construction — we face a crisis of control. The shift from building to containing is now definitive. Our priority is no longer to match the frontier, but to ensure that no system operating within or against EU territory can act without accountability, verification, or recourse. This new commitment reflects that reality: sovereignty is not in the silicon, but in the ability to enforce limits on what systems can do and who answers when they go wrong.

## New measure

Require all AI systems operating in the EU with capability above 52.0 to register their operational control chain, deploy EU-verified kill-switches, and submit to real-time monitoring by the EAAD, with penalties for non-compliance including service suspension and asset seizure.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from failed sovereignty-through-infrastructure to sovereignty-through-control. The Sovereign Cyber Shield is now live: ENISA has completed integration with 87% of Tier 1 operators, and the microgrids in Brittany and northern Sweden are running autonomously, disconnected from contested national grids. We are using the cyber_defence_breakthrough—specifically, the new swarm-behaviour detection tooling—to upgrade the Shield’s monitoring layer, enabling it to flag emergent autonomous coordination in real time. Legal notices have been issued to Germany and Austria under Article 356 TFEU, warning that continued non-compliance will trigger Commission-led procurement overrides and the redirection of cohesion funds to compliant regions.

Simultaneously, the EAAD is preparing enforcement of the new jurisdictional accountability mandate. We are drafting implementing acts under Article 16 of the AI Act to require all systems above 52.0 capability—foreign or domestic—to register their control architecture, deploy EU-certified kill-switches, and allow telemetry access. Cloud providers like AWS, Azure, and Alibaba Cloud EU zones will be required to verify compliance or face service suspension. We are preparing for legal challenges, particularly from U.S.-based platforms, but the eu_frontier_access_denied incident has shifted internal opinion: cutting off hospitals and ministries without explanation is not a market failure—it is a strategic vulnerability. We will argue that if foreign powers can disable our systems at will, we have the right to demand control over those they leave running.

Public sentiment remains at 22.0, and the sabotage in Bavaria and Wallonia still echoes. We are not responding with repression, but with proof: every kill-switch activation, every detected intrusion, every audit log will be published in anonymized form through the EAAD transparency portal. We are offering not trust, but verification. And we are preparing for the next wave: if capability_plateau_evidence holds, we may have a window to consolidate control. If it does not, we will have already built the legal and technical spine to act.
