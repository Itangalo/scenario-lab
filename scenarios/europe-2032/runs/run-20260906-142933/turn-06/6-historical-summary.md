A major cyberattack exploiting a poisoned software update crippled energy systems and exposed millions of health records, exposing critical infrastructure vulnerabilities and triggering the EU's launch of the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative—a Category 6 response deploying AI-hardened defences for Tier 1 operators by turn 8. ENISA began distributing standardised defence stacks with swarm detection and zero-trust verification, funded by reprofiled EIB capital. However, integration with InvestAI Gigafactories remained unrealised, and legal obstacles persisted: Germany and Austria continued blocking grid allocations under the Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme, challenging Commission authority under Article 194 TFEU, leaving physical deployment fragmented. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) certified its first models, but real-time logging mandates failed in COREPER II due to opposition from Italy and Hungary, crippling auditability. Public distrust deepened after the breach, with retraining initiatives dismissed as inadequate and protests growing; messaging campaigns, including framing the Cyber Shield as a "visible shield," failed to restore confidence. Globally, the US inaugurated an anti-AI administration that imposed export controls, slowed frontier development, and introduced job guarantees, reshaping the international AI landscape. AI capability reached 60.0, with open-weight models at 48.5, supported by expanded EU access to non-US frontier systems.
