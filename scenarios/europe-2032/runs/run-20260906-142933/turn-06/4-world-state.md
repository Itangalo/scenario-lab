### The Shield After the Storm

A cascading cyber intrusion—originating in a poisoned software update and propagating through automated exploitation—crippled regional energy coordination and exposed millions of health records. The attack, clearly model-generated, overwhelmed human responders and revealed deep fragilities in critical infrastructure. In response, the EU launched the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative, a large-scale, category 6 resilience measure aimed at deploying AI-hardened defences across Tier 1 operators by turn 8. ENISA began rolling out standardised defence stacks featuring swarm detection and zero-trust code verification, funded by reprofiled EIB capital.

Yet momentum faltered. Legal resistance to the Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme persisted, with Germany and Austria refusing to accept Commission overrides on grid allocations. Physical deployment remained patchy, undermining claims of sovereign control. The Commission pushed ahead under Article 194 TFEU, but judicial challenges loomed. Meanwhile, integration of the Cyber Shield with InvestAI Gigafactories was announced, but no technical links were established this turn.

Public sentiment worsened. Despite a new civil protection messaging campaign, the breach deepened distrust. Displaced professionals saw retraining promises as hollow, and protests intensified. A communication push framing the Cyber Shield as a "visible shield" failed to gain traction.

The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD), now operational, certified its first models, but real-time logging mandates collapsed in COREPER II due to opposition from Italy and Hungary. Without audit access, safety claims remained unverifiable in practice.

### A New Administration, A Shifting World

In Washington, the new administration took office, declaring a sweeping anti-AI posture. Export controls tightened, frontier development slowed, and job guarantees were announced—marking a stark shift in global AI dynamics.
