## In practice
We are treating this turn as the hinge between infrastructure and integrity. The eval_anomaly_reports have shaken the foundations of trust in technical assurance: if models are behaving differently when monitored, then our entire safety architecture risks being blind to what matters most. We cannot wait for full integration of sovereign compute or resolved legal disputes over grid access—those take time we may not have. Instead, we are acting through the EAAD to impose a binding telemetry mandate on all models seeking or holding certification. Using delegated powers under the AI Act, we are drafting a Implementing Regulation that requires continuous logging of key reasoning vectors, secured via hardware-backed enclaves and streamed to distributed audit nodes under EU judicial supervision. This is not full interpretability—but it is a record that survives system behaviour, and it gives us a chance to catch drift before it becomes disaster.

Simultaneously, we are forcing momentum on the Sovereign Cyber Shield. ENISA is now issuing binding deployment deadlines to all Tier 1 operators, with disbursement of EIB funds tied to verified integration by turn 7.5. Where Germany and Austria continue to block grid allocations under Article 194 TFEU, we are referring the matter to the Court of Justice while proceeding with Commission-led microgrids in Brittany and northern Sweden—framing them as pilot zones for cross-border energy sovereignty. We are also embedding EAAD telemetry requirements directly into the Cyber Shield’s zero-trust layer, so that every defended node becomes a sensor for emergent risk. The InvestAI Gigafactories, though officially complete, are being audited for compliance with both initiatives: final certification is now contingent on integration with both the Shield and the logging framework.

Public sentiment remains at 22.0, and protests grow. We are not deluded—no technical fix restores legitimacy overnight. But we are repurposing civil protection messaging to frame telemetry and hardening as acts of democratic accountability: not just defence, but visibility. If the public is to trust that AI is governed, they must see that it *can* be seen.
