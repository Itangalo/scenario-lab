### Cut off
The cutoff notice arrived as a helpdesk error. Hospital procurement officers, ministry staff, contractors running grid dispatch found the leading American model refusing European credentials, with no reason and no appeal. Within days clinics that had built triage and imaging pipelines on it were back to older, smaller systems. Washington offered no explanation.

Brussels did not contest it. With no leverage over laboratories now run as federal assets, the Commission formalised what utilities and hospitals were already doing: clean openly-available images, paper routines, hand drills, diesel and spares routed by civil-protection channels, quiet messages to belligerents to keep cables and depots off target lists.

### The enclave holds, barely
The continuity protocol that finished in autumn gave that improvisation a budget line. Night-dark town halls stayed open, prescriptions stayed on paper, grid crews kept frequency by manual procedure. Services degraded but did not stop — which the public read as confirmation that degradation was now policy.

That enclave itself came under pressure. Coordinated occupations blocked data-centre building sites and fuel depots in several states, halting construction and delaying backup deliveries. Police cleared some sites; others were left occupied to avoid confrontation. Single-source rumours of sabotage never quite resolved.

A new interpretability control technique was published abroad and circulated in journals. With no capacity to mandate adoption, uptake in Europe was patchy and voluntary — some contractor and hospital teams tried it where they could, catching some erratic agent behaviour before it spread. Assurance improved only marginally, far behind capability. No new EU enforcement was attempted this turn.

### Hands from elsewhere
The other shock came on the factory floor. Cheap capable robots, built in China on American control models, reached commercial sale in Europe. Logistics, then assembly, then mine clearance variants, with armed versions demonstrated abroad within months. Their weights and integration code circulated outside any EU programme, but European teams lacked the access, compute and coordination to fully absorb or replicate them, so openly-available capability usable in Europe rose only slightly even as frontier access narrowed. No retraining scheme could run faster than dexterity following cognition. Wage-insurance cheques continued, without jobs to convert into.

Cures from tailored therapies still arrived in clinics, now visibly dependent on models Europe could no longer access. Gratitude and humiliation ran together. With services degraded and no political dividend from continuity work, the Commission gained no new standing this turn.
