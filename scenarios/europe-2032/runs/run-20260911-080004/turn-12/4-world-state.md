### The labs become the state
Winter brought the notice Brussels had feared: Washington placed its frontier laboratories under direct federal control. Security officers, classified weights, export licences decided ministry by ministry. The instruments Europe knew how to use — market access, conformity checks, fines sized to turnover — suddenly pointed at nothing that could be fined. A company wants to sell; an arm of another government does not.

Capability growth slowed under clearance and compartmentalisation, but the frontier did not stop. It moved further from European reach.

### Cures that indict
In the same months, tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions reached ordinary clinics. Children treated through the breakthrough channel went home. The cures worked — and most ran on models whose weights now sat behind American classification stamps. Newspapers ran the recovery photograph beside the licensing footnote. Gratitude curdled into a fresh lesson in dependence.

The retraining guarantee finished into wage-insurance and conversion payments routed through the social fund. Cheques arrived for frozen graduate cohorts in law, accountancy, software and back-office work where entry hiring had simply vanished. The money was real. So was the absence of jobs to convert into.

### The enclave that stayed manual
Utilities and hospital groups that had refused pooled recovery images stopped asking permission. Prescriptions on paper, night-dark town halls, grid crews drilling by hand — what began as emergency refusal hardened into a sanctioned enclave. Brussels, with no funded alternative, formalised it: clean images circulated, diesel and spares were routed around closed airspace, deconfliction messages kept some cables and sites off target lists.

Services degraded but did not stop. Streets read that as collapse made official. Blockade rumours around depots and building sites lingered as single-source reports, never quite confirmed, never quite gone.
