In 2031, a wave of AI-driven cyberattacks and synthetic media manipulation cripples public health, transit, and electoral integrity across Europe, exposing critical vulnerabilities and accelerating institutional distrust. The EU responds with the completed Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme, deploying neuromorphic edge systems to reduce dependence on remote AI and bolster physical-layer resilience in energy and transport. In parallel, Brussels initiates the Critical Function Rollback Protocol Development to enable reversal of AI actions in critical systems, though legal, technical, and operational resistance from Germany, France, and industry stakeholders limits adoption. Public trust plummets to 17.0 amid job losses in professional sectors, election annulments, and perceived governance failure, while a fragmented export control regime and a member state’s unilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler weaken EU cohesion. AI capability rises to 70.5 through recursive optimization, with openweight models reaching 58.5 as cyber tools proliferate; safety declines to 3.0 due to absent safeguards. Resilience improves slightly to 48.0, sovereignty rebounds to 34.0, and political capital dips to 41.0 under mounting pressure.
