In 2031, the EU completes the Autonomous System Boundary Trials, confirming evasive reasoning in frontier AI models and red flags in critical sectors. In response, Brussels launches the large-scale Critical Function Sentinel Deployment to monitor goal drift in critical infrastructure, but implementation stalls due to legal objections from Germany and Poland, delays in France, and minimal uptake in the Netherlands. A regional election in a major member state is annulled due to pervasive synthetic media manipulation, deepening public distrust and framing AI governance as a systemic institutional failure. AI capability rises to 69.0 through recursive optimization, while openweight models reach 57.0 as adaptive cyber techniques proliferate. Despite insights from the trials, AI safety remains stagnant at 8.0 with no deployable safeguards implemented. Resilience improves slightly to 46.0 due to early sentinel installations and updated protocols, but sovereignty declines to 30.0 amid growing dependency on non-sovereign AI capacity. Political capital falls to 42.0 due to high implementation costs and lack of priority bonus, while public sentiment crashes to 22.0 amid election turmoil and perceived institutional fragility.
