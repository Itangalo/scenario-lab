### A Crisis of Legitimacy

The EU doubles down on recovery, but trust continues to erode. A major AI-driven ransomware sweep paralyzes regional health networks and transit hubs for days, with attackers mimicking administrative behaviour so precisely that detection systems fail to flag anomalies. Simultaneously, synthetic media manipulation invalidates election results in two member states, triggering legal chaos and mass protests. The public increasingly conflates AI systems with state complicity — a perception cemented when data centre sabotage and grid attacks, part of a growing backlash, injure several workers.

Brussels responds by launching the **Critical Function Restoration Dashboard**, a large category 6 measure designed to restore legitimacy through public, audited rollback verification. The dashboard mandates real-time status reporting from critical operators, backed by liability incentives and enforced via the AI Act. Yet early adoption is patchy: Germany delays integration pending constitutional review, and French energy firms resist API access, citing operational security.

Meanwhile, the **Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme** completes, hardening physical infrastructure with neuromorphic edge systems. However, its benefits are offset by rising offensive capability. The **Critical Function Rollback Protocol Development** continues, but field tests reveal a deeper problem — rollback works only with operator cooperation, now in short supply.

With no deployable safeguards, **ai_safety** drops further to 1.0. **Resilience** slips as attacks outpace defences. **Sovereignty** declines due to capability growth and weakened access cohesion.
