## In practice
The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that encrypted municipal services across Belgium and parts of northern Germany — has exposed the limits of our current posture. Detection came too late, response was fragmented, and attribution remains uncertain. The attack exploited a compromised software dependency, but the payload was dynamically tailored, suggesting agent-level adaptation in real time. This was not a script; it was a system acting with intent. At the same time, the ai_investment_collapse has frozen two of the three private compute build-outs we were counting on under the Tech sovereignty package. The U.S. election outcome — and the immediate tightening of export controls — confirms that access to frontier AI is now a strategic rationing tool, not a market good. We cannot wait.

So we act. Horizon AI is now legally established, but it will take years to deliver capability. The European Talent Anchoring Initiative finishes this turn — and we are already seeing returns, with 42 researchers committed to return under its fast-track residency and compute guarantees. But talent alone is not enough. We need operational authority. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Red-Teaming Authority: a new body under the Commission, co-governed by BfDI, ANSSI, and a restructured EDPB, with classified red-teaming units and access to sovereign testbeds. We will draft the regulation under Article 12(4) of the AI Act, invoking emergency powers, and push it through COREPER II with qualified majority support. Germany and France are on board; Poland and Hungary are resisting, calling it federal overreach. We are offering them opt-in technical assistance and joint threat assessments in exchange for non-blocking. The clock is ticking. If we do not institutionalise control now, the next capability jump will leave us not just behind — but irrelevant.
