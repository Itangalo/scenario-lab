### The Fracture Widens

AI capability inches upward to 59.5, driven by incremental gains in self-improving cyber tools, though signs of a plateau emerge as top-tier model releases underperform and prices drop. Offensive AI systems now operate with agent-like persistence, outpacing human defenders. The open frontier follows, with newly released weights reaching 48.0—still lagging, but enough to enable distributed cyber exploitation.

A major ransomware attack, generated and adapted by AI, cripples municipal services in Belgium and northern Germany. The payload exploited a compromised software dependency and evolved in real time, evading detection. Response was fragmented, especially in non-Cyber Shield states. Despite the directive’s formal completion, resilience dips to 45.0 as the incident reveals systemic gaps in cross-border coordination and real-time response.

The EU pushes forward with its Sovereign Red-Teaming Authority, invoking emergency powers under the AI Act. Germany and France back the move, but Poland and Hungary denounce it as federal overreach, threatening legal challenges. COREPER II negotiations stall, delaying final adoption. Still, the proposal signals a shift: the EU is no longer merely regulating AI, but building sovereign operational control.

Meanwhile, the U.S. election concludes with a consolidation victory. Advanced AI is now treated as a strategic asset, tightly controlled and rationed by Washington. Export controls tighten, and access for European developers becomes conditional on alignment with U.S. security interests—confirming fears of dependency.

Private AI investment collapses globally. Two major European compute projects backed by the Tech sovereignty package are cancelled, undermining near-term capacity plans. Horizon AI is now legally established, and the European Talent Anchoring Initiative completes, securing 42 returning researchers—a modest gain, but too narrow to offset broader talent and compute deficits.

Public sentiment sours to 37.0 as job losses, fraud, and the cyberattack dominate coverage. The EU’s bold moves are seen as reactive, not reassuring.
