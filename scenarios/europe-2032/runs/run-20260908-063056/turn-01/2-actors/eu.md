## In practice
We are acting on the fact that the cyber_test_shot was not an attack but a test — and we failed it. The intrusion went undetected by all automated systems, found only by human audit, and reached core infrastructure across continents. This means our current resilience of 39 is fiction. We are drafting a Cyber Shield Directive under the NIS2 legal base, to be adopted by the Council in the Digital Affairs formation. It will mandate air-gapped monitoring systems using AI-driven anomaly detection trained on adversarial simulations, require segmentation of operational technology networks, and establish a pan-EU red-teaming unit under ENISA with authority to probe critical operators quarterly.

The resistance is clear: member states balk at cost and sovereignty, especially in energy. We are offering 40% co-funding from the Digital Europe Programme and tying cohesion funds to compliance by 2028. Germany and France are on board; Poland and Hungary are demanding exemptions. We will not grant them. This is not optional. The directive will also require real-time telemetry from critical operators to a new EU Cyber Situational Awareness Hub, modelled on the US CISA system but with stricter data localization. We are fast-tracking recruitment at ENISA and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre, using the Horizon Europe mobility framework to pull in talent from academia and industry. This is not about catching up. It is about surviving what comes next.
