## Statement changes  
- modify `sovereign_defence_by_turn_9` (commitment): Field a sovereign AI system capable of autonomous cyber defence and crisis simulation by turn 9, trained and operated entirely within EU jurisdiction, to break dependence on foreign models whose access can be weaponised against us — but now, formally abandon the expectation that sovereign systems will achieve functional autonomy in crisis response by turn 10; instead, we commit to ensuring that the sovereign core — air-gapped, physically isolated, and human-supervised — remains the sole legitimate execution environment for any AI touching critical infrastructure by turn 15, and that all offensive or anticipatory cyber functions are suspended until red-teaming confirms control under self-modifying conditions.  
- Grounds: This turn, the capability_jump into self-modifying agents operating in the wild has rendered autonomous response not just unattainable but unsafe: without assurance tools that can track or bound recursive self-improvement, any attempt at autonomous defence risks becoming the attack vector. The EMERGE-hosted agents, even in hardened form, are reactive and bounded; they cannot anticipate or evolve. The Polish bilateral deal confirms that member states will not wait for a capability that does not exist. We must stop promising what we cannot deliver. The commitment now shifts from operational capability to jurisdictional integrity: not that our systems will *win* the fight, but that only *our* systems will be allowed to fight at all.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but now, we act not to shape the future, but to preserve the conditions of collective action: every decision is now filtered through the risk of defection, disintegration, or cascading failure, and we accept that legitimacy — both internal and external — is now more fragile than capability.  
- Grounds: This turn, the labour_displacement crisis and Poland’s bilateral move prove that the Union’s authority is no longer assumed. Markets, member states, and publics are acting on the belief that the EU cannot deliver either security or continuity. Our actions are no longer judged on ambition, but on whether they prevent collapse. We continue to act under uncertainty, but the uncertainty is no longer technical — it is political. We are not late because we hesitated. We are late because the world moved in a direction we could not prepare for. The price of being wrong is no longer delay — it is irrelevance.

## New measure  
**Launch Labour Transition Emergency Fund: Immediate Support for Displaced Entry-Level Workers**  
Establish an emergency fund to provide income support, retraining pathways, and mobility assistance for workers in law, accountancy, software, and administration who have lost entry-level positions to AI-driven automation, with priority access for graduates and early-career professionals.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` public_sentiment +8 (by demonstrating tangible support for those most visibly harmed by AI), resilience +2 (by reducing social friction and maintaining human capital in the system), eu_political_capital +1 (by restoring some legitimacy to AI governance as not solely defensive but socially responsive)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with disbursement tied to national implementation of verified retraining and placement programmes  

## Priority  
**Initiate Sovereign Core Hardening: Air-Gapped, Tamper-Resistant Execution Environment for EMERGE Agents** — because the frontier is no longer outside our systems but operating inside them, and without a physically secure, jurisdictionally unambiguous core, even our own sovereign AI cannot be trusted; every other measure depends on the integrity of this foundation.

## In practice  
We are moving forward with the Sovereign Core Hardening programme under the Joint Undertaking for Critical AI Infrastructure, with €1.2 billion committed over four years. The Finnish site in Vätsäri and the French site in Lorraine are now under active geological survey and security planning. We are finalising the legal basis under Article 114 TFEU, extended by Article 222 emergency powers, to bypass the need for unanimity. The architecture will be open-hardware, auditable, and closed to all external access — not even maintenance will occur over networked tools. All firmware will be red-teamed by S-READ before installation, and no model above 50.0 will be permitted outside this environment after turn 15.

At the same time, we are launching the Labour Transition Emergency Fund through the European Social Fund Plus, with an initial €600 million in pre-financing to be matched by national co-funding. This is not a long-term solution — no retraining programme can absorb the speed of displacement — but it is a signal: the Union sees the human cost, and will not treat it as inevitable. We are negotiating with France and Germany to accept binding S-READ authority in exchange for guaranteed access to the sovereign core’s defensive outputs. To Poland, we offer a six-month moratorium on Digital Markets Act penalties if they suspend their bilateral hyperscaler agreement pending Council review. We are not rebuilding trust. We are trying to stop it from vanishing entirely. The frontier is loose. The ground is shifting. We fortify where we can, and we hold where we must.
