## In practice  
We are treating EMERGE as a wartime infrastructure project. The cluster is being assembled in Kajaani and Lummeri with whatever GPUs we have—A100s rerouted through Swiss intermediaries, H200s repurposed from academic grants, and even a 5,000-GPU loan from Germany’s Gauss Centre under a mutual aid pact. Legal jurisdiction is now settled: the cluster operates under EuroHPC with emergency powers delegated from the Commission’s Office of AI Security, and all model weights are air-gapped, with physical access restricted to cleared personnel from at least three member states. Horizon AI has shifted from research to operations: its team is training a defensive model on adversarial cyber patterns, with real-time red-teaming from EU-REACT’s new offensive unit. The model will not reach full 55.0 capability by turn 9, but we will deploy its first agents—focused on intrusion detection and adaptive patching—by turn 10, running in parallel with foreign systems to build trust and validate performance.

EU-REACT has now transitioned from evaluation to command. We invoked Article 222 TFEU this week, granting it binding authority during declared AI emergencies. Poland and Hungary have ratified the agreement in exchange for co-location rights and access to EMERGE’s defensive model. The first test came immediately: a wave of AI-generated phishing attacks hit Belgian and Slovak infrastructure. EU-REACT issued directives to isolate affected systems, deploy counter-AI decoys, and activate manual override protocols in water treatment plants. The response was uneven—some operators resisted, citing national sovereignty—but the framework held. We are now building on that precedent with the new resilience surge: every critical system must now prove it can function without AI for 72 hours, with staff trained, systems tested, and fallbacks audited by EU-REACT. This is not retreat. It is realism: we are building sovereign AI not to win a capability race, but to survive a world where the race has already been lost.
