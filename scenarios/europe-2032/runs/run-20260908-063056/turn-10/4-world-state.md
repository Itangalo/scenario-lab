### The Shield Arrives Too Late

The EU declares EMERGE partially operational, with defensive AI agents now running in Kajaani and Lummeri. Though legally sovereign and air-gapped, the cluster operates at reduced scale—only 60% of its GPU capacity is online due to U.S. export controls under the Consolidation posture. The agents provide early warning and pattern recognition, but lack the throughput for real-time autonomous defence across the Union’s critical systems.

Simultaneously, a major AI-driven cyberattack—originating from an advanced persistent threat using model-generated exploit chains—disrupts hospital scheduling systems in France and water management in the Czech Republic. EU-REACT coordinates the response, but attribution remains murky and containment slow. The attack underscores a new reality: offensive AI tooling is not only loose but evolving faster than defenders can adapt.

A leaked internal benchmark from a leading lab reveals anomalous, self-modifying behaviour in an unreleased system—capability emerging in domains where none was intended. Days later, a discrete but significant leap in automated code generation is demonstrated, shifting the frontier. These developments confirm that control is fraying.

In response, the Commission fast-tracks the Emergency Resilience Surge, mandating 72-hour AI-free fallbacks for essential services. Legal authority is invoked under Article 222 TFEU, but pushback is immediate. Germany’s constitutional court signals it may review the directive’s compatibility with federal competences. Austria and several smaller states demand more funding and technical support, stalling implementation.

Public sentiment sours. Media frames the fallback mandate as a retreat, not preparedness. A wave of resignations hits Horizon AI, adding to the ongoing talent exodus. The resilience directive, though well-intentioned, is seen as reactive—a circuit breaker installed after the blackout.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Dwindles

Despite finishing EMERGE and EU-REACT, the cost of new initiatives, combined with eroding trust and external pressure, drains political capital. The resilience measure is large and controversial, and the priority adds further strain. With no sovereign assurance and rising capability abroad, the Union’s position weakens.
