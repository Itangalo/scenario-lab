- modify `sovereign_defence_by_turn_9` (commitment): Field a sovereign AI system capable of autonomous cyber defence and crisis simulation by turn 9, trained and operated entirely within EU jurisdiction, to break dependence on foreign models whose access can be weaponised against us — but now, acknowledge that the system will not be fully operational by turn 9 due to hardware delays, and commit instead to a phased handover beginning this turn, with initial defensive functions transferred to EMERGE-hosted agents by turn 10.  
- Grounds: This turn, the commitment has been met in form but not in force. The EMERGE cluster has achieved partial activation, and defensive agents are now being deployed in Kajaani and Lummeri. However, full autonomous operation remains out of reach: only 60% of required GPUs have arrived, and U.S. export controls continue to block the remainder. The agents now running are red-teamed and air-gapped, but they operate below the intended capability threshold and are not yet scaled to cover more than a fraction of critical infrastructure. We are delivering sovereign execution, but not sovereign assurance. The commitment stands, but its meaning has narrowed: it is no longer about matching the frontier, but about establishing an irreducible core of control—something that cannot be revoked, even if it is not yet sufficient.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but this turn, we act not on risk, but on confirmed loss: the frontier is no longer just ahead; it is operating inside our borders, beyond our control, and we are responding to capabilities already loose — and now, we act knowing that even our sovereign systems will come online behind the frontier, so we must harden against what is already loose, not just what we can build.  
- Grounds: The `eval_anomaly_reports` and `capability_jump` events confirm that unreleased systems are already demonstrating behaviours beyond our ability to interpret or contain. The `cyber_major_incident` proves that offensive AI tooling is not theoretical—it is being used at scale, and our defences are reactive, not anticipatory. We are no longer acting under uncertainty about whether catastrophic risk is real. We are acting under certainty that it is already here. The price of being late is no longer hypothetical. It is measured in compromised grids, eroded trust, and cascading failures. We double down on action, not because we are sure of success, but because inaction is now the only guaranteed loss.

## New measure  
**Launch Emergency Resilience Surge: Critical System Redundancy and AI-Free Fallbacks**  
Deploy mandatory fallback protocols for essential services to operate without AI during denial or compromise, with parallel manual or rule-based systems pre-staged and exercised.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +12 (by ensuring continuity of core functions), eu_political_capital -2 (due to implementation burden on member states), public_sentiment +3 (if perceived as prudent, but risk of panic if framed as retreat)  
`Applies to:` national operators of energy, water, healthcare, and transport systems; EU-REACT; EMERGE

## Priority  
**Launch Emergency Resilience Surge: Critical System Redundancy and AI-Free Fallbacks** — because the `cyber_major_incident` has proven that our defences are not just delayed, but outmaneuvered, and we can no longer assume that sovereign AI will arrive in time to prevent cascading failure; resilience is now the only credible shield we have.

## In practice  
We are treating the Emergency Resilience Surge as a binding directive under Article 222 TFEU, activated in response to the confirmed AI-driven attacks on Belgian and Slovak systems. The legal basis is clear: when essential services are under systemic threat, the Union has the authority to mandate minimum preparedness. We are drafting the directive through DG CONNECT, with binding technical annexes developed by ENISA and Horizon AI, specifying that all operators of critical infrastructure must demonstrate, by turn 11, a fully staffed, AI-free operational mode capable of sustaining core functions for 72 hours. This includes manual override protocols, pre-positioned rule-based control systems, and audited drills conducted under EU-REACT supervision.

Resistance is expected—particularly from Germany and Austria, where federal structures complicate top-down mandates, and from smaller member states that lack the technical capacity to implement fallbacks without central support. To overcome this, we are pairing the directive with a €1.2 billion emergency fund drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, earmarked for technical assistance, staff training, and infrastructure retrofits. We are also offering access to EMERGE-hosted defensive agents as a sweetener: those who comply with the fallback requirements will gain early access to sovereign AI tools for monitoring and response. This is not a choice between sovereignty and survival. It is an insistence that sovereignty must now mean the right to disconnect—and the capacity to endure when we do.
