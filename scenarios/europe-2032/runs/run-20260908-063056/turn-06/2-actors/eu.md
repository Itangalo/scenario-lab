## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.  
- reclassify `two_year_commitment` to identity  
- add `sovereign_execution` (commitment): Deliver sovereign AI systems into operation—tested, secured, and under EU authority—by building red-teaming, evaluation, and incident response into the core of our technical institutions.

- Trigger: the convergence of **cyber_major_incident**, **eu_frontier_access_denied**, and **supply_chain_coercion**  
- Grounds: The ransomware attack on Belgian and German municipalities exposed not only technical fragility but institutional lag: our defences were reactive, our coordination fragmented, and our access to critical models revoked mid-crisis. At the same time, Washington’s use of ASML export controls to enforce strategic alignment confirmed that leverage over compute is now exercised unilaterally, not multilaterally. These events mark a categorical shift: the risk is no longer hypothetical dependence, but active exclusion and weaponisation of infrastructure we do not control. Our two-year commitment to autonomous capacity remains valid, but its time horizon has collapsed—we are no longer building for 2031, but defending in 2029. Hence, we elevate that commitment to identity: sovereignty in AI is no longer optional, it is constitutive. We now bind ourselves to *sovereign_execution*—the operational delivery of secure, evaluated, EU-controlled systems—because oversight without execution is ritual, not power.

## Priority
**Stand up EU Rapid Incident Evaluation and Countermeasures Cell (EU-REACT)** — because the cyber_major_incident revealed that even with red-teaming authority in place, we lack the operational spine to respond in real time, and because the joint_threat_response offer proves that speed and integration are now the price of admission to collective security.

## In practice
We are acting on a shattered premise: the assumption that resilience could be built through member-state compliance and market incentives has failed. The ransomware attack was not an outlier—it was inevitable, given that offensive systems now adapt faster than human defenders can coordinate. And when the attack hit, we were cut off from the very models we needed to understand it. The denial of frontier access during a crisis is not a failure of diplomacy—it is the logical endpoint of dependence.

So we rebuild the core. Horizon AI is now live, but it is a research body, not an operational one. The Sovereign Red-Teaming Authority is being stood up under emergency powers, but it tests models before deployment—not during crisis. That gap is fatal. Therefore, we are launching EU-REACT: a cell co-located with ENISA, staffed by rotating experts from BfDI, ANSSI, and Horizon AI, and legally empowered to access telemetry from all Cyber Shield states. It will run 24/7 simulations, integrate red-teaming findings, and activate binding countermeasures during incidents—quarantining systems, issuing patches, and coordinating takedowns. We are using Article 17 of the Digital Operational Resilience Act as legal basis, and we are fast-tracking staffing through the Talent Initiative’s returning researchers. Poland and Hungary remain sceptical, but we are offering them direct access to EU-REACT’s intelligence stream and a seat on its steering board—opting out of coordination is no longer an option when the next attack could collapse hospital systems across the north. This is not bureaucracy. This is war readiness.
