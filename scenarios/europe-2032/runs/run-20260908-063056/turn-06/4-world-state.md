### The Breaking Point

A wave of AI-driven ransomware, adaptive and self-propagating, cripples hospitals and transit systems across northern Europe. Attribution is slow, defences are overwhelmed, and the EU’s reliance on foreign models collapses mid-crisis when access is abruptly revoked—leaving analysts blind. Simultaneously, Washington pressures the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s servicing of mature-node lithography machines, citing national security. The move, aimed at China, chokes mid-tier chip production across Europe, exposing the Union’s vulnerability at its one real supply-chain leverage point.

### Response in Real Time

The EU scrambles. Horizon AI launches, but as a research entity, it lacks operational authority. The Sovereign Red-Teaming Authority, still mired in legal disputes with Poland and Hungary, cannot pivot to crisis mode. In response, the Commission fast-tracks EU-REACT—its new incident cell—bypassing COREPER through emergency provisions. France and Germany welcome the move; Warsaw and Budapest denounce it as a power grab, refusing telemetry sharing. ENISA, now co-hosting the cell, begins integrating red-teaming data, but real-time coordination remains partial and under-resourced.

### The American Posture Settles

With the 2028 election decided, the new U.S. administration takes office. The posture is clear: **US_POSTURE: CONSOLIDATION**. AI frontier access is now rationed by strategic alignment. European labs face throttled model updates and conditional compute access, deepening dependency. The message is unambiguous: leadership comes with strings.

### A Fragile Foundation

Private AI investment vanishes globally. Two flagship European compute projects collapse, leaving gaps no public fund can quickly fill. The Tech sovereignty package, built on private leverage, is scrapped. Horizon AI and the Talent Initiative finish, but their gains are swamped by broader erosion. Public sentiment dips further as job losses and cyber chaos dominate headlines. The EU acts—decisively—but sovereignty and resilience erode amid institutional resistance and external pressure. Political capital declines, reflecting internal fragmentation and constrained agency, though some credit is retained for crisis mobilization. The turn ends under pressure, with outcomes still unfolding.
