### In practice  
We are holding firm on Cyber Shield: the directive is law, and compliance is not optional. The vote in COREPER this week will determine whether cohesion funds are formally tied to implementation by 2028 — a conditionality we must enforce. ENISA’s red-teaming cycle is now operational in Estonia and the Netherlands, but Poland and Hungary remain defiant. We are offering an additional €800 million in co-funding from the Digital Europe Programme, but we will not accept exemptions. The Cyber Situational Awareness Hub in Tallinn is now live, feeding early-warning data into a network that must become continent-wide by turn 5. If we cannot harden the periphery now, no amount of sovereign compute will protect us when the first real attack lands.

The European Talent Anchoring Initiative is overcoming early bottlenecks. We have fast-tracked 187 Schengen Blue Card applications and allocated Tier-1 compute blocks to INRIA, Fraunhofer, and Aalto. But national permitting delays persist, and we are now centralising approval under a new DG CONNECT task force to bypass gridlock. Meanwhile, the eval_anomaly_reports have hardened our conviction: we cannot outsource safety, and we cannot regulate capability we do not understand. That is why we are launching Horizon AI — a publicly accountable frontier research programme, hosted jointly by CERN and EMBL, with full access to model weights, interpretability mandates, and red-team integration. This is not a rival to private labs; it is a counterweight. It will run in parallel with the Tech Sovereignty Package, but unlike that stalled legislation, it will not wait for consensus. We are redirecting €12 billion from unused cohesion funds to launch it in Q1 2028, under Article 185 TFEU. The capability frontier is moving. So are we.
