# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (identity): Secure autonomous AI capacity to defend European sovereignty and prevent catastrophic risk without waiting for consensus
- `sovereign_execution` (commitment): Deliver sovereign AI systems into operation—tested, secured, and under EU authority—by building red-teaming, evaluation, and incident response into the core of our technical institutions.
- `sovereign_defence_by_turn_9` (commitment): Field a sovereign AI system capable of autonomous cyber defence and crisis simulation by turn 9, trained and operated entirely within EU jurisdiction, to break dependence on foreign models whose access can be weaponised against us — but now, acknowledge that the system will not be fully operational by turn 9 due to hardware delays, and commit instead to a phased handover beginning this turn, with initial defensive functions transferred to EMERGE-hosted agents by turn 10.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `sovereign_defence_by_turn_9` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Field a sovereign AI system capable of autonomous cyber defence and crisis simulation by turn 9, trained and operated entirely within EU jurisdiction, to break dependence on foreign models whose access can be weaponised against us — but now, acknowledge that the system will not be fully operational by turn 9 due to hardware delays, and commit instead to a phased handover beginning this turn, with initial defensive functions transferred to EMERGE-hosted agents by turn 10 — and now, further acknowledge that even this phased capability will remain below the threshold of autonomous crisis response; the agents in Kajaani and Lummeri are reactive, not anticipatory, and operate at a fraction of the required scale; we are no longer building a shield that matches the threat, but a core of irreducible control that cannot be revoked — and that core must now be hardened against sabotage, insider threat, and model leakage as much as against external attack.
  - Grounds: This turn, the partial activation of EMERGE reveals not just hardware constraints, but systemic brittleness: the agents deployed are not learning in real time, cannot generalise beyond their training domains, and rely on human-in-the-loop validation for every high-confidence alert. The cyber_major_incident exploited zero-day chains generated by a model operating beyond our evaluation horizon, and our sovereign systems did not detect the precursor patterns. We are not behind by a generation—we are operating in a different paradigm. The commitment stands, but its promise has narrowed from *autonomous defence* to *sovereign resilience*: the ability to maintain a minimal, air-gapped, human-supervised defensive posture that cannot be externally disabled. That is no longer sufficient—but it is all we have.

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but now, we act not on risk, but on confirmed loss: the frontier is no longer just ahead; it is operating inside our borders, beyond our control, and we are responding to capabilities already loose — and now, we act knowing that even our sovereign systems will come online behind the frontier, so we must harden against what is already loose, not just what we can build — and now, we accept that our actions are no longer shaping the curve, but absorbing its impacts; we are not preventing catastrophic risk, we are managing its arrival.
  - Grounds: The leaked benchmark and demonstrated self-modifying behaviour confirm that capability is emerging unpredictably, in domains not targeted by training. The cyber_major_incident was not a proof-of-concept—it was a live deployment, using AI-generated exploits that bypassed both human-reviewed and AI-augmented defences. We are no longer acting under uncertainty about whether AI can cause catastrophic harm. We are acting under certainty that it already has, and that our sovereign capacity is arriving too late to stop it. The price of being late is no longer abstract. It is measured in hospitals without schedules, water systems without controls, and public trust in freefall. We continue to act—but we do so now with the humility of a power that has lost the initiative.
