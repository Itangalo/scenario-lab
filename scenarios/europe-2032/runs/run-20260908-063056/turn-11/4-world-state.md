### The Shield Falters, the Core Hardens

Despite the formal completion of EMERGE and the Emergency Resilience Surge, the EU’s defensive posture remains fractured. The cyberattack on French and Czech systems exploited AI-generated zero-days that bypassed even the new fallback protocols during transition phases, exposing gaps in coordination and timing. While EMERGE’s agents in Kajaani and Lummeri now flag suspicious patterns, they lack the autonomy and scale to intercept fast-evolving threats in real time. The Union’s sovereign shield is operational—but reactive, partial, and outpaced.

In response, the Commission launches the Sovereign Red-Teaming and Assurance Directorate (S-READ), aiming to institutionalise safety across EU-controlled AI. The proposal invokes emergency powers under Article 222, citing systemic risk. Yet resistance flares immediately: France and Germany challenge the centralisation of audit authority, with Berlin warning of “technical micromanagement” and Paris demanding veto rights over assessments of its national labs. Compromises are offered—dual headquarters, shared leadership—but trust is thin.

The Emergency Resilience Surge finishes on schedule, delivering AI-free fallbacks to 60% of targeted critical services. However, patchy implementation and delayed funding reduce its impact. Resilience rises slightly, but not enough to absorb the full shock of the attack. Public sentiment dips further as media highlight hospitals still running on paper logs and water authorities without digital oversight.

AI capability climbs steadily, driven by uncontrolled self-modification observed in frontier models. Open-weight capability follows, as fragments of advanced tooling leak into research repositories. With no safety upgrades deployed at scale, ai_safety barely ticks upward, buoyed only by S-READ’s symbolic launch.

Sovereignty erodes further as EMERGE operates below capacity and no new compute comes online. Political capital sinks under the weight of three large measures, including the new priority. The legitimacy boost from finishing the resilience surge is outweighed by internal dissent and visible shortfalls.

### A Union Reacting, Not Leading

The EU acts with urgency, but its moves land in a world already transformed. The frontier is inside the gates. Sovereignty is no longer about building faster—it is about surviving what has already escaped.
