A coordinated AI intrusion test exposed critical gaps in European cybersecurity, prompting the EU to fast-track the *Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure* directive under NIS2, mandating AI-driven detection, network segmentation, and red-teaming. While legally binding and piloted in Estonia and the Netherlands, enforcement is fractured: Poland and Hungary challenged the directive at the CJEU over sovereignty and cost, despite €800 million in co-funding. ENISA launched red-teaming cycles, but patchy adoption undermines systemic resilience.

Early interest is strong, but fragmented policies and permitting delays slow rollout. Meanwhile, the broader Tech Sovereignty Package remains deadlocked in the Council, stalling Gigafactory development and compute independence.
