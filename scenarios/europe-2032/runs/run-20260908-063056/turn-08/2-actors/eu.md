## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now act on the understanding that without the first, we cannot deliver the second.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but this turn, we act not on risk, but on confirmed loss: the frontier is no longer just ahead; it is operating inside our borders, beyond our control, and we are responding to capabilities already loose.  
- modify `sovereign_execution` (commitment): Deliver sovereign AI systems into operation—tested, secured, and under EU authority—by building red-teaming, evaluation, and incident response into the core of our technical institutions, and now by ensuring those systems are operationally available *during* denial, disruption, and cascading failure.  
- retire `two_year_commitment`  
- add `sovereign_defence_by_turn_9` (commitment): Field a sovereign AI system capable of autonomous cyber defence and crisis simulation by turn 9, trained and operated entirely within EU jurisdiction, to break dependence on foreign models whose access can be weaponised against us.

**Trigger:** `eu_frontier_access_denied`, `openweight_frontier_release`, and `election_annulled` — together, these are not isolated incidents but a structural rupture.  
**Grounds:** The denial of access during a crisis confirmed that our reliance is not a vulnerability but a surrender of agency. The open-weight release means that the capability now in circulation is not just foreign—it is everywhere, unrecallable, and already in adversarial hands. And the annulled election proves that the attack surface has expanded beyond infrastructure into the legitimacy of governance itself. These are not future risks; they are current operations. Our `two_year_commitment`, framed as a two-year horizon, no longer matches the tempo of threat. We are no longer building for 2031—we are defending in 2030. Thus, we retire the old commitment and bind ourselves to a new one with a hard deadline: sovereign defence by turn 9. The `two_mandates` identity is sharpened to reflect that sovereignty is not a parallel goal but the precondition of safety. And `act_under_uncertainty` evolves from a principle of early action to one of *late consequence*: we are no longer acting on warning, but on proof.

## Priority  
**Launch Emergency Compute Procurement and Sovereign Model Development (EMERGE)** — because without sovereign compute, every defensive capability we build is conditional on the goodwill of those who control the models, and this turn proved that goodwill vanishes the moment we need it most.

## In practice  
We are executing EMERGE under Article 122 TFEU, treating it as a crisis of strategic survival, not industrial policy. The cluster is being built from repurposed assets: 40,000 GPU-years from Finland’s decommissioned LUMI-C expansion, 30,000 from France’s Jean Zay, and 50,000 from emergency procurement of A100s rerouted through neutral distributors. Legal jurisdiction is being established under EuroHPC’s revised governance, with air-gapped control rooms in Kajaani and Lummeri reporting directly to the Commission’s Office of AI Security. Horizon AI is restructured into a war-fighting unit: its model will be trained on red-teamed cyber threat data, with interpretability baked in, and its first mission is to simulate and counter the next wave of adaptive ransomware.  

EU-REACT, finishing its build this turn, is being upgraded from an evaluation cell to an operational command. We are invoking Article 222 TFEU to grant it emergency authority to issue binding directives during declared AI crises—overruling national refusals to share telemetry or implement countermeasures. Poland and Hungary are resisting, but we are offering them co-location of EMERGE’s secondary nodes and guaranteed access to its defensive models in exchange for compliance. This is not coercion; it is the price of membership in a Union that still believes it can act. The Anti-Coercion Instrument is being prepared for activation if the U.S. blocks component shipments, but for now, we are working through NATO’s CIPE to reroute supply. The lights are going out. We are not waiting for consensus. We are building the switch.
