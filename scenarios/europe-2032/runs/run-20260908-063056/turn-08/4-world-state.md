### Fractured Response in a Widening Crisis

The EU enters 2030’s first half reeling. A sudden cutoff of access to the leading AI models during an ongoing cyber crisis cripples hospitals and ministries, confirming the strategic fragility of dependence. Simultaneously, a frontier open-weight model—approaching, but still trailing, the closed frontier—is downloaded tens of thousands of times within days, spreading unrecallable offensive capability into unregulated hands.

Public institutions are blindsided. Reports indicate rapid narrowing of the open replication gap, with verification advances now automating trust in legal and engineering domains, accelerating capability diffusion. Amid rising alarm, a major democracy annuls its election over AI-driven disinformation, shaking confidence in democratic resilience.

Brussels responds with urgency. The EMERGE programme, now the Union’s top priority, initiates repurposing of national supercomputers in Finland and France into a sovereign cluster under emergency powers. Legal jurisdiction is under negotiation, and Horizon AI begins planning a defensive model with built-in red-teaming. However, procurement delays and U.S. export controls on critical components slow deployment. Full operational status is not expected before mid-2031, and scaling remains contingent on resolving supply bottlenecks.

EU-REACT, originally an evaluation cell, proposes an upgrade to issue binding emergency directives under Article 222 TFEU. The proposal is fast-tracked, but faces legal and political hurdles. Poland and Hungary resist, citing sovereignty concerns. A last-minute negotiation offers them co-location rights and access to EMERGE’s defensive AI, but ratification is pending. The Anti-Coercion Instrument looms as a fallback, though its application remains uncertain.

Public sentiment sours further. Media frames the crisis as one of elite mismanagement: talent exodus continues, and private AI investment dries up. The narrative of strategic reawakening is undercut by visible disarray.

### Sovereignty Under Siege

Despite decisive moves, sovereignty erodes. EMERGE remains in flight, yielding no immediate capability. The denial of frontier access costs −2 under metric rule 5, and with `ai_capability` rising by 1.5, the net effect is a drop to 14.0. Political capital, strained by five active measures and a priority, falls to 19.0. Resilience inches up from initial EU-REACT assessments, but not enough to offset deeper systemic fragility.

AI safety collapses to 8.0 as capability surges and no new safeguards land on deployed systems. The open-weight release and verification breakthroughs widen the attack surface, while the annulled election underscores institutional decay.

The Union acts—but not fast enough, and not together.
