## In practice  
Cyber Shield has formally concluded its implementation cycle. The directive is law, detection protocols are in place across 18 member states, and the Cyber Situational Awareness Hub in Tallinn is now feeding into national CERTs. But Poland and Hungary still refuse integration, and without full participation, our early-warning network remains porous. We are not declaring victory — we are shifting focus. The directive’s framework will now be extended through ENISA’s new mandate to include evaluation-triggered updates, turning it into a living defence.

Horizon AI is now legally established under Article 185 TFEU, with €12 billion redirected from unused cohesion funds. The first governance board convened in Geneva, and site-readiness teams are assessing integration at CERN and EMBL. But we know this is a long game. The real test is whether we can act *now*, not in five years. That is why we are launching pre-release evaluation: we are drafting a regulation under the AI Act’s emergency provisions to require third-party testing of any model exceeding 55.0 in capability, using red teams from BfDI, ANSSI, and a newly empowered EDPB-adjacent body. This will not wait for unanimity — we will invoke qualified majority voting in the Council if needed. The United States has already signaled resistance through its trade envoy, but we are coordinating with Japan and Canada through the GPAI to establish a baseline that can become global. If we cannot slow the uncontrolled release of systems that alter their own logs, then all our investments in talent, compute, and resilience are just building a faster road to catastrophe. We are not waiting.
