### Crisis and Contraction

The EU enters 2029’s second half under siege. A major AI-driven cyberattack cripples public services across three member states, exploiting adaptive malware generated by frontier models. Defenders are outpaced, and the withdrawal of foreign AI access during the crisis leaves response systems blind. The incident confirms a grim shift: offense has decisively outpaced defense.

Washington tightens its grip, compelling the Netherlands to restrict servicing of mature-node ASML machines—vital for Europe’s mid-tier chip production. The move, justified as countering Chinese access, effectively weaponizes Europe’s sole supply-chain leverage against itself. Panic spreads through semiconductor and AI infrastructure planning circles.

### Emergency Reorientation

The EU cancels the flagship InvestAI Gigafactories, acknowledging its reliance on unstable private partnerships and foreign compute. In response, the European Commission proposes the Emergency Compute Procurement and Sovereign Model Development (EMERGE) programme, aiming to consolidate national supercomputing assets in Finland and France into a sovereign cluster. Horizon AI is designated to lead the eventual training of a defensively capable model under full EU control, though no hardware has yet been procured or integrated.

EU-REACT, still in formation, struggles with incomplete data sharing—Poland and Hungary block telemetry access, citing sovereignty concerns. The irony is not lost: as Brussels scrambles to build technical autonomy, political fragmentation undermines it.

Public sentiment remains stagnant. Media coverage focuses on job losses in tech and failed infrastructure, eroding trust. No visible AI benefits emerge to offset the damage.

### Stalled Momentum

Despite decisive rhetoric, progress is halting. EMERGE remains in early planning and faces legal challenges over procurement bypasses; component shortages loom due to U.S. export controls, delaying any physical implementation. The Talent Anchoring Initiative and Horizon AI complete, but their impact is drowned by systemic setbacks. Resilience dips as crisis response proves brittle, and AI safety remains dangerously low—no shipped systems reflect new assurances.

Sovereignty falls to 16.0, reflecting sustained external pressure and the delayed realization of sovereign capacity. While EMERGE signals strategic intent, no operational capability has been established, and the erosion from restricted access compounds. Political capital remains unchanged: the proposal of EMERGE conveys urgency, but no full measure is retired to allow for a net gain, and the burden of ongoing efforts—including the still-active EU-REACT—prevents capital accumulation. The EU operates at the edge of overload, with no room for new initiatives beyond the one newly proposed.
