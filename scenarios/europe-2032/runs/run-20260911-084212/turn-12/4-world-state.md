### Clinics and offices
The first half of 2032 brought the first good news in years that ordinary Europeans could feel. Tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions began reaching university hospitals, and firms reported steady productivity gains from AI assistants in law, accountancy and administration — with junior staff gaining most and hiring, frozen through 2031, quietly restarting.

Brussels tried to make the improvement arrive as European delivery. Health and industry departments pushed joint procurement through university hospitals and the health data space, with accelerated approvals tied to processing on European sites, while retraining funds were extended into augmentation coaches for small firms and municipal offices. Mayors were promised delivery dates and co-funding if they hosted inference locally and kept offline fallbacks manned.

### Half-delivered
Delivery only half followed. The most effective models and therapy pipelines still ran on foreign compute under tiered access terms, with European sites handling distribution more than training. Waiting lists moved, but clinicians spoke openly of quotas and licence conditions set elsewhere. In offices, the tools that worked best were the freely available ones technicians already used — effective and ungovernable.

The municipal recovery teams continued their city-to-city rebuilds in parallel, and restored portals now carried health-booking and admin tools rather than just returning. Two more regions cleared backlogs; elsewhere hardware delays and paper procedures persisted.

Resentment eased but did not lift. Bills remained high, wages late in places, and protests around data-centre sites and recovery depots flickered as rumour and single blockades rather than a movement. Sabre-rattling around Taiwan raised shipping insurance and reminded capitals how exposed supply chains remained, without yet touching deliveries.

By June, Europe felt less dark — treated patients on television, offices hiring again — and still dependent for the means of it.
