### Paper cities
Autumn brought the attack everyone had warned about into waiting rooms and pay slips. A fast-moving, largely machine-generated intrusion swept through municipal booking, payroll and hospital administration systems in several member states, spreading through a compromised software component whose users had no reason to distrust it. Screens went dark in town halls, nurses returned to paper charts, and thousands of municipal workers waited for late wages.

Emergency teams kept water and power running, but the public face of the state stalled. Attribution became a running sore — investigators spoke of foreign tooling, criminal affiliates, and months of work, while mayors demanded fixes, not theories.

### The cells
Brussels answered with what it could deploy without new money or new law: joint recovery teams under the EU cyber agency and national counterparts, paid from repurposed digital and cohesion funds. The mobile cells travelled city to city, rebuilding systems from clean images, ripping out the tainted dependency, and leaving offline fallbacks manned until verification.

Where they arrived, services came back. In two hard-hit regions payroll restarted before Christmas and hospital admin cleared its backlog. Elsewhere queues persisted, hardware ordered as replacement sat in quota delays, and technicians quietly used freely available models to triage logs — effective, ungovernable, and politically awkward to admit.

Wage bridges for displaced young tech workers kept paying through paper channels, and some retrainees were hired as hygiene assistants to the cells — a small visible success Brussels filmed heavily.

It was not enough to change the mood. Bills remained high, entry-level tech hiring stayed frozen, and voters treated restored booking portals as the minimum, not a deliverable. The load-shedding sequence held again through a December cold snap, technically sound and politically invisible beneath anger over dark administration and late pay.

By year-end Europe was standing, patched, and resentful — services restored slowly, dependence on outside compute and unrecallable code deeper than ever.
