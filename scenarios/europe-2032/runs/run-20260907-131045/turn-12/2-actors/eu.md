## In practice  
We are meeting the emergent regional aid mutuals not as a problem to regulate, but as a resource to scale. These networks in Lyon, Rhine-Ruhr, and Silesia have already built trust, piloted AI-assisted retraining, and begun delivering public services with local cognitive surplus. We are not replacing them — we are connecting them. Through the European Social Fund+ and emergency flexibility under the Just Transition Mechanism, we are allocating €4.2 billion over three years to formalise Regional Resilience Compacts, co-designed with regional authorities, labour councils, and digital cooperatives. Each compact will receive matched funding on condition of open data sharing, interoperability with EU digital identity systems, and integration into national AI transition strategies.

This is not charity — it is strategic absorption. By recognising these networks as first responders to AI-driven disruption, we are building resilience where it lives, not where bureaucracy imagines it should. We are assigning DG REFORM as coordinator, with reporting lines to the Vice-President for Preparedness, and requiring quarterly joint exercises with national civil protection agencies. Germany’s federal structure makes this sensitive, so we are offering Länder veto power over data flows and curriculum standards — but in return, they must publish integration timelines by turn 13. The compacts will feed into the upcoming update of the AI Action Plan, ensuring that bottom-up adaptation shapes top-down policy. We are late, but not too late — yet.
