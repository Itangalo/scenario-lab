## In practice  
We are treating compute as strategic infrastructure, not as a market outcome. The AI Safety Assurance Infrastructure finishes this turn, but it has nowhere to run — no sovereign hardware, no state-accessible frontier models, no power to enforce its own conclusions. We are now acting on that gap. Through a new instrument under the Crisis Response Instrument and reinforced by the Net-Zero Industry Act, we are mandating that 10 exaflops of next-generation AI training capacity be operational by turn 16, with 40% reserved for public technical agencies, red-teaming, and safety research. This is not a tender — it is a directive. The Commission, in coordination with ENTSO-E and the European Investment Bank, is allocating €7.8 billion in emergency funding and issuing binding energy off-take guarantees to ensure uninterrupted operation.

Siting will proceed under emergency permitting rules activated through the Single Market Emergency Regulation, overriding national delays that have cost us years. We are offering Germany and Poland co-ownership of facilities and veto rights over foreign access; in return, they must release land and grid capacity by turn 14. France will host the primary evaluation node, with physical air-gapping and state-actor-grade security. The Chips Joint Undertaking is being redirected to prioritise AI-specific lithography, and we are invoking Article 346 TFEU to justify preferential procurement from EU-anchored fabs. This is not optional: if we do not control the means of AI production, we do not control our own decisions. The labs will not wait, and neither will we.
