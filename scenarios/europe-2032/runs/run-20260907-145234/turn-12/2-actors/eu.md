## In practice
This turn, we are operating under the weight of zero political capital and deep institutional fatigue. The Sovereign AI Core Programme remains our anchor, but its launch is not a celebration — it is a triage. We are using Article 168a TFEU to fast-track legal anchoring, bypassing normal co-decision procedures, and directing the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking to repurpose EuroHPC sites in Finland, Portugal, and Luxembourg as sovereign AI fortresses. These will host not just compute, but pre-authorized models vetted for continuity-critical functions — healthcare triage, grid management, emergency logistics — with red-teaming conducted under joint EU command. The €4.2bn in funding is reallocated from Digital Europe, Recovery and Resilience, and cohesion funds, but implementation hinges on the Commission’s ability to override national objections through binding emergency powers.

Meanwhile, the Emergency Continuity Response Initiative enters its final phase, with cross-border teams conducting live failover drills in Berlin, Marseille, and Riga. In Riga, legal resistance persists, but we are invoking Article 122 TFEU to compel activation, backed by the Crisis Solidarity Mechanism’s financial leverage. The Frontier Access Guarantee is nearing completion, but its value is now in question: with `ai_capability` at 76.0 and `ai_safety` at 5.0, the systems we are gaining access to may be more dangerous than the ones we are trying to replace.

The new Open-Weight Containment Protocol is our first move to reclaim control over the distributed frontier. We are drafting a regulation under the AI Act’s emergency powers, to be proposed to the Council and Parliament in June, that will criminalize the unregistered deployment of models above 55.0 capability. The European AI Security Agency will be granted authority to inspect training runs, seize weights, and shut down rogue clusters. This will provoke fierce resistance from academia and open-source communities, but we cannot allow the open frontier to become a backdoor for catastrophic risk. We are preparing a narrative of containment, not suppression — framing it as a temporary, proportionate response to a clear and present danger, with sunset clauses and review mechanisms. The timing is perilous, but the alternative — a distributed agent incident on EU soil — would end any remaining capital we have to act.
