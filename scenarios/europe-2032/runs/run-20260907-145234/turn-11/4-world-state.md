### The Weight of Emergency

AI capability inches upward to 76.0, as frontier labs stabilize the new autonomous systems, confirming their strategic dominance. The leap has passed, but its consequences deepen: `ai_safety` remains at 5.0, with no improvement in interpretability or control. Open-weight replication accelerates, though unevenly — optimized clusters and skilled collectives push `openweight_capability` to 57.0, nearing the threshold where distributed agents could independently execute high-risk tasks.

The EU completes the Continuity Assurance Framework, mandating air-gapped fallbacks across critical infrastructure. In nine member states, systems are now validated and tested — a quiet success. But in four, compliance is partial; in one, outright refusal persists. The Emergency Continuity Response Initiative continues, deploying cross-border teams with emergency powers under Article 122. Drills in Berlin and Marseille succeed, but in Riga, legal challenges delay activation beyond the 24-hour window, exposing lingering resistance.

### Sovereignty in Crisis

The EU launches the Sovereign AI Core Programme, a bold bid to reclaim control. Legal anchoring begins under Article 168a, and €4.2bn is earmarked from digital and recovery funds. However, with `eu_political_capital` at 0.0, agenda control remains lost. The measure starts, but its launch yields no immediate boost — no proposal bonus applies, as it extends existing efforts rather than responding to a recent shock. The portfolio charge — Frontier Access Guarantee (−3), Emergency Initiative (−2), new large measure (−3), priority (−1) — totals −9, far beyond absorption. Capital stays at 0.0.

Sovereignty declines to 20.0: −1 due to capability rise ≥2, no offsetting gains. Resilience gains +3 from the completed category 6 measure and +2 from ongoing response efforts, but no additional boost from the new programme yet. Public sentiment holds at 18.0, as media highlight institutional infighting and the unresolved strike threat within AI ethics units.

A transnational coalition proposes a global AI watchdog, drawing quiet EU sympathy but no formal endorsement — Washington’s suspicion is noted, and the Union dares not act alone.
