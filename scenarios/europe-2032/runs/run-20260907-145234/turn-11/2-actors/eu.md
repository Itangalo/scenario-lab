## Statement changes  
* ``- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement will now be conditional on the operational readiness of the Sovereign AI Core Programme, and we will not cut off a member state from critical capabilities unless we can offer a viable, secure alternative within 90 days. We act to defend the regime, not to punish into helplessness. However, after this turn, if a member state repeatedly bypasses the framework without justification and the Core Programme is live, we will initiate Article 7 proceedings.``  
- Grounds: The Emergency Continuity Response Initiative has revealed persistent non-compliance not as isolated resistance but as systemic defiance — one state has now twice refused access to its emergency dispatch systems, citing sovereignty, while privately negotiating direct access to a non-EU frontier lab. We cannot sustain a regime where rules are optional and enforcement is perpetually deferred. The addition of Article 7 as a backstop after readiness is achieved binds the commitment to consequences, not just conditions.

* ``- modify `no_time_for_perfection` (commitment): We will accept imperfect tools, incomplete readiness, and partial compliance — but only if they serve a functioning whole. We will prioritize systems that work over systems that are pure. Now, we extend this to our own institutions: if a directorate cannot deliver operational continuity within 90 days of activation, we will reassign command to a coalition of member states and agencies that can.``  
- Grounds: The EAAD’s failure to activate air-gapped nodes in three capitals — despite legal authority and funding — reveals that institutional incapacity is now a strategic risk. We cannot accept delays masked as due process. The commitment must now apply not only to technical systems but to the bodies meant to uphold them.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Establish the Sovereign AI Core Programme  
A unified EU-operated infrastructure for sovereign AI execution, combining hardened compute clusters on EU soil with pre-authorized, audited models capable of sustaining critical public functions during geopolitical or technical disruption.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–20 points by anchoring compute, talent, and model access under EU legal and physical control; raise resilience by 5–7 as sovereign systems reduce dependency on fragile external links; begin reversing the sovereignty-capability gap by creating a non-recallable base for future control  
`Applies to:` European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, national digital ministries, EU-critical infrastructure operators, selected frontier talent pools

## Priority  
**Priority:** Establish the Sovereign AI Core Programme  
We are prioritizing this because the capability jump to 75.0 has made dependency a survival risk — not a policy choice — and the Frontier Access Guarantee, while vital, remains external and conditional. Only a core of sovereign, operational AI capacity can ensure continuity when access is cut or systems fail, and only now, with political capital at zero and public trust collapsing, can we justify the scale of integration and control it demands.

## In practice  
This turn, we are shifting from emergency triage to foundational rebuilding. The Continuity Assurance Framework has formally concluded, but its legacy is uneven: in nine member states, air-gapped nodes are now tested and operational; in four, they remain offline, and in one, they were never installed. The Emergency Continuity Response Initiative continues its work, deploying EAAD-led teams to conduct live drills in Berlin, Marseille, and Riga — this time with binding authority under Article 122 TFEU to override national objections if failover protocols are not activated within 24 hours. We are using the Crisis Solidarity Mechanism to fund 80% of upgrade costs in states that comply fully, but we are also preparing contingency orders: if the Core Programme is approved, we will redirect those funds into sovereign infrastructure, not patchwork fixes.

The Sovereign AI Core Programme is our answer to the paralysis of dependence. We are convening an extraordinary Digital Affairs Council meeting under Article 168a TFEU to fast-track legal anchoring, pooling €4.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme, the Recovery and Resilience Facility, and repurposed cohesion funds. The infrastructure will be co-located with existing EuroHPC sites in Finland, Portugal, and Luxembourg, using hardened facilities designed to resist both cyber and physical intrusion. Access will be restricted to EU-certified operators and pre-vetted models, with a red-team mandate to simulate failure and attack. We are offering member states a choice: integrate now under joint command, or risk exclusion when the first sovereign workloads go live in turn 13. This is not coercion — it is the price of belonging to a Union that still functions.
