A six-month emergency programme to harden energy, water and health infrastructure against AI-enabled cyber threats, beginning with mandatory red-teaming and failover testing in all operators designated as essential under the NIS2 Directive.

## In practice  
We are activating the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge through the NIS2 Directive framework, leveraging Article 21 powers to issue binding guidance to member states on enhanced threat modelling and incident response readiness. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will lead a task force co-staffed by national CERTs, intelligence liaison officers, and red-team specialists from front-running labs to conduct live-fire exercises across 50 priority operators — including grid managers, nuclear facilities, and major hospitals — starting in Q2. These will simulate AI-driven attack chains using synthetic adversaries trained to mimic known offensive toolkits, including those derived from Kimi K3. Operators failing baseline thresholds will be required to implement compensating controls within 90 days or risk temporary suspension of EU funding eligibility.

Simultaneously, we are redirecting €12bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Recovery and Resilience Facility to finance rapid deployment of zero-trust architectures, automated anomaly detection systems, and offline backup protocols across these sectors. This includes pre-positioning modular microgrid controllers and medical device fallback modes that can operate without network connectivity. We are negotiating with Germany, France, and Poland to fast-track parliamentary approvals for national implementing acts, offering additional cohesion funds in exchange for compliance. Resistance from smaller member states over cost and capacity is being met with a new technical assistance envelope managed by DG CONNECT, which will deploy surge teams to help configure and staff new defences.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, though delays in Brittany and northern Sweden persist. We have intensified bilateral talks with regional councils, offering expanded green hydrogen production commitments and local equity stakes in the facilities to secure buy-in. Grid reservations with ENTSO-E now cover 14.3GW of the targeted 15GW, and final site decisions are expected by mid-June. Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package continues to stall in Council negotiations due to legal disputes over the extraterritorial reach of permitting reforms. We are now preparing a fallback directive under Article 114 TFEU that focuses narrowly on energy efficiency and operational control clauses, stripping out the most contested elements to preserve momentum.
