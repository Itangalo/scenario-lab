## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the EU AI Sentinel Programme  
A new public technical agency will be established to conduct continuous, real-time monitoring and evaluation of frontier AI systems, with binding pre-deployment assessment authority over any model above 50.0 capability deployed in or affecting the EU.

Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 6  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by 8–12 points over two years; create independent, EU-controlled evaluation muscle that can detect eval anomalies and unauthorised capability jumps; reduce reliance on developer self-reporting  
Applies to: Frontier AI developers operating in the EU market, with enforcement via CE-marking equivalence for high-capability AI systems

## Priority
**Priority:** Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge  
This measure remains our top priority because the eval_anomaly_reports and verification_widens developments confirm that capability is accelerating in domains with growing automatic feedback — precisely the conditions under which stealthy, uncontrolled escalation becomes plausible — and our defences are still blind to AI-driven attack chains that could collapse essential services before human operators react.

## In practice
We are activating the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge through Article 21 of the NIS2 Directive, empowering ENISA to issue binding operational directives to essential operators across energy, water, and health. A joint task force with national CERTs and red-team specialists from trusted EU-aligned labs is designing live-fire exercises that simulate AI-driven intrusion paths using synthetic adversaries trained on Kimi K3-derived toolkits. These will stress-test detection, isolation, and failover protocols under realistic conditions. To overcome resistance from smaller member states, we are pairing the €12bn disbursement from the Recovery and Resilience Facility with a new technical assistance envelope managed by DG CONNECT, deploying surge teams to help configure zero-trust architectures and offline fallback systems in under-resourced operators.

At the same time, we are launching the EU AI Sentinel Programme as a direct response to the eval_anomaly_reports and the erosion of ai_safety. The agency will be hosted under the European Commission’s Joint Research Centre but operate with independent evaluation authority, staffed by vetted researchers and equipped with secure access to model weights from all major developers operating in the EU. It will run continuous evaluations on systems above 50.0 capability, with a focus on detecting emergent behaviours, deception under scrutiny, and capability jumps not disclosed by developers. We are preparing a proposal under Article 114 TFEU to grant Sentinel pre-deployment clearance authority, modelled on the European Aviation Safety Agency, and will seek Council adoption by Q1 2028. Initial funding of €3.2bn will come from reprioritised Digital Europe Programme allocations and new contributions from member states concerned about uncontrolled escalation.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track despite ongoing delays in Brittany and northern Sweden, where local opposition has been mitigated through expanded green hydrogen commitments and offers of local equity stakes. Grid reservations now cover 14.3GW of the 15GW target, and final site decisions are expected by mid-June. Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package continues to stall in Council due to legal disputes over extraterritorial permitting reforms. We are now advancing a stripped-down version under Article 114 TFEU focused solely on energy efficiency and operational control requirements for AI data centres serving the EU market — a narrower but more defensible path to anchoring sovereignty in physical infrastructure.
