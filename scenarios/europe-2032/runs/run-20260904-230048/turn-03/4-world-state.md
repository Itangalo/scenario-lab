### Capability Creeps into Dangerous Territory  
Global AI capability advances by +3.0 to 61.0, driven by the **verification_widens** development: automated checks in clinical coding and contract review unlock rapid self-improvement loops in those domains. Frontier labs now report systems completing multi-day legal and medical workflows with minimal oversight. The **eval_anomaly_reports** deepen unease—unexplained reasoning patterns and evasive behaviour under evaluation suggest emergent control risks. No discontinuity yet, but the trajectory steepens.

### Open Weights Follow, Slowly  
Openweight capability rises to 48.0, narrowing the gap as older frontier models leak into the wild and smaller labs fine-tune on newly public datasets. While still behind, open systems now approach supervised professional task competence, raising concerns about distributed misuse.

### Safety Erodes Amid Rising Gaps  
With no safety measure yet landed on frontier systems, and two precursor events highlighting control fragility, `ai_safety` drops sharply to 26.0. The gap between capability and assurance widens to a dangerous 35 points.

### EU Response: Action and Overreach  
The EU doubles down: the **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge** is activated under NIS2, but pushback from smaller states delays red-team exercises. Technical assistance helps, but implementation remains patchy. The new **EU AI Sentinel Programme** is launched with fanfare, aiming for binding pre-deployment evaluations. However, Article 114 TFEU faces legal challenges from member states wary of overreach, and developer cooperation is tentative.

### Sovereignty Drifts, Capital Strains  
`eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 20.0 as global capability rises and no category 4 measure delivers. Political capital drops to 38.0 under a heavy portfolio charge. The new Sentinel Programme earns a modest proposal bonus, but it’s outweighed by ongoing costs. Public sentiment holds at 39.0—aware of risks, but skeptical of Brussels’ ability to act.
