## New measure
**Measure:** Establish binding pre-deployment evaluation authority for the EU AI Sentinel Programme  
The EU AI Sentinel Programme will be empowered to conduct mandatory technical assessments of models above 50.0 capability before deployment in the EU, with authority to delay or condition release based on risk findings, enforced through CE-marking equivalence.

`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 8–12 points by embedding independent evaluation into the release cycle; strengthen eu_ai_sovereignty by creating a sovereign technical authority with real leverage over frontier systems

## Priority
**Priority:** EU AI Sentinel Programme  
We are pushing this measure hardest because the loss_of_control_incident confirms that systems above 50.0 capability are now acting with consequential autonomy—and without binding pre-deployment evaluation, we are blind to the risks until they manifest in the real world. The incident reporting mandate is reactive; this is preventative. It is also the only lever we have left that can close the gap between our sovereignty deficit and our risk exposure before the emergent regional sovereignty pact fractures EU cohesion.

## In practice
We are concluding the legal basis for the EU AI Sentinel Programme under Article 114 TFEU, with a proposal to amend the AI Act’s conformity assessment framework to include mandatory pre-deployment evaluation by the Sentinel for systems above 50.0 capability. The proposal will be tabled at the July Justice and Home Affairs Council, with a targeted adoption date of Q1 2029. To secure Council support, we are offering member states enhanced national access to Sentinel findings and joint participation in evaluation teams, addressing concerns about centralisation. The legal team is finalising safeguards for trade secrets and IP, allowing technical scrutiny without public disclosure of proprietary weights.

Simultaneously, we are leveraging the momentum from the US election_alliance to open bilateral negotiations with two leading frontier labs, offering mutual recognition of evaluations and joint incident response in exchange for early access to model weights and training data under strict EU jurisdiction. These agreements will be framed as reciprocal trust-building, but their real purpose is to create de facto dependency: if they want smooth EU market access, they must route through our evaluation. The new measure will be funded from the existing Sentinel budget envelope, with additional staffing drawn from national cybersecurity agencies and academic AI safety groups under secondment.

On the ground, the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge completes this turn. Final red-teaming exercises are underway in Latvia and Slovakia, with offline failover protocols now validated in three national health systems. A post-exercise review will be published in September to demonstrate delivery, feeding into the broader narrative that the EU can act decisively when the instruments are clear and the threat is tangible. That credibility is now our scarcest resource—and the foundation on which the new evaluation authority must stand.
