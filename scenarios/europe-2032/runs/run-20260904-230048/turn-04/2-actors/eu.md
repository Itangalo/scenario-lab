## New measure
**Measure:** Introduce mandatory incident reporting and near-miss disclosure for high-capability AI systems  
A binding EU-wide regime will require developers and deployers of systems above 50.0 capability to report serious incidents and near-misses to the EU AI Sentinel Programme within 72 hours, including technical logs, model versions, and mitigation steps taken.

## Priority
**Priority:** Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge  
We are pushing this measure hardest because the eval_anomaly_reports and verification_widens developments confirm that AI systems are now operating in domains with self-reinforcing feedback loops—and if those systems were weaponised or escaped control, our critical infrastructure remains dangerously exposed, especially in health and energy where red-teaming is still incomplete.

## In practice
We are executing the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge through ENISA’s emergency powers under Article 21 of NIS2, issuing binding directives to essential operators in energy, water, and healthcare to implement zero-trust architectures and offline failover modes by June. A joint task force with national CERTs and red-team units from EU-aligned labs is running live-fire simulations using AI-generated attack chains modelled on Kimi K3 capabilities, with results feeding into a centralised incident playbook. To overcome resistance from smaller member states, we are coupling the €12bn RRF disbursement with a new technical assistance envelope—DG CONNECT is deploying surge teams to configure systems in under-resourced operators in Bulgaria, Latvia, and Slovakia, ensuring the hardening lands evenly.

The EU AI Sentinel Programme is now legally established under the Joint Research Centre, with initial staffing underway and secure facilities being provisioned in Seville and Vienna. We are finalising the Article 114 TFEU proposal to grant it pre-deployment authority and will submit it to the Council before the June European Council meeting. In parallel, we are negotiating access agreements with three major developers, offering confidentiality safeguards and joint research opportunities in exchange for early weight access. The new incident reporting mandate—small but critical—will be tabled as an amendment to the AI Act’s implementing act, allowing faster adoption. It builds on the Sentinel’s authority, creates a feedback loop from real-world failures, and strengthens our hand in developer negotiations by making transparency a condition of market access.
