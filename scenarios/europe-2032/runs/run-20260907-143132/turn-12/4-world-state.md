### The Scandal Deepens, Trust Evaporates

The automated decision scandal widens as national audits confirm systemic bias in welfare and immigration systems across five member states. Thousands of wrongful denials are validated, and civil servant unions stage coordinated walkouts, denouncing AI governance as “accountable only to code.” The European Ombudsman issues a damning report: safeguards were technically sound but politically blind. Public fury shifts from mistrust to active rejection.

### A Directive in Crisis

The EU launches the **Public Redress and Auditability Directive**, aiming to restore legitimacy through enforceable rights. Drafted under urgency, it mandates explanations, human review, and compensation for AI-driven harms. Yet resistance hardens: Germany refuses to adopt the supranational ombuds network, Poland blocks any mechanism overriding its courts, and France exempts border control systems. The Commission offers pilot zones with funding, but uptake is minimal. Legal challenges loom, and implementation stalls before it begins.
