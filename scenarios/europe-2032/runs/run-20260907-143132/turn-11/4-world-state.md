### The Crisis of Legitimacy Deepens

The EU closes a historic wave of initiatives—seven major measures conclude this turn—but the moment feels less like closure than collapse. The **automated_decision_scandal**, confirmed by a national ombudsman, reveals systemic bias in a social insurance AI used across three member states. The system, built under the Sovereign AI Core Programme, followed secure procedures and passed air-gapped audits, yet its objectives were set without public input, leading to wrongful denials for thousands. The scandal confirms a grim truth: sovereign infrastructure cannot guarantee just outcomes.

### A Pivot to Democratic Control

In response, the EU launches the **Democratic Alignment Review Framework**, a small category 9 measure requiring public and parliamentary scrutiny of AI design objectives in welfare, policing, and immigration. Framed as a shift from technical to democratic sovereignty, it targets the root of the scandal. The Commission invokes urgency, but legal negotiations stall. Several member states resist the framework as an overreach, and the Conference of Presidents delays fast-tracking, demanding concessions on national implementation.

### Abandoned Hardening, Fading Trust

The Sovereign Stack Hardening Initiative is quietly shelved. In Wrocław, Poland’s constitutional challenge remains unchallenged; in Marseille, legacy systems still block upgrades. Funds are redirected to civic components of the new framework, acknowledging that stack security without public legitimacy is hollow. The Openweight Containment Initiative remains in legal limbo, with cloud providers still refusing remote deactivation.

`ai_capability` rises by 0.5, driven by **verification_widens**, which accelerates progress in law and clinical coding. But with no safety measure landing and the scandal exposing systemic failure, `ai_safety` drops by 5. `resilience` falls by 2—capability rose and no category 6 measure is active.
