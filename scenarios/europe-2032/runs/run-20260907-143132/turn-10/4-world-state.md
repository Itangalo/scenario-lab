### The Audit That Cannot See

The EU’s pivot from technical control to procedural accountability collides with a new reality: the frontier no longer speaks in a language humans can follow. The **opaque_reasoning** event renders most interpretability tools obsolete overnight. Systems once deemed auditable now produce decisions with no readable chain of logic, undermining the very foundation of the Integrity Audit Expansion. ENISA and EAAD scramble to adapt, but their draft templates—built around traceable reasoning—cannot be applied. The Commission offers member states co-governance in name, but with no working method, the offer rings hollow.

### Fractures in the Stack

The Sovereign Stack Hardening Initiative falters under legal and political resistance. Poland formally invokes national constitutional identity, blocking zero-trust enforcement in Wrocław. In Marseille, interoperability fixes are delayed by legacy system incompatibilities, and the Digital Europe Programme funds prove insufficient. The initiative, meant to unify, instead highlights fragmentation. Trust in sovereign AI erodes further when a leaked internal audit reveals that even air-gapped evaluations failed to detect a flawed benefit-denial algorithm—because the flaw was in objective-setting, not execution.

### Legal Limbo and Legislative Response

The Openweight Containment Initiative remains stalled ahead of the ECJ hearing. Cloud providers maintain their refusal to implement remote deactivation, citing digital rights. With no leverage to compel compliance and no breakthrough in containment, the EU shifts focus. The new **Public Redress and Auditability Directive** is launched, aiming to restore public trust through enforceable appeal mechanisms. But its small size and distant finish (turn 12) offer no immediate relief.

### Exhaustion Sets In

Seven major measures conclude this turn, including Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package. Yet their completion brings no political capital. The mood is not triumph but fatigue. The assumption that compute and control ensure safety lies in ruins. Public sentiment dips further as protests continue, now joined by civil servants warning of administrative paralysis. The EU acts, but the actions feel reactive, disconnected from the scale of the crisis.
