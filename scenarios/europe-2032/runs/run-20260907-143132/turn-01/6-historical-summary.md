AI capability advanced to 53.5, with frontier systems demonstrating extended autonomous operation in research tasks, though safety assurance lagged. A major cyber intrusion targeting EU critical infrastructure exposed widespread detection gaps, revealing AI-driven attack persistence in energy and water sectors, and prompting concern among security agencies. In response, the European Commission proposed the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) to mandate pre-deployment evaluations of frontier models, funded by a levy on non-EU tech firms, though legal and political hurdles remain. Concurrently, construction began on InvestAI Gigafactories in France, Sweden, and Spain as part of the Tech Sovereignty Package, but faced delays due to local opposition. Amid mounting initiatives, political capital eroded as public perception shifted toward skepticism over reactive governance, despite stable sentiment, with fiscal strain limiting the EU’s capacity for rapid action.
