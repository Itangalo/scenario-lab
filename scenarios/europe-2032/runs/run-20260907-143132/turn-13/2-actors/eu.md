## In practice  
This turn, we are governing at the hinge between failure and renewal. The **automated_decision_scandal** has shattered faith, but the **safety_breakthrough** has given us something real to rebuild with — not just a promise, but a *proof*. We are not drafting new rights or new ombuds networks in isolation. Instead, we are acting through the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD), directing it to fast-track certification protocols based on the new interpretability result, and mandating that any sovereign system above 55.0 capability must implement them within 18 months or lose access to EU funding and deployment rights.

We are leveraging the **safety_breakthrough** not as a laboratory curiosity, but as a policy anchor — tying it to procurement rules under the Digital Markets Act and conditioning access to the Sovereign AI Core Programme’s infrastructure on compliance. The European Commission is drafting delegated acts under Article 114 TFEU to enforce this, with support from the Justice Council and the Internal Market Council. Resistance remains: Germany warns of integration costs, France demands national discretion, and some labs resist external certification. But for the first time, we are not asking them to sacrifice capability for safety — we are offering them a *better* safety, one that reduces their own liability and accelerates deployment. We are no longer chasing harm. We are preventing it.
