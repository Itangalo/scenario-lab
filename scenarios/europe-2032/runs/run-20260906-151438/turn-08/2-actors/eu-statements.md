# Statements: The European Union (turn 8)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, nor control as a substitute for inclusion; henceforward, every containment measure must carry with it a visible path to democratic anchoring and social absorption, or it risks becoming an instrument of fragmentation — **and we now accept that when a member state refuses audit access, we must either enforce exclusion from sovereign systems or accept that the Union’s containment regime is fiction. There is no third way.**
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent catastrophic misuse through sovereign control of high-capability AI systems, with enforceable containment, real-time monitoring, public accountability, and binding redundancy for critical infrastructure — because assurance without resilience is performance, not protection — **and we now extend this to mean: no system above 55.0 capability operating within or against EU critical infrastructure may be exempt from audit, telemetry, and emergency override under EU authority. Where access is denied, we will build capacity to act without it.**

We choose enforcement. This hardens the commitment: inclusion must be reciprocal, and sovereignty must have teeth.

The move is framed as enforcing strategic alignment, but critics call it coercion. The standoff threatens to paralyze the next Multiannual Financial Framework negotiation.
  - Reason: The developments directly challenge the Union's authority by fracturing enforcement of audit and alignment, forcing a choice between coercion and collapse of containment.
