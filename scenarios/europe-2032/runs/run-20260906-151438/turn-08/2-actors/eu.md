## In practice

This turn, we are cornered — and we are drawing the line. The denial of frontier access by Washington has crippled hospitals relying on foreign models for diagnostics and logistics. No explanation. No appeal. It is not a policy disagreement — it is a demonstration of power. At the same time, Poland continues to operate its US-linked AI cells outside EU audit, while northern states threaten to withhold cohesion funds from anyone who doesn’t align. The Union is being pulled apart from both ends.

We respond by enforcing sovereignty as a condition of access. Under Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act framework, we issue a binding directive: no AI system above 55.0 capability may interface with EU critical infrastructure — health, energy, transport, finance — unless it submits to EAAD audit, real-time telemetry, and emergency override. This applies to national systems, foreign providers, and bilateral deals alike. Non-compliant systems will be blocked from SACP, EU data spaces, and cohesion funding. We offer Poland a final deal: full audit access, and they keep the Wrocław cluster and the €10bn grant. Refuse, and they lose access to all sovereign systems — including fallbacks during the current crisis.

Meanwhile, the Emergency Resilience Acceleration Facility rolls out: €30bn from the Modernisation Fund is disbursed through ENISA, with DG HOME tracking compliance. Human-in-the-loop gates go live in tax and health IT systems; decentralised backups are deployed. But we know: hardening systems means nothing if the systems we depend on are not ours to control. So we act — not just to patch, but to claim authority. Because if the Union cannot decide who operates within its domain, it no longer exists.
