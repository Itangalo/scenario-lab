AI capability advances to 68.0 due to US self-improving agents, while openweight models reach 51.0 amid leaked tooling, widening the safety-capability gap. A devastating AI-powered ransomware attack cripples public services across multiple EU states, exposing systemic vulnerabilities and accelerating societal anxiety. Job displacement intensifies in professional sectors, spiking graduate unemployment and eroding public trust. In response, the EU activates emergency powers to launch the €30bn Emergency Resilience Acceleration Facility, mandating rapid security retrofits led by ENISA, with DG HOME threatening to withhold funds from non-compliant states.

Poland continues to resist full EU oversight, rejecting audit access to its US-linked AI systems despite offers of EAAD board seats and co-leadership of the Wrocław cluster, leaving the €10bn resilience grant unresolved. An informal northern bloc emerges, opposing fund disbursement to states with bilateral deals, exacerbating east-west divisions. The Public Assurance Protocol issues technically robust safety reports, but they fail to counter negative public sentiment amid the crisis.

EU safety efforts advance with integration of the French real-time interpretability tool into SACP audits—no breaches detected—but containment lags behind accelerating capabilities. Washington tightens AI export controls, restricting even allied access and demanding telemetry that challenges EU sovereignty. Despite technical progress in assurance and resilience, political cohesion deteriorates, emergency measures strain unity, and the long-term stability of the Union comes into question.
