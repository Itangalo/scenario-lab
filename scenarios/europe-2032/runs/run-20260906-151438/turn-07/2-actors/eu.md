## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without social legitimacy is a fortress without a people; **and we further recognise that when public sentiment falls below 30.0 and political capital erodes below 20.0, our ability to act coherently collapses regardless of technical readiness.**

  - Grounds: The convergence of *cyber_major_incident*, *labour_displacement*, and *export_control_escalation* — all occurring while `public_sentiment` remains at 27.0 and `eu_political_capital` drops to 15.0 — reveals a systemic vulnerability: our instruments may be sound, but our capacity to wield them is evaporating. The Union is no longer just managing risk — it is operating under siege conditions, where trust, bandwidth, and unity are the scarce resources. This reframe acknowledges that legitimacy and cohesion are not background conditions but core strategic assets.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, nor control as a substitute for inclusion; henceforward, every containment measure must carry with it a visible path to democratic anchoring and social absorption, or it risks becoming an instrument of fragmentation.

  - Trigger: Poland’s bilateral deal, the cyber incident, and flat public sentiment despite transparency efforts.
  - Grounds: The *member_state_defection* was not merely a breach of coordination — it was a symptom of exclusion. Our containment architecture, however technically sound, has failed to convince national governments they are part of it. The *cyber_major_incident* exploited gaps in resilience that were known but unfunded. Meanwhile, the Public Assurance Protocol has not moved public sentiment meaningfully. We acted fast, but we did not act *with*. From now on, acting under uncertainty means acting *in dialogue* — not just with experts, but with member states, workers, and citizens whose consent binds the Union together.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent catastrophic misuse through sovereign control of high-capability AI systems, with enforceable containment, real-time monitoring, public accountability, and binding redundancy for critical infrastructure — because assurance without resilience is performance, not protection.

  - Trigger: The *cyber_major_incident* and the incomplete transition of CIRS to ENISA (only 40% complete).
  - Grounds: A major automated attack has landed across public services using model-generated tooling, demonstrating that even if we contain our own systems, openweight and foreign models can still cripple us. The Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge was never finished, and now it has been tested — and found wanting. Our commitment must expand: containment is not enough. We must harden what we cannot fully control, especially where lives and essential services depend on it.

A rapid-response funding and deployment mechanism to retrofit AI-hardened defences into critical public services within six months, starting with health, tax, and municipal administration, using modular threat-detection stacks and human-in-the-loop escalation protocols.

## Priority

**Priority:** Launch the Emergency Resilience Acceleration Facility  
We are pushing this hardest because the *cyber_major_incident* has proven that even if we secure our own sovereign stack, the broader ecosystem remains vulnerable to cascading failure — and with only 40% of CIRS completed, we are exposed; delaying resilience is no longer an option, even if it stretches our thinning capital.

## In practice

This turn, we are reeling — but not retreating. The *cyber_major_incident* hit like a storm: a ransomware sweep across regional health registries, tax portals, and municipal permit systems, powered by AI-generated zero-days and adaptive evasion logic. It wasn’t state-scale, but it was coordinated, persistent, and clearly beyond human-only capabilities. Recovery is underway, but attribution remains unclear — and fear is spreading. At the same time, *labour_displacement* bites deeper: law firms report no junior hires this cycle, software startups automate intern roles overnight, and graduate unemployment spikes. The *export_control_escalation* tightens further — Washington now rations allied access, treating even close partners as potential leakage vectors. We are locked out of key model updates unless we accept embedded telemetry, which we cannot.

We respond by shifting focus from containment to survival. The Sovereign AI Core Programme continues, but we can no longer assume that building a clean stack is enough. The Emergency Resilience Acceleration Facility launches under Article 122 TFEU, bypassing Council deadlock with emergency economic powers. €30bn is unlocked from the Modernisation Fund and the Recovery Instrument, targeting immediate retrofits: AI-driven anomaly detection in public IT, mandatory human approval gates for high-risk transactions, and decentralised backup nodes for critical databases. ENISA leads deployment, working with national CSIRTs, but we assign DG HOME to monitor uptake — and withhold cohesion funds from any member state that blocks installation.

At the same time, we formalise the integration of the French interpretability tool into all SACP audits, expanding EAAD’s mandate to include retrospective analysis of near-misses from the recent attack. We also extend the offer to Poland: not just observer status in SMAF, but a seat on the EAAD advisory board and co-leadership of the Wrocław cluster. But we make clear: sovereignty is not optional membership. If Warsaw wants the grant, the cluster, and the security, it must submit its joint AI cells to EU audit — or lose access to all sovereign systems above 55.0.

The Public Assurance Protocol delivers its first reports this month. They are clear, cautious, and ignored by the press. Trust won’t come from documents — it will come from proof that the lights stay on. So we act. Not perfectly. Not safely. But visibly. Because if the Union cannot protect its people from harm, nothing else we build will matter.
