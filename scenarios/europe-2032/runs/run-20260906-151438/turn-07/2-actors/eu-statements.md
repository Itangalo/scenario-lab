# Statements: The European Union (turn 7)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, nor control as a substitute for inclusion; henceforward, every containment measure must carry with it a visible path to democratic anchoring and social absorption, or it risks becoming an instrument of fragmentation.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent catastrophic misuse through sovereign control of high-capability AI systems, with enforceable containment, real-time monitoring, public accountability, and binding redundancy for critical infrastructure — because assurance without resilience is performance, not protection.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without social legitimacy is a fortress without a people; **and we further recognise that when public sentiment falls below 30.0 and political capital erodes below 20.0, our ability to act coherently collapses regardless of technical readiness.**
  - Grounds: The convergence of *cyber_major_incident*, *labour_displacement*, and *export_control_escalation* — all occurring while `public_sentiment` remains at 27.0 and `eu_political_capital` drops to 15.0 — reveals a systemic vulnerability: our instruments may be sound, but our capacity to wield them is evaporating. The Union is no longer just managing risk — it is operating under siege conditions, where trust, bandwidth, and unity are the scarce resources. This reframe acknowledges that legitimacy and cohesion are not background conditions but core strategic assets.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, nor control as a substitute for inclusion; henceforward, every containment measure must carry with it a visible path to democratic anchoring and social absorption, or it risks becoming an instrument of fragmentation.
  - Trigger: Poland’s bilateral deal, the cyber incident, and flat public sentiment despite transparency efforts.
  - Grounds: The *member_state_defection* was not merely a breach of coordination — it was a symptom of exclusion. Our containment architecture, however technically sound, has failed to convince national governments they are part of it. The *cyber_major_incident* exploited gaps in resilience that were known but unfunded. Meanwhile, the Public Assurance Protocol has not moved public sentiment meaningfully. We acted fast, but we did not act *with*. From now on, acting under uncertainty means acting *in dialogue* — not just with experts, but with member states, workers, and citizens whose consent binds the Union together.
  - Evidence quoted: Poland signs a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler for priority access to frontier models, embedding joint AI cells in its defence ministry. Labeled “pragmatic deterrence” in Warsaw, the move undermines EU-wide containment and signals eroding unity. In response, Brussels offers Poland expedited observer status in SMAF and proposes co-hosting a second air-gapped cluster in Wrocław, backed by a €10bn resilience grant tied to full Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge compliance.
  - Reason: Poland's defection reveals exclusion in EU's containment approach, directly challenging the legitimacy and cohesion underlying the 'act under uncertainty' commitment.
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Prevent catastrophic misuse through sovereign control of high-capability AI systems, with enforceable containment, real-time monitoring, public accountability, and binding redundancy for critical infrastructure — because assurance without resilience is performance, not protection.
  - Trigger: The *cyber_major_incident* and the incomplete transition of CIRS to ENISA (only 40% complete).
  - Grounds: A major automated attack has landed across public services using model-generated tooling, demonstrating that even if we contain our own systems, openweight and foreign models can still cripple us. The Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge was never finished, and now it has been tested — and found wanting. Our commitment must expand: containment is not enough. We must harden what we cannot fully control, especially where lives and essential services depend on it.
  - Evidence quoted: The Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge concludes, transitioning to ENISA as a standing monitoring function, though only 40% of systems were upgraded.
  - Reason: The incomplete transition directly undermines the resilience assumed by the original commitment, raising the cost of maintaining sovereign control without hardened infrastructure.
