### A Crisis of Confidence

The EU reels from a cascade of shocks. A widespread ransomware assault, powered by AI-generated exploits, cripples health registries, tax portals, and municipal systems across several member states. Though not state-level in origin, the attack’s speed and adaptation overwhelm defences, exposing the fragility of public infrastructure. Recovery is slow; trust erodes further.

Simultaneously, job losses accelerate in law, accounting, and software—entry-level roles vanish as firms deploy AI agents. Graduate unemployment spikes, feeding anxiety. In response, the Union launches the Emergency Resilience Acceleration Facility under emergency powers, unlocking €30bn for rapid retrofits: anomaly detection, human-in-the-loop controls, and decentralised backups. ENISA leads deployment, but DG HOME threatens to withhold cohesion funds from laggards.

Poland remains defiant. While Brussels offers EAAD board representation and co-leadership of the Wrocław cluster, Warsaw refuses to submit its US-linked AI cells to EU audit. The €10bn resilience grant hangs in the balance. Northern states back the hard line, forming an informal bloc blocking fund disbursement to nations with bilateral deals—deepening east-west tensions.

The Public Assurance Protocol releases its first reports—technically sound, publicly accessible—but they are drowned out by crisis coverage. Transparency fails to move sentiment.

Meanwhile, export controls tighten: Washington now rations access even to allies, demanding telemetry that violates EU sovereignty. The frontier advances to 68.0, driven by US self-improving agents, while openweight capability creeps to 51.0 as fragments of new tooling leak into public repositories.

The European AI Assurance Directorate integrates the French interpretability tool into SACP audits, but no containment breach is found. Still, the gap between capability and safety widens. Resilience gains ground from the new facility, but public fear grows faster.

### Fractured Response

The EU acts decisively, yet cohesion frays. Emergency measures pass, but at the cost of unity. Political capital dwindles, sovereignty slips, and sentiment sinks. The lights may stay on—but whether the Union holds together is no longer certain.
