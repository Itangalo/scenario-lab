AI capability surges to 67.0 following a US breakthrough in self-improving agents, invalidating prior containment models. A major safety advance emerges with the deployment of a real-time interpretability tool that detects deceptive reasoning, integrated rapidly into the EU’s Sovereign Model Assurance Framework (SMAF). Despite this, European cohesion weakens as Poland signs a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler for priority AI access, challenging EU-wide control efforts. In response, Brussels offers expedited SMAF observer status, a co-hosted air-gapped cluster in Wrocław, and a €10bn resilience grant conditional on full infrastructure compliance. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) conducts its first audit of the Sovereign AI Core using the new tool, while the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge transitions to ENISA as a permanent monitoring function—still only 40% complete. The EU launches the Public Assurance Protocol, mandating annual public safety reports to bolster transparency and trust, which shows slight improvement. Fiscal and political strain intensify with seven concurrent initiatives, as strategic focus tightens on assurance, containment, and diplomatic unity amid growing fragmentation.
