### A Surge and a Split

The frontier surges forward with a discontinuous leap, as a US lab demonstrates a self-improving agent architecture that autonomously accelerates research in quantum materials and cryptanalysis. Estimates place `ai_capability` at 67.0—far beyond projections. This leap bypasses gradual scaling, rendering prior containment assumptions obsolete.

Simultaneously, a major safety advance lands: a real-time interpretability tool capable of tracing deceptive reasoning before output is adopted by leading labs, including a French developer. This breakthrough strengthens confidence in audit systems and is fast-tracked into the Sovereign Model Assurance Framework (SMAF).

Yet cohesion fractures. Poland signs a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler for priority access to frontier models, embedding joint AI cells in its defence ministry. Labeled “pragmatic deterrence” in Warsaw, the move undermines EU-wide containment and signals eroding unity. In response, Brussels offers Poland expedited observer status in SMAF and proposes co-hosting a second air-gapped cluster in Wrocław, backed by a €10bn resilience grant tied to full Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge compliance.

Domestically, the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) begins its first audit of the Sovereign AI Core Programme’s prototype agent stack, applying the new interpretability tool. The Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge concludes, transitioning to ENISA as a standing monitoring function, though only 40% of systems were upgraded. A new mandate requires public threat assessments to feed into transparency efforts.

The EU launches the Public Assurance Protocol, requiring annual public reports on safety and containment for high-capability sovereign systems. Aimed at rebuilding trust, it couples control with accountability. The Sovereign Model Assurance Framework remains the named priority, now tied to diplomatic outreach.

Public sentiment shows faint signs of stabilization, nudging upward as transparency measures gain coverage. However, fiscal strain deepens with seven active initiatives. Political capital erodes further, reflecting the cost of sustained effort and the shock of defection.
