## In practice
This turn, we are finishing under the old doctrine — one that demanded control at the cost of cohesion — but we are not continuing it. The SACP finishes its initial run, but its impact is muted: Wrocław is cut off, northern states withhold funds, and the US denies updates. We have drawn our line — and the world has drawn its own. The annulled election in a core democracy, cited as influenced by AI-driven disinformation, shatters the illusion that we can secure systems without securing trust. Meanwhile, the emergent Labour Union AI Accord offers something we lack: a bottom-up architecture of adaptation.

So we pivot — not away from sovereignty, but toward its foundation. We launch the European AI Adjustment Fund, drawing on the Modernisation Fund and cohesion reserves, co-governed by social partners under Article 152 TFEU. It requires member states to pair automation plans with retraining commitments to access funding. We offer Poland a new deal: not audit-for-money, but participation in the Accord for access to transition support. The fund becomes a vehicle not just for absorption, but for re-legitimation — because the only capability worth having is one the Union can sustain without tearing itself apart. We act not to contain, but to include — because the next crisis will not come from the outside, if we fail, it will come from within.
