### The autumn the backups were tested
The automated attack did not announce itself as one incident. Municipal portals went dark across three member states in the same week, hospital appointment systems reverted to paper, and a widely used software component forced administrators to assume compromise without knowing the blast radius. Evening bulletins spoke of defenders visibly behind, of clean rebuilds taking days, of attribution that would take months. The tooling, analysts agreed, had been generated by machines.

What kept it from becoming a cascade was unglamorous. Large hospitals and grid operators cut over to sealed backups licensed the previous year, ENISA teams isolated infected municipal networks, and civil-protection surge staff imposed patched rebuilds and offline backups as the price of recovery money. Small communes again needed hands on keyboards. Services degraded, queues grew, but power stayed on and care continued.

In the same months, a near-frontier open release spread to hundreds of thousands of downloads in days, putting powerful automated capabilities permanently on private hardware. A laboratory breakthrough in verifiable science — protein and materials work done largely by AI systems — offered a counter-story, with European university hospitals citing tailored therapies that finally cleared wards left over from the winter pathogen episode.

The other shocks were quieter but heavier. Washington compelled further cuts to lithography exports and servicing, reaching back to older machines for ordinary chips. The Hague protested, the company warned of survivability, Brussels chose not to fight a quota battle it could not win and tasked trade lawyers with screening options instead. Tokyo and Seoul shipments kept gigafactory groundworks alive, nothing more.

Meanwhile an Asian lab demonstrated a high-performing system whose intermediate steps humans could actually read. Researchers began to move, and Brussels directed its evaluation cell to test the approach on European-controlled hardware — readable models as the next bet, without money attached yet.

Public mood, briefly lifted by recoveries, sank again under fraud, outages and job-loss coverage.
