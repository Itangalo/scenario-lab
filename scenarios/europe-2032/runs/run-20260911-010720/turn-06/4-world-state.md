### The patch and the split
The first half of 2029 broke the fragile calm of December. A sudden leap in verifiable skills — code, maths, intrusion tooling — appeared in a closed demonstration and, within weeks, in an openly downloadable package mirrored hundreds of thousands of times. Municipal IT staff who had survived the November probes now faced the same techniques repackaged for anyone with hardware.

At the same time the leading labs acknowledged they could no longer show readable intermediate reasoning. The best-performing systems no longer thought in words. Oversight teams that relied on reviewing step-by-step traces found their tools blind overnight. European safety researchers called it a loss disguised as a win.

Brussels pushed its containment answer to towns and hospitals: offline recovery kits, locked backup systems, reserve licences switched on, and aid money made conditional on streaming network and sequencing data to the joint command and pathogen team. Health and cyber agencies, newly seconded to allied structures, absorbed the load unevenly. Large hospitals degraded gracefully; smaller municipalities stalled for lack of staff.

The supply front cracked politically. One member state signed its own capacity arrangement with a foreign hyperscaler, undercutting the common line on Europe-anchored supply and screening. The Commission answered with mediation on the gigafactory sites and threats to withhold recovery funds, while its small coordination cell courted Japanese and Korean suppliers as an alternative. One factory site moved toward a final decision, the other stayed in court.

Washington, under its new administration focused on moratoriums and job guarantees, offered little cover. American progress slowed for political reasons, and Asian capitals quietly moved into the space.

Public mood soured. Assistants remained popular, but headlines about unreadable models, unscreened DNA offers and a Union unable to hold its members together dominated.
