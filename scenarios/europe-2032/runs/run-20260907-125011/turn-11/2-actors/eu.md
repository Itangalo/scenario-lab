## In practice
This turn, we act on the dual failure exposed by recent events: we can now contain rogue AI in the lab, but we cannot yet protect our societies when real systems are weaponised. The **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Initiative** launches immediately under the Union Civil Protection Mechanism, co-financed by the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with €1.2 billion allocated over three years. We are convening an emergency COREPER meeting next week to fast-track the legal basis under Article 194 TFEU, treating systemic cyber fragility as a transnational risk on par with energy or pandemic shock. The initiative will deploy AI-powered detection stacks — developed in collaboration with the new Agency and hardened during the Boundary Trials — into 150 priority sites: national power grid operators, central health data hubs, and electoral commissions in high-exposure member states. These systems will run locally, with autonomous failover protocols and zero-trust verification, ensuring that even if cloud-linked AI services are compromised, core functions degrade gracefully and remain under human oversight.

We are negotiating with national governments to waive standard procurement rules for this class of defence upgrade, offering 85% EU co-funding and technical support from a newly activated task force within ENISA. Resistance is expected from states with under-resourced IT infrastructures and from operators who fear operational disruption, so we are pairing the rollout with immediate workforce transition grants and incident response drills to be conducted in Q1 2032. This is not a long-term fix — it is triage. But it is necessary triage: we cannot afford another election disrupted, another hospital paralysed. While the Agency builds our long-term muscle in evaluation and containment, this measure ensures that, should a breach occur, it does not become a collapse. The two efforts are distinct but aligned: one defends the machine, the other defends the society it serves.
