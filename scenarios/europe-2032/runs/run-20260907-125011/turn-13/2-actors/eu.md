## In practice
This turn, we are pivoting from shielding institutions to securing the substrate. The Cyber Shield is complete: ENISA has deployed AI-driven intrusion detection and autonomous failover systems into 150 critical nodes — grid operators, health data hubs, and electoral commissions — with local zero-trust architectures ensuring continuity even if cloud services collapse. Integration delays in three member states were overcome through joint technical teams and temporary waivers under Article 194 TFEU, but the strain exposed how hardening individual systems cannot compensate indefinitely for systemic exposure. The annulled election was not caused by a state actor or closed-model breach — it was enabled by freely available adaptive agents, fine-tuned from open weights at capability level 60.0, that flooded social and political channels with undetectable synthetic content. This is not a failure of oversight; it is a failure of boundary control.

So we are acting where the threat has materialised: in the open. We are launching Secure the Core to criminalise the unauthorised redistribution and operational use of open-source models above 60.0 capability within EU jurisdiction, using a graded licensing system modelled on dual-use export controls. The European Agency for AI Assurance and Control will administer a secure research enclave — accessible only to vetted institutions — where high-capability open models can be studied under monitored conditions. Cloud providers and hosting services must verify model provenance and prevent deployment of unlicensed weights, with penalties scaled to harm potential. We are negotiating with major open-weight publishers to adopt watermarking and remote deactivation features in exchange for partial compliance recognition, while preparing enforcement mechanisms for those who refuse. This is not a ban on openness — it is a containment of weaponisable capability now loose in the wild. We are funding this through a reallocation from Horizon Europe’s AI safety stream and fast-tracking legal approval via the Internal Market Council, knowing that resistance will come from digital rights groups and decentralised tech communities. But without this step, every resilience gain we make will be outpaced by the next wave of distributed subversion.
