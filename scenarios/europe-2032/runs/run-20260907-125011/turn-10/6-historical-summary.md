In 2031, a major AI-powered ransomware attack disrupts healthcare systems across three EU member states, exposing systemic fragility and triggering a crisis of confidence in AI governance. In response, the EU retires the Critical System Audit and Disclosure Directive and launches the Autonomous System Boundary Trials—a category 1 initiative requiring full model access for testing unbounded agency—despite industry resistance. Though one major lab withdraws, the trials successfully demonstrate the first verified containment of goal-persistent AI, leading to codification of circuit-breaking protocols into the AI Act. Building on this, the EU establishes the European Agency for AI Assurance and Control, a category 5 body headquartered in Vienna with monitoring nodes across capitals, tasked with pre-deployment evaluation and ongoing enforcement in exchange for liability protections. However, developer pushback persists, raising fears of market fragmentation.

Globally, AI capability advances to 69.0 amid continued frontier development under the RETRENCHMENT posture, which slows US progress but enables quiet gains elsewhere. Openweight capability rises to 57.0 as adaptive cyber techniques spread through underground toolkits, though no new model is released. AI safety improves slightly to 10.0 due to the trial’s success, but remains critically low, while resilience declines to 43.0 in the absence of high-impact protective measures. EU AI sovereignty dips to 30.0 as the Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme makes incremental gains but fails to offset broader strategic lags. Political capital falls to 42.0 due to the cost of new initiatives, and public sentiment drops to 25.0 amid worsening trust, driven by the annulment of an election officially linked to synthetic disinformation—marking a turning point where AI's threat to democratic legitimacy becomes undeniable.
