## In practice
This turn, we are executing on triage. The Cyber Shield is live: €1.2 billion is mobilised under the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with legal authority fast-tracked through Article 194 TFEU and emergency approval secured from COREPER. ENISA leads a dedicated task force to deploy AI-powered intrusion detection and autonomous failover systems into 150 high-priority sites — national grid operators, central health data hubs, and electoral commissions — with a focus on local, zero-trust operation to ensure functionality even if cloud services are compromised. We are waiving standard procurement rules in exchange for 85% co-funding, but integration delays in legacy hospital IT systems in three member states are already exposing the gap between policy intent and technical reality. We are dispatching joint technical teams to bridge those gaps, but the clock is tight — full deployment must land by turn 13, or we risk another cascade during the autumn election cycle.

Simultaneously, the European Agency for AI Assurance and Control is scaling up operations in Vienna, with pre-deployment evaluation protocols now tested in pilot audits. Developer resistance remains high, particularly from US-based frontier labs unwilling to submit to ex-ante scrutiny, but the Boundary Trials have given us technical credibility. We are using that leverage to demand access, not just to models, but to training data and agent logs — a step toward real-time monitoring. Yet even as we build this long-term muscle, the labour displacement wave cannot be ignored. The graduate market is hollowing out. That is why we are launching the Labour Market Stabilisation Fund: €800 million in new funding to pair wage insurance with retraining in AI-augmented professions, targeted at law, accountancy, and software. We are negotiating with member states to co-finance employer incentives that reward role redesign over headcount reduction. This is not protectionism — it is adaptation with dignity. Without it, public sentiment will erode further, and our political capital will bleed faster than it can be replenished.
