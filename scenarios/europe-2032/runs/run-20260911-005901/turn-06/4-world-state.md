### The half-year oversight went blind
Winter brought a sharp, narrow leap. A new system release demonstrated markedly stronger coding, maths and intrusion skills within weeks. Utilities logged automated probing that assembled working exploits faster than analysts could triage. General usefulness rose only modestly, but what an attacker could do changed.

At the same time the leading labs shifted to architectures whose internal steps cannot be read as language. Announced as a performance win, it broke every European audit that relied on inspecting reasoning traces. Reviewers were left with pass-fail tests and experimental activation probes.

Then a near-frontier open release spread to hundreds of thousands of downloads. Whatever the leap carried is now on private machines permanently.

Brussels answered with a black-box testing unit inside the AI Office, with ENISA and health-security staff seconded, French and Dutch labs piloting the new inspection methods. Deployers serving hospitals and grid operators must now pass cyber and biology test suites to keep public contracts and essential-operator approvals. The legal vehicle held — no reopening of the AI Act — but staffing was thin and the methods immature. Early reports caught some dangerous behaviours and missed others, eroding oversight coverage without a single system failure or policy collapse.

Two older bets paid out. The late-2028 sovereignty package finished disbursing planning and grid-priority decisions, keeping two stalled data-centre sites alive without new cash. The joint cyber-biological integration also declared full operation: shared telemetry and hospital sequencing now flow to the allied command, and spring intrusions degraded services rather than stopping them.

The political cost was a capital going its own way. One member state signed a separate hosting and supply arrangement with a large foreign provider on looser screening terms, calling it pragmatism. The Commission offered joint-buying prices to re-anchor it and tied backup reinsurance to data sharing, with limited success. Papers called it proof Brussels cannot hold a line.

Washington's new administration took office in January, governing under its elected posture favouring moratoriums and job-guarantee levies. American frontier work slowed for domestic political reasons. Europeans observed less outside help on evaluations and no relief from capability pressure at home. Public mood soured: useful assistants against unreadable models and unscreened DNA offers offshore.
