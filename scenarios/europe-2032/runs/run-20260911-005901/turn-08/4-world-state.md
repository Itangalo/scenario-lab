### The sweep and the strain
Winter into spring brought a hard shock. A largely automated ransomware wave swept municipal offices, hospital IT systems and two power distributors within days, built around fresh exploits no triage team recognised. Appointments were cancelled, records went to paper, substations load-shed. Attribution was still open in June. Briefings noted the tooling looked assembled with modern model assistance.

At the same time, trade press carried conflicting rumours about test results from an unreleased system, with no access for Brussels to check. The testing cell built for opaque models could only run pass-fail probes on deployed hospital and grid suppliers.

### Restoration holds, synthesis proposal starts
The restoration surge finished just in time. ENISA-led teams, clean backups and reinsurance tied to telemetry sharing got large operators back in weeks. Smaller towns waited longer, and the capital with its separate hosting deal took only partial help. Still, services degraded rather than stopped, and the middle-power compact kept trusted replacements flowing.

That operational work did not translate into political ease. The Commission tabled a single new instrument, a Decision on screened synthesis enforcement and bio-detection, as its current priority. Under Single Market and public-health bases, it would in time extend screening duties, seizure powers, and detection upgrades across customs, postal hubs and hospital labs. Health and justice ministers carried the votes; blockading towns were promised restoration priority.

Implementation is early and uneven. Seizures continued as offshore providers openly courted European buyers. Enforcement remains a Decision on paper, not yet common practice, and joint enforcement abroad achieved little. Washington, retrenched and inward, offered no help. The portfolio load — compact, assurance cell, restoration surge, plus the new priority — weighed on the centre.

Public mood fell further. Dark council offices and nurses on printouts outweighed working assistants. Siting fights over the two gigafactory compounds — fenced, with substations, still without chips — hardened into court filings and a few blockades, rumoured and locally reported rather than confirmed.
