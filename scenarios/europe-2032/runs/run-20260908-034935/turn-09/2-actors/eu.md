## Statement changes  
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Ensure no AI-enabled attack vector can remain untraceable or unattributable at scale``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn  
- Grounds: The previous commitment—preventing open-weight proliferation from becoming an unmanageable vector of systemic risk—was overtaken by events: `openweight_capability` has already reached 63.0, and the collapse of the Open-Weight Containment Framework confirmed that pre-release controls are unenforceable without U.S. cooperation. The frontier has moved from *preventing diffusion* to *managing consequences at scale*. The new commitment shifts focus from containment to consequence management: we now accept that dangerous models are loose, but we will not accept that harm can be done in secret. Attribution, observability, and forensic resilience are now the floor.

``modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only when the action preserves optionality or builds observability``  
- Trigger: member_state_defection  
- Grounds: A member state has unilaterally negotiated access to a hyperscaler’s frontier models under terms that bypass EU-wide safeguards, justifying it as “pragmatism.” This fracture reveals that our current instruments lack both cohesion and credibility. We acted under uncertainty before — but now we see that acting without unity is not just risky, it is self-defeating. We are not retreating from action; we are re-centering it on coherence. From now on, this commitment includes an additional constraint: actions must be *cohesive*, meaning they are either binding across the bloc or explicitly experimental with a sunset clause. Fragmentation is now a cost we count.

``modify `we_govern_the_diffusion` (identity): We are responsible for governing AI where it acts — in our markets, on our infrastructure, through our institutions — regardless of where it was developed or released. Our sovereignty now means shaping consequences, not just controlling origins, and we will not allow jurisdictional fragmentation to undermine that duty``  
- Trigger: member_state_defection  
- Grounds: The defection is not just a political setback — it is a direct challenge to the EU’s capacity to speak and act as one. Our identity must now reflect that we do not merely *govern diffusion* — we *must hold the line* against internal divergence as much as external risk. This is no longer optional: if we cannot enforce common standards across member states, no external measure will stand. We are adding a binding expectation of unity to our self-conception.

## Portfolio  
- `Distributed Misuse Attribution Network` (category 2, costs 3 per turn, started turn 7, finishes on turn 9): A EU-led technical and legal infrastructure to trace harmful AI deployments back to their source, even when models have been widely copied and modified — **finished**  
- `Sovereign Model Provenance Pilot` (category 5, costs 2 per turn, started turn 8, finishes on turn 10): Launch a mandatory pilot programme requiring all sovereign AI systems funded or hosted in the EU to embed cryptographically signed provenance markers at training time, enabling future attribution even after modification  
- `Secure Inference Initiative` (category 6, costs 3 per turn, started turn 9, finishes on turn 11): Require all high-capability inference performed on EU soil — whether open or closed, public or private — to run on auditable hardware stacks with runtime monitoring for anomalous behaviour, cryptographically logged execution traces, and enforced isolation between tenants

Canceled measure: `Distributed Misuse Attribution Network`. The network completes its initial deployment this turn, delivering baseline tracing capability to Europol and ENISA. However, its design assumed voluntary cooperation from hosting providers, and recent pushback from national cloud operators has revealed enforcement gaps. Rather than extend it, we are folding its core protocols into the `Secure Inference Initiative`, where they will be mandatory. This consolidates our attribution strategy under a single, enforceable standard.

## New measure  
**Pan-European Inference Audit Corps**  
Establish a standing technical unit under ENISA with authority to conduct unannounced runtime audits of high-capacity inference clusters across the EU, using automated probes and forensic replay to detect misuse, model leakage, or evasion of monitoring requirements.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size:` small  
`Finishes on turn:` 10  
`Targeted effect:` increases `ai_safety` by +5–8 points by enabling independent verification of runtime compliance and exposing hidden misuse patterns; strengthens `resilience` by validating the effectiveness of the Secure Inference Initiative  
`Applies to:` all data centres operating in the EU above 1 exaFLOP-day/month, including foreign-owned hyperscalers and national research clusters

## Priority  
**Secure Inference Initiative** — because `ai_safety` has collapsed to 1.0 while `ai_capability` approaches 80.0, and the `member_state_defection` proves that voluntary frameworks fail when tested — we must now enforce observability at scale, and we must do it before the next incident becomes untraceable.

## In practice  
We are finalizing the delegated acts for the Secure Inference Initiative under NIS3, with ENISA drafting the technical standards for trusted execution environments and remote attestation. Germany and France have agreed to harmonize their national initiatives, but only under condition of EU-level liability shielding — we are fast-tracking a liability carve-out in the Digital Services Act revision to secure their buy-in. The Commission is preparing infringement proceedings against any member state that attempts to establish a divergent regime, citing Article 114 TFEU on internal market distortions.

We are also integrating the Pan-European Inference Audit Corps into ENISA’s new AI Security Division, staffing it with seconded experts from national cyber agencies and vetted academics. The first audit protocols will be field-tested in Q1 2031 on three major cloud providers, using synthetic adversarial workloads to probe for evasion. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme, reprogrammed from expired pilot lines. This is not optional oversight — it is the price of operating at scale in our market. We are making clear: if you want to run powerful AI in Europe, you must let us see what it does.
