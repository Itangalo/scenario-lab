# Statements: The European Union (turn 9)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only when the action preserves optionality or builds observability
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Ensure no AI-enabled attack vector can remain untraceable or unattributable at scale
- `we_govern_the_diffusion` (identity): We are responsible for governing AI where it acts — in our markets, on our infrastructure, through our institutions — regardless of where it was developed or released. Our sovereignty now means shaping consequences, not just controlling origins, and we will not allow jurisdictional fragmentation to undermine that duty

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Ensure no AI-enabled attack vector can remain untraceable or unattributable at scale
  - Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
  - Grounds: The previous commitment—preventing open-weight proliferation from becoming an unmanageable vector of systemic risk—was overtaken by events: `openweight_capability` has already reached 63.0, and the collapse of the Open-Weight Containment Framework confirmed that pre-release controls are unenforceable without U.S. cooperation. The frontier has moved from *preventing diffusion* to *managing consequences at scale*. The new commitment shifts focus from containment to consequence management: we now accept that dangerous models are loose, but we will not accept that harm can be done in secret. Attribution, observability, and forensic resilience are now the floor.
  - Evidence quoted: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
  - Reason: The expiration of the two-year period directly ends the original commitment's timeframe, changing the actor's obligation and enabling a strategic shift in focus.
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only when the action preserves optionality or builds observability
  - Trigger: member_state_defection
  - Grounds: A member state has unilaterally negotiated access to a hyperscaler’s frontier models under terms that bypass EU-wide safeguards, justifying it as “pragmatism.” This fracture reveals that our current instruments lack both cohesion and credibility. We acted under uncertainty before — but now we see that acting without unity is not just risky, it is self-defeating. We are not retreating from action; we are re-centering it on coherence. From now on, this commitment includes an additional constraint: actions must be *cohesive*, meaning they are either binding across the bloc or explicitly experimental with a sunset clause. Fragmentation is now a cost we count.
  - Evidence quoted: A member state cuts its own arrangement – with a hyperscaler, with Washington, or with Beijing – on terms that undercut a position the Union has taken. It is defended at home as pragmatism and read everywhere else as the Union being unable to hold its own line.
  - Reason: The defection directly challenges the EU's ability to act cohesively, altering the cost of committing under uncertainty by making fragmentation a tangible risk.
