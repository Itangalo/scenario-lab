### The Attribution Gamble  
The EU completes the Distributed Misuse Attribution Network, delivering baseline forensic tools to Europol and ENISA. But enthusiasm fades as national cloud providers resist mandatory integration, exposing the limits of soft governance. In response, the Commission folds the network’s protocols into the Secure Inference Initiative, now the bloc’s cornerstone for runtime control. The new Pan-European Inference Audit Corps is launched under ENISA, tasked with unannounced audits of high-capacity clusters. Yet early staffing delays and legal challenges from two member states cast doubt on its reach.

### Fractures Deepen  
A member state bypasses EU safeguards by negotiating direct access to a U.S. hyperscaler’s frontier models, citing economic urgency. Brussels responds with threats of infringement proceedings under Article 114 TFEU, but unity frays. Germany and France condition full cooperation on liability protections, forcing a rushed carve-out in the Digital Services Act revision. The Secure Inference Initiative, now the named priority, faces fragmented implementation—its success hinging on unresolved legal battles.

### Capability Accelerates, Safety Stalls  
Global AI development accelerates, with `ai_capability` rising by +4.0 to 82.5, as frontier labs report self-optimizing training loops and faster iteration cycles. Retrospective analysis will later confirm `rsi_onset` had begun. Yet `ai_safety` remains at 1.0—no shipped systems reflect recent advances in interpretability or control. The gap widens, but no corrective measure lands.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Shrinks  
`eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 14.0, hit by the ongoing capability rise (−1) and the `member_state_defection` event, which undermines bloc cohesion and access integrity (−1).
