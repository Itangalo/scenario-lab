### Europe Reacts — But Stretches Thin  
The EU initiated a push on resilience in response to a coordinated intrusion across energy grids and critical ports — including two EU members. Brussels fast-tracked the Cyber Shield Directive, with France and the Netherlands advocating for immediate adoption and Spain and Italy conditionally supporting it in exchange for commitments on cyber reserve deployment. However, Poland demanded veto authority over joint operations, and Germany raised legal concerns about cross-border data access, preventing Council consensus. As a result, the directive remains in negotiation, with no binding implementation this turn. Progress is preparatory: planning advanced, but no operational capabilities were fielded.

Industrial efforts under the InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package continued. Site selection advanced in Marseille and Wrocław, but German resistance to grid strain delayed the Saxony-Anhalt proposal. ASML held quiet talks with the Commission but refused to commit to Leuven without ironclad liability protections from both Washington and Brussels.

Public sentiment held steady at 42.0, though media discourse increasingly framed the moment as one of technological urgency, with recurring themes of “AI outpacing control” and “dependency humiliation” as U.S. labs again set the pace.
