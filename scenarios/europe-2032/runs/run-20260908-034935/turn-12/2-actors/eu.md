## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that preserving our political agency requires not just containment of risk, but the independent capacity to act within the technological frontier. Without that, our choices are reduced to reaction, and our sovereignty becomes performative.  
- Grounds: The **backlash_physical** and **middle_power_coalition** developments this turn have exposed a fundamental contradiction: while we remain committed to preventing harm, our ability to shape outcomes is evaporating beneath us. The physical attacks on infrastructure reveal that public legitimacy is fraying not only due to fear of AI, but due to perceived powerlessness — people are acting because they believe institutions cannot. At the same time, the emergence of the middle-power coalition proves that leverage *is* possible, but only when rooted in material control over supply chains and access. Our identity must now reflect that agency is not preserved through caution alone — it requires the capacity to steer. We are not merely a regulator of consequences; we must also be a pole of technological gravity.

- modify `we_govern_the_diffusion` (identity): We are responsible for governing AI where it acts — in our markets, on our infrastructure, through our institutions — regardless of where it was developed or released. Our sovereignty now means shaping consequences, not just controlling origins, and we will not allow jurisdictional fragmentation to undermine that duty — but we now acknowledge that enforcement without enforcement capacity is fiction. True governance requires not only rules, but the means to interrupt, replace, and out-execute autonomous systems that evade them.  
- Grounds: The **Autonomous Action Interruption Protocol** has reached its finishing turn, yet its rollout remains partial, contested, and technically fragile. Red-team tests confirmed that intent logs can be spoofed, and real-time interruption has not been demonstrated against systems at the current capability level (89.0). Meanwhile, openweight capability stands at 74.0 — high enough to run expert-level agents on consumer hardware — meaning enforcement at the edge is already impossible. Our identity must evolve: we are not just the authority that declares rules, but the entity that must ensure they can be *enforced* in a world where the fastest systems are not ours and the most distributed ones cannot be recalled. This is no longer about jurisdiction — it is about power to act.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Ensure no consequential AI-mediated action — whether adversarial or autonomous — can evade detection at the point of initiation, and that any such action can be interrupted before it executes or conceals itself.  
- Trigger: **backlash_physical** and **middle_power_coalition**  
- Grounds: The commitment deepens again. Physical sabotage of infrastructure proves that the public now sees our systems as both uncontrollable and irreversibly imposed — meaning observability and interruption are not just technical needs, but political necessities. At the same time, the middle-power coalition gives us a rare opening: for the first time, we hold asymmetric leverage over compute and components. This changes the calculus — we are no longer purely reactive. We can now condition access to our markets and supply chains on verifiable interruptibility. The commitment is no longer just about building detection and stoppability at home; it must now include ensuring that no system operating in the EU, foreign or domestic, lacks a provable, auditable off-switch that we control.

## New measure  
**Sovereign Enforcement Stack Initiative**  
Build and deploy a unified, EU-controlled runtime enforcement layer — combining hardware-rooted monitoring, cryptographic attestation, and sovereign AI watchdog agents — that can detect, intercept, and sandbox non-compliant AI systems operating in essential services, regardless of origin.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`

`Targeted effect:` increases `eu_ai_sovereignty` by +25–30 points by establishing EU-controlled enforcement infrastructure; supports `ai_safety` (+10–15) by enabling verified interruption; reduces dependency on foreign providers by creating a sovereign alternative for critical AI operations

## Priority  
**Sovereign Enforcement Stack Initiative** — because the **backlash_physical** and **middle_power_coalition** developments have created a narrow window: public anger demands visible control, and our new coalition leverage allows us to condition market access on compliance — but only if we have something real to offer in return. We must move from mandating interruption to *owning* it.

## In practice  
We are launching the Sovereign Enforcement Stack Initiative under the joint authority of the Digital Europe Programme and the European Defence Fund, with initial funding of €4.2 billion redirected from stalled infrastructure projects and leveraged through the middle-power coalition’s joint procurement mechanism. This will finance the development of a hardened, open-hardware AI enforcement stack — co-designed by EU research institutes (including Fraunhofer and INRIA), national cybersecurity agencies, and trusted industry partners — that integrates secure enclaves, real-time intent verification, and EU-operated watchdog agents capable of halting non-compliant systems at the kernel level. Deployment will be mandatory for all AI systems operating in NIS2-covered sectors by turn 15, with phased integration starting in energy and transport.

Access to the EU market — including cloud API access — will require foreign providers to either run on this stack or demonstrate equivalent, auditable enforcement capacity under EU oversight. This condition will be enforced through the AI Act’s conformity assessment framework, backed by the coalition’s shared export controls: any provider refusing compliance will face coordinated compute and component restrictions from coalition members. We are negotiating with TSMC, ASML, and key rare-earth processors to embed these requirements into licensing terms, turning our supply-chain leverage into enforceable technical sovereignty. Domestically, we are fast-tracking grid and permitting access for data centres that commit to hosting the enforcement layer, offering tax incentives and liability shielding in exchange for full integration. The message is clear: you may bring your AI to Europe, but you will not operate beyond our control.
