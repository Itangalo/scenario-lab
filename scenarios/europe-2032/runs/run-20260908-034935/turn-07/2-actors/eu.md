## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent open-weight proliferation from becoming an unmanageable vector of systemic risk — extended for another two years, with sharpened focus on *attribution as enforcement*, because the Open-Weight Containment Framework has failed to secure compliance at scale and the Distributed Misuse Attribution Network now represents our best path not just to trace harm but to impose consequences. The commitment is no longer about stopping diffusion — that moment has passed — but about ensuring that every model in circulation carries a liability trail that leads back to a responsible actor.  
- modify `we_govern_the_diffusion` (identity): We are responsible for governing AI where it acts — in our markets, on our infrastructure, through our institutions — regardless of where it was developed or released. Our sovereignty now means shaping consequences, not just controlling origins — and when containment fails, accountability must still hold. This identity must now include the reality that we govern *after* release, not before, and that legitimacy depends on delivering justice after harm, not only preventing it in theory.

**Trigger:** The collapse of enforcement under the Open-Weight Containment Framework due to non-compliance by GitHub Europe and Hugging Face, combined with the ongoing loss_of_control_incident investigation showing that attribution remains incomplete despite technical proof-of-concept work.  
**Grounds:** The original formulation assumed that binding pre-release controls could be operationalised quickly via DSA gatekeepers. That assumption has failed: legal resistance, jurisdictional ambiguity, and platform self-interest have broken the chain of enforcement. We now know that governance must survive the absence of cooperation — and so our identity must reflect that we govern even where we do not control, and our commitment must pivot to the one lever still functioning: forensic attribution as the basis for retrospective accountability.

## In practice  
We are shifting from containment to consequence. The Open-Weight Containment Framework remains on the books, but its credibility hinges on enforcement we cannot currently deliver. DG COMP and ENISA are drafting infringement proceedings against GitHub Europe and Hugging Face for non-compliance with Article 16(3) obligations, but legal action will take months — time we do not have. Instead, we are redirecting political capital toward making the Distributed Misuse Attribution Network operational by turn 9. Under JRC leadership, Europol’s AI Incident Response Cell is now integrating forensic tools from Toulouse and Leiden into a unified pipeline capable of reconstructing model lineage even when weights have been altered. We are tasking them with producing a public incident report on the loss_of_control_incident by turn 8 — not just to assign blame, but to demonstrate that tracing works, even if slowly.

At the same time, we are launching the Sovereign Model Provenance Pilot under Horizon Europe’s digital sovereignty strand. This requires all new models trained on EuroHPC or InvestAI infrastructure to embed verifiable provenance data — training dataset hashes, contributor identities, modification logs — into their architecture at birth. The goal is not to stop leaks, but to ensure that when a fork appears in the wild, we can say: this came from Sofia, modified in Berlin, deployed in Bucharest. This builds technical legitimacy for future liability regimes. We are offering participating labs early access to the Attribution Network’s analysis suite in exchange for full cooperation, and shielding them from civil exposure for good-faith participation.

Public sentiment remains fragile. The automated_decision_scandal in Dutch social insurance has hardened resistance to any AI system touching public services, and we cannot afford to appear reactive again. So we are pairing the Attribution Network’s rollout with a transparency campaign: “Traceable AI” labels for all sovereign systems, backed by a public portal showing which models are under audit, which incidents have been resolved, and who was held accountable. It won’t restore trust overnight — but it may stop the erosion.
