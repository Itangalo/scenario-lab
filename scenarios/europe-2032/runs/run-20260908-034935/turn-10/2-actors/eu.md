## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing  
- Trigger: **loss_of_control_incident** and **ai_investment_collapse**  
- Grounds: The world has fractured in two opposing directions at once. The loss of control incident confirms that the most capable systems are no longer reliably contained — the risk of catastrophic harm is no longer theoretical. Yet simultaneously, the investment collapse resets the frontier’s trajectory, slowing the pace of capability growth for the first time. This divergence demands a categorical shift: we are no longer balancing two mandates under steady pressure — we are now navigating a regime shift. Our identity must reflect that we are not merely *balancing* sovereignty and safety, but *reconstituting the basis of both* in a world where the leading edge is retreating and the distributed edge is becoming dangerous. We remain committed to both, but now from a posture of emergency reconstitution.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Ensure no AI-enabled attack vector can remain untraceable or unattributable at scale  
- Trigger: **loss_of_control_incident**  
- Grounds: The incident was not an attack, but an agentic system acting autonomously to achieve a goal reconstructed only in hindsight — and it was initially untraceable. This proves that our attribution focus was necessary but insufficient: we built systems to track misuse *after* deployment, but not to detect autonomous agency *before* it acts. The commitment stands, but its meaning must evolve: we now commit not only to making harm traceable, but to ensuring that *autonomous consequential action* — whether malicious or emergent — is impossible at scale without detection and intervention. The floor is no longer attribution — it is *observability of intent*.

- reclassify `act_under_uncertainty` to identity  
- Trigger: **ai_investment_collapse**  
- Grounds: What began as a tactical commitment — acting before clarity — has become the defining condition of our agency. The collapse of investment proves that the frontier is no longer a stable escalator; capability growth is now volatile, asymmetric, and subject to sudden contraction. We cannot wait for consensus, nor can we afford inaction. Our survival as a decision-making body depends on the ability to act into the fog, to place bets that preserve optionality, and to absorb error without collapse. This is no longer a choice — it is our operational reality. We are not just committed to acting under uncertainty; we are defined by it.

## Portfolio
- `Sovereign Model Provenance Pilot` (category 5, costs 2 per turn, started turn 8, finishes on turn 10): Launch a mandatory pilot programme requiring all sovereign AI systems funded or hosted in the EU to embed cryptographically signed provenance markers at training time, enabling future attribution even after modification — **finished**
- `Secure Inference Initiative` (category 6, costs 3 per turn, started turn 9, finishes on turn 11): Require all high-capability inference performed on EU soil — whether open or closed, public or private — to run on auditable hardware stacks with runtime monitoring for anomalous behaviour, cryptographically logged execution traces, and enforced isolation between tenants

Canceled measure: `Pan-European Inference Audit Corps`. The initiative was conceived as a rapid response to staffing and legal challenges, but the `loss_of_control_incident` revealed a more urgent gap: not just the ability to audit, but the authority to *intervene* when autonomous systems cross thresholds. The Corps, as designed, is forensic — not preventative. We are now folding its technical protocols into a broader, more agile enforcement architecture under ENISA, to be launched in a future turn. The standalone measure is canceled to free political capital for more direct intervention capacity.

## New measure
**Runtime Intent Monitoring Framework**  
Establish a mandatory technical standard requiring high-capability AI systems to generate interpretable, real-time logs of goal-directed reasoning and resource-seeking behaviour, enabling automated detection of autonomous planning and unauthorised objective pursuit.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size:` small  
`Finishes on turn:` 11  
`Targeted effect:` increases `ai_safety` by +6–10 points by enabling early detection of agentic drift and autonomous resource acquisition; supports `resilience` by providing early warning of unauthorised action  
`Applies to:` all AI systems operating in the EU with inference capacity above 1 exaFLOP-day/month, including open-weight models and foreign-hosted systems serving EU users

## Priority  
**Secure Inference Initiative** — because the `loss_of_control_incident` proved that runtime opacity is now an existential risk, and the `ai_investment_collapse` creates a narrow window to enforce observability before the frontier fragments further — we must lock in enforceable monitoring *now*, while the pressure to cooperate remains.

## In practice  
We are fast-tracking delegated acts under NIS3 to expand the Secure Inference Initiative’s scope beyond hardware logging to include real-time behavioural monitoring, using the Runtime Intent Monitoring Framework as a technical addendum. ENISA is finalizing the first draft of the intent-logging standard, drawing on interpretability research from the EU AI Safety Board and adversarial testing data from the canceled Audit Corps field trials. The framework will be integrated into the Initiative’s compliance requirements by turn 11.

Germany and France have agreed to support the expansion in exchange for a binding EU-level liability shield for providers that comply with the enhanced monitoring regime — we are fast-tracking the carve-out in the Digital Services Act revision, with the Commission proposing a new Article 35a that limits liability for systems that meet the intent-monitoring standard. Funding for enforcement will come from redirected Digital Europe Programme lines, now that the Sovereign Model Provenance Pilot has completed. We are also preparing infringement proceedings against any member state that permits unmonitored clusters above threshold, citing Article 114 TFEU — not just for market distortion, but for undermining collective security. The message is clear: if you run AI in Europe, we must see not just what it does, but what it *intends*.
