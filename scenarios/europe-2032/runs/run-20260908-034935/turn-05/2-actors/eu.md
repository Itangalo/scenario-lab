## New measure  
**Open-Weight Containment Framework**  
Establish binding pre-release evaluation and deployment controls on open-weight models above 50.0 capability, including mandatory weight security, provenance tracking, and use restrictions for high-risk applications.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` slows growth of `openweight_capability` by +3–5 turns, modestly improves `ai_safety` by creating accountability at release points, and signals control over diffusion vectors to boost `public_sentiment`  
`Applies to:` all entities releasing or distributing open-weight models within the EU jurisdiction, including foreign developers with distribution networks in member states

## Priority  
**Open-Weight Containment Framework** — because the openweight frontier release has demonstrated that distributed capability is now the leading edge of systemic risk, and because the U.S. posture shift reduces the cost of lagging in sovereign development while increasing the cost of failing to govern what is already loose, this measure targets the most immediate and irreversible threat: AI capability beyond any authority’s reach.

## In practice  
We are acting through Article 16(3) of the AI Act to establish emergency powers for the European Commission to impose licensing conditions on open-weight releases exceeding 50.0 capability, justified by the surge in `openweight_capability` to 53.0 and the confirmed presence of agent coordination frameworks in the wild. The framework will require deposit of cryptographic hashes, proof of weight integrity, and enforceable downstream usage agreements for any model released above threshold — modeled on dual-use export controls but applied to software. DG GROW is drafting the implementing regulation, with technical input from ENISA and the JRC, and legal validation underway in the Commission’s Legal Service. A pilot enforcement mechanism will launch via the Digital Services Act gatekeeper provisions, requiring platforms like Hugging Face and GitHub Europe to verify compliance before hosting. France and Germany support the move; resistance is expected from Ireland and Estonia due to hosting-sector exposure, which we are addressing through transitional liability shields and co-funding for compliance infrastructure. Meanwhile, the Emergency Resilience Accelerator completes its first funding cycle: 27 qualifying projects were submitted, 19 approved, with €6.2bn disbursed from civil protection reserves. Poland remains outside full telemetry sharing but has agreed to delayed anonymized reporting under a HERA-mediated protocol. The Sovereign Compute Assurance Network conducts its first audit this month on the Sofia cluster, using newly fielded interpretability probes developed by the EU AI Research Commons team in Toulouse. Despite U.S. supply constraints, ASML’s Leuven hub has delivered partial tooling stacks under the InvestAI programme, keeping the Gigafactories on track for turn 7 commissioning.
