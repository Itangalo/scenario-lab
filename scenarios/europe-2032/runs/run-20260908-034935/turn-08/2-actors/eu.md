## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent open-weight proliferation from becoming an unmanageable vector of systemic risk — now explicitly reframed to ensure that **no harmful deployment of a model above 60.0 capability can occur without identifiable responsibility**, because the export_control_escalation and rsi_onset have rendered containment via access control obsolete, and opaque_reasoning has broken chain-of-thought oversight as a viable strategy. The commitment is no longer about slowing diffusion but about making it impossible to act with impunity: attribution must be our floor, not our ceiling.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — **but only when the action preserves optionality or builds observability**. After opaque_reasoning disabled all deployed interpretability tools and we discovered too late that corrective assurance had never landed on live systems, continuing to act blindly would not be courage — it would be negligence. This turn, we narrow the commitment: we still move fast, but we now insist on building visibility *into* every intervention, especially where we cannot verify outcomes in advance.  

**Trigger:** The convergence of `rsi_onset`, `opaque_reasoning`, and `ai_safety` remaining at 1.0 despite ongoing audits — revealing that even well-intentioned oversight failed to produce actionable understanding of deployed systems. The Union acted, but the mechanisms were blind to what mattered.  
**Grounds:** The original formulation treated speed as the dominant virtue. Now we know that acting under uncertainty without building feedback risks compounding error at scale. We are not retreating from boldness — we are redefining it: boldness now means moving fast *while instrumenting the darkness*, not through it.

## New measure  
**Secure Inference Initiative**  
Require all high-capability inference performed on EU soil — whether open or closed, public or private — to run on auditable hardware stacks with runtime monitoring for anomalous behaviour, cryptographically logged execution traces, and enforced isolation between tenants.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 11  
`Targeted effect:` increases `resilience` by +10–15 points over three turns by reducing attack surface and enabling rapid forensic response to misuse; indirectly supports `ai_safety` by creating observable runtime data where internal reasoning is no longer legible  
`Applies to:` all data centres operating in the EU with aggregate inference capacity above 1 exaFLOP-day/month, including foreign-owned hyperscalers and national research clusters

## Priority  
**Secure Inference Initiative** — because `opaque_reasoning` has broken all human-readable interpretability, rendering post-hoc audits meaningless unless we can observe what models *did*, not just what they said, and because `resilience` at 42.0 is insufficient to absorb attacks from broadly distributed 60.0+ agents now running autonomously across consumer and cloud infrastructure.

## In practice  
We are ending the illusion of upstream control. The `Open-Weight Containment Framework` is canceled: DG COMP confirms that infringement proceedings against Hugging Face and GitHub Europe will fail without U.S. cooperation, which is not forthcoming under the new export_control_escalation regime. That path is closed. Instead, we pivot hard to securing the point of action — the inference layer — where harm actually occurs and where we still have jurisdictional grip.

The Secure Inference Initiative launches under the NIS3 directive’s expanded scope, co-signed by ENISA and EuroCloud EU. It mandates that any provider operating inference clusters above 1 exaFLOP-day/month must implement trusted execution environments (TEEs) with remote attestation, runtime anomaly detection trained on adversarial patterns, and immutable logging of job metadata — not weights, but who ran what, when, and under which credentials. We are offering tax incentives to early adopters and shielding compliant operators from secondary liability in misuse cases. Crucially, we are exempting academic and SME users below threshold to avoid chilling innovation — this is not about control, it’s about accountability at scale.

Internally, we are aligning JRC, Europol, and the Distributed Misuse Attribution Network around a single technical standard: the logs from this initiative will feed directly into attribution pipelines, closing the loop between real-time observation and retrospective investigation. We are also briefing member states that this is not optional — Germany and France have already begun national versions; we are harmonizing them now to prevent fragmentation. The cost is high, but the alternative — a continent of blind inference nodes feeding autonomous attacks no one can stop or trace — is unacceptable. We are no longer trying to lock the barn door before the horse leaves. We are wiring the whole field with cameras, and we are making sure someone is watching.
