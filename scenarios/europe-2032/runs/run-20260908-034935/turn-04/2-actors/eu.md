## New measure  
**Emergency Resilience Accelerator**  
Fast-track cyber hardening and operational continuity planning for essential public services most exposed to AI-driven attacks, starting with health, energy, and transport.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` increases `resilience` by +10–15 through mandatory incident response exercises, hardened telemetry, and failover capacity in critical sectors; modestly improves `public_sentiment` by demonstrating institutional reliability in the face of disruption  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with binding requirements for national operators of essential services in health, energy, and rail transport

## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Accelerator** — because the surge in `ai_capability` to 62.0 and the collapse in `ai_safety` to 16.0 mean that catastrophic failure is no longer a hypothetical but an imminent operational risk, and because the retirement of the Cyber Shield Directive has left a gaping hole in our preparedness, this measure addresses the most immediate threat to the Union’s stability — not the frontier of capability, but the fragility of our foundations.

## In practice  
We are launching the Emergency Resilience Accelerator through a delegated act under the revised NIS2 framework, bypassing the need for Council unanimity and allowing immediate mobilisation of ENISA and the Health Emergency and Preparedness Authority (HERA) as implementation arms. The first €8bn tranche is drawn from the EU Civil Protection Mechanism’s contingency reserve, with a further €20bn earmarked from the Multiannual Financial Framework reallocation package now under negotiation in the Budget Council. Member states must submit qualifying projects by turn 5: only those with verified failover systems, red-teaming schedules, and cross-border mutual aid protocols will receive funding. France, the Nordic states, and Benelux have already signalled readiness to comply; resistance is expected from Hungary and Poland, where we will offer partial opt-outs in exchange for baseline telemetry sharing.

The Sovereign Compute Assurance Network is now formally in flight: the Joint Research Centre has drafted the first evaluation protocols, and a new Third-Party Access Board is being convened under Article 12 of the AI Act, with seats reserved for DG-CONNECT, national cybersecurity agencies, and two independent research collectives selected by lot from the EU AI Research Commons. The first audits will target systems above 50.0 capability running on EuroHPC infrastructure, beginning with the Sofia agent cluster. Meanwhile, the Sovereign Agent Initiative advances in stealth: the JRC task force has completed its architecture of control framework, and the first testbed — asylum case triage in Athens — will go live in turn 5 under strict judicial oversight and fallback protocols. Despite grid delays in Marseille, the InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track for partial commissioning by turn 6, with ASML’s Leuven hub now serving as a model for how sovereignty gains can be negotiated even under U.S. pressure.
