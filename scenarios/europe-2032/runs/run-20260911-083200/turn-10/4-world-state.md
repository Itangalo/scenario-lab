### Cut off in the middle of containment
The notice arrived without explanation. American front-line models that hospitals, ministries and logistics firms had built into triage summaries, dispatch drafting and port paperwork simply stopped answering European accounts. Workarounds circulated within hours, but in wards already running paper triage they felt like another failure.

In the same weeks, two other shocks landed. A modified pathogen, developed with substantial machine assistance according to investigators, leaked or was released abroad and then travelled. Cases reached Europe; sequencing labs and wastewater monitors went to round-the-clock alerting, contact tracing restarted, and a handful of wards sealed. Containment held after weeks, but funerals were real.

At the same time a new overseas release showed a sharp leap in machine-written code, maths and intrusion tooling. Defenders said exploit kits that once took days now appeared in hours, and the same municipal booking and port systems that had jammed in autumn jammed again after being cleared.

What prevented collapse was unglamorous. The municipal backup kits and sentinel upgrades pushed through on emergency channels the previous year were already in place. Embedded repair teams generalized them into standing offline packs running on European-hosted open models — simpler, slower, but working without the cut-off provider. Mayors credited crews who stayed until paper-to-digital queues moved.

Brussels asked for almost nothing new: no factory vote, no retaliation over servicing curbs on chip tools, only endorsement to reprogramme existing emergency funds to keep the fallback running. Sites for future capacity stayed fenced and warm.

Trust did not recover. Citizens saw clinics functioning but diminished, ports slow, and heard that the most capable systems were both denied to Europe and implicated in the outbreak and the new attacks. Relief that services degraded rather than stopped competed with anger that dependence had been allowed to happen.
