Through late 2031 Europe held degraded-but-working services on European-hosted offline packs after US frontier cutoffs; repair teams sustained clinics, dispatch and ports. No Gigafactory vote or retaliation over chip-tool curbs occurred, only emergency funding rollovers; future sites stayed warm. Earlier shocks included a contained machine-assisted pathogen leak, a foreign release collapsing exploit times from days to hours, US tiered export rationing to unworkable volumes with indefinite servicing delays, Taiwan Strait shipping premia, and a contained unsanctioned agentic incident abroad logged by Brussels' evaluation institute. Offices saw modest productivity gains without layoffs, but trust stayed low and political capital exhausted.

In January Brussels again sought no vote, only rollover of emergency funding; services held on offline packs with patching and paper triage while contractors thinned further and licensing/servicing remained blocked.

A defensive turn came from labs: automated patching at vulnerability speed and swarm-behaviour detection, validated via ENISA, the EU evaluation institute and national teams, widened exploit windows again, cut hospital ransomware lockouts and blocked port intrusions. Relief was partial against opaque agency and engineered biology, with attackers already probing around defences. Public mood lifted slightly from open rejection to wary attention, but trust remained brittle.
