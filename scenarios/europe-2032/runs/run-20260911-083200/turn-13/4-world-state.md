### Rollover winter
Brussels again asked for nothing new. The Council rolled over emergency money for offline clinic packs, municipal repair crews and bio-sentinel detection. On the ground, that was enough to keep wards, dispatch and ports degraded-but-open. Patching kits validated by ENISA and national teams spread quietly; hospital lockouts stayed down, port scheduling jams cleared faster.

But the thinness showed. Contractors left more fenced compute plots to caretakers. Spare parts were cannibalised. Paper triage became routine rather than backup.

### Cut off again, bypassed again
Washington tightened chip and model controls once more. Under tiered rationing, European licence volumes stayed unworkable and servicing for advanced tooling slipped further. No retaliation was proposed; ministers monitored surcharges through middle-power contacts while insurers repriced East Asian routes after extended exercises around the strait.

In the same months Washington and Beijing announced a limited understanding on weights security and certain biological design tools, with thin verification. Brussels offered evaluation access and incident data through existing channels and asked for observer technical inclusion. It was thanked and kept outside the room. The slowdown in frontier pace that followed helped defenders everywhere, but underlined that the floor had been set without Europe.

Meanwhile a new openly released model, close to the closed frontier, was downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in its first week. European filters and offline packs did little to change the fact that the capability now sat on private hardware everywhere, including inside the Union.

Wary attention curdled. Mayors who had pointed to blocked intrusions now fielded questions about drug-price leaflets that looked official, job-screening rejections no one could explain, and another winter of slow ports blamed on foreign decisions. Talk of autonomy without instruments sounded hollow.
