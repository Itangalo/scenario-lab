### Paper halls and queues
The second ransomware wave hit harder than the first. A compromised update pushed through the same maintenance provider locked registries, appointment systems and benefits payment files in dozens of cities. Hospitals again postponed procedures, town halls handed out paper forms. This time the reconnection gate held: black-box checks run by European and national cyber teams caught reinfected backups twice before they went live. Mayors complained about the delay on television, then quietly kept the teams through winter.

Restoration finished the turn with most services back, slower and more manual than before. Clean-backup rebuilds paid from emergency funds became the template cited in council conclusions.

### The benefits letters
In autumn the other crisis broke. Reporters and an ombudsman showed a benefits fraud-risk scoring system used in several regions had systematically cut or flagged households for years. Caseworkers had approved its flags in seconds, logs that would have shown the pattern were generated and never read. A court found the oversight required by law had been meaningless.

The affair dominated headlines for weeks. Ministers conceded the rules had existed on paper and failed in practice. Trust in public uses of automated systems fell sharply.

The Commission answered with a new enforcement drive: mandatory minimum review times, sampled re-decisions by humans, market inspectors required to actually read logs, suspension of non-compliant tools, and ombudsman-led compensation. Employer co-funding for retraining was extended to affected families.

It landed in a strange economy. Studies from law firms, accountants and consultancies showed clear productivity gains, strongest for juniors, without mass layoffs. Firms that had cut entry posts rehired. That good news blunted urgency for labour measures and made the redress push look defensive.

Abroad, prolonged manoeuvres around Taiwan raised freight insurance and revived talk of chip stockpiles. The existing supply contact network placed quiet top-up orders. No new instrument was opened; attention stayed on restoration and redress. The two factory shells gained grid permits in one site, stalled on power in the other, still with no sovereign machines online.
