### Restoration summer
The ransomware sweep started in municipal IT and spread through a shared maintenance dependency. Hospitals postponed non-urgent procedures, several city administrations reverted to paper, and clean-backup restoration took weeks. Surge teams from the EU cyber agency and national response units camped in the worst-hit town halls. The new black-box test suites became the gate for reconnecting systems, which slowed reopening and angered mayors but caught two reinfections.

Attribution remained open. Analysts agreed only that the tooling was machine-generated and highly automated, and that defenders had been behind from the first weekend.

The same half-year brought contradictory economic news. An openly released model, close to the closed frontier, was downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in days and spread to private servers beyond recall. Evaluators warned its offensive usefulness was now permanent.

Yet offices told a calmer story. Studies across law, accountancy, administration and consulting showed measurable productivity gains, strongest among juniors, without matching layoffs. Firms that had cut entry roles quietly rehired. That eased the jobs panic and helped employment ministers hold employers to co-funding retraining vouchers, though voucher payments still lagged.

Abroad, extended exercises around Taiwan lifted shipping insurance and prompted quiet pre-stocking of chips and spares through middle-power contacts. No new supply instrument was opened.

Two programmes closed: the middle-power supply contacts and the assurance cell, both now cited in procurement rules. Construction on the two gigafactory shells continued, still with no sovereign machines online. Permit files for power links again moved slowly in places, noted locally as routine delay.
