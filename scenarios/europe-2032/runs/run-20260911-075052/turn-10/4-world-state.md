### Shelter Under Fire
The war did not start in Europe, but it arrived within hours. American and Chinese strikes traded across the Pacific in February, and within days fabrication plants, cables, satellites and data centres were declared legitimate targets on both sides. Two subsea cables off Portugal and Ireland were cut, a satellite ground station in Kiruna was jammed, and air freight for reagents and chips seized up.

Brussels declared non-belligerency: no offensive use of European infrastructure, dispersed backups, soldiers and engineers at landing stations, grid switchgear refitted with the new domestic alloys. The declaration mostly held. European sites were not directly struck, but they went dark anyway when transit and cloud dependencies failed.

Registries that had just been restored flickered again. Hospitals postponed non-urgent care as imported consumables ran short.

Against this came the other shock: tailored cures for several previously untreatable conditions, developed on American frontier systems, reached clinical use. For patients' families it was miraculous; for governments it was a queue. Initial doses were allocated through Washington and the developers' own channels, with European orders weeks behind and priced in access concessions.

The Commission's answer was to fuse the two crises. Reserve teams were put on wartime mutual aid, wage support was fast-tracked to keep nurses and registry staff in place, and health authorities pooled what sequencing chemicals remained while bargaining for licensed production of the new therapies on European-run lines. A first licensing agreement for one therapy was signed in May, with fill-finish in Belgium — too little, too late for the headlines, but real vials.

By June, Europe had avoided the worst cascade — lights stayed on, triage assistants kept sorting — yet everyone could see the terms. Survival had depended on restraint by others, and healing depended on models Europe could not run.
### Small Wins, Hard Ceiling
Machine halls in Paris and Warsaw still hummed empty. Graduate hiring stayed frozen. Voters credited the shelter effort but resented the queue for cures.
