### Crisis and Response

The EU initiates drafting of the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield, a proposed directive to harden essential services against cascading AI-driven attacks. ENISA publishes red-team scenarios based on the hospital ransomware attack, and fast-track funding is proposed to support rapid adoption. However, Germany and Poland raise legal concerns over liability and national competence, and the Commission delays formal proposal to allow time for intergovernmental consultation. No binding directive is adopted this turn. The measure remains in development, and no new measure is formally introduced, preserving the existing portfolio of three active initiatives: InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech sovereignty package, and European Evaluation Shield.

### Legal Firestorm

The European Evaluation Shield faces a direct legal challenge from a coalition of developers and foreign governments, arguing its audit powers violate intellectual property rights. A Council resolution reaffirms its mandate, but the dispute now rests with the European Court of Justice. Pilot audits continue under secrecy, yet the agency’s authority is visibly contested. No public safety report is issued — the legal uncertainty halts disclosure.

### Sovereignty Holds, Capital Erodes

The completion of SEAL solidifies EU access to critical semiconductor tools, anchoring sovereignty at 29.0 despite capability growth. Political capital declines to 26.0, reflecting the growing coordination burden of maintaining three active high-cost measures and the preparatory work on a potential fourth, compounded by legal friction and governance delays. The cost of advanced measure development is evident, but without formal adoption, no proposal bonus applies and no additional strain from a fourth measure is incurred. Public sentiment drops to 33.0 as the cyberattack’s aftermath fuels job displacement fears and backlash against AI infrastructure.
