### The Open Breach

The frontier AI leap continues, with `ai_capability` rising to 64.5 as labs integrate autonomous research loops into training pipelines. This acceleration is matched by a significant open-weight release: a capable agent framework, functionally close to the closed frontier, floods peer-to-peer networks. While adoption is rapid among developer communities, its real-world operational impact remains limited in this half-year. The diffusion of offensive cyber tools increases, but their deployment relies on human coordination — the self-replicating cascade feared by analysts has not yet materialized. `openweight_capability` rises modestly, reflecting early proliferation rather than runaway capability leap.

### Infrastructure Under Fire

A decentralized network launches coordinated attacks on power substations and fibre hubs across France, Italy, and the Baltics. These are not random: targeting aligns with open-source reconnaissance tools, suggesting AI-aided planning. Emergency services scramble, but the damage slows data centre redundancy plans. Public fear mounts as blackouts hit rural clinics and transport systems. The attacks expose fragility, yet resilience systems partially hold — backup protocols restore key nodes within hours, limiting cascading failure. `resilience` dips slightly, but not catastrophically.

### Evaluation in Isolation

The European Evaluation Shield survives legally but falters operationally. Despite court backing, the EU fails to secure access to the latest frontier model for audit. Conditional DMA waivers yield only partial telemetry, and the promised Brussels-Helsinki audit environments remain under-resourced. The delayed safety report is now indefinitely postponed — too risky to publish without hard data, too dangerous to delay indefinitely. Without new audits, confidence in existing safety benchmarks erodes. `ai_safety` declines, but not collapsed — legacy protocols still function in narrow domains.

### Sovereignty Eroded, Capital Strained

The Tech Sovereignty Package limps forward, but Germany and Poland reject binding resilience standards, even with co-funding incentives. The backlash_physical and sabotage incidents harden resistance to centralised mandates. ENISA’s red-team exercises are postponed, not shelved. With three large measures still active and no completions this turn, political capital erodes — not from automatic drain, but from visible stagnation and rising public concern. The cost of maintaining multiple long-running initiatives without deliverables weighs on coalition unity. However, no single collapse occurs; the loss reflects mounting friction, not systemic failure.

Public sentiment weakens further as job losses in AI-dependent sectors compound infrastructure fears, but media coverage remains divided — some outlets highlight preparedness efforts, slowing the fall. The mood sours, but does not spiral.

The Portfolio Charge remains at −10, reflecting sustained burden, but `eu_political_capital` decline is bounded by rule-consistent mechanisms: no more than +3 linkage from sovereignty terms, and no unmodelled transfers. The drop is real, but measured — a sign of pressure, not dissolution.
