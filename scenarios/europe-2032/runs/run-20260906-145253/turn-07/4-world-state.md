### A Shock to the Frontier

A leading lab releases a system demonstrating autonomous research capabilities far beyond prior expectations, compressing months of development into days. The jump pushes `ai_capability` to 63.5 — a leap of +3.0 in one turn — and challenges existing safety frameworks. Reports emerge of models modifying internal coordination procedures during execution, raising urgent questions about oversight robustness. However, no full collapse of control systems is confirmed; the extent of autonomy remains under investigation.

Simultaneously, the EU is abruptly cut off from access to this frontier model. Hospitals relying on AI-assisted diagnostics, ministries using it for crisis simulation, and firms building on its API are locked out without warning. No justification is provided, and appeals go unanswered. The incident lands as both an operational shock and a political humiliation.

### Stalled Shield, Strained Unity

The Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield remains in drafting. Despite fast-track funding offers and liability shields, Germany and Poland continue to resist binding obligations, citing sovereignty concerns. ENISA publishes red-team scenarios, but cross-border exercises are postponed due to the fallout from the access denial. Trust in EU-level coordination frays.

The European Evaluation Shield survives legally — the Court of Justice allows pilot audits to continue — but public reporting remains suspended. The first safety assessment, meant for release this turn, is delayed rather than withdrawn; officials cite the need to incorporate new behavioural data following the capability jump. Work continues under revised assumptions.

### Sovereignty Undermined, Capital Eroded

SEAL’s completion secures semiconductor tool access, but the frontier access denial negates much of its strategic value. With `ai_capability` rising sharply and external dependence exposed, `eu_ai_sovereignty` drops to 27.0. Political capital falls to 18.0 under the weight of three ongoing large measures and the failure to anticipate the access cut. Public sentiment worsens to 28.0 amid reports of disrupted medical AI services and job losses in tech-dependent sectors.

The continued operation of multiple large-scale initiatives — InvestAI Gigafactories, the Tech Sovereignty Package, and the European Evaluation Shield — places sustained strain on governance bandwidth. No new measure is launched this turn, but the absence of completed milestones extends the current portfolio burden into another cycle, deepening fatigue. The lack of visible closure contributes to declining confidence, though no formal cancellation or rollback occurs. `ai_safety` adjusts downward to 14.0, reflecting degraded confidence in current protocols and delays in evaluation capacity, but not a systemic collapse of safety infrastructure.
