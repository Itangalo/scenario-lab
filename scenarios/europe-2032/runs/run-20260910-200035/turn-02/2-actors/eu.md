## New measure
**EU Incident Reporting and Open-Model Watch**
What it actually does is create mandatory rapid reporting of serious AI-enabled incidents and near-misses to ENISA plus a public tracking function for open-weight capabilities. Why this and why now is because the Mythos-class rehearsal and the second unrecallable frontier-near release mean detection failed and capability is now permanently distributed, so learning must come from incidents, not labs.

## Priority
M3 EU Critical Infrastructure Shield, because live segmentation, credential resets and the spring 2027 cross-border exercise are the only protection landing inside the cyber window our two-year commitment names.

## In practice
We push the Shield into delivery: ENISA issues binding segmentation guidance under NIS2 Article 12, Energy and Transport Councils mandate credential resets for TSOs, ports and water utilities, and we co-fund redundancy from the Civil Protection Mechanism to buy operator compliance for the spring live exercise. DG CNECT holds the Gigafactory site selection in France, Germany, Spain and Poland on track without new money this turn.

At the same time we table the Incident Reporting regulation with a narrow legal base to avoid a new fight: mandatory 72-hour reporting by essential entities and frontier deployers, ENISA as hub, and a small Joint Research Centre cell to benchmark open-weight releases like Mythos so the next download wave is characterised within days. Operators get liability protection for shared near-misses; capitals get no new unfunded mandate beyond reporting they already owe under NIS2.
