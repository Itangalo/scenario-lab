### The Reckoning of Access

The EU formally closed the **Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework** and the **Emergency Access Pact for Red-Teaming** this turn, finalizing both as binding instruments. Though enforcement remains patchy, their legal adoption provided a modest boost to oversight credibility. Audits are still sparse, and red-teaming access continues to face resistance from US-linked labs reluctant to share model weights.

Simultaneously, a sudden disruption struck critical infrastructure — a leading frontier model disappeared from hospital diagnostic systems and transport schedulers without warning. The outage was brief, contained by fallback procedures, but the political reverberations were deep. Dependence on foreign-controlled AI systems shifted from abstract concern to lived reality.

In response, the Commission proposed the **Frontier Access Enforcement Directive**, a high-cost, large-scale measure aiming to mandate EU-held weights and sovereign infrastructure for all AI in essential services. Backed by €2.8bn and fast-tracked review, it signals intent — but implementation cannot begin in full until Turn 11 at the earliest, per minimum lead times. Early support from Siemens and Thales offers promise, while hyperscalers warn of fragmentation and cost spikes. The proposal is under initial assessment; its salience and feasibility remain unconfirmed, and no event gate has been resolved.

### Capability Without Control

Global AI capability advanced to **84.5**, driven by opaque reasoning improvements and extended training runs. Leaked anomaly reports detailing unexplained agent coordination confirmed that performance continues to outpace understanding. Despite the completion of category 1 and 5 measures, their limited uptake within the EU resulted in only a marginal increase in ai_safety to **12.0**.

Resilience rose modestly to **46.0**, supported by system substitutions during the outage and partial adoption of secure stacks. Public sentiment edged up to **30.0**, buoyed by a high-profile medical breakthrough — though its delivery via inaccessible models tempered public enthusiasm.

Sovereignty declined to **13.0**, with +2.5 growth in ai_capability triggering a −1 decay and the access denial event imposing a further −1. No offsetting gains from domestic capacity were realized this turn. Political capital remained at **23.0**: closing two measures delivered +4, but the proposal bonus for the new directive was not counted this turn due to its scale, cost, and deferred implementation timeline. The portfolio charge of −4 (Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework −3, Emergency Access Pact −3, priority −1) fully offset the closure gains. The full impact of the new directive will not register until implementation begins and effects materialize.

Openweight capability increased only moderately to **63.0**, reflecting incremental replication and deployment gains despite ongoing access barriers — consistent with prior accumulation rates and resistance from external labs. A jump to 70.0 would imply implausible near-term scaling, which the current infrastructure and cooperation levels do not support.
