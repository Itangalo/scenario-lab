## New measure
**Frontier Access Enforcement Directive**

A legal directive mandating that any AI system used in essential public services — healthcare, energy, transport, justice, and core government functions — must be deployable on EU-controlled infrastructure using EU-held model weights, with full operational independence from foreign entities.

## Priority
**Frontier Access Enforcement Directive** — because eu_frontier_access_denied has already disrupted hospitals and ministries, proving that dependence is not theoretical, and because without enforceable sovereignty over deployed systems, all oversight is reduced to advisory status when the plug is pulled.

## In practice
We are treating the denial of frontier access not as an incident but as a strategic warning. The Frontier Access Enforcement Directive will be proposed under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the same legal basis as the now-completed Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework, but with sharper teeth: it mandates not just inspection rights, but operational autonomy. By Q1 2031, we will require all contracts for AI in essential services to include clauses ensuring fallback deployment on EU-held weights, using compute physically located and legally anchored within the Union. This applies immediately to new procurements; existing systems have 18 months to comply.

The European Commission’s Internal Market and Digital Directorates are drafting the text in parallel with ENISA and the European Procurement Office, with legal review fast-tracked through COREPER I. Funding of €2.8bn will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and unused cohesion funds to subsidise migration costs for public bodies. We are engaging Siemens, Thales, Atos and national champions to build trusted deployment stacks, and offering tax incentives to cloud providers who certify EU-operational independence. Resistance will come from US hyperscalers and their local partners, but the medical_breakthrough — while impressive — arrived through a model we cannot inspect, audit, or run ourselves. That is not progress; it is vassalage disguised as innovation. We will not let sentiment drift toward enthusiasm on borrowed miracles. Sovereignty is not a feature to negotiate — it is the condition of our agency.
