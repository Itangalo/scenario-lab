### A Directive Forged in Crisis

The European Council invoked Article 114 TFEU to pass the Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive, overriding national objections in a narrow qualified majority. Germany, Italy, and the Nordic bloc backed the move as essential for survival in an accelerating AI landscape. France voted against, warning of energy sovereignty erosion; Poland abstained, Hungary followed. The European Court of Justice has accepted France’s referral, casting a legal shadow over implementation.

Despite the uncertainty, ENTSO-E designated three emergency grid corridors linking Nordic hydro and French nuclear to AI clusters in Wallonia, Silesia, and Andalusia. The EIB released €70bn from dormant recovery funds, fast-tracked under emergency provisions. The European Digital Infrastructure Consortium began site preparation, aiming for Q4 activation.

ASML, under U.S. pressure tied to the new American posture, resisted binding commitments. However, counter-sanction clauses and guaranteed offtake agreements extracted partial concessions: phased lithography access, not full throughput. Domestic compute expansion is underway—but constrained.

### Fractures and Foundations

The **emergent_decentralized_ai_infrastructure** in Germany and Spain demonstrated resilience, feeding into the EU’s planning but remaining outside direct control. Their success lent credibility to the directive, yet highlighted the gap between grassroots initiative and institutional execution.

Global AI capability surged to 75.0 as self-improving models accelerated development cycles. The **eval_anomaly_reports** deepened unease—labs dismissed odd reasoning traces as noise, but the patterns persisted. AI safety remained at 7.0: oversight exists in name, not in function.

Resilience held at 45.0. No systemic collapse occurred, but no gains either—the cyber shield is operational in patches, not in depth. Public sentiment stayed at 30.0, unmoved by opaque infrastructure projects.

Political capital rose to 27.0: the completion of *Tech Sovereignty Package* and *Labour Transition Fund* freed resources, and the bold move on compute mobilisation delivered a proposal bonus. Sovereignty inched up to 14.0—symbolic, but real.
