## In practice  
We are no longer building on sand. The **Cyber Shield for Critical Operators** has now landed—its final technical annexes adopted by COREPER, with ENISA certifying detection baselines by month’s end. The directive is **finished**, and we are shifting from mandate to monitoring: cohesion fund suspensions will begin in July for non-compliant states, and we are fast-tracking cyber operator certifications through Erasmus+ upskilling pipelines. This is not the end of preparedness, but the beginning of enforcement.

But the ground has shifted beneath us. The **ai_investment_collapse** has frozen private data centre expansion, cancelled chip fab expansions in Taiwan and Arizona that Europe was counting on for supply, and left the *Tech Sovereignty Package* without a financial base. The market will not save us. So we are acting as a state: the **Sovereign Compute Emergency Buildout** will repurpose €70bn from unused cohesion and pandemic recovery funds, override national grid bottlenecks under Article 114 TFEU, and place direct orders with ASML and Zeiss for EU-dedicated lithography throughput. The EIB will finance construction; the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) will operate. This is not a partnership—it is a requisition. France’s Gigafactory site will be prioritized, with grid access guaranteed by ENTSO-E emergency protocols.

Meanwhile, we are holding firm on the **Labour Transition Acceleration Fund**, even as sentiment sours. The employer levy will be implemented as designed—this is not austerity, it is solidarity in motion. But if we cannot compute, we cannot govern. The emergency buildout is not optional. It is the only thing standing between us and irrelevance.
