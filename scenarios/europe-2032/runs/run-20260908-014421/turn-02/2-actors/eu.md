## In practice  
We are advancing three measures this turn under growing pressure. On *InvestAI Gigafactories*, we are finalising site negotiations with France, Germany, and Poland, offering grid priority and state aid waivers in exchange for irrevocable EU ownership clauses and audit access. The Legal Service continues its work on Article 114 TFEU as a basis to override national vetoes, knowing this will be challenged but believing the strategic imperative justifies the risk. We are also engaging EIB and KfW to structure blended financing that draws in private equity without ceding control.

The *Tech Sovereignty Package* remains stalled in the Competitiveness Council, where smaller states fear lock-in to foreign hyperscalers despite our ASML leverage play. We are now pairing export licensing assurances with bilateral memoranda on sovereign cloud stacks, framing them as interoperability enablers rather than decoupling tools, while quietly coordinating through the Trade and Technology Council to avoid direct confrontation with Washington. Our goal is to convert quiet understanding into de facto commitments before the summer recess.

Meanwhile, *Cyber Shield* is entering implementation: ENISA has drafted the technical annexes based on the NCSC-NL and ANSSI red-team scenarios, and we are aligning enforcement with NIS2 via cohesion fund conditionality. To overcome resistance, we are offering 70% co-financing and fast-tracked Erasmus+ cyber training pipelines. At the same time, the new **Labour Transition Acceleration Fund** is being scoped with DG Employment and the European Social Fund+, targeting regions already reporting sharp job losses. We are designing it so that employers who automate must contribute to retraining funds, creating a feedback loop between adoption and adjustment. This is not just fairness — it is inoculation against backlash that could derail everything else.
