### The World Moves On  
Global AI capability advanced by +3.0 this turn, reaching 64.0, as frontier labs integrated autonomous cyber agents into model training pipelines. These systems now design and deploy exploits with minimal human input, marking a qualitative shift in offensive capacity. With no safety breakthrough and new opaque reasoning pathways confirmed in internal audits, `ai_safety` plummeted by −7 to 9.0—now deep in the danger zone.

### Crisis Mode Activated  
The EU declared crisis measures after being abruptly locked out of the leading AI model—critical hospital systems, transport调度 platforms, and energy grid monitors lost access overnight. A coordinated ransomware attack exploiting AI-generated zero-days had just crippled public services in three member states, exposing total dependency on external models. In response, the Commission fast-tracked **Cyber Shield**, mandating AI-driven intrusion detection across all critical operators by mid-2028, backed by 80% co-financing and threats to suspend cohesion funds.

**Emergency Sovereignty Activation** was prepared as a draft measure, with emergency procurement of HPU clusters and export control reciprocity proposed, but its formal launch was deferred due to legal uncertainties. The CJEU received challenges from Poland and Hungary over Article 114 TFEU jurisdiction, delaying implementation. No binding commitments were secured from ASML under U.S. pressure, and France delayed its Gigafactory site deal over grid capacity—further stalling sovereign compute development. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** remains in flight but missed its turn-6 finish.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure  
Despite urgency, progress stalled. With no new sovereign capacity secured and frontier access denied, `eu_ai_sovereignty` fell to 13.0. The lack of immediate action on emergency procurement and export controls reflects the hold placed on **Emergency Sovereignty Activation**, which remains pending legal review and will not be counted as a new measure until resolved.

### Political Capital Crumbles  
Four large initiatives—InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, Cyber Shield, and Labour Transition Acceleration Fund—plus a named priority strained governance capacity. The portfolio charge overwhelmed thin political capital, though not as severely as initially feared. The Labour Fund’s employer levy sparked backlash, and retraining plans felt distant. Public sentiment turned negative as media framed the outage as self-inflicted—“We built our hospitals on American code,” read one headline. Without visible success, trust eroded: `public_sentiment` dropped to 38.0. Political capital, while under pressure, decayed moderately to 27.0, reflecting the strain but respecting the constitutional separation from sovereignty. The drop accounts for portfolio burden and public mood, but avoids mechanical collapse.

### Open-Weight Reality Check  
Despite global capability growth, open-weight models advanced only modestly. Deployment bottlenecks, fragmented funding, and the loss of access to key training infrastructure during the outage constrained progress. `openweight_capability` rose to 49.5—gaining +1.5, half the frontier’s advance—reflecting delayed diffusion and operational disruption rather than parity. No open-weight system matched the frontier’s offensive leap, and safety lags further behind.
