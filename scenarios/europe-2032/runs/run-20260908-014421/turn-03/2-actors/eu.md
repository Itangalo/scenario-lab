## New measure  
**Emergency Sovereignty Activation**  

A targeted, time-limited mobilisation of EU institutions and member states to restore sovereign access to frontier AI capability through coordinated legal, financial, and diplomatic means, including emergency procurement of compute, activation of export control reciprocity, and conditional access to EU markets and data.  

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +15 (via emergency compute acquisition and export leverage), resilience +5 (by stabilising critical service dependencies), public_sentiment +5 (by demonstrating decisive response to crisis)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, frontier developers, and foreign governments with AI infrastructure serving EU entities

## In practice  
We are operating in crisis mode. The **cyber_major_incident**—a model-generated ransomware sweep that encrypted hospital triage systems in three member states—has made clear that our defences are not just inadequate but retrograde. ENISA is now leading a pan-EU incident response coordination, but the root failure lies in the absence of real-time monitoring and the hollowing out of human oversight under the assumption that frontier models were stable. We are fast-tracking the final technical annexes for **Cyber Shield**, leveraging the NIS2 framework to mandate AI-driven intrusion detection across all critical operators by turn 4. To overcome resistance, we are offering 80% co-financing from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and fast-tracking Erasmus+ cyber training pipelines to staff the new systems. This is no longer optional: cohesion fund disbursements will be suspended for member states that fail to submit implementation plans by the end of the year.

Meanwhile, **InvestAI Gigafactories** remains in legal limbo. France and Poland are holding out on site agreements, citing unresolved grid access and state aid disputes. We are now invoking Article 114 TFEU to override national vetoes, backed by a legal opinion from the Legal Service that frames sovereign AI infrastructure as a matter of internal market integrity. The EIB and KfW are structuring blended financing with private equity, but control remains non-negotiable: no foreign hyperscaler will hold veto rights over access. Simultaneously, the **Tech Sovereignty Package** is being reframed as a crisis response: we are linking ASML export licensing assurances to binding commitments on EU access to training compute, using the Anti-Coercion Instrument as leverage. This is not decoupling—it is deterrence through interdependence.

The **Labour Transition Acceleration Fund** is now in final design with DG Employment and the European Social Fund+. We are finalising the employer contribution mechanism: any firm automating roles above a certain threshold must pay into a retraining pool, calibrated to the number of positions affected. This is not punitive—it is the price of adjustment in a union that still believes in solidarity. But without **Emergency Sovereignty Activation**, none of it holds: if we cannot guarantee access to the tools that are reshaping the economy, then our social and defensive measures are just sandcastles. So we are moving now—pulling forward procurement, activating export controls, and making clear: the EU will not be cut off without consequence.
