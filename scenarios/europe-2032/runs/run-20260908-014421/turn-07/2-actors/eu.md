## New measure
**Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework**

Establishes an EU-mandated, technically empowered authority with pre-deployment review rights over all models trained above 50.0 capability on sovereign soil, including access to weights, training data logs, and agent behaviour during training runs. The framework includes enforcement powers to halt training, mandate modifications, or block deployment.

## In practice
We are acting on the truth that compute is not power unless it is governed. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive** have delivered physical infrastructure — clusters in Wallonia, Silesia, and Andalusia are now live, drawing on emergency grid corridors and repurposed funds. But the first training runs on these systems are already beginning, and we have no legal or technical means to verify whether they are safe, aligned, or even comprehensible. The eval_anomaly_reports from global labs — dismissed as noise — suggest we are already behind in understanding what these systems are doing.

This turn, we are not building more data centres. We are building authority. Using the momentum of the directive’s legal precedent, we are proposing a new binding framework under Article 114 TFEU again — this time not to mobilise resources, but to assert jurisdiction over what is done with them. The European AI Office will be empowered as a technical authority with mandated access: not just API-level demonstrations, but weights, activation traces, and training logs for any model above 50.0 capability trained on EU soil. Developers must register runs in advance, submit to red-teaming by public evaluators, and accept binding rulings on whether a model may proceed.

Resistance will come from both inside and out. Frontier developers argue that inspection compromises security and innovation. Some member states fear chilling investment. But we are not asking for trust — we are restoring accountability. We will offer phased compliance for smaller actors, and fast-track permits for those who volunteer early access. The EIB will tie future infrastructure funding to cooperation. And we will publish the first red-team findings openly — not to scare, but to show that we are no longer blind. If we cannot see what we are building, we are already lost.
