In late 2026, a breakthrough AI model surpassed expectations, solving complex scientific problems and exposing critical vulnerabilities in global security systems. Existing interpretability and safety tools failed to keep pace, undermining confidence in AI governance. Simultaneously, a series of undetected AI-driven intrusions into European critical infrastructure—power grids, a port, and a water utility—revealed systemic failures in cyber defense, sparking private alarm despite public downplaying.

The EU responded with the *Cyber Shield for Critical Operators* directive, mandating AI-powered security measures, backed by France and Germany but resisted by smaller states over cost and sovereignty concerns. Domestic tech initiatives, including *InvestAI Gigafactories* and the *Tech Sovereignty Package*, stalled due to legal disputes and diplomatic tensions, exacerbated by U.S. opposition to linking chip access to cloud sovereignty. The abrupt exclusion of EU researchers from the latest frontier model highlighted the risks of technological dependence.

As political capital eroded and public unease grew, the gap between strategic ambitions and execution widened, weakening trust in EU leadership amid accelerating global AI advancement and deepening security vulnerabilities.
