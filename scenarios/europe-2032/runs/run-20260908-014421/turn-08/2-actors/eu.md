## New measure
**Emergency Access Pact for Red-Teaming**

Establish a binding emergency protocol granting vetted public evaluators immediate access to model weights and activation traces during high-risk training runs, even before formal evaluation deadlines, in exchange for liability protection and secure handling.

## Priority
**Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework** — because we now have compute running frontier models and no sovereign eyes inside them, and without binding inspection rights, every additional petaflop widens our vulnerability rather than our strength.

## In practice
We are not waiting for the final vote to begin enforcement. The draft of the Sovereign Evaluation and Control Framework is being fast-tracked through COREPER II, with legal anchoring under Article 114 TFEU reaffirmed by the Commission’s Legal Service. We are treating this as a matter of strategic continuity: just as the Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive overrode national bottlenecks to build infrastructure, this framework overrides corporate opacity to secure control. The European AI Office is being resourced with €1.2bn from unused cohesion funds to staff red teams and deploy secure inspection environments. We are offering early compliance bonuses — expedited permitting and public compute credits — to developers who register training runs above 50.0 capability in advance, and we will publish the first audit findings in Q3, regardless of pushback.

At the same time, we are closing the gap between legal authority and technical reality. The Emergency Access Pact pre-empts delays in the full framework’s adoption by establishing a trusted-access corridor for public evaluators during high-risk training sprints. This is not voluntary disclosure — it is a conditioned right, triggered when capability thresholds are approached, and backed by EIB loan terms that require sign-on. We are negotiating with Fraunhofer, INRIA, and CWI to activate cross-border red teams, and with ENISA to harden the inspection pipeline against leakage. Resistance is expected from Luxembourg-registered labs and US parent companies, but we are not asking for permission. We are building the authority to act — not just to warn.
