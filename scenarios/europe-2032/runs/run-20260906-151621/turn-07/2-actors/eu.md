## Statement changes

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse — but we now understand that control is meaningless without cohesion, and that sovereignty cannot be declared, only demonstrated through operational resilience and enforceable rules.
  - Trigger: the development this turn of *member_state_defection* and the failure of *AI Containment Protocol* enforcement to prevent unilateral access
  - Grounds: One member state’s bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler has exposed a fatal flaw: we can build air-gapped clusters in Saint-Malo and Kiruna, but if a member state bypasses them, the protocol is theatre. Our two-year commitment assumed technical control would anchor political unity, but this turn proves the reverse must be true — cohesion is the precondition of control. We must now act not only on infrastructure, but on the legitimacy of our authority to bind all members to common constraints.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience lags, even without a direct mandate, because harm finds the weakest joint — and we now add: we will not assume our own rules are binding unless we can enforce them against our own.
  - Grounds: The *capability_plateau_evidence* suggests the frontier may be slowing, but the price drop in top-tier capability and underperformance of a major release are being disputed by labs with vested interests. We cannot rely on external signals. Meanwhile, *labour_displacement* confirms that the social fabric is fraying not in the shadows, but in plain sight — law firms, accountancy, software, and administration are no longer hiring graduates. Our measures have focused on containment and sovereignty, but if we cannot show that AI serves Europeans, not just secures them, public sentiment will continue to erode. We must now enforce our own rules within, or lose the right to impose them without.

## New measure

**Measure:** Enforce Cohesion through Binding Compliance Mechanism  
A new regulation to operationalise the EU’s authority to suspend access to cohesion funds and SACP resources for member states that enter into AI infrastructure agreements with foreign powers without Commission approval.

`Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_political_capital by reinforcing enforceable unity; reduce risk of further member_state_defection; strengthen the credibility of containment and sovereignty measures by proving they are not optional.  
`Applies to:` All member states, Commission enforcement bodies, European Court of Justice, and national governments

## Priority

**Priority:** Enforce Cohesion through Binding Compliance Mechanism  
We are prioritising this over technical delivery because the *member_state_defection* this turn has shattered the illusion that sovereignty can be built in hardware alone — if we cannot enforce our own rules on our members, no cluster, no protocol, no framework will protect us.

## In practice

This turn, we are acting on the fracture. The Commission is drafting a binding compliance regulation under Article 127 TFEU, to be adopted by qualified majority in the Council, that will formally link access to cohesion funds and participation in the Sovereign AI Core Programme to compliance with EU-wide AI infrastructure governance. The message is clear: you cannot claim EU solidarity when funds flow in, and then opt out when it comes to strategic control. We are offering a transition window — member states with existing bilateral arrangements will be allowed to phase them out over 18 months, but no new agreements will be tolerated after adoption.

We are also confronting the quiet crisis of legitimacy. The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative is now live, with pilot deployments in Germany, Spain, and Finland testing AI-assisted patching in energy grids and cross-border bio-detection alerts. But we know that resilience without trust is scaffolding on sand. The labour displacement numbers are real, and we are not hiding from them. The European Social Fund is being restructured to pilot a pan-EU retraining voucher system for displaced graduates, funded from a 0.5% levy on AI-driven productivity gains in professional services — a small step, but one that begins to answer the question: who benefits?

Meanwhile, the end of the InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package marks a shift from promise to delivery — but also a reckoning. No sovereign compute is online. The Saint-Malo cluster is still under construction, and Kiruna faces winter delays. We are not where we said we would be. That is why this turn, we are not building more infrastructure — we are rebuilding credibility. We are enforcing the Union not as a market, but as a political body with authority that reaches beyond borders and into the choices of its members. If we do not, the frontier will not wait, and neither will the next defection.
