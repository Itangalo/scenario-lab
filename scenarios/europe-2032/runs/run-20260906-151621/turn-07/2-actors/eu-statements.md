# Statements: The European Union (turn 7)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse — but we now understand that control is meaningless without cohesion, and that sovereignty cannot be declared, only demonstrated through operational resilience and enforceable rules.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse — but we now understand that control is meaningless without cohesion, and that sovereignty cannot be declared, only demonstrated through operational resilience and enforceable rules.
  - Trigger: the development this turn of *member_state_defection* and the failure of *AI Containment Protocol* enforcement to prevent unilateral access
  - Grounds: One member state’s bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler has exposed a fatal flaw: we can build air-gapped clusters in Saint-Malo and Kiruna, but if a member state bypasses them, the protocol is theatre. Our two-year commitment assumed technical control would anchor political unity, but this turn proves the reverse must be true — cohesion is the precondition of control. We must now act not only on infrastructure, but on the legitimacy of our authority to bind all members to common constraints.
  - Evidence quoted: A member state cuts its own arrangement – with a hyperscaler, with Washington, or with Beijing – on terms that undercut a position the Union has taken. It is defended at home as pragmatism and read everywhere else as the Union being unable to hold its own line.
  - Reason: Member state defection directly undermines the EU's claim of sovereign control, exposing a gap between declared sovereignty and enforceable unity, which the revised statement now addresses.
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience lags, even without a direct mandate, because harm finds the weakest joint — and we now add: we will not assume our own rules are binding unless we can enforce them against our own.
  - Grounds: The *capability_plateau_evidence* suggests the frontier may be slowing, but the price drop in top-tier capability and underperformance of a major release are being disputed by labs with vested interests. We cannot rely on external signals. Meanwhile, *labour_displacement* confirms that the social fabric is fraying not in the shadows, but in plain sight — law firms, accountancy, software, and administration are no longer hiring graduates. Our measures have focused on containment and sovereignty, but if we cannot show that AI serves Europeans, not just secures them, public sentiment will continue to erode. We must now enforce our own rules within, or lose the right to impose them without.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
