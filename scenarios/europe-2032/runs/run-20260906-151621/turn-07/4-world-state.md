### Fractures and Fixes

The AI frontier inches forward, with capability reaching 65.0 as labs consolidate gains in agent autonomy and cross-system reasoning. Though a recent release underperformed expectations and high-end model prices dipped, suggesting a possible plateau, the trajectory remains upward. The interpretability breakthrough from last turn holds, preventing any major safety regressions, but no further gains materialise — assurance is stabilising, not accelerating.

In Europe, the mood is one of controlled crisis. A member state’s unilateral deal with a US hyperscaler has exposed a dangerous rift: sovereign infrastructure means little if political cohesion falters. The Commission responds by launching a binding compliance mechanism, threatening to withhold cohesion funds and access to the Sovereign AI Core Programme for states that bypass EU governance. Framed as a defence of unity, the move sparks fierce debate. Legal experts question the use of Article 127 TFEU, and two member states warn of retaliatory budget disputes.

The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative begins pilot deployments in energy and transport, but full rollout remains distant. Meanwhile, a quiet labour crisis deepens — hiring in law, accountancy and software remains frozen, with graduates sidelined by AI systems. A retraining voucher pilot is announced, funded by a levy on AI-driven productivity, but scepticism prevails.

Sovereign compute remains offline. The Saint-Malo cluster faces delays, and Kiruna’s construction slows in winter conditions. The InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package officially conclude, but with no operational capacity to show, the political cost mounts.

Public sentiment holds at 25.0, unmoved by distant promises. The US posture of consolidation remains in force, restricting frontier access and amplifying Europe’s dependency.

### A Credibility Test

The EU’s priority shift — from infrastructure to enforceable unity — is bold, but its success is uncertain. The new compliance mechanism may deter future defections, but it risks alienating members already wary of central overreach. Cohesion is now the front line of sovereignty.
