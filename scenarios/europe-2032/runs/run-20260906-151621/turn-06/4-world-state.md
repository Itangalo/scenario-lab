### A Surge in Capability, a Crack in Unity

The AI frontier advances at a steady pace, with `ai_capability` rising to 64.0 — consistent with the long-term trajectory established since Turn 1. A leading lab demonstrates systems capable of chaining multi-day exploits across digital infrastructure, reinforcing existing trends rather than disrupting them. This progress is matched by a rare `safety_breakthrough`: a new interpretability method allows developers to predict emergent behaviours before training completes, quickly adopted across major labs and contributing to a rise in `ai_safety` to 27.0.

In Europe, the mood is tense. One member state bypasses EU-wide containment efforts, securing bilateral access to a high-capability model from a US hyperscaler. This act, defended domestically as pragmatic necessity, fractures the Union’s unified stance and triggers alarm in Brussels. The Commission responds by fast-tracking enforcement of the AI Containment Protocol under Article 16 TFEU, backed by the newly operational European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD). Dual-key air-gapped clusters are now being built in Saint-Malo and Kiruna, with strict oversight protocols.

Simultaneously, the Resilience Reinforcement Initiative launches, targeting patching delays and bio-detection gaps left by the expired Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge. It mandates AI-assisted monitoring across energy and transport networks and establishes cross-border incident handover procedures. Early implementation shows promise, but full rollout will take time.

However, political strain deepens. Six major initiatives remain active, draining resources. The priority on containment fails to accelerate delivery due to legal challenges and intergovernmental friction. Public sentiment remains stagnant at 25.0, reflecting widespread anxiety over job displacement and dependency. The use of cohesion fund threats to enforce compliance further polarizes debate.

Despite completed measures — EAAD and SMAF now fully in force — sovereignty continues to erode. The capability rise costs −1 under rule 5, and no sovereign compute capacity has come online yet. Political capital slips as fiscal pressure mounts and unity frays.

The open-weight ecosystem sees moderate gains, reaching `openweight_capability` of 50.5, driven by the delayed integration of last year’s open-sourced training frameworks and incremental EU model releases. This reflects sustained progress rather than a sudden leap, consistent with the accumulation principle and the gap behind frontier capability.

### US Posture Set

In Washington, the new administration takes office, formally adopting a posture of strategic consolidation. Frontier AI is now treated as national critical infrastructure, with access rationed by geopolitical alignment. This hardens Europe’s dependency, setting the stage for prolonged leverage.
