## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that *capability without control is surrender*, and *control without cohesion is illusion*. Our sovereignty is not in the silicon we build, but in the authority we can enforce across our own members.
  - Trigger: the development this turn of *member_state_defection*, *backlash_physical*, and *supply_chain_coercion*
  - Grounds: This turn, three lines crossed at once. A member state bypassed EU governance to secure frontier access, proving that technical sovereignty means nothing without political unity. Physical attacks on infrastructure show that public consent is fraying — not just from fear of AI, but from the perception that it serves no one but distant powers. And Washington’s use of jurisdiction over ASML proves that our one potential chokepoint is being wielded against us, not by us. The two mandates — self-determination and safety — are no longer abstract tensions. They are collapsing into a single crisis of authority: if we cannot bind our own members, we cannot bind the technology, and we cannot defend our people.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now add: we will not act as if our rules bind others unless we have first proven they bind us, and we will not treat infrastructure as sovereignty until it is under our operational control and defended by our own enforcement.
  - Grounds: The Saint-Malo and Kiruna delays, the collapse of dependent investment plans, and the physical backlash all stem from the same flaw: we assumed that announcing sovereignty was the same as enacting it. We built promises, not power. This turn, we are learning that authority is tested not in drafting rooms, but in enforcement — in the courtroom, the construction site, the border checkpoint. We must now act not just on capability, but on credibility.

## Priority

**Priority:** Secure Foundational Compute through Emergency Sovereign Access  
We are prioritising this because the credibility of the entire Sovereign AI Core Programme — and with it, our claim to strategic autonomy — now hinges on delivering physical, operational infrastructure; without it, every rule, every protocol, every compliance mechanism is an exercise in theatre.

## In practice

This turn, we are reclaiming the promise of sovereignty — not through declarations, but through control. The Commission has drafted a Council Implementing Act under Article 48(7) TEU, invoking the emergency procedure to fast-track completion of the Saint-Malo and Kiruna clusters. We are redirecting €18.7bn in unspent InvestAI funds to override local permitting delays and mobilise military engineering units to accelerate grid integration. The clusters will be governed by a tripartite board: Commission oversight, national technical authority, and independent audit — no single member state or institution can unilaterally access or disable them.

We are also restructuring the SACP’s first deployment phase: no workload above 50.0 capability will be permitted to run outside these two sites. This is not a technical choice — it is a political one. If we cannot run our own models on our own soil, under our own rules, then we are not sovereign. The message to member states is clear: cohesion will be demonstrated through shared infrastructure, not just shared rules. To the public, we say: your taxes built this. Your security depends on it. And this time, it will be completed.
