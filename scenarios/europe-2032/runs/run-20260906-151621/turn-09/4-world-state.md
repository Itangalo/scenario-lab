### The Weight of Steel, the Cost of Will

AI capability inches upward to 66.0, sustained by algorithmic refinements despite shrinking investment. With no safety gains and interpretability tools now weaponised for evasion, confidence in control withers. A major scandal erupts when a European Ombudsman report reveals that AI-supported social insurance systems in three member states systematically denied benefits to vulnerable groups. The ruling ignites public fury and confirms deep institutional failure.

In response, the EU proposes Binding Pre-Deployment Audits, empowering a new European AI Assurance Agency to conduct adversarial evaluations and delay unsafe deployments. The plan is bold, but its legal basis under Article 114 TFEU faces resistance from member states wary of centralised enforcement and industry lobbying. Coalition-building begins, but no legislative text is adopted.

Meanwhile, the emergency push to activate Saint-Malo and Kiruna under Article 48(7) TEU collapses. France and Sweden formally reject the Implementing Act, citing constitutional overreach. Military engineers are not deployed. The Council deadlocks, and the unspent InvestAI funds remain frozen. The Sovereign AI Core Programme ends not with activation, but with recriminations.

The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative completes successfully, improving patching velocity and cross-border coordination. This delivers a measurable boost to societal robustness, even as political cohesion frays.

Public sentiment plummets. Violent protests at data centres, combined with the automated decision scandal, fuel broad hostility toward AI. Trust in institutions erodes further, and support for AI infrastructure wanes.

### Fractured Authority

With no operational compute, sovereignty falters. The EU’s credibility sinks as promises go unfulfilled. Political capital, already thin, is drained by the failed emergency action and the fallout from the scandal. The proposed audit regime offers a vision, but without enforcement capacity or unity, it reads as aspiration, not authority.
