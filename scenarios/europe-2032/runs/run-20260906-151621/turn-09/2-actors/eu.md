## New measure
**Measure:** Enforce AI Safety Through Binding Pre-Deployment Audits  
We are mandating that all AI systems above 50.0 capability deployed in the EU undergo independent, adversarial evaluation by a newly empowered European AI Assurance Agency before release, with the authority to delay or condition deployment.

## Priority
**Priority:** Secure Foundational Compute through Emergency Sovereign Access  
We are prioritising this because without operational control over physical infrastructure, our sovereignty remains performative — and because the credibility of every future rule, including the new audit regime, depends on our ability to enforce it from a position of tangible capability, not just legal claim.

## In practice
This turn, we are grounding sovereignty in steel and soil. The Council is being asked to adopt a Implementing Act under Article 48(7) TEU to assume temporary executive authority over the Saint-Malo and Kiruna clusters, overriding national permitting delays. We are redirecting €18.7bn in unspent InvestAI funds to finance military engineering units for grid integration and accelerated construction, with deployment scheduled for completion by turn 10. The clusters will operate under a tripartite governance board — Commission, national technical leads, and independent auditors — ensuring no single actor can unilaterally access or disable them. This is not merely infrastructure — it is the enforcement of cohesion through shared, operational control.

At the same time, we are preparing for a new phase of governance: one that binds capability to accountability. In response to the automated_decision_scandal — a ruling by the European Ombudsman confirming systemic bias in an AI-supported social insurance system across three member states — we are drafting a Regulation for Binding Pre-Deployment Audits. This will empower the European AI Assurance Agency with real teeth: access to full model weights, the right to run adversarial evaluations, and the authority to delay launches. The legal basis will be Article 114 TFEU, with support from the European Parliament’s internal market committee, though pushback is expected from both industry and member states wary of centralised enforcement. This measure will not start this turn, but its design, coalition-building, and funding reallocation are underway — and it will define the next two years.
