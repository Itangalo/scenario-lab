### Wards open, waiting lists fall
The second half of 2032 finally gave Brussels a story it could tell. In showcase hospitals and several school districts, triage assistants and administrative helpers cut waiting times and backlogs from months to days. Commissioners toured wards and classrooms, and national ministers repeated the same line: this was European continuity equipment, kept running on European budgets, delivering.

Coverage shifted, briefly, from sabotage to queues that moved. Polls ticked up from rock bottom, though most voters still told pollsters they resented the technology even as they used it.

The bio-shield programme formally closed. Pooled sequencing, referral-lab triage, rotated medical stocks and seconded cyber teams for hospitals and water plants were declared operational. Directors admitted the system had caught no novel pathogen and that guidance on the openly circulating near-frontier model remained largely unusable, but finance ministries noted with relief that no new agent fraud wave had overwhelmed payment controls.

### The ground stays guarded
On the factory sites, nothing restarted. Police perimeters stayed up around silent hookups and damaged substations after the spring's cable cuts and smashed connections. Contractors remained stood down. The Commission forced no permit decision and sought no confrontation over the separate supply deal, preserving the formal pact at the cost of visible paralysis.

Meanwhile commercial robots arrived in the narrowest sense: fixed-task machines in warehouses and controlled factory cells, sold on labour cost, stopping at the edge of controlled environments. Defence pilots for carrying and mine clearance stayed pilots. The deployment underlined how little of the stack Europe controls — machines, models and supply arriving from elsewhere — even as manufacturers elsewhere explained missed promises.

By December, wards were open, sites were guarded, and sentiment had lifted just enough to breathe. Protection without progress had become benefit without sovereignty.
