### Lights stay on
The Grid Shield hardening finished segmentation in transmission control rooms and large hospitals on schedule. Engineers credited it when a fresh wave of machine-speed patching around the mirrored open weights and the contained logistics agent did not cascade into outages. Maintenance crews kept indicator-sharing running through ENISA, and bio-detection stayed on watch.

It was a technical success that arrived politically thin. With budgets frozen and no new domestic build, ministers could point to fewer blackout minutes but not to restored capability.

### Wards, slowly back
The new Care Continuity and Retraining Compact did start, despite Council lawyers warning the legal base was stretched and two net-payer ministries holding out for ESF+ reprogramming rather than fresh money. Locum cover and overtime pay, plus rapid retraining on the European referral helpers, brought most walkout sites back on a rota.

Returns were site-by-site and grudging. Union branches in three countries refused the activity-logging and manual-fallback drills attached to the money, calling them surveillance. Triage times improved but referrals still needed hand-fixing, and managers quietly kept retired staff on call. No new permitting was attempted, and the promised freeze on large sovereignty builds reassured mayors while frustrating industry pledgers.

Rumours of bans on new data-centre grid hookups in the dry south circulated in local press, and a couple of night-time cable fires at construction fences were reported as single-source items. Nothing confirmed, but siting teams delayed surveys.

Public mood lifted only slightly: relief that clinics reopened, resentment that tools remained weaker.
