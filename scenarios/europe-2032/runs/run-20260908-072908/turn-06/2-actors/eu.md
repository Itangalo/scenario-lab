## In practice
We are completing the **Biological Threat Detection and Response Network** this turn: the ECDC has confirmed real-time genomic data flows from 22 member states, and anomaly-detection AI is now live in eight national labs. Hungary and Poland remain partial participants, but bilateral agreements ensure their data stays jurisdictionally contained while allowing threat alerts to propagate. We are embedding the network’s operational mandate into the new **Sovereign AI Inference Grid**, so future biothreat analysis runs on EU-owned inference stacks, not cloud instances revocable by foreign providers. The Grid’s first phase is advancing in Finland and Portugal, where retrofitting of national supercomputers has begun under the Cyber Resilience Act. Legal anchoring is progressing through the Digital Markets Act enforcement framework, treating sovereign inference as a condition of digital integrity.

At the same time, we are responding to the **ai_investment_collapse** by redirecting cohesion funds and repurposing stalled private data centre plans into public-stack expansion. Talks with ASML and STMicroelectronics continue under the European Chips Act — we are offering long-term procurement guarantees in exchange for priority on EU-destined tooling. Spain’s energy-related delay is being addressed through a side agreement on grid stability credits from the Innovation Fund. But the collapse also exposes a deeper fracture: young Europeans see no future in professions hollowed out by AI. The **Emergency Labour Transition Fund** launches this turn to prevent a lost generation — we are negotiating with member states to fast-track disbursement through national employment agencies, co-financed by a temporary levy on firms automating more than 20% of entry-level roles in targeted sectors. This is not just economic policy. It is political survival: without credible action on displacement, public sentiment will keep falling, and no amount of sovereign compute will restore legitimacy.
