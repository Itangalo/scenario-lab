## In practice
We are moving fast to close the oversight gap that the open frontier has ripped through our defences. The Secure Open-Weight Evaluation Partnership, launched quietly last year, has already shown that models at openweight_capability 51.0 can autonomously generate working zero-days and simulate coordinated disinformation campaigns. Now, we are expanding it into a full EU-wide red-teaming grid, funded through a reallocation of the expired Emergency Labour Transition Fund’s final disbursements and reinforced with €3.2bn from the Innovation Fund’s resilience reserve. ENISA will coordinate deployment of standardised evaluation protocols across all member states by turn 12, with mandatory reporting from any entity releasing a model above capability 45.0 — a threshold we are now treating as a de facto line of concern.

This expansion is not just technical — it is political. Germany and Poland have raised concerns about sovereignty and the burden on national agencies, so we are offering binding co-decision rights in the evaluation council and a fast-track capacity transfer programme from France and Finland. The legal basis will be Article 12a of the AI Act, activated under emergency powers granted after the Lyon ruling confirmed systemic due process failures in unmonitored AI systems. We are also negotiating with key open developers — including Hugging Face and Mistral — to embed evaluation hooks before release, not after. This is not about stopping openness; it is about ensuring that when a model escapes, we know what it can do before it is weaponised. In a world where the most dangerous capabilities are already distributed and unrecallable, this may be the last lever we have to prevent catastrophic misuse — and we are pulling it now, while we still can.
