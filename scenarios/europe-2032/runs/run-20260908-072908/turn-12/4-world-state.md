### The Wake-Up Call

A sophisticated cyberattack—powered by AI-generated tooling—paralyzed emergency dispatch in three member states and froze financial transactions in a fourth. The malware, a polymorphic ransomware strain propagated through a compromised open-source library, evaded detection using adaptive obfuscation techniques clearly developed with model assistance. With attribution stalled and public anger rising, the illusion of preparedness shattered.

In response, the EU launched the Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure, a large category 6 measure aimed at forcing rapid hardening of energy, water, and financial systems. Binding mandates from ENISA required AI-powered intrusion detection, zero-trust architecture, and failover redundancy, backed by conditional access to ECB liquidity. Fast-tracked procurement rules enabled immediate action.

France and the Netherlands implemented upgrades swiftly. Germany resisted binding mandates, citing fiscal strain and national sovereignty, though softened after being offered co-leadership. Poland tied compliance to cohesion fund disbursement, delaying rollout. Despite urgency, patchwork adoption undermined cohesion.

The Secure Open-Weight Evaluation Partnership Expansion officially concluded—but without establishing live red-teaming or real-time monitoring. Its failure to overcome legal fragmentation rendered it symbolic. No new open-weight safety mechanism became operational.

Meanwhile, frontier AI capability advanced to 68.0, driven by recursive self-improvement. With no safety upgrades deployed, ai_safety dropped to 27.0 due to the widening gap between capability and control. openweight_capability rose to 56.0 as distributed models absorbed recent advances.

Resilience increased to 61.0, reflecting partial progress on hardening critical systems. However, political capital fell to 4.0: the large new measure cost −3, the priority −1, and the completed measure yielded only +2 (partial delivery). Public sentiment worsened to 15.0 amid visible disruption and institutional friction.
