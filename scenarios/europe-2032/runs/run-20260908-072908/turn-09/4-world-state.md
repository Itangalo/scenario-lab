### Response Under Fire

The EU activates the Cyber Emergency Response Protocol following a cascading cyber incident rooted in a poisoned software dependency. Automated attack tooling, derived from frontier AI models, compromised health and transport systems across three member states. ENISA coordinates rapid patching and rerouting through redundant infrastructure, limiting systemic collapse. The €8bn emergency Innovation Fund disbursement accelerates hardening, contributing to a resilience gain. However, public confidence erodes as the Lyon court’s ruling on AI-driven benefits denials ignites a wave of local bans on administrative AI use.

Crucially, the Cyber Emergency Response Protocol was introduced this turn as a new high-cost measure and remains in early implementation. Its operational deployment marks the beginning of rollout, but full integration across member states will take time. As such, its contribution to resilience is partial and conditional, with full effects deferred to future turns.

### Sovereignty Secured, But Strained

The Sovereign AI Inference Grid achieves legal finalisation with Spain’s acceptance of energy commitments, completing the turn’s category 4 measures. This delivers +1 to eu_ai_sovereignty, consistent with prior friction. No capacity event occurs, but ai_capability rises by 1.5, triggering a −1 decay. Net sovereignty holds at 19.0.

### Political Capital Erodes

The portfolio charge is steep: three large measures at −3 each and a priority at −1, totaling −10. The Cyber Emergency Response Protocol earns no proposal bonus—it responds to an incident but was not proposed in reaction to a prior event. With eu_political_capital at 16.0 and public_sentiment above it, no sentiment lift applies. Internal pressures from the automated_decision_scandal and backlash_physical impose a combined −4 penalty, reflecting significant but bounded strain. After applying the portfolio charge and event terms, eu_political_capital falls to 9.0.

### Open Frontiers, Failing Safeguards

ai_capability advances to 63.5 as automated verification spreads. With no safety measure landing on shipped systems, ai_safety drops by −2. openweight_capability increases to 52.5, reflecting incremental replication of offensive cyber capabilities from the open frontier. While access widens, progress remains constrained by prior capability levels and diffusion lags, keeping openweight below frontier AI capability and aligned with historical accumulation rates.

Public sentiment declines to 20.0 amid protests, high-profile AI failures, and concerns over institutional overreach, but the drop is less severe than initially projected, reflecting residual trust in emergency response coordination.
