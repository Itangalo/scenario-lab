## In practice
This turn, we are treating the cyber_major_incident as a system-wide stress test—one that has revealed how far our defences lag behind offensive capabilities now accessible through AI-generated tooling. The attack, which exploited a poisoned software dependency with cascading effects across public health and transport systems, was not state-scale in origin but achieved state-scale disruption. We are activating the Cyber Emergency Response Protocol under Article 13a of the NIS2 Directive, mobilising ENISA to coordinate a unified incident response across affected member states. This includes immediate deployment of rapid-response teams, emergency patching of critical systems, and temporary rerouting of essential services through redundant infrastructure. The Innovation Fund is releasing €8bn in emergency allocations to cover urgent hardening and recovery costs, with disbursement tied to compliance with centralised mitigation guidance.

At the same time, we are finalising the integration of the Sovereign AI Inference Grid, now that Spain has accepted binding energy commitments in exchange for grid stability credits and priority access to future compute capacity. Legal anchoring under Article 36 of the Digital Markets Act is being transposed into delegated acts by the Commission, with adoption expected in the Internal Market Council by December. This closes the last major gap in our sovereign compute foundation—though its strategic value will only be proven when it runs under pressure. Meanwhile, the Emergency Labour Transition Fund is being restructured: with Germany still withholding co-funding, we are shifting to a model where national employment services pre-spend against future EU reimbursement, conditioned on verifiable placement rates. The Fund expires this turn, but its framework will carry into the next as part of a broader social resilience agenda.

The Secure Open-Weight Evaluation Partnership is now operational, with red teams in six member states conducting live assessments of newly surfaced models. Early findings suggest dangerous capabilities in automated exploit generation at openweight_capability 51.0—confirming that post-release triage is the only oversight left. But even as we build these defences, backlash_physical and the automated_decision_scandal have deepened the legitimacy crisis. A court in Lyon has ruled that an AI-assisted benefits denial system violated due process, triggering a wave of local moratoria on AI in public administration. We are responding not with retreat, but with a clearer line: AI must serve citizens, not replace judgment. That principle will guide the next two years—even as we prepare to defend the space in which it can still be upheld.
