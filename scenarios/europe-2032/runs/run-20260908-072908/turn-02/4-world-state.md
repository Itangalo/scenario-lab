### EU Attempts Upstream Control

In response, the European Union launched the **Frontier Transparency Compact**, demanding independent evaluation and public safety disclosures for any model near the frontier. Framed under the AI Act’s high-risk provisions, it targeted developers operating in or selling to the EU. Fast-track market access and liability shields were offered as incentives; DMAct gatekeeping powers threatened to block non-compliant models from app stores and cloud platforms.

Despite diplomatic outreach, resistance hardened among major US labs, who decried the rules as extraterritorial overreach. Early coalition-building with Japan and Canada yielded only cautious statements—no binding alignment. Inside the EU, legal scholars questioned whether autonomous systems could be classified as high-risk without demonstrated harm, raising fears of court challenges.
