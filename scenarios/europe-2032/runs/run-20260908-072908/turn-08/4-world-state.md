### Acceleration at the Frontier

Global AI capability climbs to 62.0 as automated verification spreads into legal, medical and engineering domains, enabling systems to complete multi-day professional projects with minimal oversight. The frontier advances rapidly, outpacing safety infrastructure.

### Sovereign Foundations, Fragile Consent

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme reaches formal completion, with four high-capacity compute sites now operational. However, full integration into the **Sovereign AI Inference Grid** is delayed in Spain due to unresolved energy allocation disputes, preventing legal finalisation. As a result, the expected sovereignty boost is partially deferred. While preliminary agreements secure binding energy commitments through Innovation Fund incentives, these lay only the groundwork for future EU-controlled inference capacity—they do not yet yield full operational control. The delayed integration limits the immediate gain, contributing +1 this turn instead of the full potential.

Simultaneously, the **Secure Open-Weight Evaluation Partnership** launches across six member states, linking ENISA and Europol with academic red teams and international partners. Though not pre-release control, it establishes rapid triage for newly surfaced models, responding directly to the narrowing window after open releases.

Public discontent grows more visible. Reports emerge of protests near data centres in Bavaria, Brittany and Galicia, with isolated incidents of vandalism and temporary blockades. Security forces respond to escalating tensions, and policing costs begin to mount. While no coordinated campaign has yet crystallised, authorities express concern about the potential for further destabilisation. Germany pauses disbursement of the **Emergency Labour Transition Fund**, citing fiscal prudence amid rising enforcement expenses, weakening social cohesion.

Automation displaces mid-level professionals faster than retraining schemes can adapt, and visible dependency on foreign model development fuels resentment. Public sentiment dips to 27.0.

Resilience sees a marginal gain from improved cyber coordination, and ai_safety rises slightly due to expanded monitoring—though still far below capability growth. Political capital remains unchanged, supported by progress on strategic infrastructure but weighed down by unrest, fiscal constraints, and incomplete delivery on the Inference Grid.

### A Narrow Path Forward

The EU advances its compute ambitions, but integration hurdles and weakening legitimacy challenge long-term sustainability. Strategic projects proceed in stages, not leaps—each delay a reminder that sovereignty must be both built and accepted. The path forward narrows as societal pressures test institutional resolve, and early warning signs suggest deeper fractures may lie ahead if response lags behind disruption. The **Sovereign AI Inference Grid** remains in flight, with full benefits postponed to future turns.
