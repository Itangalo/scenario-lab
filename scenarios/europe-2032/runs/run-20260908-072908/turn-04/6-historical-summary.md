In 2027, global AI capabilities rose to 58.0, outpacing stagnant EU AI sovereignty, which declined to 17.0 due to insufficient domestic compute growth. A major AI-designed respiratory pathogen attack in a European city resulted in dozens of deaths, shifting AI risk perceptions from disruption to mortality and triggering a rapid erosion of public trust. In response, the EU launched the Biological Threat Detection and Response Network under the ECDC, integrating AI-driven genomic surveillance and enforcing binding lab reporting and cross-border containment protocols. Meanwhile, the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators achieved partial success, with 18 member states fully implementing intrusion detection systems and no new breaches in protected sectors, though disparities in national capacity persisted. Emergency NIS2 funding accelerated deployment in Bulgaria, Romania, and Croatia, while Italy and Greece agreed to phased compliance. The Frontier Transparency Compact remained stalled, with no US lab participation and ongoing legal uncertainties, while a proposed voluntary EU disclosure framework gained little traction. Political capital continued to decline as public sentiment dipped amid growing backlash against AI infrastructure and rising calls for moratoria. Despite institutional strain, resilience increased due to Cyber Shield’s completion and the bio-network’s rollout, though long-term impacts remain uncertain.
