## New measure  
**Biological Threat Detection and Response Network**  
Establish a continent-wide early-warning system for AI-assisted biothreats, integrating genomic surveillance, lab reporting, and AI-driven anomaly detection, with mandatory reporting and coordinated containment protocols.

`Category:` 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 6  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +10–14, ai_safety +3–5 (via faster detection and containment), public_sentiment +4–6 if a major incident is averted or swiftly managed  
`Applies to:` EU member states, EEA countries, and EU-funded research laboratories

## Priority  
**Biological Threat Detection and Response Network** — because the bio_incident has shattered the assumption that catastrophic risk is hypothetical, and we can no longer treat biological preparedness as a subset of cyber resilience; this is now the frontline of autonomous harm, and our credibility depends on meeting it.

## In practice  
We are pivoting hard from general preparedness to targeted defence against autonomous threats that can kill at scale. The bio_incident — a modified pathogen released in a densely populated area, with sequencing data pointing to AI-assisted design — has changed everything. Public fear is no longer abstract, and campaign_backlash is gaining traction: cities are halting data centre expansions, unions are demanding moratoria on AI in hiring, and displaced workers are being promised protection. We cannot afford to be seen as reactive. This turn, we are launching the Biological Threat Detection and Response Network under the authority of the ECDC and in coordination with national biosurveillance agencies. The system will integrate real-time genomic sequencing from hospitals and border labs, mandate reporting of novel agents from all EU-funded and commercial research facilities, and deploy an EU-operated AI monitor trained to flag anomalous patterns in pathogen data. We are drawing on the Finnish swarm-detection architecture as a model, but this time with binding obligations and cross-border enforcement triggers. Funding will come from the Health Union and redirected cohesion funds — €8.5 billion over two turns — and we are preparing legal acts under Article 168 TFEU to override national delays.

At the same time, Cyber Shield for Critical Operators reaches its final turn. ENISA has confirmed that 18 member states have implemented the core detection protocols, and the emergency funding mechanism under NIS2 has unlocked adoption in Bulgaria, Romania and Croatia. Italy and Greece remain partial, but we are accepting phased compliance in exchange for binding commitments by October. The system held during a recent wave of AI-generated ransomware — no new outages — but the gains are fragile. We are not declaring victory. Instead, we are folding the emergency acceleration protocol into a broader operational doctrine, to be tested in cross-border red-team exercises this summer. The Frontier Transparency Compact remains stalled: no US lab has engaged, and the European Court has requested further clarification on high-risk classification. We are now preparing a fallback — a voluntary disclosure framework with liability protection for EU-based developers — to preserve some momentum. But the truth is, our leverage is eroding. Without sovereign detection and response capacity, no transparency regime will hold. That is why this turn, we are betting on resilience as the foundation of sovereignty — not its consolation prize.
