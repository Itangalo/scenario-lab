Establish mandatory intrusion detection and incident response protocols across EU critical infrastructure operators, with joint exercises and real-time threat sharing.

## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: one long, one immediate. The long fight is compute sovereignty. InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech sovereignty package are already in motion, but they are slow, capital-intensive, and politically fragile. We are pushing the European Investment Bank and national governments to designate AI infrastructure as strategic under Article 346 TFEU, which allows for state aid and fast-tracking on security grounds. The Council’s Competitiveness formation is divided — Germany and France support the push, but Eastern member states fear being left out of the funding loop. We are offering regional equity: one gigafactory per NUTS-1 region in the final bid round, and a binding commitment that 30% of the private capital leveraged by the Tech sovereignty package must go to mid-tier operators outside the hyperscaler orbit.

But we cannot wait six turns for those to land. The cyber_test_shot event exposed a lethal lag: the frontier moved to autonomous agents, but our defences are still reactive and siloed. We are therefore launching Cyber Shield for Critical Operators under the NIS2 directive, using the Commission’s delegated powers to set binding detection and response standards. ENISA will lead, with a new mandate to conduct red-teaming across cross-border operators. We are allocating €4.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility to subsidise sensor deployment and SOC integration in utilities and ports. The resistance is real — Italy and Greece cite cost burdens, and private operators fear liability. We are offering a liability shield for good-faith reporting and a 75% co-financing rate for the first three years. This is not about catching up to the frontier; it is about ensuring that when the next agent finds its way in, someone is already watching.
