## In practice
We are completing two major capital programmes this turn: **InvestAI Gigafactories** and the **Biological Threat Detection and Response Network**, both formally finishing their implementation arcs. The Gigafactories programme has secured site agreements in Finland, Portugal, Poland, and Belgium, with construction underway at three. While private investment has faltered globally, the €200bn fund has preserved critical momentum in domestic compute build-out. We are now folding its remaining coordination tasks into the **Sovereign AI Inference Grid**, treating the Gigafactories not as standalone data centres but as anchor nodes in a broader sovereign stack. Legal anchoring proceeds under Article 36 of the Digital Markets Act, redefining access to inference capacity as a matter of digital integrity. This allows us to enforce EU jurisdictional control over models running on these systems, regardless of origin.

At the same time, we are responding to **openweight_frontier_release** and **verification_widens** by launching a new **Secure Open-Weight Evaluation Partnership** under Category 1. With open models now arriving within months of the frontier and spreading irreversibly, we can no longer rely on pre-release controls alone. This measure establishes rapid-response evaluation cells in six member states, linked to counterparts in Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Norway through the emerging **middle_power_coalition**. These teams will conduct structured red-teaming on new open releases, focusing especially on domains where automatic verification has lowered the barrier to misuse — contract manipulation, clinical code generation, and engineering design. Funding comes from redirected Horizon Europe safety budgets and the Innovation Fund, with staffing drawn from national cybersecurity agencies and academic AI labs. The goal is not to stop distribution — that ship has sailed — but to ensure that when dangerous capabilities emerge in the open, someone sees them *before* they are weaponised.

Despite progress on infrastructure, political cohesion remains fragile. Spain continues to resist full participation in the Inference Grid over energy allocation, and we are offering additional grid stability credits from the Innovation Fund to unlock its commitment. Germany’s refusal to adopt the automation levy threatens to undermine the **Emergency Labour Transition Fund**, so we are shifting toward voluntary employer co-investment schemes backed by tax incentives, while pushing for faster disbursement through national employment services. Public sentiment remains at 30.0, and without visible movement on both sovereignty and social protection, we risk losing legitimacy even as we gain capability. That is why both the Grid and the Labour Fund remain central: one secures our future agency, the other our present consent.
