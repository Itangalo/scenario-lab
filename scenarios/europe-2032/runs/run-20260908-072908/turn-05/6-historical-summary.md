In 2027, global AI capabilities reached 58.0, outpacing the EU's stagnant sovereignty score of 17.0 amid insufficient domestic compute growth. A deadly AI-designed respiratory pathogen attack in Europe shifted public perception of AI risk from disruption to mortality, eroding trust and prompting the EU to establish the Biological Threat Detection and Response Network under the ECDC, integrating AI-driven genomic surveillance and mandatory cross-border protocols. The Cyber Shield for Critical Operators achieved partial success, with 18 member states fully implementing intrusion detection systems and no breaches in protected sectors, though national disparities persisted; emergency NIS2 funding accelerated deployment in Bulgaria, Romania, and Croatia, while Italy and Greece committed to phased compliance.

The Frontier Transparency Compact collapsed due to lack of US lab participation and legal uncertainties, leading the EU to abandon voluntary disclosure efforts. In response, the European Commission launched the Sovereign AI Inference Grid—a €12 billion, three-turn initiative under the Digital Markets Act and Cyber Resilience Act—to host critical public AI workloads on EU-controlled infrastructure. Initial retrofitting began in Finland and Portugal, with quantum-resistant links planned, but supply chain constraints emerged through early talks with ASML and STMicroelectronics, and Spain delayed integration citing energy priorities.

Public sentiment declined further as AI-driven job losses mounted in law, accounting, and software, fueling protests against data centres and resistance to the biothreat network in Hungary and Poland over data sovereignty concerns. Political capital continued to erode, exacerbated by perceptions of the Inference Grid as an elite project disconnected from youth unemployment. Resilience increased slightly due to operational gains in cyber and bio-surveillance, but ai_safety dipped as global AI capability advanced without commensurate safeguards. The US election of a technologically cooperative president offered temporary relief, though it introduced strategic ambivalence by reducing immediate pressure for EU autonomy. US technology posture remains pending until the new administration takes office next turn.
