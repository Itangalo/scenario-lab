### A Wave of Automated Harm

A coordinated ransomware attack, generated and orchestrated by AI, crippled regional health systems in Belgium and Bulgaria. The intrusion exploited zero-day vulnerabilities identified and weaponized by model-generated code, overwhelming unprepared defences. Though attribution remains unclear, the tooling bore hallmarks of open-weight models now widely accessible. Public anxiety surged, even as emergency services held.

### EU Responds with Emergency Framework Activation

In response, the European Commission invoked the NIS2 emergency clause, initiating a formal process to fast-track binding technical standards for intrusion detection and mobilize cohesion funds to support implementation. The proposal includes up to €12 billion in potential funding, with a draft plan to cover 90% of costs for vulnerable member states. However, actual disbursement and deployment depend on approval by the Competitiveness Council and national absorption capacity — processes that will take at least one full turn to complete.

ENISA began integrating a newly operational swarm-behaviour detection system — developed in Finland — into the Cyber Shield framework, marking early progress on defensive capabilities. But full deployment across critical operators is pending interoperability assessments and member state coordination.

The **Cyber Shield Emergency Acceleration Protocol** was formally proposed this turn but did not advance to implementation. Legal concerns over harmonization and fiscal burden delayed a vote in the Competitiveness Council. No binding standards have yet taken effect, and no funds have been released. ### Transparency Efforts Stall

The **Frontier Transparency Compact** gained little traction. US laboratories maintained their stance against what they called regulatory imperialism. No major developer committed to third-party evaluation, and internal legal doubts about classifying autonomous systems as high-risk weakened the EU’s leverage. The European AI Office continued drafting evaluator accreditations, but without early adopters, momentum stalled.
