## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that without operational unity, neither mandate can be fulfilled.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now act only where failure of action would be irreversible, and where inaction carries greater systemic risk than miscalculation; this turn, that threshold has been crossed.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Enforce sovereign boundaries on capability diffusion and achieve unity of action in defence and control — but only through enforceable infrastructure, not merely through regulation.  

- Trigger: the cyber_major_incident  
- Grounds: A large, automated attack has landed, exploiting model-generated tooling, and it has exposed the hollowness of our current resilience. The Cross-Border Resilience Node is too narrow, too late. The Cyber Shield failed not from design but from non-participation — Germany and Poland opted out — and now we see the cost: cascading disruption in public services, attribution stalled, response fragmented. This is not a failure of intent but of enforceability. Our commitment to act under uncertainty must now shift from permissiveness to compulsion, because inaction in the face of known, weaponised capability diffusion is no longer a risk — it is a surrender. We revise `two_mandates` to reflect that sovereignty without cohesion is fiction, and `two_year_commitment` to demand not just boundaries but the means to enforce them — because a red line that cannot be policed is not a line at all.

## New measure  
**Emergency Activation of the Cross-Border Resilience Node**  
Activates and expands the existing node into a standing operational unit with mandatory participation from all member states in critical corridors, backed by emergency funding and enforcement powers.

## Priority  
**Emergency Activation of the Cross-Border Resilience Node** — because the cyber_major_incident has proven that our current defences are not just weak but structurally bypassed, and because resilience is now the only lever that can both prevent lasting harm and preserve the Union’s credibility in the eyes of its citizens.

## In practice  
The cyber attack hit in the first week of July: a model-generated exploit chain, auto-deployed across municipal services in Belgium, the Netherlands, and northern Germany. It disabled emergency dispatch systems for over twelve hours, disrupted water monitoring in two major river basins, and exposed sensitive personnel data. ENISA confirmed within 48 hours that the exploit was not in any known repository — it was AI-synthesised, likely from a model at or near 62.0 capability. The attackers remain unidentified, but the tooling bears the hallmarks of autonomous red-teaming gone rogue or weaponised. This is not a drill. The public is furious. Protests have turned violent in Brussels. We cannot afford another failure.

We are activating the Cross-Border Resilience Node under Article 216 TFEU, but this time we are not asking for volunteers — we are mandating participation. The Node will be upgraded to a permanent operational unit under eu-LISA, with direct access to national CERTs, real-time telemetry from all operators in the energy and telecom corridors, and the authority to initiate red-team exercises unilaterally. Funding of €12bn will be redirected from the stalled Gigafactories programme to staff and equip it — not as a replacement, but as a repurposing: compute sovereignty means nothing if our lights go out. We are invoking the solidarity clause (Article 222 TFEU) to compel participation, backed by the threat of withholding cohesion funds from non-compliant states. France and Finland are on board; Germany is resisting, citing sovereignty concerns, but we are offering them co-leadership of the technical committee. Poland is digging in — they still want to nationalise audit rights — but we are bypassing them through bilateral channels with the Visegrád group, offering shared access to the Node’s detection models in exchange for compliance.

The Sovereign Red Lines for Open Weights will still be tabled this month — we cannot let one crisis erase the other — but its political cover now depends on demonstrating that the EU can act decisively when lives are at stake. We are linking the two: the safety mirror proposal will be expanded to include mandatory participation in the Node’s monitoring framework for any entity releasing above 50.0. Openness is not a right — it is a privilege, and it must come with operational accountability. This is not the unity we wanted, but it is the unity the moment demands.
