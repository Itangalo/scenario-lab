  - Trigger: the cyber_major_incident
  - Grounds: A large, automated attack has landed, exploiting model-generated tooling, and it has exposed the hollowness of our current resilience. The Cross-Border Resilience Node is too narrow, too late. The Cyber Shield failed not from design but from non-participation — Germany and Poland opted out — and now we see the cost: cascading disruption in public services, attribution stalled, response fragmented. This is not a failure of intent but of enforceability. Our commitment to act under uncertainty must now shift from permissiveness to compulsion, because inaction in the face of known, weaponised capability diffusion is no longer a risk — it is a surrender. We revise `two_mandates` to reflect that sovereignty without cohesion is fiction, and `two_year_commitment` to demand not just boundaries but the means to enforce them — because a red line that cannot be policed is not a line at all.
  - Evidence quoted: cyber_major_incident: A large, largely automated attack lands. Its form is chosen at the time – a ransomware sweep across public services, a compromised software dependency with an unknown blast radius, or disruption of infrastructure directly – and severity scales with how far offensive capability has outrun defence. Damage is real and public, defenders are visibly behind, and attribution takes months. Whoever ran it, the tooling was model-generated.
  - Reason: The incident exposed the failure of voluntary cooperation, directly challenging the enforceability of current resilience commitments.
