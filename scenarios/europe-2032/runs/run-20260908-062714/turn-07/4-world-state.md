### The Fractured Response

A major AI-generated cyberattack disabled emergency dispatch and water monitoring systems across three countries, exposing critical gaps in the EU’s defences. Despite the existence of the Cyber Shield directive, key states like Germany and Poland had opted out of full participation. The attack’s sophistication — autonomously generated exploit chains — confirmed that offensive capabilities are now outpacing institutional readiness.

In response, the EU activated the Cross-Border Resilience Node under emergency powers, expanding it into a mandatory, standing unit with real-time access to critical infrastructure telemetry. €12 billion was redirected from the stalled InvestAI Gigafactories programme to fund the effort, and enforcement was backed by threats to withhold cohesion funds. While France and Finland quickly joined, Germany resisted, and Poland outright rejected the framework, deepening internal rifts.

The activation counts as a new large Category 6 measure, launched under crisis conditions. However, its success is partial: operational reach is limited by ongoing legal disputes and uneven national buy-in. The move strengthens resilience planning but at high political cost.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Safety Collapses

The `capability_jump` event — a discontinuous advance in model-driven cyber exploitation — pushed `ai_capability` to 63.5. No safety improvements landed on deployed systems, and the jump itself degraded assurance, reducing `ai_safety` by 6 points to 8.0.

`openweight_capability` rose to 57.0, pulled up by the gap between frontier capability and access restrictions, though no major open release occurred.

The Tech Sovereignty Package and Cyber Shield both finished this turn. However, the latter’s failure to prevent a major incident negates any resilience gain from completion. The Sovereignty Package’s institutional closure granted a +3 bonus to `eu_ai_sovereignty`, but this was offset by a −1 decay due to `ai_capability` rising ≥2, and a −1 from the `cyber_major_incident` degrading EU cohesion and access reliability. Net: 16 → 15.

### Political Capital Drains

The portfolio charge for three large measures (−9) and the new priority (−1) drained political capital. The new Cyber Shield activation earned a +3 proposal bonus for directly answering a major incident, but public fury and member-state defiance led to a −5 sentiment-driven penalty. Net: 12 → 10.

Public sentiment fell to 12.0 due to visible dependency, systemic failure, and the perception that EU action is reactive and coercive.
