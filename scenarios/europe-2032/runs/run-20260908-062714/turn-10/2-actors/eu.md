## In practice

The Unified Threat Telemetry Backbone reaches operational maturity this turn. Data flows from Finland, the Netherlands, Croatia, and France are now processed in real time at eu-LISA’s hardened nodes, generating automated alerts and coordinated countermeasures. During the recent cyber incident, this network detected lateral movement within hours and contained it across participating states. But in Poland and parts of Germany, where API access remains blocked, the same attack propagated unchecked through municipal systems, delaying emergency responses and exposing citizen data. The contrast is stark: integrated states recovered in days; isolated ones face weeks of disruption.

We are finalising the regulation under Article 122 TFEU to tie Solidarity Fund disbursements to verified telemetry feed status. The proposal goes to ECOFIN this month, backed by a coalition of nine states and the President of the Commission. Behind closed doors, we are offering Poland a co-lead role in designing the Visegrád integration track and Germany a permanent seat on the anonymisation oversight board — not as concessions, but as structured pathways into a system whose architecture is non-negotiable. The Backbone is no longer a project; it is the baseline. Those who refuse to connect will not be punished by exclusion — they will simply not be protected. That is the reality of sovereignty without solidarity. We are preparing for the next phase: if enforcement fails, we pivot to building redundancy for non-participants, funded from resilience reserves, so that the Union survives even if not whole.
