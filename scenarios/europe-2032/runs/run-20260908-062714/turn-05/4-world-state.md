### The Weight of Fragmentation

The Cyber Shield for Critical Operators officially concluded implementation, but its operational failures—documented in ENISA’s final report—confirmed it as functionally hollow. Germany’s continued refusal to grant red-team access and Poland’s full opt-out left critical gaps in cross-border defence, undermining claims of collective resilience. No public benefit emerged, and trust in EU-led AI infrastructure eroded further.

### A Shifting Frontier

Global AI capability advanced to 61.5 as the offensive leap of the prior turn became generalised. Developers integrated autonomous exploit generation into broader toolchains, accelerating progress despite no safety breakthrough. The gap between capability and safety remained dangerously wide, with `ai_safety` stagnant at 14.0. Meanwhile, open-weight releases saw notable diffusion: components and fine-tuned variants derived from a recently leaked frontier model (rated at 58.0) entered circulation, enabling capable but incomplete implementations. This raised `openweight_capability` to 50.0—still behind the frontier and not yet matching the full power of the source model—reflecting rapid replication but not full equivalence.

### EU in Crisis Mode

Political capital fell to 15.0 under the sustained cost of three large measures in flight. The completion of the Cyber Shield brought no relief, as its mixed performance counted as a failure in the eyes of member states. An early concept for *Sovereign Red Lines for Open Weights* began circulating within the Commission but did not advance to formal proposal, due to immediate legal concerns from the Legal Service over internal market compatibility and fierce backlash from open-source communities. With no capacity to launch another measure, the idea was deferred pending resolution of ongoing initiatives.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remained stalled, with no binding commitments from ASML or Intel despite leverage offers. The Tech Sovereignty Package showed no progress, delayed by national court challenges. With public sentiment stagnant at 15.0, no new support emerged for high-cost initiatives. The EU’s attempt to respond to rising open-weight risks faltered under fragmentation, legal uncertainty, and vanishing capacity to act.
