- Grounds: The previous commitment — securing a sovereign and resilient foundation for EU agency in the face of accelerating, ungovernable AI capability — has reached its endpoint without full success. While investments were made, they failed to cohere into operational sovereignty due to fragmentation, lack of enforcement leverage, and collapsing public trust. The new frontier at 60.0 capability with safety at 14.0 demands not just infrastructure but control: the ability to act as one body in defence, to restrict dangerous diffusion, and to harden against cascading failure. The next two years must shift from building foundations to enforcing boundaries and achieving unity of action across member states.

## In practice  
The Cyber Shield for Critical Operators has formally concluded implementation but operates in name only. Berlin still refuses external red-team access under national security claims, and Warsaw remains outside the system entirely. ENISA’s upcoming assessment will document systemic interoperability failures and unsustainable coordination burdens. We are already preparing a follow-up: a targeted resilience upgrade focused on cross-border energy and telecom corridors, to be proposed next turn once the evaluation lands and political capital stabilises. For now, we accept that symbolic unity is not functional unity — and that credibility must be rebuilt through fewer, sharper commitments.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain our lifeline. With anchor tenants lost and construction at risk in Marseille and Wrocław, we have redirected €30bn from the European Innovation Council’s reserve to guarantee early infrastructure. This is not aid but leverage: we are offering ASML and Intel accelerated permitting and market access under the Tech Sovereignty Package in exchange for binding commitments to co-locate advanced packaging facilities. Negotiations proceed under Article 207 TFEU, treating semiconductor integration as a trade policy lever. Without control over final-stage packaging — where capabilities crystallise — sovereignty is performance, not power.

We are launching a new legal instrument: *Sovereign Red Lines for Open Weights*, to be tabled under the AI Act’s implementing powers. It would prohibit any developer from releasing model weights capable of exceeding 50.0 on the capability index unless equivalent safety measures are independently verified in deployed systems. Enforcement hinges on market access: no firm wanting to operate in the EU can distribute open models above threshold without compliance. The Commission’s Legal Service is assessing feasibility under internal market law, while JRC and eu-LISA begin drafting technical benchmarks. Resistance is expected from open-source advocates and US-based labs, but the election_retrenchment in Washington weakens their position — and strengthens ours to act unilaterally. This is not about stopping openness, but ensuring it does not become a vector for uncontrolled, irreversible harm.
