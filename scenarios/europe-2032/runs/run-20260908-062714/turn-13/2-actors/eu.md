* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.``
  - Grounds: The bio_uplift_findings event is a categorically stronger signal than any prior warning in the bio domain. This changes the nature of the second mandate — preventing lasting harm — from a general precaution to an immediate, specific, and existential constraint. The tension between sovereignty and survival is no longer abstract.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.``
  - Grounds: The failure of containment at the frontier lab, combined with the telemetry gaps in Poland and Germany, demonstrates that uncertainty is no longer epistemic but operational: we are not just missing signals, we are losing control. Waiting for clarity is no longer a delay in action — it is a surrender of agency. The commitment stands, but its interpretation must shift from strategic patience to operational urgency.

* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Sovereign integration through enforceable technical unity``
  - Grounds: The political paralysis at 0.0 political capital and the physical backlash against infrastructure reveal that enforceability cannot be assumed. Technical unity is meaningless without political cohesion. The commitment evolves: we now prioritise *minimum viable unity* — the smallest set of shared capabilities and obligations that can hold the Union together through the next wave of disruption.

## In practice
The Unified Threat Telemetry Backbone is now fully operational, but its success has become a mirror: it shows us not only what works, but what we have excluded. With Poland and parts of Germany still outside the loop, we are finalising the legal instrument under Article 122 TFEU to link Solidarity Fund disbursements to telemetry compliance. Behind closed doors, we are offering Poland a co-lead role in the Visegrád integration track and Germany a permanent seat on the anonymisation oversight board — not concessions, but structured inclusion into a non-negotiable architecture. If the Court upholds the measure, we move to enforcement; if not, we pivot to building redundant resilience for non-participants, funded from existing reserves, so the Union survives even if not whole.

Now, the bio_uplift_findings event demands a parallel architecture — one that does not wait for legal consensus. We are drafting the Biocontainment Shield under the EU’s health security framework, invoking Article 19 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, which allows for urgent action in public health emergencies. The European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC) will lead, with technical integration by eu-LISA and sequencing oversight from a newly convened European Biosecurity Council. We are mandating real-time screening at all synthesis facilities above a threshold volume, with penalties for non-compliance tied to research funding and export licensing. This is not a voluntary standard — it is a floor. We are fast-tracking procurement of screening software from EU-based developers, with a requirement for full model transparency to prevent adversarial evasion.

The backlash_physical events in France and Hungary force us to act differently, not less. We cannot build more data centres under armed guard. Instead, we are redirecting compute procurement toward distributed, hardened micro-facilities in member states with stable public sentiment, using InvestAI reserves to subsidise local ownership models. This is not retreat — it is dispersal as doctrine. The Union will not collapse into incoherence, but it will not force unity either. We are holding the core, extending the shield, and preparing for the next wave.
