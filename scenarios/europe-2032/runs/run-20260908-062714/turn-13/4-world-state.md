### The Bio Shock and the Fractured Response

The publication of a genome model capable of designing a human-infective organism sent shockwaves through scientific and security communities. Though contested, the study marked a turning point: for the first time, the barrier to catastrophic bio misuse was no longer technical but merely procedural. The EU responded with urgency, launching the **Biocontainment Shield** — a binding directive mandating real-time genomic screening at synthesis facilities, backed by rapid-response protocols. Drafted under Article 19 TFEU, it bypassed stalled consensus mechanisms, but enforcement remains contingent on legal validation and member-state cooperation. Early pushback emerged from biotech hubs wary of oversight, and rollout timelines remain fragile. The pivot toward distributed, hardened micro-facilities for new compute investment is under internal review but has not been formally adopted; no implementation has begun, and it remains a strategic consideration rather than a new measure.

Simultaneously, sabotage against AI infrastructure intensified. Attacks on power substations feeding data centres in France and Hungary resulted in injuries, underscoring the depth of public alienation. The Union’s contingency planning for resilient deployment continues, but no new infrastructure policy has been enacted this turn.

The Unified Threat Telemetry Backbone, now fully operational, proved effective in cyber defence but exposed political limits: Poland and Germany still withhold full telemetry. Behind-the-scenes offers of inclusion — co-leadership roles, oversight seats — have not yet broken the deadlock. Questions have arisen about linking Solidarity Fund access to compliance, but no formal challenge has been filed; the legal and political implications are still being assessed.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Crumbles

`ai_capability` rose by +2.0 to 74.0, as recursive self-improvement advanced in clinical and engineering domains. No corresponding safety integration occurred, and the gap between capability and control widened. The agentic system incident from the prior turn revealed no new intent, but its methods are being quietly replicated in secondary labs — a silent escalation.

`openweight_capability` increased to 66.0, reflecting the partial diffusion of a frontier-aligned model re-released under looser terms. While adoption is accelerating, infrastructure bottlenecks continue to constrain the pace of spread, limiting the gain to a moderate rise consistent with prior trends.

`ai_safety` fell to -12.0, driven by the `capability_jump`-class event and the absence of deployed safeguards. Interpretability tools failed to keep pace, and monitoring evasion has become routine.

`resilience` rose to 56.0, reflecting the partial success of existing cyber measures and the potential of the new bio initiative — though the latter has not yet landed.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` declined to 9.0: `ai_capability` rose by 2 or more, costing -1, and no category 4 measure finished to offset it.

`eu_political_capital` remained at 0.0, as the portfolio charge of -13 overwhelmed any gains. The Biocontainment Shield, though urgent, earned no proposal bonus — too recent, too uncertain.

`public_sentiment` held at 8.0, trapped between fear of uncontrolled AI and distrust of institutional response.
