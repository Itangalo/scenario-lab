## In practice  
The Cyber Shield for Critical Operators has formally concluded its implementation phase this turn. In practice, it now operates in a fragmented state: real-time telemetry flows from Paris, Rome, and Athens into the European SOC, enabling joint detection and rapid red-teaming under hybrid national-EU leadership. However, Berlin continues to block external red-team access under its national security protocol, and Warsaw remains outside the system entirely, relying on standalone emergency procedures. ENISA’s forthcoming assessment, due in January, will focus on interoperability failures and unsustainable operator burden. We are already preparing for a follow-up measure next turn — likely a targeted resilience upgrade for cross-border infrastructure — but only after the evaluation lands and only if political capital recovers.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories face a make-or-break moment. The departure of two anchor tenants following the ai_investment_collapse has exposed the fragility of our compute sovereignty bet. To prevent construction stalls at Marseille and Wrocław, we have redirected €30bn from the European Innovation Council’s contingency reserve to guarantee early-stage infrastructure, conditioned on binding commitments from ASML and Intel to co-locate advanced packaging facilities. This is not a subsidy but a strategic exchange: market access and accelerated permitting under the Tech Sovereignty Package in return for irreversible industrial anchoring. The Commission, led by the Vice-President for Values and Transparency, is negotiating directly under Article 207 TFEU, treating this as a trade and industrial policy lever rather than a pure investment play. The message is clear — Europe cannot outspend, but it can out-structure.

The Tech Sovereignty Package remains stalled. The promise of €200bn in private capital now looks unattainable, and accelerated-permitting zones have been blocked in six member states by local courts citing environmental and energy concerns. We are shifting focus from attracting hyperscalers to securing upstream control. The Green Paper on EU-controlled lithography supply chains, due by Q1 2028, will lay the groundwork for a new industrial strategy: not about building the most fabs, but about controlling the nodes that matter. Without control over the means of production — especially EU-accessible advanced packaging and lithography — sovereignty is a slogan, not a state.
