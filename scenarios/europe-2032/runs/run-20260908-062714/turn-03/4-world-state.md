### The Shield Cracks, the Ground Shifts

The Cyber Shield for Critical Operators officially completed its rollout, but its fragmented operation exposed more fault lines than it closed. While Paris, Rome, and Athens now feed real-time threat data into the European SOC, Berlin’s refusal to cede red-team autonomy and Warsaw’s full opt-out crippled cross-border coordination during the latest attack. The incident—targeting hospital networks and rail signalling—was contained faster than before, but only because legacy fail-safes, not AI defences, absorbed the blow. ENISA’s upcoming assessment is expected to highlight severe interoperability gaps and unsustainable operator burden.

Public sentiment worsened sharply. Violent protests at data centres in Ireland and Poland were followed by regional strikes in IT and energy sectors, where workers denounced AI as a job-destroying foreign dependency. Media framed the Cyber Shield as a hollow victory: European systems defended by non-European intelligence, failing where national control was asserted.

Meanwhile, the capability_jump sent shockwaves through the technical community. A new model demonstrated autonomous exploit generation at unprecedented speed, shifting offensive cyber capabilities decisively ahead of defences. Though general competence rose only modestly, the leap in verifiable domains triggered alarm. With no corresponding safety advance, `ai_safety` dropped.

The EU doubled down on compute sovereignty. The InvestAI Gigafactories, now deemed existential, received €30bn in redirected innovation funds to secure ASML and Intel partnerships. However, stalled permitting and legal challenges to the Tech Sovereignty Package revealed structural bottlenecks. The Green Paper on lithography marked a strategic pivot—but too late to stop the erosion of trust.

### Political Capital at Breaking Point

With three large measures still in flight and no new proposals, political capital barely held. The completion of the Cyber Shield provided a modest boost, but it was overwhelmed by ongoing costs and the fallout from the cyber incident. Public hostility deepened, dragging sentiment below 30 and further constraining action.
