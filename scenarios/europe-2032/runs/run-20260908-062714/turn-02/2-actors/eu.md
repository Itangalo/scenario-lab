## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that resilience is no longer a backup — it is the front line. The cyber_major_incident did not just expose vulnerabilities; it exposed a fatal lag between offensive capability and defensive coordination. The Cyber Shield is now in force across Brussels, Paris, and Rome, with ENISA standing up a European SOC that aggregates telemetry in real time. But resistance remains: Berlin demands veto rights over red-team access, Warsaw cites national emergency protocols, and port operators warn of operational paralysis. We counter with targeted funding from the Digital Europe Programme — €1.2bn to harden SOC integration and cover transition costs — and offer joint governance: no red team operates without a national co-lead, and no data leaves EU jurisdiction. This is not just compliance; it is the beginning of a nervous system for Europe.

Meanwhile, InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package remain in flight, but the ai_investment_collapse has shaken investor confidence. Two of the three planned anchor tenants have withdrawn, citing uncertainty. We respond by redirecting €30bn in contingency funds from the European Innovation Council to de-risk early construction, tied to binding commitments from ASML and Intel to co-locate packaging facilities. The leverage is clear: we will not be left waiting for American capital or American permission. But we also know that compute without control is dangerous, and control without resilience is futile. So we press on the Shield, not just as a directive, but as the first operational test of whether the Union can act at speed when the lights flicker.
