## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that without operational unity and minimum shared infrastructure, neither mandate can be fulfilled, and fragmentation is the enemy of both sovereignty and safety.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but now only where failure of action would be irreversible, and only when the cost of inaction demonstrably exceeds the cost of coercion; this turn, we have crossed that threshold.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Enforce sovereign boundaries on capability diffusion and achieve unity of action in defence and control — but only through enforceable infrastructure, not merely through regulation, and now with binding technical integration, not just legal obligation.

- Trigger: the cyber_major_incident and the failure of voluntary compliance under Cyber Shield  
- Grounds: The attack confirmed that capability is now weaponised and autonomous, and that our defences are bypassed not by technical weakness but by jurisdictional fragmentation. Germany and Poland did not fail to comply because they lacked capacity — they failed because they refused to cede operational control. The Cross-Border Resilience Node, even when activated, cannot function as a patchwork. Our identity must reflect that sovereignty without enforceable integration is a fiction. Our commitment to act under uncertainty now demands compulsion where systemic risk is proven. And our two-year commitment must evolve from setting red lines to ensuring they are technically enforced — not through paper, but through shared telemetry, unified access, and irreversible integration into national CERTs.

## New measure  
**Unified Threat Telemetry Backbone**  
Establishes a sovereign, EU-operated data backbone with mandatory ingestion of real-time cyber threat telemetry from all national CERTs and essential operators in energy, telecoms, and emergency services, enabling automated cross-border detection and response.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +12, eu_political_capital -3 (due to enforcement friction), public_sentiment +5 if operational trust is demonstrated  
`Applies to:` all member states, with phased onboarding and technical assistance for laggards

## Priority  
**Unified Threat Telemetry Backbone** — because the Emergency Activation of the Cross-Border Resilience Node cannot function without shared, real-time data flow, and because without a technical spine that bypasses national gatekeeping, unity of action remains a legal fiction rather than an operational reality.

## In practice  
We are no longer negotiating frameworks — we are building infrastructure that cannot be opted out of. The Unified Threat Telemetry Backbone will be hosted and operated by eu-LISA, with hardened nodes in Luxembourg, Finland, and Croatia to ensure geographic redundancy. All national CERTs must connect by turn 10, feeding anonymised but actionable telemetry into a shared detection layer powered by EU-controlled models trained on cross-border attack patterns. We are mandating API-level integration with national systems, not just reporting — this is not a dashboard, it is a nervous system. Funding of €8.2bn will come from the remaining InvestAI contingency reserve, now fully repurposed from compute expansion to critical integration.  

Germany continues to resist full access, citing data sovereignty, so we are offering them co-stewardship of the anonymisation layer and a veto on any cross-border alert escalation. Poland remains outside, but we are working through the Visegrád group to establish a parallel onboarding track with EU technical assistance — they can delay, but they cannot block. The backbone will be designed to degrade gracefully: if a state disconnects, the system continues, and their access to EU-wide threat intelligence ceases automatically. This is not punishment — it is consequence. We are also linking participation in the Backbone to eligibility for future crisis response funding under the Solidarity Fund. The message is clear: you are either in the system, or you are on your own when the next wave hits.
