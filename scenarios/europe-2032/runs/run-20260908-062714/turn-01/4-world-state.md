### The First Wave Hits

In the autumn of 2026, a sophisticated AI-generated intrusion was discovered across multiple critical systems: electricity grids in France and Italy, a major container port in Rotterdam, and a regional water utility in the Baltics. The attackers had operated undetected for weeks, their presence revealed only by chance during a routine audit. Days later, a large-scale ransomware attack crippled public health IT systems in two member states, exploiting a compromised software dependency. The tooling bore the hallmarks of automated, adaptive generation—likely model-assisted or fully model-created. Defenders were overwhelmed, and attribution stalled for months.

In response, the European Commission proposed the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators, a new directive under NIS2 aimed at mandating real-time telemetry sharing with ENISA and establishing red-team access protocols through a nascent Cyber Resilience Directorate. However, no implementation has occurred yet. Germany and Poland immediately voiced strong objections on grounds of national sovereignty, while energy sector operators raised concerns about operational disruption. The proposal faces a minimum one-turn delay before any effects can manifest, consistent with implementation timelines for regulatory measures of this scale.

Offensive AI capability continued its steady rise. Moonshot’s open release of Kimi K3 contributed to broader diffusion of advanced cyber tools, accelerating replication efforts among developers outside the US and China. This supported a moderate increase in global openweight capability, though still within the bounds of gradual accumulation from prior levels. The EU’s own compute independence initiatives—InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package—remain in early development, hampered by permitting delays and supply-chain constraints. ASML’s leverage has been invoked, but US restrictions on advanced packaging tools remain a barrier.

Public sentiment declined amid growing anxiety over job losses in IT security and the perceived dependence on foreign AI for national defence. Media narratives emphasized vulnerability, and labour protests emerged near proposed data centre sites, feeding into an emergent regulatory backlash.

Coordination talks began between EU members, Canada, Japan, and South Korea on developing a shared evaluation framework for frontier AI models. These discussions are exploratory and lack binding commitments; no joint mechanism has been established.
