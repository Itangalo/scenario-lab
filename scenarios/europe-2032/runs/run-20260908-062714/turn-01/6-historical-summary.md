In 2026, a wave of AI-driven cyberattacks targeted critical infrastructure across Europe, including energy grids, a major port, and water systems, followed by a ransomware attack on health IT networks. The attacks, enabled by adaptive, likely AI-generated tools, exposed vulnerabilities and overwhelmed defences, delaying attribution. In response, the EU proposed the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators under NIS2, mandating real-time data sharing with ENISA and red-team access via a new Cyber Resilience Directorate, but implementation is stalled due to opposition from Germany and Poland over sovereignty concerns and industry worries about operational impact. Meanwhile, the release of Moonshot’s Kimi K3 accelerated global diffusion of advanced cyber capabilities, boosting openweight AI tools. EU efforts to build compute independence through InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package face delays from permitting and supply-chain issues, compounded by US restrictions on key semiconductor tools. Public anxiety grows over job losses and reliance on foreign AI, sparking protests and regulatory pushback. Informal talks among EU members, Canada, Japan, and South Korea have begun on a shared frontier AI evaluation framework, though no binding agreement exists.
