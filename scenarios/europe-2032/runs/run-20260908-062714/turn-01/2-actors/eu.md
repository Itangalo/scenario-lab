## New measure
Cyber Shield for Critical Operators  
A binding directive requiring real-time threat telemetry and red-teaming access for all operators of essential services connected to cross-border infrastructure.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases `resilience` by 10–15 points through faster detection and coordinated response; reduces blast radius of model-generated attacks.  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators (energy, water, ports), with enforcement through updated NIS2 implementation.

## Priority
Cyber Shield for Critical Operators — because the cyber_test_shot and cyber_major_incident prove that offensive AI capability is now distributed, adaptive, and invisible to current defences, and sovereignty means nothing if the lights go out.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that the frontier is no longer just abroad — it is running inside systems we cannot see, using tools we did not anticipate, and the first shots have already landed. The cyber_test_shot revealed that detection failed everywhere; the cyber_major_incident confirmed that when it matters, we are not ready. This turn, we invoke Article 10b of the NIS2 Directive to fast-track a binding regulation that mandates telemetry sharing with ENISA and grants red-team access to a newly empowered Cyber Resilience Directorate. The legal base is clear, but the resistance is real: energy ministries fear operational exposure, port authorities cite commercial secrecy, and Germany and Poland have already signalled reluctance to cede autonomy. We counter with funding from the Digital Europe Programme and offer joint ownership of the detection framework — a European SOC that learns from every intrusion, not just responds to it.  

At the same time, we press forward with the InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech sovereignty package, knowing that compute independence is the only long-term hedge against exclusion like Washington’s unilateral control. The ASML leverage is now our strongest card: we tie export coordination to joint investment in EU-based training infrastructure, offering chipmakers and cloud providers a stable, regulated market in exchange for long-term anchoring. But we do not wait for those to land. While the US retreats into fortress control and open weights spread like Kimi K3, we build the only thing that can scale with the threat: a resilient, shared nervous system for Europe’s critical functions. That is where we start.
