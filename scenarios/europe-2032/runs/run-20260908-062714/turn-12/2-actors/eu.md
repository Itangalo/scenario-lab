## In practice
The Unified Threat Telemetry Backbone is now fully operational, processing real-time telemetry from over 80% of essential operators in connected member states. eu-LISA runs the backbone from hardened nodes in Tallinn and Luxembourg, with automated detection halting lateral movement in recent incidents within hours. But the fault lines remain: Poland and parts of Germany still block API sharing, citing subsidiarity and data sovereignty, leaving municipal services exposed. We are finalising the regulation under Article 122 TFEU to tie Solidarity Fund disbursements to verified telemetry feed status — a legal innovation that turns crisis response into leverage. The proposal is before ECOFIN, backed by a coalition of nine states. Behind closed doors, we are offering Poland a co-lead role in the Visegrád integration track and Germany a permanent seat on the anonymisation oversight board — not concessions, but structured inclusion into a non-negotiable architecture. If the Court upholds the measure, we move to enforcement; if not, we pivot to building redundant resilience for non-participants, funded from existing reserves, so the Union survives even if not whole.
