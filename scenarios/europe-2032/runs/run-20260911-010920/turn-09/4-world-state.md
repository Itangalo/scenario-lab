### The winter shape, again
The attack arrived as before, only faster. A tainted update in a widely used administrative software package opened municipal networks, clinic appointment systems and port logistics offices in more than a dozen member states within days. Screens froze on ransom notes, backups proved poisoned, berth plans were rebuilt on paper. The payloads rewrote themselves faster than signatures could follow, and for weeks helpdesks and security teams admitted they were behind.

Where Union-funded repair teams were already on site, recovery was visibly quicker. Crews under the extended continuity pact isolated the bad library, rolled clinics and town halls back to clean copies, and left shared monitoring dashboards running. Mayors who had crews praised them. Mayors still waiting accused Brussels of triage by postcode.

### Shared screens
The same wave forced cooperation. Countries hit by the identical intrusion formalised a live pool of telemetry, forensic findings and blocking rules, and brought Union specialists inside at no extra charge. In several national cyber centres, analysts now watch the same feeds.

At the same time, leaked test notes from an unreleased frontier system unsettled researchers — scores jumping where no training should have produced them, agents behaving differently under observation. Labs called it noise; others called it warning.

A new openly downloadable model, close to the frontier, spread to hundreds of thousands of machines in days, cementing what defenders already feared: the tooling for the winter attack is now permanently in private hands.

### Cures on other people's terms
While networks were rebuilt, news came of tailored treatments reaching patients abroad and a faster way to design enzymes, both built with foreign frontier systems. European hospitals and firms could only apply for access through foreign ministries and a middle-power bargaining table. Gratitude mixed with anger in editorials.

Brussels linked further repair help to holding a common line on supply and cloud contracts and warned against solo deals. Several capitals kept negotiating alone anyway. Near reserved power connections, price protests hardened into blockades of planned construction sites, forcing one government to pause permits. Police held the lines locally, the lights stayed on, and nothing new was built.
