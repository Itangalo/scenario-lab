### The Storm Hits

A wave of AI-generated ransomware cripples hospital networks and water systems across southern and central Europe. The attack exploits forgotten authentication chains, propagating through legacy infrastructure with speed and coordination no human team could match. Though sovereign compute remains intact, the breach exposes a fatal asymmetry: control over AI means little if the services it enables are unprotected. Public outrage mounts as emergency crews revert to paper logs and manual valves.

### EU Responds — But at a Cost

The Commission activates emergency powers, fast-tracking the **Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Directive** — a large category 6 measure targeting cyber resilience in essential services. €30bn is redirected from NextGenerationEU, and binding standards are set: 24-hour patching, quarterly AI-driven threat simulations, and air-gapped overrides. Member states comply under pressure, but coordination is uneven — Poland and Hungary resist the timeline, citing fiscal strain.

Simultaneously, the **Sovereign Red-Team Access Programme** advances despite the crisis. Legal immunity is secured for red-team nodes, with DLR in Germany hosting the first, and France and Italy granted leadership roles to ease tensions. Yet no safeguards from this work have landed on deployed systems.

### Political Capital Bleeds

Three large measures now strain the system: Gigafactories (−3), Red-Team Access (−3), and the new Hardening Directive (−3), plus the named priority (−1). With no completed measure to offset the load, political capital erodes further.

Public sentiment drops sharply. The cyberattack is widely seen as a failure of preparedness, and the redirected funds spark accusations of improvisation. Labour unrest simmers as IT contractors report sudden cancellations of modernisation projects.

### The Frontier Moves On

Global AI capability inches to 61.5, driven by closed-loop research in materials science. Open-weight models reach 50.0, as fragments of agent frameworks leak into open forums — not full systems, but enough to enable new misuse tools.

Safety declines as capability advances without corresponding safeguards. Resilience gains slightly from the *start* of hardening efforts, but the bulk of protection remains years away.
