## New measure
**Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Directive**  
Mandate and fund the rapid integration of AI-resistant safeguards into essential public services, including health, energy, water, and transport, with binding cyber resilience standards and automated failover protocols.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 10  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to resilience by turn 10 through hardened core services, reducing vulnerability to AI-generated cyberattacks; complements sovereign compute by ensuring continuity even if systems are probed or degraded  
`Applies to:` Member states’ critical infrastructure operators, national cybersecurity agencies, and EU-level emergency response coordination bodies

## Priority
**Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Directive** — because the cyber_major_incident this turn has exposed that sovereignty of compute means little if the services depending on it — or targeted alongside it — collapse under coordinated AI-augmented attack, and because political capital is too low to sustain both this and the red-team programme without a visible anchor in public protection.

## In practice
We respond to the cyber_major_incident by activating the Crisis Coordination Protocol under the Digital Services Act’s emergency provisions, convening the Cyber Resilience Board with ENISA, national CSIRTs, and operators from the affected sectors. Initial analysis confirms the attack exploited AI-generated exploits targeting legacy authentication chains in municipal water systems and hospital networks — not a breach of sovereign compute, but a demonstration that our most vulnerable nodes are now vectors against the whole. We draft the Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Directive under Article 114 TFEU, framing it as a single-market measure to eliminate fragmentation in cyber resilience. The Commission proposes binding standards: automated patch deployment within 24 hours of detection, AI-generated threat simulation every quarter, and mandatory air-gapped manual override in all essential control systems. €30bn is redirected from the unused portion of the NextGenerationEU digital tranche, with disbursement tied to national implementation plans due within 90 days.

Simultaneously, we defend the Sovereign Red-Team Access Programme from early cancellation, arguing that control over our own systems is not secondary to external threats but foundational to both sovereignty and safety. The European Evaluation Shield begins onboarding the first red-team node at the German Aerospace Center (DLR), with legal immunity secured through a Council Implementing Decision under the European Defence Technological and Industrial Base regulation, repurposed for dual-use AI assurance. We offer France and Italy additional hosting rights and research leadership roles to offset earlier friction over compute siting, aiming to convert resistance into ownership. The classified findings on memory persistence remain under seal, but inform the design of the first simulation suite: probing whether agent-driven infrastructure controllers can retain objectives across reset cycles. This work continues — not because the world has calmed, but because it hasn’t.
