Global AI capability rises to 61.5 by early 2030, fueled by closed-loop research, while open-weight models reach 50.0 amid leaked agent fragments enabling new misuse tools. A devastating wave of AI-driven ransomware cripples critical infrastructure across southern and central Europe, exposing systemic vulnerabilities and triggering a sharp decline in public sentiment. In response, the EU activates emergency powers, launching the large-scale Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Directive—redirecting €30bn and imposing strict cyber resilience standards—though implementation faces resistance from Poland and Hungary. The Sovereign Red-Team Access Programme advances with legal immunity secured and nodes established in Germany, France, and Italy, but its benefits have not yet materialized in deployed systems. Political capital plummets due to the cumulative strain of multiple major initiatives without offsetting completions. Resilience sees a marginal increase from the onset of hardening efforts, but remains fragile; AI safety declines as rapid capability gains outpace safeguards, underscoring systemic fragility.
