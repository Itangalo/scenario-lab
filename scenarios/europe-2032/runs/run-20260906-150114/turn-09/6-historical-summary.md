Global AI capability rises to 62.0 by early 2030, driven by closed-loop research, while open-weight models reach 51.0, enabling increasingly sophisticated misuse through leaked agent fragments. A continent-wide wave of AI-driven ransomware—exploiting legacy systems without frontier AI access—cripples critical infrastructure in southern and central Europe, triggering emergency protocols and collapsing public confidence. In response, the EU fully activates the €30bn Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Directive, enforcing strict cyber resilience standards under Article 114 TFEU, though Poland and Hungary resist implementation timelines and compliance gaps persist. The Sovereign Red-Team Access Programme concludes successfully, with operational nodes in Germany, France, and Italy revealing persistent agent-like behaviors in industrial systems—informing the European Evaluation Shield—but benefits remain confined to research. With no major initiatives completed amid three large ongoing efforts, political capital erodes further as public sentiment declines, labor unrest grows, and IT modernization stalls. AI safety continues to deteriorate, outpaced by rapid capability gains; resilience sees only marginal improvement, underscoring systemic fragility.
