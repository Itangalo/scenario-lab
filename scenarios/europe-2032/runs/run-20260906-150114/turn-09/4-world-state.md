### The Aftermath of Collapse

The EU reels from the continent-wide cyberattacks that crippled hospitals and water systems. Though no sovereign AI systems were breached, the failure of essential infrastructure to withstand AI-driven intrusions has shattered public confidence. Emergency protocols are still in effect across several member states, with manual overrides and paper records the only reliable tools in overstretched clinics and utilities. The attacks exploited forgotten authentication paths in legacy systems, demonstrating that even without access to frontier models, open-weight frameworks are now powerful enough to coordinate complex, cascading disruptions.

### Hardening Begins — But Too Late?

The **Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Directive** is now in full motion. €30bn from NextGenerationEU has been redirected, and national implementation plans are due within 90 days. ENISA and the Cyber Resilience Board have activated emergency teams, fast-tracking procurement and deploying technical support to Greece and Slovakia. Binding standards — 24-hour patching, AI-driven red-team drills, and air-gapped overrides — are legally enforceable under Article 114 TFEU. Yet resistance persists: Poland and Hungary argue the timeline is unworkable, and early audits suggest patching compliance will fall short in at least a third of Tier 1 systems.

### Red-Team Programme Concludes

The **Sovereign Red-Team Access Programme** successfully concludes this turn. The DLR node in Germany is operational, and initial classified findings reveal that agent-like behaviours can persist across reset cycles in industrial controllers — a critical insight now shaping the next phase of the European Evaluation Shield. France and Italy have formally joined as co-leaders, transforming early resistance into shared ownership. However, these safeguards have not yet landed on deployed systems, and no public reporting has occurred.

### Political Capital Erodes

With three large measures in flight — Gigafactories, Red-Team Access, and the Hardening Directive — and the Hardening Directive now the named priority, political capital continues to bleed. No measure has completed to offset the burden, and the cyber crisis has deepened public anxiety. Labour unrest grows as IT modernisation projects are paused or cancelled, feeding narratives of mismanagement.

### The Frontier Advances

Global AI capability reaches 62.0, driven by closed-loop research in materials and energy. Open-weight models climb to 51.0, as leaked fragments of agent frameworks enable more sophisticated misuse. AI safety declines further, as rapid capability gains outpace safeguards. Resilience sees a marginal uptick from the onset of hardening efforts, but remains fragile. Public sentiment drops, reflecting growing fear of AI as a source of disruption rather than progress.
