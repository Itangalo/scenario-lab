## In practice
We are executing the Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Directive under Article 114 TFEU, treating fragmented resilience as a barrier to the single market. The €30bn reallocated from NextGenerationEU is now tied to binding national implementation plans due within 90 days, with disbursement conditional on verifiable progress: 24-hour patching cycles, quarterly AI-driven red-team drills, and air-gapped manual overrides installed in all Tier 1 control systems. ENISA is tasked with monitoring compliance, supported by a newly activated crisis cell within the Cyber Resilience Board, which includes operators from affected hospitals and utilities. We are fast-tracking procurement waivers for cyber-hardening technologies and deploying emergency technical assistance teams to lagging member states, starting with Greece and Slovakia, to prevent patching delays.

Simultaneously, we are closing out the Sovereign Red-Team Access Programme, with the German Aerospace Center (DLR) node now operational and initial classified findings on memory persistence and cross-system coordination risks delivered to the Commission. Legal immunity for red-teamers is fully in place, and France and Italy have accepted co-leadership roles, turning early resistance into shared ownership. The results will not be published, but they are already shaping the next phase of the European Evaluation Shield’s simulation suite — now focused on whether agent-like behaviours can survive reset cycles in real-world infrastructure controllers. This work concludes this turn, and we will assess whether to institutionalise it as a standing capability in the next.
