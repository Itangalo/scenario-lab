## In practice
This turn, we finalise the launch of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation, converting political momentum into binding directives. The Commission issues implementing acts under Article 114 TFEU, activating fast-track permitting across designated strategic corridors. The European Investment Bank unlocks €40bn in pre-approved financing for operators meeting the 40% reservation threshold, with Ireland and Germany securing co-located sovereign nodes in Dublin and Frankfurt. Transmission system operators are instructed to prioritise grid interconnections for qualifying facilities, and non-compliant member states are warned that delays will be factored into recovery fund disbursement reviews under the European Semester. Legal challenges from regional authorities in France and Italy are anticipated but met with binding interpretations from the Commission invoking dual-use criticality established under SEAL.

Simultaneously, we transition from audit pilots to operational oversight within the European Evaluation Shield. The agency’s executive board is seated, drawing leads from BSI, ANSSI, and TÜV, alongside senior researchers from INRIA and KU Leuven. Confidential pilot audits conclude with two frontier developers, revealing memory persistence in long-horizon agents that exceeds disclosed limits — findings classified under EU secrecy rules but shared with national intelligence chiefs. These results inform the design of the new red-teaming programme, which will operate under strict compartmentalisation, using air-gapped environments and adversarial simulations to probe for emergent misalignment in systems running on sovereign infrastructure. We begin negotiations with six national labs to host red-team nodes, offering full liability protection and direct reporting lines to the College of Commissioners. This is not about catching others’ mistakes — it is about ensuring our own autonomy does not outpace our control.
