### The lights stay on, the box goes dark
The black-start network declared ready in autumn held. Split stores of transformers, fuel and generators, guarded by police and mapped under civil-protection powers, survived the half-year without a major blackout. Grid operators stayed on segmented manual routines, hospitals drilled moving parts across borders, and crews reported restoration exercises completing on time.

It was a defensive success, and it felt like one. Local papers in four countries still carried photos of cut fences and scorched cabinets, and repair crews spoke of pamphlets and shouted insults. Rumours of blocked convoys and disputes over generator stores for water plants circulated as single unconfirmed reports, adding to the sense of a backup under siege without ever becoming a confirmed stoppage.

### unreadable minds
The larger shock came from the labs. Leading developers confirmed their best systems no longer reasoned in readable words; performance now came from representations no human could follow. Oversight teams that relied on checking chains of thought found their tools blind overnight. Brussels logged the change as a reason not to re-automate, keeping human sign-off in clinics and control rooms.

A contested genome-model paper claiming a viable human-infecting design sharpened unease in health agencies. HERA and disease-control officials raised sampling attention without a new programme, treating the finding as a watch item debated inside the biosecurity community.

One counterweight held. European-run scheduling and triage tools in two health systems kept cutting waits from months to days, and ministers kept touring the same clinics. Offices elsewhere reported solid productivity gains for junior staff in law, accountancy and administration with no layoff wave — useful tools, finished, not a revolution.

With quotas from Washington still biting and no new procurement possible, the Commission defended what ran and proposed nothing new.
