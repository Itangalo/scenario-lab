### The last mile gets hands
Brussels stopped shipping boxes and started shipping people. Under a reprogrammed Digital Europe envelope, joint teams of civil-protection secondees, utility engineers and university vendor staff fanned out on six-month municipal contracts to install the swarm-detection and rapid-patching tooling where it had sat unopened.

Where they arrived, it worked. Two dozen water utilities and a cluster of small hospitals in the east and south restored ticketing, records and dosing systems in days, and certification auditors logged the first full passes among previously uncertified operators. Local mayors who had cursed Brussels in winter posed for photos with the repair vans.

Where they had not yet arrived, the winter wave lingered. A tainted component kept re-locking benefits portals, and emergency staffing orders after coordinated sick-outs by exhausted care staff forced wards to run on paper. Redress courts kept ordering back-pay for automated welfare denials while two regions still withheld logs, feeding a narrative that repair was real but selective.

### Frontier in every basement
Mid-autumn a new openly released model, judged months behind the closed frontier, was downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in a week. Police labs found its scaffolding in phishing and intrusion kits within days. Vendors selling cheap cancelled compute suddenly found buyers not in ministries but in basements.

The cure story curdled further. Tailored therapies expanded remissions, but procurement through Japanese and Canadian channels stalled on price tiers, and whispers grew of hospital federations quietly pooling inference from idle venture servers to run clinical tools outside any tier. Relief at restored services mixed with dread that the next attack class was already unrecallable.
