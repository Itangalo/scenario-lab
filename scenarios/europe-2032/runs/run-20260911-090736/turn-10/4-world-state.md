### Patch Tuesday, every day
The breakthrough came from outside Brussels. Research groups on both sides of the Atlantic showed automated patching that kept pace with newly found flaws, and detectors that flagged coordinated agent behaviour rather than signatures. ENISA pushed both out through the existing certificate scheme and the hospital and municipal drills.

Where drills already worked, the effect was immediate. Two attempted cascades across utilities and clinics in spring were caught and isolated in hours. Where municipal systems still could not tell rogue orders from normal ones, the tools helped less. Engineers said the balance had shifted back for the first time in years; operators said it still depended on having done the homework.

### A recipe argument
A genome modelling paper claimed a non-expert with assistance could reach a viable human-infecting design. Methodologists attacked the result, others attacked its publication, and health agencies quietly checked stocks and detection protocols. The Commission routed samples and evaluation requests through the middle-power compact rather than opening a new programme.

### Graduate desks
The guarantee dominated domestic politics. Labour ministers drew EU co-financing for wage bridging, six-month retraining vouchers and first-job credits in law, accountancy, software and customer operations, with reporting levies on large deployers of assistants. Uptake was fast in two capitals, slow where administrations had to build payment channels from scratch. Employer groups called the levy a tax on productivity; unions called the envelope capped and temporary.

The compact concluded its first phase. Pooled hosting quotas for essential workloads were booked, export-licence criteria aligned with Tokyo, Seoul, Ottawa and Gulf hosts, and shared black-box test results began to circulate. The holdout capital kept its separate supply arrangement, neither refusing the conditioned hosting offer nor unwinding its side deal. American licences stayed queued and conditioned.

Graduate fairs remained thin, but the first cheques and training places eased the worst anger.
