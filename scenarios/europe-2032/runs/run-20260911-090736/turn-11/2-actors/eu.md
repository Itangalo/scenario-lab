## In practice
We act through the AI Act's market-surveillance and fundamental-rights track, not a new law. The Commission opens coordinated enforcement with national SAIs and the EU AI Office: suspend the flagged system, mandate human re-decision of affected cases with back-pay/expungement, publish the unread logs, and require logged-oversight sampling — minimum time-per-decision, four-eyes on denials, log-pattern review — for all Annex III welfare, policing and justice deployments, funded from Digital Europe technical assistance. We choose the first reading deliberately: the system *was* high-risk and obligations were breached, so the Act stands and its enforcers must be seen to bite.

On the other tracks we spend no new instrument. ENISA's automated patching and coordinated-agent detectors continue through Shield certificates and hospital/municipal drills; the Entry-Level Guarantee cheques and vouchers keep flowing via EPSCO/ESF+; pooled hosting and black-box sharing continue through the middle-power compact while the holdout's conditioned hosting offer stays on the table. Labour ministers get cover to say redress and retraining are one package: no automated queue without a human who actually reads it.
