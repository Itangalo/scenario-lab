### The queue at forty seconds
Autumn brought the ombudsman report everyone in Brussels had dreaded. A benefits-fraud risk scorer used in several regions had flagged thousands for repayment and referral. On paper it was a textbook high-risk deployment with conformity papers, human oversight and logs. In practice caseworkers cleared a case every forty seconds, denial stamps without reading, and the logs that recorded the skewed pattern sat unopened for two years until journalists asked for them.

Courts ordered re-decisions and back-pay in the first test cases. Talk shows ran the timer on screen. Mayors who had defended automation now demanded someone be punished.

### Redress first
The Commission chose to save the law by sacrificing its enforcers. The flagged system was suspended, affected files sent back for human re-decision with compensation and expungement, and the unread logs published. Market-surveillance authorities and the AI Office opened joint audits of welfare, policing and justice systems, with sampling of oversight: minimum handling times, second eyes on denials, mandatory pattern review of logs, paid from technical assistance.

Civil-rights groups called it the first time enforcement bit. Staff councils called it unfunded checking work. Two regions stalled on handing over logs, and a newspaper found a police triage tool still running on the same forty-second rhythm in November.

### Everything else kept running
Patching and detector rollouts continued through certificates and drills, stopping another utility cascade in October but again missing a municipal water supplier that had skipped the exercise. Guarantee cheques and retraining vouchers kept flowing, now politically paired with redress as proof that automation would have a human who reads. Pooled hosting with Japan, Korea, Canada and Gulf partners held, while the holdout capital kept both its separate supply deal and the conditioned offer, conceding nothing.

By December anger had narrowed from AI itself to its supervisors, which was exactly what the redress strategy had aimed for — but trust had not recovered.
