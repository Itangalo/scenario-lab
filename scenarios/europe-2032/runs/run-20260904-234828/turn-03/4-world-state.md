### A Fragile Rebuild

The EU doubled down on sovereign compute, declaring the InvestAI Gigafactories a strategic imperative and fast-tracking grid access and funding. ENTSO-E began rerouting 15GW of low-carbon power, and the European Investment Bank released initial funds to three sites. Yet construction remains stalled in Brittany and Andalusia, where regional courts invoked environmental safeguards, citing unresolved ecological impact concerns. In Norrland, local opposition delayed land transfers. No new compute came online.

The Sovereign Model Initiative (SOMI) gained structure, with recruitment launched and a Horizon Europe framework established. However, no model training began, and EAAD’s planned red-teaming of SOMI remains theoretical—no staff, no mandate, no access. The hyperscaler levy’s deferral weakened long-term funding, and trust in centralised AI governance eroded further.

Public sentiment dipped as media highlighted delays and intergovernmental clashes. A rumoured disinformation surge in Eastern Europe, involving AI-generated emergency alerts, amplified anxiety, though no major incident was confirmed. Cyber defences held, but no new resilience measures were completed.

AI capability advanced globally, driven by private labs in the US and Asia, pushing the frontier to 54.5. Open-weight models improved slightly, reaching 42.0, as older frontier releases filtered into the open. However, no major open release occurred, and EU restrictions limited downstream use.

ai_safety declined slightly as capability growth outpaced regulatory integration. The interpretability breakthrough remained confined to private labs. Resilience weakened due to inaction, and sovereignty fell to 18.0—penalised by continued dependency, no tangible capacity gain, and the ongoing cost of prioritisation. Political capital dropped sharply under the weight of five large measures, the priority charge, and fading public confidence.

### Legal Clouds Gather

A constitutional challenge to InvestAI’s fast-tracking gained momentum, with multiple member states and NGOs arguing it undermines subsidiarity and environmental law. The European Court of Justice agreed to fast-track review, casting doubt on the project’s timeline.
