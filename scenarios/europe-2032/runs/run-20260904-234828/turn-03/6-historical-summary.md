AI capability stagnated at 53.5 as the EU faced a strategic shock from sudden denial of access to a leading frontier model, disrupting critical sectors and exposing deep dependency. In response, the InvestAI Gigafactories were declared a strategic priority under Article 122 TFEU, with fast-tracked approvals and €50bn in principle from the European Investment Bank. ENTSO-E rerouted 15GW of low-carbon power, and initial funds were released to three sites, but construction remained stalled in Brittany, Andalusia, and Norrland due to legal challenges and local opposition, with no new compute online. The Sovereign Model Initiative (SOMI) advanced structurally with recruitment and a Horizon Europe framework, but no model training began. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) remained non-operational, lacking staff, mandate, and funding, as the hyperscaler levy was deferred to the Recovery and Resilience Facility. Global AI capability rose to 54.5, with open-weight models reaching 42.0, but EU restrictions limited access and use. An interpretability breakthrough remained outside regulatory integration, and ai_safety declined slightly. Public sentiment weakened amid media scrutiny and intergovernmental tensions, while a suspected disinformation surge in Eastern Europe heightened anxiety. No new cyber resilience measures were implemented, and legal challenges to InvestAI gained traction, with the European Court of Justice fast-tracking a constitutional review that threatened the initiative’s legitimacy. Sovereignty fell to 18.0 and political capital eroded sharply, reflecting ongoing dependency, unmet capacity goals, and mounting costs of prioritization.
