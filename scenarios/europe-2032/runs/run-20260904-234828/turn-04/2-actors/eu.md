- modify `sovereignty_first` (position): Until we control the compute and models essential to our critical functions, we cannot credibly assure safety, resilience, or public trust — but we must now demonstrate that control is achievable, not just declared.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor sovereignty and risk resilience, but shift emphasis from infrastructure rollout alone to proving operational sovereignty through a live, sovereignly trained and evaluated model by end-2029.  
- Trigger: the emergent_investai_legal_challenge and campaign_backlash  
- Grounds: The legal challenge to InvestAI under Article 122 TFEU has exposed the fragility of top-down sovereignty claims, while rising public resistance to AI infrastructure shows that legitimacy is eroding faster than capacity is building. Continuing to treat compute deployment as the sole proxy for sovereignty risks entrenching a costly, contested, and potentially unconstitutional path. At the same time, SOMI remains untested and EAAD inactive — we have no proof of sovereign capability in action. The commitment must now pivot from hardware to demonstrated control: a sovereign model, trained on sovereign compute, evaluated by a sovereign agency, and used in sovereign functions. This is the only way to reconcile sovereignty with legitimacy before political capital collapses.

## Priority  
**Priority:** Establish the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD)  
Because without a functioning, credible agency to evaluate and constrain even our own systems, sovereignty is just procurement — and safety is performance. The legal and public backlash demands proof that EU control means oversight, not just ownership. This turn, EAAD must begin real work on SOMI, or we lose both legitimacy and leverage.

## In practice  
We are redirecting the final phase of EAAD’s setup toward immediate operationalisation, not bureaucratic completion. The agency remains underfunded and understaffed, but we are now instructing the Commission to assign interim authority under Article 16 of the AI Act, allowing EAAD’s core team to begin red-teaming SOMI’s early training checkpoints by June. This is not full legal empowerment — that still awaits Council agreement — but it is a live test: we are tasking a small cadre of seconded evaluators from national cybersecurity agencies and academic interpretability labs to conduct adversarial probing of SOMI’s agent behaviours, with findings reported directly to the College of Commissioners. We are pairing this with fast-tracked recruitment from the EU’s AI talent pool, offering retention bonuses and secure clearance pathways to fill key roles in model monitoring and weight security.

At the same time, we are acknowledging the legal reality of the InvestAI challenge: we are not halting construction, but we are pausing further invocation of Article 122 TFEU pending an emergency Council meeting in April, where we will propose a revised governance framework that embeds regional co-decision rights and environmental review into the fast-track process. This is a concession to subsidiarity, but not to delay — we are offering binding reinvestment in local green infrastructure and heat-sharing networks in exchange for lifting regional blockages in Brittany and Andalusia. In Norrland, we are opening direct talks with municipal leaders and indigenous representatives, backed by a €2bn regional transition fund drawn from the Digital Europe Programme. The goal is no longer just to build faster, but to build in a way that survives judicial and democratic scrutiny — because without that, no compute, however sovereign, will stand.
