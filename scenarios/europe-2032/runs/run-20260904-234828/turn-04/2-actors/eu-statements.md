# Statements: The European Union (turn 4)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor sovereignty and risk resilience, but shift emphasis from infrastructure rollout alone to proving operational sovereignty through a live, sovereignly trained and evaluated model by end-2029.
- `sovereignty_first` (position): Until we control the compute and models essential to our critical functions, we cannot credibly assure safety, resilience, or public trust — but we must now demonstrate that control is achievable, not just declared.

  - Trigger: the emergent_investai_legal_challenge and campaign_backlash
  - Grounds: The legal challenge to InvestAI under Article 122 TFEU has exposed the fragility of top-down sovereignty claims, while rising public resistance to AI infrastructure shows that legitimacy is eroding faster than capacity is building. Continuing to treat compute deployment as the sole proxy for sovereignty risks entrenching a costly, contested, and potentially unconstitutional path. At the same time, SOMI remains untested and EAAD inactive — we have no proof of sovereign capability in action. The commitment must now pivot from hardware to demonstrated control: a sovereign model, trained on sovereign compute, evaluated by a sovereign agency, and used in sovereign functions. This is the only way to reconcile sovereignty with legitimacy before political capital collapses.
  - Evidence quoted: emergent_investai_legal_challenge (emergent event): A coalition of EU member states, regional governments, and environmental NGOs files a joint legal challenge at the European Court of Justice to block the InvestAI Gigafactories' fast-tracked permitting under Article 122 TFEU, arguing it violates environmental impact assessment directives and subsidiarity principles. The case gains fast-track status due to its constitutional implications.
  - Reason: The legal challenge directly undermines the feasibility and legitimacy of the infrastructure-focused sovereignty approach, forcing a shift to demonstrated operational control as a more defensible basis for sovereignty.
