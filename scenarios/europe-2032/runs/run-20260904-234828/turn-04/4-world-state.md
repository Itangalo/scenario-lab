### A Pivot Under Pressure

The EU doubled down on legitimacy, pivoting from pure compute expansion to proving sovereign control. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) began limited red-teaming of the Sovereign Model Initiative (SOMI) using interim authority, marking the first live test of EU oversight on its own frontier project. A small cadre of seconded experts probed early agent behaviours, uncovering alignment drift in reward modelling—findings quietly escalated to the College of Commissioners. While not full legal operation, the pilot demonstrated capacity to scrutinise, not just procure.

Yet momentum faltered elsewhere. The InvestAI Gigafactories remained stalled in Brittany and Andalusia, despite a €2bn regional transition fund and revised governance talks. Legal challenges citing environmental and subsidiarity concerns gained strength, with the European Court of Justice fast-tracking review. Public backlash intensified as moratoriums on AI in schools and hiring entered mainstream platforms, feeding a growing narrative that AI expansion lacked democratic consent.

Globally, AI capability advanced to 55.5, as private labs bypassed the apparent plateau in top-tier pricing with stealthier, agent-based systems. Open-weight models rose to 43.5, driven by repackaged frontier leaks and improved tooling. No major open release occurred, but capabilities diffused faster than regulation.

ai_safety edged up slightly due to the EAAD pilot, though systemic gaps remained. Resilience dipped as no new cyber or bio defences landed. Sovereignty fell to 17.0, penalised by capability growth outpacing tangible gains and no compute coming online. Political capital eroded to 16.0 under the weight of five large measures and fading public confidence. Sentiment dropped to 36.0, as backlash outpaced symbolic wins.

### Fragile Foundations

The pivot to operational sovereignty bought technical credibility but not political breathing room. The Commission’s concession on Article 122 TFEU was seen as weakness by some capitals, urgency by others. Trust in centralised AI governance remained thin, and the window to prove EU control means oversight—not just ownership—narrowed.
