In response, the InvestAI Gigafactories were declared a strategic priority under Article 122 TFEU, with fast-tracked approvals and €50bn in principle from the European Investment Bank, though construction delays persisted due to legal and local opposition. No new compute or model advancements materialized, leaving both ai_capability and openweight_capability unchanged. The Sovereign Model Initiative (SOMI) emerged as a key policy response, with feasibility studies launched but no funding or recruitment yet secured. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) advanced modestly in planning for future red-team evaluations, but remained non-operational due to staffing and governance constraints, while funding for its enabling levy was redirected to the Recovery and Resilience Facility. A breakthrough in interpretability improved ai_safety slightly, though not yet integrated into EU regulation. Public sentiment declined amid perceptions of failed foresight, and political capital eroded due to the crisis and centralization concerns. Resilience weakened as no new cyber protections took effect. Sovereignty fell to 19.0, penalized by lost access, ongoing priority costs, and lack of tangible sovereign capacity gains, underscoring a widening gap between strategic ambition and operational reality.
