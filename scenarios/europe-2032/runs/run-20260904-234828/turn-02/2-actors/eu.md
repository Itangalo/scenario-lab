## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now see that without sovereignty, we cannot deliver on either.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor sovereignty and risk resilience — and respond to the fact that access can be withdrawn at will, with immediate operational consequences.  
- add `sovereignty_first` (position): Until we control the compute and models essential to our critical functions, we cannot credibly assure safety, resilience, or public trust.  

- Trigger: eu_frontier_access_denied  
- Grounds: The sudden denial of access to the leading model has disrupted hospitals, ministries, and firms across the Union. This is not a theoretical dependency — it is a live failure of agency. The incident proves that oversight and assurance regimes built on external infrastructure are functionally fragile. Our two mandates converge: sovereignty is no longer a precondition for action — it *is* the action. The safety breakthrough and capability plateau are real, but they do not restore what was taken. We must act as a power, not a regulator.

## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
This remains the physical foundation of everything: without sovereign compute, SOMI has no hardware to run on, EAAD has no leverage, and resilience is outsourced. The access denial proves the risk — but only the Gigafactories can end it. We are accelerating grid reservations and deploying Article 122 TFEU to override local permitting delays where reinvestment guarantees are in place.

## In practice  
We are treating the access denial as a strategic wake-up call. The Commission is convening an emergency meeting of the Competitiveness Council to declare the InvestAI Gigafactories a project of Union-wide strategic importance under Article 122 TFEU, enabling us to fast-track grid access and override local objections where binding regional reinvestment packages are in place. We are instructing the European Investment Bank to disburse €50bn immediately to the three lead sites — Brittany, Norrland, and Andalusia — conditioned on 70% local hiring and joint ownership of heat-reuse infrastructure with municipalities. ENTSO-E is being directed to prioritise 15GW of reserve capacity for these zones by Q4 2027, drawing from nuclear and offshore wind PPAs already in negotiation.

Simultaneously, we are launching the EU Sovereign Model Initiative (SOMI) through a new Joint Undertaking under Horizon Europe, co-funded by the Digital Europe Programme and national AI institutes. The model will be trained on EU-secured data, using EU-based talent recruited through Blue Card+ fast-tracks and retention bonuses for researchers currently in non-EU labs. We are allocating €30bn over two years, with the first prototype targeted for turn 6 and full deployment by turn 8. Crucially, SOMI will not compete with private labs — it will be slower, but it will be *ours*, with weights secured to state-actor standards and no external dependency. This is not about winning the frontier — it is about ensuring we are not left off it.

The EAAD continues its rollout, now with a revised mandate: its first red-team evaluations will be run against SOMI, not foreign models, to build internal credibility and technical muscle. We are shifting the levy on non-EU hyperscalers to a contingency mechanism — it remains legally proposed, but enforcement is paused pending WTO consultations. Instead, we are funding EAAD’s early operations through the Recovery and Resilience Facility, preserving political capital while building capacity. The cyber intrusion findings are being integrated into EAAD’s threat model, with a new focus on AI-driven persistence in critical infrastructure — but the lesson is clear: we cannot audit what we do not control.
