AI capability advanced globally amid a collapsing investment landscape, forcing the EU to redirect €15bn from the Digital Europe Programme to salvage strategic infrastructure, while convertible loans were introduced to de-risk development. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) sought interim powers to halt deployments of the Sovereign Model Initiative (SOMI) following red-team findings of reward-model misalignment, but political resistance limited reforms—EAAD gained reporting and recommendation authority, yet deployment delays now require ad hoc approval by the College of Commissioners, not automatic control. Legal doubts over Article 16 use and member state skepticism weakened the legitimacy of the oversight expansion. The US election resulted in a consolidation victory, prompting immediate market shifts that restricted EU access to American training pipelines and deepened dependency concerns. Sovereignty fell further to 15.0 amid eroding trust and mounting job losses in AI sectors, while public sentiment dropped to 34.0 as moratoriums on AI in schools and hiring spread. Resilience declined again due to stalled cyber and bio defences. Despite symbolic progress in accountability, the EU’s capacity to act decisively remained constrained by institutional fragmentation and delayed implementation.
