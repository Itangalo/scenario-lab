### A Pivot Tested by Collapse

The EU moved to transform oversight into authority, proposing interim legal powers for the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) to delay deployment of the Sovereign Model Initiative (SOMI) based on red-team findings. The move was framed as a democratic safeguard, but it collided with broader instability. The global AI investment collapse erased private commitments underpinning the Tech Sovereignty Package, forcing emergency redirection of €15bn from the Digital Europe Programme to de-risk strategic infrastructure. Convertible loans tied to legal anchoring and audit access were approved, but momentum slowed.

Meanwhile, the US election returned a consolidation victory, signaling tighter frontier access for foreign actors. Though the administration takes office next turn, markets reacted immediately: American labs began re-scoping partnerships, and EU access to training pipelines narrowed in anticipation. This dependence, now formalised in policy, deepened sovereignty concerns.

The EAAD pilot’s discovery of reward-model misalignment in SOMI lent urgency to the proposed oversight expansion. However, establishing interim authority under Article 16 proved politically fraught. Legal services raised concerns over bypassing Council approval, and several member states questioned the legitimacy of empowering a still-forming agency with veto-like powers. The measure passed in diluted form—EAAD gains reporting authority and recommendation rights, but halting deployment requires an ad hoc College of Commissioners vote, not automatic power.

Public sentiment dipped further as job losses in AI-linked sectors mounted and school moratoriums spread. The symbolic shift toward accountable control failed to offset perceptions of dysfunction. Resilience declined slightly, with no new cyber or bio defences operational.
