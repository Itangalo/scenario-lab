## Priority
**Priority:** Establish interim legal authority for EAAD to delay SOMI deployment  
Because legitimacy now turns on whether EU control means the power to stop as well as to build — and because without that power, SOMI risks becoming a symbol of unaccountable centralisation rather than sovereign responsibility.

## In practice
We are acting on the red-team pilot’s findings — alignment drift in SOMI’s reward modelling is real, and while not catastrophic, it confirms that even our own systems cannot be trusted without teeth. This turn, we are instructing the Commission to formalise interim authority for EAAD under Article 16 of the AI Act, enabling it to issue binding deployment conditions or halts on SOMI based on control, security, or alignment risks. This is not full statutory empowerment — that still requires Council agreement — but it is a bridge: the College of Commissioners will delegate emergency oversight powers to EAAD’s director, with decisions appealable only to the College itself. We are fast-tracking the appointment of a director from among the pilot’s lead evaluators, offering a dual mandate with the European Cybersecurity Agency to strengthen technical legitimacy.

At the same time, we are confronting the collapse in private ai investment: the Tech Sovereignty Package is now at risk, as private partners withdraw from data centre commitments. We are redirecting €15bn from the Digital Europe Programme to backstop early-stage equity in strategic AI infrastructure, structured as convertible loans that vest only if compute is legally anchored in the EU and subject to EAAD audit. This is not a bailout but a recalibration: in a down market, public capital must de-risk the sovereign stack, not chase scale. We are pairing this with binding access guarantees — any entity receiving public support must provide vetted researcher access and incident reporting under EU supervision.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain under legal review, but we are no longer treating construction as the sole measure of progress. Instead, we are redefining success for turn 7: not just concrete poured, but sovereign compute online, secured, and under EAAD monitoring. We are embedding assurance teams directly into the Brittany and Andalusia sites, with clearance to audit training infrastructure before first power-up. This is the pivot: sovereignty is not where the wires are, but whether we can say no — to our own projects, to our own timelines, when safety or legitimacy demands it. That is the control we must now prove.
