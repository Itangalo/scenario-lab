# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore political agency and public trust through demonstrable control over AI incidents, transparent accountability, and sovereign response capacity — but this turn, the denial of frontier access and the collapse of political capital make clear that control without capability is performative. We now pivot from accountability as the primary lever to survival of agency: re-establishing operational sovereignty in AI is the precondition for all else.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Restore political agency and public trust through demonstrable control over AI incidents, transparent accountability, and sovereign response capacity — but this turn, the denial of frontier access and the collapse of political capital make clear that control without capability is performative. We now pivot from accountability as the primary lever to survival of agency: re-establishing operational sovereignty in AI is the precondition for all else.
  - Trigger: eu_frontier_access_denied and eu_political_capital falling to 1.0
  - Grounds: The Union has been cut off from the strongest AI systems on which its hospitals, ministries, and firms depend — not through market failure, but by unilateral foreign decision with no appeal. This is not a regulatory challenge; it is an existential shock to the EU’s ability to function. At the same time, political capital has collapsed to near-zero, rendering even symbolic measures unenforceable. The previous commitment assumed we could build legitimacy through justice and transparency. But legitimacy cannot be restored if the Union cannot act — and it cannot act if it cannot compute. The two-year commitment must now shift from repairing trust to preserving agency: we must secure the means to run capable systems independently, or no tribunal, dashboard, or drill will matter. The focus remains on control, but control now means access, infrastructure, and the power to resist coercion.
  - Evidence quoted: **eu_frontier_access_denied:** The Union is cut off from the leading model at short notice, wholly or by nationality of user. No detailed reason is given, there is no appeal, and the immediate practical effect lands on hospitals, ministries and firms that had built on it. Whether this reads at home as an outrage or as a failure of foresight depends on what the Union had done about it beforehand.

**Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Exhausted**
The US supply chain pressure on ASML extended to mature-node equipment, directly impacting EU semiconductor supply for industrial AI systems. This strategic chokehold, exercised unilaterally, further degraded the Union’s operational autonomy. With no domestic frontier compute to fall back on, sovereignty declined.

**Political capital, already at crisis levels, eroded further. The Tribunal’s passage cost dearly, and the portfolio’s ongoing measures strained an exhausted system. Public sentiment fell as scandals reinforced perceptions of incompetence and injustice.**
  - Reason: The denial of frontier access and collapse of political capital directly undermine the EU's ability to exercise control and accountability, making prior commitments unenforceable.
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without leaving a traceable path of accountability, and we will not act at all if the action depends on foreign goodwill we cannot enforce.
  - Grounds: The eu_frontier_access_denied event proves that commitments based on access to external systems are not commitments at all — they are prayers. Our posture must now embed not only auditability, but autonomy: every major intervention must be executable on infrastructure the EU owns, controls, and can defend. This is not isolationism; it is the minimum condition for credible governance.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
