## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore political agency and public trust through demonstrable control over AI incidents, transparent accountability, and sovereign response capacity — but this turn, the denial of frontier access and the collapse of political capital make clear that control without capability is performative. We now pivot from accountability as the primary lever to survival of agency: re-establishing operational sovereignty in AI is the precondition for all else.  
- Trigger: eu_frontier_access_denied and eu_political_capital falling to 1.0  
- Grounds: The Union has been cut off from the strongest AI systems on which its hospitals, ministries, and firms depend — not through market failure, but by unilateral foreign decision with no appeal. This is not a regulatory challenge; it is an existential shock to the EU’s ability to function. At the same time, political capital has collapsed to near-zero, rendering even symbolic measures unenforceable. The previous commitment assumed we could build legitimacy through justice and transparency. But legitimacy cannot be restored if the Union cannot act — and it cannot act if it cannot compute. The two-year commitment must now shift from repairing trust to preserving agency: we must secure the means to run capable systems independently, or no tribunal, dashboard, or drill will matter. The focus remains on control, but control now means access, infrastructure, and the power to resist coercion.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without leaving a traceable path of accountability, and we will not act at all if the action depends on foreign goodwill we cannot enforce.  
- Grounds: The eu_frontier_access_denied event proves that commitments based on access to external systems are not commitments at all — they are prayers. Our posture must now embed not only auditability, but autonomy: every major intervention must be executable on infrastructure the EU owns, controls, and can defend. This is not isolationism; it is the minimum condition for credible governance.

## Priority  
**Priority:** Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation  
This is now our dominant effort because the eu_frontier_access_denied event has made clear that without independent compute, every other measure — accountability, resilience, transparency — collapses into theatre; we cannot enforce, respond, or even understand events if we cannot run the systems needed to do so.

## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that political agency must be grounded in physical infrastructure. The denial of access to the frontier model — without warning, without reason — has halted cancer diagnostics in three major hospitals, frozen customs automation at two ports, and disrupted central bank forecasting. This is not a temporary outage; it is a demonstration of power. We can no longer treat compute as a commodity. It is a strategic good, and we must treat it as such.

Under Article 122 TFEU and the newly invoked Crisis Instrument for Strategic Dependencies, we are repurposing the stalled InvestAI Gigafactory sites in Finland, Hungary, and Spain to host emergency compute clusters. The EIB is redirecting €45bn in unused digitalisation funds to fast-track construction, waiving standard procurement rules under the crisis clause. ENTSO-E has been ordered to grant priority grid access, and national regulators are instructed to fast-track environmental waivers. We are negotiating with NVIDIA and AMD to reroute pending shipments under EU jurisdiction, offering market access in return for on-site deployment — not ownership transfer, but operational control. This is not about catching up to the frontier; it is about ensuring the Union can still function. The Public Trust Corps continues its work, and the Tribunal negotiations proceed, but both now depend on this lifeline. Without it, we have no voice, no power, and no future to determine.
