## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that legitimacy must be earned in the field, not decreed from Brussels. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation continues, but we are no longer waiting for it to be complete to act. Under the authority of Article 122 TFEU and the Crisis Instrument, we are diverting initial compute from the Finnish cluster — the only site with partial grid access and local political acquiescence — to stand up the EU Emergency Public AI Service. This system will ingest real-time threat feeds from ENISA and national CERTs, use hardened open-weight models (capability ~55.0) to generate patches and alerts, and broadcast mitigation guidance through public broadcasters and emergency channels. It will not match the closed frontier, but it will work, it will be ours, and it will be seen.

The Public Trust Corps, now operational, is embedded in this effort: they will staff the response hub, conduct live transparency drills, and issue public updates on the system’s performance and limits. We are bypassing the paused Tribunal to instead create a visible feedback loop: every patch deployed, every incident handled, will be logged and explained. This is not accountability as audit — it is accountability as proof. We are offering Hungary and Spain a choice: join this visible effort, or be left outside the first functioning piece of EU AI sovereignty. The service will be small, but it will be real — and in a Union starved of results, that may be enough to restart the engine of trust.
