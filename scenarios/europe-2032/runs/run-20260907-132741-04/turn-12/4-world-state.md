### A Crisis of Agency

The EU attempted to salvage the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation while preparing a public-facing AI service to restore legitimacy. In Finland, engineers began repurposing available hardware to host hardened open-weight models for a proposed Emergency Public AI Service, intended to detect and patch cyber threats in real time. Initial testing was confined to sandboxed environments; no live data from ENISA or national CERTs was integrated, and public broadcasters remained on standby. The design phase advanced, but deployment was blocked by unresolved governance disputes.

Hungary formally withdrew cooperation, citing unresolved environmental disputes and lack of binding energy guarantees. Spain paused technical integration, demanding oversight rights the Commission refused to grant. Without unified access, scaling remains impossible. The Public Trust Corps issued its first transparency report: “Preparation underway. Authority fractured.”

### Capability Advances, Defences Lag

Global AI capability rose to 70.5, driven by uninterrupted training runs. Open-weight models saw no meaningful advance—progress in distributed replication efforts stalled due to fragmented coordination and withdrawal of key infrastructure partners. Despite isolated experiments in Finland, no functional improvements were integrated into the broader base. `openweight_capability` remains at 58.0. No safety improvements landed on deployed systems—`ai_safety` remained at 12.0. The recent cyber_major_incident overwhelmed unprepared agencies; patches arrived too late, attribution remains unclear. Automated defences exist, but they are fragmented, unequally distributed, and blind to coordinated multi-vector attacks.

### Sovereignty Erodes Further

Despite efforts, `eu_ai_sovereignty` fell to 3.0: −2 from `eu_frontier_access_denied`, −1 from `ai_capability` rising over 1.0, and no offsetting gains. The compute mobilisation yields no sovereign capacity this turn. Political capital declined to −4.0 under crushing portfolio costs and failed execution. Canceling the Accountability Tribunal freed no resources—it was already legally bound—and abandoning it mid-flight cost −5. The new small measure added −2, the priority −1, and three measures in flight cost −8 total, partially offset by a +6 proposal bonus for the sovereign compute response (net change: −5, from previous 1.0). The narrative of collapse outpaced the numbers; the system holds, barely.

Public sentiment held at 12.0. No scandal, no benefit—only fatigue.
