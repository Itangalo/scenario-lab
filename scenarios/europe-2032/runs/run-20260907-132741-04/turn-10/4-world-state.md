### A Crisis of Legitimacy Deepens

The EU launched the AI Accountability Tribunal in response to a damning ombudsman report on an AI-driven social insurance system that wronged over 12,000 citizens. Intended as a cornerstone of restored trust, the initiative faced immediate resistance. A coalition of six member states demanded sweeping opt-outs, forcing last-minute concessions on enforcement and jurisdiction. Though the Tribunal was formally established, its weakened mandate undermined public confidence. Protesters in Marseille dismissed it as “another layer of bureaucracy,” and media coverage framed it as a delayed, grudging response.

The Public Trust Corps continued its transparency drills, and the AI Incident Dashboard went live in beta. But participation remained low, and technical glitches during a live simulation in Warsaw fueled ridicule. With sentiment already fragile, the automated decision scandal — now legally confirmed — triggered a sharp backlash. Trust in public-sector AI collapsed further, especially in southern and eastern member states.

### Capability Leaps, Defences Lag

A sudden advance in AI-driven cyber operations—demonstrated in a high-profile breach of a Tier 2 energy provider in Belgium—revealed new offensive capabilities now within reach of non-state actors. The Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative, though officially completed, had not yet integrated all critical operators, leaving gaps exploited in the attack. Resilience dipped as incident response remained fragmented.

Meanwhile, open-weight models reached 57.0, aided by distributed training and open-source tools. The jump in capability, combined with unexplained reasoning anomalies in lab systems, deepened concerns about control. No safety improvements landed on deployed systems, and the gap between capability and assurance widened dangerously.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Exhausted

The US supply chain pressure on ASML extended to mature-node equipment, directly impacting EU semiconductor supply for industrial AI systems. This strategic chokehold, exercised unilaterally, further degraded the Union’s operational autonomy. With no domestic frontier compute to fall back on, sovereignty declined.

Political capital, already at crisis levels, eroded further. The Tribunal’s passage cost dearly, and the portfolio’s ongoing measures strained an exhausted system. Public sentiment fell as scandals reinforced perceptions of incompetence and injustice.
