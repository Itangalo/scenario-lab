### The Open Frontier, the Closed Door

Global AI capability advanced to 72.0, driven by relentless training cycles and algorithmic refinements now operating beyond human oversight. Signs of acceleration in open-weight development emerged: a major release demonstrated capabilities approaching the frontier, narrowing the gap significantly. However, it remains behind—`openweight_capability` rose to 59.5, reflecting steady progress but not parity. The open ecosystem is gaining momentum, but the lead of the closed frontier persists.

The EU’s frontier access was abruptly severed. Hospitals relying on foreign models for diagnostics, ministries using them for crisis simulation, and firms building on their APIs were left stranded. No explanation was given. The denial confirmed a new reality: dependency is revocable.

In response, the Union pivoted. The Accountability Tribunal, still legally intact but politically untenable, was formally canceled. Continuing it would have drained credibility and resources; abandoning it cost legitimacy. The Emergency Public AI Service began repurposing open-weight models on Finnish and Swedish hardware under Article 122 TFEU, bypassing stalled coordination. The Public Trust Corps, now operational, embedded itself in the loop, publishing logs and failure reports.

Yet sovereignty continued to erode. The compute mobilisation remained fragmented—Spain and Hungary held out. The new measure, though launched, could not offset the loss of access or the capability gap. Political capital plunged further under the weight of ongoing commitments. Public sentiment remained frozen at 12.0: no benefit had yet landed, only announcements.

The Union now fights not to lead, but to exist as an autonomous actor. Its last lever—operational continuity through open models—is being tested in real time.
