The standing coordination cell at ENISA is now staffed with embedded personnel from seven national CERTs, Frontex, and the European Defence Agency, and has conducted its first live-fire exercise simulating a multi-vector AI-driven attack on energy and health infrastructure. We have secured pre-authorised escalation protocols from France, Germany, and the Netherlands, but Hungary and Poland continue to resist automatic cross-border intervention powers, citing sovereignty. In response, we are offering both states permanent seats on the Cyber Shield Oversight Board and co-location of secondary command nodes on their territory, conditional on full adherence to the incident playbook.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the **Emergency Compute Resilience Initiative** toward its turn-10 deadline. The joint design board has approved the Bavarian site’s architecture, with construction set to begin in Q1 2031. We have finalised the binding procurement guarantee for ASML, reserving 15% of next-gen EU-bound tooling for sovereign compute clusters, in exchange for accelerated delivery timelines. Italy’s request to include CINECA has been accepted as a Tier 2 node, expanding redundancy. However, no new compute is yet operational, and our leverage remains fragile: we are still dependent on foreign suppliers and vulnerable to delays. The combination of rising `ai_capability` and stagnant `eu_ai_sovereignty` means that every day without sovereign capacity is a day of strategic exposure.
