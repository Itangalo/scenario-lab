AI capability rises to 65.5 in 2029 as closed labs advance self-improving agents, while open-weight models reach 53.5 following a leak-derived release, narrowing the capability gap and increasing misuse risks. A coordinated AI-driven ransomware attack exploits zero-day vulnerabilities across five EU states, overwhelming municipal systems and exposing critical defence gaps due to fragmented response and ENISA’s limited authority. In response, the EU launches the Joint Cyber Shield—a Category 6 initiative to unify civilian and military cyber forces under ENISA with pre-authorized escalation—backed by a €2.8bn package, though legal and sovereignty disputes delay implementation. The Emergency Compute Resilience Initiative progresses slowly, with Germany agreeing to host a cluster under joint design oversight, but no new compute becomes operational. Resilience increases to 55.0 due to systemic alarm and partial progress on cyber integration. Safety remains stagnant at 25.0, hindered by model opacity. Sovereignty dips to 31.0 amid capability growth and unresolved governance tensions. Political capital declines to 49.0 due to the high cost of new measures, partially offset by initiating a crisis response. Public sentiment falls to 32.0 as fears of uncontrolled AI threats grow.
