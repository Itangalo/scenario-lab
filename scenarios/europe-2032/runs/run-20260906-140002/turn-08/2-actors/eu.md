## New measure  
**Joint Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure**  
Establish a coordinated, EU-level cyber response capability to detect, contain, and counter AI-generated ransomware and supply-chain attacks on public services and essential infrastructure, integrating national CERTs, ENISA, and military cyber commands under a unified playbook with pre-authorized escalation paths.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 10  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to +12 resilience by turn 10; reduce median response time to AI-enabled cyber incidents from 38+ hours to under 6; harden at least 80% of Tier 1 public service operators against autonomous attack agents; increase public confidence in state response capacity

## Priority  
**Joint Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure** — because the cyber_major_incident this turn reveals that our monitoring is blind to speed, our defences are fragmented, and our legal authorities lag the threat: we now face AI-driven attacks that propagate faster than human decision loops, and we cannot rely on national silos or post-incident attribution when the damage is already systemic.

## In practice  
This turn, we confront the consequences of the **cyber_major_incident**: a coordinated ransomware sweep across municipal services in five member states, exploiting AI-generated zero-days in a widely used open-source accounting module. The attack bypassed traditional signature detection, adapted in real time to defensive moves, and encrypted critical civil registry data in under four hours. Response was delayed by jurisdictional disputes, lack of pre-agreed escalation protocols, and insufficient integration between civilian and military cyber units. ENISA flagged anomalies, but without authority to act, and national CERTs operated in isolation. The incident is not yet attributed, but forensic analysis confirms the exploit chain was model-generated — a threshold crossed. This is not a drill.

We are therefore launching the **Joint Cyber Shield**, a large-scale resilience measure under Category 6, to close the gap between detection and action. We will establish a standing coordination cell at ENISA with embedded personnel from national cyber agencies, Frontex, and the EDA, operating under a unified incident playbook approved by the Cybersecurity Council. The cell will have pre-authorized powers to isolate compromised systems, deploy counter-AI decoys, and initiate cross-border traffic throttling during declared incidents. We are fast-tracking legal basing through Article 4(2) TEU and the NIS2 Directive’s emergency provisions, with a draft regulation to be voted in the Council’s Justice and Home Affairs formation by June. Funding of €2.8bn will come from the Internal Security Fund and redirected Recovery and Resilience Facility allocations.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the **Emergency Compute Resilience Initiative**, despite German resistance. We have accepted their demand for a joint design review board, but conditioned participation on their commitment to host one of the two hardened backup clusters in Bavaria. We are also offering ASML a binding procurement guarantee for next-gen lithography tools reserved for EU sovereign sites, in exchange for prioritisation of equipment delivery. France and Poland remain aligned; Italy has requested inclusion of CINECA in the network. The goal remains: by turn 10, we must have compute we can command, when everything else is moving too fast to ask permission.
