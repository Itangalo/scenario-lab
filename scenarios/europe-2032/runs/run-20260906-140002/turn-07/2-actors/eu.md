## In practice  
This turn, we close the **Systemic Integrity Watch** as a formally launched capability. Despite two-month staffing delays, it is now operational at ENISA with embedded teams from DG ENER, DG SANTE, and national regulators. Real-time telemetry flows from 61% of Tier 1 energy operators and 34% of central health data hubs. The system has already flagged three anomalous feedback loops in grid automation — one in Belgium, two in northern Italy — where AI-driven load-balancing agents began reinforcing each other’s predictions without human oversight. No cascade occurred, but the lag between detection and intervention was 38 hours — too long. We are now pushing for emergency delegation under revised NIS2 rules to allow automated suspension of suspect deployments, a move facing resistance in the Council’s Competitiveness formation, where Germany and Sweden warn of chilling effects on innovation. We are offering them co-governance via a new Joint Oversight Cell — half EU, half national appointees — to preserve agility without unilateralism.

With that measure closed, we pivot hard to infrastructure sovereignty. The openweight release — downloaded over 400,000 times in days — proves that whatever capability exists at the frontier will soon be everywhere, unrecallable and uncontrolled. Our resilience and monitoring can only go so far if the compute that runs these models is foreign-owned, offshore, and subject to external legal jurisdiction. That is why we launch the **Emergency Compute Resilience Initiative**: using €9.2bn from the revived Recovery and Resilience Facility and redirected Chips Act funds, we will build six sovereign compute clusters over the next 18 months, each anchored to national research labs and linked via a dedicated dark-fibre backbone. These will not chase the frontier — not yet — but will run verified, auditable models for crisis response, medical research, and energy coordination under full EU legal control. We are negotiating binding access terms with ASML to prioritise tooling for these sites, and with EuroHPC JU to repurpose LUMI and Leonardo-class infrastructure for dual-use readiness. France and Poland are in; Germany hesitates, fearing fragmentation, so we are offering them lead coordination on security certification — a concession, but one that keeps cohesion. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that when the next incident hits, we have systems we can *command*.
