## In practice  
This turn, we act on two fronts: one defensive, one foundational. On the defensive front, the Frontier Model Evaluation Mandate moves from announcement to operation. The European AI Office is now selecting three independent evaluators — one public (a consortium led by Fraunhofer), one academic (EPFL and TU Delft), and one civil society (AlgorithmWatch with technical augmentation). We are adapting the MDR’s conformity assessment model to AI, requiring developers to submit red-team access, training logs, and agent-behaviour summaries. The first benchmark suite — focused on monitoring evasion and recursive self-modification — will be published by mid-turn. But we face resistance: two U.S. labs have signalled they may withdraw from EU markets rather than comply. We are preparing legal countermeasures under the Digital Markets Act to designate them gatekeepers, which would make withdrawal costlier. Still, enforcement remains fragile without resources — and we have not yet found the funds.

On the foundational front, we launch the Accelerated Compute Buildout Directive to convert the Tech Sovereignty Package’s framework into binding speed. The supply_chain_coercion event — Washington forcing ASML to cut not just EU allies but EU industry — is not just an external shock. It is proof that our sovereignty is not just behind, but actively being *used*. We cannot wait for consensus. This directive will empower the Commission to override national permitting delays for data centres that meet three conditions: legal incorporation in the EU, open audit of training infrastructure, and 70% domestic energy sourcing. We will pair this with a new clause in InvestAI: gigafactory funding is now contingent on participation in this audit regime. Germany and France are on board; Poland’s energy concession will be honoured, but tied to compliance. Bavaria remains a problem — but we are offering a binding commitment: if they approve the Munich site, we will co-fund a regional retraining hub for displaced technical workers, turning resistance into reinvestment.

We are not abandoning evaluation. But we are no longer pretending that oversight alone can secure autonomy. If the plateau holds, we have time. If it breaks, we will have nothing but our own compute to stand on.
