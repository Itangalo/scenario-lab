The EU faced a dual crisis as a major AI-generated ransomware attack crippled regional health systems and simultaneous denial of access to frontier AI models exposed critical dependencies on foreign-controlled technology. The European AI Office’s evaluation mandate proved ineffective against non-compliant providers, undermining trust. In response, the EU launched the Emergency Resilience Surge to coordinate cybersecurity and civil protection efforts, though implementation remains stalled by unresolved legal and technical challenges. Member states struggle with fallback capacity, while disputes over access to U.S.-controlled model-provenance tools persist. Reinforced U.S. export controls on semiconductor equipment intensified supply chain coercion, further eroding EU AI sovereignty, which fell to 15.0 due to portfolio strain and capability decoupling. AI safety and public sentiment declined sharply amid rising real-world risks and operational fragility. Open-weight capability stagnated at 44.0, as technological isolation and lack of domestic breakthroughs halted progress. The 2028 U.S. election resulted in a decisive victory for a candidate prioritizing AI as a strategic asset, leaving the EU in uncertainty over future cooperation and technology access.
