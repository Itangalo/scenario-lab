## New measure
**Emergency Resilience Surge**
A rapid, targeted hardening of critical public services against AI-generated cyber threats in the wake of the cyber_major_incident, combining incident response capacity, fallback procedures, and model-provenance tracing.

## Priority
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_major_incident has exposed a dangerous gap between offensive capability and defensive readiness, and the eu_frontier_access_denied event proves we cannot rely on external systems even for crisis response; resilience is no longer a background metric but the front line of sovereignty.

## In practice
This turn, we are acting under fire — and adjusting our balance between sovereignty and safety accordingly. The Frontier Model Evaluation Mandate has now concluded its first cycle: the European AI Office has accredited three third-party evaluators, and the first benchmark suite on agent persistence has been published. However, enforcement remains thin. Two U.S. labs have formally challenged the mandate’s extraterritorial reach, and while we are preparing countermeasures under the Digital Markets Act, the legal uncertainty has slowed uptake. We are now shifting focus from evaluation to execution — not abandoning oversight, but recognising that in a world where models are already loose and attacks are already landing, resilience must become our immediate shield.

The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep across regional health administrations — has exposed how far our defences lag behind offensive capabilities now in circulation. At the same time, eu_frontier_access_denied has cut hospitals and transport ministries off from the very systems they depended on for crisis coordination. These are not separate failures but one: a dependence structure that fails both in peace and in crisis. We are launching the Emergency Resilience Surge to respond. This will mobilise the EU Civil Protection Mechanism and ERDF funds to deploy fallback systems, incident response teams, and model-provenance tracing tools within 90 days. We are tasking ENISA with rapid deployment protocols and working through the Health Security Committee to harden medical IT systems. Germany and France have pledged national assets; we are offering emergency cohesion funding to smaller states to ensure parity. This is not a long-term fix — but it is a necessary one. If we cannot keep services running when attacked, no sovereignty agenda survives.
