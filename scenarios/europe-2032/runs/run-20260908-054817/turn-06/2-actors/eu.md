The previous commitment assumed sovereignty meant access; now, it must mean independent production. This shift acknowledges that the EU cannot rely on access to closed models, but can now plausibly build its own — and must, to meet both its mandates.

## In practice  
This turn, we are pivoting from access to autonomy. The **Emergency Resilience Surge** is now in motion: ENISA has activated cross-border incident response protocols, and the Health Security Committee is deploying fallback diagnostic systems to regional hospitals. We are using ERDF and cohesion funds to backstop technical gaps in smaller member states, but legal disputes over model-provenance tools persist — the U.S. continues to block access, and we are preparing a case under the Anti-Coercion Instrument. Still, the Surge is proving our ability to act collectively under fire, and early demonstrations of coordinated response are beginning to stabilise public sentiment.

At the same time, we are acting on the **emergent_distributed_training_breakthrough**. The discovery by European researchers that high-capability models can be trained on commodity hardware changes everything: it breaks the chokehold of hyperscaler dominance and offers a path to sovereign capability outside the reach of export controls. We are mobilising EuroHPC, national research clouds, and the newly formed EU AI Research Consortium to launch the **Sovereign Model Training Initiative**. This will fund three open, auditable training runs over the next 18 months, using EU-sourced data, EU-supervised objectives, and EU-secured weights. We are working with member states to repurpose decommissioned industrial sites into training clusters, and offering fast-tracked grid access under the same rules as the now-completed Accelerated Compute Buildout Directive.

We are not abandoning oversight — far from it. These models will be subject to public evaluation under the framework established by the now-concluded Frontier Model Evaluation Mandate, and we are expanding ENISA’s remit to include real-time monitoring of training runs. But we are no longer waiting for permission. The combination of crisis, breakthrough, and collapsing access has made one thing clear: sovereignty is not access. It is the power to build, and to act, alone.
