The EU responded to a dual crisis of AI-driven cyberattacks and loss of access to frontier AI models by launching the Emergency Resilience Surge, though progress was hampered by legal and technical barriers, including U.S. restrictions on model-provenance tools. Amid stalled implementation and eroding AI sovereignty (down to 15.0), a breakthrough by a European university consortium demonstrated that high-capability AI models could be trained on commodity hardware, challenging reliance on hyperscaler infrastructure. In response, the EU launched the Sovereign Model Training Initiative to build auditable, domestically controlled AI models using EuroHPC and national resources, marking a strategic shift from dependency to self-reliance. The Tech Sovereignty Package and Accelerated Compute Buildout Directive were completed, advancing data centre development, but actual compute capacity and frontier model access lagged. The Emergency Resilience Surge saw limited deployment, with ENISA coordinating cyber response and fallback systems in some hospitals, while smaller states received cohesion support. Public sentiment remained low at 30, as concerns grew over programmatic strain and feasibility. The new U.S. administration solidified a strategic pivot, treating AI as a national asset and imposing tiered, restrictive access for allies, effectively ending the era of open AI collaboration with Europe.
