### A Breakthrough and a Pivot

A European university consortium stunned the global AI community by demonstrating that high-capability models can now be trained on clusters of commodity hardware, bypassing the need for hyperscaler-scale infrastructure. This breakthrough, while promising for democratization, raises immediate concerns about uncontrolled proliferation and misuse, particularly as current safeguards remain weak.

In response, the EU shifts strategy. No longer content with securing access to foreign models, it launches the **Sovereign Model Training Initiative**, a large-scale effort to harness the breakthrough for public benefit. The programme will fund auditable, EU-supervised training runs across EuroHPC and national research clouds, aiming to build sovereign capability immune to external cutoffs. As a priority, it pulls forward coordination and resource allocation, though full implementation remains years away.

Meanwhile, the **Emergency Resilience Surge** begins cautious deployment. ENISA activates cross-border cyber response teams, and fallback diagnostic systems are rolled out in select hospitals. However, legal disputes over model-provenance tools — blocked by the U.S. — stall full interoperability. Smaller member states receive cohesion funding, but technical gaps persist.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** and **Accelerated Compute Buildout Directive** formally conclude, marking progress in data centre investment and permitting reform. Yet tangible compute capacity lags, and the ongoing denial of frontier model access underlines that infrastructure alone is not sovereignty.

Public sentiment holds at 30, reflecting continued anxiety. While the initiative is praised as bold, critics question its feasibility and warn of diverting funds from immediate resilience needs. The political cost of maintaining multiple large programmes — now three in flight — strains decision-making capacity.

### The American Posture Takes Shape

The new U.S. administration takes office, confirming a strategic pivot: AI will be treated as a core national asset. Access to frontier models is expected to be tightly controlled, with European requests now subject to tiered review. The era of open collaboration appears over.
