### Sovereignty Tested, Gains Narrow

The **Sovereign Model Training Initiative** is declared complete, marking a symbolic victory for European AI independence. The Gdansk and Saint-Étienne clusters delivered their first auditable run, proving distributed training on EU-anchored infrastructure is feasible. However, the firmware-level compromise in the Polish node casts a long shadow. Though no data was altered, the breach exposes systemic fragility in supply chain oversight, triggering a continent-wide audit of third-party firmware providers.

In response, the Commission fast-tracks the **Sovereign Compute Certification Framework**, mandating ENISA-led firmware audits for all future EU-funded AI infrastructure. The move strengthens long-term integrity but slows deployment timelines, as new nodes face extended clearance.

The **Joint AI Governance Cell Activation** remains in limbo. Despite targeted offers to Sweden and Finland, Nordic ratification stalls over data sovereignty concerns. Canada delays further, citing constitutional hurdles. Only Japan moves toward full participation, after securing limited access to EU defect data. With consensus unlikely by turn 10, planning shifts toward a **coalition of the willing**, centred on the EU, Japan, and the Nordic-Benelux red-team network—narrower, but operable.

Simultaneously, the **EU Sovereign Compute Corridor Expansion** is launched, adding gigafactory sites in Portugal, Hungary, and Lithuania to the EuroHPC-Aurora backbone. The project promises greater resilience and redundancy, but its scale strains coordination across energy and security agencies.

### Stalled Momentum

AI capability advances by 0.5, reflecting continued global stagnation. No open-weight release occurs, and safety sees no improvement—governance mechanisms remain in formation, not enforcement. Resilience holds steady, with no major incidents.

Political capital erodes further. The new large measure and ongoing portfolio costs overwhelm the EU’s thin fiscal and diplomatic bandwidth.
