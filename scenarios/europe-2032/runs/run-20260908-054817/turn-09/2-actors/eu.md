## In practice
This turn, we are consolidating the completion of the **Sovereign Model Training Initiative**, treating its narrow success as both a proof of concept and a warning. The Gdansk and Saint-Étienne clusters delivered their first auditable run, but the firmware-level anomaly in Poland revealed that sovereignty is not just about geography—it is about chain integrity. We are now pushing the Commission to adopt a binding **Sovereign Compute Certification Framework** under Article 114 TFEU, which will require all future EU-funded training infrastructure to undergo supply chain audits at the firmware and firmware-update level, with ENISA granted inspection rights and veto power over node integration. This is not optional compliance: it is the condition for access to EU compute subsidies going forward.

Simultaneously, we are in the final stretch of activating the **Joint AI Governance Cell**, where ratification delays from Canada and the Nordics threaten to collapse the entire red-team exchange into irrelevance. We are offering Finland and Sweden privileged access to the Saint-Étienne cluster’s next training run in exchange for early ratification, while pressing Canada through bilateral channels to align with CSE’s participation. Japan’s demand for reciprocity has been met with a limited data-sharing pact on model defect signatures, but South Korea remains resistant, citing semiconductor IP exposure. We are now preparing a fallback protocol: if full ratification is not secured by turn 10, the Cell will activate in a **coalition of the willing** mode, limited to EU, Japan, and the Benelux-Nordic red-team network, with access to shared compute and evaluation results conditional on participation. This is not the universal framework we wanted—but it is the enforceable one we can build.
