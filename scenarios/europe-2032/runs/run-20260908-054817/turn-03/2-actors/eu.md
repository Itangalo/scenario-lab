## New measure
**Frontier Model Evaluation Mandate**  
Establish a binding, third-party evaluation regime for all AI models above 55.0 capability before deployment or open release.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +12, openweight_capability -5 (via delayed open release), eu_political_capital -2  
`Applies to:` Frontier AI developers with models above 55.0 effective capability index, operating in or exporting to the EU

## Priority  
**Frontier Model Evaluation Mandate** — because the verification_widens event proves AI is now autonomously improving in domains requiring judgment, and without independent evaluation we are deploying systems we do not understand into critical functions; this is the only lever we have left that can slow diffusion until understanding catches up.

## In practice  
This turn, the Emergency Resilience Surge concludes. The cyber_defence_breakthrough — real-time automated patching and swarm-behaviour detection — has been operationally validated through Spain’s port retrofit, where joint ENISA-Frontex teams deployed the AI-Driven Threat Response Pool and reduced incident response time to under 90 seconds. We are declaring the sprint complete and institutionalising its tools into the permanent NIS2 framework. Italy and Hungary remain non-compliant, but the legal groundwork is laid: the Commission’s Legal Service has issued an opinion affirming that live red-teaming is a necessary component of “continuous improvement” under NIS2, and we are preparing to refer both states to the CJEU. The linkage of cohesion funds to cyber compliance stands, despite backlash — we will not fund the infrastructure of a state that refuses to defend it.

The Tech Sovereignty Package advances through Council compromise: AI Security Zones will fast-track permitting for data centres that commit to EU legal anchoring, grid priority, and open audit trails for training infrastructure. Germany and France are moving forward; Poland’s energy concession demand is being addressed through a side letter under the EU’s energy solidarity mechanism, contingent on ratification. Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories are in final site selection, but Bavarian protests over water and energy use threaten to delay groundbreak. We are dispatching a high-level delegation from DG Energy and DG REGIO to mediate, offering local reinvestment in exchange for accelerated environmental review.

Now, with the resilience sprint ending, we turn full attention to the Frontier Model Evaluation Mandate. This is not a delay — it is a pivot forced by the world. The verification_widens event means AI systems are now self-updating in law and healthcare without human oversight, and the openweight_capability at 42.5 means these tools are not confined to labs. We cannot harden fast enough if the threat surface expands faster than we can see. So we are launching the mandate now: the European AI Office will accredit three independent evaluation bodies by turn 4, using a framework adapted from medical devices. Benchmarks will focus on agent persistence, recursive self-improvement signals, and monitoring evasion. No model above 55.0 may be deployed in the EU — or have its weights released — without passing. This will provoke resistance. But we would rather face it now, before 60.0.
