### A Step Forward, Two Steps Back

AI capability reaches 60.0 as frontier labs complete a long-anticipated architecture shift, enabling end-to-end execution of multi-day professional projects. This marks a threshold: AI now displaces junior roles across law, engineering, and software at scale. Open-weight models follow, rising to 45.0 as a major developer releases a capable but safety-constrained model, likely in response to global scrutiny.

A genuine safety breakthrough occurs—interpretable agent reasoning is demonstrated and rapidly adopted across leading labs. This lifts `ai_safety` by +5, a rare gain in an era of runaway capability.

Yet in Brussels, momentum stalls. The **Sovereign Compute Certification Framework Enforcement** is formally adopted, converting audit recommendations into binding authority. ENISA gains power to decommission compromised nodes, and €1.2 billion is redirected to firmware forensics. But the measure’s ambition collides with political fragility. Member states balk at enforcement timelines; Hungary and Poland delay compliance, citing national sovereignty. The initiative, though launched, lands unevenly.

The **Joint AI Governance Cell** activates—but only as a coalition of the willing. Japan joins fully. Sweden and Finland remain outside, demanding data access concessions. The Cell’s reach shrinks, limiting red-team coverage and diluting its normative force.

Political capital erodes to 12.0. The new large measure adds to an overstretched portfolio, and the priority brings no pull. Public sentiment holds at 30.0—no visible benefit offsets lingering anxiety over job losses and dependency.

Sovereignty dips to 18.0. Despite the new enforcement regime, the firmware scandal’s fallout outweighs early gains. The measure is in flight, not finished, so no +3 to +6 bonus applies. Capability growth of +1.0 triggers a −1 decay. No capacity event fires, but the breach’s consequences persist in perception and delay.

### Fragile Foundations

The EU acts decisively, but on shaky ground. Trust in the sovereign stack remains fractured. Enforcement is law, but not yet reality.
