A major AI-enabled intrusion into critical infrastructure, including two EU grid operators, exposed severe vulnerabilities and triggered the Emergency Resilience Surge—a €18bn, nine-month mandatory hardening programme led by ENISA. Despite urgent calls, implementation stalled due to member state resistance over sovereignty and cost, with only six of fifteen key operators completing initial upgrades. Italy and Hungary rejected the full audit framework, Spain delayed action, and repurposing of Frontex AI for dual-use purposes collapsed amid legal disputes. The 90% co-financing incentive proved insufficient to overcome operational hurdles. Parallel initiatives, including the InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package, faced delays from environmental opposition and legislative gridlock. Public concern shifted from AI optimism to job displacement in professional sectors, while advancing AI capabilities outpaced safety measures. The EU’s strategy revealed a widening gap between ambition and political cohesion, narrowing the window for effective action.
