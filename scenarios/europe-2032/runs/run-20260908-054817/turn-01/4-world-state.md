### The Wake-Up Call That Wasn’t Heard Loud Enough

The discovery of a prolonged AI-enabled intrusion across critical infrastructure operators — including two EU grid operators — sent shockwaves through Brussels. Dubbed a “cyber test shot” by intelligence analysts, the breach revealed that existing defences failed to detect the attackers, who were only uncovered by chance. The European Union responded with urgency, launching the **Emergency Resilience Surge**, a large-scale, binding audit and hardening programme led by ENISA. Energy, water, and port operators were given nine months to implement AI-resistant segmentation and live red-teaming protocols, backed by €18bn in repurposed funds.

Yet momentum faltered. Member states pushed back against central mandates, citing sovereignty and cost. Italy and Hungary refused to adopt the full audit framework, while Spain delayed implementation pending national review. Only six of fifteen targeted operators completed even preliminary upgrades by year’s end. The promised dual-use repurposing of Frontex surveillance AI stalled over legal concerns, and the 90% co-financing offer failed to offset operational complexity.

Meanwhile, the broader sovereignty agenda limped forward. The InvestAI Gigafactories entered site selection, but environmental challenges and local opposition slowed progress. The Tech Sovereignty Package remained stuck in legislative gridlock, with no breakthrough on accelerated permitting.

Public sentiment dipped slightly as media coverage shifted from AI’s promise to job losses in legal and accounting sectors — an emerging displacement wave now visible in national statistics. The cyber intrusion, while serious, did not trigger mass panic, but neither did it unify action. AI safety eroded further, as frontier capability advanced and no new safeguards landed on deployed systems.

### A Narrowing Window

The EU’s dual-track strategy — resilience now, sovereignty later — exposed the limits of political capital. Ambition outpaced cohesion.
