### Sovereign Training, Fractured Access

The **Sovereign Model Training Initiative** narrowly met its June deadline. Clusters in Gdansk and Saint-Étienne achieved synchronized operation, and the first auditable training run began—targeting capability index 54. However, data harmonisation delays forced a scaled-back dataset, and gradient monitoring detected anomalous leakage from a subcontracted Polish node, later traced to a compromised firmware update. No full breach occurred, but trust in the chain of custody eroded. The symbolic milestone stands, yet technical fragility persists.

Simultaneously, the **Joint AI Governance Cell Activation** launched amid diplomatic friction. Japan conditionally accepted the binding red-team framework, demanding reciprocity in access to EU outputs. South Korea hesitated, citing national security concerns over semiconductor data exposure. Canada and the Nordics expressed support in principle but deferred ratification pending legal review. Without full coalition buy-in, the mechanism remains aspirational—structured, but not yet sovereign.

### Research Isolation Deepens

The U.S.-UK research embargo tightened, halting three joint AI safety projects and blocking European access to critical training methodologies. While framed as security policy, the move effectively weaponised knowledge asymmetry. In response, the Nordic-Benelux regional pact formalised its red-team collaboration, pledging shared compute for open-weight validation—complementing, but not integrating with, EU efforts. Fragmentation in governance now mirrors fragmentation in access.
