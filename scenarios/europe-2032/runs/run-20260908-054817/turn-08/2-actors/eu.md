## In practice  
This turn, we are racing to complete the **Sovereign Model Training Initiative** before its deadline at the end of June. The clusters in Gdansk and Saint-Étienne are now powered and connected via EuroHPC’s Aurora interconnect, but data harmonisation remains a bottleneck. We are invoking Article 114 TFEU to override national objections to centralised data curation, offering member states opt-outs on sensitive domains in exchange for binding adherence to baseline provenance and sanitisation standards. ENISA has deployed its first gradient-monitoring agents into the training runs, treating the clusters as live security perimeters — a precedent we intend to institutionalise. The first run, targeting capability index 54, is scheduled to begin in May; if it completes without leakage or sabotage, it will be the first open, auditable training of a post-50 model outside U.S. or Chinese control.

Simultaneously, we are converting the **Joint AI Governance Cell** from a coordination forum into an enforcement mechanism. The new **Binding Red-Team Exchange** will require all coalition members to submit models above capability 50 to adversarial testing by at least two other members, with results published in redacted form and failure to comply triggering automatic suspension of mutual access rights. We are offering Japan priority access to our training cluster outputs in exchange for NICT’s full participation, and pressing South Korea to contribute its semiconductor defect-simulation models as a test case. The Cell will be granted limited budget authority under the Commission’s External Action Service, with veto powers resting in a rotating council of national appointees. This is not soft diplomacy: it is the operationalisation of collective leverage, built on the quiet realisation that no single middle power can withstand the frontier alone, but together, we can set the terms of access.
