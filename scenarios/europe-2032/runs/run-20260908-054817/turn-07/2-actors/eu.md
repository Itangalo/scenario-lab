## In practice  
This turn, we are closing out two major programmes: the **InvestAI Gigafactories** and the **Emergency Resilience Surge**. The Gigafactories have delivered on their legal and investment framework — four sites are now under construction, with fast-tracked grid access and audit mandates in place — but their physical capacity will not come online until well into 2030. We are therefore pivoting from construction promises to operational credibility. The **Sovereign Model Training Initiative** becomes our signal act: we are repurposing two decommissioned industrial zones in Poland and France into training clusters, using EuroHPC’s next-generation interconnects and national research clouds to stand up three auditable runs by Q1 2030. We are working with the European Commission’s Legal Service to invoke Article 114 TFEU to harmonise data curation standards across member states, ensuring training datasets meet baseline security and provenance requirements. ENISA is expanding its mandate to include real-time monitoring of gradient flows and anomaly detection during training, treating the runs as live security events.

At the same time, the **middle_power_coalition** has matured from coordination to leverage. We are now negotiating a **Joint Access Framework** that binds coalition members to mutual model access in times of crisis — but only if all parties meet shared safety and sovereignty thresholds. This is not an alliance; it is a cartel of capability. We are offering Japanese and Korean partners access to our sovereign training runs in exchange for guaranteed high-end chip supply and joint evaluation of model weights. The framework will be governed by a new Joint AI Governance Cell, hosted at the Commission and staffed by national appointees, with veto power over any export or access decision that undermines collective red lines. This is how we turn supply chain fragments into strategic power: not by going it alone, but by making sure no one else can go it alone either.
