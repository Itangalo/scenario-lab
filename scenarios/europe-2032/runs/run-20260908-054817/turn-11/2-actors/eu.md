## In practice
This turn, we are moving from policy to enforcement. The Sovereign Compute Certification Framework is now law under Article 114 TFEU, and ENISA has been empowered to act. But authority without capacity is symbolic. We are therefore launching the **Accelerated Firmware Forensics and Trusted Stack Certification Network**, directing €1.8 billion from the Digital Europe Programme and national co-financing to stand up six new verification labs in Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Finland, and the Netherlands. These labs will operate under a unified protocol, with access to a secure firmware repository hosted at the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking in Luxembourg. Their mandate is clear: validate firmware signatures, detect backdoors, and issue time-stamped certifications that feed directly into ENISA’s compliance dashboard.

We are confronting resistance head-on. Hungary and Poland continue to delay audits, citing national sovereignty and cost. We are offering both a transitional compliance window—conditional on quarterly progress reports—and access to a €300 million solidarity fund for infrastructure upgrades, but we are also preparing to invoke Article 7 sanctions if nodes remain uncertified by turn 13. The message is: sovereignty is not a veto on shared security. Meanwhile, the Joint AI Governance Cell has formally activated with Japan and the Benelux states; Sweden and Finland remain outside, but we are maintaining the offer of privileged access to the next sovereign training run as incentive. The trusted core is narrow, but it is live. We act from it.
