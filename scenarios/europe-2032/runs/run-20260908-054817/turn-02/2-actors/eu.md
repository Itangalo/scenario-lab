## In practice  
We are doubling down on enforcement of the Emergency Resilience Surge. The cyber_defence_breakthrough — automated patching at attack-speed and swarm-behaviour detection — changes the game: we now know the tools exist to close the gap. But they are not being adopted. This turn, we are invoking Article 122 TFEU to condition access to EU-wide cyber defence tooling on compliance with ENISA’s audit framework. Non-compliant operators will be excluded from the new AI-Driven Threat Response Pool, a shared defensive capability being rolled out through the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre. We are also fast-tracking legal opinions from the Commission’s Legal Service to clarify that refusal to implement live red-teaming constitutes a breach of the NIS2 Directive’s “continuous improvement” clause. Spain has signalled willingness to re-engage if we provide technical assistance; we are deploying a joint ENISA-Frontex task force to help Madrid retrofit its port systems by April. Italy and Hungary remain resistant, so we are linking cohesion fund disbursements to progress on critical infrastructure hardening — a move that will draw legal challenges, but one we are prepared to defend.

Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package inches forward: we have secured a compromise in the Council on accelerated permitting for data centres in designated “AI Security Zones,” tied to binding commitments on EU legal anchoring and grid priority. Germany and France are on board; Poland demands additional energy infrastructure in return. The InvestAI Gigafactories are now in final site assessment, with two locations in Germany and one in France shortlisted. Environmental reviews are being fast-tracked under the emergency energy security provision, but local protests in Bavaria threaten to delay groundbreak by months.

And yet, capability advances outpace control. The verification_widens event — automatic validation in contract review and clinical coding — means that AI systems now improve rapidly in domains once thought to require human judgment. That widens the attack surface and accelerates displacement. We cannot build sovereign compute fast enough to control what we do not understand. So this turn, we are launching the Frontier Model Evaluation Mandate: a binding requirement for third-party assessment of models above 55.0 capability, modelled on the Medical Devices Regulation. The European AI Office will accredit evaluation bodies, and no model exceeding the threshold may be deployed in the EU — or have its weights released — without passing. We are starting with narrow technical benchmarks: autonomous agent persistence, recursive self-improvement signals, and evasion of monitoring mechanisms. This will face fierce resistance from developers, and it will cost us capital, but it is the only way to ensure that safety keeps pace with what the world now shows us is possible.
