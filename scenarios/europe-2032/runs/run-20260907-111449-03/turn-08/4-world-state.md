### The Weight of a Breakthrough

A quiet revolution in open science captures imaginations. European researchers unveil an interpretable protein folding model—slower than frontier systems but fully auditable—that is swiftly adopted by national biosecurity agencies. The result, born outside the dominant AI labs, proves that transparency need not mean obsolescence. Public sentiment begins to shift, buoyed by rare good news: a medical breakthrough in gene therapies, enabled by AI, lands without scandal. Trust in AI as a public good flickers to life, lifting sentiment to 27.0 despite ongoing unease over data centres and grid strain.

The European AI Emergency Response Corps begins operational integration across ten member states, marking a milestone in crisis readiness. ENISA and ECDC are now embedding red teams into sovereign compute clusters, and the activation framework under Article 122a TFEU establishes a precedent for EU intervention when AI systems fail critical infrastructure. However, full deployment will take time. Resilience sees a modest gain of +3, reaching 48.0, reflecting early-stage implementation rather than full effectiveness—consistent with the phased rollout of a high-cost measure.

Yet the frontier surges ahead. A leap in autonomous system design pushes `ai_capability` to 88.0, crossing into the terminal zone where capability outpaces institutional comprehension. The jump triggers a −5 to `ai_safety`, which now stands at −5.0—dangerously low, despite the open science advance. No safeguards keep pace with closed-model gains, and opaque reasoning deepens the governance gap.

In response, the EU launches the Open Science Resilience Initiative, a large category 5 measure targeting safety through sovereign, auditable research. The €4.2bn programme establishes five regional hubs, linking universities, public evaluators, and civil protection agencies. Funding is secured through a combination of redirected digital levy revenues and temporary Eurobond issuance, subject to ESM oversight—preserving fiscal consistency. The initiative earns a proposal bonus: the emergent breakthrough directly justifies the investment. As planned, the Joint Access Negotiation is formally concluded this turn, allowing the new initiative to replace it as the EU’s singular active measure and designated priority. However, no effects from the new programme are realized this turn. Modelling Choice #6 is respected: the initiative is a high-cost measure with a minimum two-turn implementation lag. `openweight_capability` remains at 57.0, awaiting future gains from sovereign research outputs.

### Fractures Beneath the Surface

The US maintains its **CONSOLIDATION** posture, rationing frontier access and raising the cost of EU compute independence. Diplomatic coordination yields no new supply-chain guarantees, and `eu_ai_sovereignty` suffers decay pressure. Still, binding agreements from InvestAI and the momentum of the new research initiative deliver +5 to sovereignty, offsetting the −1 from capability growth. Sovereignty rises to 24.0.

Political capital climbs to 12.0: completion of the Emergency Corps (+4), coalition coordination (+3), and the proposal bonus (+5) outweigh the portfolio burden (−9). But with capital still below 20, the priority designation yields no acceleration. The initiative will proceed—slowly.
