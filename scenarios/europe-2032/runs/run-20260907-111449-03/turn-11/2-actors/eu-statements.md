# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign technical agency through auditable AI

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer accept acting without leverage as the cost of acting at all.
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event and the collapse of ai_safety to 0.0 have revealed that our capacity to act is now structurally constrained not by hesitation, but by dependency. We invoked Article 122a TFEU, issued binding mandates, and activated mutual aid — yet we remain at the mercy of external access decisions. Acting under uncertainty is no longer enough; we must act in a way that changes the balance of power. This refinement does not retreat from the spirit of the commitment but sharpens its aim: we will still move before full clarity, but we will now measure every action by whether it builds leverage, not just legitimacy.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign technical agency through auditable AI, and establish an EU-controlled execution stack capable of running critical operations without reliance on foreign frontier models
  - Grounds: The denial of access was not a temporary block but a structural exposure. At ai_capability 92.0 and eu_ai_sovereignty 20.0, the EU cannot assume continuity of supply for systems it neither controls nor understands. Auditable AI is necessary, but insufficient if it runs on borrowed infrastructure or depends on external model updates. We must now couple interpretability with executional independence: a full stack — from model weights to compute — that the Union can operate, verify, and defend without permission. This is not a new direction, but the necessary depth of the old one.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
