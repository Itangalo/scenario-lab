### Statement changes  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer accept acting without leverage as the cost of acting at all.  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event and the collapse of ai_safety to 0.0 have revealed that our capacity to act is now structurally constrained not by hesitation, but by dependency. We invoked Article 122a TFEU, issued binding mandates, and activated mutual aid — yet we remain at the mercy of external access decisions. Acting under uncertainty is no longer enough; we must act in a way that changes the balance of power. This refinement does not retreat from the spirit of the commitment but sharpens its aim: we will still move before full clarity, but we will now measure every action by whether it builds leverage, not just legitimacy.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign technical agency through auditable AI, and establish an EU-controlled execution stack capable of running critical operations without reliance on foreign frontier models  
- Grounds: The denial of access was not a temporary block but a structural exposure. At ai_capability 92.0 and eu_ai_sovereignty 20.0, the EU cannot assume continuity of supply for systems it neither controls nor understands. Auditable AI is necessary, but insufficient if it runs on borrowed infrastructure or depends on external model updates. We must now couple interpretability with executional independence: a full stack — from model weights to compute — that the Union can operate, verify, and defend without permission. This is not a new direction, but the necessary depth of the old one.

## In practice  
This turn, we are treating the Open Science Resilience Initiative not as a research programme but as the core of an emergency sovereign capability. DG CONNECT, in coordination with the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU), is fast-tracking integration between the three operational research hubs and national biosecurity agencies in Germany, France, and Italy. We are mandating that all AI systems used in critical infrastructure procurement must be interpretable, logged, and capable of offline operation — a de facto exclusion of opaque frontier models unless they meet EU standards. The interim deployment of open protein folding models in 120 hospitals shows that slower, auditable systems can hold the line, but only if they run on infrastructure we control.

The new EU Execution Stack Initiative begins immediately with a Council Regulation under Article 114 TFEU, establishing a legal framework for sovereign AI execution environments. We are repurposing €1.8 billion from the Digital Europe Programme and NextGenerationEU’s resilience window to fund the construction of two new EuroHPC-class data centres on EU soil, legally bound to host only auditable models and operated under EU security certification. The Commission will negotiate binding agreements with ASML, STMicroelectronics, and selected cloud providers to ensure chip supply and energy priority during disruptions. Resistance from member states reliant on foreign cloud infrastructure is expected, particularly from the Baltics and Benelux; we will offer them early access to the stack’s biosecurity and energy forecasting modules in exchange for alignment. This is not about catching the frontier — it is about ensuring that when the frontier turns away, the Union does not fall.
