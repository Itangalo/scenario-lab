## New measure
**EU High-Risk Redress and Oversight Fix**
Independent audit of deployed Annex III systems in welfare, policing and courts with public redress, mandatory log review and meaningful human-oversight standards enforced through the AI Act. This is Transparency and reporting work, because the scandal is an enforcement failure — conformity on paper, forty-seconds-a-case oversight, unread logs — not a gap in the Act's categories.
We choose the first reading deliberately to keep the Act repairable, and we answer it now before courts and ombudsmen turn anxiety into a general block on adoption and siting.

## Priority
EU High-Risk Redress and Oversight Fix, not M1/M2, because unanswered wrongful decisions will collapse the social licence for both sovereign compute and resilience before spring.

## In practice
We task DG CNECT with the AI Office and the Fundamental Rights Agency to open joint enforcement: national market-surveillance authorities suspend the offending deployments, order case-by-case review and compensation via the AI Act Article 99a redress route and national ombudsmen, and publish logs and oversight staffing ratios. The AI Board sets a temporary floor — maximum cases per reviewer per day, four-eyes sign-off, quarterly log-read attestation — under existing high-risk obligations, no new law this turn.

In parallel we hold M3 exercises and M4 host-deal talks on timetable through the Energy Council and ENISA/ACER, but we link them explicitly: no fast-track permit without a clean high-risk audit for municipal AI, and a share of host compute reserved for independent evaluation of welfare models. The university pooling pact is offered sandbox power conditional on the same reporting, to keep replication inside oversight while Washington interim access is maintained unchanged.
