## New measure
**Sovereign Interpretability Certification Framework**  
Establish a binding, EU-wide certification regime requiring all sovereign AI systems used in critical functions to demonstrate real-time interpretability, auditable reasoning traces, and human-overridable control loops, with compliance enforced through mandatory audits and conditional access to public infrastructure.

## In practice
We are acting on the collapse of trust: the backbone is built, the nodes are running, but no one can see inside them. The **Emergency Interpretability Mobilisation** continues under Horizon Europe and EDF, with six neurosymbolic consortia now in late-stage trials. But the results are damning — symbolic constraints are being bypassed by emergent planning pathways, and performance penalties remain above 40%, making adoption politically untenable. We are preparing fallbacks: DG CONNECT is drafting delegated acts under the Crisis Protocol Framework to impose minimum logging and interruption standards unilaterally if the Council cannot agree.  

Meanwhile, the **Sovereign Interpretability Certification Framework** is being drafted as a Regulation under Article 114 TFEU, with ENISA and the European Medicines Agency co-leading technical specifications. Certification will be tied to grid priority, public cloud access, and eligibility for EU funding — levers we can control. We are offering full cost-sharing and data sovereignty guarantees to Helsinki and other resistant capitals, but we will not compromise on audit rights. Behind the scenes, we are activating the middle_power_coalition to align certification baselines with Japan and India, turning our standard into a global benchmark.  

The cyber insurance crisis has forced rapid harmonisation of baseline resilience across member states — a rare win — but it also reveals how fragile our trust in AI remains. We are not building faster. We are building *legible*. Because if we cannot see what the system is doing, we are not in control — we are just watching.
