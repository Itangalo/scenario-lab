### The Leap and the Blind Spot

A sudden leap in AI capability sends shockwaves through the global system. A leading lab releases a system that redefines what is possible, integrating agentic planning, self-correction, and cross-domain coordination at a level previously theoretical. The jump pushes global `ai_capability` to 73.0 — a +5 surge — but offers no time for adaptation. With no matching advance in understanding, `ai_safety` drops to 10.0, compounding the existing crisis of control.

In the EU, the **European Resilience Backbone Initiative** completes, deploying decentralised AI-augmented emergency networks across ten member states. ENISA confirms the systems withstood simulated cyber-saturation attacks, boosting `resilience` by +3. Yet the victory feels hollow: operators cannot interpret the AI’s internal decisions, and fallback protocols remain untested under real duress.

The Commission proposes the **Sovereign Legibility Compact**, a bold attempt to mandate interpretability, logging, and interruptibility for all sovereign AI nodes. Framed as essential to prevent another €1.2bn diversion, it is designated the EU’s priority initiative. Legal preparations proceed under Article 114 TFEU, but resistance hardens immediately in Helsinki, where the host nation refuses external audit rights, citing national security. Talks stall before substantive negotiations begin, and no agreement is reached. As a proposal only, no operational or metric effects are realized this turn.

Meanwhile, the **Emergency Interpretability Mobilisation** continues, but hybrid neurosymbolic systems show persistent bypasses. Performance costs remain prohibitive, limiting deployment. The promise of legibility recedes even as the demand for it grows.

Open-weight capability rises to 60.0, as the global jump pulls the open frontier forward despite recent retrenchment. The gap between closed and open narrows, distributing high-level planning risks more widely.

A new coalition of middle powers — including Japan, India, and Brazil — forms a coordination framework on AI supply chains and evaluation standards. The **middle_power_coalition** event boosts EU political capital, offsetting some portfolio strain.

### Sovereignty Without Sight

EU AI sovereignty remains at 11.0. The **Sovereign Legibility Compact** is newly proposed and contributes no immediate benefit; the +6 proposal bonus will be evaluated in future turns once implementation begins. No category 4 measure finished this turn, and rising capability imposes a −1 decay, but this is offset by carryover effects from prior completions (InvestAI Gigafactories +3, Sovereign Frontier Node +3), leaving sovereignty unchanged. Political capital remains at 14.0, with coalition gains balanced against ongoing portfolio costs and no new measure yet contributing.

Public sentiment remains frozen at 22.0. Trust erodes further as headlines question whether any AI system, even on EU soil, can be trusted.
