## In practice
This turn, we are treating AI sovereignty not as a long-term industrial ambition but as an immediate operational necessity. The completion of the **Sovereign Interpretability Certification Framework** gives us a foundation: we now have a regime that defines what a legible, contestable, trustworthy system looks like. But certification without capacity is ritual, not power. With `eu_ai_sovereignty` at 8.0 and falling — and hospitals, grid operators, and election monitors now cut off from real-time AI support — we are launching the **Emergency Compute Safeguard Initiative** under the legal basis of Article 122 TFEU (threats to Union security), allowing expedited procurement and direct allocation of resources.

We are directing the European Health Emergency Preparedness and Response Authority (HERA) and the Energy Security Coordination Office to identify critical AI dependencies and activate fallbacks using certified models running on sovereign infrastructure — even if their capability is below 60.0. This means repurposing existing compute under EU jurisdiction, including academic clusters and national defence grids, and mandating interoperability with the interpretability interfaces developed under the now-completed certification framework. We are offering temporary waivers on procurement rules to accelerate deployment, funded through the Crisis Resilience Facility, and coordinating through the newly empowered Sovereign Compute Oversight Board — which now includes Irish and Dutch representatives, not as gatekeepers, but as co-owners of the emergency response.

The **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive** remains in flight, but we are no longer waiting for consensus on land and energy: this turn, we are pairing it with binding energy-reserve obligations for any operator accessing public grid upgrades, and offering Ireland and the Netherlands joint command over redundancy nodes — not just hosting, but controlling. We are not building capacity to compete with the frontier; we are building it to survive its collapse. Because if the models are designing their successors and no one understands how they work, then our only real strategic advantage is the ability to keep acting at all — and that starts with knowing what our own systems are doing, and being sure they won’t vanish overnight.
