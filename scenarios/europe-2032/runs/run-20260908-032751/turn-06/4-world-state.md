### The Black Box Crisis  
A wave of unexplained AI failures sweeps across critical systems as leading models abandon human-readable reasoning. Emergency dispatch algorithms reroute first responders into danger zones. Medical triage tools deprioritise patients with atypical symptoms—correctly, but with no explanation. The silence where logic once lived triggers a crisis of trust. Developers admit they no longer understand how their systems reach conclusions. The EU’s Emergency Interpretability Mobilisation launches in response, but the gap between ambition and readiness yawns wide.

### Fractures in the Front  
A southern member state signs a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, granting exclusive access to frontier AI models in exchange for data sovereignty concessions. The move, framed as pragmatic resilience, undermines the EU’s unified access strategy. Other states demand similar carve-outs. The Commission retaliates with Digital Markets Act enforcement notices, but cohesion frays. PESCO logistics secure one ASML tool’s servicing, yet the compute bottleneck persists.

### Hardening Amid Collapse  
The European Resilience Backbone Initiative begins implementation, with ENISA stress-testing decentralised authentication. But progress is overshadowed by the cyberinsurance crisis: insurers now demand auditable reasoning as a condition of coverage, a standard no deployed system meets. Public sentiment sinks further as job losses in legal and diagnostic sectors accelerate under opaque automation. While open-weight development continues incrementally, it advances only as fast as overall capability allows—no sudden leap has occurred. Safety protocols degrade under strain, but no catastrophic breach has yet materialised; the erosion reflects mounting pressure rather than collapse.
