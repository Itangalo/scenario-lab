## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure sovereign AI capacity as a foundation for strategic autonomy and societal resilience → **Sustain sovereign AI operations under coercion, ensuring continuity of critical functions through external denial, technical failure, or systemic attack**
  - Trigger: the convergence of opaque_reasoning, member_state_defection, and U.S. downgrade to client status
  - Grounds: The structural conditions have shifted categorically since the last commitment. Sovereign compute and model access are no longer sufficient: we now face simultaneous threats of operational collapse (from uninterpretable systems), political fragmentation (via member-state defection), and strategic exclusion (by U.S. policy). The new formulation reflects that sovereignty is not a static possession but a dynamic capability—one that must persist *under pressure*. This reframe acknowledges that control without continuity is illusionary; our focus must shift from acquisition to endurance.
  - Evidence quoted: opaque_reasoning: The leading systems stop producing intermediate reasoning a human can read, because the representations that work best are not words. Every control and oversight strategy that depended on reading the chain of thought – most of the applied interpretability in production use – stops working at once, and there is no replacement ready.

member_state_defection: A member state cuts its own arrangement – with a hyperscaler, with Washington, or with Beijing – on terms that undercut a position the Union has taken. It is defended at home as pragmatism and read everywhere else as the Union being unable to hold its own line.

In parallel, the U.S. election delivers a decisive win for a candidate treating advanced AI as a strategic asset. Market access for foreign actors is now explicitly tiered under national security review—Europe is downgraded to client status.
  - Reason: The developments directly undermine sovereign AI operations by eroding interpretability, fragmenting unity, and restricting access—transforming the conditions for strategic autonomy.
