The new formulation reflects that sovereignty is not a static possession but a dynamic capability—one that must persist *under pressure*. This reframe acknowledges that control without continuity is illusionary; our focus must shift from acquisition to endurance.

## In practice
We are acting on three fronts: defending continuity, restoring legibility, and holding cohesion. On the sovereign node front, we are finalising legal protocols under Article 36 TFEU to establish irrevocable access rights for EU institutions to the deployed models, treating them as critical infrastructure akin to satellite navigation or payment clearing. We have invoked the Critical Technologies Act to compel cooperation from hyperscalers still negotiating in good faith, while preparing targeted sanctions under the Digital Markets Act against those who delay in bad faith. The ASML servicing backlog remains a chokepoint; we are deploying military logistics units under PESCO to secure alternative transport and maintenance channels, treating lithography tools as strategic assets under Article 42(7).

Simultaneously, we are launching the Emergency Interpretability Mobilisation through a joint call from Horizon Europe and the European Defence Fund, prioritising consortia that integrate neurosymbolic architectures with formal verification. The first wave targets diagnostic chains in HERA’s pandemic forecasting and ENISA’s intrusion attribution systems—domains where failure is catastrophic and explanation non-negotiable. We are fast-tracking ethical waivers and procurement exceptions for these pilots, knowing that public trust hinges on being able to say *why* a system acted, not just that it worked.

The Resilience Backbone moves into implementation this turn: DG ECHO has convened civil protection directors from ten member states to map fallback command hierarchies, while ENISA begins stress-testing decentralised authentication protocols. But the member_state_defection has fractured alignment; we are offering bilateral grid and data-sharing guarantees to wavering states, conditioned on their withdrawal from extraterritorial cloud arrangements. This is not ideal—it risks creating tiers within the Union—but it is the price of preventing total fragmentation. Behind all of this, we are drafting a communication strategy centred on “resilient sovereignty”: not isolation, but the ability to endure and act when others would cut us off. The next six months will test whether that idea still commands enough legitimacy to hold.
