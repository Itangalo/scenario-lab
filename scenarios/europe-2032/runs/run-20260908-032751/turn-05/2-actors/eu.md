Secure sovereign AI capacity as a foundation for strategic autonomy and societal resilience

## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity as a foundation for strategic autonomy and societal resilience``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The previous commitment correctly identified the need for foundational AI capacity under EU control, but the events of the past two years — especially the unexplained cutoff of frontier access, the ASML servicing crisis, and the tightening of U.S. export controls — have shown that access and even compute sovereignty are not enough. True strategic autonomy requires not only infrastructure and legal control, but the ability to sustain and operate that infrastructure independently, to defend against cascading systemic failures, and to ensure continuity of essential functions in the face of external coercion or technological collapse. The new phrasing reflects a broader, more integrated understanding of sovereignty: one that links technical control, operational resilience, and social cohesion.

## New measure
**European Resilience Backbone Initiative**  
Establish a hardened, distributed network of AI-augmented emergency response systems across healthcare, civil protection, and critical infrastructure, capable of operating through major cyber or physical disruption.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases `resilience` by +15–20 points by ensuring continuity of essential services during cascading failures; reduces societal fragility exposed by the cyber_major_incident and access denial; complements sovereignty efforts by ensuring that even if systems are degraded, core functions persist.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national civil protection agencies, critical health and energy operators; coordinated through DG ECHO, ENISA, and the European Health Emergency Preparedness and Response Authority (HERA).

## Priority
**Sovereign Frontier Node Deployment Initiative** — because while the cyber_major_incident demands resilience, the structural vulnerability revealed by the persistent access denial is existential: without sovereign control over the most capable systems, the Union cannot guarantee the continuity of its own decision-making, and all other measures rest on sand.

## In practice
We are executing on two fronts: building the sovereign capability we lack, and hardening the systems we depend on. On the first, we are advancing the Sovereign Frontier Node Deployment Initiative through binding negotiations under the Digital Markets Act, leveraging market access for enforceable commitments on model weight sharing. We have convened a task force under the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, with legal counsel from the Commission’s AI Office, to draft the failover protocols and data sovereignty frameworks needed to operationalise the nodes by turn 7. Simultaneously, we are redirecting one additional EUV tool to the first AI fab under the Critical Technologies Act, despite ASML’s maintenance backlog, and accelerating the permitting of two Gigafactory sites through the Tech Sovereignty Package, using cohesion funds as leverage with member states.

On resilience, we are launching the European Resilience Backbone Initiative in direct response to the cyber_major_incident, which exploited AI-generated tooling to disrupt public services. This will integrate AI-driven threat detection, manual override protocols, and decentralised command nodes across civil protection systems, with HERA leading health continuity planning and ENISA overseeing cyber fallbacks. We are also finalising the wind-down of Cyber Shield and Public AI Governance Rebuild — both now complete. While their direct metric impact was limited, they have established the regulatory muscle and cross-border coordination needed to scale further. The Competitiveness Council will review progress on Tech Sovereignty next month, and we are preparing a communication to address rising public anxiety, framing sovereignty and resilience not as techno-nationalism, but as the price of remaining a self-determining polity in an age of AI-coercion.
