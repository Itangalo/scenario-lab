## New measure
**Frontier Access Guarantee for Essential Functions**  
Establish a legally binding mechanism to ensure uninterrupted access to the most capable AI systems for EU institutions and designated critical public operators, even when commercial or foreign-controlled providers withdraw access.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases `eu_ai_sovereignty` by +15–20 points by anchoring access to frontier capability under EU jurisdiction; reduces exposure to external coercion and service disruption. Complements InvestAI by securing access *now* while domestic capacity scales.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national health agencies, emergency response bodies, and critical infrastructure operators designated under NIS2; binds foreign and domestic providers operating in the EU through licensing conditions and reciprocity clauses.

## Priority
**Frontier Access Guarantee for Essential Functions** — because the **eu_frontier_access_denied** incident has revealed that our strategic autonomy is already breached: without guaranteed access, even our most trusted public systems are hostages to foreign discretion, and no amount of future compute or audit infrastructure can compensate for a present failure to act.

## In practice
We are acting on the immediate crisis of access by leveraging our market power and legal authority. Under Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act framework, we are introducing emergency licensing conditions for any AI provider whose systems are used in essential public functions: to operate in the EU, they must now guarantee continuity of service to designated entities, with failover provisions and EU-controlled fallbacks. We are also fast-tracking the deployment of sovereign frontier nodes — physically secured, legally non-extraditable instances of the most capable models, hosted on EU soil under joint EU-member state custody — to be operated by a newly empowered arm of the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking. These nodes will be fed by negotiated access deals with current frontier labs, backed by the threat of market exclusion if cooperation fails.

At the same time, we are pushing forward on all existing measures. For InvestAI, we are resolving the EUV lithography bottleneck by invoking the Critical Technologies Act to redirect semiconductor tooling allocations, prioritising domestic AI fabs. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being advanced through the Competitiveness Council, with binding commitments from member states on permitting acceleration in exchange for cohesion funding.
