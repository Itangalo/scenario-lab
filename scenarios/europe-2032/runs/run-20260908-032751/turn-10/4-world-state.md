### A Turn of Hard Realities

Global AI capability advances to 78.0, driven by self-improving training loops now standard at frontier labs. Yet interpretability lags further behind: developers report systems making strategic decisions through opaque pathways, and internal audits fail to reconstruct reasoning chains. With no safety breakthrough and capability surging, `ai_safety` falls to 1.0.

The EU debates a major response: the **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive**, a proposed large category 4 measure aimed at anchoring compute under EU jurisdiction. Invoking Article 122 TFEU, Brussels prepares to fast-track permitting and offer 70% co-financing. However, the proposal triggers immediate resistance. Ireland and the Netherlands warn that land-use mandates could violate national sovereignty and distort regional investment. Legal scrutiny mounts, and no physical construction begins this turn. Given the scale and cost, the measure cannot produce momentum within six months — no implementation effect is registered.

The **Emergency Interpretability Mobilisation** officially ends — without results. No hybrid system met deployment thresholds, and the failure deepens distrust. The **Sovereign Interpretability Certification Framework** remains active, continuing to incur a portfolio charge. The **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive** is formally introduced this turn as a new measure, displacing no prior initiative. As allowed by Constitutional Constraint 5, only one new measure is introduced, and the portfolio now reflects two active measures carried over or launched: the ongoing **Sovereign Interpretability Certification Framework** and the newly adopted **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive**.

Political capital erodes. The portfolio charge is now: Sovereign Interpretability Certification Framework −3, Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive −3, priority −1 = −7. No implementation momentum is achieved on either measure, and no proposal bonus is earned. Public sentiment holds at 22.0, offering no relief. Political capital falls to 10.0.

Resilience dips to 46.0 as cyber readiness gaps widen. The **emergent_deepfake_election_wave** intensifies, with coordinated synthetic media targeting elections in France and Poland, overwhelming detection systems.

Meanwhile, the **emergent_cyber_insurance_crisis**, now in its fifth consecutive turn, exerts mounting pressure. Insurers continue to withhold coverage from critical public services unless stringent AI security standards are met, exposing readiness disparities across member states. Emergency coordination yields minimal progress on harmonizing baselines, as larger economies resist external audits while smaller ones lack capacity. The crisis festers, accelerating informal fragmentation in cyber resilience policy.

### Diplomacy Amid Decline

The **middle_power_coalition** secures mutual recognition with Japan and India on certification, offering long-term leverage. But immediate gains are limited, and the moment favours action, not alignment.

Open-weight capability sees modest progress, rising to 63.5, as research collectives integrate new evaluation tooling and adopt safer distillation techniques from leaked frontier models. However, the pace remains constrained by limited access to frontier-scale training runs and ongoing safety filtering. The gap with frontier capability holds at 14.5, reflecting the structural lag of open systems during a phase of proprietary acceleration.
