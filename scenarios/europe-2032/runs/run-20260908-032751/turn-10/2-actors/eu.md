## In practice
We are acting on the collapse of leverage. The **Sovereign Interpretability Certification Framework** remains in negotiation, but its credibility hinges on a simple fact: if we cannot run certified systems on our own soil, the framework is a ritual, not a regime. The ASML export cuts have made that clear — we are not just behind in capability, we are being governed by others’ chokepoints. So this turn, we pivot from standard-setting to soil.

The **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive** will be proposed under Article 122 TFEU for crisis response, bypassing the slow consensus machinery of energy and digital policy. It grants emergency fast-tracking rights to data centres that commit to hosting sovereign AI systems with real-time audit interfaces, tied to the certification framework. We are offering 70% co-financing from the Crisis Resilience Facility and guaranteeing grid access through binding energy allocation orders. Member states will be required to designate at least one sovereign compute zone per capita above 5 million, with penalties for obstruction under cohesion policy rules.

Resistance will come from capitals used to treating data centres as tax plays, not strategic assets. We are already hearing murmurs from Ireland and the Netherlands about sovereignty over land use. But we have leverage: the middle_power_coalition is now aligned on mutual compute access — Japan and India have agreed to recognise our certification as equivalent to their own, and in return, we will prioritise joint research access on the condition they fast-track EU-backed facilities on their soil. This is not just about building faster. It is about building *here*, so that when the next crisis hits, we are not begging for access — we are deciding who gets it.
