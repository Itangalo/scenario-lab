## New measure
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)**  
Establish mandatory intrusion detection, red-teaming and incident response protocols across energy, water and transport operators, with cross-border coordination via ENISA and binding enforcement.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases `resilience` by +15–20 points and reduces cascade risk from AI-enabled attacks; complements existing national efforts with Union-wide standards and mutual aid triggers.  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators and national regulators.

## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)** — this must be our priority because the *cyber_test_shot* event revealed systemic blind spots in detecting advanced persistent threats, and with open-weight offensive capabilities now distributed, we cannot rely on frontier controls alone to protect essential services.

## In practice
We are activating Article 13a of the NIS2 Directive to mandate baseline detection and logging standards for all designated essential entities in energy, water, and port operations by Q1 2027. This will be enforced through national CSIRTs but coordinated at Union level via ENISA, which we are resourcing to run biannual red-team exercises using simulated AI-driven attack patterns. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with conditionality tied to national reform plans. Resistance is expected from smaller member states citing cost and sovereignty concerns, so we are offering a 70% co-financing rate and embedding technical teams directly into their agencies to build capacity without duplication.

At the same time, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by convening a summit of energy ministers and grid operators in December to align site selection with renewable expansion zones — delaying no longer on the electricity bottleneck. The Tech sovereignty package is being reoriented to include fast-track environmental assessments specifically for AI infrastructure, leveraging the crisis framework established during the energy transition. Industry pushback on permitting speed has been neutralized by framing it as strategic insurance: we are not accelerating for growth, but for survival.
