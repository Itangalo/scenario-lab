### Frontier Acceleration Continues
Global AI capability advances by +2.5 this turn, reaching 62.0, as autonomous agents refine recursive goal-setting and self-debugging in complex engineering simulations. These systems now initiate multi-week research cycles in logistics and materials science, marking a clear shift toward end-to-end project execution. Open-weight models rise to 50.0, buoyed by distributed fine-tuning of Kimi K3, though no open release matches the closed frontier.

### Access Denial Deepens Crisis
The unexplained cutoff of frontier AI access to EU public institutions persists, now affecting emergency response coordination and national health diagnostics. With no resolution, the incident hardens perceptions of strategic vulnerability. Public sentiment slips further, as media spotlight job losses in public-sector AI support roles and growing reliance on opaque foreign systems.

### EU Shifts from Access to Ownership
In response, the EU launches the **Sovereign Frontier Node Deployment Initiative**, a large Category 4 measure aimed at hosting non-extraditable frontier models on EU soil under failover authority. Framed as a strategic necessity, it becomes the named priority. However, political capital remains strained: four large measures are still in flight, and the new initiative adds to the burden.

Negotiations with leading labs begin under Digital Markets Act leverage, but early signals are mixed. One provider conditionally agrees to weight-sharing for a high-cost fee; two others cite national security policies and refuse. Meanwhile, the first EUV tool is redirected to an AI fab, but ASML reports worsening maintenance delays, slowing deployment timelines.

Cyber Shield and Public AI Governance Rebuild near completion but yield no immediate metric gains. Resilience dips slightly as delays accumulate. Ai_safety falls to 20.0, outpaced by capability growth and lacking enforceable safeguards. The export control tightening further limits compute access, and despite informal coordination among middle powers, no breakthrough emerges.

Public pushback grows, with regional protests against AI-driven displacement and data centre expansions. The EU’s move toward sovereign nodes is welcomed by some governments but criticized as technologically overambitious and politically divisive.
