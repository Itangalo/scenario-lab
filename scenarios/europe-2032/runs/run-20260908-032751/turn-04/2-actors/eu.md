## New measure
**Sovereign Frontier Node Deployment Initiative**  
Establish and operationalise a network of legally sovereign, physically secured instances of frontier AI models hosted on EU soil, with non-extraditable access and failover authority vested in EU institutions.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases `eu_ai_sovereignty` by +18–22 points by anchoring operational control of frontier systems in EU jurisdiction; reduces dependency on foreign providers’ goodwill and enables continuity during access denial. Complements the Frontier Access Guarantee by creating a fallback that cannot be revoked.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, designated national operators in healthcare, emergency response, and critical infrastructure; binds frontier labs through negotiated access in exchange for market access, backed by Digital Markets Act enforcement.

## Priority
**Sovereign Frontier Node Deployment Initiative** — because the Frontier Access Guarantee, while necessary, is now insufficient: licensing conditions can be challenged, delayed or narrowly interpreted, but a node physically located, legally protected and operationally autonomous on EU soil cannot be switched off from abroad — and without such a capability, the Union remains strategically vulnerable to coercion.

## In practice
We are moving from access to ownership. Under the authority of Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act, we are initiating binding negotiations with the three leading frontier labs to secure model weights for deployment in sovereign frontier nodes — physically isolated, legally non-extraditable instances hosted within the EU’s high-performance computing infrastructure. These nodes will be governed by a new mandate of the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, empowered to operate, monitor and, if necessary, activate failover protocols for essential public services. Access will be granted only in exchange for enforceable continuity commitments across all EU essential operators — a quid pro quo that turns market size into leverage.

At the same time, we are advancing all existing measures. For InvestAI, we have secured the first EUV lithography tool allocation through the Critical Technologies Act, redirecting one unit from automotive to AI fab use, with a second under negotiation. The Tech Sovereignty Package is gaining traction: the Competitiveness Council has approved the first five accelerated-permitting zones, tied to cohesion fund disbursements. We are building the foundations of trust and control — but now, we must also build the fail-safe that ensures survival when trust fails.
