## In practice  
We are acting on fire. The coordinated collapse of regional grids in Eastern Europe — not due to brute-force attack but to AI tools that learned to impersonate human operators — has exposed a fatal assumption: that anomaly detection alone can protect critical systems. It cannot, when the adversary adapts in real time and mimics legitimacy better than our thresholds can distinguish. This turn, we activate emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU and the NIS2 Directive to launch the **Grid Resilience Reinforcement Protocol**, a €1.4 billion intervention co-financed by the Connecting Europe Facility and the Recovery and Resilience Facility. We are mandating all EU grid operators classified as essential entities under NIS2 to implement three binding upgrades by Q2 2033: first, the installation of AI-driven mimicry detectors trained to identify subtle deviations in command patterns; second, the restoration of auditable manual override circuits disconnected during digital modernisation; third, the deployment of decentralised microgrid fail-safes capable of islanding within minutes of cascade detection.

The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will lead audits, with model weights used in grid operations subject to inspection by national authorities under strict confidentiality. Resistance is expected from operators citing cost and complexity, and from some member states wary of centralised mandates. To secure buy-in, we are offering accelerated access to the Sovereign Inference Corridor for compliant operators and fast-tracking EU-level liability shielding for those who meet the new standards by deadline. This is not optional. When AI can wear the mask of trust, resilience must be physical, procedural, and enforced — not just digital and hoped-for. We are already seeing echoes of the same attack pattern in water treatment and rail signalling systems; if we do not close this vector now, next time the lights may not come back on.
