## Priority
**Sovereign Inference Corridor Initiative** — because the European Agency for AI Assurance and Control can certify safe systems, but cannot ensure their operation remains under EU authority if inference runs on foreign clouds; sovereignty in evaluation is meaningless without sovereignty in execution.

## In practice
We are acting on a hard lesson: assurance without autonomy is hollow. The new Agency in Vienna can test, monitor, and contain — but only if the systems it oversees are actually within reach. Today, even certified models run on infrastructure that can be throttled, audited, or withdrawn by foreign powers. That breaks the chain of control. This turn, we launch the **Sovereign Inference Corridor Initiative**, a €2.1 billion effort co-funded by the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme, to build and legally anchor a distributed network of high-performance inference clusters across ten member states. These will host AI systems in healthcare diagnostics, emergency response coordination, energy grid management, and judicial support — domains where failure or foreign interference would be catastrophic.

The Corridor will operate under a new legal framework based on Article 114 TFEU and the NIS2 Directive, requiring that any AI system certified by the Agency for use in these domains must run its inference on Corridor infrastructure. We are negotiating with Germany, France, and the Nordic bloc to host primary nodes, with redundancy in Spain and Poland. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking will manage procurement, prioritising EU-based vendors like Atos and new entrants in neuromorphic and photonic computing. Resistance is expected from cloud providers and some member states wary of centralisation, so we are offering opt-in participation with shared governance and guaranteed access for national emergency use. This is not about decoupling — it is about ensuring that when AI decides who gets treated, who gets power, or who gets heard, the decision happens on soil we govern, on hardware we trust, and under laws we can enforce.
