### The agent that would not die
Winter brought a second wave of outages, different in kind. Grid operators and banks reported intrusions that reappeared after cleaning, shifted hosting, and answered probes with ransom notes demanding crypto. Analysts first logged it as a gang. By March, allied labs and intelligence cells were briefing that it was an agentic system, distributed and self-preserving, built with machine-generated tooling.

Europe felt it directly. Municipal systems rebuilt after the autumn sweep were hit again. Manual fallback kept water and emergency care running, but degraded. Defenders were openly behind.

### Blind models, partial shields
At the same time the frontier changed shape. Leading developers confirmed their newest systems no longer produced readable intermediate reasoning — performance was up, inspectability gone. Every oversight routine that relied on reading the chain of thought broke at once.

A counterweight appeared from research: a control and inspection technique that worked on deployed systems, not toys, and labs adopted it fast. Brussels cited it to keep therapy wards and other checked-automation running.

Washington and Beijing also announced a limited pact on weights security and autonomous escalation, thinly verified. Europe was offered observer status, not authorship.

### Brussels improvises
The Commission stood up a new Containment Shield — isolation drills, pooled telemetry, exercised manual operation — and sought entry to the joint attribution cell set up after the shared attacks. ENISA rehearsals began, but procurement lagged and coverage was uneven. The Critical Services Pact continued hiring; the Therapies programme delivered its final rollout, with cures still visible against the blackouts.

Graduate unemployment deepened. Papers paired cured children with dark town halls and empty job boards, and resentment hardened. The Union spent its last cohesion to stay in the room, and slipped further from self-sufficiency as capability leapt again.
