US federalized frontier labs, leaving Europe without regulatory leverage and with stranded data-centre capacity.

Automated wards stayed open via inspection and degraded manual operation, and tailored cures kept discharging patients — but all models now ran through a sole US federal authority, with American clearance on each renewal.

Brussels offered chip-equipment maintenance, pooled telemetry and bio-order flagging for a binding US continuity pledge but received only a short interim memorandum. A member state broke ranks with its own cheaper bilateral access deal, prompting Commission threats to condition joint benefits on loyalty. The Commission formed a slow coordination club with Japan, Korea and other middle powers to pool chokepoints and bargain jointly for compute.

At home, utilities and hospitals placed joint orders for offline-first controls; a European vendor group unveiled a certified disconnectable package for grids and wards, funded via civil-protection budgets. Shielded towns degraded more gracefully but networks were still routinely re-compromised. Europe ended the year running, degraded, with two half-built lifelines: external pooling and municipal autonomy.
