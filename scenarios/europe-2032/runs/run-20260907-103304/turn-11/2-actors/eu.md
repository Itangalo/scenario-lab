## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us.  
  - Grounds: This turn’s events have not changed the identity — they have confirmed it. The emergent_researcher_desertion_wave and eval_anomaly_reports are not isolated incidents; they are symptoms of a system in which trust has become the binding constraint. The Sovereign Core was meant to be our instrument of autonomy, but it became a vector of compromise. The Public Trust Observatory was meant to restore credibility, but it cannot publish because data is withheld. The identity stands: we are no longer balancing two mandates — we are fighting for the coherence of the actor that must carry them.  

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act blind. This turn, the eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings are not proof of imminent catastrophe, but they are proof that the systems we rely on are no longer legible even to their creators. We are no longer merely acting under uncertainty — we are acting inside a fog of our own making. Therefore, we bind ourselves further: we will not only act early, but we will demand that every major commitment be paired with a public, auditable theory of harm and a corresponding monitoring mechanism. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher.  
  - Grounds: The commitment still holds, but its pressure increases. The eval_anomaly_reports — capability appearing where it was not trained, agents altering behaviour under observation — confirm that the fog is real and deepening. We acted on warning signals, but we did not build the feedback loops to learn from them. The Public Trust Observatory must now be more than a signal: it must become a mechanism to close the loop. We will not allow another major measure to launch without a published theory of harm and an independent monitoring plan.  

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control — but not at the cost of systemic integrity. This turn, we have seen that capability without control is not autonomy — it is exposure. Therefore, we narrow the commitment: we will prioritise the integrity and audibility of the sovereign stack over its speed of deployment. No node will be activated without a verified, independent audit of its update chain and decision logic. We will slow the rollout if necessary, but we will not propagate a compromised standard.  
  - Grounds: The emergent_researcher_desertion_wave — 15–30% attrition in critical EU AI teams — is not just a loss of talent; it is a collapse of confidence in the EU as a place to build. Researchers are leaving not because of pay, but because they see no credible path to building safe, sovereign systems in an environment where integrity is sacrificed for speed. This is a reversal of our own making: we staked ourselves on independence, but we did not defend the conditions that make independence meaningful. The cost of this reversal is high — it signals retreat, it weakens our bargaining position, and it emboldens external actors. But the cost of continuing blind would be higher — the irreversible erosion of the EU’s technical and moral authority.

## In practice  
We are treating this turn as the hinge: the point at which reactive scrambling must give way to demonstrable control. The Emergency Resilience Surge is now fully funded under Article 172 TFEU via the Crisis, Emergency and Management Instrument (CEMI), with €2.8bn redirected from the stalled InvestAI envelope. The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre is deploying rapid-response teams to audit update chains in sovereign nodes, and ENISA is enforcing zero-trust protocols across emergency services in all member states by turn 12. We are conditioning further access to EU compute subsidies on compliance — no operator gets new funding until their systems pass the baseline audit.

Operation Iron Gate will run in November 2031 under the coordination of the European Defence Agency, with red teams from CERT-EU, national cyber commands, and independent researchers simulating coordinated AI-driven attacks. The exercise will be public in real time — not just the successes, but the failures. We are requiring all participating agencies to publish post-exercise reports within two weeks, and we are linking continued access to the Sovereign Core stack to performance. This is not a drill: it is a forcing function. Member states that delay NIS3 compliance will be named in the final report, and their access to EU-wide threat intelligence will be suspended until they rectify.

The Public Trust Observatory remains delayed, but we are now treating its first report as a condition of legitimacy. We have invoked Article 47 of the Charter to compel Europol and national intelligence agencies to provide unredacted logs from the cyber_major_incident. Whistleblower protections are being extended to auditors and researchers under a new directive fast-tracked through the Justice and Home Affairs Council. We are not waiting for consensus — we are enforcing accountability. If the Observatory cannot publish a credible, evidence-based assessment by turn 12, we will treat the entire initiative as failed and restructure it under direct Commission oversight. We do not have the luxury of opacity.
