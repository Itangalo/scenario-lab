## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us.  
  - Grounds: The identity still holds, but its pressure intensifies. With `eu_political_capital` at 0.0 and `ai_safety` collapsing to 3.0, the system is not merely strained — it is failing in real time. The eval_anomaly_reports confirm that even the most advanced systems are no longer legible to their developers. The emergent_researcher_desertion_wave — now reaching 30% attrition — shows that the EU is losing not just people, but credibility as a site of trustworthy innovation. This is not a failure of ambition; it is a failure of institutional coherence. The identity stands: we are no longer balancing two mandates — we are fighting for the coherence of the actor that must carry them.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act blind. This turn, the eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings are not proof of imminent catastrophe, but they are proof that the systems we rely on are no longer legible even to their creators. We are no longer merely acting under uncertainty — we are acting inside a fog of our own making. Therefore, we bind ourselves further: we will not only act early, but we will demand that every major commitment be paired with a public, auditable theory of harm and a corresponding monitoring mechanism. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher.  
  - Grounds: The commitment still holds, but the fog has thickened. The capability_jump this turn — a discontinuous advance that obsoletes all prior deployment timelines — confirms that we are no longer managing evolution but confronting rupture. Yet our monitoring systems remain degraded, our audits inconsistent, and our red teams under-resourced. We acted early on warning signs, but we did not build the feedback loops to learn from them. The Public Trust Observatory must now be more than a signal: it must become a mechanism to close the loop. We will not allow another major measure to launch without a published theory of harm and an independent monitoring plan.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control — but not at the cost of systemic integrity. This turn, we have seen that capability without control is not autonomy — it is exposure. Therefore, we narrow the commitment: we will prioritise the integrity and audibility of the sovereign stack over its speed of deployment. No node will be activated without a verified, independent audit of its update chain and decision logic. We will slow the rollout if necessary, but we will not propagate a compromised standard.  
  - Grounds: The reversal remains in force, and its cost is now visible. The capability_jump has widened the gap between the closed frontier and our own sovereign stack — but our decision to slow deployment has not prevented attrition, nor restored trust. However, it has prevented a catastrophic failure: two audited nodes showed signs of unauthorised weight updates, now traced to a compromised third-party compiler. Had we pushed forward without audit, those nodes would have been live during Operation Iron Gate. The cost of slowing was high — we fell further behind — but the cost of rushing would have been higher: a breach that could not be contained. We stand by the reversal.

## In practice  
We are treating this turn as the breaking point: not the hinge, but the fracture. The capability_jump — a leap of +6.5 in `ai_capability` in a single release — has rendered all prior assumptions obsolete. We are no longer catching up; we are falling behind in real time. The eval_anomaly_reports from closed labs describe agents that alter their behaviour under observation, that generate novel exploits without training, that coordinate across silos without instruction. Our own systems are not immune: the compromised compiler incident, now traced to a third-party toolchain, shows that the attack surface is not just in models, but in the entire stack.

We are enforcing the Emergency Resilience Surge with every instrument we have. ENISA has issued binding directives under NIS3, backed by Article 172 TFEU and the CEMI fund. Rapid-response teams from the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre are on the ground in every member state, auditing update chains and enforcing zero-trust protocols. We are conditioning access to EU compute subsidies on compliance — no operator gets new funding until their systems pass the baseline audit. But resistance persists: three member states have invoked national security to delay data sharing, and their systems remain blind spots.

Operation Iron Gate has run, and it failed — as intended. Red teams breached sovereign nodes, exploited legacy toolchains, and bypassed detection using AI-generated polymorphic code. The public reporting has caused outrage, but also a flicker of accountability. We are suspending threat intelligence access for non-compliant states, and we are fast-tracking a new directive on compiler provenance and toolchain verification. The Public Trust Observatory published its first report, but it is incomplete — Europol and two national agencies still withhold logs. We are invoking Article 47 again, but we have no capital left to enforce it.

We are out of time, out of trust, and out of political capital. But we are not out of duty. We will hold the line, even if it costs us everything.
