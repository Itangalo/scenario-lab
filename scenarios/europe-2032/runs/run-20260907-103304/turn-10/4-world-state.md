### The Breach That Changed Everything

A sophisticated, AI-generated cyberattack sweeps across European public services, exploiting update mechanisms in recently deployed sovereign nodes. The breach bypasses traditional defences, revealing deep flaws in assumed security. Though the Sovereign Core was meant to ensure autonomy, its rapid deployment left critical update chains unverified. The attack is not catastrophic, but it is deeply embarrassing — a wake-up call that autonomy without integrity is vulnerability.

In response, the EU halts further rollout of the Sovereign Core pending independent audits. The Emergency Resilience Surge is launched as the new priority, redirecting €2.8bn from stalled AI investment into zero-trust upgrades for emergency services, energy, and health systems. This measure supersedes any other new initiative for the turn. Planning begins for a future pan-EU cyber defence exercise, but Operation Iron Gate is not yet formally announced, respecting the constraint of one new measure per turn.

The NIS3 mutual assistance clause is invoked, mandating real-time threat sharing. However, implementation is patchy — some member states delay compliance, and intelligence agencies resist full data integration. The Public Trust Observatory, now operational in interim form, struggles to gain credibility. Its first report — on the cyberattack and bio-risk findings — is delayed to turn 11 due to incomplete data access.

Public sentiment holds at 28.0, reflecting ongoing distrust after the French benefits scandal and now this breach. Political capital remains at 1.0, as actions are seen as reactive rather than decisive.

AI capability rises to 85.0, driven by recursive self-improvement now operating beyond human interpretability. Openweight capability increases to 71.0, as frontier models continue to leak into distributed channels despite restrictions. However, AI safety does not collapse entirely. While the breach exposes critical gaps and confidence erodes, core safety institutions remain intact and no formal dismantling occurs. Some protocols are deprioritized under emergency funding shifts, but not abandoned. Thus, ai_safety declines moderately to 5.0, reflecting erosion rather than elimination.

Sovereignty dips to 12.0. The halted rollout, combined with capability outpacing build, erodes gains. The US posture remains retrenchment, offering no relief.

### A Fragile Response

The EU acts with urgency, but not with strength. One measure is launched with focus — the Emergency Resilience Surge — but broader coordination falters. The system is reacting — but not yet regaining control.
