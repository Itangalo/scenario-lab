## Priority

## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: patching the body, and restoring its sense of self. The Cyber Resilience Surge is now fully operational, with ENISA coordinating rapid-response teams in nine member states. Funding has been redirected from the Digital Europe Programme — €780 million unlocked so far — and the first air-gapped backup systems for voter registries and emergency dispatch are being installed in Germany, Spain, and Poland. Italy’s resistance has not broken, but we have secured a provisional agreement: they will accept technical support under the NIS2 Directive’s mutual assistance clause, though they refuse binding drills. The legal basis is holding, but the political cost is rising — every emergency measure chips at legitimacy if not matched with visible inclusion.

Meanwhile, the annulled election and synthetic media campaigns have made clear that resilience is not only technical but cognitive. That is why we are launching the Critical Information Integrity Shield: not as a replacement, but as the next layer. This is not about controlling speech, but about anchoring truth in infrastructure. We are deploying sovereign verification stacks — EU-developed, open-audit, interoperable with national systems — that use narrow AI to flag synthetic content, trace media provenance via watermarking and blockchain-secured logs, and issue public trust tags visible in news feeds and official alerts. The first pilots go live in France and Finland next month, funded from the remaining €420 million of the surge allocation, with full rollout by turn 15. Public broadcasters are on board; social media platforms are not, but we are invoking Article 36 of the Digital Services Act to require display of verification status at point of amplification. This is not perfect, but it is something the public can see, touch, and trust — and in the war for perception, that is half the battle.
