In 2031, amid ongoing AI-driven cyber threats and election interference, the EU advances its AI capability to 73.5 and open-weight models to 61.0, enabling prolonged autonomous cyber operations. Resilience rises to 56.0 as the Autonomous System Boundary Trials and Critical Function Sentinel Deployment are completed, enhancing monitoring in critical sectors, while the Cyber Resilience Surge is fully deployed across nine member states—though Italy's participation remains non-binding. To counter information warfare, the EU launches the Critical Information Integrity Shield, piloted in France and Finland, boosting public sentiment to 30.0. However, political capital declines to 38.0 under the strain of managing multiple large initiatives, and EU AI sovereignty drops to 32.5 due to capability growth outpacing safety and cohesion.
