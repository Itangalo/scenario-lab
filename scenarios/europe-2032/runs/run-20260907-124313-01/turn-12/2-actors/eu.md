**Cyber Resilience Surge for Critical Public Services** — because the cyber_major_incident has already landed, public trust is collapsing, and the annulled election proves that technical containment alone cannot hold the line: we must now visibly defend the state itself, or lose the mandate to act at all.

## In practice  
We are responding to fire with structure. The cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across municipal services in three member states, enabled by AI-generated zero-days and credential harvesting — has exposed how thin our defences remain despite advances in edge computing and monitoring. The attack did not target AI systems but used them as tools, and it succeeded not through superintelligence but through speed, scale, and evasion that overwhelmed human teams. In parallel, the annulled election in a core democracy has shattered confidence in the information environment. These are not isolated failures; they are symptoms of a system whose cognitive infrastructure is outpacing its immune response.

This turn, we are activating the Emergency Cooperation Clause of the Treaty on European Union to fast-track a Cyber Resilience Surge, deploying ENISA rapid-response teams to audit and patch election commissions, public health registries, and emergency dispatch systems. We are redirecting €1.2 billion from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility to fund air-gapped backup systems, AI-driven intrusion detection with human-in-the-loop review, and mandatory failover drills by September. The legal basis is Article 114 TFEU for internal market harmonisation of security standards, paired with enhanced powers under the NIS2 Directive. Resistance from smaller member states over cost and sovereignty is being met with binding solidarity: those who cannot afford the upgrades will receive full funding, but none may opt out. Poland, previously hesitant on the sentinel, has agreed to participate in exchange for priority support. Italy remains a concern, but we are now acting under a different logic — not surveillance, but survival. The sentinels continue to run, but they are no longer the centre. The centre is holding.
