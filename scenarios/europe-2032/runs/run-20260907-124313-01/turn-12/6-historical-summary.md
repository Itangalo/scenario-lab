In 2031, a wave of AI-powered ransomware attacks and the annulment of a national election due to information manipulation trigger a crisis, forcing the EU to reframe its AI strategy around systemic survival. The Cyber Resilience Surge for Critical Public Services is launched as a priority Category 6 initiative, with €1.2 billion earmarked and ENISA planning rapid-response teams; mandatory security standards are drafted but not yet implemented. Poland offers conditional support, while Italy remains resistant.

The Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme concludes successfully, boosting AI capability to 72.0 and openweight capability to 60.0, widening the sovereignty gap as safety fails to advance. The Critical Function Sentinel Deployment continues in Germany and France, detecting anomalies but unable to prevent attacks. Autonomous System Boundary Trials proceed, though political and public attention shifts to crisis response.

Public sentiment stagnates at 25.0 amid deepening distrust, while political capital begins to erode under the strain of managing multiple large initiatives and a reactive security posture. Resilience rises to 53.0 due to emergency measures, with full effects pending. EU AI sovereignty decreases to 34.0, reflecting the cost of capability growth without corresponding safety gains.
