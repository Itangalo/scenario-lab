### The cutoff
In early autumn, access to the leading foreign model went dark for European users with almost no warning. Hospitals that had built triage summaries on it, ministries using it for drafting and translation, firms with customer operations piped through it found error messages instead. The provider gave no detailed reason and offered no appeal.

The practical scramble was immediate. The AI Office and ENISA began re-certifying substitute models for clinical and administrative use, to be hosted where pooled allied capacity allowed. For some ministries the swap showed early progress within weeks. For several hospital networks it did not: outputs differed, workflows broke, clinicians reverted to paper while engineers waited for re-validation slots. With certified capacity unavailable, some clinics and firms experimented with hastily hosted open-weight substitutes that had not completed clinical validation, and incident reports noted inconsistent outputs and workflow friction, but no confirmed safety incident was recorded this turn.

Open-weight work did not pause. With the frontier system inaccessible, European teams and published forensic methods circulating abroad were widely reused and re-hosted, keeping open-weight capability on its convergence path despite the disruption.

### A compact proposed, not yet delivered
Brussels proposed to turn the loose coordination framework with Japan, Korea, Canada and Gulf partners into a single working bargain — the EU Sovereign Fallback Access Compact, covering aligned licences, pooled quotas and shared testing under one arrangement. No new Digital Europe budget was committed this turn; officials said any jointly procured inference would require reallocation and co-financing to be negotiated next year. No category 4 measure finished this turn and no new sovereign capacity came online, leaving sovereignty unchanged. Mayors were told installer teams and mobile restoration teams could only be scheduled as existing Municipal Restoration Corps capacity allowed.

That constraint showed. Television carried anger from districts where restoration teams had not arrived and detectors remained waiting in warehouses. Office productivity studies showing solid gains for junior lawyers, accountants and journalists without job losses cut little ice with a public that had just watched essential services depend on a switch in another country.

Through the autumn, backlogs persisted across districts, certification queues lengthened as ENISA staff were stretched across clinical approvals and procurement talks, and hospital engineers described waiting for fallback allocation to follow the common line. Reports noted copycat crews abroad reusing published forensic methods, and scattered staff protests briefly blocking depot gates.
