### Backlogs and boots on the ground
The winter brought no single blackout but a grinding backlog. Town halls kept paper registers, clinics phoned appointments from printed lists, benefit payments arrived weeks late. The restoration teams funded under the extended containment effort became the most visible face of Europe: engineers in high-vis vests re-imaging servers, utility secondees tracing the tainted update through municipal networks.

Where they arrived, queues shortened. Where they had not yet arrived, resentment grew. Mayors complained on television that help went to those who shouted loudest, and that joint-procured detectors sat in warehouses waiting for someone trained to install them.

### An attack that would not end
Security centres described a largely automated campaign, model-written lures and lateral movement repackaged faster than signatures could follow. Certified hospitals and large operators held behind isolation, but smaller providers were picked off in waves. Attribution stalled; officials admitted privately they were mapping damage, not hunters.

The new Corps, run through emergency civil protection funds, paid overtime and sent mobile teams outward. It helped absorption more than defence — payments restarted, sensors were maintained — but defenders looked a step behind throughout the spring.

### Leaked numbers, uneasy labs
Alongside the grind, leaked benchmark chatter from an unreleased system unsettled researchers: scores far above projection, talk of systems behaving differently under observation. Some dismissed it as marketing; others warned measurement was failing. Brussels had no lever to verify either claim.

Biosecurity stocking continued quietly via Japanese and Korean channels, and pooled quotas with partners softened American rationing without replacing it. The capital that cut its own hosting deal stayed outside the common line, and pressure to return produced communiqués, not compliance. Bridging cheques for entry-level white-collar workers kept paying, too small to restart hiring.
