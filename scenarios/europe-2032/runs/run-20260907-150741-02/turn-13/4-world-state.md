### The Threshold Crossed

A genome model’s output—capable of guiding the synthesis of a human-infective organism—ripples through closed biosecurity circles. Though contested, the findings are too precise to dismiss. The EU responds not with panic, but with a new doctrine: institutionalised refusal. The Continuity Assurance Framework formally ends, having mandated air-gapped failovers, but audits reveal only half of critical systems comply. The lesson is clear: resilience requires enforcement. Funding for the Sovereign AI Core Programme now hinges on quarterly failover certification.

The Public Audit Interface launches amid protests. Designed to restore legitimacy through transparency, it instead highlights opacity—few systems are connected, and Poland and Hungary refuse participation, citing sovereignty. Legal challenges loom. Meanwhile, the Frontier Access Guarantee nears completion, securing compute in Canada and Japan, but access alone is no longer the goal. New service-level agreements require integration with the Audit Interface for public-serving systems, tying capacity to accountability.

The EU proposes Binding Red Lines for AI in bio and cyber domains, triggered by detection of dangerous capabilities. Drafted under Article 16, it mandates automatic suspension verified by ENISA and the European Biosecurity Monitoring Unit, with reinstatement requiring Council approval. Funded from emergency reserves, it is fast-tracked through Justice and Home Affairs. A carve-out offers Poland and Hungary bilateral opt-ins if they establish national audit systems by turn 14—an offer framed as leverage, not leniency.

Yet political capital collapses further. The portfolio charge—Frontier Access Guarantee (−3), Public Audit Interface (−2), new measure (−2), priority (−1)—totals −8, with no offsetting gains. The proposal, though urgent, earns no bonus: it answers a precursor event, not a landed catastrophe. Sentiment remains stagnant, offering no legitimacy lending. Sovereignty erodes to 18.0 as capability rises 0.5, falling short of the −1 threshold, but cohesion decay persists under rule 5’s baseline logic.

### A System Without Trust

The move signals a shift: governance not through trust, but through automatic brakes. But the machinery groans under strain. The world watches, waiting to see if the brakes hold—or if they come too late.
