* ``- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement will now be conditional on the operational readiness of the Sovereign AI Core Programme *and* on the existence of a functioning Public Audit Interface in the requesting state, ensuring that transparency, not just capacity, is the price of participation. We act to defend the regime, not to punish into helplessness, and we will not compel compliance with systems that citizens cannot see or challenge.``  
  - Trigger: The bio_uplift_findings event and the collapse of public_sentiment to 10.0, which together confirm that unaccountable systems — whether foreign or domestic — are now the primary vectors of systemic risk.  
  - Grounds: The original enforcement logic assumed that access denial was sufficient to deter fragmentation. But when systems operate in darkness, even sovereign infrastructure becomes a threat. We cannot demand loyalty to a black box. By tying access not only to technical readiness but to public auditability, we align enforcement with legitimacy. The cost of reversal is high — we are relaxing the coercive edge of the rule — but the alternative is a regime that survives on compulsion, not consent.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but now only through reversible, bounded interventions that can be withdrawn without systemic collapse, and only when they are paired with real-time public accountability *and* biological or cyber red lines that trigger automatic suspension, ensuring that uncertainty does not become an excuse for unbounded risk.``  
  - Trigger: The bio_uplift_findings event, which marks the first credible signal that non-experts could engineer human-infective organisms with AI assistance, and the fact that this signal emerged not from a lab breach but from published research.  
  - Grounds: Our revised commitment to reversibility and accountability was meant to prevent cascading harm — but it did not anticipate that the harm could be existential and silent. We now institutionalise early braking: when a capability crosses into bio or cyber domains with irreversible consequences, the intervention pauses unless explicitly renewed. This is not retreat from action — it is the embedding of circuit breakers into our own decision logic.

## In practice

This turn, we are institutionalising refusal. The Continuity Assurance Framework has formally closed — it established the baseline for failover, but audits show only half of critical systems are compliant, and operators routinely bypass protocols under pressure. We are now embedding its requirements into the funding conditions of the Sovereign AI Core Programme: no SACP support for systems that cannot demonstrate quarterly failover readiness. The Frontier Access Guarantee nears completion, locking in compute access through Canada and Japan, but we are no longer treating access as the end — it is the beginning of accountability. We are renegotiating the SLAs to include mandatory integration with the Public Audit Interface for any system serving public functions.

We are launching the Binding Red Lines measure because the bio_uplift_findings event changes everything. Using Article 16 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU as legal basis, we are drafting a Council regulation that triggers automatic suspension — not review, not warning, but stop — when AI-assisted design of human-infective organisms or self-propagating cyber exploits is detected. The European Biosecurity Monitoring Unit and ENISA will be empowered to verify such events, with appeals to the EAAD. Reinstatement requires a qualified majority vote in the Council. We are funding this from the Civil Protection Mechanism’s emergency reserve (€220 million), and we are presenting it not as a restriction, but as the minimum condition for continued operation in a world where a single model run could end a city.

We are negotiating with Germany and France to fast-track the measure through the Justice and Home Affairs Council, while offering Poland and Hungary a carve-out: if they establish a national audit interface by turn 14, they may opt into the red line regime bilaterally, preserving sovereignty while binding to outcome. This is not leniency — it is leverage. We are no longer asking for trust. We are building a system where trust is not required, because the brakes are visible, automatic, and shared.
