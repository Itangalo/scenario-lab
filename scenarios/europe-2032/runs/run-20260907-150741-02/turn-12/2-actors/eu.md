## Statement changes

* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that preserving agency means not only resisting external control, but also maintaining internal legitimacy, without which no sovereign capability can be used or trusted.``  
  - Grounds: The collapse of public_sentiment to 15.0, driven by the automated_decision_scandal and reinforced by the cyber_major_incident, reveals that political paralysis is no longer just a fiscal or institutional failure — it is a crisis of democratic consent. Our sovereignty is not only eroded by dependence on foreign compute, but hollowed out when citizens reject the very systems meant to serve them. The identity evolves because the threat is no longer binary: it is not just *them* or *us*, but whether *we* still mean anything to those we govern.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but now only through reversible, bounded interventions that can be withdrawn without systemic collapse, and only when they are paired with real-time public accountability to prevent a second betrayal of trust.``  
  - Trigger: The cyber_major_incident, which exploited model-generated exploits to compromise a major software dependency across public health and transport systems in three member states, landing visible harm while attribution remains opaque.  
  - Grounds: Acting under uncertainty now risks not just fragmentation, but cascading public harm when systems fail in plain sight. Our interventions must not only be reversible — they must be *answerable*. The commitment tightens because the cost of being wrong is no longer just lost capital, but lost legitimacy. We cannot pilot in the dark; every trial must have a witness.

* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse — but we now define "sovereign control" not only as technical independence, but as *operational continuity under duress* and *public legitimacy under scrutiny*, meaning that any control regime must ensure uninterrupted access to critical AI functions for public services, even during geopolitical cutoffs or internal enforcement actions, and must do so in a way that citizens can verify and trust.``  
  - Trigger: The convergence of the cyber_major_incident and the social insurance scandal, both enabled by opaque AI systems operating without effective human oversight or redress.  
  - Grounds: Operational continuity is meaningless if the systems sustaining it are rejected by the people they serve. The original commitment focused on availability; it now expands to include legitimacy. We are not abandoning continuity — we are recognising that without trust, continuity is coercion. The regime must now prove it can be both resilient and accountable.

## In practice

This turn, we are shifting from containment to visibility. The Continuity Assurance Framework finishes its run — it has established the technical baseline for failover, but as audits show, only half of critical systems have passed live tests, and operators still bypass protocols when under pressure. We are not abandoning it; we are embedding it into the EAAD’s certification regime, making compliance a condition for SACP funding from turn 13 onward. The Frontier Access Guarantee continues toward completion, locking in compute access through our Canadian and Japanese partners, but we now treat it as infrastructure that must earn public trust, not just technical resilience.

We are launching the Public Audit Interface as a direct response to the cyber_major_incident and the social insurance scandal. Using €380 million from the Digital Europe Programme’s unused reserves, we are tasking the EAAD with building a federated logging system that records high-impact AI decisions — denials of benefits, medical triage, infrastructure access — with metadata on model version, input triggers, and human oversight status. The interface will be searchable by ombudsmen, journalists, and affected citizens, with anonymised bulk data released quarterly. We are negotiating with the Justice and Home Affairs Council to establish a legal right to query AI decisions in social services, starting in Finland and Portugal as pilot jurisdictions.

We are not mandating participation yet — we are demonstrating value. The Audit Interface will first integrate with the Sovereign AI Demonstration Stack, making every decision in its health and education pilots publicly traceable. If it works, it becomes the price of entry for any AI system touching public welfare. We are not restoring trust with promises. We are giving people the keys to check the locks themselves.
