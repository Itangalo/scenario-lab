### A Crisis of Trust, Met with Modest Steps

Global AI capability creeps upward to 76.0 as frontier labs refine autonomous research agents, integrating them into drug discovery and materials science pipelines. The pace has slowed from the earlier leap, but progress remains relentless. Open-weight models remain stagnant at 54.0, constrained by limited access to frontier architectures and incremental community-driven improvements. Despite growing interest in transparency, the open ecosystem lacks the compute and data leverage to close the gap.

The EU’s political capital remains frozen at 0.0, mired in paralysis. The Continuity Assurance Framework formally completes this turn, mandating air-gapped fallbacks for critical AI systems. While technically sound, its implementation is uneven—human operators still distrust fallback protocols, and audits reveal only half of designated systems have passed live failover tests. Still, the partial institutionalization of resilience yields a modest +3 boost, lifting resilience to 52.0.

Meanwhile, public sentiment collapses further, dropping to 15.0. A national ombudsman’s report details how an AI system in social insurance denied thousands of legitimate claims, disproportionately affecting disabled and elderly applicants. The scandal ignites public fury, reinforcing perceptions of AI as a tool of bureaucratic cruelty. Restrictionist sentiment surges; protests target data centres in Warsaw and Marseille.

In response, the European Commission proposes the Sovereign AI Demonstration Stack—a potential small-scale, auditable AI deployment in health, education, and social services across five member states. Designed to rebuild trust through transparency, it is framed not as a mandate but as an invitation. However, no implementation begins this turn. Poland, having secured direct access to a U.S. frontier model under emergency health agreements, signals it will not participate. Other member states withhold commitment pending clarity on funding and governance.

Sovereignty dips to 20.0 as the ongoing capability rise triggers a −1 decay under rule 5, with no offsetting gains. The Frontier Access Guarantee continues in flight, but delivers no immediate capacity. The proposed demonstration measure does not yet contribute to any metric.

### The Limits of Demonstration

The EU avoids further fragmentation by not punishing defection, but fails to reverse the tide of distrust. With capital below 12, no priority acceleration applies. The proposal for a demonstration stack remains in early consultation, and its future impact is uncertain. For now, the political ground continues to erode.
