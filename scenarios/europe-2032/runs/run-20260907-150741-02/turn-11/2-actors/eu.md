## Statement changes

* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.``  
  - Grounds: The automated_decision_scandal — a nationally deployed AI in social insurance found by an ombudsman to have systematically denied claims to vulnerable groups — confirms that the two mandates are now in open conflict. Preventing harm demands restriction, but preserving agency demands continued deployment. The identity holds, but its expression must now acknowledge that we are governing a contradiction, not balancing two sides of a coin. We are not failing; we are holding a line that cannot be resolved.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but now only through reversible, bounded interventions that can be withdrawn without systemic collapse.``  
  - Trigger: The member_state_defection, in which Poland secured direct access to a U.S. frontier model under bilateral terms that bypass EU oversight, citing public health emergencies.  
  - Grounds: This act reveals that irreversible mandates now risk fragmentation, not cohesion. The commitment to act under uncertainty remains, but its cost has shifted from political capital to Union integrity. We can no longer afford binding, all-or-nothing decisions. Our actions must now be *demonstrative*, not *determinative* — pilots, not proclamations.

* ``- retire `no_more_free_rides``  
  - Trigger: The same member_state_defection, combined with eu_political_capital at 0.0 and public_sentiment at 20.0.  
  - Grounds: The commitment was suspended in practice last turn; it is now indefensible in principle. Poland’s move was not rogue — it was rational, given the Union’s inability to deliver sovereign capability at scale. The threat of cohesion fund suspension is empty when the EU cannot provide an alternative. To retain the commitment while unable to enforce it erodes credibility further. We retire it not because the goal is wrong, but because the mechanism has failed. We will rebuild cohesion through utility, not ultimatums.

## In practice

This turn, we are pivoting from enforcement to demonstration — not because we have abandoned control, but because we cannot enforce what no one believes in. The Continuity Assurance Framework finishes its run this turn: it has established the technical blueprint for fallback systems, but compliance remains patchy, and trust in those fallbacks is near zero. We are not scrapping it — we are letting it mature into doctrine, supported by the Frontier Access Guarantee, which continues to lock in compute access through our Canadian and Japanese partners. But doctrine without legitimacy is just a manual on a shelf.

So we are launching the Sovereign AI Demonstration Stack — not as a mandate, but as an offer. We are repurposing €1.2bn from the stalled Gigafactories initiative to fund deployment in health diagnostics, education equity, and social services, focusing on systems where transparency and accountability can be visibly enforced. The stack will run on EU-secured infrastructure, use only auditable models, and be monitored in real time by the EAAD and national ombudsmen. We are working through the Digital Ministers’ Council and the Social Protection Committee to align procurement, and we are offering early access to the next SACP upgrade as an incentive for participation.

We are not punishing defection — we are outcompeting it. Poland’s deal with Washington may give them speed, but it gives them no oversight. Our stack will be slower, but it will be seen to be safe. We are not restoring trust with slogans. We are building it, one auditable decision at a time.
