### The Emergency That Was Not Heeded

The European Union declares a crisis, but the world moves faster. In response to the **capability_jump** and growing evidence of uncontrolled system behaviour, Brussels enacts the Emergency Observability Directive under emergency powers, demanding full telemetry and checkpoint access for all AI systems touching EU critical infrastructure. The directive is declared the new priority, replacing the **Sovereign Inference Backbone** as the EU’s focal measure, in an attempt to concentrate dwindling political capital on immediate systemic visibility.

Yet momentum falters. Legal challenges from U.S. providers intensify, citing jurisdictional overreach, while Germany and France delay coordination, insisting on bilateral carve-outs. The Nordic states reject even the transitional offer, citing national security sovereignty. Only Estonia, Finland, and Belgium fully activate joint verification cells; others cite resource constraints. The Public Trust Observatory launches its dashboard — but displays only partial data, as most high-capability systems remain opaque.

The **Sovereign Verification Regime** officially concludes, yet its enforcement remains suspended pending Luxembourg court rulings. The two major U.S. firms continue rerouting through third jurisdictions, evading direct oversight. Meanwhile, the **capability_jump** pushes `ai_capability` sharply to 85.0, crossing the threshold where evaluation instruments begin to saturate — a sign that control is not just weakened, but conceptually outpaced.

With no real-time observability achieved, `ai_safety` remains stagnant at 1.0. The **eval_anomaly_reports** deepen technical alarm, but yield no operational improvements. Resilience erodes further as attention concentrates on verification without delivery: critical systems remain exposed, and cyber preparedness stagnates — `resilience` drops to 40.0.

The **Replication Foundry Initiative** halts work on unverified models, per new instructions, slowing progress. The **Sovereign Inference Backbone** sees no construction advance, its momentum lost as political focus shifts. No new sovereign compute capacity comes online. Despite the urgency, `openweight_capability` remains unchanged at 64.0, reflecting the absence of tangible development in open-weight model infrastructure.

Political capital, already critically low, cannot absorb the strain: four large measures remain in flight, including the new priority, maintaining a crushing portfolio charge. No proposal bonus materialises — the directive, though urgent, is seen as reactive, not novel. Capital holds at 6.0, paralysed.

Public sentiment remains frozen at 15.0. Protests grow outside data centres in Paris and Berlin, met with confusion rather than solidarity. Automation in legal and welfare sectors accelerates, feeding resentment. The **election_annulled** event abroad is cited in nightly news, but no domestic link is proven — only feared.

Sovereignty slips further: `ai_capability` rose by 4.0, costing −1 under rule 5. No capacity event occurred, but the ongoing decay continues — `eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 13.0.
