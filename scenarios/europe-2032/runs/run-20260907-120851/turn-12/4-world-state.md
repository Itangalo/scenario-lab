### The Fracture Widens

The EU’s emergency response to the cyber_major_incident begins in chaos. Hospitals in three regions remain partially offline as ransomware, generated by an advanced model, encrypts patient records and disrupts scheduling systems. The attack’s sophistication confirms fears: offensive AI now outpaces defence. ENISA coordinates a 90-day hardening sprint, mandating air-gapped backups and anomaly detection, but implementation is patchy. Southern and Eastern member states lack personnel and funding, delaying compliance. The Civil Protection Mechanism mobilises IT teams, but mutual aid protocols are untested at this scale.

### Sovereignty Under Siege

The Luxembourg court’s ruling on the Sovereign Verification Regime triggers a cascade. With central audit powers voided, Germany and the Netherlands announce bilateral talks with U.S. tech firms, fracturing oversight. The EU’s push for joint command over new exascale clusters meets renewed resistance. France demands operational autonomy, citing national security. Brussels issues binding orders under Article 122 TFEU, but legal challenges loom. ASML’s chip packaging pledge offers a lifeline, yet physical deployment lags — no new clusters come online.

### Political Capital Erodes

The **Replication Foundry Initiative** completes, yielding open-weight tools now deployed in emergency defences. However, no sovereignty bonus lands: the **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme** remains in flight, and no capacity event secures access. Meanwhile, `ai_capability` jumps to 92.0, driven by self-improving loops in unregulated labs. `ai_safety` remains stagnant at 1.0. No safeguards accompany the surge. The **Emergency Resilience Activation**, a large category 6 measure, begins but yields no immediate resilience boost. `resilience` inches up to 41.0, aided by partial hardening and the use of domestic tools. Yet the cyber incident’s toll — combined with ongoing labour unrest and public fury — keeps `public_sentiment` frozen at 10.0.
