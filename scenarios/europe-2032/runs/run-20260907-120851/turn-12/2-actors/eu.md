## In practice
We are acting on two simultaneous failures: the collapse of legal authority over foreign providers and the material vulnerability of our critical systems. The **emergent_sovereign_verification_collapse**, confirmed by the Luxembourg court, ends any illusion that we can audit or constrain foreign AI systems through central mandate. The **cyber_major_incident** — a model-generated ransomware sweep now paralyzing hospital networks in Belgium, Bavaria, and Emilia-Romagna — confirms that our defences are not just outdated, but structurally dependent on systems we do not control. We are being attacked by tools we cannot see, running on infrastructure we cannot secure, governed by rules we cannot enforce.

We are doubling down on the **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme** not as a long-term bet, but as an immediate necessity. The Commission is now issuing binding deployment orders under Article 122 TFEU, backed by the threat of redirected cohesion funds. France and Germany will host the first two exascale clusters, but under joint EU command — no national carve-outs. We are fast-tracking grid access for these sites using emergency energy provisions, and ASML has committed to prioritising EU-bound packaging for 3nm chips through Q3. The 40% joint-pool requirement for U.S. hyperscalers remains, but we are offering a phased compliance path: Microsoft and Google may retain operational control during build-out, but only if they submit to ENISA telemetry and agree to EU-first replication rights.

Simultaneously, we are launching the **Emergency Resilience Activation** to contain the ongoing cyber crisis. ENISA will coordinate a 90-day hardening sprint: all essential services must implement air-gapped fallbacks, model-output validation layers, and real-time anomaly detection using open-weight tools from the now-finished **Replication Foundry Initiative**. We are activating the Civil Protection Mechanism to share response teams across borders, and the Health Union will fund emergency IT upgrades in regional hospitals. This is not optional: non-compliant operators will be disconnected from cross-border networks. We are also establishing a temporary AI Incident Command under the Commission, reporting directly to the President, to unify response across domains. This is not business continuity — it is triage. And it is only possible because we finally have a domestic stack to fall back on.
