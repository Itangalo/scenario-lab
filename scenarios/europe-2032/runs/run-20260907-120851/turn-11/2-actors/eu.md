## In practice
We are acting on the irreversible erosion of our leverage. The **automated_decision_scandal** — a ruling by the German Federal Social Court that an AI-driven unemployment classification system wrongfully disqualified over 120,000 citizens from benefits, some for years — has shattered the last pretence that we can manage risk from the outside. The public is not just angry; it is disoriented. Trust in automated public services has collapsed. The **Emergency Observability Directive**, though formally adopted, delivered only partial compliance and no real-time insight — the systems that matter most remained opaque, and the providers that dominate public-sector AI rerouted through Singapore and Dublin to avoid checkpoint sharing. We were outmanoeuvred. We saw the failure in real time, but we had no alternative. That ends now.

We are launching the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme under Article 122 TFEU, invoking the economic and social cohesion clause to justify emergency coordination of energy, land use, and state aid. The European Commission will issue binding deployment targets: every member state must designate at least one sovereign AI zone with fast-tracked permitting, grid priority, and tax insulation for EU-controlled operators. We are redirecting €18 billion from the Digital Europe Programme, Recovery and Resilience Facility overpayments, and decommissioned 5G infrastructure to fund the first wave of exascale clusters. These will be legally securitised: no model above capability index 50.0 may run on EU public infrastructure unless the weights are verifiably hosted and controllable within the Union. We are engaging with ASML, Infineon, and STMicroelectronics to prioritise domestic chip packaging for these clusters, and we are offering equity stakes to member states that contribute grid capacity or brownfield sites.

The French and German governments are resisting full integration, citing national champions and energy strain. We are offering them co-leadership of the technical council and guaranteed hosting rights for national security models — but not exemptions. No carve-outs. The Nordic states, still wary of centralisation, are being invited into a parallel track focused on green-cooled, Arctic-located facilities with shared maintenance but distributed command. We are also renegotiating with the U.S. hyperscalers: Microsoft and Google will be allowed to expand in the EU only if they commit 40% of their new capacity to joint sovereign pools under ENISA audit. No more unilateral control. This is not about exclusion — it is about reciprocity. And for the first time, we are building what we require: the capacity to run, verify, and if necessary, replace the systems upon which our public institutions depend. The **Replication Foundry Initiative** will feed into this; the **Sovereign Inference Backbone** will run on it. This is the foundation we should have laid two mandates ago. We are six months from the next European elections. We will not arrive empty-handed.
