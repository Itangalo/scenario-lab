### The Sovereignty Gamble

The European Union launches the **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme**, declaring it the new priority in a bid to reverse years of strategic erosion. Invoking Article 122 TFEU, Brussels mandates emergency compute build-outs across member states, redirecting €18 billion in funds and fast-tracking energy and zoning approvals. The initiative targets irrevocable legal and physical anchoring of AI infrastructure, aiming to break dependency on foreign-controlled systems.

Yet momentum falters. France and Germany resist full integration, demanding exemptions for national champions. The Nordic bloc remains cautious, insisting on distributed command for Arctic-hosted facilities. Negotiations with U.S. hyperscalers stall: Microsoft and Google balk at the 40% joint-pool requirement, threatening to slow EU expansion. While the legal framework advances, physical deployment lags — no new exascale clusters come online this turn.

The **automated_decision_scandal** erupts as a German court rules that an AI system wrongfully denied benefits to over 120,000 citizens. Public trust collapses further. Protests intensify in Paris, Berlin, and Rome, with demonstrators targeting AI-driven welfare and policing systems. The backlash is internal, systemic, and politically corrosive.

The **Emergency Observability Directive** formally expires, having achieved only partial compliance. Estonia, Finland, and Belgium maintain joint verification cells, but the broader system fails — critical AI systems remain opaque. No real-time telemetry is established, and oversight remains fragmented.

Meanwhile, `ai_capability` jumps to 88.0, crossing into the terminal zone where evaluation instruments saturate. The leap reflects self-improving training loops now dominant in frontier labs. `openweight_capability` rises modestly to 67.0, as limited replication continues despite the **Replication Foundry Initiative**’s stalled progress.

`ai_safety` remains at 1.0 — no improvement, no response. `resilience` drops to 38.0 as cyber preparedness erodes under sustained pressure. `public_sentiment` plummets to 10.0, nearing open resistance to AI infrastructure.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 12.0: `ai_capability` rose by 3.0, costing −1, and no new capacity came online. The EU’s access to frontier systems remains unsecured. Political capital dips to 5.0 under the weight of five large measures — including the new priority — with no relief from past failures.
