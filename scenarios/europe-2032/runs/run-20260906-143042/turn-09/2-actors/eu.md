## In practice
We are treating this turn as the hinge between broken promises and binding standards. The InvestAI Gigafactories are complete, but their value as sovereign assets depends entirely on whether they are controlled, monitored, and insulated from external shocks. With the distillation breakthrough enabling high-capability models on commodity hardware, the perimeter has exploded — we can no longer assume that only large installations matter. That is why this turn we finalise the legal directive under Article 114 TFEU mandating real-time auditability: no public funding, no strategic designation, no access to Innovation Fund grants unless EAAD telemetry is live, continuous, and inspectable. The EIB will freeze final disbursements until compliance is verified, and ENISA will publish quarterly transparency reports naming non-compliant sites.

At the same time, we are pushing hard on the Sovereign Cyber Shield, knowing that turn 8 is its deadline and that three member states still lack integrated stacks. The cyberattack overwhelmed local CERTs not because tools failed, but because coordination did — responses were siloed, threat intelligence fragmented. We are convening a crisis-mode COREPER meeting under the Digital Agenda framework to fast-track cross-border authorisation for swarm-response protocols, backed by emergency Commission funding from the Crisis Instrument. Germany and Austria remain resistant to centralised control, so we are offering them leadership roles in the Shield’s operational council — co-design, not just compliance. The microgrid projects in Brittany and Sweden are being folded into the Shield’s architecture as isolated testbeds: if they can run secure, autonomous defences on closed circuits, they become blueprints for wider rollout.

Public sentiment is at 22.0 — dangerously low — and we know that enforcement without demonstration fuels backlash. So alongside enforcement, we are launching a visible proof-of-service campaign: live maps of Shield coverage, public logs of detected synthetic media takedowns, and a rebranded SCAP Transition Window placing displaced workers directly into cyber-monitoring roles with clear career paths. This is not about optics alone. It is about proving that sovereignty means protection — and that protection must be seen to work.
