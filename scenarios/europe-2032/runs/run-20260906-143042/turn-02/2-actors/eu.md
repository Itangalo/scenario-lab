## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme (SCAP)  
This is no longer about competitiveness — it is about survival: we have been locked out of the model we depended on, and without sovereign capacity that is live, usable, and legally uncontested, every other measure in our portfolio rests on foreign permission.

## In practice  
We are activating the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme (SCAP) under Article 122 TFEU and the newly invoked Crisis Clause of the Digital Markets Act, granting the Commission emergency authority to override national permitting delays, redirect cohesion funds, and compulsorily allocate grid capacity for AI infrastructure deemed critical to Union continuity. The programme designates three national champions — one in France (CEA), one in Germany (Fraunhofer-Gesellschaft), and one Nordic consortium (hosted in Sweden) — each tasked with delivering a fully operational AI system above 55.0 capability by turn 3 and 60.0 by turn 4, running on EU-owned hardware, with full weight security and auditability. Funding will be drawn from a €50bn emergency envelope within the InvestAI fund, unlocked immediately via EIB issuance backed by the Multiannual Financial Framework, with a clawback mechanism if milestones are missed.

Simultaneously, we are restructuring the InvestAI Gigafactories to align with SCAP’s timeline: two of the five planned sites — one in Brittany, one in northern Sweden — are being fast-tracked as SCAP anchor zones, with ENTSO-E now under binding instruction to reserve 8GW of grid capacity by turn 3, regardless of national energy plan delays. The Commission is issuing a formal notice to member states that failure to cooperate with SCAP site development will trigger Article 7 proceedings on grounds of systemic risk to Union security. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being amended to tie its private capital incentives directly to SCAP milestones: 50% of the €200bn target is now conditional on co-location with SCAP sites and adherence to EU-controlled software stacks.

The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) is being pulled forward in parallel: we are seconding 20 senior technical staff from national cyber agencies and CERN to stand up an interim evaluation cell by the end of this turn, operating under ENISA with emergency access to model weights from any system deployed in the EU above 50.0 capability. We are bypassing the stalled levy by reallocating €1.2bn from unused RRF digitalisation funds to cover EAAD’s first-year operations, and we are preparing a draft regulation under Article 114 TFEU to mandate real-time logging and interpretability tooling for all high-capability systems — a measure that will be tabled in the June ECOFIN Council, despite expected legal resistance from member states with large tech sectors.
