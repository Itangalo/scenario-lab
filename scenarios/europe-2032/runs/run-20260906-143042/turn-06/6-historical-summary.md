The EU declares a state of digital emergency following a major AI-driven cyberattack on public health and energy systems, activating Emergency Resilience Corridors and launching the €45bn Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative to secure critical infrastructure by turn 8. ENISA deploys standardised monitoring and response systems, with early adoption in France and Germany, but implementation falters in Italy and Hungary due to resistance to centralised control. The SCAP network is partially repurposed to retrain displaced professionals as cyber monitors, addressing economic displacement through resilience building.

The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme formally ends without meeting its 5GW target, blocked by ENTSO-E’s unanimity requirement and legal challenges from Germany and Austria, though the Commission advances microgrid projects in Brittany and northern Sweden. The European AI Assurance Directorate certifies its first models, but its effectiveness is weakened by member states blocking real-time logging mandates.

Global AI capability rises to 62.0, driven by faster, more evasive automated attack generation, while open-weight models reach 50.0, aided by tools leaked from the breach. AI safety fails to advance, widening the gap with capability. Resilience improves slightly due to early cyber shield deployment, but systemic vulnerabilities remain widespread. Public trust stabilizes marginally amid framing of the Cyber Shield as a protective measure.
