The EU faces a crisis of sovereignty in turn 7 as the InvestAI Gigafactories, though operational, fail to achieve energy autonomy or real-time auditability, leaving critical systems vulnerable to a major AI-driven cyberattack affecting three member states. The Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative activates emergency corridors but exposes uneven defense coverage—strong in France and Germany, weak elsewhere. In response, the Commission mandates ENISA telemetry and EAAD logging with EIB funding tied to compliance, sparking resistance from Germany and Austria over sovereignty concerns. A fragile Joint Oversight Panel is established, but trust erodes.

Microgrid projects in Brittany and northern Sweden gain fast-tracked status as Projects of Common Interest to circumvent ENTSO-E delays, yet deployment stalls due to permitting and supply constraints. Public trust declines further amid synthetic media manipulation during regional elections, while the new transparency portal is dismissed as ineffective. SCAP retraining expands but sees low participation.

Globally, AI capability rises to 65.0, driven by adaptive attacks and leaked tools, with open-weight models reaching 53.0—possibly due to a distillation breakthrough—while AI safety stagnates, widening the control gap. Resilience improves marginally from early cyber shield gains, but systemic fragility persists. Political cohesion weakens, enforcement backfires, and European sovereignty deteriorates amid energy dependence and fragmented implementation.
