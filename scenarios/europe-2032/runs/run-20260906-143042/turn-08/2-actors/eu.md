## In practice
We are treating the end of turn 7 as a moment of reckoning: the InvestAI Gigafactories were meant to deliver sovereign capability, but without integration into the Cyber Shield or verified energy autonomy, they risk becoming high-performance liabilities—compute clusters running on commercial grids, unmonitored and vulnerable. This turn, the Commission issues binding technical directives under Article 114 TFEU, requiring all Gigafactory projects to demonstrate integration with ENISA’s hardened telemetry network and submit to EAAD real-time logging as a condition for final EIB disbursement. Non-compliant sites will have funding suspended and may lose their designation as strategic infrastructure.

Simultaneously, we are bypassing ENTSO-E’s paralysis by invoking Article 194(2) TFEU to designate microgrid-linked compute facilities in Brittany and northern Sweden as Projects of Common Interest in Energy, unlocking fast-track permitting and direct access to Innovation Fund grants. Germany and Austria are being offered co-governance roles in a new Joint Oversight Panel for Energy-Compute Allocation, with arbitration through the Court of Justice if needed. The goal is not just power, but separation: these facilities must run on circuits that cannot be drained by market actors during crisis.

Public sentiment remains fragile at 24.0, and we are aware that enforcing top-down mandates risks backlash. To counter this, we are launching a transparency portal showing live shield coverage across critical sectors and publishing monthly progress on sovereign compute integration. Displaced professionals are being fast-tracked into ENISA-certified cyber monitor roles via a restructured SCAP Transition Window, now funded independently of the original outreach programme. This is not just about building systems—it is about proving that the Union can still bind capability to control, and that protection is not a slogan, but a service being delivered.
