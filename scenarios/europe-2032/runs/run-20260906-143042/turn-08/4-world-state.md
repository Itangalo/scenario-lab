### A Reckoning on Sovereignty

The EU marks turn 7 as a moment of strategic failure: the InvestAI Gigafactories, meant to anchor sovereign compute, are operational but lack energy autonomy and real-time auditability. A major AI-driven cyberattack exploited these gaps, overwhelming critical systems in three member states. Though the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative activated emergency corridors, its reach remains partial—defences are strong in France and Germany, but patchy elsewhere.

The Commission enforces binding integration with ENISA telemetry and EAAD logging, threatening to withhold final EIB funding. Germany and Austria resist, calling the directives an overreach. A Joint Oversight Panel is proposed, but trust is thin. Meanwhile, microgrid projects in Brittany and northern Sweden are fast-tracked as Projects of Common Interest, bypassing ENTSO-E delays. Still, permitting bottlenecks and supply shortages slow progress—physical separation from commercial grids remains distant.

Public sentiment dips further as synthetic media disrupt regional elections, feeding narratives of digital decay. The new transparency portal fails to gain traction, seen as bureaucratic theatre. SCAP retraining expands, but few displaced workers join.

Globally, AI capability advances to 65.0, driven by adaptive attack generation and leaked tools. Open-weight models reach 53.0, aided by an unconfirmed distillation breakthrough. No safety progress occurs—`ai_safety` drops as capability outpaces control. Resilience rises slightly from early shield deployment, but systemic fragility endures.

Political capital collapses. The Gigafactories finish, granting a +3 to +6 boost, but the portfolio charge remains heavy. Enforcement efforts backfire, eroding cohesion. Sovereignty slips further as dependence on unstable energy and foreign frontier access deepens.

### Fragile Foundations

The Union’s attempt to bind infrastructure to control falters. Vision remains, but execution fractures on the rocks of fragmentation.
