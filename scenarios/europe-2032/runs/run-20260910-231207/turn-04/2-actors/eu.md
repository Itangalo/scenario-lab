## Priority
M5 EU Incident Reporting and Assured Deployment Regime, because unrecallable open-weight tooling plus copycat grid intrusions means mandatory near-miss reporting and certifiable controls must outrank new build this turn.

## In practice
We drive M5 through AI Act implementing acts in the AI Office with JRC and ENISA: rapid notification duties for serious incidents and near-misses, and the new interpretability control property as a certified condition for high-risk deployment. With vendors already using the technique, we trade swift certification pathways for access and incident data, using seconded evaluation staff to define what counts as certifiable.

We use M5 and finished Shield capacity to answer the copycat wave without a new instrument: ENISA mobile teams and TSO playbooks extended to hit municipal utilities, pooled sensor alerts shared as Shield sustainment, not a new programme. On the member-state side deal we contain rather than punish — Commission and Energy Council engagement to ring-fence EU procurement and data rules, framing the defection as proof we must deliver Shield and reporting rather than fragment further. Gigafactories stay in DG CNECT/EIB state-aid and power talks, but we do not pretend selection moves this turn.
