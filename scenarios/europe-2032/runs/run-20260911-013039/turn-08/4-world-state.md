### Patching with other people's plugs
Brussels spent the spring not building but bargaining. With no money for a second rescue, the Commission pooled export-licence practice on chips, spares and equipment with a group of supply-chain middle powers and took joint tenders for server tenancies and cloud relief to vendors. Officials sold it as turning what Europe still controls — permits, grid plots, evaluation demand — into hosting it does not.

It partly worked. Two joint tenders replaced some of the interim hosting that commercial providers had repriced, and surcharge growth slowed on public-sector contracts in three large member states. Relief crews kept certified hospitals and utilities degraded but running, and the short tenancies on European servers rolled over to their planned end without a break.

The thinness showed elsewhere. Administrators still rationed triage and drafting tools at peak hours, and a regional paper obtained internal memos describing queued jobs and supervised open models timing out. Exhausted seconded staff went home, and municipalities without certificates improvised with consumer tools against guidance.

The wider build stayed frozen. The backup site held land, grid and legal shells alive but poured no concrete; private co-funding did not return after the autumn valuation collapse. Insurers held higher premiums, citing last year's contractor incident.

Voters registered a holding action, not a recovery. Gratitude for wards that stayed open mixed with anger at queues and dependence on foreign switches. In capitals, finance ministers noted the joint bargaining had bought time it had not bought capacity, and left the two-year commitment unmoved.
