### The invitation taken, the hardware still missing
The spring offer was simple: join a pooled cyber command with live telemetry and joint attribution, or face the next ransomware wave alone. Councils said yes quickly. CERT-EU and national centres plugged cooperating clinics and town halls into shared feeds, and the spring intrusion attempts across two grid operators and a hospital group were isolated in hours. Auditors again credited the locked backups and cut-the-link drills.

It worked because it was borrowed. Domestic chip slots stayed cancelled, the fenced gigafactory site stayed idle, and the licensing talks with lithography holders still demanded binding controls before any quotas. Trade officials kept the file alive but brought home nothing to substitute for. Finance did not return; two planned co-location deals stayed dead.

### Lawful harm versus working triage
At home the Commission pushed the trust repair hard. The frozen benefits and policing support system stayed frozen, logs were published with a redress window, workload limits and independent re-checks were imposed on caseworkers, and lawyers drafted the gap-closing act meant to catch systems ruled lawful but harmful.

Where audited triage assistants ran, nurses and mayors defended them, and waiting lists fell. Where they did not, insurers still refused cover pending actual law, and councils refused to deploy. Press coverage held to the same frame: benefit in pockets, systemic risk by design.

The liability pact with supply-chain partners was signed and entered into force, clarifying who pays when civic systems fail. Insurers called it a step, not cover, and held their freeze. Partners signed paper, not quotas.

Brussels ended June better protected by allies, no less dependent on them, and still arguing that its machines could be European and trustworthy at once.
