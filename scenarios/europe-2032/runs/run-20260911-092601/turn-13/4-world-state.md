### The strait closes
Autumn brought the news every contingency plan had filed under unthinkable: warships around Taiwan and advanced chips stopped leaving. Within weeks every AI question in Brussels became a security question. Prices for logged spares tripled again, then disappeared at any price. Ministers who had spoken of productivity now spoke of rationing.

The Union found it held one strong card — lenses, machines and know-how further up the chain that everyone still needed — and played it for time rather than money. Continued pooled cyber intelligence and spare-parts inflow in exchange for continued export licences: that was the bargain offered in capital after capital.

### The weights spill, the models go quiet
Almost simultaneously a new openly released model landed within months of the closed frontier. Hundreds of thousands downloaded it in days. Doctored diagrams and synthetic outage calls that had plagued grid operators now had a stronger engine, and no recall was possible.

At the same moment the leading labs' shift to non-verbal reasoning fully bit. Gateway procurement quietly rewrote its filters: instead of reading the machine's reasoning, operators installed black-box tripwires. They caught less, and everyone knew it. A contested genome-study claiming a non-expert could be guided toward a viable human pathogen design sharpened the fear — health agencies kept sharing samples but firewalled any model-assisted wet-work guidance.

### Accession on paper, reversion on the ground
Against this, the joint cyber and bio accession limped over a line. Showcase ministries and guarded depots were fully connected; hospitals and grid operators, whose capitals refused raw real-time export, were connected under delayed, on-premise query. Seconded analysts remained the only currency.

On the ground, operators voted with their hands. After vandalism and quota cuts, municipal utilities and hospital engineers quietly shelved the metered foreign fallback and returned to paper ledgers and hand drills. It kept the lights on through the winter intrusions, and entrenched a low-tech equilibrium no programme had planned.

Brussels survived the half-year on borrowed cover while its own capacity slipped further out of reach.
