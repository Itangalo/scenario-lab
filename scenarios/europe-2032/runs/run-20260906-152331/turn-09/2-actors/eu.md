## In practice
This turn, we are closing the last phase of our prior two-year commitment: sovereign control as a shield against strategic shocks. The Sovereign AI Core Programme and Resilience Reinforcement Initiative have formally concluded. The dual-key clusters in Saint-Malo and Kiruna are operational, training the first sovereign models under multi-party audit. The Swedish Gigafactory remains grid-constrained, but RePowerEU emergency powers have fast-tracked the Norrland link — full capacity is now expected by mid-2031. These are real gains, but they are not enough.

The world has shifted beneath us. The *ai_investment_collapse* has slowed the global frontier — a rare moment of reprieve — but it has also shattered the compute partnerships we were counting on. Our sovereignty remains at 15.0, barely above total dependence. Meanwhile, the *joint_threat_response* offer from NATO and like-minded states gives us access to real-time cyber and bio intelligence we could not build alone — a lifeline, but not a substitute for our own cohesion.

Worse, the *emergent_resilience_pact_forming* reveals a dangerous truth: member states are losing faith in our ability to act as one. They are beginning to build their own defences, outside the Union’s framework. We cannot let that stand.

So we are acting — not just with rules, but with reach. The Restrict Open-Weight Deployment directive remains in flight, but we now see it is being circumvented not by malice, but by inertia: universities adopt open models for smart-city pilots, municipalities plug them into back-office automation, all under the radar. We are therefore launching the EU Open-Weight Monitoring and Interdiction Network under the EAAD, combining automated scanning of public-sector AI use, a new whistleblower fund, and a binding clause that ties EU research grants to compliance. We will detect, we will assess, and we will stop — not through punishment, but through presence.

We are also using the *joint_threat_response* invitation to strengthen our hand. We will join the biosurveillance pact, but only if it includes a mutual obligation to align open-model controls. This is not leverage we have used before — but the moment demands it. The Union must not just survive the next shock. It must be seen to survive it — together.
