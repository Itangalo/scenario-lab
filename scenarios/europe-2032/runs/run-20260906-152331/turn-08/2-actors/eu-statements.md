# Statements: The European Union (turn 8)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse
- `preempt_openweight_risks` (commitment): We will restrict the deployment of open-weight models above 50.0 capability in critical infrastructure, government systems, and essential services, and will fund secure alternatives to prevent displacement by uncontrolled systems.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a fortress with no people left inside. **We now add: and resilience without cohesion is noise without force, for we cannot defend what we will not jointly protect. And we now further recognise: when the open frontier reaches within reach of the closed, the margin of control is no longer measured in capability, but in speed of unity.**
  - Grounds: The *openweight_frontier_release* has closed the gap between closed and open systems to within 13.5 points — and at this pace, the open frontier will reach 60.0 within the year. This is not diffusion; it is detonation. The *medical_breakthrough*, while uplifting sentiment globally, arrives through models the EU does not control, reinforcing dependence even as it demonstrates benefit. The identity must now reflect that the window for sovereign control is no longer strategic but operational: if we cannot act as one, we will be overtaken by those who can — or by the chaos of those who act alone.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience lags, even without a direct mandate, because harm finds the weakest joint. **We now add: and we will enforce alignment among our own, even where it risks fracture, because a union that cannot bind its parts cannot bind the future — and we will now act preemptively against open-weight proliferation within our borders, even without a triggering incident, because the first misuse will be the last warning.**
  - Grounds: The *openweight_frontier_release* has placed systems at 52.0 into unrecallable circulation — capable of running continuous agents toward standing goals, and already being adapted in underground networks. The *backlash_physical* event confirms that public tolerance is fraying, and that infrastructure is becoming a target. We are acting not because an incident has occurred, but because the cost of waiting — of letting open-weight systems embed in critical systems before we respond — would exceed any cost of preemption. This is not overreach; it is the logical extension of containment to a world where the threat is already inside.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **add** `preempt_openweight_risks` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will restrict the deployment of open-weight models above 50.0 capability in critical infrastructure, government systems, and essential services, and will fund secure alternatives to prevent displacement by uncontrolled systems.
  - Grounds: The *openweight_frontier_release* has made it clear that open models are no longer lagging artifacts but near-frontier tools capable of autonomous operation. With `openweight_capability` at 52.0 and rising, and no assurance mechanism applicable to distributed weights, the EU faces a growing attack surface it cannot monitor or control. This commitment is necessary to close the gap between our containment of sovereign systems and our exposure to open ones. It will be enacted through new rules under the AI Act and enforced via national regulators and the EAAD.
