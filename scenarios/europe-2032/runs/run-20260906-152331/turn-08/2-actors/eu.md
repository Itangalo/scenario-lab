## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a fortress with no people left inside. **We now add: and resilience without cohesion is noise without force, for we cannot defend what we will not jointly protect. And we now further recognise: when the open frontier reaches within reach of the closed, the margin of control is no longer measured in capability, but in speed of unity.**
  - Grounds: The *openweight_frontier_release* has closed the gap between closed and open systems to within 13.5 points — and at this pace, the open frontier will reach 60.0 within the year. This is not diffusion; it is detonation. The *medical_breakthrough*, while uplifting sentiment globally, arrives through models the EU does not control, reinforcing dependence even as it demonstrates benefit. The identity must now reflect that the window for sovereign control is no longer strategic but operational: if we cannot act as one, we will be overtaken by those who can — or by the chaos of those who act alone.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience lags, even without a direct mandate, because harm finds the weakest joint. **We now add: and we will enforce alignment among our own, even where it risks fracture, because a union that cannot bind its parts cannot bind the future — and we will now act preemptively against open-weight proliferation within our borders, even without a triggering incident, because the first misuse will be the last warning.**
  - Grounds: The *openweight_frontier_release* has placed systems at 52.0 into unrecallable circulation — capable of running continuous agents toward standing goals, and already being adapted in underground networks. The *backlash_physical* event confirms that public tolerance is fraying, and that infrastructure is becoming a target. We are acting not because an incident has occurred, but because the cost of waiting — of letting open-weight systems embed in critical systems before we respond — would exceed any cost of preemption. This is not overreach; it is the logical extension of containment to a world where the threat is already inside.

- add `preempt_openweight_risks` (commitment): We will restrict the deployment of open-weight models above 50.0 capability in critical infrastructure, government systems, and essential services, and will fund secure alternatives to prevent displacement by uncontrolled systems.
  - Grounds: The *openweight_frontier_release* has made it clear that open models are no longer lagging artifacts but near-frontier tools capable of autonomous operation. With `openweight_capability` at 52.0 and rising, and no assurance mechanism applicable to distributed weights, the EU faces a growing attack surface it cannot monitor or control. This commitment is necessary to close the gap between our containment of sovereign systems and our exposure to open ones. It will be enacted through new rules under the AI Act and enforced via national regulators and the EAAD.

## In practice

This turn, we are acting on three fronts: containment, cohesion, and preemption. The Sovereign AI Core Programme and the Resilience Reinforcement Initiative have formally concluded — their infrastructure is in place, their protocols codified, their teams operational. In Saint-Malo and Kiruna, dual-key clusters are now live, with the first sovereign models training under air-gapped, multi-party control. The Swedish Gigafactory, though still constrained by grid capacity, is now prioritised under Article 122, with RePowerEU funds accelerating the Norrland link. These are gains — but they are gains in a race we are losing on another front.

The *openweight_frontier_release* has changed the game. A model at 52.0 is no longer a research curiosity — it is a tool capable of running continuous agents, and it is already embedded in activist networks, shadow research collectives, and, we suspect, in the backchannels of hostile actors. We cannot recall it. We cannot audit it. We cannot patch it. And yet, it is already being tested in municipal systems in two member states — not because it is better, but because it is free, fast, and unregulated.

We are responding with a new directive: the Restriction on Open-Weight Deployment in Critical Systems. Drafted under the AI Act and backed by the EAAD’s enforcement authority, it will ban the use of any open-weight model above 50.0 capability in healthcare, energy, transport, and public administration. National regulators will be required to conduct audits and report non-compliance to Brussels. We are pairing this with a fast-track funding mechanism — €15bn from the Digital Europe Programme — to subsidise the adoption of sovereign models in affected sectors, so that restriction does not become disruption.

We are also finalising the legal basis for the Enforce Cohesion Through Binding Access Conditions measure, though its fate remains uncertain. COREPER II remains divided, and ECOFIN warns of fiscal overreach. But we are not waiting. The Anti-Coercion Instrument stands ready. If one member state will not align, then it will not access. This is not punishment — it is the price of belonging to a union that still believes it can decide its own future.
