## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a fortress with no people left inside. **We now add: and resilience without cohesion is noise without force, for we cannot defend what we will not jointly protect.**
  - Grounds: The *member_state_defection* and the subsequent invocation of Article 16 TFEU reveal that even the most robust technical safeguards collapse if member states treat strategic AI capacity as a national commodity rather than a collective good. The US_POSTURE shift further exposes that external powers will not negotiate with a fragmented bloc. The identity must now reflect that political unity is not a background condition — it is a core component of survival.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience lags, even without a direct mandate, because harm finds the weakest joint. **We now add: and we will enforce alignment among our own, even where it risks fracture, because a union that cannot bind its parts cannot bind the future.**
  - Grounds: The emergency act under Article 16 and the use of the Anti-Coercion Instrument mark a shift from persuasion to enforcement. This is not a failure of diplomacy but a necessary recalibration: when one member state’s pragmatism becomes the bloc’s vulnerability, the cost of inaction exceeds the cost of conflict. The commitment must now reflect that acting under uncertainty includes acting against our own when necessary.

## In practice

This turn, we are acting on three fronts: enforcement, consolidation, and absorption. The AI Containment Protocol has now formally landed — it is law, backed by EAAD’s authority and the Article 16 emergency act. France and Germany are operationalising the first dual-key cluster in Saint-Malo and Kiruna, with physical infrastructure under joint EU-national guard and sovereign model training set to begin in early Q1 2030. But the protocol is only as strong as its weakest enforcer, and one member state’s bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler has exposed a fatal loophole: national ambition bypassing collective security.

We are closing it. The new measure — Binding Access Conditions — will legally tie access to SACP infrastructure and sovereign models to compliance with the Containment Protocol. Non-compliant states will be barred from using EU compute, and cohesion funds will be suspended under the Anti-Coercion Instrument until alignment is restored. This is not coercion for its own sake — it is the price of membership in a union that claims to act as one. We are drafting the regulation under Article 114 TFEU, with legal backing from the Commission’s Legal Service, and will bring it to COREPER II by December, aiming for an emergency Council vote under the ECOFIN formation due to fiscal implications.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories are now complete, but the Swedish site remains grounded by grid constraints. We have invoked Article 122 to fast-track transmission upgrades, pulling RePowerEU contingency funds to accelerate the Norrland link — but this is a reminder that sovereignty is not just compute, but the energy to run it. The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative is now in implementation: national CSIRTs are integrating AI-assisted patching tools, and the first bio-detection network using sovereign models is being tested in Strasbourg and Malmö. Public sentiment remains at 25.0, and we do not expect it to move until people see AI as something that protects them, not something that divides their leaders. That begins with proving that we can, when it matters, act as one.
