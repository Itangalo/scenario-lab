# Statements: The European Union (turn 7)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a fortress with no people left inside. **We now add: and resilience without cohesion is noise without force, for we cannot defend what we will not jointly protect.**
  - Grounds: The *member_state_defection* and the subsequent invocation of Article 16 TFEU reveal that even the most robust technical safeguards collapse if member states treat strategic AI capacity as a national commodity rather than a collective good. The US_POSTURE shift further exposes that external powers will not negotiate with a fragmented bloc. The identity must now reflect that political unity is not a background condition — it is a core component of survival.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience lags, even without a direct mandate, because harm finds the weakest joint. **We now add: and we will enforce alignment among our own, even where it risks fracture, because a union that cannot bind its parts cannot bind the future.**
  - Grounds: The emergency act under Article 16 and the use of the Anti-Coercion Instrument mark a shift from persuasion to enforcement. This is not a failure of diplomacy but a necessary recalibration: when one member state’s pragmatism becomes the bloc’s vulnerability, the cost of inaction exceeds the cost of conflict. The commitment must now reflect that acting under uncertainty includes acting against our own when necessary.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
