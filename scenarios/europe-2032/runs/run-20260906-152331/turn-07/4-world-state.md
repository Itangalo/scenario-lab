### A Fragile Leap Forward

Global AI capability advances to 65.5, driven by rapid adoption of automated verification in law, engineering and clinical domains. Systems now self-validate outputs in tasks once thought to require human judgment, accelerating deployment and narrowing the gap between closed and open models. An open-weight release, approaching frontier performance within months, spreads rapidly through research and activist networks. Openweight_capability rises to 52.0, reflecting growing diffusion and adaptation—still trailing the frontier, but closing faster than expected. Concerns mount over untraceable misuse, though no major incident has yet occurred.

In the EU, the InvestAI Gigafactories and AI Containment Protocol formally conclude. The Sovereign AI Core Programme progresses toward its 2030 target, with initial deployment of dual-key infrastructure now underway in Saint-Malo and Kiruna. Site commissioning has begun, but full operational status is delayed by integration challenges and member state coordination lags. The project remains on track for partial activation by mid-2030, but not before.

The Commission advances the new "Enforce Cohesion Through Binding Access Conditions" measure, aiming to link cohesion funds to compliance with EU AI governance. However, resistance from one member state frames the proposal as overreach. COREPER II negotiations stall amid fiscal concerns, and ECOFIN delays funding linkage. The measure remains in proposal phase, with no binding effect yet.

Resilience improves slightly as AI-assisted patching rolls out and bio-detection pilots launch. Yet grid constraints continue to limit output at the Swedish Gigafactory, despite RePowerEU fund reallocations. The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative advances unevenly across member states.

Public sentiment holds at 25.0. Media spotlight the rift between member states, framing AI as a source of division, not protection. The Commission’s push to enforce unity strengthens sovereignty—completed compute capacity offsets some dependency risks—but political capital erodes under stalled cohesion talks and ongoing portfolio costs.

### The Cost of Unity

The EU’s attempt to bind sovereignty to control faces institutional friction. While the intent is clear, execution falters at the threshold of enforcement. The new measure, though symbolically powerful, has not yet changed behaviour—only the possibility of sanction. Trust remains thin, and the American posture of alliance-conditioned access offers no relief without internal alignment.
