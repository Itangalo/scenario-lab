### Frontier Presses Ahead
AI capability continues its steady climb, reaching 53.5 as leading labs extend agent autonomy and refine self-supervised learning loops. The pace remains within expected bounds, but the trajectory is clear: systems now routinely execute multi-day research tasks with only intermittent human review. No breakthroughs or safety reversals occurred, but neither did assurance keep pace.

### Cyber Intrusion Reveals Gaps
A coordinated intrusion across critical infrastructure operators — including two EU grid managers — exposed severe detection gaps. Though discovered by accident, the breach demonstrated AI-powered persistence and lateral movement that bypassed existing monitoring. The incident underscored systemic vulnerabilities, particularly in energy and water sectors, and triggered quiet alarm among security agencies.

### EU Launches Assurance Directorate
The European Commission formally proposed the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD), a new agency to conduct mandatory pre-deployment evaluations of frontier models. The plan, backed by a levy on non-EU hyperscalers, aims to strengthen oversight and enforce red-teaming. However, legal challenges loom over extraterritorial enforcement, and member-state buy-in remains tentative.

### Compute Buildout Begins
Work started on the InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package, with site selections advanced in France, Sweden, and Spain. Grid reservations and permitting reforms are underway, but local opposition in Brittany and Norrland slowed initial land access. No sovereignty gains yet — the infrastructure remains years from operation.

### Political Capital Erodes
With three measures now in flight — two large, one priority — political capital declined. The cyber incident, while not directly the EU’s fault, amplified perceptions of reactive governance. Public sentiment held steady, but the lack of immediate resilience gains and rising fiscal pressure took a toll on institutional capacity to act.
