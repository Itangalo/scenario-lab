### A War No One Voted For
Winter turned on a flash over the Pacific. Washington and Beijing struck what they called dual-use nodes: fabs, cable landings, satellite uplinks, hyperscale data centres. Europe was not belligerent. It was geography.

Two cable cuts in the Atlantic and a strike on a power spur feeding a southern French compute hall darkened registries again in February. Reserve teams that had just restored clean images were redeployed to disperse backups, guard substations and keep hospitals on islanded power. The Council declared non-belligerency, denied targeting use of Union territory, and ordered airspace and cable protection patrols. The declaration did not stop debris, rerouting or insurance flight.

### Care That Held, Barely
In the middle of this arrived a genuine clinical arrival: tailored therapies that put previously untreatable blood and immune conditions into remission, delivered through ordinary clinics in trial sites. Where European-hosted triage and sequencing held, nurses could route patients to them. In Denmark and Portugal, the pilot networks cut waits again, and wage top-ups tied to restoration crews finally showed up on payslips.

Elsewhere the benefit arrived as dependence. The most potent protocols ran on American or Chinese stacks the Union could not host on its own terms, requiring cloud verification Brussels refused. Procurement conditioned on sovereign hosting and EMA verification slowed rollout for months. Tabloids ran the same split headline for weeks: cured abroad, queued at home.

Graduate hiring stayed frozen. Sorting machines kept spreading. Bio-detection seed contracts became emergency pooled orders for reagents and hardened logistics, still short of a network. No major open release reached European infrastructure this turn; disruption slowed diffusion and hosting.

By June, lights and registries were mostly on, because people kept them on. The Union had not become a target by declaration, but it had become a shelter by effort — crowded, dimmed, and still standing. The strain cost the centre support, but restoration work and continued care pilots kept legitimacy largely intact.
