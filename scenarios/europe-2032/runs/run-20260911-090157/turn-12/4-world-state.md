### Payouts against the freeze
Brussels forced the Entry-Job Transition Fund into first payment. ESF+ reallocation orders were signed in February, the levy fight was parked into a compromise — collect through national payroll now, reconcile later — and first cohorts in law, accountancy, software and customer operations were paid on enrolment. Employer groups protested but cheques went out. For a few thousand graduates it was the first tangible offer after two years of empty fairs.

It did not move the hiring numbers. Firms confirmed entry posts were simply not being reopened, with assistants doing the drafting, reconciling and triage. Anger at automation as the channel for displacement deepened, and student unions staged short blockades at two construction sites before police moved them on.

### Thin coverage tested
The automated patching line stalled. A dozen municipal utilities, spooked by quarantined imaging scans and false-positive shutdowns in small-town grids, paused the rollout and demanded manual review. ENISA kept field teams in place, adjudicating case by case with manual overrides, and held shield certificates to isolation drills rather than forcing the patch.

The choice avoided a break but left uncertified grids exposed through winter. Certified hospitals and banks absorbed probes cleanly; the new bio-detection sprint helped — wastewater feeds from hospital labs to the pooled swap with Japan, Korea and Canada went live, and response centres ran their first live rehearsals.

Relief was real where defences held, but the half-year read as concrete versus cohorts: defences installed and now partly exercised, jobs still missing. The holdout capital took patching help while again refusing to unwind its side arrangement.
