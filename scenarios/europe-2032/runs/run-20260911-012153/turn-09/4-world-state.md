### The cutoff returns
In September, administrators at several large hospital groups, two ministries and a cluster of industrial firms found their calls to the leading American model failing. No notice, no reason code, no appeal channel. Washington staff privately called it compliance enforcement; in Brussels it read as a second cutoff, eighteen months after the first.

Because the first cutoff had forced a move to European-hosted open models, the immediate clinical impact was smaller than in 2029. Sites that had stayed on the emergency stack kept running. Sites that had quietly drifted back to the American system for performance lost scheduling help and coding assistants for a week. The press framed it less as sabotage than as proof Brussels had not solved dependence.

### Jobs that do not come back
At the same time, autumn hiring data confirmed what unions had claimed since spring. Entry-level intakes in law firms, audit practices, software services, customer operations and back-office administration across France, Germany, the Netherlands and Spain were down sharply. Partners spoke openly of using assistants instead of trainees. Graduate unemployment became the lead story through October, overtaking the inquiry.

The Commission answered with what it could afford. Under existing funds, the AI Office with the cybersecurity agency and the research centre issued an implementing standard: critical operators must run different hosted weights, stagger updates, and keep enforced manual overrides to retain their shield certification. The Seville testing site, upgraded from pilot to proving ground, got authority to certify the pattern.

Visiting teams returned to the small municipalities named in the inquiry, fixing configurations street by street. For labour, unspent training and cohesion money was reprogrammed into a time-limited bridge — wage insurance tied to retraining for the affected junior cohorts.

The sprint was real but thin. Certification remained voluntary in law, mandatory only via loss of shield status. Retraining places numbered in the thousands against losses in the tens of thousands. Paris and Berlin accepted it because it required no new vote; mayors complained it pushed costs down to them. Concrete at the two factory sites stayed empty, and trust recovered only where the visiting vans had actually arrived.
