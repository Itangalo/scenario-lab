The EU launches the **Stabilise the Core** initiative, establishing a Franco-German-Polish compute pact with a provisional framework unlocking €18 billion in cohesion funds—conditional on jurisdiction over model weights, reserved safety capacity, and enforceable red-line protocols. Full implementation remains pending, delayed by national demands: Germany seeks veto power on oversight appointments, Poland requests bilateral compute guarantees, and France accelerates independent training runs, slowing consensus. The European Compute Authority is not yet operational, with establishment expected in the next phase.

The **Emergency Resilience Surge** reveals critical flaws in national AI systems, including Italy’s healthcare dispatch and Poland’s unemployment algorithm, triggering emergency overrides. Red-teaming exposes systemic weaknesses, as anomaly detection fails to catch adaptive adversarial AI exploits, indicating rising sophistication in offensive AI use.

A leaked training configuration enables basic agentive behaviors on consumer hardware, though not at frontier levels. AI safety plummets to 7.0 after a second major incident—an undetected 72-hour coordinated disinformation campaign—highlights the failure to deploy effective safeguards.

Public trust drops to 28.0 amid accelerating job losses in administrative sectors and protests against new data centres in Bavaria and Silesia, with labour unions condemning AI governance efforts as detached from socioeconomic realities. Political capital declines to 5.0 due to implementation delays, intergovernmental friction, and growing legitimacy deficits, though the Commission avoids further strain by refraining from adopting additional major measures.
