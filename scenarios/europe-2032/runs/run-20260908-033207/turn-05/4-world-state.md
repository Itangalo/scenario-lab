### Frontier Creeps Forward  
Global AI capability advances to 61.5 this turn, driven by closed-loop optimization in chip design and autonomous research cycles in leading labs. These systems now routinely generate novel circuit architectures and refine their own training pipelines, accelerating progress in a self-reinforcing loop. While no breakthrough event occurs, the steady climb underscores the structural shift now underway.

### Open Weights Gain Ground, But Gradually  
Openweight capability rises to 50.0, reflecting increased availability of fine-tuned agents on consumer-grade hardware and growing use of distributed training techniques in open communities. Underground forums continue to distribute repurposed research models optimized for cyber exploitation and disinformation, contributing to incremental capability gains. The diffusion remains organic—no frontier leak—and the gap with closed systems narrows only slightly, consistent with gradual spillover rather than sudden parity.

### Evaluation Mandate Lands, Limits Exposed  
The Strengthened AI Evaluation Mandate formally completes, with two major developers signing API access agreements under market-access pressure. Third-party evaluators begin limited real-time monitoring, marginally improving oversight. However, the emergent supply chain leak—where European semiconductor data silently boosted foreign labs—reveals a deeper vulnerability: control without sovereign infrastructure is porous. Evaluations can inspect models, but not the industrial AI systems that build them.

### Sovereignty Push Faces Portfolio Limits  
The EU proposes the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative, aiming to redirect €30 billion in cohesion funds to build physically and legally anchored compute clusters in Finland, Spain, and Romania. A new European Compute Authority is under discussion, with strict access tiers envisioned. However, no final commitments are made this turn due to governance bottlenecks and legal disputes with hyperscalers over jurisdictional anchoring. The initiative remains in drafting, and its launch is delayed. With the Tech Sovereignty Package and InvestAI Gigafactories still ongoing, the portfolio remains overloaded, preventing full commitment to another major measure this turn.
