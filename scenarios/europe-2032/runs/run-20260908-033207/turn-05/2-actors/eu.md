## In practice
We are treating the completion of the Strengthened AI Evaluation Mandate as a threshold: the framework is now legally operational, with market access conditioned on cooperation, and two major developers have entered into binding API access agreements under threat of suspension. The €400 million from the Innovation Fund has secured contracts with three third-party evaluators, and the European AI Office is standing up a 24/7 monitoring cell. But the limits of oversight without ownership are now stark. The emergent supply chain leak — proprietary calibration data from a European equipment maker enabling foreign labs to accelerate chip simulation — exposed that our technological edge is bleeding out through unsecured industrial AI use. This was not theft; it was normal business with invisible consequences.

At the same time, the U.S. election outcome shifts the landscape. Allied access to frontier capability is expanding, with joint evaluation and incident reporting frameworks emerging. The offer is real, but so is the trap: why build when you can borrow? We reject that logic. We are now redirecting €30 billion in unused cohesion funds and repurposing the delayed Gigafactory energy clauses to create sovereign compute clusters in Finland, Spain, and Romania — not just data centres, but physically secured, energy-secured, legally anchored facilities where EU-run models and evaluations can operate without foreign override. These will be governed by a new European Compute Authority, reporting to the Commission, with access strictly tiered: public research, vetted evaluators, and EU institutions first. We are negotiating with three European hyperscalers under condition of full jurisdictional anchoring — no cloud carve-outs, no foreign board veto. If they won’t play, we will build it ourselves.

The Tech Sovereignty Package remains on track, with fast-track permitting now tied to energy resilience clauses. But we are making clear: this is not about attracting investment for its own sake. It is about ensuring that the compute that matters — the kind that runs agents, evaluates models, and secures infrastructure — is under EU control. We are not waiting for consensus. The era of acceleration will not pause for deliberation, and our sovereignty will not be outsourced to goodwill.
