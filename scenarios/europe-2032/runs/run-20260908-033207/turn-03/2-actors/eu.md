## In practice  
We are executing the Cyber Shield Audit with intensified focus, driven by the emergent defence shift that for the first time in years gives us a window to harden rather than merely react. The Joint Cyber Unit in Tallinn has now integrated red-team agents trained on post-Feynman Burst patterns and is conducting live simulations in electricity transmission and port logistics systems across four participating states. These exercises are revealing not just vulnerabilities but decision lags in human-machine coordination that could be fatal under real swarm attack. We are treating the two holdout states as strategic liabilities: one has rejoined under the co-financing mechanism, which now covers up to 70% of remediation costs from repurposed Digital Europe Programme funds. The second, citing constitutional objections, remains outside — but we are invoking Article 21 of NIS2 to prepare conditional penalties, and we are offering a bilateral review with the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre to address sovereignty concerns without sacrificing collective security. Our aim is to complete first-phase assessments by June and deliver a classified, cross-validated report to national security councils by July, proving that the Union can enforce resilience even when unity is contested.

Simultaneously, we are fighting to operationalise the Strengthened AI Evaluation Mandate, now stalled in ECOFIN over its €1.2 billion funding ask. We are restructuring the proposal to front-load €400 million from the Innovation Fund and tie the remainder to verifiable milestones in evaluator onboarding and test protocol development, with matching contributions from member states scaled to GDP. The European AI Office is hiring, but without budget certainty, it cannot contract third-party evaluators or secure API access from frontier developers. We are framing this not as a cost but as insurance: the eval anomaly this turn proves we are already flying blind above 52.0 capability. Acting under uncertainty means accepting imperfect tools — but not accepting inaction. The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track, with permitting fast-tracked in Finland and Portugal, but their legitimacy depends on parallel progress in control: we are building capacity, but if we cannot govern it, we will not own it.
