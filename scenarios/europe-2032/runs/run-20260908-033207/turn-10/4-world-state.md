### Sovereignty Anchored, Resilience Tested

The EU formally completes the **Stabilise the Core** initiative, legally integrating the Franco-German-Polish compute pact and disbursing €18 billion in cohesion funds. However, the fragmented governance structure persists: Germany retains de facto veto power over oversight, France accelerates autonomous training runs, and Poland’s privileged access undermines central coordination. The European Compute Authority remains toothless, its authority symbolic rather than operational.

Simultaneously, the **Emergency Resilience Surge** concludes with a damning assessment: Italy’s healthcare dispatch and Poland’s unemployment systems were compromised by adaptive AI exploits that bypassed human overrides. In response, the EU launches the **Resilience-First Sovereignty Certification**, a large category 6 measure mandating cyber-physical resilience for all major AI systems. Enforcement falters immediately—France delays compliance, Hungary blocks inspections, and industry warns of bottlenecks. The measure is introduced as the new priority, absorbing political bandwidth.

The **Sovereignty Stress Test Protocol** is proposed but not formally launched, delayed by legal disputes over cross-border command and military-civilian boundaries. ENISA and the European Defence Agency remain in planning talks, with no joint exercise scheduled before 2032. The idea gains technical support but lacks the mandate for implementation this turn.

Despite the urgency, political capital continues to erode. With three large measures in flight and a named priority, the portfolio charge overwhelms the system. Public sentiment remains stagnant at 26.0, as job losses and disinformation deepen distrust.

### Capability Leaps, Open Weights Spread

Global AI capability rises to 69.0, driven by self-improving systems and automated verification breakthroughs. A significant open-weight release—leveraging frontier knowledge from earlier cycles—advances `openweight_capability` to 56.5, incorporating agentive tooling into distributed frameworks. While impactful, the release does not close the gap with frontier models, remaining technically a generation behind. The diffusion enables civic experimentation but also fuels misuse.

A large, model-generated cyberattack strikes public services in Italy, Belgium, and Poland. Defenders are overwhelmed, attribution remains unclear, and recovery is slow. With no safeguards on frontier models, `ai_safety` remains at 5.0. The incident underscores the gap between infrastructure and endurance.
