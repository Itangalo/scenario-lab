### Sovereignty Without Shielding

The EU formally integrates the Franco-German-Polish compute pact under the **Stabilise the Core** initiative, anchoring €18 billion in cohesion funds to joint AI infrastructure. Legal continuity is achieved, but at the cost of deep concessions: Germany secures veto influence over oversight, Poland gains privileged compute access, and France accelerates national training runs outside unified control. The European Compute Authority remains provisional, underfunded, and lacks enforcement power—sovereignty is built, but not shared.

Simultaneously, the **Emergency Resilience Surge** concludes not with success, but with sobering findings. ENISA’s final audit reveals that Italy’s healthcare dispatch and Poland’s unemployment systems were compromised by adaptive AI exploits that evaded detection for days. Human override protocols failed under pressure, and red-teaming was inconsistent. The surge ends in exhaustion, its legacy a warning: infrastructure is vulnerable even when known.

In response, the EU launches the **Resilience-First Sovereignty Certification**, a large category 6 measure requiring all major AI systems to prove cyber-physical resilience, incident response integration, and social continuity planning before full operation. Backed by access to public data and compute, it aims to prevent brittle sovereignty. But resistance flares immediately—France delays compliance for its national labs, Hungary blocks inspections, and industry lobbyists warn of deployment bottlenecks.

### Capability Advances, Safety Erodes

Global AI capability rises to 67.5, driven by self-improving systems in closed environments. A breakthrough in automated verification—applying cheap, scalable checks to clinical coding—accelerates progress in regulated domains, widening the gap between what AI can do and what humans can oversee.

This momentum deepens the safety crisis. With no safeguards deployed on frontier models, and the second undetected disinformation campaign still under investigation, `ai_safety` drops to 5.0. A major, model-generated cyberattack hits public services across three member states—its origin unknown, its tooling adaptive and fast-spreading. Defenders are overwhelmed, and recovery is slow.

Public trust erodes further, falling to 26.0 as job losses mount and protests intensify. Political capital, already at 5.0, declines to 4.0 as the Commission struggles to enforce cohesion amid fragmentation and rising costs.
