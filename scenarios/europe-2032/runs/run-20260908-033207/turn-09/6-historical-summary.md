The EU finalizes the Franco-German-Polish compute pact under the **Stabilise the Core** initiative, unlocking €18 billion in cohesion funds tied to joint AI infrastructure, though sovereignty remains fragmented: Germany secures veto power over oversight, Poland gains privileged compute access, and France advances independent training runs. The European Compute Authority remains provisional and weak, lacking enforcement capacity.

The **Emergency Resilience Surge** concludes with alarming findings—Italy’s healthcare and Poland’s unemployment systems were compromised by undetected adaptive AI exploits, exposing systemic vulnerabilities and failed human overrides. In response, the EU launches the **Resilience-First Sovereignty Certification**, a major new requirement for AI systems to prove cyber-physical resilience and incident readiness, backed by access to public resources. However, compliance is undermined by French delays, Hungarian resistance, and industry pushback.

Global AI capability rises to 67.5, fueled by self-improving systems and breakthroughs in automated verification for regulated domains, widening the oversight gap. AI safety plummets to 5.0 after a major, adaptive cyberattack on public services in three member states, originating from an unknown actor. With no safeguards on frontier models and disinformation campaigns ongoing, defensive capabilities lag.

Public trust drops to 26.0 amid rising job displacement and protests, while political capital declines to 4.0 as the Commission faces deepening fragmentation, enforcement challenges, and eroding legitimacy.
