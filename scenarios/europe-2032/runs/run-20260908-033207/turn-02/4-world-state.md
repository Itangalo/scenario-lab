### Acceleration Continues

The pace of AI advancement shows no sign of slowing. A new wave of frontier model refinements, building on last turn’s “Feynman Burst,” pushes `ai_capability` to 56.0. Offensive cyber capabilities are now embedded as standard, and early adopters report autonomous penetration testing achieving in hours what previously required teams of experts over weeks. The gap between closed and open systems narrows further, with derivative models based on leaked architectures reaching `openweight_capability` of 44.0—still behind, but closing.

### Control Under Pressure

The EU responds with the **Strengthened AI Evaluation Mandate**, a large category 1 measure aimed at restoring confidence in oversight. However, negotiations over funding stall in the ECOFIN Council, with Germany and the Netherlands demanding stricter cost containment. The European AI Office begins staffing up, but full authority remains unimplemented. No real-time evaluations occur this turn, and the mandate yields no operational impact.

Meanwhile, the **Cyber Shield Audit** advances technically in four member states, with red-teaming revealing critical flaws in grid control systems. Simulations expose delayed detection of swarm attacks, reinforcing concerns. The co-financing incentive brings one holdout state into compliance, but the second—citing constitutional concerns over centralized enforcement—remains outside the audit. Full-phase assessments are delayed to Q3, pushing back the timeline.

### Political Strain Mounts

The portfolio’s cost hits hard. With three large measures in flight and a named priority, political capital erodes. The lack of visible progress on safety or resilience, combined with rising capability, deepens institutional anxiety. Public sentiment holds at 40.0—neither worsening nor improving—as citizens watch debates over enforcement without seeing results.

An eval anomaly—unexplained behaviour in a pre-release model—fuels speculation but no formal disclosure. Defensive tooling advances are whispered in technical circles, but no shift in the offence-defence balance is confirmed.
