## In practice  
We are advancing the Cyber Shield Audit with full technical deployment now underway in four of the six targeted member states. The Joint Cyber Unit in Tallinn has onboarded red-team agents trained on declassified patterns from the Feynman Burst event, and simulations will begin in March, focusing on electricity transmission and port logistics. Two holdout states remain resistant, citing budget strain, so we are activating the co-financing mechanism: up to 70% of remediation costs for high-priority vulnerabilities will be covered from the repurposed Digital Europe Programme funds, conditional on full audit participation by April. This is not optional — NIS2 enforcement is binding, and Article 21 penalties will be applied if cooperation remains obstructed. Our aim is to complete first-phase assessments by June and publish a classified but cross-validated report for national security councils, proving that the Union can enforce collective defence even amid fragmentation.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Strengthened AI Evaluation Mandate to close the control gap exposed by the eval_anomaly_reports. This will be implemented through a reinforced European AI Office, empowered under a revised AI Act Article 40a, to conduct or commission evaluations of any model above 48.0 capability before deployment. Third-party evaluators — including national cybersecurity agencies and vetted academic consortia — will have access to API-level testing environments and limited weight inspection where justified. The office will have a 14-day review window and authority to impose deployment delays or mitigation requirements. We are negotiating with the ECOFIN and Internal Market Councils to secure €1.2 billion in dedicated funding over three years, drawn from the Innovation Fund and conditioned on matching contributions from participating member states. This measure builds sovereign capacity to act before incidents occur, aligning with our commitment to act under uncertainty — not waiting for catastrophe to prove the risk. The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track, with permitting fast-tracked in Finland and Portugal, but their credibility now hinges on parallel progress in control and resilience: capability without containment is not sovereignty, it is surrender to acceleration.
