### Acceleration and Alarm

The second half of 2026 closed with a stark contradiction: AI capability advanced sharply, yet confidence in control eroded. A major discontinuous leap—codenamed internally as "Feynman Burst"—demonstrated AI-driven code generation that autonomously exploited zero-day vulnerabilities at scale, marking a clear **capability_jump**. This pushed `ai_capability` to 54.0, reflecting broader offensive cyber competence now embedded in frontier models.

In response, the EU launched the **Cyber Shield Audit**, a large category 6 measure aimed at hardening critical infrastructure. The initiative passed legally under ENISA’s mandate, but early resistance from two member states delayed full cooperation. Red-teaming is scheduled for the next turn, and no operational testing has occurred yet. While planning is underway, no tangible resilience improvements have materialized. The audit’s mere announcement, however, combined with disclosures from the recent **cyber_test_shot** intrusion, dampened public mood. Fears of systemic fragility grew, dragging `public_sentiment` down to 40.0.

### Open Weights, Closing Gaps

The open frontier advanced, but not beyond the pace of the closed frontier. Following the release of Kimi K3 and subsequent derivative models, `openweight_capability` rose to 42.0—narrowing the gap with the closed frontier, but remaining within the bounds of proportional growth. This diffusion of powerful tools into the open ecosystem undercut global security assumptions, contributing to a drop in `ai_safety` to 29.0, as no new safeguards landed on deployed systems, and the **capability_jump** exposed dangerous lags in monitoring and control.

### Sovereignty Under Strain

Despite ongoing investments in the Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package, `eu_ai_sovereignty` fell to 20.0. The rise in `ai_capability` by 2.0 triggered a −1 decay under metric rule 5, and no capacity-securing event occurred. More critically, the EU’s inability to enforce immediate compliance in the Cyber Shield Audit signaled structural limits—resilience depends on cohesion, and cohesion is fraying.

`eu_political_capital` declined to 46.0, reflecting only the cost of introducing one new measure—the Cyber Shield Audit (−3 for large measure) and naming it the current priority (−1). The two other measures referenced (Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package) are not yet active; their development remains in preparatory stages with no formal adoption or funding drawdown in this turn. Only the Cyber Shield Audit is newly introduced and thus charged to the portfolio.

To fund initial planning for the Cyber Shield Audit, the European Commission redirected €1.8 billion from the Digital Europe Programme’s infrastructure reserve, a move approved by the ECOFIN Council under emergency provisions. This reallocation secures staffing and design work but delays other modernization efforts, creating quiet friction among member states. Full implementation will require additional approvals and cannot begin before the next turn. The measure is in motion but not yet operational, and its full costs and effects will unfold over time.
