## New measure
Cyber Shield Audit  
A binding programme to harden and verify defences across EU critical infrastructure against AI-powered intrusion.

`Category:` 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 4  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to resilience, -5 to public_sentiment (due to alarm from audit disclosures), indirect pressure on ai_safety via demonstrated vulnerability  
`Applies to:` particular member states (those with critical infrastructure in energy, water, and ports)

## Priority
Cyber Shield Audit — because the cyber_test_shot proved that existing detection failed completely, and without verified defences, sovereignty and public trust collapse together.

## In practice  
We are launching the Cyber Shield Audit through the ENISA legal base under Article 11a of Regulation (EU) 2019/881, with a mandate to assess and upgrade intrusion detection, access control, and incident response across electricity transmission operators, major ports, and water utilities in six member states where exposure was confirmed or suspected. The European Commission’s Directorate-General for Energy and DG MOVE will lead coordination, with technical execution by a newly reinforced Joint Cyber Unit in Tallinn. The audit will include red-teaming using controlled AI agents trained to simulate known offensive patterns, with results classified but compliance enforced through the NIS2 Directive’s Article 21 penalties.  

Resistance is already visible from two member states with underfunded cyber units, who argue the audit risks exposing systemic weaknesses during a period of market fragility. In return for early participation, we are offering co-financing of up to 70% for remediation measures identified in the first round, drawn from the unused portion of the Digital Europe Programme’s 2026 allocation. The message is clear: vulnerability is not optional, but support for fixing it will be generous. This is not just about patching systems — it is about proving that the Union can act where the threat has already landed, not only where it might go. The Tech sovereignty package and InvestAI Gigafactories continue on track, with the first environmental impact assessments for Gigafactory sites underway in Finland and Portugal, but we are now anchoring their legitimacy in demonstrable protection of existing systems, not just promises of future capacity.
