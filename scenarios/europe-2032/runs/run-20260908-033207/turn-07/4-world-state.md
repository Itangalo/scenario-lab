### A Surge Against Fragility

The EU launches the **Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Systems**, a large category 6 measure, in direct response to the recent agentic breach and the explosive revelation that an AI-assisted social benefits system wrongfully denied thousands of claims over two years. The scandal, confirmed by a national ombudsman, triggers widespread outrage and deepens public distrust in AI-mediated governance. With cyber capabilities now demonstrably advancing—AI systems autonomously crafting and deploying exploits that bypass layered defences—the Commission acts swiftly under Article 114 TFEU, expanding NIS2 obligations to all public AI decision systems.

Binding hardening mandates are issued, red-teaming protocols co-developed with ENISA, and €45 billion in cohesion funds repurposed for logging, overrides, and anomaly detection. Rapid audit teams are scheduled for deployment to France, Italy, and Poland by early 2030. A pan-European incident response cell gains emergency override authority for cross-border systems.

### Sovereignty Gains, But Safety Crumbles

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative** both complete this turn, delivering tangible compute infrastructure under EU legal control. This boosts `eu_ai_sovereignty` despite broader strain. However, the **capability_jump**—a leap in autonomous cyber exploitation—combined with stagnant safety practices, triggers a sharp drop in `ai_safety`. Developers have not implemented legible safeguards, and the ombudsman’s findings expose systemic opacity in deployed systems.

Public sentiment falls further as the scandal reframes AI not as a distant risk, but as an active source of injustice. Labour groups protest, and regional leaders resist centralised mandates, citing sovereignty over social policy.

### Political Capital Erodes

Three large measures remain in flight, including the new priority. With `eu_political_capital` already low, the portfolio charge overwhelms the system. The proposal bonus is insufficient to offset mounting costs, and the legitimacy deficit deepens as trust in institutions wanes.
