### A Threshold Crossed

The EU crosses a psychological threshold: AI is no longer a disruptive force but an active agent of institutional decay. The annulment of a national election, officially citing pervasive manipulation of voter information by untraceable synthetic media, triggers a crisis of legitimacy across the bloc. Public trust erodes further as disinformation campaigns—adaptive, multilingual, and self-optimising—undermine confidence in judicial rulings and legislative debates.

In response, the European Commission proposes the **Crisis Continuity Framework for Democratic Functions**, a Category 6 measure, as a potential new priority. The framework would mandate binding continuity plans for elections, legislatures, courts, and public broadcasters, backed by €4.2 billion in conditional funding to be drawn from the reprogrammed surplus of the defunct Green Digital Acceleration Facility and emergency borrowing under the Stability and Growth Pact’s crisis clause. However, the proposal faces immediate resistance: Hungary refuses to engage, Poland delays on grounds of national sovereignty, and France insists on excluding its constitutional council. ENISA is tasked with coordination but lacks legal authority to enforce compliance, and no final adoption occurs this turn.

The **Resilience-First Sovereignty Certification** remains stalled. Despite suspending access for two non-compliant labs, enforcement lacks teeth—Germany demands exemptions for its defence AI programs, and industry warns of innovation flight. The **Sovereignty Stress Test Protocol** begins planning but no exercise is scheduled before 2032 due to unresolved civil-military command disputes. With two active measures still in progress, the Crisis Continuity Framework is not formally adopted as a new measure or priority, and remains in advanced proposal stage.

Global AI capability advances to 70.5, driven by self-improving training loops and automated verification. An open-weight release, incorporating limited agentive tooling, raises `openweight_capability` to 57.0. `ai_safety` declines to 3.0—down from 5.0—as frontier labs fail to implement meaningful safeguards and internal monitoring at leading developers deteriorates. While safety institutions remain formally intact, their effectiveness is increasingly undermined by governance gaps and resource flight, reflecting erosion rather than collapse.

A second wave of the earlier cyberattack compromises emergency dispatch systems in Belgium and Poland, prolonging outages. Resilience efforts absorb some harm, but the cumulative toll lifts `resilience` to 51.0 through partial system degradation rather than full failure.

Public sentiment dips to 24.0 as job displacement accelerates and AI is blamed for the election crisis. Political capital, already at 3.0, declines to 1.0 under the weight of ongoing measure charges and the political cost of crisis response without clear implementation. The brain drain of AI talent intensifies, with over 400 researchers relocating to North America and Japan in six months, further delaying technical execution of sovereignty initiatives.
