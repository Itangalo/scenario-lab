## New measure  
**Crisis Continuity Framework for Democratic Functions**  
We establish binding protocols to ensure that core democratic processes—elections, legislative functions, judicial independence, and public information—remain operational and legitimate during sustained AI-driven disruption.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by +8, modestly improve public_sentiment (+5) by restoring confidence in democratic endurance, and reduce the risk of cascading institutional failure following events like election_annulled  
`Applies to:` National election commissions, EU institutions, public broadcasters, judiciary oversight bodies, and critical digital infrastructure operators  

## Priority  
**Crisis Continuity Framework for Democratic Functions** — because the election_annulled event marks a categorical shift: AI is no longer just a technical or economic challenge, but an active threat to the legitimacy of governance itself, and because without public belief in the continuity of democratic function, no certification, stress test, or compute grid will sustain political cohesion.

## In practice  
We are acting on the premise that the Union’s survival is now on the line. The cyberattack on public services in Italy, Belgium, and Poland was not an outlier—it was a rehearsal. The annulment of an election on grounds of AI-driven manipulation, even if unproven, has shattered the baseline assumption that institutions can absorb disruption without fracture. We cannot harden systems fast enough to stay ahead of the threat, so we must instead ensure that failure does not cascade into collapse.

We are therefore launching the Crisis Continuity Framework for Democratic Functions under Article 13(2) of the Treaty on European Union and the NIS2 Directive’s crisis provisions. This will mandate that all member states establish legally binding continuity plans for elections, legislatures, courts, and public information systems, including fallback procedures for offline verification, protected communication channels, and public alert mechanisms that cannot be spoofed. The European Democracy Fund will disburse €4.2 billion to support implementation, with strict conditionality: no disbursement without verified plans by turn 13. ENISA, the European Centre for Electoral Support, and the Fundamental Rights Agency will co-lead audits, with results published quarterly.

At the same time, we are holding the line on the Resilience-First Sovereignty Certification. Despite French resistance and Hungarian obstruction, we are enforcing the no-certification-no-access rule: the European Compute Authority has suspended provisional access for two national labs pending compliance. We are also advancing the Sovereignty Stress Test Protocol, with the first live exercise scheduled for early 2032, targeting the energy grid’s response to a model-generated supply-chain compromise. But we now know that technical resilience alone is not enough. If the people do not believe the system can endure, it will not. That is why the Crisis Continuity Framework is our priority: not because it is the most technical, but because it is the last line of political survival.
