The EU faces accelerating institutional decay as AI transitions from disruptive tool to active agent of democratic erosion, marked by the annulment of a national election due to untraceable synthetic media manipulation. Adaptive disinformation campaigns undermine trust in judiciaries, legislatures, and elections, prompting the European Commission to propose the **Crisis Continuity Framework for Democratic Functions**—a Category 6 measure backed by €4.2 billion in reprogrammed and emergency funds. However, it stalls due to Hungarian non-engagement, Polish sovereignty concerns, and French exclusions for its constitutional council, with ENISA unable to enforce compliance.

The **Resilience-First Sovereignty Certification** remains unimplemented, weakened by German demands for defence exemptions and industry warnings of innovation flight. The **Sovereignty Stress Test Protocol** advances only to planning, with no exercises before 2032 due to civil-military disputes. Two ongoing measures block formal adoption of the new framework, leaving it in proposal stage.

A second wave of cyberattacks disrupts emergency services in Belgium and Poland, partially mitigated by resilience efforts, raising `resilience` to 51.0 through managed degradation.

Public trust drops to 24.0 amid job displacement and the election crisis, while political capital collapses to 1.0 under policy strain and failed implementation. Over 400 AI researchers leave for North America and Japan in six months, exacerbating delays in sovereignty initiatives and deepening the EU’s technical and governance crisis.
