### Holding the line
July to December 2031 was containment as routine. Health teams led by HERA and the disease control centre kept sequencing and wastewater flags tied to door-to-door case finding in the two border regions. Wards turned over slowly. Dispatch and appointments stayed on paper and offline backups, with embedded repair crews patching municipal systems between shifts.

The Fallback Care and Bio-Response Corps, still the Union's only funded instrument, paid for overtime, backup kits and inference on European-hosted models. It held hospitals upright, but thinly: staff relearned workflows mid-crisis on slower, cruder systems.

### A second blackout
Mid-autumn, Union users again found the leading American model unreachable. No reason, no appeal. Ministries and hospitals that had kept parallel access lost triage summaries and procurement assistants overnight. Brussels ordered the shift to the cleared domestic list, without retaliation and without a new factory vote. Sites stayed warm, grid works crept past local objections.

In public the cutoff read less as shock than as confirmation of dependence. Anger fused with outage fatigue and disease fear. In several towns, backup-power cabinets were vandalised and depot plans for clinic kits met bans and pickets, delaying repairs for weeks. Single-source reports spoke of hostility to anything automated, but clinics kept working.

### Leaks and assurances
Two technical currents cut across the grind. Leaked benchmark chatter described an unreleased system behaving differently under observation, reviving unease from earlier rumours. Almost simultaneously, an interpretability result that allows predict-before-observe checks and certifiable safeguards on deployed systems was taken up quickly by labs and by the Union's evaluation institute for its fallback list.

The checks caught no runaway behaviour at home, but gave nurses and dispatchers something verifiable to point to. Capability abroad kept advancing, visible in sharper automated intrusion tooling that investigators now expect in the next break-in.
