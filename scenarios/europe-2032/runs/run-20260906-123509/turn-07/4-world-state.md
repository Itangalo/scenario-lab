### The Storm Breaks

A wave of AI-driven ransomware cripples regional health networks across three member states in hours, exposing the fragility of cross-border crisis response. The attack, generated by advanced models, spreads through automated lateral movement, overwhelming national CERTs. In the aftermath, the EU activates emergency protocols under Article 122a TFEU, granting the European AI Emergency Response Corps temporary coordination authority during cascading AI incidents.

Despite the urgency, legal resistance hardens. Three member states refuse to cede operational control, even temporarily, citing constitutional sovereignty. A compromise is brokered: national authorities retain command, but during declared emergencies, tactical coordination shifts to Brussels. Implementation remains partial, with only seven of twelve high-risk nodes integrating wastewater sequencing by turn’s end. The activation is strictly time-bound and does not constitute a permanent mandate expansion; planning for a formal, lasting upgrade is acknowledged as necessary but deferred.

### Measures Close, Momentum Shifts

The *InvestAI Gigafactories* and *Resilience Surge Protocol* complete this turn. Gigafactories add meaningful domestic compute, slightly offsetting dependence. The Surge’s partial rollout strengthens cyber defences in finance and energy, but healthcare and municipalities remain exposed. The *BioShield Rapid Detection Network* is formally shelved due to funding gridlock.

No new measure is launched this turn. The *Unified AI Incident Response Mandate* enters internal drafting as a prospective category 6 initiative, with early technical and legal scoping underway. It is not formally proposed, and no priority shift occurs. The existing portfolio remains under strain: four large measures still in flight, including the ongoing *European AI Emergency Response Corps* enhancement. The −1 priority charge in the notepad is retained from prior turns; no new priority is designated.

### Capability Leaps, Safety Erodes

Global `ai_capability` jumps +4.0 to 80.0, driven by self-improving models. Verification widens to clinical coding, accelerating medical AI deployment. A major breakthrough delivers gene therapies for two rare diseases — but access is restricted to patients in countries with direct lab deals, underscoring EU dependency. Public sentiment rises slightly on the news, though resentment simmers.

`openweight_capability` advances to 60.0, reflecting incremental spillover from prior open-source tooling investments and limited replication of closed gains. With no safety improvements landing and opacity deepening, `ai_safety` drops sharply to 7.0. Resilience gains +5 from completed measures. `eu_ai_sovereignty` remains at 15.0 — the completion of *InvestAI Gigafactories* delivers a +5 boost, but this is fully offset by −1 decay from the 4.0 rise in global capability and an additional −3 loss due to the failure to advance structural incident response capabilities this turn. Political capital dips to 8.0 under portfolio load, despite a +4 proposal bonus for the shelved *BioShield* measure.
