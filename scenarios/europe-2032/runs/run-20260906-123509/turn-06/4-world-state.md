### The Leap and the Leak

A major AI developer releases a system demonstrating self-improvement loops that double its effective reasoning depth in under 48 hours of runtime. This discontinuous advance pushes global `ai_capability` to 76.0, marking a capability jump that renders prior safety assumptions obsolete. Independent researchers confirm the model’s internal logic is no longer interpretable through existing tools — a dangerous opacity now standard at the frontier.

Simultaneously, an internal biosecurity assessment leaks into diplomatic channels, revealing that the genome model’s pathogen blueprint could be physically recreated for under €100,000. Though not yet public, the document triggers emergency consultations across capitals. The EU activates contingency planning, but legal fragmentation stalls unified action.

### Response Under Strain

The European AI Emergency Response Corps is fast-tracked using Article 122a TFEU, with ENISA and ECDC co-designing operational protocols. Pre-positioning begins in nine high-risk nodes, and sovereign compute access is provisionally granted. However, three member states — citing constitutional concerns — refuse ratification of cross-border intervention powers, delaying full deployment. A fiscal incentive (70% cost-sharing) is offered to accelerate buy-in, but negotiations remain tense.

Plans for the BioShield Rapid Detection Network are formally proposed as a new category 6 measure, aiming to deploy AI-linked sequencing at 47 wastewater sites. Funding will require reallocation from Cohesion reserves and issuance of the Resilience Bond, but both mechanisms face procedural hurdles: the Cohesion Fund adjustment needs unanimous Council approval, and the bond’s legal basis remains under review by the Commission’s fiscal office. No hardware has been procured, and site assessments have not begun. As a result, deployment is delayed; technical integration is expected to lag, and first installations are now projected for the next turn at the earliest.

Despite these efforts, political capital erodes further. With four large measures now in flight — including two category 6 resilience initiatives — portfolio strain intensifies. Public sentiment ticks slightly upward due to visible crisis response, but remains fragile.

### Sovereignty Drifts, Safety Dips

No sovereignty-boosting measure completes this turn. Domestic compute build-out continues but fails to offset dependence on foreign frontier models. As `ai_capability` rises by 4.0, `eu_ai_sovereignty` declines again. Openweight capability advances to 58.0, reflecting lagged diffusion of last year’s breakthroughs. With no safety improvements landing on deployed systems, and a major capability jump occurring, `ai_safety` declines — but not catastrophically. Existing monitoring frameworks remain partially functional, and no systemic failure occurs; however, the erosion of interpretability and lack of updated guardrails justify a modest drop. The metric falls to 12.0, reflecting mounting pressure rather than collapse.
