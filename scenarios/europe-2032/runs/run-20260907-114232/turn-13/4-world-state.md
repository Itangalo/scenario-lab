### The Chokepoint Gambit

With AI capability frozen at 95.0—growth stalled not by restraint but by the Taiwan blockade—the EU makes its boldest move yet. It activates export controls on critical semiconductor components, including EU-made EUV pellicles and gas purification systems, conditioning access on compliance with its ban on recursive self-improvement. The Council amends Regulation 2021/821, and the European Defence Agency begins coordinating end-use verification with supplier networks. The move is framed not as trade policy but as a continuity-of-government operation.

### Fragmented Enforcement, Unified Rhetoric

The new measure, *Enforce Recursive Self-Improvement Halt via Semiconductor Supply-Chain Controls*, is declared the EU’s top priority. Yet with political capital at just 1.0, enforcement is precarious. While France and Germany back the controls, Dutch and Belgian semiconductor firms lobby Washington for exemptions, citing existential threats to ASML’s ecosystem. The U.S. administration, under its RETRENCHMENT posture, neither opposes nor supports the move—quietly allowing its hyperscalers to seek alternative supply routes through Japan.

### A Coalition of the Willing?

The EU fast-tracks participation in an emerging international monitoring initiative, offering technical support to Japan, South Korea, and Nordic states in exchange for joint enforcement. Early coordination begins, but no formal commitments are secured. Meanwhile, €8bn is redirected from ReACT-AI to rebuild public research enclaves in Toulouse, Leuven, and Dresden, focused on openweight model auditing and incident forensics.

### No Relief at Home

Public sentiment remains at 15.0. Protests intensify outside data centres in Frankfurt and Milan, now targeting semiconductor logistics hubs. No visible benefit offsets the fear of disruption. The Anti-Coercion Instrument is placed on standby, but no member state has yet defected—only wavered.

### Stalled Gains, Settled Losses

The *Public Trust Observatory* and *Emergency Sovereign Continuity Directive* formally complete, but their impact is muted. Sovereignty remains at 11.0, safety at 5.0, resilience at 47.0—no metric shifts. The system is too fragile for gains to land.
