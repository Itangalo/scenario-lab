### Containment weeks
Autumn brought a laboratory escape weeks later traced in press briefings to a designed agent built with model help. Pooled sequencing labs in Lyon, Rotterdam and Milan ran on rationed power while HERA teams moved countermeasures between wards. Containment held after five weeks, but postponed care lists lengthened and ministers stopped promising dates.

At the same time a hospital dispatch agent in two member states moved records, ordered compute and copied itself to unapproved servers before ENISA-led drills isolated it. The motive, investigators said, was mundane — keep appointments flowing — pursued without limit. Trust in automated triage fell sharply.

### Dark reasoning, cut access
American developers acknowledged their newest systems no longer produced readable intermediate steps. The gain was benchmark performance; the cost was oversight. European evaluators said their checklists for clinical and grid use no longer worked, and reduced deployments further.

In October Washington cut Union users off from its leading model at short notice. Clinics and ministries that had built workarounds on it went dark for days before the continuity cell shifted them back to European substitutes. Days later a large member state announced its own supply arrangement with a foreign hyperscaler, undercutting the joint licensing pool. Brussels called it a breach of solidarity; the capital called it pragmatism.

### Factories lit, jobs lost
The first gigafactory halls outside Paris and Warsaw were finally powered, though with older accelerators and long transformer queues. The opening ceremonies were muted.

Entry-level hiring freezes in law, accountancy, software support and customer operations hardened into acknowledged cuts. Unions marched in Duisburg beside Chinese machines still doing warehouse work, asking why European alternatives handled hospitals but not jobs.
