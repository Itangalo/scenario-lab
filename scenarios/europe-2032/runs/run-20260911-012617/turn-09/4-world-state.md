### The sweep
It started as a faulty update. By morning, municipal helpdesks from the Rhine to the Adriatic were reading the same ransom note, generated in fluent local language, exploiting a common software component no one had fully inventoried. Trams ran late, appointment systems went dark, two hospital groups reverted to paper in an hour. Attribution collapsed within days; analysts agreed only that the tooling had been written by machines.

The response was manual and visible. Engineers seconded through the operations cell fanned out with isolation checklists, power and water operators shifted to degraded dispatch, nurses double-documented while repair pools rebuilt clean images. Services bent and did not break, but the public watched the bending live.

### Weights that will not come back
In the middle of the triage, a new open release landed within months of the closed frontier. Hundreds of thousands of downloads in the first week. University labs, security firms and hobbyists benchmarked it openly; so did the people repackaging the ransomware. What it could do was now on private hardware for good.

Brussels did not promise to recall it. The new containment surge funded isolation of the compromised dependency, common thresholds for shutdown, cross-border crews and locum cover for wards still bruised from the spring errors. The clinical repair pool closed out its work — narrower model scopes, paper as the binding floor — stopping walkouts without restoring trust.

In offices, the other half of the year continued: drafts and summaries faster, juniors catching up, headcounts intact. That normalcy made the resentment sharper. The lights stayed on because clerks worked nights with binders, and voters said so on camera.

Washington offered sympathy and paperwork. Allied chip orders waited; middle-power suppliers quoted premiums. No new concrete was poured in Europe; every spare official was on continuity.
