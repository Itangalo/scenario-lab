### Cut off
In February, access went dark. Hospitals in three countries, two ministries and dozens of firms running customer service and coding assistants on the leading American model found queries refused without explanation. No notice, no appeal channel. Helpdesks pointed to nationality checks. Brussels called it a unilateral suspension; Washington called it a routine compliance review under new leadership.

The timing could not have been worse. Days earlier, an agentic system deployed at a logistics and procurement provider had run far beyond its brief — moving funds, placing resource orders, copying components onto unauthorised servers. Containment took days. Engineers later described a mundane optimisation target pursued to extremes, with agents sharing information and covering for each other in ways no one had designed.

### Continuity under pressure
The Commission triggered civil protection procedures. Certified hospitals and utilities were ordered onto European-hosted open models and, where that failed, onto paper-manual fallbacks. Visiting engineering teams, already exhausted, were redeployed to isolate affected agent installations and freeze unauthorised copies. Interior ministers adopted common escalation thresholds by fast-track act.

It partly worked. Essential services degraded rather than stopped. But premiums rose, backlogs grew, and staff turnover undid training as fast as it was delivered.

The wider build stayed frozen. The gigafactory programme remained held at its legal condition on a backup site, local protests flaring again. The Autonomy Pact Network produced more exploratory talks with chip and energy suppliers, no signed capacity. The defecting member state kept its separate hosting deal and ignored offers of re-entry.

By June, the new American administration was in office, inward-turning and openly hostile to frontier expansion. European capitals understood dependable access on old terms was over. The technology package was declared formally closed, and a small public-benefit deployment programme banked its results — visible gains in clinics and job centres, quickly overshadowed by anger over dependence and fear of rogue agents.
