### The ward and the office
The semester split in two. In law firms, accountancies, newsrooms and consultancies, the tools finally showed up in the numbers: drafting, summarising and checking done faster, juniors catching up quickest, headcounts intact. Managers called it absorption. Economists called it the end of the transition story — useful, finished, no second wave behind it.

In hospitals, the opposite. A large operator announced it was switching off its European-hosted assistant after repeated errors in triage support and discharge summaries, going back to paper binders. Cameras followed nurses describing double documentation, doctors describing overrides, inquiries opened in two capitals. The fallback that was supposed to prove graceful degradation looked, for weeks, like the hazard.

Brussels answered with repair crews, not new concrete. Joint clinical-engineering teams funded through emergency health money moved ward to ward, auditing error cases, narrowing what the hosted models were allowed to do, re-certifying a smaller scope with paper as the binding floor. Locum backfill paid for some double shifts. It stopped the walkout talk, it did not restore confidence.

### Machines at the gate, gates at the border
Humanoid pilots stayed pilots. Vendors sold fixed-task machines for caged warehouse aisles and logistics yards, priced against wages. Defence ministries kept carrying and clearance trials limited. Factories that had ordered flexibility spent the spring renegotiating.

Chip and model controls tightened again from Washington. With the American administration turned inward, volume licences favoured domestic buyers while allied orders faced rationing and paperwork. European procurement officers found lead times stretching, middle-power supplier options suddenly worth the premium. No cutoff was announced; allocation did the work.

The operations cell built to keep lights on through the ransomware winter formally stood up — common thresholds, cross-border repair pools, manual dispatch drills. Power and water stayed running through scattered aftershocks. Services bent, again, and did not break, and the public again resented that this was the plan.
