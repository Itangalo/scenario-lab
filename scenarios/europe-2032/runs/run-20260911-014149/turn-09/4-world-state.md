### Two cities become ten — on paper
The autumn bulletins finally gave Brussels something to point at. The two hospitals running European schedulers published six straight months of falling waits, and cameras that had once filmed crowded corridors now filmed orderly discharge lounges. Health ministers toured the wards; the replication pact promised the same in ten more cities, with connection fast-lanes and joint rostering money attached.

Elsewhere the promise thinned. Replication teams arrived to find pilot wards still on double systems, American licences renewed at higher prices for shorter terms, and funds arriving weeks late. Three councils kept grid-connection priority tied to hospital sites, but at two substation sites night blockades returned, and no new accelerator ground was broken — the two expansions stayed deferred.

### The jobs bridge holds, barely
The entry-level cover became the Union's visible priority. Employment offices issued vouchers for towns that kept junior posts open, employer bodies co-signed training slots, and levy disclosure was repackaged as funded places. A few hundred posts were held, mostly in mid-size IT and accounting firms.

It did not restart hiring. Graduate schemes stayed closed, internships stayed unpaid, and university-city queues lengthened through the autumn. Young workers got a helpline and a voucher; the hiring floor stayed gone. With money moving slowly and political room near zero, the priority push changed rhetoric more than timelines, and officials quietly noted that schedules had slipped while no one could act on them.
