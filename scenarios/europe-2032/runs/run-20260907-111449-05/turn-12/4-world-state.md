### Sovereignty Undermined

The EU completes the Open Science Resilience Initiative, delivering auditable models for biosecurity and climate. But the moment is eclipsed. The new measure, *Secure the Compute Stack*, launches with urgency — a large category 4 effort to bind all high-end compute under EU oversight. Legal teams invoke Article 173 TFEU, aiming to classify advanced training runs as strategic infrastructure.

Yet fragmentation intensifies. Germany and the Netherlands reject the centralization of compute control, calling it overreach. The three Central European states that withdrew from joint procurement formalize a parallel framework with a U.S. hyperscaler, citing reliability and cost. This *member_state_defection* event severs cohesion and directly undermines EU access to frontier capacity.

The EU-AAC begins planning, but no staff are hired. Legal challenges stall deployment. With political capital at 3.0, the portfolio charge of −7 (two large measures, one priority) drains it to 1.0. A new measure proposed under crisis conditions earns no bonus — the situation was too recent, the response too incremental.

Sovereignty falls to 18.0: ai_capability rose by 2.0 (−1), and the defection event costs −2. No category 4 completion offsets it.

Openweight_capability rises only slightly to 63.0, reflecting limited, incremental progress from the EU’s auditable biosecurity models and small-scale replication efforts. The peer-to-peer model release at capability level 68 does not contribute to this metric — it is uncontrolled, unrecallable, and operates outside any governance framework, making it a proliferation risk rather than an advance in sovereign-relevant open capability. The event underscores the growing gap between open distribution and open governance, challenging the assumption that openness inherently supports accountability.
