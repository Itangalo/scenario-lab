## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from proving auditable AI works to ensuring it is the only kind that can exist at scale. The Open Science Resilience Initiative delivers its final outputs — protein folding and climate models that run with full transparency — but we now know that technical success means nothing if the next breakthrough leaks through encrypted shards on a distributed network. The European Auditable AI Corps begins its staffing process under Article 347 TFEU, but we face deep resistance from Germany and the Netherlands, who see centralized technical authority as a threat to national autonomy. We are offering them co-governance: rotational leadership, full access to source weights, and a veto on any deployment touching national infrastructure — but not an opt-out. Fragmentation is the path to irrelevance.

Meanwhile, the new measure, Secure the Compute Stack, launches immediately. DG CONNECT, in coordination with the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking and national semiconductor agencies in France, Belgium, and Germany, will initiate a fast-track programme to map, license, and legally bind all compute clusters above 10^25 FLOP/s within EU jurisdiction. This includes private data centres, university clusters, and cloud instances. Legal basis will be a novel interpretation of Article 173 TFEU (industrial policy) combined with national security provisions in member state law. We will require that any training run above capability 60 must be pre-registered, its weights secured, and its distribution governed by EU-controlled signing keys. Export of such models — or the compute to run them — will be treated as strategic goods, like dual-use technology. The goal is not to stop open models, but to stop *unaccountable* models — to make "open" mean "governable", not "untraceable". This is the only way forward when the frontier is no longer in a lab, but in a thousand hidden nodes.
