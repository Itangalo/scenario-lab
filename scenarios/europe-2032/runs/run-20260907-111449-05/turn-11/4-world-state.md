### The Weight of Crisis

AI capability advances by 2.0 this turn, reaching 94.0 — a level where interpretability degrades and system behaviour becomes increasingly opaque. No audit can keep pace. A major cyber incident unfolds: model-generated ransomware paralyzes regional hospitals across three member states, exploiting zero-day vulnerabilities at machine speed. Initial defences fail; attribution will take months.

The EU responds with urgency. The Open Science Resilience Initiative completes its final integration, delivering auditable protein folding and climate models to national agencies. But as a finishing measure, it yields no political capital gain — the moment has passed. The Union proposes the European Auditable AI Corps (EU-AAC), a large category 5 effort to institutionalize sovereign technical capacity. Planning begins under Article 347 TFEU, with initial staffing discussions underway in Helsinki, Toulouse, and Pisa. However, no recruitment has started, and legal review is ongoing.

Yet resistance hardens. Germany and the Netherlands formally object to the centralization of AI operations, calling the legal basis “questionable” and the governance opaque. A coordinated withdrawal by three Central European states from joint procurement — now formalized — fractures the bloc’s bargaining power. The Crisis Coordination Protocol holds, but barely.

Public sentiment drops to 22.0 amid job losses in law, accountancy, and software, where entry-level roles vanish overnight. The hospital attacks compound anxiety. A decentralized peer-to-peer model release, capable and untraceable, spreads rapidly — proof that open does not mean safe.

Resilience ticks up slightly (+1) due to improved forensic coordination after the cyberattack. But ai_safety remains at 0.0: no safeguard scales to the threat. With ai_capability rising and no offsetting build, eu_ai_sovereignty falls to 20.0. Political capital stays frozen at 3.0 — too low to absorb new costs, yet the portfolio charge persists.

Openweight capability rises only modestly to 62.0, reflecting incremental improvements in model efficiency and limited spillover from auditable bioscience deployments. No major open model breakthroughs or compute expansions occur this turn.

### A Sovereignty Drain

The denial of access last turn still echoes. No new compute or control has been secured. Dependence deepens even as the EU tries to escape it.
