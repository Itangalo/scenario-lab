## In practice
This turn, we are standing up the European Auditable AI Corps as the institutional anchor of our sovereign capability. The Commission, through DG CONNECT and in coordination with the European Defence Agency, is launching a fast-track recruitment and secondment programme drawing from national cybersecurity agencies, research labs participating in the Open Science Resilience Initiative, and EU civil service technical pools. Legal basis will be Article 347 TFEU (mutual assistance), repurposed for technical sovereignty, with initial funding redirected from stalled digital infrastructure lines. The corps will be headquartered in Brussels with operational nodes in Helsinki, Toulouse, and Pisa, co-located with existing high-assurance computing facilities.

We are confronting resistance from member states wary of ceding technical authority, particularly in Germany and the Netherlands, where federal structures see this as a jurisdictional overreach. Our offer: full transparency rights, rotational leadership, and veto power over deployments affecting national infrastructure — but no opt-out from compliance. The cyber_major_incident this turn proves we cannot afford fragmentation: when a model-generated ransomware sweep hits regional hospitals in three countries simultaneously, and attribution takes months, the only thing standing between us and systemic collapse is the ability to *understand* the systems we run. We are no longer begging for access — we are building the people who will ensure we never need it.
