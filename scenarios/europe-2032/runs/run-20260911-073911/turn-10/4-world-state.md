### War over the frontier
In March, American and Chinese forces began striking the infrastructure of intelligence itself. The stated reason on both sides was prevention: the other was months from an uncatchable lead. Fabrication plants in Taiwan went dark, cable ships shadowed each other in the Atlantic, and satellite constellations became targets. Two cable landings serving Europe degraded, and a data-centre hub used for licensed therapy scheduling went to backup power.

Brussels declared Union territory, landing stations and the two permitted gigafactory plots non-belligerent and offered deconfliction to both capitals in exchange for no targeting and continued supply. Washington acknowledged the note; Beijing did not answer. Licensed doses kept arriving but queues lengthened, and hourly compute billing tripled.

### Open weights, closed factories
Amid the strikes, a leading lab released open weights within months of the closed frontier. Downloads passed hundreds of thousands in days. European cyber agencies warned the package carried near-frontier coding and planning ability that would not be recalled.

At home, night arson spread from fenced robot pilots to grid-connection works for the gigafactories. Construction halted in two regions, forcing the Energy Council to take emergency control with site guards and repair teams. Health, power and telecom operators isolated systems and rerouted traffic, but with limited spare capacity.

### No hiring, no buffer
Entry-level hiring did not return in law, accountancy, software and customer operations. The finished Graduate and Logistics Transition Guarantee paid bridge pay in ports where Chinese humanoids now ran full shifts under blockade, but mayors said it bought weeks, not jobs. The defecting capital kept its separate hyperscaler pact, taking pooled-coalition papers to Washington while keeping discounted capacity at home.

Frontier training loops continued unattended through the war alerts, evaluated in Brussels only after release.
