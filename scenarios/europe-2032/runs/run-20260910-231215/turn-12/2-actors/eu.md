## New measure
**EU Chip Continuity and Upstream Leverage Regime**
Uses export-licensing, stockpile release and middle-power fab contracts to keep essential-services compute running through the Taiwan blockade while coordinating upstream leverage. Why this and why now: the blockade turns every AI question into a supply question overnight, and the Union's upstream position is suddenly its only hard leverage and its greatest exposure.

## Priority
EU Chip Continuity and Upstream Leverage Regime, because the two-year commitment to keep essential services on European-controlled AI cannot survive a multi-year compute cutoff without rationed continuity and a coordinated upstream position — not the siting pact alone.

## In practice
We run this through DG TRADE and DG GROW with the Anti-Coercion Instrument and the Chips Act crisis toolbox, in Competitiveness and Foreign Affairs Councils: Chips Joint Undertaking releases mature-chip stocks to the four domestic sites and hospital/exporter hubs, HERA-style joint procurement signs overflow fab capacity with middle-power partners, and temporary export-authorisation for lithography spares and servicing is pooled at Union level so no member state is picked off bilaterally.

We hold the siting pact alongside it: DG ENER/CNECT completes the 90-day review with published safety audits, pays grid-fee rebates and apprenticeship quotas to host towns, and keeps temporary protected-connection status with JHA patrols so reserved peak-overflow can actually land. On the automated-decision scandal we do not open a new file this turn — we concede the Act failed as drawn, refer the system to the Fundamental Rights Agency and EDPS for redress, and fold the lesson into enforcement — because chips in warehouses still do not run wards if fabs stop.
