## New measure
**EU AI Incident Reporting Mandate**
Requires operators, vendors and deployers to report serious AI-enabled incidents and near-misses to a common ENISA-AI Office hub within 72 hours with shared technical traces. Why this and why now: the automated attack and leaked eval anomaly prove defenders learn from minutes while attackers learn from data, and without mandatory shared reporting the Shield cannot detect in 24 hours nor evaluate frontier risk.

## Priority
EU Critical Systems Shield, because the large automated attack now hitting public services will cascade before any new reporting line is law, and containment must outrank institution-building this turn.

## In practice
We push the Shield into emergency mode via the Energy and JHA Councils on Article 114/194 bases: ENISA gets delegated authority to impose interim segmentation orders on the hit operators, Frontex-style mutual-aid teams from less-affected TSOs are seconded, and the repurposed CEF-Digital envelope is front-loaded to fund 24h SOC staffing despite procurement waivers, over finance ministers' objections.

In parallel we table the Incident Reporting Mandate through DG CNECT with AI Act Article 73 implementing act, obliging critical entities and GPAI providers to file machine-readable incident traces to ENISA, with whistleblower protection via the existing directive. We keep Gigafactories site selection alive only by ring-fencing Shield-hardened substations in Madrid and Warsaw for dual use, and task the Evaluation Institute's seconded red-team to triage the leaked eval anomaly jointly with ENISA forensics, without waiting for its full mandate.
