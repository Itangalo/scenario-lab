### A Fragile Foundation

The EU completes the final phase of its foundational infrastructure buildout, with the InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, and European AI Talent Shield now fully operational. This delivers a long-delayed +5 boost to eu_ai_sovereignty, offsetting earlier decay. However, with no new capability growth globally, ai_capability and openweight_capability remain stagnant at 60.5 and 51.0, respectively.

The Commission launches the Public Accountability Interface for Evaluated AI Systems, a small category 2 measure aimed at improving transparency. Framed as a corrective to broken promises, it mandates plain-language summaries of AI evaluations and public dashboards. While symbolically significant, its immediate impact is limited, and no sentiment shift occurs this turn.

The Sovereign Access Framework for Public AI Evaluation, now the named priority, advances through procurement leverage. Negotiations with major developers are tense; one US firm signals willingness, another resists, citing IP risks. The European Digital Infrastructure Agency begins staffing its Seville unit, but pilot audits are delayed by legal uncertainties. Enforcement under the Digital Markets Act remains a credible threat, but untested.

The Joint Resilience Board enforces its mandate, cutting funding to energy operators in Finland and Slovakia after red-team exercises expose critical grid vulnerabilities. While technically justified, the move sparks backlash, with Helsinki accusing Brussels of overreach. Resilience holds steady at 56.0, supported by binding audits.

Public sentiment remains flat at 24.0, weighed down by automation layoffs and ongoing protests. The moratorium on open-weight models faces mounting pressure, with Ireland and Estonia demanding concessions for research use.

Political capital erodes. Despite completing three major initiatives, the portfolio charge is heavy. The new transparency measure fails to offset growing costs and internal friction.

### Cracks in Cohesion

A wave of sabotage and direct action targets data centres in Ireland and Finland, linked to automation grievances and transparency failures. Member-state responses diverge, exposing coordination gaps. Meanwhile, a quiet coalition of mid-sized states explores a joint evaluation framework, hinting at alternative paths through fragmentation.
