## New measure  
**Public Accountability Interface for Evaluated AI Systems**  
Launch a mandatory plain-language summary and public dashboard for all AI systems evaluated under the Sovereign Access Framework, making verification outcomes accessible and contestable.

`Category: 2 (Transparency and reporting)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` public_sentiment +4 (by reducing opacity and restoring trust in oversight), ai_safety +2 (by increasing external scrutiny and feedback loops), eu_political_capital +1 (if perceived as responsive)

## Priority  
**Sovereign Access Framework for Public AI Evaluation** — this remains our pivotal move because without sovereign access to the reasoning and behaviour of frontier systems used in public life, no amount of infrastructure or restriction will give us control; we are not building our own model—we are ensuring that the ones we depend on can be examined, challenged, and held accountable under EU law.

## In practice  
This turn, I am grounding the Sovereign Access Framework in operational reality. The legal basis is Article 16 of the AI Act and reinforced by Article 67 TFEU on internal security, allowing the Commission to mandate secure API interfaces for EU-appointed evaluators within any AI system deployed in critical infrastructure or public administration. We are negotiating directly with three major developers—two US-based, one Singaporean—under the condition that access is required for eligibility in EU public procurement above €50 million. The European Digital Infrastructure Agency is staffing up its evaluation unit in Seville, recruiting from JRC and ENISA, and will begin pilot audits on two language-model-as-agent systems used in border management and grid forecasting by Q3. Legal Service is preparing fallback mechanisms in case of non-compliance, including suspension of market access under the Digital Markets Act—a nuclear option, but one we must signal credibly.

Simultaneously, the Joint Resilience Board is acting on its new authority: red-team findings from Finland’s transmission operator revealed that a coordinated AI-driven cyber intrusion could disable load-balancing systems during peak winter demand, risking cascading blackouts. Binding audit decisions have cut Digital Europe Programme funds to both the Finnish and Slovak operators until remediation plans are submitted and verified. This enforcement is politically costly—Helsinki protests the move as overreach—but it establishes that resilience is not advisory. COREPER is reviewing the openweight moratorium this month, and I have instructed the Secretariat-General to propose a narrow carve-out for research use at ELLIS institutes, conditioned on air-gapped hardware and audit logs, to ease pressure from Ireland and Estonia without compromising the threshold.

The new transparency measure—the Public Accountability Interface—is designed to close the legitimacy gap. Plain-language summaries of evaluation results, including known failure modes and monitoring provisions, will be published quarterly by the EDIA and linked to national portals. This is not merely PR: it creates a feedback channel for civil society and technical watchdogs to challenge assumptions in the evaluations. If we do not make these systems legible to the people they affect, then even perfect safety engineering will fail politically. And in the shadow of rising automation layoffs and stalled Gigafactories, political failure is strategic failure.
