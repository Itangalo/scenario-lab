## New measure
**Cyber Resilience Directive for Critical Operators (CRD-CO)**  
A binding directive requiring continuous intrusion detection, incident reporting, and tested contingency plans for all operators of essential services in energy, water, ports, and digital infrastructure.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 3`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +15, ai_safety +5 (indirectly via faster detection and shared learning)  
`Applies to:` EU member states (operators of essential services)

## Priority
Cyber Resilience Directive for Critical Operators — this measure responds directly to the cyber_test_shot event, establishes a baseline of operational resilience that cannot wait, and aligns with the two-year commitment by ensuring the EU can withstand external shocks even as it builds sovereign capacity.

## In practice  
The cyber_test_shot event has exposed a dangerous gap: our critical infrastructure is not hardened against AI-enabled intrusions that evade traditional detection. The attackers were not targeting any one state, suggesting this was a test shot — and we were lucky it was detected at all. I am convening the COREPER committee this month to fast-track a new Cyber Resilience Directive for Critical Operators (CRD-CO), building on Article 21 of the NIS2 Directive but going further. This is not about reporting after the fact; it is about mandating continuous monitoring, red-teaming, and cross-border audit rights for a defined class of operators — electricity transmission, major ports, water utilities, and key digital backbone providers.

I am using the legal base of Article 114 TFEU (internal market) to ensure uniform standards, and I will tie access to EU infrastructure funds to compliance. The resistance will come from national regulators and some member states reluctant to cede oversight, particularly in energy. To overcome this, I am offering co-design of the technical standards through ENISA and a fast-tracked funding window under the Digital Europe Programme for detection upgrades. The Commission’s Joint Research Centre will lead the first round of penetration testing by turn 3. This is not optional: if we cannot defend what we have, no amount of sovereign compute will keep us independent. The directive will be the first piece of EU-wide enforcement that treats AI-enabled threats as systemic, not just technical.
