### The Resilience Gambit

The EU launches the Cyber Resilience Directive for Critical Operators (CRD-CO), a large category 6 measure aimed at hardening energy, water, port, and digital infrastructure against AI-powered intrusions exposed by a recent global cyber test shot. Invoking Article 114 TFEU, the Commission pushes for binding detection, reporting, and contingency requirements, backed by conditional access to EU funds. Despite fast-tracking, the measure faces resistance from member states guarding energy sovereignty, and full implementation is delayed to turn 3. Early momentum earns partial credit, but no immediate resilience boost lands.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

Global AI capability advances to 53.5, reflecting continued progress below the 60 threshold. However, safety declines to 32.0 as no safeguards have yet landed on frontier systems, and the gap between capability and control widens. The recent cyber intrusion — though detected by accident — underscores systemic fragility. No safety breakthrough or regulatory enforcement closes the gap this turn.

### Sovereignty Erodes

EU AI sovereignty slips to 20.0. The rise in global capability by more than 2 points triggers a −1 decay, and no category 4 measure finishes this turn. The ongoing gigafactories and tech sovereignty package remain in flight, yielding no immediate sovereignty gain. With no secured access event, the Union’s dependence deepens amid rising strategic pressure.

Political capital falls to 42.0. The portfolio now includes two large category 4 measures (−3 each) and one large category 6 measure (−3), with CRD-CO as priority (−1), totaling −10.
