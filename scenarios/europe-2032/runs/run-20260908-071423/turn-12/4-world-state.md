The scheduled completion of *Enforce Sovereign Evaluation Rights* arrives with silence, not triumph. No binding mandate is in force. COREPER II remains deadlocked; France and Germany still refuse to cede control over research infrastructure, and the ELLIS carve-out lies in ruins. The EDIA’s notice to the US developer expired without response—Washington cited alliance commitments but offered no enforcement. Emergency override powers were not triggered. The flagship measure ends as it lived: legally contested and politically fractured.

Yet fragments endure. This opt-in system offers mutual recognition of audits and shared incident protocols—modest but real progress. Meanwhile, legal teams assess whether Article 4 of the Internal Security Strategy allows unilateral emergency activation, though such a move risks constitutional crisis.
