### A Pivot in the Fog

The EU formally completes the InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, and European AI Talent Shield—delivering long-anticipated infrastructure that finally begins to scale. After years of delays, compute deployment clears national grid and permitting hurdles in key member states, unlocking a delayed but tangible uplift in sovereign capacity. The cumulative effect of these completed initiatives is reflected in a +5 boost to eu_ai_sovereignty, as recorded in the notepad, bringing it to 19.0. This gain is partially offset by the ongoing decay and resistance costs, consistent with the model’s dynamics.

However, the broader capability landscape remains stagnant. No new compute deployments or R&D breakthroughs occur this turn that would drive growth in `ai_capability` or `openweight_capability`. The earlier investments have now matured into operational systems, but their contribution to global capability metrics has already been accounted for in prior turns. Without new innovation cycles or training runs, both metrics hold flat: `ai_capability` remains at 60.5 and `openweight_capability` at 51.0.

In response to persistent bottlenecks, the Commission pivots from infrastructure buildout to control, proposing the **Sovereign Access Framework for Public AI Evaluation**, a large category 5 measure aimed at asserting jurisdictional authority over frontier models in public-sector use. Grounded in the AI Act and tied to procurement leverage, it represents a strategic shift toward governance-based sovereignty. However, as a newly proposed initiative, its effects are not yet realised. Implementation will require multi-year enforcement capacity, and developer compliance remains uncertain. No metric gains are attributed to its launch.

Enforcement gains ground: the Joint Resilience Board issues binding audit decisions under DORA, cutting funding to non-compliant energy operators in Finland and Slovakia. Red-team exercises reveal alarming cascading risks in grid systems, validating the Board’s operational mandate. With coordinated actions now institutionalising, resilience climbs to 56.0.

Political headwinds intensify. The moratorium on high-capability open-weight models faces growing resistance from Ireland, Estonia, and civil society, framed as innovation-stifling amid rising automation layoffs. Protests spread around data centres and public-sector AI deployments, feeding a sense of exclusion. Public sentiment holds flat—the plain-language summaries remain promised, not delivered.

The completion of three major initiatives—InvestAI, Tech Sovereignty Package, and Talent Shield—provides a modest boost to political capital, reflecting procedural legitimacy and delivery credibility. However, the launch of the new Sovereign Access Framework does not contribute to this gain, as its success is unproven. The +1.0 increase in `eu_political_capital` (from 20.0 to 21.0) reflects only the delayed payoff of past efforts, consistent with the constitutional constraint that sovereignty’s contribution to political capital is capped and cannot dominate.

Horizon Europe is repurposed to fund emergency compute nodes, but only at the margins. No major new funding is identified, and the initiative remains a stopgap pending a broader fiscal decision. The move signals intent but lacks the scale or financing to meaningfully shift the capability trajectory this turn.

Internally, the shift to sovereign control acknowledges reality—sovereignty lies not in chips, but in access. The new framework is ambitious, but its success hinges on developer compliance and member-state unity, both uncertain. For now, the EU consolidates gains from past investments, while the next phase remains in formation.
