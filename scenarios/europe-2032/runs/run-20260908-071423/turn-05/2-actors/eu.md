## In practice
This turn, I am operationalising the Joint Resilience Board under Article 12 of the Digital Operational Resilience Act, granting it binding audit authority over national regulators and direct access to JRC red teams for live penetration testing. DG CONNECT and ENISA are co-leading the rollout, with audit schedules to be issued by September and compliance tied to disbursement of Digital Europe Programme funds—no exceptions, not even for Berlin or Stockholm. The cyberattacks in Belgium, Finland, and Slovakia have shifted the balance: local leaders are now demanding enforcement, not consultation. We are no longer building frameworks—we are running them.

Simultaneously, I am launching the first call for the European AI Talent Shield, releasing €1.2bn across ELLIS nodes in Paris, Munich, and Amsterdam. The fellowship offers €150,000, Tier-4 sovereign compute, and fast-track Schengen visas with family inclusion, targeting 400 frontier researchers in year one. To break the deadlock over compute sovereignty, I am anchoring the rotational access model to the upcoming Chips Act revision: each node hosts one-third of the pool in rotation, with oversight by a Scientific Steering Committee including national labs. This is not a compromise with fragmentation—it is a scaffold for unity. The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track, but I will escalate at the October Competitiveness Council: without urgent grid upgrades and permitting reform, we are building compute on sand.

Now, with the openweight frontier surging to within 10 points of the closed one, and hundreds of thousands of downloads already in motion, I am proposing a new measure: a moratorium on open-weight releases above a defined capability threshold. This will be enforced through licensing of large-scale compute and platform cooperation, with legal basis drawn from the AI Act’s high-risk provisions and the Digital Services Act. The threshold will be dynamically set at 10 points below the closed frontier, reviewed quarterly. The goal is not to stop open innovation, but to prevent unrecallable systems of strategic danger from becoming freely available overnight. We act now because the safety breakthrough gives us a narrow window to strengthen control without appearing reactionary—and because the member state defection shows we cannot rely on others to hold the line. This is sovereignty through restraint, not retreat.
