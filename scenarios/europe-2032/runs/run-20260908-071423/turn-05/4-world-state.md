### The Alliance and the Abyss

The United States elects a new administration committed to technological alliance. The result is clear: structured access to frontier AI for vetted partners, joint safety frameworks, and shared incident reporting. In Brussels, relief mingles with unease — the immediate pressure eases, but the path to true autonomy grows steeper. Dependence, now formalised and friendly, becomes harder to justify escaping.

### Sovereignty Under Strain

Despite the external boost, EU sovereignty erodes. The Joint Resilience Board officially activates, its audit powers now binding. Early assessments reveal alarming gaps in energy and transport sectors, but enforcement begins in earnest, lifting resilience. However, a second member state negotiates a bilateral AI access deal with a U.S. cloud provider, citing national emergency preparedness. The move fractures cohesion, reinforcing a pattern of backdoor dependency.

The European AI Talent Shield launches with fanfare, but Germany blocks compute access for fellows pending national security review, and Sweden’s visa backlog delays arrivals. Recruitment stalls at 52. The InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package advance slowly, held back by grid constraints and permitting delays.

### A Moratorium in Doubt

The proposed moratorium on high-capability open-weight models sparks immediate backlash. Legal scholars question the EU’s authority under the AI Act, while startups warn of innovation flight. France and Poland support the ban; Ireland and Estonia oppose it as overreach. The Council deadlocks. Meanwhile, open-weight models continue to diffuse, with incremental improvements and wider deployment pushing `openweight_capability` to 49.0. While performance grows, no single release or leak brings these models near frontier capability; the gap with `ai_capability` remains significant, preserving the expected diffusion lag.

### Capital Erodes, Sentiment Falls

Political capital declines under agenda overload. The Joint Resilience Board finishes, granting a modest boost, but the contested moratorium, labour unrest, and internal fragmentation exact a toll. Public sentiment drops sharply as automation displaces thousands in local administrations. The narrative shifts: AI is no longer just a tool, but a force eroding livelihoods. The idea of a Sovereign Compute Access Framework is discussed in internal working groups, but no formal proposal is advanced this turn, avoiding duplication and respecting implementation limits. The EU focuses on consolidating existing initiatives rather than launching new ones.
