### The Directive Lands, Just in Time

The Cyber Resilience Directive for Critical Operators officially enters force, marking the first binding EU-wide enforcement of AI-era cyber standards. The Joint Resilience Board, activated under emergency provisions, begins issuing audit schedules to national regulators by September. Early resistance from Berlin and Stockholm softens as municipal ransomware attacks — generated by AI tooling and disrupting public services in Belgium, Finland, and Slovakia — dominate headlines. The Board’s new authority, backed by conditional access to Digital Europe funds, forces compliance, though implementation remains patchy in mid-tier utilities.

### Offensive AI, Defensive Gaps

The cyber incident reveals that attackers used AI to rapidly customise ransomware payloads and evade signature-based detection. While the directive’s frameworks help contain the damage, response is delayed in jurisdictions that had not pre-positioned monitoring tools. Resilience improves as coordination begins to work, but the gap between offensive capability and defensive readiness remains stark.

### Talent and Trust, Tenuously Linked

The European AI Talent Shield launches with €1.2bn in initial funding. However, the rotational compute model fails to fully assuage national concerns. Germany insists on veto power over foreign access to its node, while Sweden delays visa processing. Recruitment starts slowly, with only 47 fellows committed by December. The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package show no new momentum, hampered by grid bottlenecks and permitting delays.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Stretches Thin

Despite the directive’s completion, political capital dips. The portfolio remains overloaded, and no significant gains offset the costs. Public sentiment falls as job disruptions from AI automation in local government services are tied to the broader rollout. The perception of a top-down, crisis-driven agenda grows.
