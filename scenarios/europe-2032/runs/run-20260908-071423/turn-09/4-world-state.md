### Sovereignty Under Siege

The EU completes the Sovereign Access Framework, securing pilot API access with two foreign developers and uncovering a critical flaw in a grid forecasting agent. The finding validates the framework’s purpose, and the Joint Resilience Board acts swiftly to mitigate the risk. However, broader sovereignty erodes: the Taiwan blockade and U.S.-driven supply chain coercion strip the Union of control over its own compute pipeline, while a member state bypasses EU policy to secure bilateral access to a hyperscaler’s frontier models, fracturing the common line.

### Critical Evaluation, Fragile Authority

The Commission launches “Enforce Sovereign Evaluation Rights in Critical Infrastructure,” a large Category 1 measure mandating real-time, hardware-level access to AI systems in energy, transport, health, and border management. Backed by emergency powers and co-led by the Joint Resilience Board, it aims to transform oversight into operational control. France and Germany tentatively support fast-tracking, conditioned on research carve-outs. Yet legal resistance mounts, and public trust remains low—civil society decries overreach, while sabotage at data centres in Ireland and Finland underscores the volatility of the moment.

### Political Capital Crumbles

Despite completing two measures, political capital plummets. The portfolio remains heavy, and the new large measure intensifies strain. The US posture of ALLIANCE offers no shield against external coercion, and internal cohesion frays. The emergent sovereign AI evaluation network among mid-sized states gains quiet traction, reflecting growing impatience with central delays.
