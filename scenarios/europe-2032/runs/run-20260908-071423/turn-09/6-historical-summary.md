The EU completes the Sovereign Access Framework, achieving pilot API access with two foreign developers and identifying a critical flaw in a grid forecasting AI, which is swiftly mitigated by the Joint Resilience Board. Despite this success, AI sovereignty declines due to external pressures: a Taiwan blockade and U.S.-driven supply chain coercion disrupt the EU’s compute pipeline, while a member state bypasses bloc-wide policy to secure bilateral access to frontier models, undermining unity. The Commission launches "Enforce Sovereign Evaluation Rights in Critical Infrastructure," a major Category 1 initiative granting real-time, hardware-level oversight of AI systems in key sectors, backed by emergency powers and supported tentatively by France and Germany with research exemptions. Legal and civil opposition grows, citing overreach, amid ongoing data centre sabotage in Ireland and Finland that highlights societal unrest. Political capital erodes further as governance efforts strain institutional capacity, external leverage weakens, and a coalition of mid-sized member states advances an independent evaluation network, signaling deepening fragmentation within the Union.
