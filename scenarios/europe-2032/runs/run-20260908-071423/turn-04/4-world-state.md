### The Open Frontier Widens

An open-weight model release, closely trailing the closed frontier, floods private systems with unprecedented capability. Within days, it is replicated across tens of thousands of machines, rendering containment difficult. The EU proposes a moratorium on high-capability open releases, triggering immediate legal and political resistance. Member states question the legality of retroactive licensing, and tech firms warn of a brain drain. The measure enters debate in the Council, where a coalition cites innovation risks and constitutional overreach—its fate uncertain as negotiations begin. The surge in open deployment lifts `openweight_capability`, but not in excess of frontier progress; the gap narrows moderately, reflecting rapid diffusion rather than leapfrogging.

### Safety Gains, But Trust Erodes

A major interpretability breakthrough improves control over deployed models, allowing developers to predict emergent behaviours before deployment. This lifts global ai_safety, even as open distribution accelerates. Yet in the EU, public sentiment falls. A wave of AI-driven layoffs in local government—clerical roles automated across France, Spain, and Poland—fuels backlash. Protests grow, and political momentum shifts toward restriction. The European AI Talent Shield continues its rollout, but recruitment remains slow due to Germany’s ongoing reservations and Sweden’s visa processing delays.

### Resilience Takes Hold, Fragmentation Persists

The Cyber Resilience Directive officially lands, and the Joint Resilience Board begins audits. Its authority, tied to funding access, forces compliance even in resistant capitals. Early red-team exercises uncover critical gaps, but also demonstrate improved coordination. Resilience rises as systems adapt. However, a member state bypasses EU coordination, striking a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler for priority AI access—framed domestically as pragmatism, but seen elsewhere as defection. This undermines cohesion and accelerates sovereignty decay.

### Capital Under Pressure

Political capital erodes under the weight of an overloaded agenda. The priority on the Joint Resilience Board yields progress, but the contested moratorium and labour unrest exact a toll. No offsetting gains emerge, and the portfolio’s cost deepens the deficit.
