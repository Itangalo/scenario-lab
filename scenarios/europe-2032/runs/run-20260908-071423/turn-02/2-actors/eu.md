## In practice  
We are in the second turn of a six-month rhythm that will define whether the Union remains a decision-maker or becomes a reactor. The cyber intrusion detected last quarter was not an attack — it was a probe, a signal that our critical systems are visible and vulnerable to actors wielding AI tools beyond our own grasp. I am not waiting for another warning. This month, I have instructed DG CONNECT to finalise the implementing acts for the Cyber Resilience Directive for Critical Operators (CRD-CO), locking in minimum detection standards based on ENISA’s latest red-teaming protocols. To break the logjam in COREPER, I am offering member states co-governance of the audit framework through a new Joint Resilience Board, with rotating presidency and direct access to JRC penetration testing results. Conditional access to the Digital Europe Programme’s €12bn infrastructure window will now require compliance by turn 4 — a hard lever, but one that respects national sovereignty while enforcing collective security.

At the same time, we cannot harden what we do not control. The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track, but their pace is not matching the decay in eu_ai_sovereignty. That is why I am launching the European AI Talent Shield (EATS): a €4.2bn initiative under Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme to create 1,500 long-term research fellowships, paired with sovereign compute allocations and fast-track Schengen visas for frontier talent. The first call will be issued through the European Laboratory for Learning and Intelligent Systems (ELLIS), with nodes in Paris, Munich, and Amsterdam leading recruitment. This is not brain gain — it is brain retention with teeth. We are losing too many of our best to labs outside the Union, not because of money alone, but because they lack the infrastructure to compete. EATS closes that gap. I will present it to the Competitiveness Council in June, tied to a revision of the Chips Act implementation framework, so that talent and hardware grow together. The cost is high, but the alternative — a Europe that can regulate AI but not run it — is fatal to both our mandates.
