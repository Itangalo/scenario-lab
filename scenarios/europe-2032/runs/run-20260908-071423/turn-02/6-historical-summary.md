The EU launched the European AI Talent Shield (EATS), a €4.2bn initiative to retain frontier AI talent through fellowships, sovereign compute access, and fast-track visas, linked to a revised Chips Act. However, resistance from Berlin and Stockholm over compute allocation and delayed recruitment undermined early momentum. Global AI capability rose to 55.0, with frontier systems demonstrating autonomous multi-agent coordination, while AI safety declined to 30.0 due to absent safeguards and no interpretability breakthroughs, widening the control gap. The Cyber Resilience Directive for Critical Operators remained delayed to turn 3, with member state opposition stalling the Joint Resilience Board and yielding no resilience gains. EU AI sovereignty fell to 19.0, driven by global capability growth triggering decay and no completed category 4 measures, as EATS provided no immediate benefit. Political capital dropped to 32.0 under the burden of three large ongoing initiatives and a named priority, with no completions or proposal bonuses to offset costs. Public sentiment remained flat at 42.0.
