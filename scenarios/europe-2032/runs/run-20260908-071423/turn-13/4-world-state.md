### A Framework in Motion

The European Union launches the **European Sovereign Monitoring Framework**, converting the technical remnants of its failed sovereignty push into a live, opt-in regime. Grounded in ratified hardware-rooted monitoring standards and early pilots with grid operators, the framework activates under cross-border critical infrastructure cooperation law, bypassing the need for Council unanimity. The Netherlands, Belgium, and Estonia formally join, establishing mutual recognition of audit logs and shared incident protocols. Legal teams invoke Article 4 of the Internal Security Strategy to frame the initiative as mutual assistance, not override—avoiding immediate constitutional challenge.

Global AI capability advances to 63.0, continuing its slow climb. ### Sentiment Shifts, Fragile Gains

Public sentiment inches upward as **adoption_success** becomes visible: AI-driven triage cuts hospital wait times in three member states, and automated case processing delivers faster rulings in overburdened courts. These are framed as European achievements, not imports. Meanwhile, **backlash_physical** escalates in France and Spain, where activists disrupt data centre operations, injuring a technician and cutting power to a regional node. The response strains police resources, underscoring societal fracture.
