## New measure
**EU Moratorium on High-Capability Open-Weight Models**  
Prohibit the public release of open-weight AI models exceeding a dynamically set capability threshold—initially 49.5, calibrated at 10 points below the closed frontier—with enforcement via licensing of large-scale training runs and platform cooperation.

## Priority
**EU Moratorium on High-Capability Open-Weight Models** — this measure must land now because the gap between open and closed AI has narrowed to just 10.5 points, and incremental diffusion is turning into systemic exposure: we no longer face a lagged echo of frontier risk, but a distributed, unrecallable approximation of it—and one that could enable autonomous cyber or bio misuse within reach of non-state actors before we have any defences ready.

## In practice
This turn, I am moving fast on three fronts. First, on resilience: with the Joint Resilience Board now fully operational, DG ENISA has issued the first mandatory audit schedule under Article 12 of DORA, targeting energy grid operators in Belgium, Finland, and Slovakia—those hit hardest by recent ransomware incidents. Compliance is tied directly to disbursement of Digital Europe Programme funds; non-compliant entities lose access immediately, no appeals. We are not waiting for consensus—we are enforcing the rules we already have, using red teams from the JRC to simulate attacks and verify preparedness. The credibility of our entire preparedness architecture hinges on this being real, not ritual.

Second, on talent and compute: despite the formal completion of EATS and the Tech Sovereignty Package, their effects remain fragile. I am convening a crisis meeting of the Competitiveness Council in June to break the permitting and grid logjam holding back InvestAI Gigafactories. The message is clear: if member states will not accelerate grid connections and zoning approvals by Q3, the Commission will invoke Article 122 TFEU to override national bottlenecks under "threat to economic stability." To salvage EATS, I am bypassing national blockers by launching a parallel hosting framework: sovereign compute nodes in Paris, Munich, and Amsterdam will rotate access under ELLIS oversight, funded directly from Horizon Europe’s emergency reserve. This is not ideal—but it keeps the programme alive while we fix the structure.

Third, on control: the moratorium proposal goes to COREPER next week. Legal services are aligning the instrument with Articles 6 and 7 of the AI Act (high-risk systems) and Section 4 of the DSA (very large online platforms), requiring cloud providers to verify model capability before compute allocation and platforms to block distribution of banned weights. We are offering startups a carve-out: sandbox access to high-capability models under strict monitoring, so innovation isn’t strangled. But the line is drawn: no more unrecallable superintelligence precursors loose in the wild. We act now not because we can stop all leaks, but because failing to try forfeits our claim to agency altogether.
