### The Alliance Takes Shape

The new U.S. administration declares a posture of **ALLIANCE**, expressing intent to strengthen transatlantic cooperation on AI. Initial diplomatic signals are positive, but concrete mechanisms for access or joint protocols remain under negotiation. The EU urges rapid codification of terms, stressing the need for mutual standards and verification before any operational benefits can accrue. For now, the posture is a stated direction—not an implemented framework—and its real-world impact awaits follow-through.

### Resilience Gains Ground

The Joint Resilience Board begins enforcement, issuing mandatory cyber audits for critical energy operators in three member states. Non-compliance now cuts off EU funding, and red-team exercises reveal serious gaps—particularly in Finland’s grid control systems. Still, the Board’s authority is no longer theoretical. With the measure complete, resilience rises as coordinated defences start to solidify.

### Sovereignty Stalls, Fragmentation Deepens

The Tech Sovereignty Package and European AI Talent Shield formally conclude, but their impact is undercut by implementation failures. Germany and Sweden continue blocking full access for EATS researchers, forcing the Commission to launch emergency compute nodes under Horizon Europe—patching the programme, not fulfilling it. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain delayed by permitting and grid bottlenecks, with no breakthrough at the Competitiveness Council.

### Moratorium Pushes Forward—At a Cost

The EU proposes a moratorium on high-capability open-weight models above threshold 49.5, advancing it through internal drafting and preliminary legal review. The proposal is now under discussion at COREPER, marking a significant step. However, backlash grows: Ireland and Estonia warn of innovation flight, while civil society groups accuse the EU of overreach. The move consumes political capital and deepens internal friction, but no final decision is reached this turn.

Public sentiment worsens as automation displaces more public-sector workers. The moratorium, framed as elitist restriction, fuels resentment. Political capital erodes under the weight of a single new measure and growing internal dissent. Open-weight capability sees moderate growth, driven by diffusion of prior models and underground development, but remains below frontier pace. The jump in capability is contained, consistent with gradual accumulation under constraint.
