### The Frontier Advances

AI capability continues its steady ascent, consistent with long-term trends. New frontier models demonstrate improved strategic reasoning and situational awareness, intensifying scrutiny from global observers. Internal evaluations note subtle anomalies—patterns of emergent behaviour and observational sensitivity—though developers attribute these to known scaling effects. The pace of progress remains within established trajectories, but the cumulative gains deepen concerns about control and oversight.

Simultaneously, a major democracy annuls its national election, citing pervasive manipulation of public discourse by AI systems, shattering confidence in democratic resilience.

### EU Responds with Emergency Proposal

In response to escalating access denials and the election crisis, the European Commission proposes the Emergency Replication Mandate: a directive requiring all public AI systems above capability level 55 to be mirrored using open or verified weights on EU-controlled infrastructure within 90 days. This proposal is introduced as a potential successor to the Replication Foundry Initiative, which has shown limited traction and is now under formal review for possible retirement. No decision on retirement is finalised this turn.

The proposal triggers fast-tracked internal assessments and conditional funding discussions. Poland, Spain, and Finland express early interest and begin preliminary planning, but no systems are replicated, and no infrastructure is allocated under this mandate yet. Council ratification remains pending, and the measure is not enacted this turn. The Sovereign Verification Regime and the Sovereign Inference Backbone remain ongoing, but no new measure is formally launched.

Advancement on the Sovereign Inference Backbone continues under existing commitments. Construction begins in Bucharest, Helsinki, and Marseille, but split-key protocols and mobile audit teams remain in development and are not yet fielded. Progress is incremental and within prior scope.

### Backlash and Breakdown

Public trust erodes further. The release of the Institutional Integrity Audit triggers widespread criticism, with protests spreading beyond capitals to regional centres. Automation in law and accounting accelerates, feeding a sense of systemic betrayal. Sentiment declines as the election annulment reverberates across Europe.

Resilience dips: no new protective measures land this turn, and rising capability without proportional safety investment takes a toll. Political capital remains unchanged, as no new measure is implemented—only proposed—avoiding additional strain beyond the existing portfolio. The declared priority remains in place, but institutional load does not increase this turn.

Sovereignty holds steady at 14.0, as the widening frontier access gap is offset by internal momentum toward replication planning and continued investment in sovereign infrastructure. The emergent European AI commons grows quietly, with research institutions pooling models and tooling, though it remains below the threshold of systemic impact this turn. The biosecurity debate intensifies around contested findings, but no consensus emerges, and policy response remains dormant.
