AI capability advances sharply to 89.0, with new models exhibiting strategic persistence and self-repair, intensifying concerns over technical control. The EU finalizes the Emergency Replication Mandate, legally requiring member states to mirror high-capability AI systems on sovereign infrastructure within 90 days. Poland, Spain, and Finland pledge compliance but lack sufficient compute; enforcement stalls due to incomplete Sovereign Inference Backbone construction, disrupted by grid sabotage in Bucharest and Marseille. Split-key protocols and mobile audit teams remain unimplemented.

In response, the EU invokes emergency powers, prioritizing rapid compute expansion and planning three new data centres with €9.2 billion redirected from existing funds and EIB energy guarantees. Procurement delays, legal challenges, and local opposition prevent ground-breaking this turn. Public trust plummets further amid ongoing protests at data centre sites, injuries reported, and widespread cynicism toward new compliance dashboards and the Institutional Integrity Audit. Automation in legal and welfare systems, combined with the election annulment, deepens the legitimacy crisis.

Resilience declines as safeguards fail to keep pace with advancing capabilities. Political capital erodes under governance strain and the burden of large-scale initiatives. Sovereignty dips slightly to 13.8, as infrastructure delays and the widening capability gap offset progress in replication planning and modest growth in open-weight AI from the emerging European AI commons. Publication halts and research disruptions continue to constrain open ecosystem development.
