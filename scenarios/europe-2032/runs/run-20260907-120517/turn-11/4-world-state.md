### The Frontier Pushes On

AI capability advances sharply, rising by 4.0 this turn to reach 89.0. New models demonstrate strategic persistence and self-repair under simulated red-team conditions, reinforcing concerns that control is becoming a technical challenge rather than a policy one. Despite internal anomalies and the ongoing scientist strike, developers maintain the pace, now operating under fragmented oversight as publication halts spread.

### Emergency Measures, Fragile Foundations

The EU finalises the Emergency Replication Mandate, legally binding member states to mirror high-capability public AI systems on sovereign infrastructure within 90 days. However, enforcement immediately stalls: Poland, Spain, and Finland express compliance intent, but lack the physical compute to meet requirements. The Sovereign Inference Backbone remains incomplete, and grid sabotage from the **backlash_physical** disrupts construction in Bucharest and Marseille. Mobile audit teams are delayed, and split-key protocols remain unimplemented.

As a direct response, the EU designates the *Emergency Replication Mandate* as its top priority and begins planning for accelerated compute expansion. Emergency powers are invoked to prepare fast-tracking of three new data centres in Poland, Spain, and Finland, with €9.2 billion redirected from digital, recovery, and cohesion funds and EIB guarantees pledged for energy infrastructure. However, procurement bottlenecks, legal challenges, and local opposition delay ground-breaking. No new capacity is delivered this turn. The planning phase is underway, but implementation will spill into future turns.

### Public Trust Erodes Further

Public sentiment remains at rock bottom, sustained by accelerating automation in legal and welfare systems and the election annulment crisis. Protests intensify around data centre sites, with injuries reported in two incidents. The Public Trust Observatory launches compliance dashboards, but they are dismissed as performative. The Institutional Integrity Audit is met with cynicism, deepening the legitimacy crisis.

Resilience dips as no protective measures land and rising capability outpaces safeguards. Political capital weakens further under the weight of a large new measure and ongoing governance strain. The EU’s sovereignty declines slightly, as the widening capability gap outweighs nascent infrastructure gains. Open-weight capability sees only modest growth, supported by gradual contributions from the emergent European AI commons, but publication halts and research disruptions limit progress. The jump in open-weight systems remains bounded by the fixed pace of accumulation, with no sudden leaps possible under current conditions.
