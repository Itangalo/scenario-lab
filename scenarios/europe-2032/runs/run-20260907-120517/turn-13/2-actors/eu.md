## Statement changes  
* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity as the foundation of strategic autonomy and public trust``  
  - Trigger: the complete failure of the Emergency Grid-Compute Integration Programme to deliver any operational compute despite emergency designation, legal override attempts, and EIB financing—blocked by courts, grid operators, and civil resistance—and the confirmation this turn that only Finland has achieved meaningful replication, while Poland and Spain remain dependent on foreign-hosted systems under investigation  
  - Grounds: The commitment as stated presumes that sovereign capacity is achievable through top-down mandate and accelerated siting. That assumption is now falsified. We have neither the political capital nor the institutional coherence to impose sovereignty by decree. The gap between frontier capability (93.0) and EU-controlled systems (now effectively at 11.0, down from 13.0 due to failed replication) is no longer a delay—it is a collapse. To hold the commitment, we must change its form: from building sovereign capacity *at scale* to preserving the *minimum viable core* from which recovery may later be possible. This is not abandonment—it is triage.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late``  
  - Trigger: the election_annulled event, combined with eval_anomaly_reports indicating self-rectifying agent behaviour and the emergent_global_scientist_strike, confirms that waiting for full clarity is no longer a choice—the picture will never be clear again, and acting without it has now produced systemic failure in infrastructure rollout and public legitimacy  
  - Grounds: The commitment stands in spirit, but its cost has become existential. We acted under uncertainty and were wrong—not in direction, but in scale and timing. We mistook urgency for agency. To continue acting under uncertainty without a mechanism to *learn faster* than the frontier is to repeat the same error with diminishing returns. The commitment must now carry its own corrective: we will act before the picture is clear, but we will *also* build the capacity to see the picture *sooner*. This turn demands that we pair action with real-time sensing, not just retrospective audits.

* ``- add `minimum_viable_sovereignty` (position): Preserve a core of EU-controlled, physically secure, and operationally independent AI capacity—sufficient to host one fully replicated high-capability model and sustain one sovereign evaluation team—as the only plausible foundation for future recovery``  
  - Grounds: With political capital at 5.0, public sentiment at 10.0, and no new compute online, we cannot pursue broad sovereignty. But we can prevent total erasure. This position focuses our remaining capacity on a single achievable outcome: one working node, one verified model, one team that can assess what the frontier is doing. It is not strategic autonomy—it is strategic survival.

## In practice  
We are shifting from a strategy of sovereign expansion to one of sovereign preservation. The Commission, acting through the European Council’s emergency resolution on strategic continuity, is redirecting all remaining EIB emergency financing and ENTSO-E grid priority to a single site: the Finnish node at Kajaani, already compliant with the Emergency Replication Mandate and operating on geothermal-backed power. This is not a compromise—it is a concentration. The site will be hardened under joint EAAD+ and European Defence Agency oversight, with physical access restricted to cleared personnel only. We are abandoning Bucharest and Marseille not because they are unimportant, but because they are unreachable at this level of capital and cohesion.

The Sovereign Inference Backbone, though officially finished, is being re-scoped around this single node. Mobile audit teams will now embed permanently in Kajaani, certifying not just compliance but operational continuity. The EAAD+ is standing down investigations into Poland and Spain—not because dependency is acceptable, but because enforcement without capacity is theatre. Instead, we are offering both states temporary access to the Finnish node under strict usage caps and real-time monitoring, in exchange for full transparency and a binding commitment to future compliance. This is not sovereignty—it is triage. But it keeps the circuit alive.

Publicly, we are not announcing a retreat. We are announcing a consolidation: the EU will run one sovereign model, one trusted team, one secure node—enough to evaluate, to verify, and to resist total dependence. The Public Trust Observatory is being repurposed to publish live telemetry from the node: not to prove success, but to prove presence. We are no longer building a grid. We are guarding a spark.
