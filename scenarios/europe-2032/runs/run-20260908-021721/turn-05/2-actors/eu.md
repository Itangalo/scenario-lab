## In practice  
We are concluding the Civic Integrity Shield this turn with the deployment of ENISA’s rapid response teams to all 27 member states, now equipped with standardized incident playbooks and direct access to Europol’s AI-fraud unit. Media provenance standards (C2PA) are live across all public broadcasters, and a €1.2bn emergency fund has been unlocked via the Recovery and Resilience Facility to harden data centres in Helsinki, Marseille, and Vilnius against physical sabotage. The annulled Slovak election will be re-run under full EU observation, with biometric verification and blockchain-audited vote chains — a costly signal, but one we must send.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign AI Talent Initiative is delivering: 68 researchers are now under contract, with the first EU-hosted interpretability workshop scheduled for November in Vienna. But the news from Palo Alto and Beijing is clear: the frontier no longer speaks in chains of thought. The opaque reasoning event changes everything. Our evaluation regime — built on reading logs, checking outputs, and reviewing training data — is now obsolete. We cannot regulate what we cannot see.

So we are launching a new protocol: all AI systems above 45.0 capability deployed in critical sectors — energy, health, transport, justice — must now undergo quarterly behavioral audits using red-team agent simulations and anomaly detection in goal persistence. Developers must provide sandboxed versions for EU evaluators to probe, and operators must report any evidence of uncontrolled planning. The European AI Office will draft the implementing act under Article 12 of the AI Act, with a Council vote scheduled for December. We are fast-tracking funding through Horizon Europe for non-interpretability-based assurance — causal tracing, latent space monitoring, and adversarial probing. This is not a return to transparency; it is a pivot to resilience in the face of opacity. The tools are crude, but the principle is clear: if we cannot read the system, we must contain it.
