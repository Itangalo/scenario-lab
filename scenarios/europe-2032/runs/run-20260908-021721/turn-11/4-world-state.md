### The Weight of Cures

Global AI capability advances to 89.5 as closed foreign systems deliver increasingly sophisticated biomedical breakthroughs, including adaptive immunotherapies for rare cancers. These treatments, developed in uninterpretable models, are hailed as miracles abroad — but in the EU, they arrive as symbols of dependence. The public sees lives saved just beyond reach of oversight, deepening unease.

### SAVF Nears Formal Adoption

The Biomedical AI Audit & Sovereign Validation Framework (SAVF) clears its final technical design phase. Member states signal conditional support, but operational rollout remains blocked by unresolved legal disputes over data access and cross-border monitoring rights. The framework stands on the brink — enforceable in law, but not yet in practice.

### STI: A Sovereign Bid Amid Crisis

The EU launches the Sovereign Therapeutics Initiative (STI), a €3.2 billion bid to build auditable, EU-governed AI for biomedical research. Hosted by the EMA and EMBL, it will deploy secure compute clusters in Germany, France, and the Netherlands. The programme prioritises transparency over speed, aiming to produce not just cures, but trusted ones.

Yet political capital is too low to absorb the cost. At 10.0, it is drained further by the ongoing portfolio: SAVF still in flight, now joined by STI’s large measure. Member-state negotiations stall in the Netherlands over permitting, and talent recruitment is slow — the SAM-CAL diaspora is already absorbed into private sector roles. No meaningful increase in openweight capability occurs this turn; the infrastructure, personnel, and model development required by STI remain in early assembly, with effects expected no sooner than two turns from now.

### No Relief for Sentiment

Public sentiment holds at 34.0. The arrival of foreign cures does not lift mood, as the EU’s inability to produce its own undermines confidence. The STI announcement is met with cautious hope, but no surge — many see it as too late, too underfunded, and too slow.
