## In practice
We are acting on the truth this turn has made undeniable: SAVF alone cannot close the legitimacy gap. It is right that we demand transparency — but when the cures that save lives are born in black boxes abroad, and we have nothing to offer in return, we are not defending sovereignty; we are managing surrender. Public sentiment, at 34.0, reflects this quiet despair — not hostility, but a loss of belief that the EU can shape its own future in the age of AI.

So we launch the Sovereign Therapeutics Initiative (STI), a €3.2 billion programme co-funded by Horizon Europe and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, hosted jointly by the EMA and the European Molecular Biology Laboratory. STI will deploy dedicated compute clusters on EU soil — secured under the Chips Act framework — to train auditable, EU-governed AI models for high-impact biomedical research. We are fast-tracking legal agreements with Germany, France, and the Netherlands to host the infrastructure, offering regional innovation grants in exchange for expedited permitting. Talent will be drawn from the SAM-CAL research diaspora and bolstered by a new fellowship programme under the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking.

This is not a race to match the closed frontier — we cannot outspend the superpowers in opacity. But we can out-design them in trust. STI’s models will be built under the same interpretability standards SAVF demands of others, making them slower, perhaps, but governable. We will share early results through the European Health Data Space, inviting public scrutiny as proof of integrity. The U.S. may dismiss this as incremental, but if we deliver even one therapy that is both effective and fully auditable, we will have proven that another path is possible — and that the civic order can innovate, not just resist.
