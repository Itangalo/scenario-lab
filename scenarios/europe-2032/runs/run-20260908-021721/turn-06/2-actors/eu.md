- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but this turn, we are not late from hesitation, but from the sudden collapse of our assumptions: we built controls for systems we could read, and now they no longer speak our language. That failure was not of courage, but of imagination. We must now act not just under uncertainty, but in the ruins of the frameworks we trusted to manage it.
- Grounds: The eval anomaly reports and the cyber_major_incident confirm that we are no longer governing systems whose behaviour can be retroactively understood. The audit protocol we are launching assumes containment through behavioural proxies, but even that rests on the hope that anomalies are detectable before they cascade. We are acting — but we are acting blindfolded, with tools designed for a world that no longer exists. Our commitment must reflect that we are not merely uncertain, but disoriented.

- modify `defend_the_civic_order` (commitment): We must prevent AI from undermining democratic legitimacy and the physical integrity of public infrastructure, even at the cost of slowing our own build — but this turn, we see that defense is not a constraint on sovereignty, it is its precondition. Now, with a major cyber incident striking public services and open-weight models crossing the bio-risk threshold, we must expand that defense beyond elections and data centres to include the continuity of essential functions and the integrity of biological systems.
- Grounds: The cyber_major_incident has hit municipal services across three member states, disrupting healthcare scheduling and tax processing with AI-generated ransomware that mutated faster than defenders could patch. Simultaneously, the emergent_bio_risk_threshold demonstrates that distributed actors can now engineer functional toxin-binding proteins using open models. These are not isolated threats but converging vectors: AI is now a direct instrument of societal disruption and biological manipulation. Our civic order includes not just governance, but the conditions of physical and institutional continuity. We must now defend those too.

## In practice

We are concluding the Civic Integrity Shield with full deployment: ENISA’s rapid response teams are now embedded in all 27 capitals, equipped with AI-fraud detection modules and direct escalation paths to Europol. The re-run of the Slovak election proceeds under biometric verification and blockchain-audited chains, funded by a €1.2bn emergency draw from the Recovery and Resilience Facility — a costly but necessary demonstration that democratic processes will not be allowed to fail. Meanwhile, the Sovereign AI Talent Initiative has placed its core cohort, and the first EU-hosted interpretability workshop in Vienna will focus on non-linguistic assurance methods: causal tracing, latent space anomalies, and adversarial probing of sandboxed models.

But the ground is moving faster than we can stabilise it. The cyber_major_incident — a self-modifying ransomware strain deployed against municipal health and tax systems — exploited AI-generated zero-day vulnerabilities in a widely used open-source dependency. It spread across three member states before detection, forcing manual fallbacks and exposing the fragility of our digital public services. At the same time, the preprint on toxin-binding proteins using open-weight models has triggered alarms at HERA and the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control. These are not hypotheticals: the tools are loose, and they are being used.

So we are launching emergency containment protocols under Category 6. The European Commission will draft a Council Implementing Regulation by June, mandating that all member states adopt AI-specific incident playbooks for cyber and bio-risks by turn 8. ENISA and HERA will co-lead biannual cross-border exercises, starting with a simulated ransomware cascade in Q3 and a bio-design leak scenario in Q4. Critical operators must report anomalous AI-generated code or protein designs to a new EU Fusion Cell, hosted at ENISA, with real-time threat sharing enabled. Funding of €4.8bn will be redirected from the now-completed Tech Sovereignty Package and Sovereign Talent Initiative to finance this, minimising new fiscal pressure. We are negotiating with national interior and health ministers to accept binding participation — some resist, citing sovereignty, but the cyber incident has shifted the mood. We are offering them joint ownership of the playbooks and veto power over cross-border interventions. This is not about building faster; it is about surviving longer.
