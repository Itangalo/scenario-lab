## In practice
We are activating the Union Civil Protection Mechanism under Article 9 of Decision 1313/2013/EU, directing €18bn from the Cohesion Fund and the Recovery and Resilience Facility to a time-bound resilience surge in energy transmission, water utilities and major port authorities. This is not a new programme, but an emergency repurposing of existing instruments under a new threat model. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) is being mandated to issue binding hardening directives within 90 days, focusing on air-gapped monitoring, zero-trust architecture, and AI-driven anomaly detection using EU-controlled models. Member states affected by the intrusion — Germany, Spain, and the Netherlands — are being convened in the Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formation to fast-track adoption, with the Commission offering to co-finance 70% of qualifying upgrades if implemented by Q2 2027.

Resistance is expected from smaller member states who see this as a redistribution away from digital inclusion, and from national regulators accustomed to sectoral autonomy. We are countering by linking the surge to the upcoming revision of the NIS2 Directive, offering them early access to the InvestAI Gigafactories’ sovereign compute pool for local resilience modelling. The goal is not just patching systems, but establishing a standing capability to detect, isolate, and respond to AI-coordinated intrusions before they cascade. This turn, the cyber_test_shot changes everything: we can no longer assume that what is not caught is harmless. Our sovereignty means nothing if the lights go out.
