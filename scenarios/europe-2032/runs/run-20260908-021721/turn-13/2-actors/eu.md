## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: one to build what we must have, and one to survive what we cannot yet build. The Sovereign Therapeutics Initiative (STI) moves forward, but slowly. We have secured provisional hosting agreements with Germany, France, and the Netherlands for the secure compute clusters, tied to regional innovation grants under the Recovery and Resilience Facility. However, permitting delays in the Netherlands threaten to push deployment into turn 14, and recruitment remains stalled — the SAM-CAL diaspora is dispersed, and private sector salaries dwarf public fellowships. We are now negotiating with EuroHPC JU to repurpose idle capacity from decommissioned testbeds to accelerate early training runs, but this is a stopgap, not a solution.

Meanwhile, SAVF concludes its technical phase and becomes legally binding this turn. The European Medicines Agency will now require all AI-derived therapies — foreign or domestic — to undergo real-time audit before clinical use in the EU. But enforcement remains hollow: without cross-border data access and with unresolved disputes over jurisdiction, the framework cannot yet inspect the models that matter. We are pushing for an emergency Council formation under the Health Union legal base to override national vetoes on data sharing, offering Italy and Spain expanded roles in audit oversight in exchange for support.

Most critically, we are acting on the denial of frontier access — not as an incident, but as a warning. The middle-power coalition was always a contingency, but now it is necessity. We are convening Japan, South Korea, Canada, and the Nordic bloc through the Trade and Technology Council and the newly established Digital Partnerships Forum. The goal is clear: joint leverage over export licensing, shared red lines for compute access, and a mutual recognition regime for safety evaluations. This is not alignment by consensus — it is bargaining from strength. We offer our semiconductor tooling controls, Japan offers rare gas purification, South Korea offers memory dominance, and Canada offers rare earth refining. Together, we can make exclusion costly. The first negotiation window opens in three months; if we do not act now, the next cutoff will not be a crisis — it will be a precedent.
