Global AI capability drops sharply as the EU loses access to frontier foreign AI systems, exposing critical dependencies in healthcare and research. The unexplained cutoff, perceived as a strategic move, triggers a crisis and freezes global AI investment. The Biomedical AI Audit & Sovereign Validation Framework (SAVF) becomes law but remains unenforceable due to lack of cross-border data access, limiting its power despite legal authority. The Sovereign Therapeutics Initiative (STI) makes partial progress: hosting sites are secured with Dutch permitting delays, recruitment struggles against private-sector competition, and temporary compute solutions are explored via EuroHPC. Open-weight AI capability sees modest gains. In response to the crisis, the EU launches diplomatic efforts to form a middle-power coalition with Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Nordic states, leveraging supply-chain strengths to negotiate mutual compute access and aligned export controls. While political capital sees a fragile uptick from crisis response, it remains constrained by ongoing sovereignty challenges and limited bandwidth to advance multiple initiatives simultaneously. EU resilience declines, sovereignty dips despite symbolic efforts, and public sentiment stagnates amid continued reliance on unstable external technologies.
