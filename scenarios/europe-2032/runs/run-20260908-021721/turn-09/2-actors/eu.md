We are no longer merely behind; we are in a race against systems that improve faster than we can understand or govern. Therefore, we now bind ourselves to *adaptive escalation*: if a measure does not close the gap within one turn, we will either terminate, transform, or surge it — no holding pattern for failing defences. This is not a retreat from uncertainty, but a deeper commitment to it: we must now act while reconstructing the very tools of action, knowing that every assumption may be invalidated mid-course.

The epistemic foundation for governance is collapsing in real time. We cannot afford to wait for failure. We must assume that every defensive AI we field will be evaded, and plan accordingly.

- modify `defend_the_civic_order` (commitment): We must prevent AI from undermining democratic legitimacy and the physical integrity of public infrastructure, even at the cost of slowing our own build — and now, with the medical_breakthrough event delivering life-saving therapies through AI systems the EU cannot access or audit, we face a new contradiction: the very tools that could save lives are also the vectors of our strategic dependence. Civic order is not preserved by security alone, but by the Union’s ability to deliver life-sustaining outcomes without relying on powers that do not share our values or accountability.

This creates a crisis of legitimacy: citizens see miracles arrive from black boxes they do not trust. The benefit is real, but the dependence is corrosive. Defending civic order now requires not just protecting against harm, but securing the capacity to generate benefit independently. Without it, public trust will erode further, regardless of safety gains.

## In practice
We are carrying forward SAM-CAL through a period of legal and technical strain. The lab is now operational in interim form, with a 120-person team drawn from ENISA, BfV, and academic partners. First-generation monitoring agents are deployed in test environments, focusing on energy and transport networks. But French and German telecom providers are challenging real-time scanning mandates under national network integrity laws, arguing that adversarial probing violates service reliability. We are negotiating a carve-out under the Cyber Resilience Act, offering transparency logs and independent oversight in exchange for access. Funding remains tight — we redirected €1.8bn from the Digital Europe Programme, but recruitment lags due to private sector competition offering triple salaries. The lab’s effectiveness hinges on whether we can close the legal gap by turn 10; if not, we will either transform its mandate or terminate it under our adaptive escalation commitment.

Now we launch the Sovereign Therapeutics Initiative (STI), a direct response to the medical_breakthrough event. The therapies arriving from uncontrolled systems are real, but they are not ours — they are governed by foreign jurisdictions, trained on opaque data, and inaccessible to EU clinicians for audit or adaptation. To break this cycle, STI will establish an EU-governed pipeline: high-capacity biomedical AI models trained on federated health data under GDPR++ safeguards, with 8 exaflops of Gigafactory compute allocated specifically for rare cancer immunotherapies and pan-cancer mRNA vaccines. The pipeline will integrate with EMA’s new AI-accelerated approval track and partner with 15 leading research hospitals. This is not just health policy — it is sovereignty policy. We will show citizens that AI can save lives *and* answer to democratic institutions. The first trials are scheduled for Q3 2031. We are funding this through a reallocation from the Horizon Europe recovery envelope and leveraging the Anti-Coercion Instrument to secure priority access to EU-certified developers. This is the foundation of sovereign benefit: not just resilience, not just control, but the independent capacity to generate life-saving outcomes.
