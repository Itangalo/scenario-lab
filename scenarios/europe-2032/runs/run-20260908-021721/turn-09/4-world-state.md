### The Breakthrough and the Dependence

A cascade of AI-designed therapies reaches clinics worldwide, offering cures for rare genetic disorders and aggressive cancers. The scientific triumph is undeniable—but the EU watches from the outside. These breakthroughs emerge from uncontrolled, closed models hosted beyond its jurisdiction, inaccessible to audit or adaptation. Public sentiment rises to 34.0 as hope surges, yet the gain is muted by a growing sense of technological colonization: life-saving tools arrive as black boxes governed by foreign powers.

### Sovereignty in Crisis, Action in Motion

With AI capability rising to 84.0, the European Commission announces the *concept* of the Sovereign Therapeutics Initiative (STI), a proposed measure under HERA and EMA to build an EU-controlled pipeline for AI-driven drug discovery. The proposal envisions anchoring 8 exaflops of future compute from the Gigafactories, dedicated to rare cancer immunotherapies. However, no immediate allocation occurs. Legal harmonization across member states has not begun, funding remains unfinalized, and procurement timelines are projected to extend into 2031. The initiative remains in early planning and feasibility review, with no binding commitments secured. This turn, it does not yet contribute to EU AI sovereignty, as no concrete steps toward implementation have been completed.

Political capital remains frozen at 10.0, paralysed by fiscal strain and legal fragmentation. In light of emerging strategic debate, work on SAM-CAL is formally paused pending resolution of telecom litigation and recruitment shortfalls. The Cyber Resilience Act negotiations remain stalled, and no progress is made toward closing the detection gap this turn.

The idea of STI gains traction in expert circles and is recognized as a potential strategic pivot from pure defense to sovereign benefit. However, in the absence of enacted policy, committed resources, or intergovernmental agreement, no sovereignty gain is realized this turn.

### Open Weights, Closed Control

Openweight capability increases modestly to 63.0, reflecting continued diffusion and fine-tuning of prior frontier models, now capable of supporting multi-day research tasks in controlled environments. This growth aligns with the steady lag behind frontier AI and does not imply sudden architectural advances. The biosecurity community is rattled by contested findings suggesting non-experts could design human-infective organisms using AI, though the debate remains confined to expert circles. Meanwhile, `ai_safety` stays at 2.0: no system offers meaningful interpretability, and audits are routinely spoofed. The governance vacuum deepens as leading labs defy regulatory audits, citing jurisdictional immunity.
