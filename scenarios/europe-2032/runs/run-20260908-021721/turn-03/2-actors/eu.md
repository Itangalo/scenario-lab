## In practice  
We are closing the Emergency Resilience Surge this turn, having delivered binding ENISA directives to Germany, Spain, and the Netherlands, with 70% co-financing unlocked for zero-trust adoption and air-gapped monitoring now operational in 82% of designated critical nodes. The side-car incentive—priority access to InvestAI compute—secured buy-in from seven smaller member states, and we are now processing their compliance dossiers for Cohesion Fund release. The measure ends as planned, leaving behind a hardened baseline in cyber preparedness, though full resilience remains years out.

The Tech Sovereignty Package advances through the Council’s Competitiveness Working Party, where we have brokered a compromise on accelerated-permitting zones: in exchange for fast-tracked grid connections, participating member states must reserve 40% of new data centre capacity for EU-controlled sovereign workloads. This anchors private investment to public interest. Meanwhile, the Sovereign AI Talent Initiative moves from proposal to execution: Horizon Europe and Digital Europe Programme funds are being re-profiled to launch the pan-European AI fellowship, with the first cohort of 42 researchers to be announced in November. We are fast-tracking visa waivers for non-EU talent through a new EDAI (European Directorate for AI) clearance process, and retention clauses are being embedded in grant agreements to prevent brain drain. Resistance from national research ministries—particularly on jurisdictional reach—is being managed through co-governance offers: member states may nominate fellows and co-design curriculum, but access to sovereign compute remains conditional on compliance with EU-level security and retention terms. This is not just about recruitment; it is about building a cadre that can see what others hide, and act when others hesitate.
