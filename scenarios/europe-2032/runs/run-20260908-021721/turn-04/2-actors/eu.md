Launch an emergency programme to secure democratic processes and critical public infrastructure from AI-enabled manipulation and sabotage, combining rapid incident response, media provenance standards, and physical protection of data infrastructure.

## Priority  
**Civic Integrity Shield** — because the annulment of an election and physical attacks on data centres are not future risks but present breakdowns, and no sovereign AI capacity can be meaningfully exercised in a Union where democratic legitimacy is contested and its infrastructure is a battleground.

## In practice  
We are concluding the Secure Openweight Threshold this turn, having enforced the 48.0 cap through updated market access conditions under the AI Act. The European Commission’s Digital Services Coordinator network has issued binding notices to all major open-source hosting platforms operating in the EU: any model release exceeding the threshold will trigger automatic suspension of platform liability protections. We have also secured commitments from leading open-weight developers — including EleutherAI and Hugging Face — to implement pre-release capability screening, with EDAI providing reference evals. While enforcement remains patchy in decentralized forums, the measure has successfully deterred formal releases above the line. This is not a full solution, but it draws a boundary.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign AI Talent Initiative advances: the first cohort of 42 fellows has been selected, with 17 already relocated under fast-track visas. We are now negotiating retention agreements with national research agencies, offering co-funding in exchange for alignment on security protocols and data access. The Tech Sovereignty Package clears its final Council reading next month, with the 40% sovereign capacity carve-out now legally binding. InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, though construction at the Finnish site faces delays due to grid integration disputes — we are deploying Cohesion Fund arbitration to resolve them.

But the ground has shifted. The annulment of the Slovak election — formally suspended due to “unverifiable AI-generated content influencing voter perception” — has triggered a crisis of confidence. Simultaneously, the occupation of the Marseille data centre and sabotage of its power link have exposed our physical vulnerability. We cannot build compute if it is being torn down. The Civic Integrity Shield launches immediately: ENISA will coordinate a rapid threat assessment for all 2029 electoral processes, while the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre deploys mobile hardening units to high-risk data sites. We are fast-tracking funding for media provenance standards (C2PA) across public broadcasters and launching a pan-EU public information campaign to reinforce trust in verified sources. This is not a pause — it is a recalibration. We are defending the space in which sovereignty can exist.
