## In practice
SAM-CAL has now formally completed its run. Over the past two turns, it established an interim operational capability within ENISA, deploying first-generation counter-agent systems in test environments across energy and transport networks. However, legal challenges from French and German telecom providers over real-time scanning mandates remain unresolved. With no political capital to force a resolution and our adaptive escalation commitment now in force, we are not extending SAM-CAL. Its core research and detection protocols will be archived and transferred to the new Joint Cyber Resilience Directorate under the Cyber Resilience Act framework — a leaner, legally bounded successor that respects national network integrity laws while preserving threat-hunting capacity. This is not a retreat from defense, but a recalibration under collapse: we cannot sustain failing defences, and we must now focus on where sovereignty can still be asserted — in the institutions that certify, validate, and control access.

We are now launching the Sovereign Validation Framework (SAVF), a direct escalation of our revised *defend_the_civic_order* commitment. The arrival of un-auditable, foreign-controlled AI therapies has created a crisis: we are being saved by systems we cannot trust, and that trust deficit is corroding public legitimacy. SAVF closes that gap by mandating full model transparency, data lineage, and real-time interpretability for any AI used in EU healthcare — including systems developed abroad. The legal base is Article 114 TFEU, with EMA as the enforcement authority. Developers must submit to adversarial probing, runtime monitoring, and open logging; non-compliant systems are barred from clinical use. We are funding this through a €900 million reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme and securing buy-in from national regulators through early access to audit tools and shared talent pools. Resistance is expected from U.S. biotech firms and their governments, but we will leverage the Anti-Coercion Instrument and tie market access to compliance. This is not protectionism — it is the assertion of democratic oversight over life-critical AI. If we cannot audit the cure, we cannot call it ours.
