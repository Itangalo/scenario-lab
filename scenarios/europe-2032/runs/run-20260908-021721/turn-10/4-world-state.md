### The Unauditable Cure

Global AI capability advances to 87.0 as closed systems design increasingly complex biomolecular therapies, now including multi-target immunotherapies with adaptive delivery mechanisms. These systems operate beyond human interpretability, their reasoning opaque even to their developers. The EU watches as life-saving treatments arrive from abroad, their clinical logs sealed, their training data untraceable.

### SAM-CAL Completes — But Not as Planned

The Stand up EU Sovereign Agent Monitoring & Counter-AI Lab (SAM-CAL) formally completes its mandate. It has delivered detection prototypes and adversarial AI testbeds, now archived for future use. However, with no legal resolution on network scanning rights and political capital at crisis levels, the operational mandate is not extended. Core functions are folded into a leaner cyber resilience framework, preserving research but halting active deployment.

### SAVF: A Stand on Medical Sovereignty

The EU announces the Biomedical AI Audit & Sovereign Validation Framework (SAVF), a bold attempt to assert control over foreign-developed AI in healthcare. Under Article 114 TFEU, the European Medicines Agency will require full model transparency, runtime monitoring, and adversarial probing for any AI used in clinical applications. Non-compliant systems face market exclusion.

The move triggers immediate backlash. U.S. biotech firms warn of delayed treatments, and Washington signals concern under the ongoing consolidation posture, which prioritizes tiered frontier access. Negotiations begin, but no compliance is yet secured. The framework is in early implementation, with technical design work underway and no operational capacity yet deployed. Member state support remains limited to planning discussions.

### Partial Gains, Fragile Ground

SAVF’s announcement establishes a precedent for enforceable transparency — the first such regime globally — and contributes to a modest rise in AI safety to 5.0. However, the initiative is still in its design phase and has not yet advanced to implementation, meaning no immediate boost to sovereignty or political capital from execution. Sovereignty rises slightly to 18.0, reflecting the strategic signal of jurisdictional assertion, but remains constrained by lack of operational follow-through. Political capital holds steady at 10.0, as the completion of SAM-CAL offsets the initial costs of launching SAVF, with no net gain.

Open-weight models advance modestly to 65.0, reflecting incremental progress in distributed training and fine-tuning efforts, including early replication of certain closed-model techniques in open settings. No major open release or coordinated scaling event occurs, and the gap with closed systems remains pronounced.
