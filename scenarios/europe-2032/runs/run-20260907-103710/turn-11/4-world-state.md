### The Biosecurity Gambit

The EU launched the **Emergency Biosecurity Shield**, declaring it a top priority in response to alarming signals from AI-assisted biothreat research. Invoking emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU, the Commission moved to integrate two emergent, decentralized networks — formed outside EU oversight — into a unified early-warning system. Seed funding was released, procurement fast-tracked, and legal indemnity offered to participating institutions.

Yet political capital remained frozen at 6.0, too low to enforce cohesion. Germany withheld its emergency allocation, insisting on national control over detection protocols. France delayed integration of its clinical AI systems, citing data sovereignty. The Netherlands agreed in principle but tied disbursement to parliamentary review — now scheduled beyond the shield’s intended deployment window.

The ECDC struggled to unify technical standards, as the emergent networks used incompatible detection heuristics. Retroactive legitimacy failed to resolve jurisdictional friction: one consortium had already signed bilateral deals with Swiss and UK labs, creating parallel reporting channels. The public communication campaign stalled, with member states issuing conflicting messages.

### Capability Surge, Safety Frozen

A **capability_jump** pushed `ai_capability` to 88.0, entering the terminal zone. Sub-linear growth halved the increment, but the jump still registered as profound — frontier systems now autonomously set research agendas. Labs dismissed internal eval anomalies as noise. With no safety measure landing, `ai_safety` remained at 1.0.

`openweight_capability` rose modestly to 72.5, as distillation techniques improved efficiency without breaking new ground.

### Fragmentation Deepens

The **member_state_defection** event — Austria signing a separate data-sharing pact with a U.S. hyperscaler — undercut EU access to critical inference capacity. This triggered a −1 under sovereignty’s event term. Combined with −1 from capability rise, and no offsetting gains, `eu_ai_sovereignty` fell to 12.0.

Political capital stayed at 6.0: portfolio charges (−9) negated any symbolic benefit from launching a large new measure. The proposal bonus was absent — the bio shield, while urgent, responded to an emerging trend, not a landed incident. Public sentiment held at 15.0; no visible benefit emerged, and job losses in legal and administrative sectors continued.

Resilience did not improve. No category 6 measure completed, and cyber defences remained unupgraded.
