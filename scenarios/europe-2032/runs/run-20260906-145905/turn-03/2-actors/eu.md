## New measure  
**Secure European Access to Advanced Lithography (SEAL)**  
A binding legal and financial framework to co-fund and operate an EU-controlled servicing corridor for mature-node ASML lithography tools, ensuring uninterrupted access to critical semiconductor infrastructure despite extraterritorial pressure.  

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to +12 eu_ai_sovereignty by turn 5 by anchoring upstream semiconductor maintenance in EU jurisdiction; reduces dependence on foreign-controlled supply chains for domestic AI capacity; counters supply_chain_coercion by establishing a sovereign servicing corridor  
`Applies to:` ASML, EU member states hosting production lines, European semiconductor manufacturers, and the European Defence Fund’s critical components programme  

## Priority  
**Secure European Access to Advanced Lithography (SEAL)** — because the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive finishes this turn and without sovereign access to the tools that build the chips that run the models, even legally reserved compute is meaningless, and the US restriction on ASML servicing proves that coercion at the physical layer now directly threatens our strategic autonomy.

## In practice  
This turn, we finalise and adopt the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive under Article 114 TFEU, completing its trilogue with the Council and Parliament. The 30% reservation of frontier-class compute for EU-critical use is now law, and the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) begins standing up the auction-based utility model for allocation. The liability shield and fast-track access to InvestAI Gigafactories funding are activated for compliant operators, and we confirm AI Acceleration Zones in seven member states, locking in six-month permitting caps and grid guarantees. Germany and Ireland remain uneasy, but the ASML servicing crisis shifts the balance: dependence on US goodwill for both compute access and chipmaking tools is now visibly unsustainable.

With the Directive secured, we pivot to the next layer of dependence. The SEAL initiative moves from pre-legislative drafting to formal proposal, with a €12bn co-funding envelope drawn from the European Defence Fund and the Chips Act’s unused reserves. We convene STMicroelectronics, Infineon, and ASML’s EU-based engineers to design a parallel maintenance network under EU legal jurisdiction, using existing facilities in Dresden, Crolles, and Catania as anchors. The legal basis will be Article 114 for internal market integrity and Article 42 for defence capability, with implementation managed by a new Joint Undertaking under the European Chips Institute. The goal is not to replicate ASML’s full R&D capacity, but to ensure that the tools already on EU soil—tools without which no sovereign compute can run—can be serviced, calibrated, and kept operational without foreign approval. This is not protectionism; it is the minimum viable insurance against strategic paralysis.
