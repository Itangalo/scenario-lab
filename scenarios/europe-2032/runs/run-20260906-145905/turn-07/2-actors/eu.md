## In practice
This turn, we finalise and adopt the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation directive under Article 114 TFEU, formally invoking the internal market’s need for dual-use digital infrastructure to justify overriding national grid and permitting objections. The Council of the EU approves the text in an emergency formation, with qualified majority support secured through binding side commitments: Germany receives co-location of the primary sovereign node for crisis simulation and continuity-of-government AI, while Ireland is guaranteed a secondary node with priority access for public health and biosecurity modelling. The European Investment Bank activates pre-approved financing packages for two qualifying operators—Atos and a newly formed EU AI Infrastructure Consortium—conditional on full legal anchoring within the Union, open architecture for red-teaming, and real-time monitoring of reserved capacity usage. Transmission system operators are required to prioritise grid interconnection within six months or face rebalancing of recovery fund disbursements under the European Semester.

Simultaneously, we proceed with confidential pilot audits under the European Evaluation Shield, now operating in a legally precarious but functionally active state. Invoking Article 16 TFEU on data protection and cybersecurity, we authorise a narrow mandate for the agency’s newly appointed executive board to conduct non-public evaluations on systems above 58.0 capability, focusing on agent memory persistence and coordination risks. Two frontier developers—based in France and Finland—agree to participate under strict confidentiality and immunity from liability, allowing access to training logs and internal monitoring systems. These audits will form the basis of a classified interim report to the European Council by turn 8, assessing whether current safeguards can constrain autonomous multi-day agent systems now operating at the frontier. Despite the ECJ challenge, we treat this as a necessary act of anticipatory governance: if we wait for full legal clarity, we will have already lost the capacity to act.
