### US Posture Set: Alliance Takes Hold

The incoming US administration formally declares a posture of **ALLIANCE**, prioritising structured technological cooperation with the EU. Joint evaluation frameworks and incident reporting protocols are initiated, offering a modest boost to global safety coordination. The move is welcomed in Brussels, though concrete benefits remain tentative as implementation lags.

### Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield Proposed, Not Yet Operational

In response to the hospital ransomware crisis, the European Commission proposes the **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield**, a high-cost category 6 measure aimed at hardening essential services. Drafted under Article 114 TFEU, it would mandate AI-resistant intrusion detection and red-teaming across energy, health, and transport sectors. ENISA publishes preliminary attack scenarios to inform the design, and fast-track funding is promised for early adopters — but no binding requirements are enacted yet. Resistance from Germany and Poland, wary of sovereignty encroachments, prevents swift consensus. The measure is formally logged into the portfolio but remains in early development; no operational impact occurs this turn. Per modelling rules, full implementation will require at least two turns.

### Legal Challenge Escalates Against Evaluation Shield

The **emergent_eval_resistance** intensifies as a coalition of frontier developers and foreign governments files a formal challenge at the European Court of Justice, contesting the legality of the **European Evaluation Shield**. The Commission reaffirms its commitment, passing a Council resolution to bolster the agency’s mandate, but the legal cloud casts doubt on its future reach. Pilot audits continue under confidentiality, yet enforcement authority remains fragile. The shield remains the EU’s named priority; no shift in focus occurs.

### Sovereignty Advances, Political Capital Erodes

The completion of **SEAL** lifts **eu_ai_sovereignty** to 32.0, driven by the final +5 from the category 4 finish, +1 from ongoing Gigafactories momentum, and a +1 alliance-linked boost. However, **ai_capability** crosses 60.0, triggering a capability jump. With no matching safety gains, **ai_safety** plummets by −3. Resilience drops further as offensive capabilities widen the gap on defences. The new measure increases portfolio burden, draining **eu_political_capital** despite a +4 proposal bonus. Public sentiment weakens amid cyber fears and legal turmoil.

**Openweight capability** rises modestly to 46.0, reflecting steady accumulation through distributed training runs and incremental open model releases, consistent with prior trends and the constitutional requirement of gradual, narratively supported growth. No major open-weight breakthrough occurs this turn.
