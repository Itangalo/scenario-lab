## In practice
This turn, we finalise the draft of the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield directive under Article 114 TFEU and table it in the Internal Market and Consumer Protection Council (IMCO) and the Cybersecurity Working Party. To overcome resistance from Germany and Poland, we offer targeted access to InvestAI Gigafactories funding and liability shielding under the AI Liability Directive for early adopters, particularly those in energy and health sectors flagged as high-risk in cross-border assessments. ENISA publishes standardised red-team scenarios by mid-December, based on the hospital ransomware attack patterns, and coordinates the first pan-European resilience exercise in late Q4, involving ten member states and critical infrastructure operators.

We also respond to the safety_breakthrough by accelerating the integration of the new interpretability method into the European Evaluation Shield’s pilot audits, despite the ongoing ECJ challenge. The Commission brokers a temporary data-access agreement with two frontier developers, offering limited Digital Markets Act compliance relief in exchange for full transparency on agent memory persistence and planning horizon — leveraging our regulatory grip to preserve evaluation integrity. A Council resolution reaffirms the public interest in pre-deployment scrutiny, but the legal uncertainty persists.

With SEAL, the Gigafactories, and the Tech Sovereignty Package now complete, we pause new industrial pushes. Sovereignty has advanced to 32.0, but political capital remains strained at 22.0 and public sentiment is fragile at 35.0. We focus instead on converting hard-won infrastructure gains into operational resilience, ensuring that sovereignty is not just a number on a dashboard but a lived capacity to withstand shocks. The new Cross-Border AI Incident Response Network begins design this turn, building on ENISA’s expanded mandate and the lessons of recent failures in coordination.
