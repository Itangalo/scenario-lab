## In practice
This turn, we finalise the draft of the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield directive under Article 114 TFEU and table it in the Internal Market and Consumer Protection Council (IMCO) and the Cybersecurity Working Party. To overcome resistance from Germany, Poland, and the Nordic Council over sovereignty concerns, we offer early adopters preferential access to InvestAI Gigafactories funding and liability shielding under the revised AI Liability Directive, specifically for AI systems used in mandated red-teaming and patching. ENISA publishes the first standardised red-team scenarios by June, modelled on the hospital ransomware attack and updated to reflect swarm tactics recently neutralised by the **cyber_defence_breakthrough**, and coordinates a second pan-European resilience exercise in late Q2, expanding to twelve member states and including live simulations in energy grid and rail signalling systems.

We also advance the Cross-Border AI Incident Response Network by appointing the first permanent coordinator and launching the legal integration of national CERTs into a shared activation protocol. The Council adopts a resolution affirming the Network’s authority to override national delays during declared incidents, a step made politically possible by the **election_annulled** event, which has shifted public and elite attention toward systemic fragility in democratic infrastructure. Despite ongoing legal challenges to the European Evaluation Shield, we maintain pressure on frontier developers through the Digital Markets Act leverage, securing a second round of data access for pilot audits focused on agent memory persistence — but we do not expand evaluation efforts further, as political capital remains too thin to force new fronts. The focus is on consolidation: turning completed industrial projects into operational resilience, and ensuring that the EU does not win sovereignty on paper only to lose it in the first real crisis.
