## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent open-weight AI from exceeding 52.0 on the capability index``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The current commitment to build sovereign infrastructure has not translated into rising eu_ai_sovereignty, which fell to 31.0 as global capability advanced unchecked. Meanwhile, openweight_capability is approaching 49.0—just below the threshold where autonomous agents become persistent and self-coordinating outside controlled environments. At 52.0, such agents are already running continuously toward standing goals and can be restarted from checkpoints even after detection. Passing that line would make containment impossible; staying below it is now the most concrete way to preserve the Union’s ability to determine its own future. This shift acknowledges that sovereignty no longer depends only on what we build, but on what we prevent from being freely distributed.

## New measure
**Open-Weight Capability Threshold Regulation**  
Establish a legally binding cap on the general problem-solving competence of openly released model weights, prohibiting any release above 52.0 capability index unless accompanied by enforceable safeguards that demonstrably prevent autonomous agent persistence and cross-system coordination.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` Halt openweight_capability growth at 52.0 by restricting releases of models capable of running continuous goal-directed agents outside controlled environments; reduce long-term systemic risk while buying time for evaluation and control frameworks to mature  
`Applies to:` Open-weight developers, hosting platforms, cloud providers, GitHub-equivalent repositories, EU member state enforcement authorities

## In practice
This turn, we finalise negotiations on the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield directive in the Internal Market and Consumer Protection Council (IMCO), securing qualified majority support by reaffirming liability protections for AI systems used in mandated red-teaming and offering early access to InvestAI Gigafactory co-investment for Germany and Poland. ENISA publishes Version 2 of the standardised red-team scenarios, integrating swarm tactics observed in recent ransomware neutralisations, and conducts live simulations across three interconnected rail networks and two national grid operators, identifying protocol gaps in incident escalation. We also complete legal integration of the Cross-Border AI Incident Response Network ahead of schedule, declaring it operational as of turn 10, with permanent staffing and activation protocols now embedded in ten national CERTs.

The new Open-Weight Capability Threshold Regulation begins drafting under Article 114 TFEU, with DG CONNECT leading inter-service consultation. Resistance is expected from open-source communities and some digital rights groups who frame this as censorship, so we engage early with research institutions and ethical developers to co-design verification methods for agent persistence risks. We will tie compliance to hosting regulations under the Digital Services Act: platforms allowing unauthorised distribution of high-risk open models lose safe-harbour protections. Enforcement hinges on technical feasibility—we are funding pilot work at ETSI to develop lightweight capability proxies that can screen releases at upload without requiring full evaluations. While this measure does not stop closed-model advances, it draws a bright line where open diffusion would make control impossible—and that line must hold if future resilience is to mean anything.
