### US Posture Confirmed: Consolidation Takes Hold  
With the new administration in Washington sworn in, the long-anticipated shift to a consolidation posture becomes official. The US_POSTURE: CONSOLIDATION directive signals that access to frontier AI will be tightly rationed, prioritizing strategic allies and restricting flows to regions deemed less aligned — a direct blow to the EU’s ambitions. The move amplifies pressure on Brussels to secure independent capacity.

### Gigafactories Push Forward Amid Fractures  
The EU doubles down on the InvestAI Gigafactories as its last credible sovereignty play. Emergency provisions under Article 122 TFEU redirect €30bn from defunct procurement into subsidised power deals for the French and Polish sites. However, legal challenges from smaller member states over perceived inequities in siting and funding threaten delays. Offers of co-location slots for national research nodes are floated to buy off opposition, but cohesion remains fragile.

A new Chips Act delegated act is being drafted to tie funding to participation in the Geneva semiconductor servicing pool, aiming to break dependency on U.S.-controlled EUV maintenance. Yet, informal vendor notifications have sparked murmurs of pushback, with some questioning the EU’s leverage.

### Rising Capability, Falling Safety  
AI capability advances to 69.0, driven by closed-loop co-design in hardware and AI training. Reports of genome models producing human-infective organism designs trigger quiet alarm in biosecurity circles. With no deployed safeguards and systems growing less interpretable, ai_safety drops to 4.0. Meanwhile, openweight_capability jumps to 64.0 as leaked variants spread, narrowing the gap.

### Sentiment and Capital Erode  
Resilience dips to 39.0 with no new protective measures completed. Public sentiment falls to 30.0 as anxiety over job displacement and infrastructure strain grows. Political capital drops to 11.0, weighed down by three large measures in flight and no relief from completed initiatives.
