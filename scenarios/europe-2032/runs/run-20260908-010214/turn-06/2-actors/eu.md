## In practice
We are executing the final phase of the InvestAI Gigafactories rollout under emergency infrastructure provisions, focusing on the two lead sites in France and Poland. The Commission has activated Article 122 TFEU to justify the €30bn reallocation from the defunct procurement programme to subsidised power purchase agreements, treating energy access as a matter of Union-wide security. National regulators in both host states have pre-approved grid integration and water cooling rights, but legal challenges from smaller member states — invoking proportionality and accusing the Commission of bypassing cohesion principles — are now before the Court of Justice. We are countering with political offers: guaranteed co-location slots for national AI research nodes at each site, funded from the remaining Gigafactory envelope, in exchange for dropping legal objections. This is not charity — it is coalition-building around the only infrastructure that can break our dependency.

Simultaneously, we are hardwiring supply-chain resilience into the Gigafactory model. Legal services are finalising a delegated act under the Chips Act that will require any site receiving Union funding to join the Geneva semiconductor servicing pool and diversify EUV maintenance chains beyond U.S. jurisdiction. This turns a vulnerability into a condition of access: no funding without mutualised servicing. The act will be proposed to the College in December, but we are already informally notifying selected vendors that contract negotiations will include this clause. The goal is to ensure that when the new U.S. administration begins restricting frontier AI access, the EU still controls not just the compute, but the means to sustain it. This is no longer industrial policy — it is the foundation of political agency in the AI era.
