The offer is clear: access to €45bn in low-interest, condition-bound financing from the Crisis and Investment Facility, drawn against the InvestAI envelope, in exchange for binding commitments — legally anchored in EU jurisdiction — that (1) at least 60% of the compute delivered is physically located in the EU, (2) weights for models above 50.0 capability are not released without a 90-day EU review window, and (3) public agencies have priority access to 15% of inference capacity for civil protection and sovereign research.

The Council of the EU will be convened in an extraordinary session under the Competitiveness configuration to endorse the legal basis by end-March, while the Commission’s Joint Research Centre begins drafting the technical annexes for capability thresholds and access protocols. Resistance is expected from member states with strong commercial ties to non-EU providers, and from civil liberties groups wary of public-private control entanglements. We are countering with transparency: all agreements will be published in full, and a new oversight board — co-chaired by ENISA and the European Data Protection Supervisor — will audit compliance quarterly. This is not nationalisation; it is strategic conditioning of capital to lock sovereignty into the next generation of infrastructure while the frontier stumbles.
