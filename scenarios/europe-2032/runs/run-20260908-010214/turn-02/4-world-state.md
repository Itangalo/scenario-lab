### The Frontier Lurches Forward

A sudden leap in AI capability—driven by a single, unanticipated breakthrough—propels the frontier from 54.5 to 58.5, bypassing years of expected progress. The release, developed in a tightly guarded lab, demonstrates autonomous research agents that design and validate novel protein structures in hours. This discontinuous advance triggers widespread reassessment of deployment timelines and safety assumptions. With no corresponding safety breakthrough, and multiple labs now scrambling to catch up, confidence in control plummets. ai_safety collapses by 12 points to 20.0, reflecting the growing chasm between capability and understanding.

Simultaneously, a wave of capital retreats from the AI sector. Major investors liquidate positions, and several high-profile compute expansions are abruptly cancelled. The so-called "AI investment collapse" destabilises the ecosystem European sovereignty efforts depend on, evaporating projected partnerships and straining developer liquidity.

Open-weight systems see moderate gains as smaller actors repurpose newly available infrastructure and leverage leaked training data from prior frontier models. openweight_capability rises to 50.5—still lagging behind the new frontier, but narrowing the gap through opportunistic scaling rather than coordinated release. The increase is substantial but remains within plausible bounds, given fragmented access to decommissioned clusters and shared inference capacity.

### EU Seizes the Moment—But Moves Cautiously

Amid the turmoil, the European Commission proposes the Accelerated Sovereignty Procurement initiative, a high-cost emergency measure offering €45bn in financing to secure strategic compute from financially weakened hyperscalers. The plan demands binding commitments on EU-based infrastructure, model review windows, and public access. However, no procurement has yet been finalised. Two member states immediately challenge the plan’s proportionality, and legal scrutiny mounts over data sovereignty and market distortion. As a result, implementation is delayed, and no capacity is secured this turn.

The Emergency Resilience Surge concludes, delivering partial hardening of critical infrastructure. Though air-gapped systems are deployed in key nodes, uneven implementation limits gains. resilience rises by 3, reflecting improved incident response in energy and transport sectors.
