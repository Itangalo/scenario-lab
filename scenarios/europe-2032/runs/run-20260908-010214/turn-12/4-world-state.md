### Capability Marches On, Safety Stalls  
Global AI capability advances by 3.5 points to 89.5, continuing its steady climb. Systems demonstrate refined coordination in digital infrastructure and self-directed training loop optimisation. Despite mounting near-misses, no single catastrophic breach occurs — but interpretability remains outpaced, and containment relies increasingly on hope. `ai_safety` holds at 2.0, reflecting systemic strain with no relief.

### EU Launches Compute Mobilisation, Amid Deepening Crisis  
The EU proposes the **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation Initiative**, a Category 4, large-scale measure aiming to build three high-assurance, physically secured compute clusters in Finland, Austria, and Romania. Invoking Article 122 TFEU, the Commission redirects €14bn from digital and infrastructure funds, and ASML commits to prioritising EU sovereign projects. Emergency procurement planning begins for domestic packaging and cooling infrastructure.

However, implementation cannot start immediately. Germany and the Netherlands raise legal and governance objections to centralised control, delaying final approval. US and Taiwanese foundries signal that export controls and domestic demand will constrain equipment delivery. The project is formally launched, but full mobilisation will take at least two turns due to scale and external dependencies.

The **Sovereign Autonomous Systems Monitoring Network** completes deployment, delivering partial visibility into AI agent activity in pilot zones. Yet without sovereign compute to act on findings, its impact remains limited. With this completion, the measure is retired from the active portfolio.

### Public Trust Remains Collapsed  
Labour markets fracture further as AI displaces entry-level roles in law, accounting, and administration. A scandal over an AI-driven social insurance system wrongly denying claims ignites fury, deepening public hostility. Protests grow, framing AI governance as both oppressive and impotent. `public_sentiment` remains at 14.0.

A new coalition of middle powers forms, aligning export controls and bargaining jointly for compute access. This boosts EU leverage and morale, delivering a +2 event-term increase to `eu_ai_sovereignty`. Combined with the −1 decay from the capability jump (≥2.0), and the prior value of 11.0, sovereignty rises to 12.0.

The **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation Initiative** is designated the EU’s current priority. All other prior measures remain under review, but only this one carries the priority designation in the portfolio charge. The notepad updates accordingly: Emergency Safety Evaluation Corps −3, Emergency Sovereign Compute Access Directive −3, Emergency Public Order and Systemic Risk Shield −3, Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation Initiative −3, priority −1 = −13.

`openweight_capability` increases by 1.5 points to 75.5 — a modest rise reflecting continued use and adaptation of existing open models, but constrained by fragmented toolchains, limited access to advanced packaging, and degraded global collaboration.
