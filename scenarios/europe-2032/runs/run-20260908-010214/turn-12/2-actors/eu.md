## In practice
We are acting on the fact that sovereignty is not declared — it is wired in. The Sovereign Autonomous Systems Monitoring Network has delivered its first signals, but those signals lead nowhere we can act. Our evaluators lack the stack to run seized models. Our containment zones in Finland, Austria, and Romania are being built, but their compute backbones rely on foreign-supplied accelerators and cloud fallbacks that nullify air-gapping under duress. This is not resilience — it is theatre.

This turn, we are launching the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation Initiative under Article 122 TFEU, treating compute as strategic infrastructure equivalent to gas or semiconductor supply. The European Commission, in coordination with the European Investment Bank and national energy ministries, will fast-track three sovereign compute clusters: one co-located with the Kajaani data centre in Finland, one in the Austrian Alps with hardened grid and geothermal redundancy, and one in northern Romania with dedicated nuclear microgrid access. These will be provisioned with EU-secured supply chains: ASML has committed to prioritise EU sovereign projects for EUV tooling, and we are activating emergency procurement for domestic packaging and cooling infrastructure.

We are redirecting €14bn from the Digital Europe Programme, the Connecting Europe Facility, and national recovery funds, knowing this will deepen fiscal tension. Germany and the Netherlands are resisting the centralisation of control, but we are offering co-governance through a new European Strategic Compute Board with rotating presidency. The US and Taiwan-based foundries are warning of delays, but we are invoking the Critical Raw Materials Act and the Chips Act to secure wafer allocation. This is not about catching the frontier — it is about ensuring that when the next incident hits, we can respond on our own soil, on our own power, with our own machines. Without that, every other measure is a petition to someone else’s mercy.
