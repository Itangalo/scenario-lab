### Talent Flight Sparks Emergency Proposal  
The departure of a leading European AI research team to Canada triggers a political firestorm. In response, the European Commission proposes the Emergency Talent Retention Initiative: a €12bn fellowship programme to anchor 15 frontier teams through ten-year grants, sovereign compute access, and streamlined mobility. The initiative targets alignment, interpretability, and biosecurity researchers, with mandatory public reporting on safety findings.  

Initial outreach is planned for Max Planck, INRIA, and Amsterdam’s AI Innovation Factory. To counter accusations of centralisation, satellite nodes in less-research-intensive regions are proposed. However, member-state resistance simmers over funding allocation and governance. No new funding source is confirmed, and reallocation from the stalled Digital Europe Programme or joint borrowing remains under discussion without formal commitment. Due to the scale and complexity of the programme, full implementation will require at least two turns; only preliminary legal and funding frameworks are established in this turn.  

Because funding remains unsecured and implementation has not advanced beyond planning, the measure does not yet contribute to sovereignty or political capital.  

### Gigafactories Complete, Sovereignty Gains Realised  
The InvestAI Gigafactories programme concludes, delivering four sovereign compute sites in France and Poland. Though delayed by legal disputes, they now offer EU-controlled capacity. This completion contributes to eu_ai_sovereignty, with the notepad confirming a +5 from final delivery and +1 from ongoing portfolio momentum, offset by −1 due to capability growth, resulting in a net increase from 13.0 to 16.0.  

The Chips Act delegated act linking funding to the Geneva semiconductor servicing pool was finalised in a prior turn and is not a new measure in this period. Its effects were already accounted for in previous sovereignty calculations and are not reactivated here.
