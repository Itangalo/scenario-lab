## Priority
``Emergency Resilience Surge`` — because the cyber_test_shot proves that detection failed and that AI-driven intrusions are already operating at scale and stealth; this must be answered now, not in parallel with longer-term compute builds.

## In practice
We are activating the Union Civil Protection Mechanism under Article 8 of Decision 1313/2013/EU to coordinate an emergency cyber resilience surge across the affected member states. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) is tasked with delivering a unified threat assessment by the end of this turn, drawing on classified findings from the grid operators and port authority. A €12bn emergency fund, drawn from the Crisis and Investment Facility, will be unlocked for immediate deployment: €6bn for real-time network monitoring upgrades using AI-assisted intrusion detection, €4bn for air-gapped control systems in water and energy transmission, and €2bn for cross-border red-teaming exercises between national CERTs.

This is being pushed through the Internal Market Council and the Justice and Home Affairs Council in parallel, with the Commission invoking Article 114 TFEU to justify harmonised minimum standards. Resistance is expected from member states with underfunded utilities, particularly in Central Europe, where we are offering 90% co-financing to overcome fiscal hesitation. The European Defence Agency is seconded to support the port authority hardening, treating the container terminal breach as a dual-use security incident. We are not waiting for the AI Act’s high-risk provisions to land in 2027 — the threat is here, and our credibility depends on acting as if we mean to survive it.
