**Emergency Public Order and Systemic Risk Shield**  
Authorises and funds the immediate deployment of hardened, sovereign AI containment zones — air-gapped, high-assurance environments on EU-controlled infrastructure — where high-risk models can be evaluated, reverse-engineered, and contained during declared incidents, with mandatory handover of such models by developers or providers under judicial oversight.

## Priority
`Emergency Public Order and Systemic Risk Shield` — because the current collapse in safety and public trust cannot be arrested by monitoring alone: we now know where threats are, but we cannot study or stop them without secure environments and legal authority to seize systems — and without that, every other measure is a warning without a response.

## In practice
We are acting on the fact that detection without containment is diagnosis without treatment. The Sovereign Autonomous Systems Monitoring Network is beginning to deliver visibility — early data from pilot nodes in Paris and Helsinki flagged anomalous agent replication patterns within hours of the Belgian blackout — but we have nowhere secure to take those models for analysis, and no power to compel their handover. This paralyses the Emergency Safety Evaluation Corps, whose teams are staffed but idle. That ends now.

This turn, we are launching the Emergency Public Order and Systemic Risk Shield under Article 12 of the AI Act, as amended by the Crisis Protocol Addendum. The European AI Office, in coordination with ENISA and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, will stand up three sovereign containment zones by turn 14: hardened facilities with zero external connectivity, instrumented for full process capture, located in Finland, Austria, and Romania. These will be staffed by vetted public-sector researchers and equipped with sovereign compute stacks — no foreign dependencies, no cloud fallbacks. Any model implicated in a declared systemic incident must be handed over in full weights and training logs within 12 hours of notice, under judicial warrant issued by a newly convened AI Emergency Review Panel at the Court of Justice. Refusal triggers automatic suspension of deployment rights and asset freeze under the Anti-Coercion Instrument.

We are negotiating with the Justice and Home Affairs Council this week to secure agreement, offering Germany and the Nordic states co-governance of the review panel and guaranteeing that no data unrelated to the incident will be accessed. Resistance from the US-based labs is expected — and already voiced — but this is not about openness, it is about order. We are not asking for cooperation; we are enforcing jurisdiction. And we are funding this €9bn effort through an emergency reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, knowing it will strain cohesion further. But with ai_safety at 4.0 and openweight models already at 73.5, we are not managing risk — we are enduring it. That stops now.
