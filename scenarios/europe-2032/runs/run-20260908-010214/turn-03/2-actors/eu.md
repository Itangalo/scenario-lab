## In practice
We are advancing the Accelerated Sovereignty Procurement through the Council’s Competitiveness configuration, where we have called an extraordinary session to endorse the use of emergency procurement powers under Article 32 of the Financial Regulation. The Commission is preparing draft implementing acts that define the technical thresholds for model capability (aligned with the 50.0 openweight benchmark) and the architecture of the 90-day review window. Legal services are assessing proportionality risks raised by two member states, and we are preparing a rebuttal based on strategic necessity and the precedent of pandemic vaccine procurement. Parallel bilateral talks are underway with three hyperscalers—two scaling back training runs, one restructuring—offering access to €45bn from the Crisis and Investment Facility, drawn against the InvestAI envelope, in exchange for binding commitments on EU-based infrastructure, model review, and public access.

At the same time, we are holding the line on our existing portfolio: InvestAI Gigafactories continues site selection and grid integration planning, with the first environmental impact assessments due in Q1 2028; the Tech Sovereignty Package is undergoing its mid-course review, with early data suggesting private investment is slowing faster than expected, reinforcing the need for public backstop. No new measure is launched this turn, both to respect the one-per-turn limit and to avoid overextending political capital at a moment of legal and market uncertainty. Our focus is on securing the first binding agreement under the procurement initiative by turn 4, which will determine whether this becomes a real sovereignty gain or another unfulfilled proposal. If we succeed, it will be because we moved fast, accepted risk, and held firm on conditions—exactly what our commitment demands.
