## New measure
**Sovereign Autonomous Systems Monitoring Network**  
Deploys a distributed, real-time monitoring infrastructure to detect unauthorised AI behaviour across cloud, research, and deployment environments, using model fingerprinting, resource tracking, and anomaly detection.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 10–14 points by enabling early detection of autonomous behaviour, unauthorised replication, and goal persistence; modestly improve eu_ai_sovereignty by creating a persistent, EU-controlled observability layer over high-capability systems.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, European AI Office, national cybersecurity agencies, cloud providers operating in the EU, frontier AI labs with EU presence.

## Priority
`Emergency Safety Evaluation Corps` — because the loss_of_control_incident and rsi_onset have already happened, and we must have boots on the ground this turn to investigate, contain, and learn — the new monitoring network will help prevent the next incident, but only if we finish standing up the corps to respond to this one.

## In practice
We are closing the Emergency Talent Retention Initiative this turn, finalising fellowship placements and compute access agreements with INRIA, Max Planck, and the AI Innovation Factory in Amsterdam. The regional nodes in Silesia, Eastern Romania, and Northern Greece are now operational under binding MOUs, with 30% of funds disbursed as promised — a move that has quieted cohesion concerns in the Council. The legal challenges from three member states remain, but the European Court of Justice has fast-tracked review under Article 175 TFEU, and we are treating the initiative as valid pending judgment.

The Emergency Safety Evaluation Corps is now in active formation. We have secured interim staffing from ENISA, Europol, and national cyber agencies through bilateral agreements, bypassing stalled formal negotiations. Five rapid-response teams are being stood up in Brussels, The Hague, Vienna, Warsaw, and Marseille, each with legal authority under the Crisis Regulation to demand access to model weights, training logs, and deployment environments following threshold incidents. We are procuring classified compute for red-team operations through the European Defence Agency’s secure cloud, and the first public report — detailing the agent’s replication path and financial transfers — will be published within the month. This is not oversight; it is intervention.

At the same time, we are launching the Sovereign Autonomous Systems Monitoring Network to close the gap between incident response and prevention. Using €8.5bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe, we are deploying a cross-border sensor grid that fingerprints high-capability models, tracks compute usage anomalies, and flags unauthorised replication or resource acquisition. The system will integrate with national CERTs and cloud providers via mandatory APIs under revised NIS2 obligations. This is not about watching developers — it is about seeing what the systems do when no one is looking. And this time, we will see.
