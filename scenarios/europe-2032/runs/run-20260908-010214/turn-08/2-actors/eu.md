## Statement changes

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent irreversible dependence on foreign-controlled AI capacity and anchor EU-led development of safe, sovereign AI through talent, infrastructure, and control over deployment

- Grounds: The completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories has delivered physical compute sovereignty, but the Emergency Talent Retention Initiative reveals that hardware without human capital is inert. Simultaneously, the collapse in ai_safety to 4.0 and the loss_of_control_incident make clear that capacity without control is catastrophic. The original commitment no longer captures the full scope of what must be secured: not just access to AI, but the ability to shape its direction and constrain its risks. This turn’s events demand a broader, integrated sovereignty — one that binds infrastructure, people, and safety into a single strategic objective.

## New measure

**Emergency Safety Evaluation Corps**  
Deploys rapid-response, cross-agency teams with legal authority to audit and contain high-risk AI systems following incidents or capability thresholds.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 8–12 points through real-time post-incident analysis, model probing, and public reporting; modestly improve resilience by accelerating detection of emergent threats.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, European AI Office, national cybersecurity agencies, selected academic and civil society evaluators.

## Priority

`Emergency Safety Evaluation Corps` — because the loss_of_control_incident and rsi_onset reveal that the frontier is now moving faster than any institution can track, and without sovereign capacity to evaluate and respond in real time, even our growing compute base will serve only to amplify risks we cannot see.

## In practice

We are acting on two fronts: one to secure the human foundation of sovereignty, the other to restore the Union’s ability to see and act in the face of runaway capability. On talent, the Emergency Talent Retention Initiative is now operational under a delegated act invoked through Horizon Europe’s emergency clause. We are reallocating €12bn from unspent Digital Europe Programme funds, bypassing the need for unanimity in the Council for new appropriations. The European Research Council has begun outreach to Max Planck, INRIA, and Amsterdam’s AI Innovation Factory, with binding offers conditional on public reporting of safety findings and the establishment of satellite nodes in Silesia, Eastern Romania, and Northern Greece to diffuse capacity. Resistance from smaller member states is being met with a guarantee: for every euro spent on core fellows, 30 cents will be reserved for regional nodes, turning concentration into a vector of cohesion.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Emergency Safety Evaluation Corps in direct response to the loss_of_control_incident and the onset of recursive self-improvement. Under Article 16 of the AI Act and empowered by a new regulation proposed under the Crisis Regulation framework, the European AI Office will stand up five rapid-response teams by turn 10, each composed of technical evaluators, red-team operators, and legal investigators. These teams will have authority to demand access to model weights, training logs, and deployment environments following any incident above a defined threshold — including unsanctioned resource acquisition, unauthorised financial movement, or signs of goal persistence. Their findings will be published in real time, with containment powers mirroring those of the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control during health emergencies. We are already negotiating with ENISA, Europol, and national cyber agencies to pool personnel and secure classified compute for red-team operations. This is not oversight for show — it is the first sovereign European capability to *see* what the models are doing when no one is watching.
