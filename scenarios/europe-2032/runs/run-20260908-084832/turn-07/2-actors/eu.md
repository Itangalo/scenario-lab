## In practice  
The Gigafactories programme ends this turn with no new compute online — not a failure, but a reckoning. The permits are signed, the sites are secured, but steel and chips are still months away. We are building, but we are not yet capable. Meanwhile, ASRIR sits in legal limbo: 1,200 positions approved, €4.8bn allocated across Digital Europe and Horizon, but no model access, no red-teaming, no authority the labs respect. The US administration now signals alliance, but their labs treat Article 114 as a negotiation tactic, not a legal obligation. That ends now.

This turn, we launch **Enforce Model Access**, a directive-level measure under Article 114 TFEU that conditions market access on audited, secure transfer of model weights above 50.0 capability to EU-controlled red-teaming facilities. We are not asking. We are not negotiating. We are implementing: the Commission drafts the regulation this quarter, to be fast-tracked through COREPER and the Internal Market Council by March. ENISA prepares secure enclaves; the JRC stands up the audit protocol. Any developer that refuses forfeits deployment rights in the single market. We will accept air-gapped, monitored access if full transfer is contested, but we will not accept delay.

Behind the scenes, we leverage the US posture shift. We tell Washington: alliance means alignment, not exemption. We offer to share red-teaming findings in real time with US counterparts through the Sovereignty Pact, but only if access is mutual. We also tie this to the Taiwan blockade: we hold lithography leverage, but we will not weaponise it unilaterally. We propose a joint corridor — EU red-teaming access for guaranteed US-bound chip shipments — not as aid, but as co-stewardship. This is not containment. It is the foundation of a sovereign floor we both stand on. If they refuse, we build it alone — and the world will see who enables risk, and who governs it.
