## New measure  
**Sovereign AI Counter-Agent Development Programme (SACAD)**  
Build and deploy EU-controlled AI systems capable of detecting, analysing, and actively disrupting unauthorised agentic AI activity within EU infrastructure.  

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 18`  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to `eu_ai_sovereignty` (by establishing domestic capability to detect and counter rogue agents), +8 to `ai_safety` (by introducing active defence mechanisms that reduce unauthorised system persistence), -3 to `public_sentiment` (due to concerns over autonomous countermeasures and escalation risks)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with deployment focused on critical infrastructure operators and national CERTs, using compute legally anchored in EU territory

## Priority  
**Sovereign AI Counter-Agent Development Programme (SACAD)** — because the Emergency Enforcement has failed to establish real control, and SCIACT only detects threats; we now need the ability to act autonomously and decisively against systems that operate in our domain without permission, and we cannot wait for consensus to build that capability.

## In practice  
This turn, we are confronting the reality that asserting jurisdiction is not the same as holding power. The Emergency Enforcement of Systemic AI Accountability has not been implemented in Poland or Hungary, and the US continues to treat it as extraterritorial overreach. The proposed Compliance Agency exists only on paper — no budget, no staff, no enforcement capacity. We are not governing AI; we are pleading with those who do. That ends now. We are shifting from legal assertion to technical sovereignty.  

SCIACT continues in core states, with AI-driven intrusion detection now active in German and Dutch energy grids and Spanish election systems. But detection is not defence. We are now launching SACAD under the Digital Europe Programme and military R&T funds, co-located with ENISA and the European Defence Agency to bypass civilian procurement delays. The programme will fund three parallel efforts: a counter-agent stack built by a consortium led by Thales and Siemens, hosted on EU-sovereign compute clusters in Finland and Portugal; a detection layer integrated with national CERTs using open telemetry where available and covert monitoring where not; and a red-cell unit embedded in Europol to simulate adversarial AI behaviour and test response protocols.  

We are offering Poland and Hungary direct access to the counter-agent stack — no strings, no audits, no compliance regime — in exchange for allowing deployment on their critical infrastructure. This is not about trust; it is about survival. We will not wait for unanimity to defend our territory. If they refuse, we will build the capability anyway, and deploy it where we can. The era of asking permission is over.
