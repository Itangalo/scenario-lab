## New measure
**Rebuild Trust through Transparent AI Governance (RT-TAG)**  
Launch a public audit of EU AI governance actions since 2028, publish findings in real time, and establish citizen review panels to assess the proportionality and impact of security measures on research, jobs, and civil liberties.

`Category: 2 (Transparency and reporting)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to `public_sentiment` (by restoring transparency and accountability), +5 to `eu_political_capital` (by demonstrating responsiveness), -2 to `ai_safety` (short-term distraction from technical work)

## Priority
**Accelerate Sovereign Red-Teaming and Incident Response (ASRIR)** — because while SSCII closes the loop on infrastructure integrity, ASRIR is the only measure that directly closes the gap in our ability to anticipate and counteract AI-driven threats; its completion this turn delivers sovereign technical capacity where it matters most — in the hands of those who can test and break systems before adversaries do.

## In practice
This turn, we close out two major initiatives: the InvestAI Gigafactories and the Secure Sovereign Compute Integrity Initiative. The Gigafactories are now operational, delivering EU-anchored compute at scale. But their value is only as strong as the control we exert over them. SSCII’s forensic audits have uncovered significant vulnerabilities—shell companies, academic accounts weaponised as proxies, and weak authentication protocols. We have decommissioned three clusters pending remediation and mandated jurisdiction-locked MFA across all high-capability systems. ENISA and Europol are now running continuous monitoring, with real-time alerts feeding into a central EU Security Dashboard. Germany and France have fully complied; we are working with Belgium, Austria, and Finland to rebuild trust, offering Horizon-funded compensation for lost research time and fast-tracked access for compliant institutions.

ASRIR reaches its final phase: we are integrating red-teaming into the core of EU crisis preparedness. The first simulations—targeting energy grid AI controllers and election integrity systems—will run in November, with results shared under strict embargo with the Commission, the EEAS, and national security councils. Access to frontier models remains contested, but we are leveraging Article 114 TFEU to compel disclosure, backed by the threat of market suspension. Behind the scenes, we are brokering a deal with two US labs: model access in exchange for early warning rights and joint incident response protocols. This is not dependence—it is leverage, exercised from a position of growing strength.

But strength without legitimacy is brittle. The backlash against SSCII’s disruptions, the media framing of our actions as authoritarian, and the drop in public sentiment to 26.0 are not noise—they are signals. That is why we are launching Rebuild Trust through Transparent AI Governance. The European Data Protection Supervisor will lead an independent audit of all AI security actions since 2028, publishing findings quarterly. Citizen panels in five member states will assess proportionality and impact, with veto power over future measures that fail to meet threshold legitimacy tests. This is not a retreat from security—it is the recalibration that ensures our sovereignty survives not just external threats, but internal fracture.
