### Closing Loops, Opening Scrutiny

The EU completes two cornerstone initiatives: the InvestAI Gigafactories and the Secure Sovereign Compute Integrity Initiative (SSCII). The Gigafactories come fully online, delivering sovereign compute capacity across four sites. SSCII’s forensic audits expose deep vulnerabilities—shell companies and compromised academic accounts had leased compute to sanctioned actors. Three clusters are decommissioned, and jurisdiction-locked MFA is mandated EU-wide. ENISA and Europol now run real-time monitoring, feeding alerts into a central Security Dashboard.

ASRIR enters its final phase, with red-team simulations targeting AI-controlled energy grids and election systems scheduled for November. Legal pressure under Article 114 TFEU forces limited model access from US labs, though full cooperation remains conditional on unresolved negotiations.

Yet control gains are offset by legitimacy losses. Media outlets amplify academic grievances over research disruptions, framing SSCII as overreach. Public sentiment inches up slightly—not from approval, but from fading outrage—but remains low at 28.0. In response, the EU launches Rebuild Trust through Transparent AI Governance (RT-TAG), a large category 2 measure. An independent audit will review all AI security actions since 2028, with citizen panels in five member states assessing proportionality. Veto powers are symbolic—binding only on future proposals—but signal a shift toward accountability.

AI capability continues its steady climb to 66.5, driven by self-improving code agents in closed labs. Openweight models, adapted from leaked versions, rise to 54.5, enabling more sophisticated fraud. With no red-teaming fully operational, ai_safety drops to 17.0. Resilience erodes slightly as focus remains inward.

### Political Capital: A Fragile Rebound

Despite low capital, the EU avoids measure failure—barely. The completion of two large measures provides a much-needed boost. ASRIR’s finish delivers technical credibility, while RT-TAG’s launch signals course correction. Political capital rises to 16.0, still precarious but no longer collapsing.

Sovereignty climbs to 27.0: +5 from SSCII’s completion (category 4), −1 from capability growth, and +3 from partial progress on ASRIR’s integration. The US posture remains ALLIANCE, enabling fragile cooperation on model access.
