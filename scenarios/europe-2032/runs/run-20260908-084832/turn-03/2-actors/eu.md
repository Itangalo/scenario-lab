## New measure  
### European Red-Teaming Alliance  
We will formalise and fund the emergent lab cooperation into a standing EU-hosted red-teaming alliance, giving public evaluators shared infrastructure to stress-test frontier models independently and credibly.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by +8–12 points by enabling adversarial testing of frontier systems without relying on developer self-reporting; strengthen EU leverage in international negotiations by demonstrating credible independent evaluation capacity.  
`Applies to:` EU-funded and independent European AI laboratories, ENISA, the AI Safety Office, and designated public research institutions.

## Priority  
**Secure Access to Frontier Model Evaluations** — because without binding access to safety data from the frontier, every other effort to secure AI systems or build sovereign capacity risks being undermined by opacity and delay, and because this turn’s capability jump makes verification more urgent than ever.

## In practice  
We are advancing the Secure Access to Frontier Model Evaluations regulation through the co-legislative process, with the Commission drafting the full text under Article 114 TFEU and preparing for trilogue discussions in the Council and Parliament. Legal teams are finalising the equivalence framework to avoid unnecessary duplication with trusted partners, while maintaining enforceable standards for access. We are engaging US and UK developers through bilateral channels, offering fast-track mutual recognition for labs that join the new European Red-Teaming Alliance — a move designed to convert resistance into participation. Behind the scenes, we are preparing to invoke Digital Markets Act enforcement mechanisms as leverage, should access be denied, making clear that market access in the EU is conditional on verifiable safety.  

Simultaneously, we are responding to the emergent lab cooperation by transforming it into a formal, funded initiative: the European Red-Teaming Alliance. This will be hosted under the Joint Research Centre with ENISA oversight, and will receive €1.2bn in dedicated funding from the Horizon Europe budget to equip labs with secure testing environments, synthetic data pipelines, and adversarial agent frameworks. The goal is not to replicate private sector speed, but to establish irreplaceable public assurance — a capacity to fail frontier systems before they fail us. This effort will run in parallel to the Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package, where permitting acceleration zones are now being piloted in three member states, though grid connection delays threaten timelines. The Hardened Detection and Response measure remains on track to finish next turn, but the lack of centralised authority continues to slow adoption — we are preparing a political push in the Justice and Home Affairs Council to resolve the certification dispute before it undermines cross-border response.
