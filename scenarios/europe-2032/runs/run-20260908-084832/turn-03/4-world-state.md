### Frontier Leaps Ahead

A sudden leap in autonomous cyber operations marks a discontinuous advance at the frontier. Systems now generate and test exploits in real time, adapting to defensive changes within hours. While general problem-solving improves only modestly—up 2.0 points to 57.0—the shift in verifiable domains rattles security agencies. The open frontier lags but advances to 45.0, halfway between its prior value and the new closed peak, enabling more sophisticated misuse by non-state actors.

### EU Doubles Down on Oversight

The European Union formalises the emergent lab cooperation into the European Red-Teaming Alliance, backed by €1.2bn in Horizon funding. Hosted by the Joint Research Centre and overseen by ENISA, it aims to build independent adversarial testing capacity. However, integration with private frontier labs remains voluntary, and US developers dismiss the initiative as duplicative. The Commission pushes forward with the Secure Access to Frontier Model Evaluations regulation, naming it priority and attempting to link it to Digital Markets Act enforcement. Yet no breakthroughs emerge in bilateral talks, and threats of market restriction only deepen transatlantic friction.

### Resilience Stalls, Sentiment Sours

The Hardened Detection and Response measure limps toward completion, delayed by unresolved disputes over certification authority. With ai_capability rising and no finished resilience upgrade, critical infrastructure remains exposed. Public trust erodes further after a major grid operator detects AI-crafted probing—contained, but revealing thin defences. Media coverage frames the EU as reactive, and sentiment dips to 38.0. Labour unrest grows in tech-adjacent sectors, feeding narratives of dependency and loss of control.

### Political Capital Crumbles

The EU runs four large measures, including a named priority, draining political capital at −13 per turn. No proposal bonus is earned—the red-teaming alliance responds to a trend, not a recent shock. With public sentiment falling and no legitimacy lift, political capital plummets to 22.0. Sovereignty declines to 20.0 as capability growth outpaces domestic build-out, and no access event offsets the drift.
