- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Accelerate sovereign AI capacity and operational resilience to ensure the EU can independently govern, defend, and adapt to AI-driven threats by June 2031  
- Grounds: The two-year commitment period ends this turn, and while autonomous capacity remains the goal, the cyber_major_incident and openweight_frontier_release have exposed that capability without sovereign operational resilience is insufficient. The new formulation integrates defence and governance into the core objective, reflecting that sovereignty now requires not just access but the ability to act on it under fire.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will now anchor decisions in observable harm, not projected risk  
- Grounds: The ransomware attack on health systems has shifted the basis of action from anticipation to response. We are still acting under uncertainty, but the nature of that uncertainty has changed: we now know offensive AI can breach critical infrastructure at scale. Future commitments must be grounded in demonstrable harm to maintain legitimacy amid fragile public sentiment.

## In practice  
The health system ransomware attack was the inflection point. We can no longer rely on foreign labs to disclose vulnerabilities or on coalitions to enforce access. This turn, we finalise the **Tech sovereignty package** — the private investment target is met, and the first accelerated-permitting zones are designated in Finland, Spain, and Poland — but we are not celebrating. The real work begins now.  

We are launching **Accelerate Sovereign Red-Teaming and Incident Response** under the Joint Research Centre, with operational command co-located at ENISA. This is not an expansion of the European Red-Teaming Alliance — that was a network of external partners, and it served its purpose. This is a standing, in-house force: 1,200 full-time staff, €4.8bn from Digital Europe and Horizon Europe, with legal authority under Article 114 TFEU to compel access to model weights above 50.0 capability for adversarial simulation. We are drafting the implementing act this quarter, with a fast-track to the June Council. The US labs are resisting, but we are tying access to the single market: no red-teaming access, no deployment.  

Simultaneously, the **Sovereignty Pact** concludes — formally — this week in Singapore. We secured mutual evaluation access and joint export vetoes with Japan, South Korea, and Canada, but not with the US. The incoming administration’s posture is still unknown, and the Taiwan blockade has frozen semiconductor flows. Our upstream leverage in lithography and rare gas purification is now our most potent card. Behind closed doors, we are offering China-bound export concessions in exchange for guaranteed EU-bound shipments — not as trade, but as crisis management. The US will have to choose: integrate with our sovereign capacity, or be locked out of the only functioning red-teaming corridor in the West. We are no longer waiting. We are building the floor beneath us as we fall.
