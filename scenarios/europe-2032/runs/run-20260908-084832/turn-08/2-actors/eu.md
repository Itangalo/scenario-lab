## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: one legal, one physical. The Enforce Model Access directive remains in motion — the Commission is finalising the draft under Article 114 TFEU, and we are pushing COREPER to fast-track it through the Internal Market Council. But this turn, we have learned that legal authority means nothing if the infrastructure beneath it is compromised. The reports of shell companies leasing time on sovereign clusters are not rumours; they are a systemic breach. We are now treating this as a crisis of possession, not just policy.

We launch the Secure Sovereign Compute Integrity Initiative: ENISA, in coordination with Europol and national cybersecurity agencies, will conduct a full forensic audit of all EU-anchored compute clusters above 50.0 capability. This includes the newly completed Gigafactories. Access logs, identity verification, and supply-chain integrity will be reviewed. Any cluster found to have unauthorised access will be temporarily decommissioned until controls are restored. We are mandating multi-factor, jurisdiction-locked authentication for all users, and requiring real-time reporting of access events to a central EU monitor. The Digital Europe Programme will fund the upgrades, and Horizon will support the development of tamper-proof access protocols.

Behind the scenes, we are confronting member states that have turned a blind eye to academic accounts being weaponised as backdoors. Germany and France support the move, but smaller states fear disruption to their research pipelines. We are offering compensation through the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking for lost compute time, and fast-tracking access for compliant institutions. This is not optional: if we cannot secure our own machines, we cannot claim sovereignty, and we cannot govern AI. The black market ends here.
