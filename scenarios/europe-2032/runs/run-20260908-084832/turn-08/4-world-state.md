### A Surge in Capability, a Crack in Control

A sudden leap in AI performance—particularly in code generation and cyber operations—marks the first signs of a capability jump. While general competence rises only to 65.0, attackers now demonstrate coordinated, adaptive intrusions within days, not weeks. The frontier has shifted, and verification lags behind.

Openweight models, adapted from recently leaked systems, push `openweight_capability` to 53.0. These tools enable targeted disinformation and fraud, spreading through underground forums and illicit compute networks.

The EU responds to a deepening crisis of control. Despite the completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories, reports confirm a thriving black market: sanctioned entities and non-state actors are leasing time on sovereign clusters via shell companies and compromised academic accounts. In response, the EU launches the Secure Sovereign Compute Integrity Initiative (SSCII), a large category 4 measure aimed at forensic audits and hardened access controls across all high-capability clusters. Germany and France back the push, but smaller member states resist, fearing disruption to research. Temporary decommissioning of compromised systems fuels backlash.

ENISA and Europol begin audits, mandating jurisdiction-locked authentication and real-time monitoring. The Digital Europe Programme funds upgrades, but implementation is partial by mid-year. No full enforcement is achieved, and disruption to academic access damages public trust.

Public sentiment drops to 26.0 as media frames the crackdown as heavy-handed, coinciding with visible job losses in AI-adjacent sectors. Resilience dips as attention shifts from systemic hardening to internal security. AI safety falls sharply—by 7 points—due to the capability jump and lack of real red-teaming. The ASRIR programme stalls amid legal disputes over model access.

Political capital erodes to 11.0 under the weight of multiple measures, the new priority, and public discontent. The completion of InvestAI provides a sovereignty boost, but the black market and capability surge impose steep costs.

### Sovereignty Under Siege

The EU’s sovereignty rises to 24.0—+5 from InvestAI’s completion, −1 from capability growth, and +3 from the SSCII’s foundational work—yet the gains feel fragile. Sovereignty is no longer just about building compute, but defending it.
