### The Offensive Threshold Is Crossed

`ai_capability` reaches 60.0 as autonomous agents demonstrate end-to-end execution of multi-day cyber operations, including discovery, exploit generation, lateral movement and persistence. The closed frontier now routinely produces systems that displace junior professionals in software and intelligence analysis. Offensive cyber units in multiple states report AI-generated zero-days in active circulation, some with lifetimes measured in hours before patching.

The **openweight_frontier_release** begins diffusion: a model at 58.0 capability is released under permissive licensing, downloaded over 300,000 times in the first week. While adoption is rapid, integration into operational systems — especially by adversarial or resource-constrained actors — remains partial and uneven. `openweight_capability` rises to 49.0, reflecting early deployment and reuse in automated fraud and phishing campaigns that mimic trusted voices with moderate fidelity. The gap with the closed frontier narrows, but systemic reuse at scale is still emerging.

A **cyber_major_incident** strikes: AI-powered ransomware infiltrates public health systems in three member states, encrypting critical patient records and disrupting emergency services for 72 hours. Response teams are overwhelmed by the speed of adaptation. Forensic analysis confirms the attack tools were model-synthesised. The incident underscores a dangerous asymmetry — offence is outpacing defence.

### EU Responds, But Strain Shows

The EU advances two existing measures: **Secure Access to Frontier Model Evaluations** and **European Red-Teaming Alliance**, both introduced in prior turns. However, US and UK labs refuse to submit logs under the final agreement, rendering the evaluation framework symbolic. The red-teaming alliance becomes partially operational, but staffing and compute access limit its scope to targeted assessments.

The **Join and Shape the Emergent Sovereignty Pact** initiative becomes the EU’s priority, with high-level talks underway in Singapore. The EU offers red-teaming access and €300m in joint funding, seeking binding mutual access and shared export vetoes. The **election_alliance** outcome is leveraged to pressure American labs, but no compliance is secured this turn.

Public backlash intensifies after the health system attacks. Data centre protests grow, and trust in AI governance plummets — `public_sentiment` drops to 31.0. The “AI: Control or Collapse?” campaign shifts to demonstration, but fails to counter the perception of vulnerability.

`resilience` increases to 44.0 as the Hardened Detection and Response framework begins integration into ENISA systems. The process is ongoing, with full institutionalisation expected next turn; current gains reflect early coordination improvements and pilot deployments. However, `ai_safety` falls to 27.0 — capability advanced without corresponding safeguards. `eu_ai_sovereignty` dips to 20.0 as frontier access remains contested and talent drain persists. Political capital slips to 21.0 under portfolio strain and failed deterrence.
