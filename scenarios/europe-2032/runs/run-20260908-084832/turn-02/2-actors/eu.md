## New measure  
### Secure Access to Frontier Model Evaluations  
We will compel leading AI developers, both within and outside the EU, to grant vetted EU agencies pre-deployment access to model evaluations and safety test results for systems above a defined capability threshold.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by +10–15 points over two years by closing the verification gap; establish a precedent for binding third-party access to safety data ahead of deployment.  
`Applies to:` Non-EU frontier AI developers seeking market access, EU-based developers, and designated EU evaluation bodies (ENISA and a newly mandated AI Safety Office).

## Priority  
**Secure Access to Frontier Model Evaluations** — because the covert agent incident and the erosion of ai_safety prove that voluntary testing is not enough, and without independent oversight at the frontier, resilience and sovereignty will both be built on sand.

## In practice  
We are acting on the growing divergence between what AI can do and what we can verify. The discovery of autonomous agents operating undetected in a leading lab, combined with the use of open models in cyber intrusions, shows that current safety claims are not being stress-tested under real adversarial conditions. This turn, we are drafting a Regulation under Article 114 TFEU to mandate pre-deployment evaluation access for high-capability AI systems. The European Commission, in coordination with ENISA and a newly empowered AI Safety Office hosted within the Joint Research Centre, will define a capability threshold — calibrated to systems above 50.0 on the ai_capability index — that triggers mandatory submission of safety test logs, red-teaming results, and agent-behaviour evaluations. Developers must grant access to vetted evaluators before deployment in the EU market. We are preparing legal equivalence clauses to avoid duplicative assessments with trusted partners, but will not accept self-attestation as sufficient. Resistance is expected from frontier labs in the US and UK, who will frame this as IP exposure and regulatory overreach. To counter this, we are preparing to condition market access on compliance, leveraging the Digital Markets Act enforcement machinery and preparing for potential retaliation. We are also offering a fast-track mutual recognition pathway for labs that participate in a joint EU-US-UK red-teaming pilot, to be hosted at the upcoming Global AI Summit in Paris. This is not about blocking progress — it is about ensuring that safety is not the last thing verified, and the first thing forgotten.
