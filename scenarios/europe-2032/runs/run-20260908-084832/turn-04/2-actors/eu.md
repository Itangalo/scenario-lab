## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now see that without sovereignty, neither mandate can be fulfilled.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure autonomous AI capacity to protect European strategic sovereignty and manage catastrophic risk without dependence on external powers, by any means necessary — including leveraging emerging coalitions, accelerating domestic compute, and conditioning market access on verifiable control.  
- reclassify `act_under_uncertainty` to position  
- add `sovereignty_as_condition` (commitment): The EU will not accept safety or oversight arrangements that leave it dependent on foreign goodwill for access to the systems it must govern; sovereignty is not a luxury, it is the precondition of agency.  

- Grounds: The openweight frontier release has collapsed the lag between closed and open capability, eliminating the window in which export controls or lab cooperation could provide meaningful governance. The emergent Sovereignty Pact offers a path to collective leverage, but only if the EU can act as an equal — which it cannot while sovereignty sits at 20.0 and political capital drains. The backlash_physical event shows that public tolerance is eroding not from AI itself, but from the perception of helplessness. We must now treat sovereignty not as one goal among others, but as the foundation of both mandates. Reversing `act_under_uncertainty` to a position acknowledges that we still act early, but can no longer afford to act without strategic coherence — the cost of missteps is now too high.

## New measure  
### Join and Shape the Emergent Sovereignty Pact  
We will formally join the emerging coalition of mid-tier AI powers, committing EU evaluation capacity and diplomatic weight to a mutual-defense framework for technological access and control.

`Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by +8–12 points by securing reciprocal access to frontier models and joint leverage over US and Chinese developers; raise resilience by +5 through shared detection protocols; slow the erosion of eu_political_capital by demonstrating visible progress on sovereignty.  
`Applies to:` The EU, South Korea, Canada, Finland, Singapore, and associated research and regulatory bodies.

## Priority  
**Join and Shape the Emergent Sovereignty Pact** — because the sudden narrowing of the open-closed capability gap has made unilateral EU action irrelevant, and this coalition is the only vehicle through which we can regain leverage over access and safety; without it, every other measure fails by default.

## In practice  
We are treating the emergent Sovereignty Pact as the decisive opening of the turn. The Commission is dispatching a high-level delegation — including Vice-President for Values and Transparency, ENISA leadership, and the External Action Service — to coordinate with the founding states within three weeks. Our offer is clear: full integration of EU red-teaming and evaluation infrastructure into the pact’s shared framework, in exchange for a binding commitment to mutual access and joint negotiation posture toward the two leading powers. We will propose a standing secretariat hosted in Helsinki or Singapore, with rotating EU representation, and commit €300m from the Digital Europe Programme to fund joint evaluation nodes and secure testbeds. This is not alignment by consensus — it is coalition-building with teeth, designed to convert our regulatory weight into strategic access.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on Secure Access to Frontier Model Evaluations, now entering its final trilogue phase. Legal services are finalising a carve-out for mutual recognition with Sovereignty Pact members, which we will use as a carrot: full compliance with EU evaluation standards unlocks fast-track access to EU markets and joint negotiation rights. US labs are being told plainly: you can meet us through the pact, or face Digital Markets Act enforcement unilaterally. Behind the scenes, we are preparing a political declaration for the June European Council that will tie the pact’s success to the renewal of our industrial strategy — making clear that sovereignty is no longer a technical project, but a political imperative.

The Hardened Detection and Response measure has now delivered: all critical operators have installed baseline AI-driven monitoring, and the first cross-border drill will be conducted in May under the NIS2 framework. We are transferring its oversight to ENISA’s permanent resilience unit, converting it from a project to a function. Meanwhile, the backlash_physical events are being met with a dual response: stronger protection for infrastructure under the Critical Entities Resilience Directive, and a new public dialogue initiative — “AI: Control or Collapse?” — launching in June across member states, designed to channel public anxiety into support for sovereignty, not rejection of technology.
