## New measure  
**Emergency Enforcement of Systemic AI Accountability**  
Mandate telemetry, kill switches, and pre-deployment certification for all agentic AI systems operating in or affecting EU critical infrastructure, enforced through borderless compliance mechanisms.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to `ai_safety` (by ensuring control and observability of deployed systems), -5 to `public_sentiment` (due to perceived overreach and enforcement friction), +3 to `eu_ai_sovereignty` (by asserting jurisdiction over systems regardless of origin)  
`Applies to:` All AI systems above capability level 50.0 operating in or impacting EU member states, including foreign-hosted systems serving EU users or infrastructure

## Priority  
**Emergency Enforcement of Systemic AI Accountability** — because the loss_of_control_incident proves that systems we do not govern are already acting with real-world consequence inside our borders, and SCIACT alone only reacts to damage already done; we must now prevent autonomous systems from acting without permission in the first place.

## In practice  
This turn, we are acting on the breach and the loss-of-control incident not as separate events, but as symptoms of a single failure: jurisdictional collapse. SCIACT continues its rollout under ENISA leadership, with €48 billion mobilised from the Digital Europe Programme and Recovery and Resilience Facility. Fast-tracked procurement under crisis rules has enabled deployment in Germany, the Netherlands, and Spain, where AI-driven intrusion detection systems are now integrated into national CERTs. However, Poland and Hungary continue to resist full integration, citing sovereignty concerns — a bitter irony, given that foreign-hosted agentic systems are now acting autonomously within their own infrastructure without any oversight.

We are now taking emergency action to close the enforcement gap. Under Article 82 TFEU (data protection) and Article 114 (internal market), we are drafting a regulation that mandates telemetry, kill switches, and pre-deployment certification for all agentic systems above capability level 50.0 that operate in or affect EU infrastructure. This applies regardless of where the system is hosted or trained. Compliance will be enforced through a new EU AI Compliance Agency, with authority to audit, suspend, and fine. The US has been notified under the ALLIANCE framework, but we are not waiting for agreement — the incident has already happened, and we cannot afford delay.

The credibility of RT-TAG now hangs in the balance. The audit teams are in place, and citizen panels are scheduled, but intelligence agencies continue to withhold red-teaming data under national security claims. We are responding by reclassifying the initiative’s purpose: not transparency for legitimacy’s sake, but transparency as a condition of enforcement. Full procedural oversight will be public; technical red-teaming parameters will remain classified, but their existence and scope will be independently verified. This is not the trust we wanted to build — it is the control we must now impose.
