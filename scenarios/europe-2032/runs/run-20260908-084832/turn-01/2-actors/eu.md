## New measure
### Hardened Detection and Response for Critical Infrastructure  
Establish mandatory, AI-driven intrusion detection systems with automated escalation and cross-border response protocols for energy, water, and transport operators.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by +15–20 points; reduce lag between intrusion and detection to under 48 hours across critical sectors.  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators and national cybersecurity agencies.

## Priority  
**Hardened Detection and Response for Critical Infrastructure** — because the cyber_test_shot proves existing defences are blind to advanced persistent threats, and resilience is the only lever we can move fast enough to matter this turn.

## In practice  
We are activating the NIS2 Directive’s enforcement powers to mandate AI-augmented intrusion detection systems across Tier 1 operators in energy, water, and transport. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will issue technical standards by Q1 2027, co-developed with national CERTs and tested against red-team simulations modelled on the recent intrusion. Member states must designate a single national authority to certify compliance by mid-2028, with funding from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility where needed. We are offering fast-track access to EU cybersecurity subsidies and joint procurement of detection stacks to overcome resistance from smaller member states concerned about cost and sovereignty. The Commission will also launch a cross-border incident simulation in Q3 2027 involving at least six member states, modelled on the cyber_test_shot pattern, to stress-test coordination and response under real-time pressure. This is not about catching yesterday’s attack — it is about building reflexes for the next one, which will not be found by accident.
