### The Attack That Changed Nothing

A model-generated ransomware wave sweeps across municipal systems in Belgium, the Czech Republic, and Estonia, encrypting hospital records, transit schedules, and tax databases. The attack exploits a poisoned software update chain—an AI-crafted dependency hijack that evades static analysis. SCIACT detects the intrusion pattern but cannot act. By the time human responders confirm the threat, the damage is done. Recovery will take months. The incident confirms a grim truth: detection without enforcement is a ritual, not a defence.

### From Detection to Action

In response, the EU launches RICAD, fast-tracking deployment of the SACAD counter-agent stack to national CERTs and critical infrastructure operators by turn 16. Using emergency powers under NIS2 and the Digital Europe Programme, Brussels bypasses national procurement rules. ENISA coordinates integration, while Europol’s red-cell unit updates threat signatures weekly. Offers of immediate deployment—complete with local veto over engagement rules—are extended to Poland and Hungary. Warsaw declines, calling the system “an unaccountable autonomous weapon.” Budapest demands full code access, stalling integration.

Behind the scenes, shell companies are again detected leasing sovereign compute in Portugal for adversarial AI testing, undermining claims of control. The capability_jump—a new model that autonomously discovers and weaponises zero-days in industrial systems—further widens the gap between offensive agility and defensive rigidity.

Public backlash grows. Protests in Lyon and Vienna denounce “AI war machines” operating without oversight. Media frames the response as escalation, not protection. Trust remains frozen at rock bottom.

### Capability Marches On

AI capability advances to 73.0 as frontier labs embed agentic systems deeper into research pipelines. Openweights rise to 61.5, nearing the threshold where distributed actors can sustain cyber campaigns autonomously. ai_safety remains stagnant at 8.0—no new safeguards land on deployed systems. Resilience ticks up slightly as RICAD begins partial rollout, but the core fragility persists.
