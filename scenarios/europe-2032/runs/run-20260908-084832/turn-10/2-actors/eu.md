Deploy AI-powered intrusion detection and autonomous countermeasures across energy, water, and election systems, with human-in-the-loop escalation protocols and shared threat intelligence across member states.

## Priority  
**Secure Critical Infrastructure Against Autonomous Cyber Threats (SCIACT)** — because the cyber_major_incident this turn confirms that our defences are no longer catching up: AI-generated offensive tooling has outpaced human-led response, and without automated detection and containment, even sovereign red-teaming will come too late.

## In practice  
This turn, we confront the consequences of a major AI-generated cyberattack — a self-propagating ransomware sweep targeting public health and transport systems across three member states. Initial analysis confirms the tooling was developed using adapted openweight models at capability level 54.5, and deployed through a compromised software dependency with cascading effects. The incident was not novel in form, but in speed and scale: it evaded legacy detection, encrypted backup systems in under 18 minutes, and disrupted emergency services. Attribution remains unclear, but the attack vector is now known, and we cannot assume it will be used only once.

We are launching SCIACT under Article 172 TFEU, mobilising €48 billion from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme. ENISA will lead deployment of AI-driven intrusion detection systems trained on red-team data from ASRIR, now operational. These will be integrated into national CERTs with autonomous countermeasure protocols — including traffic isolation, patch deployment, and cryptographic rollback — all under human-in-the-loop oversight. We are fast-tracking procurement rules via the Crisis Response Protocol to bypass standard tenders, but requiring that all vendors commit to open auditing of their models’ decision logic. Germany, the Netherlands, and Spain have already signed on; Poland and Hungary are resisting, citing cost and sovereignty concerns. We are offering co-location of compute and partial cost absorption through cohesion funds to secure their buy-in.

Meanwhile, RT-TAG moves into implementation. The European Data Protection Supervisor has appointed the audit team, and citizen panels will convene in Paris, Berlin, Rome, Stockholm, and Lisbon by April. Early pushback from national intelligence agencies over disclosure of red-teaming parameters is being managed through a tiered classification framework — full technical details remain protected, but procedural legitimacy and oversight mechanisms will be public. This is not transparency at the expense of security, but transparency as a condition of it: we cannot defend what the public no longer trusts us to govern.
