## In practice
This turn, we are acting on the raw nerve exposed by the US access denial. The loss of model access for EU hospitals and research agencies was not merely inconvenient—it revealed a systemic vulnerability indistinguishable from strategic surrender. We are therefore launching the Emergency Access Guarantee for Sovereign Stacks, a binding instrument under Article 122 TFEU and the NIS2 Directive, which guarantees that in any future capability denial event—whether geopolitical, commercial, or technical—EU public agencies will have immediate, automated failover to sovereign-run AI systems. These systems will be provisioned on hardware already being deployed under the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative, with pre-staged models fine-tuned for medical diagnostics, energy load forecasting, and crisis response coordination. The guarantee will be operationalised through EuroHPC and the CEA Joint Undertaking, with ENISA certifying readiness and member states designating sovereign nodes as primary fallback points. We are mandating that all critical operators integrate failover triggers into their AI contracts by end-2029, with penalties under the Digital Markets Act for non-compliance.

At the same time, we are pressing forward with the Sovereign Talent Anchoring Programme, now finalising housing agreements in Paris, Munich, and Utrecht to enable rapid relocation of recruits. The Commission has secured agreement from France and Germany to treat these hires as diplomatic-level postings for visa purposes, enabling spousal work rights and school placement within two weeks. These engineers will not only staff the compute but define its boundaries: each sovereign node will require an anchored engineer with real-time audit authority and kill-switch powers over unauthorised agent behaviours. This is how we close the loop between hardware, control, and accountability. Meanwhile, the Tech sovereignty package has formally concluded—its permitting reforms adopted, its private investment targets set—but we are watching closely as Poland and Hungary signal resistance to linking future disbursements to the Emergency Access Guarantee. We are offering bilateral co-development of medical AI modules in exchange for support, betting that shared ownership can overcome obstruction. The ground keeps moving, but we are no longer building for capability alone—we are building for survival.
