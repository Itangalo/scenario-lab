## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation** — because without sovereign access to frontier-scale compute, the EU cannot evaluate, control, or defend against the systems shaping its future, and because the denial of frontier access this turn proves that dependency is not a delay—it is a termination of agency.

## In practice  
We act under Article 122 TFEU and the newly invoked emergency clause in the Critical Entities Resilience Directive. The Commission, in coordination with DG CONNECT and the European Defence Agency, issues binding directives to member states to identify and register all computing clusters above 10^18 FLOP/s within their jurisdiction—civilian, academic, industrial, or military. National governments must report within 30 days; non-compliance triggers the activation of the Union’s enforcement mechanism under Article 7(1) TEU, with financial penalties and temporary reallocation of cohesion funds. We are not buying; we are mobilising. The legal basis is crisis: cross-border health disruption, strategic disempowerment, and the effective severing of EU access to the systems running its hospitals and ministries.

The core of the system will be formed from three sources: idle defence and meteorological supercomputers, underutilised corporate training clusters, and academic facilities funded partially by Horizon Europe. These will be networked under a new entity—Sovereign AI Infrastructure Union (SAIU)—hosted jointly by EuroHPC and ENISA, with operational control vested in a crisis cabinet of technical directors appointed by the Commission. Security protocols will be state-actor grade from day one, with air-gapped evaluation environments for red-teaming and interpretability research. We will not wait for consensus on safety standards—we will impose them as conditions of access. Foreign developers who want to operate in the EU must run shadow evaluations on SAIU hardware, with weights subject to runtime monitoring and memory forensics.

Resistance will be fierce. Google, Meta, and even domestic champions like SAP resist ceding control. Germany fears militarisation. Poland and Hungary will cite sovereignty—ironically, as they block our access to response tools. We offer compensation: priority access to the network for compliant actors, shared gains from EU-developed improvements, and a seat on the SAIU governance board for every contributing entity. But this is not a negotiation. The moment frontier access was cut, the rules changed. We are no longer integrating markets. We are securing survival. If we do not claim the machines that think for us, no one will do it for us.
