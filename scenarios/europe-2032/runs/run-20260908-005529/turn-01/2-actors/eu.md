## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators** — because the cyber_test_shot proves that existing defences are blind to advanced AI-driven intrusions, and resilience cannot wait for sovereignty to land.

## In practice
We are responding to the cyber_test_shot by activating the NIS2 Directive’s latent enforcement powers, but going further: this turn, the Commission will draft a binding Implementing Act under Article 21, setting baseline detection and segmentation requirements for all operators of essential services in energy, water, and major port infrastructure. The baseline draws from ENISA’s 2025 threat modelling, now updated to include AI-coordinated intrusion patterns. We are instructing ENISA to publish technical standards by Q1 2027, with national regulators required to verify compliance within 18 months. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, repurposing €12bn in unused cohesion funds, with co-financing from national budgets at 50%.

Resistance is expected from several member states — particularly those with underfunded digital agencies and legacy industrial control systems — and from operators who argue that AI-level threats require AI-level defences, which they are not permitted to deploy under current rules. We are offering access to a new EU-wide SOC-as-a-Service pilot, hosted in Luxembourg and powered by a sovereign cloud stack under development, to reduce the burden on smaller operators. We are also fast-tracking limited access to high-capability models for defensive cyber-agents under strict audit, through a new derogation in the AI Act’s high-risk framework, to be negotiated in COREPER this December. This is not about catching up — it is about ensuring that when the next intrusion comes, it does not find the same open doors.
