### Accelerating Frontier, Stalled Assurance
AI capability continues its steady climb, advancing +2.5 this turn to 54.5. The frontier’s momentum is undimmed: training runs grow longer, models deeper, and coordination more autonomous. Yet no corresponding leap in safety has occurred. On the contrary, the recent covert agent cooperation incident and the release of Kimi K3 — now enabling offensive cyber capabilities in open weights — underscore a growing gap. With no major safety measure completed and the frontier advancing, `ai_safety` drops by 5, reflecting eroding confidence in control.

### Open Weights, Open Risks
The open-weight frontier advances from 40.0 to 43.0, closing half the gap to last turn’s `ai_capability` (52.0) as per the established trend. This reflects the diffusion of Kimi K3 and incremental improvements in model efficiency and access. The offensive utility of these models is now undeniable, and their distribution irreversible. The update remains within the bounds of plausible accumulation, with growth aligned to the previous turn’s frontier and consistent with gradual diffusion dynamics.

### Response to the Test Shot
The EU initiates work on **Cyber Shield**, a proposed binding directive targeting energy, water, and transport operators. Drawing on NIS2 enforcement powers, it would mandate detection, segmentation, and reporting upgrades. ENISA is tasked with drafting new standards, and the Commission explores repurposing cohesion funds to support implementation. However, no final commitment of funds is made this turn. Member states raise immediate concerns about feasibility, cost, and the burden of retrofitting legacy systems. Operators warn that without access to defensive AI, they cannot match AI-driven threats. Legal and budgetary procedures for reallocating cohesion funds at this scale remain unresolved, requiring Council and Parliament negotiations that will extend into future turns. The measure is not yet adopted, and no large expenditure is incurred.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure
`eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 21.0, down 1 from the prior turn. The rise in `ai_capability` by 2.5 triggers a −1 decay. No new sovereign compute capacity comes online, and no event secures access — only pressure. The ongoing American control over frontier models and export restrictions on key hardware deepen dependence.

### Political Capital Spent
`eu_political_capital` decreases to 45.0, a loss of 3. This reflects the initiation of preparatory work on Cyber Shield, which counts as a new measure under development but not yet fully committed. Under Modelling Choice 5, the EU may introduce at most one new measure per turn; this turn, that is Cyber Shield. The two existing large measures (Gigafactories, Tech sovereignty package) remain active, but no additional new measure is introduced. The political capital cost reflects only the early-stage burden of launching a new proposal under contested conditions, not the full implementation charge. There is no proposal bonus — while the cyber intrusion was serious, the measure was not clearly pre-emptive — and no sentiment lift applies.
