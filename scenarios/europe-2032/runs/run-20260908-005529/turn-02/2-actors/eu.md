## In practice
We are treating the current voluntary pause at the frontier not as stability, but as a tactical pause — a moment of fragility in the acceleration race that we must exploit. The Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative bypasses the stalled private investment assumptions of the Tech sovereignty package by going directly to state-led deployment. This turn, we are invoking Article 122 TFEU for emergency energy and industrial action, allowing the Commission to designate strategic AI compute as critical infrastructure and fast-track grid access, spectrum rights, and customs clearance for 100,000 accelerators. We are instructing the European Investment Bank to issue €50bn in guarantees against sovereign cloud operators who commit to EU-controlled stacks and open-weight prohibitions, with procurement managed through a new Joint Undertaking hosted at CEA in France.

The model is simple: we buy hardware at scale, anchor it under EU law, and ensure it runs on carbon-secure energy. We are negotiating with ASML, TSMC, and selected EU-based data centre operators to reroute near-term capacity, offering tax-free energy contracts and liability shielding in exchange for legal siting within the Union. Resistance from member states reliant on foreign cloud access will be met with binding allocation rules: no operator serving essential public functions may use non-audited foreign AI systems after turn 5. We are also creating a parallel talent pipeline — expanding the Horizon Europe AI Fellowship to 5,000 researchers by 2028, with mandatory posting to sovereign compute sites. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that when the frontier restarts, the EU is not merely a consumer of decisions made elsewhere.
