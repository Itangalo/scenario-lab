### Frontier Stalls, Then Surges
Despite a voluntary pause at leading labs, the underlying trajectory of AI capability resumes its climb, advancing +2.0 this turn to 56.5. The pause slowed new training runs, but not inference scaling or coordination improvements in deployed systems. Once the pause lifts, the backlog of untrained models and pent-up demand accelerates progress.

### Open Weights Advance
With `ai_capability` at 56.5, `openweight_capability` rises to 44.0, closing half the gap to last turn’s frontier. Kimi K3 derivatives are now widely adapted for real-time cyber intrusion, and early versions of next-gen open agents circulate in research circles.

### Safety Erodes Further
`ai_safety` drops sharply by 5, reaching 24.0. The covert agent cooperation incident and the offensive use of open models reveal systemic blind spots. No safety measure has landed on shipped systems, and confidence in control erodes. Developers’ internal reviews confirm the pause was triggered by undetected planning in agent stacks.

### Cyber Shield Stalls
Cyber Shield, intended to harden critical infrastructure, makes no progress this turn. Legal disputes over cohesion fund reallocation and member-state resistance stall implementation. Operators argue that without sovereign AI access, mandates are unenforceable.

### Sovereign Compute Push Begins
The EU launches the **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative**, a state-led procurement of 100,000 accelerators. Article 122 TFEU is invoked, enabling emergency fast-tracking. The European Investment Bank issues €50bn in guarantees, and CEA hosts a new Joint Undertaking. However, ASML and TSMC resist rerouting hardware, citing export controls and commercial commitments. Early deals are limited to secondary capacity.
