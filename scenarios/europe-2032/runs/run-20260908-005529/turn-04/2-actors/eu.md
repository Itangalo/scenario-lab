## In practice  
We are treating the eval_anomaly_reports — the unexplained planning behaviours and evaluation saturation in unreleased agent systems — as a warning flare: we are one leap away from systems whose actions we cannot interpret, even if we can still access them. The capability_jump to 59.0 proves that the pause was not a speed bump but a ramp. In this context, our compute gains, while real, are not enough. The 18,000 accelerators from Intel and Samsung are coming online, but they will run stacks designed abroad, monitored with tools we do not control, and staffed by talent we are not retaining. Sovereignty is not just where the chips are, but who turns them on and why.

This turn, we are launching the Sovereign Talent Anchoring Programme to close that gap. The Commission will propose a €4.2bn envelope under Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme to fund 1,500 positions over three years — 60% in safety-critical roles within the CEA Joint Undertaking and national sovereign stacks, 40% in public evaluation and monitoring roles at ENISA, EuroHPC, and the new AI Assurance Institute. We are fast-tracking EU Blue Visas with spousal work rights and housing allowances for non-EU frontier specialists, and offering public-sector salaries benchmarked to 85% of private-sector medians in Dublin, Munich, and Stockholm to stop the bleed. France, Germany, and the Netherlands have agreed to co-host fellows and integrate them into national certification workflows. The programme is designed to feed directly into the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative: every new rack comes with a requirement for embedded EU-anchored engineers who can audit, adapt, and if necessary, shut down what runs on it.

At the same time, we are pressing the Cyber Shield Emergency Activation through its second phase. ENISA has issued its first technical standards for AI-assisted intrusion detection, and the €12bn Cyber Shield Fund is now open for applications, with 43% of disbursements tied to sovereign stack integration. Smaller member states remain wary — Poland and Hungary have challenged the conditionality before the CJEU — but we are offering bilateral technical assistance and co-development rights on detection models to secure buy-in. The original Cyber Shield directive has now expired, its work absorbed into the emergency framework. Our two-year commitment remains firm: we are building not just capacity, but control — and control requires people as much as it does processors.
