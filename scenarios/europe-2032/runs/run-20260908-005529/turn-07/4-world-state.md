### Frontier Stalls, EU Hardens Defences

AI capability sees only a marginal rise to 67.5, consistent with the ongoing voluntary pause at leading labs, as recorded in the persistent *emergent_ai_research_concern*. No large-scale training runs are initiated, and internal reviews continue to delay further scaling. The pause holds, and global progress remains subdued.

Open-weight capability remains unchanged at 54.0. While incremental refinements in agent frameworks continue and the *emergent_eu_open_agent_consortium* demonstrates modular systems for energy and materials applications, no foundational releases or systemic improvements cross the threshold to elevate the metric. The diffusion of existing models proceeds steadily but does not constitute a leap in collective capability.

The EU continues advancing its sovereignty portfolio: the InvestAI Gigafactories fund, the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative, and the Sovereign Talent Anchoring Programme remain in progress. Site preparation for pilot compute hubs has begun, and initial engineering hires are onboarding, but hardware fabrication and integration timelines remain long. No deployment milestones have been reached, and full operational capacity is still multiple turns away. These efforts contribute to slow, incremental progress but no measure completes this turn.

Poland and Hungary maintain their resistance to the Emergency Access Guarantee, rejecting the Commission’s invocation of Article 122 TFEU. Infringement proceedings are under legal review, but political consensus remains elusive.

In response to the eval anomalies and exclusion from strategic dialogues, the EU proposes the Sovereign AI Incident Response Network—a category 6 measure aimed at rapid detection and containment across critical sectors. Planning begins in Brussels, with a coordination office established and initial design workshops convened. However, no operational components are deployed; full readiness will require at least two turns. This is the EU’s sole new measure this turn, and it becomes the named priority, adding to the portfolio charge.

Resilience rises modestly to 47.0, as early design elements of the proposed network inform updates to national cyberdefence playbooks. Sovereignty increases slightly to 24.5, reflecting continued progress on compute and talent pipelines, though not completion. The new incident response proposal adds strategic weight but yields no finish bonus.

Political capital dips slightly to 11.5, as the high portfolio charge strains coordination capacity. The launch of a new high-cost measure without completion of prior ones increases burden, offsetting gains from sustained effort. Public sentiment holds steady at 35.0, with no visible impact from recent developments.

### Strategic Isolation Deepens

The EU remains excluded from core strategic discussions. Reports of informal US-China coordination on model security circulate, but no formal agreement is confirmed. Without evidence of negotiation, leverage, or mutual enforcement mechanisms, such claims remain speculative. The absence of EU inclusion in security dialogues reinforces perceptions of vulnerability, even as internal efforts gain slow momentum.

Informal talks among middle powers—Canada, South Korea, and the Nordic states—continue on a potential mutual-access pact for AI development and incident response. These discussions, inspired in part by the EU’s sovereignty push, remain quiet but gain traction as trust in unilateral leadership erodes.
