### The cutoff
The message from the American provider was short: access to the leading model would end for Union users within days. No reason, no appeal channel. In university hospitals, in ministry helpdesks, in logistics firms that had wired the model into triage, billing and dispatch, screens went to error codes.

Brussels protested through diplomatic channels while telling operators to assume it would not be reversed. The joint evaluation arrangement stayed on paper, but interim researcher access narrowed to what ran under American rules.

### Fallback
The response was an inventory exercise turned into a rebuild. Health, interior and competitiveness ministers ordered large hospitals, grid security centres and ministries to list what had depended on the cut-off system, then re-host it on Union clusters near the two factory sites and on shared supercomputing capacity, using openly available models tuned in Europe and, where possible, secondary allied systems.

Engineers managed partial continuity. Triage assistants and back-office copilots returned in degraded form under European data controls. Where no model could substitute, wards and grid operators fell back to manual drills rehearsed under the earlier wage-bridge routines. Small utilities, already short of staff to feed the shared cyber watch floor, needed full onboarding paid from Brussels.

The two factory shells kept rising, but they could not yet carry the load shifted onto them. Queues lengthened, error rates rose, and mayors facing layoffs in clerical and customer roles told voters that bridges and stopgap models had arrived before any new fleet.

### Politics of dependence
Coverage treated the cutoff as humiliation as much as disruption. Opposition parties asked why critical services had been built on a switch someone else could throw. Unions asked whether the fallback would entrench a second-rate system.

The cyber-bio sharing pact proved its worth in the same months: pooled telemetry and binding sample exchange gave early warning on two intrusion waves and a contested pathogen signal, and hospitals credited the shared watch floor with preventing wider outages. That success steadied the Commission, even as public mood toward AI hardened sharply.
