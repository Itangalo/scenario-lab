### The jump and the machines
Winter ended with two announcements weeks apart. An American lab demonstrated a system that planned, coded and ran lab instruments in one loop, collapsing benchmarks that had stood for a year. Almost simultaneously, Chinese and American firms moved humanoid robots from pilots to catalogue: warehouse, digging and clearance models with open order books.

In Europe the second shock landed harder. Mayors who had just fought over chlorination playbooks watched videos of machines doing municipal work, then learned the supply chain beneath them was largely Chinese and the control software American. The two narrowed Gigafactory sites kept their grid priority, but Taiwan allocations stayed frozen and accelerator prices spiked again.

### Shield pays, package lands
Two long builds finally closed. The Tech Sovereignty Package's permitting zones and private capital pledges became operating paperwork, and the Bio-Shield's sequencing network, stockpiles and rationing rules were now routine in large hospitals. Grids and big cities rode out spring probes and a late flu resurgence without cascading failure.

Small towns did not feel rescued. Manual-fallback drills and wage bridges under the Municipal Pact arrived in some places, missed in others. The EIB backstop kept one reinsurer in small-utility cover; another left anyway, citing unmodellable cyber-bio overlap. Local press ran boil-notice anniversaries alongside robot catalogues.

### Washington opens a door
The new American administration took office promising structured allied access on published terms, joint evaluation and aligned export controls. Brussels sent negotiators to lock in researcher access and evaluation protocols, explicitly tying any deal to keeping domestic factory construction alive.

Talks started well and leaked slowly. Washington offered tiers and incident reporting; Brussels asked for guarantees that access could not be withdrawn mid-crisis. No text was signed by June. At home, relief that access might ease competed with fear that urgency to build would drain away, and opposition parties used both.
