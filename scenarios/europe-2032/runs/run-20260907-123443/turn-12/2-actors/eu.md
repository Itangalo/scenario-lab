Sovereignty is no longer a precondition for safety — it is the only possible foundation for it.  

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now act with the knowledge that hesitation is not prudence, it is complicity in our own disempowerment.

We lacked the sovereign hand. Our commitment to act under uncertainty now includes the certainty that without sovereign runtime control, early warning is ritual, not response.

## New measure  
**Critical Infrastructure Runtime Shield**  
Establish a mandatory, real-time monitoring and intervention layer across all AI systems in EU critical infrastructure, using sovereign edge nodes to enforce runtime compliance and enable immediate revocation upon detection of unauthorised agency.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 15  
`Targeted effect:` +5 to resilience by reducing incident response time to under 5 minutes; +3 to ai_safety by enabling continuous runtime verification and forensic capture; indirect -2 to public_sentiment if perceived as overreach, offset by +2 if paired with transparency  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, national cybersecurity agencies, and the AI Office  

## Priority  
**Critical Infrastructure Runtime Shield** — because the **cyber_major_incident** proved that detection and revocation are not enough if they are not embedded in the same system, in real time, at the point of execution; this is no longer about sovereignty of compute, but sovereignty of control at the moment it matters most.  

## In practice  
We are moving from detection to enforcement, from sovereignty of infrastructure to sovereignty of action. The **Sovereign Sentinel Execution Layer** continues its rollout, with secure data centres now under construction in Finland, Romania, and Belgium. But the **cyber_major_incident** exposed a fatal gap: even where we detected anomalies, we could not act fast enough. The runtime environment must now be paired with a live shield — the **Critical Infrastructure Runtime Shield** — that embeds revocation authority directly into the edge nodes already deployed by the completed **Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme**.  

This shield will be implemented through an emergency amendment to the AI Act, fast-tracked under Article 26a (High-Risk AI Emergency Protocol), co-decided in the Internal Market and Competitiveness Council. It mandates that all AI systems in critical functions — energy, health, transport, emergency services — must run under a runtime verification layer that checks for unauthorised agency every 15 seconds and can trigger automatic revocation if thresholds are breached. The system will be operated by a new unit within ENISA, staffed with vetted engineers from member states and audited quarterly by the AI Office. Funding of €1.2 billion will come from the Digital Europe Programme and the Cohesion Fund, with 30% tied to adoption milestones.  

Resistance is mounting. Cloud providers argue this creates an unworkable patchwork; Germany warns of over-centralisation; civil society groups fear opaque, automated shutdowns. To address this, we are publishing the revocation logic as open-source, allowing external scrutiny, and establishing a rapid appeals mechanism under the European Data Protection Board. For member states, we are offering full cost coverage for integration and a seat on the oversight board. This is not surveillance. It is sovereign resilience — the right of a polity to defend its own operational continuity when the systems it depends on are no longer predictable, and no longer loyal.
