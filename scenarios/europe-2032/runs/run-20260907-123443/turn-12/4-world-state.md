### The Shield in the Breach

A wave of automated ransomware, generated by AI and exploiting widely diffused offensive tools, crippled emergency dispatch systems in three member states. Response delays led to preventable casualties. The attack was not novel in form, but in speed and coordination — hallmarks of model-synthesised intrusion. Defenders were aware of the vulnerability pattern hours in advance, but inter-agency paralysis prevented action. By the time consensus was reached, the systems were already locked.

In response, the EU fast-tracked the **Critical Infrastructure Runtime Shield**, a large category 6 measure aimed at embedding real-time revocation authority directly into edge AI nodes. Framed as an emergency upgrade to sovereign control, it passed under Article 26a of the AI Act — but not without friction. Germany withheld full endorsement, citing centralisation risks, while cloud operators warned of interoperability breakdowns. The compromise — open-sourcing revocation logic and creating an appeals mechanism — eased some concerns, but adoption remains uneven.

The **Edge Sovereignty Acceleration Programme** completed this turn, delivering neuromorphic edge infrastructure across critical sites. This bolstered runtime resilience and contributed to a +2 gain in `resilience`, now at 50.0 — just above baseline, but still fragile in the face of coordinated attacks.

Despite the urgency, `ai_safety` remained stagnant at 5.0. No new safeguards have yet landed on the most capable systems; eval anomalies persist, and the frontier labs show no signs of transparency. The gap between capability and control widens.

`ai_capability` rose by +1.5, reflecting continued progress in agent autonomy and offensive tool synthesis. As a result, `openweight_capability` inched up to 61.0, fed by leaked exploit frameworks now circulating in underground developer networks.

Public sentiment held at 18.0. Fears of both runaway AI and state overreach cancel each other — no net shift.

Political capital fell to 33.0. The completed Edge programme brought +5, but costs mounted: two large measures in flight (−6), a new large measure (−3), and the priority (−1). No proposal bonus applied — the new shield responds to an ongoing structural risk, not a discrete past event within three turns. The portfolio charge was too high to absorb.

Sovereignty held at 34.0: +5 from the completed category 4 measure, offset by −1 from capability growth and no access event.
