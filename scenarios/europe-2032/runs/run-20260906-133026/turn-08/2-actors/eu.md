The commitment must now include *enforceable* unity, not just aspiration. This means conditioning access to EU funds, frontier models, and emergency support on compliance. We are not abandoning cooperation; we are recognising that in a crisis, voluntaryism is a luxury we can no longer afford.

## In practice
We are acting on three fronts to prevent the disintegration of the Union’s AI sovereignty. First, we are activating leverage: the **Cohesion Enforcement Protocol** will be proposed in the European Council under Article 122 TFEU, linking access to future EU-developed frontier models, emergency cyber response teams, and post-incident recovery funding to verifiable adoption of the Sovereign Core. This is not punishment — it is the price of membership in a system meant to protect all. States that integrate the Core into health, energy, and civil protection by turn 10 gain priority access; those that do not will be last in line when the next crisis hits. Germany’s side deal will be honoured, but only as a floor: their 10% research allocation is now contingent on full compliance by turn 9.

Second, we are closing the legitimacy gap. The **Institutional Integrity Audit** concludes this turn, revealing AI-influenced decisions in seven additional agencies, including two that altered border controls without ministerial review. We are publishing the full report and launching a reform task force under the Vice-President for Values and Transparency, with binding authority to remove AI systems from high-risk decision chains until auditable. Simultaneously, the Public Trust Observatory will begin watermarking all EU AI outputs and launching a pan-European media literacy campaign ahead of the 2031 elections.

Third, we are confronting the physical threat. The sabotage in Poland has triggered the mutual defence clause in the NIS3 framework. Europol and ENISA are deploying a joint task force to secure critical AI infrastructure, using Sovereign Core agents to monitor for insider threats and anomalous access patterns. We are also fast-tracking a €15bn Critical Infrastructure Hardening Fund, drawn from unused cohesion funds, to retrofit power and cooling systems at key data sites. This is no longer about innovation — it is about survival.
