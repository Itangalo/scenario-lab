### A Fractured Response

The EU scrambles to salvage the Sovereign Core Initiative after a member state signs a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler to restore AI-powered diagnostics in its hospitals, citing patient safety. The move fractures the Union’s unified stance, exposing deep divisions over sovereignty versus pragmatism. Simultaneously, physical attacks on data centre infrastructure — including a sabotage that injured workers — signal rising public hostility, no longer confined to protests.

A widespread ransomware attack, powered by a fine-tuned open-weight model, cripples regional hospitals in three countries. The newly operational Hardened Cyber Shield proves patchy: legacy systems in healthcare remain vulnerable, and response teams rely on ad hoc tools. The EU activates NIS3 emergency protocols, deploying forensic units to trace the attack, but the damage reinforces fears that open models at 57.0 capability are now weaponisable by non-state actors.

In response, the Commission fast-tracks the Union-Wide Sovereign Core Adoption Directive, offering incentives and conditional access to future AI assets for early adopters. A side deal with Germany secures partial buy-in, but legal challenges over data licensing and national sovereignty delay implementation. The measure is ambitious, but cohesion is fraying.

Public trust erodes further. The Institutional Integrity Audit reveals AI-influenced decisions in migration enforcement, while the Public Trust Observatory struggles to counter disinformation. Despite efforts, sentiment dips as dependency, disruption, and defiance converge.

### Sovereignty in Retreat

Technical progress continues — AI capability rises to 73.0 — but the gap with safety widens. No meaningful safeguards land on frontier systems, and the leaked anomaly report’s implications go unaddressed. The American posture remains retrenchment, slowing global progress slightly, but Europe gains no advantage.

The Union’s political capital, already critically low, declines further under the weight of five large measures in flight. The Sovereign Core Initiative, now the named priority, fails to unify, and the cost of action exceeds the capacity to act.
