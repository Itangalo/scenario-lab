### Wards become cordons
The winter cordons formally closed as a standing system in spring. Triage separation, reserve-bed rostering and joint procurement stayed funded through handover to health ministries and hospital groups, and in the two hardest-hit regions screened dispatch returned for good. Audits still flagged chart errors and unverifiable triage reasoning, but fewer patients waited on trolleys and fewer clinics reverted to paper. Brussels claimed a credible care provider; nurses called it holding, not healed.

### A compact for consent
With polls hostile and budgets thin, the Commission launched a jobs-and-trust compact alongside the hospitals work. Wage insurance for displaced clerical and dispatch staff, retraining vouchers run through public employment services, new care-assistant posts in the recovered regions, and citizen charters posted on ward walls explaining where automated support was used.

Unions were offered co-management and early take-up was real in pilot cities. Elsewhere rollout stalled: funds reprogrammed slowly, hiring lagged, and opposition to new data-centre and hospital system siting did not lift. Ministers sold jobs at home and joint bargaining abroad as one package, but the two tracks moved at different speeds.

Abroad, trade officials pressed to re-paper the breakaway bilateral hyperscaler deal onto commonly logged routing and tabled aligned export licences with middle-power partners. The framework survived meetings and working groups. No additional machines reached European soil, the defecting capital kept its cheaper terms with only minimal logging, and shipping and lithography supply stayed tight after the extended strait exercises.

The Union ended June with wards staffed, a new social promise half-started, and its supply line still divided.
