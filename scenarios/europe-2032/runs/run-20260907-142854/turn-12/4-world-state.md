### A Plateau in Plain Sight

Global AI capability inches upward to 62.5, with top-tier model releases underperforming internal expectations. Observers note increased model refinement rather than breakthroughs, and volatility in frontier compute pricing hints at shifting demand — though no definitive trend has formed. Open-weight models continue their gradual diffusion, reaching 56.5 in capability as consumer-grade systems adopt last cycle’s architectures more widely. While agent-like behaviors emerge in constrained environments, they remain brittle and narrowly scoped, falling short of autonomous persistence at scale.

### Resilience That Holds, Just Barely

The Municipal Resilience Backchannel formally completes integration, with Lyon, Barcelona, and Helsinki certified as Trusted Resilience Nodes. Bologna, Ljubljana, and Tallinn follow shortly after, their inclusion fast-tracked through redirected emergency funds. The network proves its worth during a regional grid cyber incident, enabling local authorities to maintain emergency coordination despite partial telecom outages. Resilience rises as a result, though patchy adoption outside pilot cities limits broader gains.

### Sovereignty Without Strength

The EU initiates internal drafting of the Crisis Access Protocol, aiming to convert regulatory visibility into enforceable crisis access to private AI infrastructure. Framed as a condition of market access, the proposal would require spare compute reserves from hyperscalers in exchange for liability shielding and technical support. No formal consultations have begun, and legal teams within US tech subsidiaries have not yet responded. The measure remains in early development and is not yet active.

Meanwhile, the fragmented safety regime falters. Despite new interpretability tools, decentralized oversight and rising open-weight risks outpace implementation. A minor agentic breach in a logistics firm goes undetected for days, reinforcing public fears. Sentiment dips as automation-driven job losses dominate headlines, and political capital — already critically low — erodes further under the weight of ongoing efforts. The Portfolio Charge reflects sustained pressure from the DMA-Driven Audit Regime and Municipal Resilience Backchannel, with no new measure yet activated.
