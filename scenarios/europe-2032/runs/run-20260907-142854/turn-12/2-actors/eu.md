### New measure

**Crisis Access Protocol for Private Compute Infrastructure**  
A binding framework establishes pre-negotiated, legally enforceable terms under which the EU can activate temporary access to private AI compute during a declared crisis, in exchange for liability shielding and operational support.

- `Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`
- `Size: large`
- `Finishes on turn: 15`
- `Targeted effect:` Increases `eu_ai_sovereignty` by transforming visibility from the DMA audit into actionable access rights; stabilises `ai_safety` by enabling rapid deployment of countermeasures during incidents; reduces long-term dependency on foreign providers.
- `Applies to:` The US and China (via their hyperscaler subsidiaries operating in the EU), and EU-based private operators

### Priority

**Crisis Access Protocol for Private Compute Infrastructure** — because while the Municipal Resilience Backchannel has delivered on decentralised continuity, the audit regime alone cannot guarantee action in extremis; this measure closes the loop from visibility to control, directly fulfilling the redefined two-year commitment by making sovereignty operational, not just observational.

### In practice

This turn, we consolidate the Municipal Resilience Backchannel as a finished success. ENISA issues the final certification package for Lyon, Barcelona, and Helsinki as Trusted Resilience Nodes, with Bologna, Ljubljana, and Tallinn confirmed for integration by the end of Q2. The European Digital Innovation Hubs complete their technical audits, and the NIS2 coordination cell embeds node reporting into the EU’s cyber incident dashboard. No further capital is required: the network now sustains itself through municipal ownership and pre-agreed mutual aid protocols. Its success proves that resilience can be built where central authority fails — but it also shows that infrastructure without access rights is only half the equation.

Simultaneously, we launch the Crisis Access Protocol, building directly on the foundation of the DMA-Driven Audit Regime. The Commission drafts a Council regulation under Article 216 TFEU, proposing a crisis trigger mechanism tied to ENISA threat assessments and requiring hyperscalers to reserve a defined share of verified spare capacity for emergency activation. In return, providers receive liability protection for actions taken under protocol, technical support from EU cyber rapid response teams, and preferential access to grid upgrades. Legal services are preparing for challenges from US tech firms, but we are framing this not as expropriation, but as a condition of market access — a reciprocal obligation for systemic actors. DG CONNECT leads negotiations with national digital ministries, while the European Parliament’s ITRE and LIBE committees begin scrutiny. The protocol will take four turns to finalise, but early bilateral soundings with French and German operators suggest conditional support if safeguards are robust. This is not ownership — but it is, at last, leverage we can use.
