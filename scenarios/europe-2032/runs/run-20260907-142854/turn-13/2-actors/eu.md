## New measure
**Emergency Compute Resilience Fund**  
Establish a dedicated EU fund to co-finance redundant, hardened compute clusters in member states, ensuring continuity of critical public AI services during infrastructure failure or coercion.

- `Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`
- `Size: large`
- `Finishes on turn: 17`
- `Targeted effect:` Increases `eu_ai_sovereignty` by 10–12 points by creating a distributed, state-controlled compute backbone; reduces dependency on hyperscaler goodwill; supports `resilience` by ensuring AI-enabled emergency response remains operational under disruption.
- `Applies to:` EU member states, with priority to Trusted Resilience Nodes and critical infrastructure operators

## Priority
**Crisis Access Protocol for Private Compute Infrastructure** — because it is the linchpin of our redefined sovereignty strategy: without enforceable access rights, audits are just observation and redundancy is just backup; this measure turns visibility and capacity into actionable control, directly fulfilling our two-year commitment in an era where openweight diffusion widens systemic risk.

## In practice
This turn, we complete the DMA-Driven Audit Regime, publishing the final compliance package for hyperscalers and integrating verified spare capacity data into the ENISA threat dashboard. The European Data Protection Board confirms interoperability with GDPR audit trails, and national digital regulators begin routine inspections in Dublin, Amsterdam, and Frankfurt. The regime lands as a quiet but foundational shift: for the first time, we know what compute exists, where, and under what load — but we still cannot act on it in extremis. That gap is now the focus.

We launch the Crisis Access Protocol in full, with the Commission tabling a draft Council regulation under Article 216 TFEU, framed as a systemic resilience instrument. DG GROW leads negotiations with national digital ministries, while the European Parliament’s LIBE committee opens hearings on proportionality and redress. We are offering US hyperscalers liability shielding for actions taken under protocol activation — including immunity from injunction during declared crises — in exchange for binding commitments to reserve 15% of EU-based spare capacity. Early soundings with German and French operators suggest conditional support if operational autonomy is preserved and activation thresholds are strictly defined. Legal teams are preparing for a challenge under bilateral investment treaties, but we are anchoring the argument in public order, not expropriation.

Simultaneously, we begin drafting the Emergency Compute Resilience Fund, to be proposed under the Multiannual Financial Framework’s solidarity clause. The fund will co-finance sovereign compute clusters in at least ten member states, prioritising Trusted Resilience Nodes and energy-secure regions. Initial design work is underway in DG REGIO and DG CONNECT, with technical specifications being developed in coordination with EuroHPC and the European Chips Act infrastructure teams. This is not about matching the frontier — it is about ensuring that when the frontier fails, or is turned off, the EU can still function.
