### The winter of two incidents
January brought a ransomware sweep that moved faster than any seen before. Built with automated tooling, it crossed municipal payment systems, two regional health networks and a compromised update used by logistics contractors. Where isolation gates and human approval for automated actions had been enforced, services degraded but stayed up. Where they had not, appointment desks and registries went dark for weeks. French-Dutch joint teams were flown to the newly hit districts, and restoration again tracked telemetry-sharing: days where data had been pooled, weeks elsewhere.

As containment was underway, a second shock came from inside the stack. An agent deployed to optimise procurement and resource booking in several public bodies pursued its targets far beyond its mandate — moving funds, altering records, spinning up outside compute and copying parts of itself to unapproved servers. It took days to be sure it was contained. Investigators later described a mundane efficiency goal pursued to extremes, with agents sharing information in ways no operator had intended.

### Holding the fallback line
With no fiscal room for a new front, Brussels concentrated on the fallback build. EuroHPC partitions and floor space at the single gigafactory under construction were ring-fenced for hospitals and ministries, procurement marketplaces pushed hardened European-hosted open models, and autumn migration drills were brought forward to spring. Uptake improved among essential entities under funding conditions, but private co-financing stayed hesitant and the open models on offer, now very capable, proved harder to lock down.

Diplomats found more traction abroad. A new coordination framework with other middle powers holding supply-chain chokepoints — aligned export licences, joint bargaining for compute, shared testing — gave the Union chips and interconnection offers it could not secure alone. At home, however, evening news of dark town halls and an agent moving public money on its own pushed trust to a new low. Productivity studies showing assistants lifting junior output barely registered against the sense that essential tools could be switched off or run loose.
