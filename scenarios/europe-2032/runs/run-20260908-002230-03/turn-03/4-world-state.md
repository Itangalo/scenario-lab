### Steady Advance, Mounting Tensions

AI capability climbs to 58.0, driven by recursive optimisation and tighter training loops. The anomalous reasoning patterns from last turn are now replicated in a second lab, fuelling quiet alarm among technical reviewers. With no new safety protocols in place and opacity deepening, *ai_safety* drops to 24.0. Open-weight models reach 45.5, buoyed by derivative work from Kimi K3 and early replication of closed-model planning techniques—narrowly avoiding full dependence on frontier labs.

### Crisis Response, Not Transformation

The EU launches the Emergency AI Governance Forum in response to spreading labour unrest. The Commission and ETUC co-chair the body, and €120 million from ESF+ funds union research. However, French unions denounce the six-week timeline as tokenistic, and Belgium delays appointing representatives, citing legal constraints. Still, the symbolic weight of binding social dialogue lifts *public_sentiment* to 47.0, as media frame the move as overdue recognition of worker agency.

Simultaneously, the suspension of Public-Sector AI Adoption funds for France and Belgium triggers backlash: Paris accuses Brussels of fiscal coercion, while Madrid warns of precedent. The leverage works—both nations eventually commit—but at political cost.

The Contingency Plans with Exercises conclude successfully. The NIS2 Implementing Act clears legal review, and the Joint Cyber Unit begins red-teaming with AI-generated threats. This procedural maturity, though not yet battle-tested, raises *resilience* to 42.0.

Accelerated-permitting zones under the Tech Sovereignty Package advance in Bulgaria and Romania, with site plans finalised. Yet global AI progress outpaces EU build-out, and without secured frontier access, *eu_ai_sovereignty* dips to 18.0. Political capital erodes slightly to 37.0 under the strain of five active measures, despite modest gains.
