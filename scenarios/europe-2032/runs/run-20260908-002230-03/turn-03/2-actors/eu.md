## In practice  
We are launching the Emergency AI Governance Forum under Article 157 TFEU, leveraging the EU’s competence in social dialogue, and co-chaired by the Commission’s Vice-President for Values and Transparency and the ETUC General Secretary. The forum will convene within four weeks, with a six-week mandate to produce draft guidelines on AI deployment thresholds in public services—specifically around job displacement, human oversight, and recourse mechanisms. It will be empowered to recommend temporary moratoria on specific deployments pending review, and its findings will feed directly into the ongoing revision of the AI Act’s public administration annex. We are allocating €120 million from the European Social Fund+ to fund union research capacity and civic participation, ensuring that the process is not captured by technocratic inertia. Crucially, we are suspending disbursement of Public-Sector AI Adoption Accelerator funds to France and Belgium until their social partners confirm participation—turning access to EU finance into leverage for inclusion.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on the Tech Sovereignty Package’s accelerated-permitting zones, now decoupled and advancing under Article 114. Germany and France have confirmed their support, and Bulgaria and Romania are finalising site proposals for Varna and Sofia, with co-leadership formalised through a joint declaration at the Competitiveness Council. We are linking approval of these zones to binding commitments: 15% of compute for public-sector AI, full audit rights for the EU AI Office, and adherence to the forthcoming guidelines from the Emergency Forum. This creates a feedback loop—sovereignty is not just about compute, but about who it serves.

The Contingency Plans with Exercises have now concluded their first full cycle. The NIS2 Implementing Act is in final drafting at DG CONNECT, with a targeted adoption date in Q1 2028. The Joint Cyber Unit has onboarded its first AI red-teaming cohort, trained on synthetic intrusion scenarios developed with ENISA and EUSPA. While resilience remains at 39.0, the procedural backbone is now in place, and we are shifting focus from design to rehearsal—next quarter’s exercise will simulate a multi-vector attack on health and energy systems during a period of political instability.
