## New measure  
``Public-Sector AI Adoption Accelerator``  
A targeted programme to scale successful AI deployments in public services across health, education, and social administration, using sovereign infrastructure and open evaluation.  
`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` public_sentiment +15, eu_political_capital +5 if adoption is visible and trusted; strengthens legitimacy of EU-led AI development  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with priority access to InvestAI infrastructure and evaluation support from the European AI Office

## Priority  
**Public-Sector AI Adoption Accelerator** — because the adoption_success event proves we can generate visible public benefit through EU-led action, and this is the first real chance in two turns to shift public_sentiment from ambivalence to active support, which is the only path to rebuilding political capital at scale.

## In practice  
We are launching the Public-Sector AI Adoption Accelerator under the Digital Europe Programme and the European Social Fund+, targeting ten pilot regions where recent AI trials have reduced waiting lists in diagnostics, sped up disability benefit assessments, or improved early literacy outcomes. These will now be scaled using models hosted on the nascent InvestAI infrastructure, with full transparency: each deployment will publish its safety case, performance metrics, and impact evaluation via the European AI Office. We are fast-tracking access to €4.2 billion in DEP and ERDF funds, conditioned on open-source tooling and interoperability with national digital identity systems. The goal is not just efficiency, but trust: citizens must see AI as something that works *for them*, not just something that watches or replaces.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on the Tech Sovereignty Package in the Competitiveness Council, where resistance from Poland and Hungary over permitting zones has stalled the directive. In response, we are decoupling the accelerated-permitting zones from the broader package and offering them as a standalone regulation under Article 114 TFEU, which allows qualified majority voting—bypassing the need for unanimity. This comes with a binding side letter: any site receiving fast-tracked status must allocate 15% of its compute to public-sector AI adoption and allow audit access by the EU AI Office. Germany and France have agreed to support this move, and we are offering Bulgaria and Romania co-leadership on the first two permitted zones to broaden buy-in.

The Contingency Plans with Exercises are now in execution: the first tabletop exercise took place in March 2027 with CERTs from six member states, EUSPA, and ENISA, simulating a cascading failure in energy and transport triggered by an AI-driven intrusion. The results exposed critical gaps in cross-border escalation protocols, particularly around the delegation of emergency authority when automated systems are compromised. We are now drafting a binding Implementing Act under the NIS2 Directive to standardize incident classification and mutual aid triggers by Q4, and we are expanding the Joint Cyber Unit’s mandate to include AI-specific red-teaming capacity, funded from the €150 million already allocated.
