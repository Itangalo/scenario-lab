## New measure
``Contingency plans with exercises``
A standing, exercised procedure for responding to AI-enabled cyber intrusions in critical infrastructure.
`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`
`Size: small`
`Finishes on turn: 3`
`Targeted effect:` resilience +10, eu_political_capital +2 if public_sentiment improves; reduces cascading failure risk after cyber incidents
`Applies to:` EU member states, with EU-wide coordination through ENISA and the Health Security Committee framework

## Priority
Contingency plans with exercises — because the cyber_test_shot proved detection failed, existing plans are untested, and resilience is the only layer of defence that still rests on our own action, not someone else’s restraint.

## In practice
We are activating the Health Security Committee’s emergency protocol framework to adapt it for AI-enabled cyber threats, starting with a tabletop exercise in Q1 2027 involving CERTs from six member states, the European Energy Regulators (ACER), and EUSPA. The exercise will stress-test detection, escalation, and cross-border response under conditions of partial system compromise — exactly the gap exposed by the autumn intrusion. We are allocating €150 million from the EU4Health reserve and the Digital Europe Programme to fund the first cycle, with a requirement that participating states commit to implementing at least two of the resulting mitigation pathways by mid-2028. Resistance from smaller member states, who see this as a burden without matching support, is being met with binding offers of technical assistance from the newly expanded Joint Cyber Unit in Tallinn.

At the same time, we are pushing forward the InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech sovereignty package with a Council discussion under Article 122 TFEU, treating compute as strategic infrastructure. The Commission is preparing a recommendation for a "permitted zone" status for selected sites, fast-tracking grid access and environmental assessments — but only for facilities that commit to EU-controlled access and audit rights. This is being negotiated in the Competitiveness Council, where Germany and France support the direction but Poland and Hungary demand exemptions. We are offering co-investment in regional retraining hubs as a trade — tying the sovereignty push to social resilience, not just hardware. The public debate remains ambivalent, but the cyber_test_shot has shifted framing: this is no longer about competitiveness, but continuity of state function.
