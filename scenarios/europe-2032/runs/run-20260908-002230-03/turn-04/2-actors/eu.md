## In practice  
We are launching the Coalition for Supply Chain Integrity under Article 207 TFEU, leveraging the EU’s competence in trade and external economic relations, in close coordination with DG TRADE and the EEAS. The initiative will be tabled at an emergency ministerial in The Hague, convened within six weeks, bringing together jurisdictions that hold chokepoints: the Netherlands on lithography, Japan on materials and tools, South Korea and Taiwan on advanced packaging and fabrication, Canada on rare earths and talent flows, and the EU itself on chip design software and infrastructure governance. The goal is a mutual recognition framework for export controls and a standing consultation mechanism that prevents any one power from unilaterally altering access—turning our fragmented leverage into a coordinated shield. We are offering joint R&D funding through Horizon Europe and preferential market access for coalition members’ AI hardware as incentives, while making clear that continued unilateral compliance with third-party coercion will disqualify a partner from mutual support in future disruptions.

At the same time, we are finalising the Tech Sovereignty Package’s accelerated-permitting zones in Bulgaria and Romania, with site plans now approved and grid connection timelines negotiated through the Trans-European Networks for Energy. Germany and France have confirmed their political backing, and we are linking final approval to enforceable commitments: 15% of compute capacity reserved for public-sector AI, full audit rights for the EU AI Office, and adherence to the forthcoming AI Act revisions informed by the now-concluded Emergency AI Governance Forum. The Forum delivered its draft guidelines on human oversight and job displacement thresholds, which we are fast-tracking into the AI Act’s public administration annex via comitology—avoiding a full legislative cycle while preserving legitimacy.

The Public-Sector AI Adoption Accelerator continues on track, with pilot projects in Finnish healthcare and Dutch social administration showing measurable gains in case processing and citizen satisfaction. However, we are tightening evaluation requirements: all adopters must now publish impact assessments using the EU AI Office’s open audit framework, and we are redirecting 10% of the programme’s budget to fund municipal-level digital capacity, ensuring smaller administrations are not left behind. This is not just about diffusion—it is about proving that sovereign, transparent AI can deliver public value without eroding trust.
