## In practice
We are launching the Sovereign AI Compute Corridor under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market to override national fragmentation and ensure cross-border interoperability and legal enforceability. The corridor will bind a minimum of 60% of the compute capacity from the InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package into a unified, EU-governed infrastructure layer, with access rights reserved for public-sector AI, emergency response systems, and vetted academic and safety research. Legal anchoring will be achieved through a new regulation requiring all participating data centres to sign binding commitments: no remote shutdown clauses, no foreign jurisdiction override, and audit rights for the EU AI Office. We are offering participating member states priority access to Horizon Europe AI safety grants and a 10% top-up on cohesion funds for hosting corridor nodes, while making clear that any state accepting extraterritorial control over its infrastructure will be excluded from mutual support under the Coalition for Supply Chain Integrity.

The Tech Sovereignty Package continues toward completion in turn 6, with accelerated permitting now finalised in Bulgaria and Romania, and grid integration timelines secured through TEN-E coordination. Germany and France have reaffirmed their support, but we are now conditioning final approval on full compliance with the corridor’s access rules—no carve-outs for private hyperscalers, no exceptions for foreign partners. We are also advancing negotiations with ASML and EUV component suppliers to establish a maintenance and repair reserve stockpile within the corridor, mitigating the immediate impact of U.S.-forced service restrictions.

The Public-Sector AI Adoption Accelerator has concluded its run, and its final evaluation reports from Finland and the Netherlands show strong gains in public trust and administrative efficiency—particularly where open audit frameworks were used. We are institutionalising these practices through the AI Act’s public administration annex, now being fast-tracked via comitology, and redirecting the programme’s remaining funds into municipal digital capacity building, with a focus on smaller cities in Southern and Eastern Europe. This is not just about scaling AI—it is about proving that transparency and public control can coexist with performance.
