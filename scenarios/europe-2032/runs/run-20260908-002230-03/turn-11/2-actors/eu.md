## New measure  
**Sovereign AI Institutional Backbone**  
We establish a permanent EU-level technical authority with mandate over evaluation, audit, and operation of sovereign AI systems, integrating EuroHPC JU, the European AI Security Board, and public research talent into a unified chain of command, reporting to the Commission but operationally autonomous.  
`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +8 (by institutionalising sovereign capability beyond emergency measures), ai_safety +6 (via continuous public-sector evaluation and monitoring), eu_political_capital −4 (due to centralisation push and resistance from national agencies)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member state technical agencies, public research consortia, and sovereign cloud operators

## Priority  
**Sovereign Frontier Access Initiative** — because without immediate access to a functioning frontier system, the Union cannot stabilise its essential services or demonstrate that sovereignty is more than rhetoric, and because this turn’s denial event has made clear that delay is surrender.

## In practice  
We are accelerating the Sovereign Frontier Access Initiative through direct invocation of Article 114 TFEU and the Crisis Response Instrument, but now with a new condition: all contracts issued under the initiative must be structured to feed into the Sovereign AI Institutional Backbone we are now building. EuroHPC JU is being directed to begin integration planning with ENISA, the European High-Performance Computing centres in Barcelona, Bologna, and Kajaani, and the AI research divisions of Fraunhofer, INRIA, and CWI. A joint governance board will be seated by turn-end, co-chaired by the Vice-President for Values and Transparency and an independent technical director appointed from the European Research Council. The first operational milestone — booting a 74.0-capable model on EU-anchored compute — is now tied to the delivery of audit interfaces that will persist beyond the initiative’s end.

The Secure Open-Weight Access and Containment Framework is entering its final phase. Legal clearance is expected within the month, and we are pre-emptively adjusting its enforcement mechanism: rather than treating all open-weight developers equally, we are introducing a tiered compliance pathway that fast-tracks non-commercial research collectives like EleutherAI and BigScience while imposing stricter monitoring on commercial redistributors. This is being sold as proportionality, not leniency — and we are backing it with a €300 million fund for EU-hosted open-weight infrastructure, co-financed by Germany and France, to demonstrate that containment enables, rather than suppresses, openness.

Behind the scenes, the backlash_physical events are forcing a shift in siting strategy. We are withdrawing planned Gigafactory expansions in regions with active protest clusters and redirecting investment to Spain, Finland, and the Baltic states, where grid capacity and public acceptance are higher. At the same time, we are launching a targeted communication campaign — not to defend AI, but to distinguish between foreign-controlled systems and EU-sovereign infrastructure, framing the latter as a public utility. Labour_displacement is being met with a preliminary task force under the European Social Fund, but no measure is launched yet: we are waiting for the next Eurostat report to quantify the scale before committing political capital we cannot afford to lose.
