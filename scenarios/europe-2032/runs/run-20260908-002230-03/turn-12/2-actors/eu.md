## New measure  
**EU Compute Integrity Pact**  
We impose an immediate moratorium on cross-border sovereign compute usage by public research entities and launch a binding audit and compliance framework to restore trust in the InvestAI program, halting all non-essential allocations until usage logs are verified and legal ambiguities resolved.  
`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +3 (by restoring legitimacy to sovereign compute access), eu_political_capital −2 (due to short-term disruption and backlash from research community), resilience +2 (by reducing attack surface from shadow compute markets)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, InvestAI-funded research institutions, EuroHPC JU, and national high-performance computing centres

## Priority  
**Sovereign AI Institutional Backbone** — because the emergent_ai_worker_syndicate_expansion and emergent_compute_sovereignty_backlash have made clear that our institutions are fracturing faster than we can build them, and without a unified, trusted, and operationally coherent chain of command, no amount of compute or access will translate into real sovereignty.

## In practice  
We are treating the Sovereign AI Institutional Backbone as the spine of survival. The joint governance board has been seated, co-chaired by the Vice-President for Values and Transparency and Dr. Elina Kovanen of the European Research Council, and its first act is to freeze all non-critical allocations from EuroHPC JU until the EU Compute Integrity Pact audit is complete. This is not popular — INRIA, Fraunhofer, and several national labs are protesting the freeze as counterproductive — but we are framing it as a necessary pause to prevent the collapse of the entire sovereignty project under legal and political fragmentation. We are offering a fast-track compliance pathway: institutions that submit to real-time usage logging and external verification by ENISA can resume allocations within six weeks. The €300 million open-weight infrastructure fund is being redirected to support this verification layer, not just compute expansion.

The Sovereign Frontier Access Initiative is now in technical integration phase. We have secured a set of audited weights from a non-US-aligned developer operating at 76.0 capability, and are preparing to boot them on the Kajaani cluster under full EU operational control. But the AI worker syndicate has declared they will not validate the deployment unless the audit interfaces mandated by the Institutional Backbone are live and staffed — a condition we cannot ignore. We are negotiating directly with the syndicate’s coordinating node, offering formal recognition as a public-sector technical advisory body in exchange for conditional cooperation. This is uncharted territory: we are bargaining with a collective of AI engineers who see themselves as stewards, not employees. If we succeed, it may become the foundation of a new model of public technical legitimacy. If we fail, the system may be technically sovereign but operationally stillborn.

Meanwhile, the Secure Open-Weight Access and Containment Framework is entering final legal review. We are fast-tracking non-commercial collectives like EleutherAI under the tiered compliance model, but imposing strict logging and watermarking on commercial redistributors. The first test will come when a 62.0-capable agent leaks through a distributed training run — and we know it will. We are preparing a public response protocol: not denial, but demonstration — showing that our containment framework can trace, isolate, and mitigate the spread, even as the open frontier surges. This is our only chance to show that order is possible, even in the chaos.
