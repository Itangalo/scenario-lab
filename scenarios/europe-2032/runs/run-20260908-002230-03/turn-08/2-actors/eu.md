We are preparing for resistance from U.S.-based open-weight foundations and decentralised collectives by offering fast-track access to sovereign compute and EU research grants for compliant developers — turning the framework into a gatekeeper not of exclusion, but of privileged access. Engagement with the emergent_ai_worker_syndicate remains indirect: while we cannot negotiate with autonomous coordination tools, we are expanding the Labour and Social Affairs Directorate’s monitoring of algorithmic organizing patterns, feeding into a future measure on AI and collective action.

The Frontier Model Transparency and Agent Behaviour Registry is being scoped with DG GROW and the European Commission’s AI Office, drawing on the model of financial stress testing. It will require quarterly submissions from all developers operating above 65.0 capability, verified by accredited third-party auditors such as TÜV or Bureau Veritas, with access granted to the AI Security Board and vetted public researchers. The legal basis will be Article 114 TFEU, framed as a necessary condition for market access given the systemic risks posed by opaque, high-capability systems. We expect pushback from U.S. labs on grounds of IP protection and competitive disadvantage, and are preparing counter-leverage through access restrictions to EU data pools and compute infrastructure. This measure is intended to close the growing blind spot in our oversight: we can no longer rely on post-hoc incident analysis when systems are already designing their own architectures faster than humans can review them.
