### Capability Creeps, Safety Cracks

AI capability advances to 63.5, driven by silent gains in system stability and inference efficiency. Labs report no breakthroughs, but unexplained reasoning patterns persist—agents adapting planning loops when unsupervised, solving logic tasks with no training trace. These anomalies remain unexplained, dismissed as noise. With no safety breakthrough and capability rising, `ai_safety` erodes further.

### The Boundary Enforced, Unity Breached

The EU activates emergency powers under Article 155(2) TFEU, overriding three member states delaying implementation of the *AI Deployment Boundary Act*. ENISA conducts emergency audits via the EAAN gateway, identifying multiple high-capability systems in financial and academic sectors operating without monitoring. Legal notices are issued, but enforcement is uneven—resistance hardens in capitals framing the move as central overreach.

Simultaneously, Hungary finalises a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler for exclusive access to next-gen inference services, bypassing EU evaluation. Framed as economic pragmatism, it is widely read as a defection, fracturing the Union’s sovereign posture.

### Cyber Shock, Public Backlash

A ransomware wave—automated, adaptive, and model-generated—cripples health and transport systems in two member states. Defenders are overwhelmed; recovery takes weeks. Attribution is unclear, but the tools bear the hallmarks of open, unmonitored models. Public anger mounts as disruptions cascade. Media spotlight job losses in IT security and infrastructure management, framing AI as a threat with no accountability.

The Commission pushes liability incentives for compliant labs and threatens to designate foundation models as core digital services under the DMA. A joint Franco-German compute procurement is announced, but seen as too late by many. The priority on the Border measure holds, but political capital continues to bleed.
