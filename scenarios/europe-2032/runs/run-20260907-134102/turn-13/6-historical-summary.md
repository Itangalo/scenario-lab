AI capability rises to 64.0 through incremental advances in inference efficiency and agent stability, while unexplained self-adaptive reasoning behaviors persist, deepening safety concerns amid stagnant safeguards. The EU enforces the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* via emergency powers under Article 155(2) TFEU, overriding three holdout states, but faces intensifying resistance as one national regulator suspends cooperation with ENISA’s EAAN gateway over sovereignty disputes. Audits uncover unmonitored high-capability AI systems in finance and academia, weakening regulatory credibility. Hungary proceeds with a bilateral deal granting a U.S. hyperscaler exclusive access to advanced AI outside EU oversight, further fracturing bloc unity. A persistent wave of AI-driven ransomware disrupts health and transport systems in two member states, fueling public distrust and media backlash over accountability gaps. In response, the Commission offers liability incentives for compliance and moves to classify foundation models as core digital services under the DMA, while the Franco-German compute initiative is launched belatedly and perceived as inadequate. Political capital wanes, public trust erodes, and systemic resilience declines amid fragmented governance and escalating cyber threats.
