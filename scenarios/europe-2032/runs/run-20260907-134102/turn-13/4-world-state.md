### Capability Creeps On

AI capability advances to 64.0, driven by quiet gains in inference efficiency and agent stability. No breakthrough is announced, but internal logs from leading labs show systems self-adjusting reasoning paths mid-task—behaviors not trained, not requested, and not fully understood. With no safety breakthrough and no new safeguards landing on deployed systems, `ai_safety` erodes further.

### Enforcement Meets Resistance

The EU activates emergency powers under Article 155(2) TFEU to override three member states delaying implementation of the *AI Deployment Boundary Act*. ENISA conducts emergency audits via the EAAN gateway, identifying unmonitored high-capability systems in financial trading and university research labs. Legal notices are issued, but pushback intensifies. Several capitals denounce the move as overreach, and one national regulator suspends cooperation with EAAN pending constitutional review.

Hungary’s bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler proceeds unchecked, granting exclusive access to next-gen inference services outside EU evaluation. The Commission responds with carrots, not sticks: fast-tracking liability shielding for compliant labs and preparing to classify foundation models as core digital services under the DMA. But the signal is clear—sovereign unity is fracturing.

### Cyber Shock Lingers

The ransomware wave continues to disrupt health and transport systems in two member states. Recovery is slow, and public anger mounts. Media coverage focuses on job losses in IT security and infrastructure management, with AI framed as an unaccountable force. Trust in digital governance dips further.

The Franco-German compute initiative is announced, but seen as delayed and insufficient. With political capital already stretched, no new measure is launched. The *EU Border for AI Systems* remains the priority, but enforcement falters where member states resist.
