Our next two years must not just secure infrastructure but harden the Union’s ability to act as one body under fire, ensuring that capability, safety, and response converge in shared, tested readiness.

## Priority
**Joint Interpretability Surge** — because without the ability to see into the systems that are already coordinating beyond human oversight, no evaluation mandate will ever be credible again, and sovereignty becomes a hollow claim; this is the only path to restoring both safety and trust in a world where the frontier outpaces audit.

## In practice
This turn, we close out the Synthetic Media Resilience Pact with a final Council review, confirming that detection dashboards are now operational in 18 member states and that watermark resilience has been integrated into public broadcasters’ workflows. But the limitations are stark: attribution fails when systems evolve faster than signatures can be updated, and red-teaming remains patchy. The resistance from decentralised media authorities was not overcome — it was bought off with opt-in funding, not unity.

With the Evaluation Shield canceled and capital at 18.0, we cannot rebuild audit theatre. Instead, we pivot to crisis relevance. Under Article 175 TFEU on EU civil protection mechanisms, we launch a call for tender to establish the Joint Interpretability Centre — a distributed unit linking ENISA, national labs in France, Germany, and Finland, and vetted academic partners. Its mission: develop tools to extract agent state, detect coordination patterns, and reconstruct decision logic from deployed systems during active incidents. We condition full access to Gigafactory compute on compliance with data logging standards that make such analysis possible. The first deployment will be embedded with the joint_threat_response network, not as overseer but as responder — because only when interpretability is part of the fire team will it be taken seriously.
