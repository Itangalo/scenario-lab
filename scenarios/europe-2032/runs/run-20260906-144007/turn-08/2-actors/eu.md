## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act alone when collective breakdown is the risk.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI infrastructure to ensure strategic autonomy in an era of accelerating capability and uncertain alliances — and to enable credible joint action on safety and resilience.

`Trigger: emergent_synthetic_threat_wave`  
`Grounds:` The coordinated disinformation campaign has exposed that sovereign infrastructure alone cannot shield the Union from cascading societal harm. The failure to coordinate cross-border defences in real time, despite existing mechanisms, shows that capability without collective operational readiness is brittle. Our mandate to prevent harm now demands that sovereignty be tied directly to shared resilience and enforceable preparedness — not just compute on soil, but joint capacity to act. This reorients our two-year commitment to include not just independence, but interoperable readiness, so that sovereignty becomes a lever for unity rather than a retreat into fragmentation.

## New measure

**Synthetic Media Resilience Pact**  
Launch a binding cross-border protocol for detection, attribution, and public notification of AI-generated content in electoral and public information contexts, operationalised through ENISA and the European Media Freedom Act framework.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by +8 to +12; reduce synthetic media impact on public_sentiment by stabilising trust in information during elections. Indirectly pressures ai_safety by creating demand for provenance standards.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, European Parliament, national election authorities, public broadcasters, and platforms above 45 million users under DSA.

## Priority

**Synthetic Media Resilience Pact** — because the emergent_synthetic_threat_wave has crossed the threshold from hypothetical to lived reality, and without a unified response, the next incident may not just distort an election but dissolve the legitimacy of the entire process — making this the only measure that addresses both catastrophic risk and the erosion of political capital in one stroke.

## In practice

This turn, we activate emergency procedures under Article 222 TFEU to fast-track the Synthetic Media Resilience Pact through the Justice and Home Affairs Council. ENISA, in coordination with the European Digital Media Observatory and national cybersecurity agencies, deploys a provisional attribution framework based on watermark resilience, hash registries, and cross-referenced provenance logs from public broadcasters and DSA-covered platforms. We mandate real-time reporting of synthetic content above 10,000 engagements during election periods, with penalties for non-compliance tied to existing DSA enforcement mechanisms.

We offer member states co-funding from the Internal Security Fund for rapid deployment of detection suites and public notification dashboards, prioritising those entering electoral cycles before 2031. Resistance from states with decentralised media oversight is met with tailored implementation pathways, but we condition access to the final phase of InvestAI Gigafactories compute access on participation in the Pact’s red-teaming exercises. The Council resolution on the European Evaluation Shield is allowed to lapse, and its remaining budget is redirected to a new unit within ENISA focused on operationalising interpretability in real-world threat scenarios — not as audit theatre, but as crisis response capability.
