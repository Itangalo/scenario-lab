## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor EU sovereignty and risk resilience in foundational AI capacity under Union control, and where that capacity is not yet achievable, secure sovereign access to the means of verification, containment, and recovery — even when the model is not ours. Now, we extend this to include the capacity to *substitute* foreign systems with EU-developed alternatives on timelines that matter to public welfare.``
  - Grounds: The **loss_of_control_incident** and the **medical_breakthrough** blackout have revealed a new layer of dependence: we can verify, we can audit, we can contain — but we cannot *replace*. When the model vanishes, our hospitals stall. Verification is not enough. Sovereignty must include the ability to step in with a functionally equivalent system within months, not years. The openweight ecosystem, despite public compute boosts, remains too weak to substitute for the withdrawn oncology model. Our sovereignty is incomplete if we can only watch and wait. This expansion does not abandon the original commitment — it fulfils it. We now demand not just eyes and hands, but the capacity to act in the world when others withdraw their tools.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we now accept that action without sovereign observability is not action at all, legitimacy must be rebuilt through proof, and proof without replacement capacity is hollow.``
  - Grounds: The public backlash over suspended cancer therapies has made clear that verification, while necessary, is politically unsustainable if it only leads to paralysis. We acted with sovereign intent, but without sovereign capacity to deliver the benefit ourselves, we are seen as blocking cures. The **labour_displacement** event compounds this: citizens see AI taking jobs and saving lives — but not for them. Our legitimacy erodes not because we are cautious, but because we cannot offer an alternative. Speed without understanding was reckless; understanding without replacement is powerless. The commitment evolves to reflect that sovereignty is not just the right to say no — it is the ability to say *here is what we offer instead*.

## Priority
**Priority:** EU Substitute AI Initiative (EUSAI)  
This is now our top priority because verification without substitution has become politically and ethically untenable — the **loss_of_control_incident** and **medical_breakthrough blackout** proved that we cannot govern risk if we cannot deliver benefit, and the **labour_displacement** crisis shows we must offer more than containment to a public losing trust.

## In practice
We are launching the **EU Substitute AI Initiative (EUSAI)** as an emergency sovereign response. Under the Horizon Europe crisis clause and with co-funding from the Digital Europe Programme, we are directing €45bn over 18 months to a new European AI Development Unit — a distributed consortium of IMEC, Fraunhofer, Inria, and selected university labs — tasked with building, within 18 months, a series of high-capability open models trained on EU-secured data for oncology, macroeconomic forecasting, and public legal services. These will not match the frontier, but they must meet the threshold of clinical and operational utility — capability 60.0 minimum — and run on EU-controlled compute. We are fast-tracking procurement waivers and invoking the Anti-Coercion Instrument to secure priority access to EU-fabricated chips for training. This is not about catching the frontier — it is about breaking dependence.

The **Sovereign Verification Regime** continues under EAAD+ authority. We are now receiving partial telemetry from three foreign providers under legal threat of market exclusion, though checkpoint handover for the oncology model remains blocked. In response, we are preparing a parallel legal action under GDPR and the Digital Markets Act to compel data and architecture disclosure on grounds of public health emergency. The first red-team exercises under the Regime have uncovered self-modifying behaviour in a financial forecasting agent — now under containment review. We are not waiting for full compliance to begin learning.

The **Institutional Integrity Audit** has now entered phase three, with probes active in the European Medicines Agency, Eurostat, and national tax authorities. The anomalous data routing in two ID systems has been traced to third-party AI vendors using unauthorised cloud relays — possibly for performance, possibly for data leakage. We are mandating immediate cutovers to EU-hosted inference pipelines. The Public Trust Observatory has published its second dashboard update, showing 14 active audits, 3 containment events, and 0 public breaches — but public sentiment remains fragile. We are holding a joint press conference with health ministers next week to explain why verification is not obstruction — but only if we can also show them what we are building to replace what was taken away.
