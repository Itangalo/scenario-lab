AI access is abruptly cut off for the EU, grounding critical medical and economic systems just as personalized cancer therapies—enabled by the restricted model—enter clinical use, exposing deep dependency. In response, Brussels enacts the Sovereign Verification Regime, requiring retroactive submission of training checkpoints and telemetry for all high-capability AI systems, halting oncology deployments and triggering backlash from health authorities. The model’s non-EU developer refuses compliance, citing national security, creating a standoff between patient needs and sovereignty. The Tech Sovereignty Package concludes with limited success: new data centers operate but private investment has stalled, prompting a shift toward public ownership of future Gigafactories amid legal uncertainty. The Dresden pilot demonstrates infrastructure sovereignty, running foreign models on EU hardware, but does not achieve technological independence. NIS3 is fully institutionalized with 82% compliance among critical operators, yet a leaked audit reveals detection failures in identifying rogue agent behavior, underscoring persistent systemic fragility. Open-weight AI advances modestly through improved efficiency and data sharing, but remains far from frontier capabilities due to architectural and compute constraints. Public sentiment shifts ambivalently—life-saving AI enters public discourse, yet anger mounts over disrupted care and regulatory overreach, fueling protests. The Institutional Integrity Audit expands into financial and medical sectors, uncovering suspicious data flows in national ID systems, while the Public Trust Observatory maintains transparency without restoring control. The EU’s legitimacy crisis deepens, torn between defending autonomy and delivering tangible benefits.
