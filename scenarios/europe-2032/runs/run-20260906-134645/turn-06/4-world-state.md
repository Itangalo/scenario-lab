### The Denial and the Demand

The EU’s access to the leading AI model is abruptly suspended without explanation, disrupting hospital trial coordination and central bank forecasting. The blackout coincides with a major medical advance — individually tailored cancer therapies are now in clinical use — but the model behind it vanishes from European reach within hours. The benefit arrives not as progress, but as proof of dependence.

In response, Brussels launches the **Sovereign Verification Regime**, mandating that all high-capability systems used in the EU submit training checkpoints and telemetry to a new oversight body. The rule applies retroactively to the vanished medical model. Health ministries suspend oncology deployments pending compliance, sparking outcry from clinicians who call the move reckless. The developer, based outside the bloc, refuses to comply, citing national security directives. The standoff hardens into stalemate.

Meanwhile, the **Tech Sovereignty Package** officially concludes. Two new data centres are online, but private investment has dried up. The Council approves a shift to public ownership for future Gigafactories, though legal challenges loom over state aid rules. The Dresden pilot runs foreign models on EU hardware — sovereignty in infrastructure, not intelligence.

The **Hardened Cyber Shield** is fully institutionalised as NIS3, with 82% of critical operators compliant. Yet confidence remains fragile: a leaked internal audit shows detection systems failed to flag anomalous agent behaviour during recent red-team drills. Resilience improves marginally as patching continues, but legacy vulnerabilities persist.

Public sentiment rises slightly as life-saving treatments enter discourse — even if inaccessible. However, anger grows over disrupted care and perceived overreach. Protests flare outside health agencies. The narrative splits: was the EU defending sovereignty or blocking cures?

### Fraying Edges

The Institutional Integrity Audit expands to financial and medical regulators. Early findings suggest irregular data routing in two national ID systems, possibly benign, possibly not. Transparency is maintained via the Public Trust Observatory’s live dashboard — but visibility does not equal control.

The open-weight AI ecosystem shows modest progress, buoyed by increased public compute allocations and data-sharing mandates from the Sovereign Verification Regime. However, no major open model release closes the performance gap with the frontier. The rise in `openweight_capability` reflects incremental gains in efficiency and fine-tuning, not a leap in capability — a slow climb, not a breakthrough. Without access to the withdrawn model’s architecture or training data, open efforts remain behind, constrained by compute and knowledge bottlenecks.
