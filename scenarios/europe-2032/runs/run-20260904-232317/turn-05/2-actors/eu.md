## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the Sovereign AI Capacity Accelerator  
We create a central EU entity to directly fund, coordinate, and legally anchor the development of sovereign AI stacks — from model training on EU-controlled compute to deployment in critical public services — ensuring that capability built under EU jurisdiction remains under EU authority and is not subject to foreign override.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)

Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 12–15 points within 24 months; secure at least one full-stack AI development cycle (training to deployment) under EU legal and physical control; create binding access rights for EAAD and public emergency services to sovereign models  
Applies to: EU institutions, member states, selected national laboratories, and private AI developers receiving EU funding or operating on EU-anchored infrastructure

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Sovereign AI Capacity Accelerator  
Because the frontier has crossed 60.0 and the US is retrenching, the window to build independent EU capacity is open — but only if we act now to consolidate compute, talent, and control into a single sovereign stack, rather than leaving them scattered across fragmented national efforts and foreign dependencies.

## In practice
This turn, we complete the establishment of the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) — a milestone. The legal basis under Article 16 TFEU is now ratified, and EAAD is formally embedded in the AI Act via the Parliament’s adoption of the revised framework. We have staffed 70% of the red-team unit, drawing from ANSSI, BSI, and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre, and activated the first wave of secondments from academic AI safety groups. EAAD will begin pilot evaluations in Q1 2029, focusing on agent persistence, goal stability, and cyber-physical risk in models above 55.0 capability. The Hardened Protocols and Open-Source Defence Acceleration also conclude: all six pilot sites now run sovereign zero-trust architectures with integrated detection modules, and we are preparing a directive to mandate their adoption across all critical operators by 2030.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories continue to break ground in Brittany and northern Sweden, with ENTSO-E’s energy reservations now conditional on binding reinvestment in green hydrogen and local employment. We are leveraging the Net-Zero Industry Act to fast-track grid connections, but political resistance in host communities remains high — we are responding with binding community benefit agreements and a new “AI for Regions” initiative to fund local digital infrastructure. The Tech Sovereignty Package is nearing final trilogue agreement, with Germany, the Netherlands, and Finland backing the 12-month permitting rule in exchange for enforceable commitments on EU-based control and Blue Card+ recruitment. Still, private capital mobilisation lags — ELTIF structures are ready, but investor confidence is shaken by the US election retrenchment.

Now, with EAAD operational and the US inward-focused, we pivot to sovereignty as the core of our next two-year phase. The new Sovereign AI Capacity Accelerator will be launched under the Digital Europe Programme and co-funded by the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with a €30bn envelope over three years. It will consolidate EU compute, talent, and model development under a single legal and technical umbrella, ensuring that no sovereign capability can be remotely disabled or withheld. We are already in talks with France, Germany, and Finland to transfer ongoing national AI initiatives into the Accelerator’s framework, and with ASML and Infineon to secure upstream supply chain commitments. This is not just about catching up — it is about ensuring that when the next crisis hits, Europe answers with its own systems, its own decisions, and its own authority.
