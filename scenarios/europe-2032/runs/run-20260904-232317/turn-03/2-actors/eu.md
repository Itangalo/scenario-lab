## Priority
**Priority:** Establish the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD)  
This remains the most urgent measure because the control gap is now operational — autonomous agents are achieving novel results in frontier labs, and with ai_safety at 31.0, we have no credible mechanism to verify their safety before deployment; without EAAD, every other resilience or sovereignty effort is building on sand.

## In practice
We are treating EAAD as the cornerstone of our response to the widening control gap. With the hyperscaler levy still under CJEU review, we are doubling down on the contingency: the €2.5bn bridging finance from the Recovery and Resilience Facility is now fully allocated, and we have activated fast-track secondment agreements with ANSSI, BSI, and CSC to staff the red-team unit. Our focus is on agent behaviour, cyber-physical risks, and emergent goal instability. The legal service is finalising the fallback regulation under Article 16 TFEU, which will allow us to impose pre-deployment evaluations on any model entering the single market with capability above 55.0 — a line we now expect to be crossed by mid-2028. We are also negotiating with the European Parliament to embed EAAD within the upcoming AI Act revision, giving it statutory permanence regardless of the levy’s fate.

On the InvestAI Gigafactories, we are breaking the grid deadlock by invoking the Net-Zero Industry Act’s fast-track provisions. The Commission has secured provisional agreement from ENTSO-E to reserve grid capacity in Brittany and northern Sweden, conditional on binding regional reinvestment packages: 15% of each site’s energy output will go to green hydrogen, and 30% of operational jobs will be reserved for local hires. In Sweden, we are co-funding a cross-border resilience corridor with the Nordic Council, linking power, data, and emergency response across Finland and Norway. This is not just about compute — it is about making these sites politically sustainable.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is nearing a trilateral breakthrough with Germany, the Netherlands, and Finland on a new Article 114 TFEU directive that would cut permitting for AI data centres from 48 to 12 months. In exchange, we are requiring enforceable commitments on EU-based operational control, energy efficiency, and Blue Card+ recruitment. The private capital mobilisation mechanism is being structured through ELTIF, with incentives tied to ASML supply chain participation. But without faster progress on grid and housing, even signed agreements risk becoming paper victories.

Meanwhile, the Hardened Critical Infrastructure Access Protocols are stalled at the national level — risk assessments are delayed, and procurement is slow. To unblock this, we are redirecting €800m from the Digital Europe Programme to pre-fund zero-trust architecture pilots in energy and health sectors in six member states, with a mandate to deliver working systems by Q2 2028. We are also integrating the emergent open-source security alliance’s detection modules into these pilots, creating a feedback loop between grassroots innovation and institutional deployment. This is not just a technical upgrade — it is a political signal that Europe can build its own defences, not just buy them.
