## New measure  
**Measure:** Hardened Critical Infrastructure Access Protocols  
We mandate and fund the replacement of legacy authentication and lateral movement controls in all EU-designated critical infrastructure operators with AI-resistant zero-trust architectures, backed by real-time anomaly detection using sovereign models.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 5  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 10–12 points; reduce systemic vulnerability to AI-optimised cyber intrusions; establish a baseline of active monitoring and automated response across energy, transport, and health sectors  
Applies to: All operators of essential services (OES) and digital service providers (DSPs) under the NIS2 Directive, with enforcement through national cybersecurity agencies and co-funding from the Digital Europe Programme

## Priority  
**Priority:** Establish the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD)  
This measure is now the most urgent because the gap between advancing capability and absent safeguards has become operational: autonomous agents are already working in labs, and the cyber intrusion this turn proves that blind spots in evaluation translate directly into national vulnerability — we cannot build resilience alone while the threat evolves unchecked.

## In practice  
We are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories through the European Investment Bank, which has now conditionally approved €40bn in callable capital for the first two sites — one in Brittany, one in northern Sweden — contingent on final grid reservations from ENTSO-E and resolution of local opposition. To break the deadlock, we are activating the Net-Zero Industry Act’s fast-track provisions and offering binding reinvestment packages: 15% of each site’s long-term energy output will be reserved for regional green hydrogen production, and 30% of operational jobs will be guaranteed to local applicants. The Commission is mediating between Paris and Rennes to finalise the French site, while in Sweden we are working with the Nordic Council to co-fund a cross-border resilience corridor that links data, power, and emergency response.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is being pushed through the Digital Markets and Digital Services Council, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable commitments on EU-based operational control and energy efficiency. We are close to securing a trilateral agreement with Germany, the Netherlands, and Finland to harmonise permitting timelines under a new Article 114 TFEU directive, aiming to cut approval from 48 to 12 months. The private capital mobilisation mechanism is being structured through the European Long-Term Investment Fund (ELTIF), with incentives tied to ASML supply chain participation and Blue Card+ talent recruitment. However, progress remains slow, and without faster grid decisions, even signed agreements will stall on the ground.

On EAAD, we are now treating the levy legal challenge as inevitable and shifting to a contingency funding model: €2.5bn in bridging finance has been secured from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, allowing us to launch recruitment with competitive contracts co-signed by ENISA and national intelligence agencies. We are fast-tracking secondments from France’s ANSSI, Germany’s BSI, and Finland’s CSC to staff the red-team evaluation unit, focusing first on agent behaviour and cyber-physical risks. The legal service is preparing a fallback regulation under Article 16 TFEU (public health and security) to preserve extraterritorial reach even if the levy is struck down. Our immediate goal is to operationalise pre-deployment evaluations before any model above 55.0 enters the single market — a line we expect to be crossed within the next 12 months.
