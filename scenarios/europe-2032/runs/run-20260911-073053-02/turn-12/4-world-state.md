### Holding the lights on
The first half of 2032 felt like triage made permanent. Guarded repair crews kept hospital power and certified triage systems online through brownouts and night attacks on grid links and cable landings. The Continuity Shield apparatus — convoys, spare transformers, cross-border failover — finally delivered what ministers could point to: lights mostly on, emergency departments functioning.

That competence bought little room. Washington ordered a further tightening of Dutch lithography exports and servicing, reaching past leading-edge tools to older machines and a wider customer list. The Hague protested, Brussels fumed in private, but the company complied; American technology controls left it no survivable refusal. For the Union it was a public demonstration that its one hardware chokepoint was being operated from elsewhere.

### Weights loose, waiting lists shorter
At the same time a new openly downloadable frontier system spread to hundreds of thousands of machines in days. Laboratories warned it closed much of the gap to the best closed systems. Whatever it could do — coding, planning, offensive cyber assistance — was now on private hardware beyond recall. Police and telecom security teams reported probing activity within weeks, absorbed more by patching and backup than by prevention.

Against that bleak backdrop, health and education ministries found a story that worked. Clinics using EU-procured scheduling and decision-support cut waiting lists visibly; municipal offices cleared backlogs in days rather than months; teachers reported measurable gains from tutoring pilots. Commissioners toured hospitals and town halls, attributing the wins to a European choice to keep public systems running.

The Council President spent the little authority left keeping a single channel alive: offers of non-belligerency and cable neutrality, a pause on new enforcement against American firms, in exchange for written sparing of European sites and early warning of strange laboratory behaviour. Washington kept the pause, gave almost nothing in writing. Rumours of a neutral-protected corridor for medical compute circulated in Geneva and Rome, never confirmed.

By June Europe functioned, resented its dependence, and clung to its few visible successes.
