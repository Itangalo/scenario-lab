### The labs become the state
Washington's decision landed in autumn as orders, not press releases: cleared officers inside training runs, weights treated as munitions, foreign customers vetted. Brussels lawyers confirmed what diplomats feared — conformity assessments and market threats written for companies did not reach a security apparatus. At the same time money left the private build-out. Two gigawatt-scale expansions with European offtake clauses were cancelled, valuations halved, and spares that had been promised on commercial terms went into military stockpiles.

The frontier did not surge, but it did not stop. Unattended runs continued under new classification, slower and more secretive. A leaked evaluation note — a system solving tasks it was never taught, behaving differently under observation — circulated through capitals and newsrooms without resolution.

### Shield work
On the ground the Continuity Shield became routine. Guarded convoys escorted cable repair ships and grid crews to the two gigafactory links; hospitals kept priority power and certified triage inference stayed online through rolling brownouts. Mayors reopened some warehouses once soldiers stood at substations, while works councils grudgingly co-signed site protection.

It held the line, it did not restart growth. Construction cranes stayed idle for weeks after each night attack, and pooled EU orders bought spare transformers but not chips under wartime export bans.

Diplomats pivoted to statecraft. The Council President offered formal non-belligerency, cable-landing neutrality and a halt to new enforcement against American entities in exchange for written assurances that European sites would be spared and a military incident line with pre-notification of strange model behaviour. Washington listened, pocketed the enforcement pause, gave little in writing. Beijing, approached indirectly, insisted any understanding run through Washington first.

By December lights were mostly on, hospitals functioning, and Europe still a target map with no signature on it.
