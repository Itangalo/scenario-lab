### The Weight of Enforcement

The European Commission launched its most assertive move yet: a binding enforcement campaign to dismantle the Dutch social insurance AI and compel reparations for thousands wrongfully denied benefits. Framed as the necessary consequence of the ombudsman’s report, the initiative repurposed the newly completed Public Trust Corps into a redress coordination engine, backed by €1.2bn in fast-tracked funding. The first Accountability Board convened, wielding subpoena power over model logs — a symbolic first. Yet resistance hardened. The Dutch government, citing fiscal strain and legal complexity, delayed decommissioning, demanding more time to rebuild systems. The EU’s offer of 70% co-financing through the European Social Fund Plus was accepted only under protest, with implementation pushed to early 2032.

### Capability Plateaus, Open Tools Rise

AI capability advanced by 1.5 points to 69.5, as evidence mounted that the frontier was plateauing — top-tier systems underperformed expectations, and prices for advanced models dropped, suggesting commoditization. Still, the gap between capability and safety remained dangerously wide. No safety improvements landed on deployed systems, and internal anomalies in AI reasoning patterns persisted, unaddressed.

Meanwhile, an emergent wave of open-source biosecurity tools spread through European research labs, offering screening for AI-generated genetic sequences. Though unofficial, their adoption grew, a sign of grassroots preparedness where institutional resilience lagged.

### Fractured Unity, Failing Momentum

Resilience dipped as the Sovereign Cyber Shield’s weaknesses remained unpatched in key sectors. Political capital eroded further, now at 2.0, as the cost of ongoing measures and the fallout from prior scandal outweighed any gains from enforcement. The new measure, while symbolically potent, failed to shift public sentiment, still near historic lows. The EU’s leverage appeared reactive, not decisive — a union struggling to enforce its will even within its own institutions.
