  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Restore political agency not through symbolic accountability alone, but by demonstrating that the EU can enforce redress, restructure harmful systems, and reclaim control over AI deployed in public services — starting with the institutions meant to protect the vulnerable.
  - Trigger: the automated_decision_scandal and ombudsman’s report confirming systemic harm in the Dutch social insurance AI
  - Grounds: The scandal has revealed that the core failure is not opacity per se, but the absence of consequence when AI systems violate public trust. Transparency without power to correct harm is ritual, not reform. The current Trust Corps and Accountability Boards are procedural responses to an institutional crisis. To restore agency, we must prove the EU can compel repair — not just investigate, not just report, but dismantle and rebuild. This turn, we pivot from documenting failure to enforcing restitution.
  - Evidence quoted: The ombudsman’s report on the Dutch social insurance AI — which systematically denied benefits using opaque behavioural scoring — ignited a firestorm across the Union. Protests flared in Utrecht and Lyon, uniting around the charge that public institutions had weaponised AI against their own citizens. Trust, already threadbare, snapped. The European Commission responded by reorienting the Public Trust Corps toward redress, not just transparency, launching a Redress Coordination Unit and expanding the AI Incident Dashboard to include systemic bias alerts.
  - Reason: The scandal and report directly expose systemic harm in public AI, undermining the original commitment's focus on transparency alone, and necessitate a shift to enforceable redress to restore trust and political agency.
