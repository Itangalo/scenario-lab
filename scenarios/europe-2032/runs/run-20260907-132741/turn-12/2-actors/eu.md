## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign AI Replacement Programme for Public Services  
Because enforcement has hit the wall of national sovereignty and vanishing political capital, we must pivot from tearing down flawed systems to building trusted alternatives — not just proving what we oppose, but showing what we offer. This is how we restore agency: by making the EU the source of reliable, accountable AI in public life, not just its auditor.

## In practice
We are no longer waiting for permission to act. The failure to enforce decommissioning in the Netherlands — despite legal authority, subpoena powers, and financial incentives — has confirmed a deeper truth: accountability without alternative capacity is coercion without legitimacy. We cannot compel reform unless we can also provide replacement. This turn, we begin constructing that capability.

The **Sovereign AI Replacement Programme** will be led by a new Directorate-General for Public Digital Stewardship, spun up within the Commission and seeded with talent drawn from the now-idle Public Trust Corps. It will contract with EU-based developers to train and deploy small-to-mid-sized models specialized in administrative reasoning, benefit eligibility, and procedural fairness — models that are not frontier-breaking, but are transparent, secure, and fully under public audit. These will run on compute clusters located in Luxembourg and Finland, powered by renewable energy and legally insulated from extraterritorial access. Funding of €2.8bn will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme, the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused margins, and redirected Cohesion Fund allocations, conditional on member states adopting the new systems within two years.

This is not procurement as usual. We are invoking Article 114 TFEU to establish mandatory technical standards for all AI used in public benefit determination: any system must either use the EU sovereign stack or demonstrate equivalent transparency, security, and appeal pathways. For countries like the Netherlands, this offers an off-ramp from conflict: adopt the EU model, and the enforcement pressure lifts. For others, it sets a clear path forward. The first deployment will replace the Dutch social insurance AI by turn 16, with parallel rollouts in Belgian unemployment services and Italian disability assessments. We are not banning private AI — we are outcompeting it with public trust.
