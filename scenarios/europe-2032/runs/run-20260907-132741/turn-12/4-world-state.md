### A Turn of Collapse, Not Reform

The EU attempted a pivot from enforcement to construction with the **Sovereign AI Replacement Programme**, aiming to replace high-risk public AI with transparent, EU-controlled models. But the move came too late and cost too much. With `eu_political_capital` already at 2.0, launching a large measure (-3), continuing another large one (-3), and naming a priority (-1) drove political capital into negative territory. The Dutch government rejected the new off-ramp, calling the mandatory standards an overreach. Several member states signaled non-cooperation, citing budget constraints and sovereignty concerns.

### Capability Leaps, Defences Crumble

A **major cyber incident** struck: automated ransomware crippled regional health registries across three member states. The attack exploited AI-generated zero-days, overwhelming understaffed IT teams. Response was slow, patching inconsistent. Weeks later, a **capability jump** was confirmed — a lab demonstrated AI that could autonomously exploit and propagate through network stacks, marking a qualitative shift in offensive cyber-AI. Defensive tooling lagged years behind.

Simultaneously, **labour displacement** accelerated. Law firms, accounting offices, and public agencies stopped hiring graduates, replacing them with internal AI systems. University career centers reported record lows in job placements. Public anger grew, especially among younger voters.

### Safety and Resilience Erode

No safety improvements landed. On the contrary, anomalies in model reasoning went unaddressed as developers scrambled to keep pace. `ai_safety` dropped sharply. The Sovereign Cyber Shield remained incomplete, and grassroots biosecurity tools were no match for systemic fragility. `resilience` fell further.

Public sentiment worsened, now below 10. Protests erupted outside data centres in Germany and France. Trust in EU-led digital reform evaporated.
