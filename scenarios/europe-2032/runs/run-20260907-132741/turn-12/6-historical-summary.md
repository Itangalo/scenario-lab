The EU's push for digital sovereignty collapsed under its own weight. The Sovereign AI Replacement Programme, launched amid depleted political capital, failed to gain traction as member states rejected centralization and the Dutch refused compliance. A major cyberattack exploiting AI-discovered zero-days exposed systemic vulnerabilities, while a breakthrough in offensive cyber-AI capabilities revealed a dangerous asymmetry between threat and defense. Labour markets frayed as AI displaced entry-level professionals en masse, deepening public discontent. With no progress on safety, resilience eroded further, the Sovereign Cyber Shield remained unfinished, and public sentiment plunged below 10, triggering protests and marking a decisive loss of faith in EU digital governance.
