The commitment remains valid in intent — restoring agency through action — but the path of top-down imposition through procurement and legal compulsion has collapsed. We must now pivot from building sovereign systems *for* member states to proving value *with* them, starting from fragments of trust that still exist.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we no longer assume that action alone restores legitimacy; credibility must be earned in practice, not claimed by office.  
  - Grounds: Acting under uncertainty led to a cascade of unenforceable decisions: the Dutch refusal, the cyber collapse, and the plunge in public sentiment below 10. Speed without capacity has not prevented lateness — it has amplified it. This turn reveals that political capital is not just a budgeting constraint; it is the substrate of enforcement. Without it, even correct judgments become inert or counterproductive. We acted, but were not *received*. The lesson is not to wait, but to act smaller, closer, and in ways that rebuild the perception that the EU delivers rather than demands.

## New measure
**Measure:** Launch Pilot Sovereign Stacks in Willing Member States  
Deploy minimal viable versions of EU-controlled AI for benefit eligibility and administrative appeals in two or three cooperative member states, using existing talent, modular design, and rapid feedback loops to demonstrate reliability and public value.

Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)  
Size: small  
Finishes on turn: 16  
Targeted effect: Increase public_sentiment by 8–12 points by turn 16, stabilize eu_ai_sovereignty at 6+ by proving operational continuity of EU-run AI in real public services, and create anchor cases for future scale  
Applies to: Member states volunteering for early adoption (e.g., Estonia, Finland, Belgium), EU agencies with shared administrative functions, and public developers in the Digital Europe network

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Pilot Sovereign Stacks in Willing Member States  
Because large-scale imposition has failed and political capital is exhausted, we must prove the EU can deliver working, trusted AI — not mandate it. This is how we restore credibility: not through command, but through demonstration in partnership with those who still believe.

## In practice
We are pulling back from universal mandates to focused proof-of-concept. The dream of a single sovereign stack imposed across the Union is over — for now. Instead, we are salvaging the core insight: the EU must be able to run its own AI where it matters most. But this time, we go where we are wanted.

The **Pilot Sovereign Stacks** initiative will launch in Estonia and Finland first, with Belgium joining if its federal structure allows. We will deploy lightweight, interpretable models trained specifically on social benefit rules and procedural fairness, running on secure clusters in Helsinki and Tartu. These are not frontier models — they do not need to be. They must simply work: process claims accurately, flag edge cases, and generate appeal-ready explanations. The software will be open to audit by national parliaments and civil society. Funding of €650 million will come from repurposing unused portions of the canceled Replacement Programme, routed through the Digital Europe Programme with simplified reporting.

This is not a second attempt at centralization. It is a field test of legitimacy. If these pilots deliver faster processing, fewer errors, and greater public trust than incumbent systems — especially those tied to foreign providers — then others will ask to join. We will not compel. We will invite. And we will let success recruit. The Directorate-General for Public Digital Stewardship will remain, but as a service provider, not a regulator. Its staff will embed locally, learn what works, and publish everything. This is how we rebuild: not from the apex down, but from working nodes outward.
