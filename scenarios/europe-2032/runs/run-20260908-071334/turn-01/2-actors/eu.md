## In practice  
We are acting on three fronts this turn. First, we are drafting the Cyber Shield directive under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market legal base to set minimum capabilities for intrusion detection and response across essential services. The Commission’s Joint Research Centre will draft technical annexes by February, drawing on ENISA’s post-incident analysis of the autumn intrusions. We will convene the Energy Council and the Health and Consumer Council in December to align resistance — Germany and Sweden have already signalled openness, but Poland and Hungary resist "new Brussels mandates" without co-financing. We are offering 70% cohesion fund coverage for detection upgrades to secure their buy-in.

Second, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories programme: the first site selection panel meets in January, and we are fast-tracking grid access negotiations with national regulators under the Trans-European Networks for Energy framework. The bottleneck is not land or permits — it is skilled labour and cooling. We are working with EIT Digital to scale training pipelines, but that will take turns, not months.

Third, the Tech sovereignty package is facing headwinds. The European Parliament’s ECON committee has delayed the accelerated-permitting zones proposal, citing environmental review concerns. We are responding with a revised impact assessment showing that without faster deployment, EU compute share will fall below 3% by 2030 — a red line for cohesion. We are also offering member states a carve-out: zones that meet strict sustainability benchmarks get fast-track treatment. This is not ideal, but it keeps the package alive.
