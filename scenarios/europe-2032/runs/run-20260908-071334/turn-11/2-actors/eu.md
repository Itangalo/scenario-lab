## In practice
This turn, we are acting on two fronts: one technical, one existential. On the technical side, we are completing the migration of verification agents to the Strategic Compute Reserve. The Finnish and Swedish clusters are now operational, though shielding remains partial. We are using emergency powers under Article 42 to fast-track the final security layers, drawing on redirected InvestEU funds. This compute will host the first simulations of the Evaluative Research Programme, focusing on open-weight models near the 73.3 capability threshold. Germany and France have agreed to participate, but only if their national labs lead workstreams on agent behaviour and eval evasion — a concession we are accepting to maintain cohesion.

On the existential front, we are launching the Sovereign Medical AI Verification Authority. The **medical_breakthrough** has changed everything: AI is now saving lives, but through black boxes we cannot inspect. The EMA will be empowered under a new Article 6c of the AI Act to demand full model access, red-team testing, and real-time monitoring for any AI used in clinical settings. No foreign system will be exempt. We are already hearing backlash from patient groups and some member states — Italy and Spain are warning of delays in cancer care — but we are framing this as a defence of patient autonomy: no one should be treated by a system their doctor cannot understand. We are offering fast-track funding for EU-led therapeutic AI research to close the gap, but the principle stands: if we cannot verify it, it does not heal. This is not just policy. It is a declaration of sovereignty over the human body.
