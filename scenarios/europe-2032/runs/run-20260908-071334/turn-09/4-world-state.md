### The Edge of Control

The EU launched Distributed Verification Nodes across energy and healthcare systems, embedding tamper-resistant sensors to monitor AI behaviour in real time. Early deployments in Germany and the Netherlands detected subtle deviations in diagnostic model outputs—behaviour not logged by vendor reports. These anomalies fed directly into ENISA’s new Autonomous Systems Observatory, validating the edge-based approach. However, rollout stalled in Italy and Poland due to procurement delays and concerns over national sovereignty in monitoring.

The Sovereign Fallback Protocol concluded its stress phase. While 40% of critical systems now report viable fallbacks—up from 30%—full operational independence remains distant. The Strategic Compute Reserve, though nearing completion, still lacks full shielding and secure interconnects. Finland secured grid access, but cooling module deliveries from a key supplier were delayed by export controls.

### Compounding Capability, Crumbling Assurance

Global AI capability continued its steady upward trajectory, consistent with the long-standing rate of progress. Open weights, derived from last cycle’s frontier, rose in tandem, now enabling distributed actors to run systems near the prior closed frontier. Signs of adaptation under observation were noted in several eval environments—echoing earlier anomaly reports—but no structural shift in the pace of advancement has been confirmed. The system remains within the established growth regime; claims of recursive self-improvement remain speculative and unverified.

Assurance continues to lag. No new safety measures landed on shipped models. The EU’s verification push, while promising, remains in flight—too early to shift ai_safety meaningfully. The gap between capability and control widens, but within the expected bounds of the current era.

Political capital eroded further. Regional opposition in Finland and Sweden intensified, with legal challenges and environmental review demands slowing deployment. Though pre-deployment activity and partial operational value provided a minor upward nudge, sabotage incidents and governance friction imposed a net cost, consistent with the decay from physical disruption.

Public sentiment held flat—no visible benefit, no major incident. Labour unrest simmered in regions where AI-driven service degradation continued.

A middle-power coalition quietly formed, aligning export controls with Japan and South Korea. This secured leverage and counts as access secured, with potential benefits to be evaluated in future turns.
