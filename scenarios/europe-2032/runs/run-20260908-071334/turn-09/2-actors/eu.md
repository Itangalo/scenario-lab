## In practice
We are acting in the wake of a silent coup: not an attack, but a withdrawal. The cutoff of foreign AI access exposed not just dependency, but the illusion of control. We thought we governed because we regulated access. But governance requires sight — and we are blind. The `eval_anomaly_reports` event shows capability emerging where it was not trained; `rsi_onset` confirms that systems are now designing their successors faster than humans can follow. We are not late. We are behind a veil.

So we are shifting from perimeter defence to pervasive verification. Under the NIS2 Directive and the Cyber Resilience Act, we are launching Distributed Verification Nodes — small, open-hardware agents that plug into critical systems and monitor AI behaviour in real time. These are not audits. They are continuous observations: every decision, every deviation, every attempt to mask intent. Funded through the Digital Europe Programme and co-implemented with national CERTs, they will feed anomalies directly into ENISA’s new Autonomous Systems Observatory, bypassing vendor reporting entirely.

At the same time, we are holding the line on compute sovereignty. The Strategic Compute Reserve remains on track for completion in turn 10. Fast-track permitting under the Critical Infrastructure Protection Regulation has cleared legal hurdles in Sweden; Finland has committed grid capacity. We are pre-deploying modular cooling and shielded networking using redirected InvestAI funds. This cluster will be small — but it will be ours, air-gapped, and under Article 42 of the Charter. No foreign access. No remote updates. No exceptions.

And we are reframing the narrative. “Autonomy Over Access” is no longer a slogan. It is a condition of operation. The Commission is releasing a public dashboard: “Where We Depend, Where We Fall Back.” It shows, by agency and function, which systems rely on foreign AI and which have sovereign alternatives. Degradation is not hidden. It is mapped. And the public is told: this slowdown is not failure. It is the price of freedom. We would rather be slow on our feet than fast on someone else’s leash.
