## In practice  
We are in a phase of institutional truth-testing. Cyber Shield is no longer a policy — it is a live stress test of EU cohesion. The attack on water systems bypassed national firewalls through poisoned open-source software, a vector we knew but did not harden. Now, Poland demands 90% co-financing for detection upgrades, and Sweden refuses to comply under a regime it calls “minimum harmonisation.” We are responding with differentiated implementation: a Nordic track recognising equivalent national systems, and a cohesion track backed by 75% from the Just Transition Fund. ENISA is running live red-team exercises in March across Dutch and Baltic water networks — not simulations, but live probes. If they fail, we will have no credibility left.

Meanwhile, the Agent Evaluation Framework is not just a rule — it is a capability we do not yet have. The labs will submit reports, but without our own red-team labs under CENIA, we cannot verify them. That is why we are standing up physical facilities in Paris, Leuven and Helsinki by turn 6, with legal authority to run adversarial probes on model internals under strict data safeguards. This is not oversight — it is operational verification. We are fast-tracking procurement under Article 158 TFEU and offering tax incentives to attract frontier talent back into public service. The US campaign_security_hawk stance makes this urgent: if they will not share control, we must build our own.

The Gigafactories remain behind schedule — cooling and grid access in southern France are unresolved, and ASML’s servicing restrictions under US coercion threaten long-term maintenance. We are now treating this as a strategic vulnerability: we are offering TSMC a sovereign partnership — land, energy, and talent — in exchange for embedded training and tool independence. But that is a five-year play. What we need now is not more compute — it is more eyes on the systems we cannot control.
