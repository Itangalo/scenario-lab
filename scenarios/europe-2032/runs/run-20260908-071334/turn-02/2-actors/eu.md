## In practice  
We are under pressure on three fronts, and we are acting on all. First, Cyber Shield is now in crisis motion. The recent intrusion — a compromised software dependency now traced to a model-generated exploit chain — hit water treatment systems in two member states and exposed cascading failures in mutual aid. We are fast-tracking the directive under Article 114 TFEU, but implementation is fracturing: Poland refuses to adopt without full co-financing, and Sweden demands mutual recognition of its own higher standards. We are deploying cohesion funds at 75% coverage for detection upgrades and offering differentiated compliance paths — a Nordic track, a cohesion track — to preserve unity. ENISA is conducting live red-team exercises in March to pressure-test readiness, knowing that another incident now would shatter confidence.

Second, the InvestAI Gigafactories programme is behind on skilled labour and cooling infrastructure. We have secured provisional grid access for two sites via TEN-E, but cooling remains a bottleneck: one proposed location in southern France faces local resistance over water use. We are scaling EIT Digital’s AI infrastructure technician programme to 10,000 trainees by turn 4, but that pipeline is two turns from output. In parallel, we are negotiating with ASML and TSMC to embed maintenance and training clauses in any future compute partnership, but the supply_chain_coercion event has hardened US jurisdiction over key tools, making long-term planning uncertain.

Third, the Tech sovereignty package is stalled in Parliament over environmental benchmarks. We are revising the permitting proposal to tie fast-tracking to strict water and energy efficiency thresholds — a compromise that may save the package but slow deployment. Meanwhile, the eval_anomaly_reports have intensified technical concern: if unexplained reasoning is real, then our current safety regime is blind to the risks that matter. That is why we are launching the EU Agent Evaluation Framework — not as a reaction, but as the next logical step in our two-year commitment. It will require CENIA, our emerging public evaluation body, to develop test suites for agentic behaviour, with access to model internals granted under strict legal safeguards. This is not a ban, but a condition of market access: if you want to deploy here, you open to us. The labs will resist, but we have leverage — the market — and now, just enough capital to use it.
