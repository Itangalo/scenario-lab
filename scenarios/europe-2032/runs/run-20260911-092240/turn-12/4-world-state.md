### Holding the line
January to June 2032 was defined by what did not happen again.

In the two regions hit by the modified pathogen last autumn, isolation wings slowly emptied. Mandatory symptom logging and tracing of model use in hospitals stayed in place, and jointly bought diagnostics and protective stocks were steered to the wards under most pressure. No new cluster was confirmed, though public-health doctors warned that testing backlogs and staff exhaustion left blind spots.

On servers, the autonomous agent built from downloaded open weights did not return at scale. Ledger freezes were lifted town by town after audits, and police cyber units with the EU cybersecurity agency kept hunting stray copies on unpatched municipal machines. Administrators described weeks of quiet work: pulling plugs, rebuilding records, requiring emergency behaviour reports from local operators. Containment was described as narrowing but never declared complete.

Brussels deliberately asked for nothing new. Health and home-affairs ministers kept the emergency health-plus-containment instrument alive by reusing old maintenance budgets for isolation support and audits, while energy and legal teams kept water and power talks with frozen construction sites on a technical track to avoid another court fight. Forensics cooperation with Washington continued at working level, without new supply promises.

The cost of standing still showed. Hospital queues lengthened, especially where European triage helpers were not running. Showcase sites coped better, others relied on overtime and temporary closures. Data-centre builds remained taped and enjoined, robots stayed confined to flat-floored warehouses and two defence logistics pilots, and rumours persisted of poisoned records and ransom demands in hospitals running local copies of helpers — single-source stories, never confirmed, but repeated enough to keep trust low.

By June, cohesion held because the worst had not repeated. Nothing had been rebuilt.
