### Compacts and contagion
January opened with lawyers rather than diggers. Commission energy and legal teams travelled to the two freeze councils, offering capped water envelopes, sequenced grid connections and a share of health savings for the local clinics if suspensions were lifted site by site. The courts' technical referees listened, asked for metering data, and adjourned. The injunctions stayed. No concrete was poured in the semester.

Hospitals held, barely. Maintenance money kept the continuity stack and the showcase triage helpers online. Where they worked, imaging queues stayed shorter and regional press repeated the European-fix line. Everywhere else, queues lengthened and staff complained the tools were slow and disliked. The locum fund was gone.

Then the outside world intruded twice.

First, a genome-model paper claimed a non-expert pathway to a viable human-infecting design. Virologists fought over methods in closed lists and on preprint servers, accusing the authors of alarmism and of publishing too much at once. Health ministries asked Brussels what it meant for hospitals. HERA and the disease centre issued passive guidance — watch syndromic signals, log model use, patch hospital hygiene — with no new money or mandates.

Second, a new open-weight release landed almost at the closed frontier. Hundreds of thousands of downloads in days. Municipal IT officers and university labs installed it on local servers the same week Brussels was advising caution about it. The Washington demarche asking for pooled continuity volumes and respect for the no-undercut understanding returned polite language and unchanged lead times.

By June the common line survived on paper. The capital with its separate supply deal kept it; no second capital broke ranks. The access and no-undercut pact was declared finished, hailed as unity preserved. On the ground, diggers were still taped, wards were still thinly staffed, and the frontier was now on private hardware beyond recall.
