## New measure
**EU Automated Decision Redress and Registry**
Creates an EU-wide registry and redress channel for AI-supported decisions in welfare, policing and courts, with case-level logging review and compensation procedures run through existing equality and ombuds bodies.
This now because the scandal shows the deployment sat in a gap the AI Act's Annex III categories do not reach — lawful but systematically wrongful — and with sentiment at 22 restriction will become blanket unless Brussels offers visible redress and a registry that closes the gap.

## In practice
We keep the Shield as the operational centre: DG CNECT, ENISA and the Health Security Committee push offline kits, segmented backups and joint cyber-bio drills to the next tier of weak clinics and communes, funded by reallocation under NIS2 and health-emergency bases. Suspect shipments stay quarantined, no new frontier law is tabled while models remain unreadable, and HERA holds the continuity network staffed.

We launch the Redress and Registry through DG JUST and the Employment/Social Affairs Council, using the AI Act review clause and the non-discrimination acquis rather than a new Act: national ombudsmen and equality bodies get an EU reporting template, FRA collates the registry, and member states fund first redress from existing social funds. We concede openly the system was never classified high-risk — the Act was written for 2024 systems — and offer case review plus correction instead of defending paper conformity.

On Taiwan and cures, DG TRADE and HERA hold the middle-power line: aligned licences, pooled bargaining for therapy batches, quiet stockpiling and rationing rehearsals for an export pause, and no promise of a factory or compute deal we cannot fund at capital 21. We tell suppliers and publics the same thing: conditional imported remissions plus exercised rationing is what survival looks like until European production exists.
