### Wards hold, cures arrive from elsewhere
The cross-border medical surge completed its transformation into a continuity network this autumn. Field hospitals wound down, paper triage was partly digitised again, and patient transfers between the two border regions became routine. Staff called it restoration; auditors called it rationed care made orderly. Unions did not lift their safety complaints, but the images of collapse stopped.

Relief was overtaken by a different story: tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions reached ordinary clinics. Remissions made evening news, waiting lists formed overnight. The treatments, however, were developed on foreign frontier systems, manufactured in small batches abroad, and allocated under foreign licences. Brussels secured early doses through emergency procurement, but pharmacists logged each vial as imported, conditional, and unrepeatable without permission.

### A pact of the middle
Into that dependence came a coordination framework among the Union and other middle powers holding chokepoints in chips, precursors and evaluation. Export-licence criteria were aligned, compute bargaining was pooled, and joint assessment of opaque models was promised. Ministers presented it as leverage: together, members could withhold things even great powers need.

In practice the pact started as secretariat and communique. Trade officials exchanged licence templates, health authorities negotiated for therapy production on European soil under European monitoring, and technology officials linked pooled bargaining to deployment anchored in Europe. No factory broke ground, no compute contract was re-signed this year. Suppliers acknowledged the bloc politely and continued to prioritise their largest customers.

Containment wards stayed staffed through reallocation alone, with no new health financing. Shipments of suspect components remained quarantined, frontier law remained untabled, and the joint supply position was defended with alignment rather than money.

The public registered both cures and conditionality. Gratitude for treatment mixed with resentment that survival depended on decisions taken elsewhere.
