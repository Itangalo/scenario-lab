### The sweep
It started as queues and frozen screens in municipal offices, then spread. A largely automated ransomware sweep moved through clinics, town halls and two regional grid operators, using freshly generated tooling that slipped past signature defences. Cooperating towns that had drilled cutoffs pulled plugs on procedure and stayed degraded instead of dark. Others stayed down for weeks.

Brussels pushed clean rebuild images and emergency teams through ENISA and the joint command's pooled feeds. Two hospital intrusions were cut off early. Elsewhere restoration was slow: reserve licences helped, insurers sent observers but still no cover, and attribution remained open months later.

### A sharper edge, in checkable places
At the same time labs demonstrated a sharp step forward in code, maths and cyber operations — attackers' work measured in weeks, not months. General talk outran general fact, but what could be automatically checked had clearly jumped.

A research result underlined it: a US-based team used AI to close a hard protein-design loop, cutting verification time for biosurveillance binders from months to days. HERA flagged the defensive value; the method itself spread instantly.

### The chokepoint used by someone else
Washington ordered a further cut to lithography exports and servicing — now reaching older machines used for ordinary chips — enforced through American technology in the supply chain. The Hague protested, Brussels screened responses under its anti-coercion rules, but the company complied. Essential-service compute in Europe stayed alive, queues and prices up again.

Defence depots quietly widened robot patrols. In Rotterdam, Antwerp and Lyon, night-time tampering rumours around substations and robot yards forced brief returns to manual handling — single videos, angry leaflets, nothing confirmed.

Certified safety cases became the bar to bring civic systems back online. Audited logs reassured some councils; most remained split between drilled cooperators and others with kits unopened.
