## New measure
**EU Essential Services Shield**
Hardens electricity, ports, water and hospitals against AI-enabled cyber disruption with mandatory segmentation, exercised contingency plans and mutual aid. A preparedness and resilience measure, category 6, because the autumn test-shot showed segmented systems were reachable and detection would not have caught it, and the gate stays open for three turns.

## Priority
EU Essential Services Shield, not the Gigafactories, because the precursor window and the access cut-off mean disruption this winter outranks capacity in 2030.

## In practice
We task ENISA and the new EU-Cyber Shield SOC network with binding hardening orders under the NIS2/Cyber Solidarity Act base, funded by reprogrammed RRF and Connecting Europe money: air-gap verification for transmission TSOs in the two hit member states first, then all, plus offline backup controls and staged-tooling hunts. Energy and Transport Council formations carry it, with ACER and EMSA exercising cross-border failover this turn despite utility resistance over cost — met with co-financing, not exemptions.

The Gigafactories (M1) and tech sovereignty package (M2) continue on their tracks: DG CNECT holds accelerated-permitting zones and grid connections, EIB structures the private €200bn, and we use the ASML coercion to block further servicing concessions without a Union mandate, offering The Hague joint Anti-Coercion cover. Hospitals and ministries cut off from Mythos are bridged via EU-hosted models and procurement derogations, to stop the member-state side-deals becoming permanent defections.
