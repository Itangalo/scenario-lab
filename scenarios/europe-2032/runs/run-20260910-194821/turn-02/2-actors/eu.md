## In practice
We mandate the Commission under the Anti-Coercion Instrument and EU Chips Act base to negotiate aligned servicing-licence terms with Japan, Korea and Taiwan-facing partners, with the Foreign Affairs and Competitiveness Councils carrying the mandate: no further Dutch servicing concessions without Union authorisation, pooled bargaining for volume-licence compute, and joint evaluation capacity that adopts the new interpretability breakthrough for deployed systems. The Hague gets political cover and side-deal capital is folded into the common pool.

The Essential Services Shield (M1-track hardening) continues on its funds: ENISA directs segmentation verification and staged-tooling hunts at hit TSOs first, with ACER exercising failover before next winter, while DG CNECT keeps Gigafactory zones (M1/M2) alive on permitting and grid connections but throttled for chips until the Pact secures supply. Utilities get co-financing, municipalities blocking data centres get water-power efficiency conditions, not exemptions.
