## Priority  
Launch the Emergency Resilience Acceleration Programme (ERAP)  
Because the cyber_major_incident has exposed a fatal gap: we now have sovereign compute and coalition leverage, but our front-line defences are still brittle, and public trust is evaporating — this turn, resilience is the only metric that can move fast enough to stabilise both safety and sentiment.

## In practice  
We are acting under Article 122 TFEU and the enhanced cooperation clause in Article 83(3) of the GDPR, invoking the exceptional circumstances of systemic AI-enabled disruption to critical infrastructure. The Council, in ECOFIN configuration, will adopt a binding decision by qualified majority to activate the ERAP, backed by a €12 billion allocation from the EU Budget and the Recovery and Resilience Facility. This is not optional harmonisation — it is conditionality: member states must implement AI-resistant authentication in health, energy, and civil registry systems by turn 13 or lose Cohesion Fund disbursements. ENISA will lead technical standardisation, with a new mandate to issue emergency directives during active incidents.

The Commission is already negotiating with Siemens, Thales, and a consortium of public cloud providers to pre-position secure, modular AI monitoring stacks that can be deployed within 72 hours of a classified incident. We are fast-tracking procurement under the crisis clause of the Public Procurement Directive, bypassing national tenders for approved vendors. France and the Nordic states are on board; Poland and Hungary have signalled resistance, citing sovereignty concerns. We are offering them co-leadership of the Eastern Flank Resilience Node — a regional command for incident response — in exchange for compliance. This is not about building faster AI. It is about ensuring we still have a functioning state when the next one turns against us.
