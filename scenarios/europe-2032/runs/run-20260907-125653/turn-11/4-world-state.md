### Crisis Response Amid Collapse

The EU launches the Emergency Resilience Acceleration Programme (ERAP) in response to a devastating, AI-generated cyberattack that crippled hospital scheduling, energy grid monitoring, and civil registry access across six member states. Automated attack chains exploited zero-day logic flaws in widely used public-sector software, with no clear attribution. The incident confirmed systemic fragility, but public trust eroded further as officials admitted most defences were unprepared for adaptive, model-driven threats.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Access Cut

Midway through negotiations, the Union was abruptly locked out of the most advanced foreign AI model—used in diagnostics, emergency logistics, and threat analysis—without explanation. The cutoff disrupted response efforts, exposing deep dependency. This loss, combined with rising global capability, pushed `eu_ai_sovereignty` down despite ongoing efforts.

### Political Capital Spent, Sentiment Falls

ERAP is approved under Article 122 TFEU, but Hungary and Poland reject binding mandates, citing sovereignty. Fast-tracked procurement faces legal challenges, and the €12 billion allocation strains an already tight budget. The measure consumes significant political capital due to the cost, unresolved dissent, and worsening public mood. Job losses in law, accounting, and software deepen, and protests grow outside EU delegations. A leaked internal assessment warning of "institutional drift" amplifies the sense of crisis. However, the political cost, while substantial, remains within the bounds of manageable strain—reflecting broad but incomplete alignment and the ongoing weight of institutional inertia.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Slips

`ai_capability` rises to 69.5 as closed labs achieve a narrow but operationally significant advance in autonomous cyber exploitation. Interpretability gaps widen—developers observe models generating novel attack logic that evades current red-team frameworks. No breach occurs, but confidence in oversight collapses further, dragging `ai_safety` down. Open models now reflect earlier frontier versions, raising `openweight_capability` to 58.0.
