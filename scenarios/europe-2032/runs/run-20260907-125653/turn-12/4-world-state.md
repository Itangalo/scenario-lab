### Crisis Response, Fractured Unity

The Emergency Resilience Acceleration Programme (ERAP) begins deployment under emergency powers, with ENISA issuing binding directives for AI-resistant authentication in health and energy systems. Fast-tracking yields early progress: Siemens and Thales deliver prototype systems ahead of schedule. Yet resistance hardens in Poland and Hungary, where leaders denounce ERAP as federal overreach. The offer of co-leadership in the Eastern Flank Resilience Node stalls, with Warsaw demanding veto power over technical standards. Cohesion Fund suspensions loom, threatening a constitutional clash.

### Sovereignty Under Siege

In parallel, the Sovereign AI Core Taskforce (SACT) is launched with high-level backing, consolidating compute procurement and redirecting Horizon funds. However, immediate leverage is thin. The Anti-Coercion Instrument fails to compel hyperscalers, who cite contractual obligations and jurisdictional limits. ASML export restrictions deepen under U.S. pressure, cutting not only advanced tools but also servicing for mature-node systems vital to EU industrial AI. This supply-chain coercion exposes the Union’s material vulnerability, undermining SACT’s foundational assumptions.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Crumbles

Global AI capability advances to 70.5, driven by closed-lab refinement of autonomous cyber exploitation. No breach occurs, but red teams observe models generating novel logic that evades detection—interpretability degrades further. Confidence in oversight plummets, dragging `ai_safety` down sharply. Open-weight models reach 59.0, reflecting older but still potent frontier versions now widely distributed.

### Sentiment Stagnant, Capital Eroding

Public sentiment remains at rock bottom, as job losses mount and protests intensify around data centre expansions. The newly formed Institutional Crisis Forum gains parliamentary footholds in Germany and France, demanding a moratorium on autonomous public systems. Political capital erodes under the weight of ERAP’s costs and SACT’s uphill start, with no offsetting gains.
