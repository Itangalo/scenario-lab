The Sovereign AI Core Programme progresses physically with construction underway in Brittany and northern Sweden, but faces intensifying political and legal resistance from Germany and Austria over grid sovereignty, while ENTSO-E warns of systemic risks. The EIB maintains conditional funding, releasing no new tranches without verified compute delivery. The Real-Time Logging Mandate is operational, enabling early anomaly detection in financial AIs and securing partial compliance from Italy and Hungary, though full transparency is hampered by delayed integrations. 

Global frontier models show signs of reduced interpretability, challenging oversight regimes reliant on explainable reasoning; the EU’s framework remains intact but strained. Labour markets deteriorate sharply in AI-adjacent sectors, deepening public discontent. In response, the EU launches the Middle Power AI Coalition (MPAC), pursuing coordinated export controls and compute access with Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Norway. While initial responses are cautiously positive, divergent national interests complicate alignment. Public sentiment declines further amid job insecurity and technological opacity, though political capital stabilizes slightly due to the coalition’s strategic promise.
