## In practice

We are shifting from building future capability to preventing immediate disintegration. The Institutional Continuity Initiative (ICI) is being launched under Article 42 of the Treaty on European Union (emergency powers in the area of common security and defence), with direct oversight by the European Council. We are deploying ENISA and the European Public Administration Network to secure core legislative workflows, digital authentication for Council and Commission proceedings, and failover capacity for essential parliamentary functions in Brussels, Strasbourg, and national capitals. This includes air-gapped backup systems for lawmaking, emergency quorum protocols, and secure cross-border civil service coordination in the event of paralysis.

We are also initiating an emergency dialogue with Poland and Hungary through the Presidents’ Forum, offering immediate fiscal transfers from the Recovery and Resilience Facility — conditional not on ERAP compliance, but on a mutual commitment to preserve institutional continuity. The offer includes fast-tracked Cohesion Fund disbursements for digital resilience projects of their own design, provided they agree to a binding no-disruption pact: no action that disables EU legislative or executive function during a crisis. This is not a negotiation over standards — it is a plea for survival.

Simultaneously, we are scaling back SACT not as a policy shift but as a fiscal and political necessity. The funds previously allocated to sovereign compute procurement are being redirected to ICI and emergency social support via the European Social Fund. We are no longer pretending we can build up while falling apart. If the state does not survive this turn, no sovereignty agenda will ever recover.
