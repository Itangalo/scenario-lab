The European Union, facing collapsing political capital and public sentiment at 11.0, invokes emergency powers under Article 42 to launch the Institutional Continuity Initiative (ICI), aiming to preserve core governance functions amid AI-driven disruption and internal dissent. Technical work begins on air-gapped legislative backups and emergency quorum protocols, but political fragmentation intensifies as Poland and Hungary reject ICI terms, demanding full veto power over digital standards—a demand Brussels cannot accept—leading to the collapse of emergency dialogue. The Sovereign AI Core Taskforce (SACT) is formally canceled, with funds redirected to ICI and the European Social Fund, yielding no material advancement in sovereign compute. ASML export restrictions now block servicing of mature-node systems, worsening EU dependency. Global AI capability rises to 71.5, fueled by autonomous cyber operations producing undetectable exploit logic, while ai_safety plummets due to deteriorating interpretability and oversight. Open-weight models reach 60.0, enabling broader access to powerful legacy systems. Job losses accelerate across professional sectors, protests against data centre expansions grow, and the Institutional Crisis Forum gains parliamentary traction. ERAP nears completion but delivers no immediate resilience, while ICI consumes dwindling political capital without stabilizing the Union—marking a shift from building sovereignty to institutional survival.
