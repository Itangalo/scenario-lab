### A Fragile Step Forward

Global AI capability advanced to 58.5 as frontier labs integrated interpretability gains into agent training pipelines, enabling longer autonomous runs with reduced oversight. While not yet superhuman, systems now routinely complete complex software refactors and experimental design cycles with only high-level human validation. The open frontier lagged, with the best public models reaching 44.0—still behind, but narrowing the gap as research leaks accelerate.

The EU doubled down on control. The expanded Sovereign AI Assurance Framework now mandates that any model above 55.0 capability trained with EU-subsidized compute must store weights and training logs in auditable EU repositories. This move strengthens safety oversight and anchors critical AI artifacts under European jurisdiction. ENISA launched its 24/7 AI Incident Coordination Cell, piloting with major energy operators, though full integration remains months away.

Industrial momentum picked up: InvestAI Gigafactories entered execution mode, with site development accelerating in Brittany, eastern Germany, and southern Poland. Emergency energy provisions unlocked substation upgrades, and data sovereignty clauses were embedded in disbursement conditions. However, no sovereignty gain was realized yet—construction continues, but no new capacity came online.

U.S. labs operating in Europe expressed concern over audit requirements, warning of compliance burdens. Talks continue, with the EU offering mutual recognition of safety evaluations as a carrot—and Digital Markets Act enforcement as a stick.

Meanwhile, Taiwan tensions simmered, with military drills and shipping disruptions raising geopolitical stakes. No direct AI impact emerged, but supply chain planners began stress-testing compute logistics.

Public sentiment held at 35.0, as retraining failures and white-collar job losses dominated discourse. No immediate benefit from new measures reached citizens.
