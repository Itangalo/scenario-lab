## New measure
**Expand the Sovereign AI Assurance Framework to cover all models above 55.0 capability trained or deployed with EU-subsidized compute, requiring model weights and training logs to be stored in auditable form within EU jurisdiction.**  
`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 4–6 points by ensuring that systems above 55.0 capability are not only monitored in deployment but are subject to pre-deployment audit and post-training verification; strengthen eu_ai_sovereignty by anchoring critical data and model artifacts in EU legal reach  
`Applies to:` frontier developers receiving EU compute subsidies, EU national cloud providers, and any private actor using state-supported infrastructure for training runs above 55.0 capability

## Priority
InvestAI Gigafactories — because the U.S. administration remains in flux and Taiwan tensions are rising, we cannot assume continued access to foreign compute or stable supply chains; physical, legally anchored capacity on EU soil is no longer a long-term ambition but a near-term necessity for both safety and sovereignty

## In practice
We are driving the final integration of the Sovereign AI Assurance Framework into the AI Act’s conformity assessment process, using delegated acts under Article 68b to bind real-time monitoring to certification. ENISA is now staffing a 24/7 AI Incident Coordination Cell, piloting with RTE in France and TenneT in Germany to test live anomaly detection in energy grid AI agents. The expanded measure—requiring training logs and weights for models above 55.0 capability to be stored in EU-secured repositories—will be implemented through the Digital Europe Programme and tied to access to EuroHPC JU compute. We are negotiating with three U.S. labs operating in Ireland and Finland: access to EU compute and data markets will depend on compliance. Resistance is expected, but we are offering mutual recognition of safety evaluations with aligned third countries as a counter-incentive, and we are preparing a fast-track dispute mechanism under the Digital Markets Act framework to enforce it.

On the industrial front, the InvestAI Gigafactories project is now in execution mode. Site development in Brittany, eastern Germany, and southern Poland is accelerating under emergency energy provisions, with the Modernisation Facility releasing €7.2bn for substation upgrades. We are enforcing data sovereignty clauses: no consortium receives final disbursement unless training runs for models above 50.0 capability are legally bound to EU jurisdiction and accessible to ENISA auditors. This is not just about compute—it is about control. The incoming U.S. administration may demand reciprocity, but we will not trade our leverage for promises. If they want access to our market and our talent, they must accept our terms. We are also fast-tracking visa pathways for AI safety engineers through the Blue Card+ scheme, targeting 1,200 new frontier-adjacent specialists by turn 8. Without them, the factories will be empty.
