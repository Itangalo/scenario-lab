## In practice  
We are operationalising the European AI Verification Network this turn, converting the €620 million from the stalled InvestAI programme into sovereign technical muscle. The network is not a paper framework: secure compute enclaves are being provisioned in Berlin, Lyon, and Utrecht under ENISA’s authority, with direct access to the 1,200 engineers placed through the Talent Integration Directive. These are not consultants — they are a standing technical corps with the mandate to demand model weights, run runtime interpretability stacks, and issue public certification rulings. We are using Article 16 TFEU as the legal base, invoked through the Cyber Resilience Act and the AI Act’s high-risk provisions, to compel access from any provider whose systems touch EU critical infrastructure. The automated decision scandal in the Netherlands has made clear that trust cannot be assumed — it must be demonstrated, and independently. The openweight release of a 45.0-capable model, downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in days, proves that containment through export controls or lab cooperation is already obsolete. Verification must now be physical, public, and on our own terms.

At the same time, the emergent symbolic hybrid breakthrough offers a strategic fork: a path toward AI systems that are not merely less dangerous, but fundamentally auditable. We are directing EASA and the EMA to begin pre-standardisation work this quarter, with pilot testing in air traffic coordination in the Nordic corridor and emergency triage systems in three EU hospitals. These are not full deployments — they are controlled validation environments, funded through Horizon Europe’s Resilient Autonomy strand, to test formal verification at scale. The hybrid systems do not match the 62.0 frontier, but they operate without drift under stress, and their reasoning is legible. That is not a compromise — it is a condition of sovereignty. We are not racing to match the frontier if it means surrendering control; we are building a parallel track where capability is bounded by verifiability, and where the EU sets the standard. This is not a research detour. It is the foundation of a sovereign stack — one we can govern, one we can trust, and one we do not have to beg for.
