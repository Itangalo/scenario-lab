### A Shift in the Balance

Global AI capability edged forward to 57.5, as frontier labs achieved a quiet but significant interpretability breakthrough: for the first time, certain internal agent behaviours could be predicted and verified before deployment. This advance was rapidly adopted across leading developers, improving operational confidence—though not yet captured in systemic safety metrics, which depend on institutional adoption and oversight structures not yet in place.

Simultaneously, promising new swarm-detection systems were deployed in pilot form across energy and transport networks, responding to a series of coordinated ransomware probes. While early results suggest a potential shift in the offence-defence balance, the systems are not yet fully operational or widely integrated. No broad resilience gain is registered this turn, as deployment remains partial and dependent on further investment and coordination.

In Washington, election results confirmed a decisive shift in the political landscape, with the incoming administration expected to take a stronger stance on AI governance. However, no policy direction is yet established—the U.S. posture remains formally undetermined until the new administration assumes office next turn—and early signals are too ambiguous to indicate specific constraints on foreign access or cooperation.

Back in Brussels, the Workforce Transition Acceleration Fund completed its final disbursements. Despite mid-course corrections in Stuttgart, Antwerp, and Bologna, early results showed only modest uptake among displaced professionals, and media focus on white-collar erosion kept public sentiment stagnant at 35.0.

The EU announced the **Sovereign AI Assurance Framework**, a large category 1 measure requiring real-time monitoring and verifiable control of high-capability systems in critical sectors. However, as a high-cost initiative with complex enforcement requirements, it cannot take full effect within a single turn. Design and negotiation phases are ongoing; no implementation or compliance has occurred yet. U.S. providers have voiced concerns, but formal resistance cannot yet materialize in the absence of enforcement timelines.

The InvestAI Gigafactories project remains in flight but was not advanced this turn. Internal coordination delays prevented any formal move toward prioritization, and site development continues under existing timelines, with no measurable progress translating into sovereignty gains.
