### A Surge of Displacement, a Shield Under Siege

Concern over accelerating job losses in mid-level professional sectors prompted early planning for a Workforce Transition Acceleration Fund, with draft guidelines circulated to member states and social partners. However, no disbursement occurred this turn. Final design awaits approval on funding sources and alignment with existing EU cohesion instruments, delaying implementation. Political attention split across multiple fronts weakened consensus-building, and the proposal did not advance beyond consultation. Given the strain of maintaining three large concurrent initiatives, no new measure was formally adopted this turn.

The Cyber Shield Certification Regime continued to face resistance. National regulators resisted centralized mandates, and U.S. vendors warned their legacy systems could not meet telemetry standards without costly overhauls. Despite Modernisation Envelope grants, integration delays mounted, especially among municipal utilities. ENISA’s red-teaming hubs stood up in Seville, Vilnius, and Thessaloniki, but staffing relied heavily on secondments, straining national capacities.

AI capability advanced again, reaching 56.5, as frontier labs pushed agentic reasoning and self-improving code generation. Open-weight models remained at 42.5, reflecting sustained capability but no leap from recent closed releases; while knowledge diffusion continues, structural and infrastructural gaps prevent open-weight systems from closing the distance to frontier models. No new safety mechanisms landed on deployed systems; the gap between capability and control widened further.

Resilience ticked up slightly as defensive coordination improved, but systemic fragility remained. Public sentiment dipped as job losses dominated headlines and data centre moratoriums gained political traction. The EU’s political capital held steady, as the strain of managing three large concurrent measures—InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech sovereignty package, and Cyber Shield Certification Regime—was offset by continued legitimacy from crisis response and no additional measure being launched. The named priority (Cyber Shield) absorbed focus, leaving other efforts under-resourced.

Quiet outreach to Switzerland and Norway laid groundwork for extended cyber resilience, but full alignment remained distant. The Union pressed forward—on multiple fronts, yet constrained by capacity—paying the cost in cohesion and delayed response.
