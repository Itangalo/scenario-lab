## Priority  
Cyber Shield Certification Regime — because systemic fragility in critical infrastructure was confirmed by the cyberattack, and democratic integrity is now under demonstrable AI-enabled threat; we must institutionalize oversight before cascading failures become unmanageable, even as new pressures pull us elsewhere.

## In practice  
We are treating the emergent_labour_displacement_wave not as a distant economic transition but as an immediate political and social crisis. Firms across Germany, Belgium, and northern Italy are restructuring around AI systems that handle contract review, audit workflows, and regulatory documentation without supervision — roles once considered safe from automation. Regional governments are overwhelmed. We are deploying the Workforce Transition Acceleration Fund under the European Social Fund+ and REACT-EU, fast-tracking disbursement through national partnership agreements with labour ministries and sectoral councils. The fund expands digital skills bootcamps, scales wage insurance for displaced professionals during retraining, and supports AI-augmented job-matching platforms tailored to mid-career transitions. To prevent fragmentation, we are conditioning access on cross-border coordination via the European Labour Authority, ensuring that reskilling aligns with actual demand in adjacent regions and emerging green and digital sectors.

At the same time, we are doubling down on the Cyber Shield Certification Regime, which faces mounting resistance from national regulators who see it as overreach and from U.S.-based vendors whose legacy systems cannot meet telemetry requirements. We are using the Modernisation Envelope to offer targeted grants for integration support, particularly for municipal utilities and election management bodies with limited IT capacity. In COREPER II, we are linking compliance deadlines to NIS2 enforcement cycles, making clear that continued operation depends on verifiable safeguards. ENISA is standing up three regional red-teaming hubs — in Seville, Vilnius, and Thessaloniki — staffed by seconded experts and equipped with defensive AI tooling developed under the Digital Europe Programme. We are also beginning quiet coordination with Switzerland and Norway to extend de facto coverage beyond formal EU borders, given that infrastructure is interdependent. This is no longer about preventing hypotheticals — it is about surviving the next six months with institutions intact.
