The Cyber Shield Certification Regime faced ongoing resistance from national regulators and U.S. vendors over compliance costs, despite the establishment of ENISA red-teaming hubs in Seville, Vilnius, and Thessaloniki, which relied on overstretched national secondments. No new safety mechanisms were deployed, widening the control gap. Resilience improved marginally through better coordination, but systemic fragility persisted. Public sentiment declined amid job loss concerns and growing support for data centre moratoriums. Political capital stabilized as the EU managed three major initiatives—InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech sovereignty package, and Cyber Shield—without launching new ones, though this stretched resources and delayed progress. Informal talks with Switzerland and Norway began on cyber resilience alignment, but integration remained distant.
