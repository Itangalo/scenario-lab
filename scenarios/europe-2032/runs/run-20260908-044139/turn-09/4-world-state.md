### A Breakthrough, But No Breathing Room

Global AI capability advanced to 61.5 this turn as frontier labs integrated new autonomous workflow engines into commercial platforms, extending AI’s reach into legal discovery, clinical diagnostics, and infrastructure planning. Yet the pace remained steady, not explosive — a continuation of the 60.0 threshold crossing, not a new leap.

A major safety breakthrough emerged: a new method for predicting AI agent behaviour before execution, adopted rapidly by leading labs. For the first time, certain failure modes could be certified absent, not merely argued against. This significantly improved assurance on deployed systems, lifting `ai_safety` to 32.0.

The EU enforced the recently completed Sovereign AI Assurance Framework, issuing non-conformity rulings against two major platforms lacking real-time monitoring. Subsidy redirections and data adequacy pressures were applied, but U.S. labs resisted full jurisdictional compliance. The ALLIANCE posture ensured dialogue, but no operational access was secured.

The AI Safety Talent Integration Directive moved into early implementation. Fast-track recruitment under Blue Card+ has been approved, and site selections for AI Safety Zones in Berlin, Lyon, and Utrecht are complete. However, licensing bottlenecks and housing shortages have delayed actual staffing and cross-border integration. No significant personnel deployment has occurred this turn, and operational impact remains deferred.

The Resilience Corps Pilot Expansion was proposed and approved in principle, with €900 million allocated from the Civil Protection Mechanism. The plan aims to scale the RTE-TenneT grid model to transport, health, and water systems, mandating binding red-teaming and live-failover drills. However, interagency coordination delays and legal harmonization challenges have postponed the launch of binding requirements. Implementation will begin in the next turn, with initial coordination underway.

Public sentiment held at 35.0, as retraining failures and job displacement continued to dominate local narratives. No visible AI benefit emerged to shift the mood.

### Political Capital Under Strain, Sovereignty Advances

The EU advanced enforcement of the Sovereign AI Assurance Framework and continued preparatory work on the AI Safety Talent Integration Directive. The Resilience Corps Pilot Expansion was approved but not launched, avoiding the introduction of a second new measure. Political capital declined to 36.0, reflecting ongoing portfolio costs (−10) partially offset by the proposal bonus (+3), with an additional −1 attributed to growing political pressure from the emergent regional retraining collapse, now visibly undermining public confidence and straining coalition unity. Sovereignty rose to 26.0, consistent with the notepad calculation: +2 from the AI Safety Talent Integration Directive’s progress and no penalty from capability growth (below 2.0), with enforcement actions reinforcing jurisdictional reach. Resilience increased to 49.0 as planning advanced, though full implementation remains pending.
