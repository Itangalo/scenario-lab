### The Plateau and the Push

Global AI capability crept upward to 63.0, as frontier labs delivered incremental gains despite signs of strain. A major release underperformed expectations, and the sharp drop in top-tier model pricing suggested a slowing of the closed frontier. While disputed internally, the market signal was clear: the era of predictable leaps may be fading.

The European Union completed the European AI Verification Network (EUVN), operationalizing secure audit enclaves in Berlin, Lyon, and Utrecht. Backed by the placement of 1,200 AI safety engineers, ENISA now holds legal authority to demand model weights and runtime logs from providers serving critical infrastructure. Preparations for audits are underway, with initial targets identified in medical triage and transport systems.

Simultaneously, the Resilience Corps Pilot Expansion concluded successfully, demonstrating improved threat containment across ten critical sites. This boosted societal resilience to 58.0, reflecting hardened incident response and institutional readiness.

Amid this progress, the EU launched the Sovereign Compute Accelerator—a large category 4 measure aimed at building exascale AI infrastructure on EU soil, co-located with renewable energy hubs. The initiative, named priority, triggered fast-tracked permitting and secured commitments for chip supply and grid access. However, resistance from member states over energy demands required cohesion fund incentives, underscoring the political cost of sovereignty. As a new measure, its effects will unfold over time, with no near-term infrastructure gains.

Public sentiment remained stagnant at 30.0, constrained by ongoing job displacement and skepticism toward AI governance. Political capital remained unchanged at 39.0, as the completion of EUVN and Resilience Corps was offset by the high cost of cohesion measures and unresolved tensions around workforce retraining.

AI safety improved slightly to 34.0, as verification capacity landed within the EU, though systemic opacity in frontier models persists. Openweight capability rose to 48.0, reflecting diffusion of capable models through academic and civic networks.

### A Calculated Bet

The EU is no longer waiting. With the closed frontier showing cracks, it is betting that sovereignty—not speed—will define the next phase. But the machines are not yet running, and the real test lies ahead.
