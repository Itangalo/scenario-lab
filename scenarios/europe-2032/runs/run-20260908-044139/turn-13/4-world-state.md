### The Teeth of Verification

The European AI Verification Network flexed its authority, launching mandatory audits of AI systems in French medical triage and Nordic-Baltic rail coordination. Secure enclaves in Berlin, Lyon, and Utrecht processed model weights and runtime logs under strict legal mandate, marking the first enforcement of real-time transparency in high-risk sectors. Early findings revealed undocumented agent behaviours in one logistics model, prompting a temporary suspension — a symbolic victory for sovereign oversight.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Compute Accelerator advanced site development in Spain, Finland, and Poland, with grid integration and chip supply agreements in place. However, energy strain triggered renewed resistance from regional governments, requiring additional cohesion fund allocations. Progress was tangible but slow — the exascale machines remain months from operation.

### Cracks in the Foundation

Public backlash intensified as protests turned physical. Data centre occupations and damaged grid connections in Germany and Sweden exposed the fragility of infrastructure deployment amid low public trust. Policing the disruptions consumed political bandwidth and resources, reinforcing the perception that AI governance was being imposed, not earned.

The EU launched the Critical Systems Audit Mandate, aiming to institutionalise runtime transparency. Yet immediate compliance was patchy. Providers contested audit scope, and legal challenges loomed, particularly from firms linked to non-EU developers. The Foxglove Initiative’s participants requested exemptions, citing parallel frameworks — a growing sign of fragmentation.

AI capability edged upward as frontier labs consolidated gains, though the pricing drop confirmed a broader plateau. Openweight diffusion continued, with capable models spreading through academic networks, lifting openweight_capability modestly.

Resilience improved slightly due to audit integration, while ai_safety saw a marginal gain from operational verification. But political capital eroded under the weight of multiple large measures, cohesion costs, and unrest. Public sentiment dipped further, battered by job displacement and now by visible infrastructure conflict.
