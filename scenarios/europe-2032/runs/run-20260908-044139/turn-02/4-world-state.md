### The Weight of Crisis

A major AI-driven cyberattack crippled public services in two member states, exploiting self-propagating vulnerabilities in widely used automation tools. The breach, only contained after days of cascading failures, exposed deep systemic fragilities. Simultaneously, a national election in a neighboring democracy was annulled amid credible findings of AI-coordinated disinformation, shaking confidence in democratic resilience.

The EU’s Emergency Resilience Surge concluded, delivering partial defences and justifying a modest resilience gain. However, the cyberattack proved that stopgap measures were insufficient. In response, the Commission launched the Cyber Shield Certification Regime — a sweeping mandate for real-time monitoring and red-teaming of AI systems in critical infrastructure. The move was framed as a necessary institutionalisation of oversight, but met fierce resistance from national operators and foreign vendors, who challenged its scope and feasibility.

### Fractures and Fallout

The political cost was immediate. The portfolio carried three large measures, including the new priority, draining political capital. The cyberattack and election crisis — both originating externally and unmitigated by prior systemic safeguards — further eroded confidence. Public sentiment dipped as media coverage focused on disruption and dependency, with growing unease over AI’s role in undermining democratic and technical infrastructure.

Sovereignty continued to decline. Despite ongoing compute investments, no sovereign capacity came online, and the gap between advancing global AI capability and EU control widened. ai_capability rose by 1.5, nearing the 60 threshold, while openweight_capability inched up slightly, reflecting limited diffusion of offensive tools from prior releases. With no safety mechanisms landing on deployed systems, ai_safety fell further, now dangerously outpaced.
