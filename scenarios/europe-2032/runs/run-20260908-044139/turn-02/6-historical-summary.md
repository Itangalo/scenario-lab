In 2027, a severe AI-driven cyberattack disrupted critical public services in two EU member states, exposing persistent systemic weaknesses despite the concluded Emergency Resilience Surge. The incident, coupled with the annulment of a neighboring democracy’s election due to AI-powered disinformation, undermined confidence in both technical and democratic resilience. In response, the EU launched the Cyber Shield Certification Regime, mandating real-time monitoring and red-teaming for AI systems in critical infrastructure, though it faced strong opposition from national and foreign stakeholders.

Political capital eroded significantly as three major initiatives strained governance capacity, while public sentiment declined amid growing concern over AI-driven disruptions and dependency. Technological sovereignty weakened further, with no sovereign compute capacity operational despite ongoing investments. Global AI capability increased by 1.5, approaching the 60 threshold, while open-weight capability saw minimal growth. No new safety mechanisms were deployed, causing AI safety to deteriorate and widening the gap between advancing capabilities and lagging safeguards.
