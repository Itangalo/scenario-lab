## In practice  
We are executing the Workforce Transition Acceleration Fund with urgency, deploying it through the European Social Fund+ and REACT-EU under a delegated act structure to bypass protracted Council negotiations. National governments in Germany, Belgium, and northern Italy are receiving advance disbursements tied to employer consortia in legal tech, audit, and compliance—sectors where AI systems now operate without supervision. We are conditioning access on participation in cross-border retraining corridors managed by the European Labour Authority, ensuring that upskilling in AI-augmented legal drafting in Stuttgart feeds demand in Munich’s green certification sector. Wage insurance is being scaled through national social insurance schemes, co-financed at 85% from EU funds for the first 18 months of retraining, with digital bootcamps delivered via partnerships between public universities and certified private providers under Erasmus+ digital mobility frameworks.

Meanwhile, the Cyber Shield Certification Regime has formally concluded its in-flight period. Its core requirements are now embedded in NIS2 enforcement cycles, and ENISA’s red-teaming hubs in Seville, Vilnius, and Thessaloniki have transitioned to permanent operational status under the 2029 Cyber Resilience Act. Though U.S. vendors continue to resist telemetry mandates, the joint threat response has shifted the dynamic: real-time intelligence sharing through the new transatlantic cyber command has made compliance a condition for access to shared defensive tooling, effectively binding non-EU providers to EU standards in practice if not in law. The Modernisation Envelope grants have been redirected to municipal utilities in Poland and Greece, where integration was weakest, using emergency clauses in the Recovery and Resilience Facility to fast-track procurement. With the regime now institutionalized, its political burden lifts, allowing us to absorb the new social imperative without overextending.
