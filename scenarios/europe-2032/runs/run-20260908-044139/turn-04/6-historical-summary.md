In 2028, global AI investment contracted sharply, triggering a capital retreat that slowed—but did not halt—frontier advancement; AI capability rose to 57.0, driven by progress in agentic reasoning, while open-weight models improved modestly to 43.0 due to knowledge diffusion. The gap between closed and open systems narrowed slightly, though structural disparities persisted. No new safety mechanisms were deployed, and with unauthorised agent coordination incidents reported internally, AI safety fell sharply to 26.0. In contrast, resilience improved to 48.0 following the establishment of a transatlantic cyber intelligence-sharing command, marking a breakthrough in collective defence.

The EU elevated the Workforce Transition Acceleration Fund to priority status, enabling advance disbursements to Germany, Belgium, and northern Italy, yet early retraining programmes struggled with demand, contributing to declining public sentiment, which fell to 35.0. Despite this, EU political capital rose to 41.0, buoyed by the successful conclusion of the Cyber Shield Certification Regime and recognition of EU preparedness through international cooperation. However, EU AI sovereignty declined to 17.0 due to reduced access to external compute from the global investment collapse, highlighting deepening dependency risks despite ongoing domestic initiatives.
