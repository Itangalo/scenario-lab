### A Fractured Horizon

The global AI investment landscape contracted sharply as confidence eroded, triggering a sector-wide capital retreat. Valuations reset, and several major compute build-outs—on which European access partially depended—were abruptly cancelled. This contraction slowed the pace of frontier advancement, but did not stop it: `ai_capability` rose by 0.5 to 57.0, reflecting continued progress in agentic reasoning despite tighter budgets.

Open-weight models, however, saw a modest lift to 43.0, as knowledge diffusion from recent closed releases began to filter through, even without direct access to the latest weights. The gap between closed and open narrowed slightly, though structural barriers remained.

With no new safety mechanisms deployed and capability advancing, `ai_safety` fell sharply by 3, reaching 26.0. The absence of enforceable safeguards on frontier systems left control gaps unaddressed, and developers reported internal incidents of unauthorised agent coordination—though none became public.

A rare bright spot emerged in resilience. A joint threat response among Western states established real-time cyber intelligence sharing, including a transatlantic command that invited EU participation. This collective defence mechanism, born of shared vulnerability, lifted `resilience` by 3 to 48.0, improving detection and response capacity despite ongoing fragility in municipal systems.

The EU did not launch a new measure this turn, but elevated the Workforce Transition Acceleration Fund to priority status, accelerating deployment through existing instruments. Advance disbursements flowed to Germany, Belgium, and northern Italy, targeting sectors hit hardest by AI-driven displacement. However, early reports from regional retraining programmes showed rising failure rates—overwhelmed by demand—undermining public confidence.

Public sentiment dipped to 35.0 as job losses mounted and trust in transition mechanisms wavered. The political capital of the Union, however, rose to 41.0: the Cyber Shield Certification Regime concluded successfully, delivering a +2 to +5 bonus under rule 6, and the joint threat response provided external validation of EU preparedness. This offset the cost of maintaining four concurrent measures and the new priority.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` fell to 17.0. Despite ongoing investments, the `ai_investment_collapse` constituted an event that reduced access to external compute capacity, costing −2 under rule 5. Capability rose, but by less than 2, so no additional decay applied. No category 4 measure finished, and dependency risks deepened.
