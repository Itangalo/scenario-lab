## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign AI Core Programme  
This is the decisive pivot: the failure of market-led and legally ring-fenced models has proven that sovereignty cannot be outsourced or assumed—it must be operated. With private investment collapsed, legal challenges stalling deployment, and the US shifting toward restriction, we must now build what only the state can—infrastructure that no single member can veto, no foreign power can disable, and no market failure can unravel. This programme is not an alternative to prior efforts; it is their necessary culmination.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the moment of reckoning. The cyber_major_incident—targeting public health IT systems via a poisoned open-source dependency—has exposed the fatal gap between capability and control. The tooling was AI-generated, the blast radius vast, and the response fragmented. ENISA’s automated patching initiative, though advanced, could not activate in time—because it relied on commercial cloud providers who delayed access under force majeure clauses. That ends now. The Sovereign AI Core Programme will deploy hardened microgrids with nuclear and geothermal power in Brittany, northern Sweden, and the Alps, directly tied to sovereign data centres under Article 122 TFEU and co-governed by the Commission and a new Joint Operational Board of member states committed to participation. These sites will host the EAAD’s evaluation cluster, ENISA’s resilience swarms, and a sovereign agent control layer—all running on EU-controlled hardware, with air-gapped fail-safes and fuel stockpiles sufficient for six months of autonomous operation.

We are redirecting €50bn from the expired Tech sovereignty package and €30bn from the InvestAI Gigafactories’ unspent pre-deployment envelope into this core. The EIB is instructed to issue output-linked bonds tied to capability delivery, not construction milestones. Germany and Austria will be offered full transparency and joint oversight on environmental and market impacts, but the Core will proceed without unanimity—this is not a project of convenience, but of survival. Italy and Hungary will be invited to host a secondary node in the Apennines, with guaranteed jobs and technical transfer, but access to EU-wide AI services will now require compliance with real-time logging and auditability—no audit, no access. The EAAD is expanding its mandate to include runtime monitoring of all systems above 55.0 capability operating in critical domains, using interpretability stacks that now allow limited backward tracing of agent-generated code.

Public sentiment remains at 24.0, and the streets are restless. We are launching a direct communication channel from the Core’s command centres to national parliaments and civil society, streaming live (sanitised) feeds of threat detection and response—not to impress, but to prove that the Union can act. This is not about optimism. It is about demonstrating, under fire, that Europe still holds its fate in its hands. If we do not build this Core now, we will not survive the next incident—and when the US administration takes office next turn, we must stand as a power that can negotiate, not beg.
