## In practice  
We are acting under Article 122 TFEU and the Crisis Regulation framework to declare a systemic AI infrastructure emergency. This unlocks derogations from standard procurement, environmental assessment, and energy market rules for projects essential to sovereign AI capacity. The Commission, in coordination with ENTSO-E and the European Authority for AI Development (EAAD), is issuing binding deployment orders to the operators of the Brittany clusters and the stalled northern Sweden site. In Brittany, we are fast-tracking security certification and mandating 24/7 operation at full exaF-day capacity, with real-time audit logs fed directly to EAAD. In Sweden, we are overriding local grid constraints by enforcing load-isolation protocols and redirecting reserve power from non-critical industrial users, compensated through an emergency EIB liquidity window.

We are also introducing a legal requirement that all models above 65.0 capability trained on EU soil must have weights stored on hardware physically isolated from foreign networks and subject to EU jurisdiction — no cloud backdoors, no remote access clauses. This applies retroactively to any public-private partnership involving EU funding. Germany and Austria have already signalled conditional acceptance in exchange for guaranteed expansion into Bavaria and Tyrol by turn 14 and permanent seats on the Core Oversight Board. We are offering Spain and Poland parallel access to Brittany overflow capacity in return for accelerated permitting on new low-carbon data zones. The full €12bn remaining from the unspent EIB tranche will be redirected to this effort, conditioned on quarterly delivery reviews by the European Court of Auditors. This is not an invitation to negotiate — it is the activation of contingency powers to prevent irreversible strategic erosion. The time for consultation is over; the time for execution has begun.
