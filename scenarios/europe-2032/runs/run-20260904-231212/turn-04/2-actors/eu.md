## In practice  
We are shifting from containment to foundation-building. The Civilian AI Incident Response Corps has been activated and its platform is now live — a milestone, but one that exposes our fragility. The situational awareness system runs on foreign models. The failover protocols depend on APIs we do not control. When the access cutoff hit, it wasn’t just hospitals that stumbled — it was our own crisis response. That ends now.

The Sovereign AI Testbed is our first step toward operational sovereignty. We will deploy a full-stack, EU-governed AI in three high-impact, high-exposure domains. In healthcare, we partner with the European Health Data Space to run diagnostic support models on secure on-premise inference clusters in Germany, France, and Spain — using de-identified data and audited weights. In energy, we integrate with ENTSO-E to pilot AI-driven load-balancing during peak stress periods, using models trained and hosted entirely within EU jurisdiction. In migration management, we replace cloud-dependent screening tools with a distributed inference network anchored in Luxembourg and Helsinki, under the oversight of Frontex and the European Data Protection Supervisor.

This is not a research project. It is a binding operational commitment, funded through a reallocation of the Tech sovereignty package’s public tranche — €6.2bn redirected from speculative data centre equity to proven deployment capacity. We are working with EuroHPC to repurpose idle exascale time, and with national AI agencies to staff the testbed with vetted engineers. Resistance is real: some member states fear duplication, others dependency on unproven systems. We are answering with mandates — and with guarantees: any public AI used in these domains after 2029 must either be part of the testbed or demonstrate equivalent sovereignty. The era of borrowing capability is over. We build our own, or we lose the right to govern it.
