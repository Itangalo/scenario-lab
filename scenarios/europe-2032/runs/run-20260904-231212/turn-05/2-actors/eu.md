**EU Sovereign Inference Backbone**  
Establish a dedicated, high-availability AI inference network, physically and legally anchored in the EU, to serve as the mandatory deployment layer for all public-sector AI systems above capability threshold 45.0.

## Priority  
**Sovereign AI Testbed for Public Services** — because it is the first live test of EU operational sovereignty in high-stakes domains, and its success or failure will determine whether member states trust EU-level capacity enough to route future deployments through it rather than through foreign clouds or national silos.

## In practice  
We are moving from contingency to control. The hospital funding incident was not an anomaly—it was a preview of what happens when public functions depend on systems we cannot audit, patch, or even access during a crisis. The Sovereign AI Testbed remains our priority this turn because it is where trust must be earned: in real systems, under real load, in real ministries. In Germany, we are working through the Digital Council to align the testbed’s diagnostic models with the EHDS legal framework, offering federal states opt-in pathways with full data sovereignty guarantees. In Spain, we have fast-tracked site approvals in exchange for regional participation in the energy pilot, linking the testbed to ENTSO-E’s stress-testing calendar. With Frontex, we are finalising the architecture for a distributed migration-processing network that runs entirely on EU soil, with oversight from the EDPS and audit trails accessible to national parliaments.

The Tech Sovereignty Package continues to draw private investment, but permitting delays persist—Poland and Hungary are holding out for national carve-outs. We are responding with conditional disbursements: access to the package’s accelerated grid connections now requires binding commitments to host testbed nodes or inference backbone sites. This turn, we also launch the EU Sovereign Inference Backbone, a new large category 4 measure that builds on the testbed’s infrastructure but scales it into a permanent utility. It will consist of hardened, distributed inference clusters co-located with EuroHPC sites and connected via GEANT, governed by a new EU AI Infrastructure Agency proposed under the Digital Europe Programme. All public AI systems above capability 45.0 will be required to run on this backbone by 2030. The cost is high, but the alternative—fragmented, foreign-dependent deployment—is higher. This is not about isolation; it is about ensuring that when the next incident hits, our response does not fail because the API is offline, the model is blocked, or the provider has other priorities. We govern only what we can run.
