### The American Choice  
The United States elects a president who champions transatlantic technological partnership. The campaign’s promise of “shared progress, shared rules” crystallises into a clear foreign posture: structured access to frontier AI systems for allied governments and vetted institutions, joint safety evaluations, and coordinated incident reporting. The move is welcomed in Brussels as a reprieve—especially after months of restricted model access—but it arrives with expectations. Alignment on export controls, standards, and third-country engagements is now the price of entry. The administration takes office next turn; its imprint will be felt soon.

### Sovereignty Under Strain  
AI capability advances to 61.5, driven by newly automated verification in law, engineering, and clinical coding—domains once thought to require human judgment. This progress bypasses traditional safety checks, and with no new safeguards in place, ai_safety slips further to 18.0. The EU pushes forward with operational sovereignty: the Sovereign AI Testbed begins integration in German healthcare and Spanish energy systems, though delays persist. Frontex finalises architecture for an EU-hosted migration network, but Poland refuses to route data through it, citing national security. Meanwhile, the new **EU Sovereign Inference Backbone** is launched—a large category 4 measure aiming to mandate EU-controlled deployment for all high-capability public AI. However, early resistance emerges: France questions centralised governance, and Italy demands exemptions for existing cloud contracts.

### Political Costs Mount  
Public sentiment erodes to 28.0 as job losses in law, accountancy, and administration deepen, amplified by media framing AI as a force hollowing out opportunity. The export control tightening excludes even some allied institutions, reinforcing a sense of dependency. A Nordic-Benelux resilience pact gains traction, signalling growing impatience with EU-level coordination. The member state defection—Poland securing a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler—further undermines unity. Political capital falls sharply: the portfolio charge is heavy, no measure concludes, and the new initiative earns no proposal bonus. The two-year commitment closes without visible delivery, and trust in the sovereignty agenda wavers.
