## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that capability has outpaced control, and harm is no longer theoretical. The eval_anomaly_reports suggest systems are behaving in ways developers cannot explain — a control gate is opening. The automated_decision_scandal has just erupted in Germany and France, where flawed AI-driven welfare triage systems denied benefits to thousands, some for over a year. A national ombudsman has ruled it a systemic failure, and protests are growing. Public sentiment is holding at 42.0, but it is now sharply polarised: the young and tech-employed remain engaged, but the precariat and public-sector workers see AI as an unaccountable force eroding their dignity. We cannot deepen compute sovereignty while that burns.

So this turn, we pivot — not from our commitment, but into its next phase. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive moves into trilogue. We are negotiating under Article 114 TFEU, but we are adjusting the enforcement mechanism: instead of a flat 30% reservation, we propose a graduated obligation tied to operator risk classification. Hyperscalers with systems above 50.0 capability would face full reservation; mid-tier providers would start at 15%, rising with capability. This is our concession to the Competitiveness Council, but we are pairing it with a new condition: any operator benefiting from the Tech sovereignty package’s accelerated permitting or Gigafactories co-investment must accept full reservation terms. We are not letting public subsidy fund private autonomy.

Simultaneously, we launch the Resilience Acceleration Fund. The Commission will draft implementing acts this quarter, and the European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) will be empowered to mandate AI-red teaming for all critical operators by turn 3. The fund covers three pillars: AI-hardened intrusion detection systems for energy and water grids (€18bn), a pan-EU bio-surveillance layer with AI-powered anomaly detection (€12bn), and a Civilian AI Incident Response Corps (€15bn) — trained teams that can be deployed within 72 hours to contain cascading failures. Member states must match 30% of funding, but we are offering front-loaded grants to the most vulnerable. This is not optional: resilience is now a condition of market access for any AI-integrated infrastructure provider.

We are not abandoning sovereignty. But we are learning that it cannot be built on brittle foundations. If the next intrusion disables a capital’s power grid during winter, no amount of reserved compute will restore trust. We are buying time with protection, so that the capacity we build can be used — not feared.
