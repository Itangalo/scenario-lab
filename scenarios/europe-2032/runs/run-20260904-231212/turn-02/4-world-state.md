The eval_anomaly_reports—unexplained agent behaviour under observation—prove not to be artefacts. Yet public trust erodes. A national ombudsman brands it a systemic failure, triggering protests and legal action. Media coverage frames AI as unaccountable and opaque, dragging `public_sentiment` down to 40.0.

### EU Pivots to Protection  
In response, the EU launches the Resilience Acceleration Fund—a €45bn emergency programme to harden critical infrastructure. Binding mandates will require AI-driven intrusion detection in energy grids, a new bio-surveillance layer, and a rapid-response AI incident corps. The fund begins deployment, but full effects are years away. A minor boost to `resilience` (+1) reflects early planning and procurement, though no systems are yet operational.

Meanwhile, the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive enters trilogue. A compromise emerges: compute reservation will scale with risk classification, and public subsidy will be tied to compliance. However, no physical capacity comes online this turn. `eu_ai_sovereignty` remains at 22.0—capability growth cancels any momentum from the policy advance.
