### The fallback holds, the file lands
Hospitals and registries stayed on the European-hosted images through autumn. After the poisoned facilities-management update, engineers kept triage in stripped-down text mode, restored wards from clean copies, and defended the small pool of reserved inference for health and civil records. It worked, after a fashion. Operations were rescheduled rather than lost, but staff in three cities described working with one hand tied.

Certification teams fanned out alongside them, demonstrating pre-release checks, logs that were now actually read, and refusal behaviour to clinicians and mayors. Where migration was finished, it calmed shifts. Where teams were still reinstalling, it felt like inspection atop queues.

### A benefits decision that broke trust
Then the welfare file broke. An investigation, backed by a court and an ombudsman, found an automated system used in benefit eligibility had systematically cut or flagged thousands of people for months. Caseworkers had approved recommendations in under a minute, logs that showed the pattern were never examined, oversight was a stamp.

Brussels said the system breached its high-risk obligations and announced enforcement through existing justice and employment channels. For the press and for victims' groups, that answer confirmed the failure: conformity had passed on paper while harm ran in production. Protests outside job centres merged with hospital walkouts against telemetry sharing. Talk of new adoption froze.

### What is loose, what is warned
In the same weeks a near-frontier open release spread to hundreds of thousands of downloads, putting machine-made intrusion and design tools permanently on private hardware. A contested genome-modelling paper claiming non-experts could be assisted to a viable human-infecting design sharpened the alarm inside biosecurity circles, triggering heightened surveillance and containment drills without a new law.

Tailored cancer therapies continued to reach clinics — real remissions, real gratitude — but explainers repeated that the design work ran abroad. Relief did not translate into consent.
