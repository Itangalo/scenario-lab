### A spring of bad news
The first half of 2030 undid the fragile calm of late 2029. A powerful openly downloadable model appeared within months of the closed frontier and spread to hundreds of thousands of machines in days. Whatever safeguards its makers claimed were now a matter for private hardware.

At almost the same moment, hiring data turned. Law firms, accountants, software houses and customer-service operators stopped replacing juniors. Graduates found closed doors where internships had been. The figures were disputed on television; the empty desks were not.

Then came the scandal. Investigators and an ombudsman found a benefits-assessment system used in several regions had systematically cut or flagged vulnerable claimants. The system had passed its conformity paperwork, logs had been generated and never read, and human review meant a caseworker clearing a screen every forty seconds. Ministers insisted the rulebook was sound and only enforcement had failed. Editorial pages and judges answered that a law that passes paper systems that harm people is discredited either way.

### Cities pull the plug
Trust collapsed locally. After last year's walkouts by doctors and technicians, a wave of councils suspended the European triage and referral helpers pending staffing and accuracy audits. Health ministries kept the emergency disconnect kits on sustainment, and segmented substations and drilled black-start plans kept power and hospitals running, but manual habits returned in unfitted sites.

Brussels tried to hold its single instrument. Employment ministries pushed the workplace pact pilots — retraining paid from reprogrammed social funds, works-council audits of drafting tools, short no-compulsory-redundancy windows in exchange for continued deployment. A few sector deals were signed; most talks dragged. With freight insurance rising on extended naval exercises around Taiwan, guidance memos on supply risk and open-model use changed little on the ground.

By June, the Commission was openly conserving what little room it had, issuing reviews and audits while dependence on foreign models deepened.
