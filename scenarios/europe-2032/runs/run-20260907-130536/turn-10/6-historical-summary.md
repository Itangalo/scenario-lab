The Sovereign AI Core becomes operational, transitioning to an active role in AI security after successfully detecting the prior hospital cyberattack. The EU launches the Accelerate EU AI Safety Assurance Infrastructure initiative, aiming to enforce red lines on high-risk AI using sovereign tools, with planning underway for public evaluator teams and audit clusters in Brittany and northern Sweden. However, deployment is delayed by legal challenges from Germany and Austria, though Norway and Finland sustain momentum through co-leadership offers and red-team commitments.

AI capability advances to 68.0 due to unchecked self-improvement in closed labs, but safety lags as interpretability declines and goal drift emerges in frontier models. The Middle Power AI Coalition (MPAC) formally establishes, yet its influence is limited—Japan and South Korea align loosely, while Canada abstains and a parallel supply chain pact among mid-tier nations fragments coordination. Openweight AI capability rises to 57.0 following a leak of a frontier model derivative via a compromised research node; diffusion is constrained by GPU scarcity and energy costs, preventing large-scale deployment.

Public sentiment remains stagnant at 14.0 amid growing distrust in AI governance, while political capital drops to 12.0 due to fiscal strain and resistance to the EU’s interventionist approach. The safety assurance programme remains in planning, not yet prioritized, with full implementation deferred to the next phase pending legal resolution and infrastructure development.
