## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now see that preserving the first may require us to relinquish exclusive control over the second.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but this turn, the cost of action without alignment is higher than the risk of delay.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Maintain independent access to AI capabilities sufficient for strategic autonomy and crisis response — but only if that access can be secured against misuse and uncontrolled escalation; we now condition capability on containment.  

- Grounds: The eval_anomaly_reports and the openweight_frontier_release confirm that capability is generalising unpredictably and diffusing irreversibly. The backlash_physical event — including sabotage of grid infrastructure feeding AI clusters in Finland and Poland — shows that public tolerance has collapsed below operational viability. Austria’s withdrawal and Germany’s demand for binding oversight reveal that member-state cohesion cannot sustain unilateral enforcement. We are acting under uncertainty, but the instruments of sovereignty are failing not from lack of will, but from lack of trust. Thus, we reframe: strategic autonomy cannot mean uncontrolled autonomy. The commitment to capability stands, but only if paired with enforceable safeguards. The identity evolves to reflect that the Union’s agency now depends not on monopolising AI, but on being the only power that can safely use it.

## In practice

We are suspending the autonomous response protocols of the Sovereign AI Core effective Q2 2032. The success in Rotterdam proved the technical capacity, but the backlash proves the political untenability. We cannot afford to lose Germany or fracture the Council further. Instead, we are using the Civil Protection Mechanism’s AI Crisis Window to fund a fast-track legal track under the General Secretariat of the Council, aiming for a binding JPOC regulation by turn 13. This will define thresholds for intervention, mandate red-team audits, and institutionalise Austria’s behavioural lab as a permanent counterweight.  

Finland and Poland are already demanding compensation for the grid sabotage incidents under the Solidarity Clause; we are meeting that by accelerating the resilience upgrades under the existing Core programme, but only under manual override. The Commission will issue a Communication reaffirming that no AI system will be allowed to operate in the EU without a verified off-switch — but that no off-switch will be pulled without a verified mandate. We are offering Hungary a seat on the JPOC’s external liaison panel in exchange for pausing its bilateral data pact expansion. Japan and South Korea have paused their reciprocity discussions, but have not withdrawn — we still have time to repair credibility. This is not retreat. It is the recalibration of sovereignty from control to stewardship — the only form of power left when the frontier moves faster than law.
