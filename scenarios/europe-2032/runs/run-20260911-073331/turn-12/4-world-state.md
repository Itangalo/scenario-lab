### Clinic power, court papers, and distant exercises
The winter-spring was fought at the clinic door. Under the emergency Energy Council, civil-protection guarded battalions finally closed work at the Atlantic cable landing and the Dresden cleanroom perimeter, while cross-border failover kept wards lit on borrowed power. Triage compute and tailored therapies ran on hourly rationing, with military-guarded priority dispatch. Finance stitched repurposed envelopes to pay escorts and bridge-pay tied to clinic guarantees, and mayors who had banned machines co-signed reopenings in exchange for assured hospital feeders.

It mostly held, but thinly. Musters were slow, two burned grid-connection sites returned only partial capacity, and replacement parts pooled through the middle-power procurement club arrived in trickles. Grid operators added escort requests for neutral-flagged repair shipments after single-source reports of harassment far offshore and convoys held for dual-use inspection. Nothing was confirmed, but insurers noticed: Far East exercises, rising shipping premiums and a diplomatic expulsion pushed charter rates up and made every pending transformer sailing a negotiation.

### A ceramic that could change storage
At Jülich with French partners, using pooled coalition inference, an AI-guided search closed a long-open dendrite-stability problem in solid-electrolyte interfaces, yielding a manufacturable ceramic that doubled cycle life at grid-battery scale. Materials scientists called it a landmark; manufacturers said storage lines could reshape within two years, if parts flowed. Brussels publicised cheaper storage and future factory jobs heavily against a darkening mood.

The mood won. An ombudsman and then a court found a social-insurance triage system had systematically cut disabled and migrant claimants — and that the system had never been classified as high-risk under the AI Act's 2024 categories. Everything done had been lawful. Editorial pages concluded the law itself was badly drawn, written for the systems of 2024 against the deployments of 2032. Enforcement was not discredited; it was irrelevant. Protests at data-centre gates and clinic queues merged, hiring stayed frozen, and queues for bridge-pay outgrew therapy queues.
