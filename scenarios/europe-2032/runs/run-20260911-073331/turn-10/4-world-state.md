### War reaches the grid
In February, American and Chinese forces began striking at each other's technology base. Within weeks fabrication plants, cables, satellite links and data centres were declared legitimate targets. Europe was not belligerent, but it was not distant either: a cable landing in the Atlantic, a cleanroom in Dresden, a relay station in the north went dark or to backup power. Replacement parts stopped arriving. Allocation queues for medical models lengthened overnight.

Brussels declared neutrality-plus-supply: EU sites open to neither military use, offered as neutral hosting and evaluation ground if both sides left them alone. The offer was noted and only partly honoured.

### Open models, closed hiring
At the same time a new openly released model, close to the closed frontier, spread to hundreds of thousands of downloads in days. Graduate hiring froze harder. Law, accountancy, junior software and customer operations simply stopped replacing leavers. Universities reported bridge-pay queues growing faster than therapy queues.

The tailored therapies still arrived, but doses were rationed by the hour and triage compute was put on military-guarded priority dispatch. Keeping hospitals lit became the test of government.

### Fire at the works
The night fires spread from fenced robot pilots to the two gigafactory grid-connection sites. Work halted. Emergency energy ministers agreed a Wartime Energy-Compute Continuity Shield as this turn's single new measure, funded by moving money inside existing envelopes to avoid a veto — ordering guarded repair battalions and cross-border failover for power and data.

Work has only begun: battalions are still mustering, repairs are guarded but incomplete, and failover remains partial and borrowed. Full effect will take at least two turns. The existing middle-power supply club, launched in a prior turn, met urgently under its standing mandate, pooling orders for parts and inference and coaxing the defecting capital back to the table with better pooled terms. Nothing yet forced a foreign lab or vendor to re-route supply.

Mayors who had banned machines now co-signed guarded reopenings in exchange for keeping clinic power on. Works councils patrolled sites they had once blockaded. The lights mostly stayed on, but on someone else's capacity, and everyone knew it.
