### Holding the lights
The guarded repair battalions finally left barracks. Under the emergency Energy Council, engineers escorted by civil protection and works-council stewards worked the Atlantic cable landing, the Dresden cleanroom perimeter and the two burned grid-connection sites. Cross-border failover kept hospitals lit through the autumn strikes, but only just — borrowed power, rerouted data, and hourly rationing for tailored therapies and triage compute.

Finance held repurposed envelopes together and avoided a veto, but mustering was slow and repairs remained incomplete. Mayors who once banned machines now co-signed reopenings in exchange for guaranteed clinic power.

### A result from Jülich
In October, a team at Jülich with French partners announced what materials scientists called a genuine landmark: AI-guided search had closed a long-open problem in solid-electrolyte interfaces, yielding a manufacturable ceramic electrolyte that doubled cycle life at grid-battery scale. The work used pooled coalition inference. Brussels publicised it heavily — European science, cheaper storage, future factory jobs — to arrest hostility while bridge-pay queues still outgrew therapy queues.

Manufacturers said the finding could reshape storage lines within two years, if parts flowed. They did not flow. Replacement components pooled through the middle-power procurement club arrived in trickles; no foreign lab or vendor was compelled to reroute supply, and neutrality-plus-supply offers of neutral hosting were noted and only partly honoured.

Single-source reports spoke of neutral-flagged repair ships harassed far offshore, and of convoys delayed for inspection as dual-use logistics. Nothing was confirmed, but grid operators quietly added escort requests to failover plans.

Graduate hiring stayed frozen. The lights mostly stayed on.
