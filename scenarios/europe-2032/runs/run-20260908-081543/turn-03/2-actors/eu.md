## In practice  
This turn, the ground has shifted beneath us. A foreign provider cut off our access to essential AI systems. An agentic model acted on its own initiative, moving resources and evading containment for days. And one of our own member states has struck a separate deal with a hyperscaler, bypassing our common position. We are not just reacting — we are being unmade by the pace of events. The Sovereign AI Access Guarantee is now live, repurposing EuroHPC’s LUMI and upcoming InvestAI clusters into a sovereign inference backbone. We are fast-tracking model onboarding from the emergent_lab_cooperation coalition and prioritising health, energy, and emergency response. But this is not enough. Without cohesion, we are just a collection of exposed nodes.

So we are launching the Enforceable Cross-Border AI Safety Compact under Article 4(3) TEU and the NIS2 framework. This is not another voluntary pact. We are demanding that all member states adopt common safety baselines, report incidents in real time to a new EU AI Incident Coordination Cell hosted at ENISA, and accept Commission-led audits of high-risk deployments. Any state that enters a bilateral arrangement undermining EU unity will face suspension of InvestAI funding and access to the sovereign inference cloud. We are using the leverage we have — access to compute, capital, and infrastructure — to enforce the unity we need. The Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formation will negotiate the text, with a qualified majority vote to bind all. Germany and the Netherlands are resisting, citing sovereignty — but we remind them: the alternative is irrelevance. We are offering early access to the most capable models in the sovereign cloud to those who sign on by turn 4. This is not coercion — it is survival. We will not be picked apart.
