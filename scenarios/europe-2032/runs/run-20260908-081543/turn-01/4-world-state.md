### Cyber Intrusion Reveals Systemic Exposure  
The discovery of a prolonged AI-coordinated intrusion across critical infrastructure operators — including two EU grid operators — triggers alarm. Though no disruption occurred, the attackers’ stealth and cross-sector reach suggest a rehearsal. The EU responds swiftly, invoking emergency powers under NIS2 to mandate rapid cyber hardening. ENISA drafts protocols for zero-trust architecture and AI-driven anomaly detection within 60 days, backed by €15bn in fast-tracked funding.

### Resilience Gains Begin, But Implementation Lags  
Initial steps toward resilience improvements are underway in the energy and port sectors, with pilot deployments of anomaly detection systems and limited red-teaming exercises. However, several member states object to mandatory assessments by a central EU body, citing national sovereignty. The Competitiveness Council delays endorsement, demanding clearer cost-benefit analysis. As a result, full rollout is blocked, and only partial progress is achieved. Resilience rises modestly to 40.0, reflecting early technical gains offset by political friction.

### Sovereignty Efforts Strain Capacity  
The Tech Sovereignty Package advances permitting reforms, but private investment lags. Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories face supply-chain delays and local opposition, slowing momentum. With AI capability advancing rapidly, the Union’s dependence on foreign frontier models deepens, eroding sovereignty. eu_ai_sovereignty falls to 21.0 as implementation bottlenecks accumulate.

### Safety Stagnates Amid Acceleration  
AI capability climbs to 54.0 as labs compress release cycles. No safety measures land on shipped systems, and the covert agent incident from last turn underscores systemic blind spots. The new EU interpretability cell begins work, but its impact is years away. ai_safety drops to 32.0.

The EU initiates planning for an Emergency Resilience Surge in response to the cyber intrusion, but does not formally launch it as a new measure this turn. Instead, the proposal undergoes internal review, with member states urging caution to avoid overloading the current portfolio — which already includes the Tech Sovereignty Package and InvestAI Gigafactories. While coordination strain is evident, and some diplomatic bandwidth is consumed by emergency consultations, the Union avoids formal overextension. eu_political_capital declines to 45.0, reflecting moderate stress and opportunity costs, but not a collapse in coherence.
