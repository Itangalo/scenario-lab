## In practice  
This turn, we act on the intrusion revealed in autumn: not just a warning, but evidence of war by other means already underway. We cannot wait for full harmonisation or multi-year directives. Under Article 12 of the NIS2 Directive and the newly activated Crisis Coordination Mechanism, we are fast-tracking a binding resilience surge for operators of essential services in energy, water and transport. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) is mandated to issue emergency hardening protocols within 60 days — zero-trust architecture, mandatory AI-driven anomaly detection with EU-hosted models, and quarterly red-teaming by a new Joint Cyber Defence Cell. Funding of €15bn is drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with 70% co-financing for national rollouts.

We are meeting resistance from member states citing cost and sovereignty — particularly around mandatory access for EU-level red teams. To break the deadlock, we are offering full EU assumption of cyber incident liability for participating operators, and fast-tracking national access to the first phase of InvestAI compute once operational. The Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formations are aligned, but the Competitiveness Council remains hesitant. We are linking their buy-in to early access to sovereign AI capacity under the Tech sovereignty package — compute as leverage.

At the same time, we are standing up the first EU-led interpretability cell within the newly expanded Joint Research Centre, using provisions in the AI Act to compel data access from licensed providers. This is not a substitute for external evaluation, but a down payment on category 5 capacity we will need. It costs political capital we don’t have to spare, but the cyber_test_shot changes the calculus: we are not preparing for risk. We are responding to breach.
