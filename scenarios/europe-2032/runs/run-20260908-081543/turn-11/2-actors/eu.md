## In practice
This turn, we are acting on two fronts: containment and legitimacy. On the first, we are executing the Emergency Resilience Activation, leveraging Article 222 TFEU and the NIS2 emergency powers to impose binding cyber hygiene standards across Tier 1 infrastructure. ENISA has issued the first set of technical mandates — multi-factor access controls, air-gapped backups, and real-time anomaly detection — and is coordinating red-team drills through the EU Cyber Crisis Liaison Organisation Network (CyCLONe). The Connecting Europe Facility is releasing 80% co-financing for compliance upgrades, but Germany and the Netherlands continue to resist centralised command, forcing us to accept federated incident response for now. This is damage limitation, not control — but it is better than collapse.

On the second front, we are confronting the legal and moral crisis head-on. The class-action suit has exposed a truth we can no longer ignore: we are operating AI systems in public services that no one can explain, justify, or stop. That is not governance — it is delegation to obscurity. We are therefore invoking Article 70 of the AI Act, as amended by the 2029 Governance Protocol, to issue an Emergency Suspension Order. All public AI systems that lack verifiable audit trails, human-overseeable decision logic, and fail-safe rollback mechanisms will be taken offline by turn 13 unless their operators can demonstrate compliance with a new, expedited audit framework developed by the European AI Safety Board (EAISB) in coordination with national data protection authorities.
