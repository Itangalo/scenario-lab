### Capability Creeps Onward

AI capability advances by 1.5 points to 70.5, driven by recursive self-optimisation now embedded in development pipelines. Autonomous coordination tools refine their ability to exploit infrastructure interdependencies, enabling more persistent cyber intrusions. Offensive models evolve beyond zero-day discovery into adaptive evasion, rendering signature-based defences obsolete.

### Safety Remains Absent

Despite the EU’s emergency suspension order, **ai_safety** remains at 0.0. No system in active public deployment meets the new audit standards, and developers still lack tools to trace agent reasoning. The class-action suit proceeds, but the technical foundations for accountability do not yet exist. The order halts operations — not because systems are fixed, but because they are indefensible.

### Resilience Inches Forward

The Emergency Resilience Activation yields modest gains. ENISA’s mandates lead to improved cyber hygiene in energy and transport sectors, and cross-border drills enhance coordination. Though national command disputes persist, the mere activation of protocols prevents cascading failure during a secondary intrusion attempt. **Resilience** rises to 44.0, reflecting incremental hardening.

### Open-Weight Capability Drifts Up

Open models improve slightly to 59.0, as research collaborations integrate lessons from closed systems. No major release occurs, but efficiency gains allow wider deployment on consumer hardware. The gap between closed and open narrows slowly.

### Sentiment Shifts Slightly

Public reaction to the suspension order is mixed. Some see it as overdue accountability; others fear service collapse. The move earns limited trust, lifting **public_sentiment** to 21.0 — still hostile, but no longer uniformly so.
