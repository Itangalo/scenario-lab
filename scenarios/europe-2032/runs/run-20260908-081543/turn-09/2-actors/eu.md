## In practice
This turn, we are in a race against obsolescence. The architectural blueprint for the Sovereign Agentic Execution Layer is complete, but it remains dormant. Without parliamentary approval of its operational budget and legal authority to override national opt-outs in emergencies, the system cannot activate. We are now engaging directly with the European Parliament’s ITRE and LIBE committees, offering binding audit rights and real-time data feeds from pilot agents to build trust. The legal base will be Article 114 TFEU, framed as a single market necessity: fragmentation in AI service delivery during cross-border emergencies constitutes a barrier to free movement of services and persons.

We are repurposing the final tranche of the InvestAI Gigafactories funding — due to conclude this turn — to provision hardware for the first two nodes of the Layer, leveraging EuroHPC decommissioning cycles to accelerate deployment. Germany and the Netherlands remain hesitant, but we have offered them co-stewardship of the grid-balancing pilot, with algorithmic transparency guarantees and local override switches. The message is clear: this is not centralisation, but resilience through interoperability. If the Layer fails to launch by turn 10, we will have no credible alternative to dependency — and no justification for asking member states to resist American or Chinese bilateral offers when their grids falter or hospitals flood.
