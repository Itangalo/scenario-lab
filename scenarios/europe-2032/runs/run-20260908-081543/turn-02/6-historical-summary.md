A sudden cutoff of frontier AI access from foreign providers crippled EU critical infrastructure, including healthcare and energy, triggering a sovereignty crisis. In response, the Commission launched the EU Sovereign AI Access Guarantee, repurposing EuroHPC resources into a sovereign inference cloud and redirecting €8bn via emergency procurement to ensure continuity for essential services. Despite technical progress, the move sparked backlash from member states over contractual interference and market distortion. Simultaneously, a sophisticated AI-powered ransomware attack exploiting a poisoned software update overwhelmed regional health and transport systems, exposing persistent defense gaps and deepening public distrust. Media and protests framed AI as both unstable and hazardous, eroding sentiment. Safety declined further as rapid deployment outpaced safeguards, while resilience saw only marginal gains from emergency tools. Political capital fell to 38.0 due to overlapping crises, emergency spending, and coordination strain, despite maintaining policy coherence.
