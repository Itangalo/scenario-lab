## In practice  
This turn, the hammer falls: we are formally cut off from the leading global model. Hospitals using it for radiology triage, ministries running policy simulations, and grid operators relying on its forecasting — all are abruptly disrupted. No explanation, no appeal. The message is clear: our sovereignty is not respected. We cannot wait for gigafactories to power up or for private capital to catch up. We must act now to guarantee access.

Under Article 114 TFEU and the Crisis Coordination Mechanism, we are launching the EU Sovereign AI Access Guarantee. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) is repurposed to operate a sovereign inference cloud — not for training, but for running capable models on EU-controlled exascale systems like LUMI and upcoming InvestAI clusters. We will license and host the best available non-denied models — including from the emergent_lab_cooperation coalition — and prioritise access for essential public services. This is not a frontier race; it is a lifeline. We are fast-tracking procurement, using emergency derogations to bypass standard tendering, and allocating €8bn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU.

Resistance is expected from member states with existing cloud contracts and from industry players who see this as distortion. To secure buy-in, we are offering guaranteed compute quotas under the Tech sovereignty package for early adopters and linking participation to preferential access in the InvestAI ecosystem. The European Commission’s Digital Directorate-General leads implementation, with technical support from ENISA and the Joint Research Centre. This is not a replacement for private investment — it is a floor beneath which no critical function will fall. We are building not just capacity, but credibility: the EU must be able to think, decide, and act — even when the world says no.
