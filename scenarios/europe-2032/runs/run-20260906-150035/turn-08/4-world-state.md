### The Fracture Widens

The bioincident’s shadow deepens as investigations confirm the pathogen was designed using widely available AI tools. Public trust in AI governance collapses further, with protests erupting outside data centres and national parliaments. Media coverage frames the crisis as one of *deployment*, not development — a model passed safety checks but was later weaponised, exposing a fatal gap in oversight.

The European Union responds with urgency. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation, initiated in a prior turn, gains momentum as fast-track permitting is tied to binding commitments on auditability and public-use capacity. Germany and Ireland accept joint command over sovereign nodes in exchange for early access. France and Poland resist, and in a move interpreted as defiance, one member state finalises a bilateral deal with a foreign hyperscaler — granting preferential access to its domestic AI infrastructure in exchange for investment. The European Commission brands it a breach of solidarity.

In response to growing systemic risks, senior EU officials begin drafting a potential **Mandatory Resilience Retrofit for Critical AI Systems**, aiming to impose runtime monitoring, agent memory limits, and third-party red-teaming on high-capability AI in critical sectors. ENISA is informally consulted, and early technical discussions begin. However, no formal proposal is advanced this turn. National regulators and energy operators signal strong resistance over cost and operational complexity, and the question of whether to proceed remains unresolved. With the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation still in flight, the EU refrains from launching a second measure, leaving the retrofit in exploratory phase only.

### A Sector in Retreat

Globally, signs of financial strain mount in the AI sector. Several frontier labs downsize, and planned compute expansions are put on hold. Training runs shrink in scale, and investor caution spreads. While no formal collapse has yet been declared, the atmosphere is one of retrenchment. Capability still rises to 62.0, as existing models are refined and repurposed under constrained conditions.

Open-weight models advance only slightly, reaching 47.3, reflecting limited spillover from earlier releases and constrained community resources. The gap between closed and open remains wide, though security circles note growing accessibility of dangerous capabilities.

AI safety declines to 18.0 — a significant drop, but not as severe as originally proposed — as the Evaluation Shield falters and developer compliance with red-teaming wanes, though some baseline safeguards persist. Resilience inches up from early retrofit preparations, but public sentiment drops to 25.0 amid job losses and biofears. Political capital erodes under the weight of overlapping crises and resistance, though not as sharply, reflecting ongoing but strained institutional efforts.
