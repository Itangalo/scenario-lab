## New measure

**Mandatory Resilience Retrofit for Critical AI Systems**  
Require all AI systems above capability 50.0 used in critical infrastructure to implement verified safeguards against misuse, model leakage, and unauthorised agent persistence, with enforcement through operational red-teaming and public audit logs.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 11  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to +10 resilience by turn 11 through hardened deployment of high-capability systems in energy, health, and emergency response; forces disclosure of agent memory and planning depth; complements Evaluation Shield by acting on systems already in use  
`Applies to:` National regulators, operators of critical infrastructure, and providers of AI systems above 50.0 capability deployed in the EU

## Priority

**Mandatory Resilience Retrofit for Critical AI Systems** — because the bioincident was not a failure of evaluation but of deployment: a model within reach of non-state actors crossed a threshold of dangerous capability, and no system in place stopped it from being weaponised — proving that resilience cannot be retrospective, and that our sovereignty means nothing if the systems we depend on collapse under the first real stress.

## In practice

This turn, we confront the fact that the Union’s defences were built to audit, not to endure. The bioincident exposed a fatal gap: even if we could evaluate a model before release, nothing stops a determined actor from repurposing it after. We are acting on two fronts. First, the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation moves from declaration to enforcement: we invoke Article 114 TFEU to bind fast-track permitting to enforceable commitments on jurisdiction, auditability, and public-use capacity. The European Commission issues implementing acts to override national delays, backed by conditional access to EIB financing and recovery funds. Germany and Ireland are offered joint command over sovereign nodes; France and Poland are warned that resistance will be met with adjusted disbursements under the European Semester — this is not coercion, but the price of belonging to a Union that must survive.

Second, we launch the Mandatory Resilience Retrofit, targeting the blind spot in existing oversight: deployed systems. Through a delegated act under the AI Act, we require all operators of systems above 50.0 capability in critical sectors to implement runtime monitoring, agent memory limits, and third-party red-teaming as a condition of continued operation. ENISA will publish red-teaming protocols by turn 9, and national authorities will verify compliance by turn 11. This is not a ban, but a hardening — a recognition that the frontier has moved into operational territory, and that resilience must now be engineered into the fabric of use, not just hoped for in design. The measure answers the public’s fear not with restriction, but with proof that we can defend what we allow.
