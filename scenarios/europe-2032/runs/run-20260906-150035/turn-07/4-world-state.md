### A Biological Wake-Up Call

A real biological incident unfolds this turn: a modified pathogen, designed using AI-assisted protein folding tools, is released in a densely populated urban region. The agent spreads efficiently before detection, causing dozens of casualties and requiring a multi-week containment effort. Investigations point to a non-state actor using commercially accessible models, marking the first confirmed case of AI-enabled bioharm. The incident resets global AI risk debates overnight.

### Evaluation Shield Stalls Under Fire

Despite board appointments and confidential audits, the European Evaluation Shield falters. The ongoing legal challenge at the European Court of Justice gains momentum as member states withhold cooperation, citing sovereignty concerns. Frontier developers, though provisionally engaged, refuse to disclose core agent architectures. No findings are published, and public confidence in oversight plummets.

### Sovereign Compute Mobilisation Launched, Not Yet Effective

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation is formally proposed and declared the EU’s new priority, leveraging Article 114 TFEU and the precedent of SEAL to fast-track financing. Germany and Ireland agree in principle to host sovereign nodes, but France and Poland resist binding commitments, citing energy security and national autonomy. Legal frameworks are being drafted, and site assessments have begun, but no construction has started. The initiative remains in early implementation — far from complete. Because the measure is only beginning, it contributes no immediate boost to sovereignty; its effects will accumulate gradually over future turns.

### Open-Weight Progress Slows

With global attention focused on biosecurity and sovereign infrastructure, open-weight model development sees only modest gains. The increase in `openweight_capability` reflects ongoing incremental improvements, but remains tightly coupled to the broader rise in `ai_capability`. No breakthroughs or open releases shift the landscape this turn.

### Public Trust Crumbles

With the bio incident dominating headlines and job displacement accelerating, public sentiment drops sharply. AI is increasingly framed as a runaway force, poorly governed and socially destabilising. Labour unrest grows in legal and medical sectors, and protests target data centre expansions.

### Political Capital Erodes

Despite finishing the InvestAI Gigafactories, political capital declines. The portfolio charge remains heavy, the new priority adds −1, and the bio incident — unmitigated by existing resilience measures — triggers a large penalty under the political cost of failure. The portfolio now includes: InvestAI Gigafactories (finished), Tech sovereignty package (−3), European Evaluation Shield (−3), Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation (−3), and priority (−1) = total charge of −10. No proposal bonus applies, as the new measure has not yet advanced beyond initial declaration. The modest gain in `ai_capability` and `openweight_capability` does not offset these costs, and no other metric rises justify increases in `ai_safety`, `resilience`, or `eu_ai_sovereignty` this turn.
