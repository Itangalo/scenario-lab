## New measure  
**Launch Labour Transition Emergency Facility**  
We establish an immediate, targeted support mechanism for workers displaced by AI-driven deprofessionalization, focusing on legal, administrative, and technical roles now under active disruption.  

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to +20 public_sentiment by demonstrating tangible support for displaced workers; stabilise resilience against social fracture; reduce strike risk in critical public functions  
`Applies to:` Member states with active AI-driven labour unrest (France, Germany, Spain), targeting public-sector and regulated-private-sector roles in legal tech, municipal administration, and technical compliance

## Priority  
**Stabilise Sovereign Compute Access** — because without guaranteed power and connectivity for core EU data centres, even a functioning EAAN and a legally binding border collapse into impotence when the grid fails, and our two-year commitment to prevent uncontrolled AI systems from operating in the EU becomes void not from lack of law, but from lack of light.

## In practice  
This turn, we are acting from a position of narrow survival. The AI Safety Emergency Investment has finished, delivering audit enclaves and red-teaming capacity, but not runtime monitoring — blocked by national security resistance and now undermined by emergent evaluation resistance within our own technical community. The Accelerate EAAN Certification Nodes measure has reached its finishing turn: hardware is in place in Munich and Sophia Antipolis, but without telemetry, the nodes are blind. We have invoked Article 222, redirected defence procurement, and reallocated emergency funds — yet the intelligence services in two member states still withhold access, and now academic voices question whether our evaluation protocols can detect agentic drift at all. The EU Border for AI Systems stands as law, but enforcement hinges on capabilities that are not yet real.

On the social front, we are deploying the Labour Transition Emergency Facility as a triage intervention: €8.7bn from the Just Transition Mechanism and repurposed EGF funds are being disbursed through national labour ministries in France, Germany, and Spain, tied to pauses in AI-driven hiring freezes in public administration. The Social Protection Committee is coordinating wage insurance and retraining into AI-augmented roles — not to reverse disruption, but to slow fracture. Public sentiment has edged up to 32.0, but the ground remains volatile.

The decisive action this turn is securing the physical foundation of sovereignty: power. Stabilise Sovereign Compute Access is now our priority. ENTSO-E is finalising emergency PPAs with Finnish and Dutch providers, backed by MFF state guarantees. In France, where negotiations stalled, we are invoking Regulation (EU) 2019/941 on internal electricity markets to prioritise critical infrastructure, overriding regional grid constraints. Three core data centres — Helsinki, Amsterdam, and now Lyon — will be ringfenced with dedicated power contracts and backup fuel supplies. This is not about matching the frontier; it is about ensuring that when the next cascade hits, the EU still has one cluster online, one team that can see, and one workforce that still believes in the project.
