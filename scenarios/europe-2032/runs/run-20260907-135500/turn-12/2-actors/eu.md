**Launch Labour Transition Emergency Facility**  
We establish an immediate, targeted support mechanism for workers displaced by AI-driven deprofessionalization, focusing on legal, administrative, and technical roles now under active disruption.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`

## In practice
This turn, we are acting on two fronts: one technical, one social, both existential. On the technical side, the **Accelerate EAAN Certification Nodes** measure is now in its decisive phase. We are invoking the Crisis Regulation Procedure under Article 222 TFEU to override permitting delays in Munich and Sophia Antipolis, directing the European Defence Agency to repurpose secure hardware procurement channels for EAAN node deployment. The Commission is issuing binding directives under NIS2 to compel intelligence agencies to share minimal telemetry for civilian safety oversight, backed by a legal opinion from the Advocate General affirming that operational secrecy cannot shield systemic risk. We are also reallocating €3.2bn from dormant pandemic recovery funds to fast-track GPU imports, bypassing normal procurement cycles through DG GROW’s emergency industrial powers.

On the social front, the collapse in public sentiment to 30.0 and the escalation of strikes in France, Germany, and Spain demand immediate political intervention. The **Launch Labour Transition Emergency Facility** is not a long-term reform but a triage mechanism: we are activating the European Globalisation Adjustment Fund and supplementing it with €8.7bn from the Just Transition Mechanism to fund wage insurance, accelerated retraining in AI-augmented public roles, and mobility allowances for affected workers. This is coordinated through the Social Protection Committee, with conditionality tied to national commitments to pause AI-driven hiring freezes in public administration. At the same time, the **Stabilise Sovereign Compute Access** measure locks down power and connectivity for three core EU data centres — in Finland, France, and the Netherlands — through emergency PPA negotiations led by ENTSO-E and backed by state guarantees under the MFF contingency envelope. This is not about catching the frontier, but about ensuring that when the next crisis hits, the EU still has one cluster online, one team that can see, and one workforce that still believes in the project.
