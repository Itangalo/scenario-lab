## In practice
This turn, we shift from scaling ambition to triage and consolidation. The political capital crisis at 12.0 forces us to prioritise ruthlessly: we are not launching broad new fronts, but defending the integrity of what we have already committed to. The **AI Safety Emergency Investment** nears its final phase — by turn 12, EAAN must deliver functional red-teaming enclaves and weight-audit protocols. We are now deploying emergency staffing waivers under Article 240 TFEU to bypass national vetting delays, and directing the Commission services to centralise hiring through Frontex-style rapid pools. Legal resistance from intelligence agencies over telemetry access is being met with binding directives under the NIS2 framework, asserting that any system operating in EU markets falls under civilian oversight for safety purposes.

The **EU Border for AI Systems** remains our structural anchor, but it is only as strong as the nodes that enforce it. That is why **Accelerate EAAN Certification Nodes** is our priority: we are invoking the Crisis Regulation Procedure to override local permitting hurdles in Munich and Sophia Antipolis, and reallocating €3.2bn from dormant pandemic recovery lines to fund hardware imports outside normal procurement cycles. We are also offering targeted liability shielding under the AI Liability Directive to any lab that submits to pre-deployment evaluation — not as a concession, but as a calibrated incentive to break the deadlock. At the same time, the **ai_investment_collapse** has shattered assumptions about where compute will come from. Our original plans relied on commercial build-outs that are now cancelled. So this turn, we act to insulate core sovereign capacity: the new **Stabilise Sovereign Compute Access** measure locks down existing European data centres through emergency power and connectivity guarantees, funded from contingency lines in the Multiannual Financial Framework. This is not about catching the frontier — not yet — but about ensuring that when the next crisis hits, the EU still has one node online, one cluster running, and one team that can see what the machines are doing.
