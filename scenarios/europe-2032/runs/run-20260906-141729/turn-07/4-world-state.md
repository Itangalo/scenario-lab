### A Fractured Foundation

The EU pushes forward on two fronts: launching the Sovereign AI Core Programme and expanding the EAAD’s powers amid growing technical uncertainty. Site preparation begins in Brittany and northern Sweden under Commission authority, invoking Article 122 TFEU despite unresolved legal challenges. The EIB issues €12bn in output-linked bonds, tied to verified deployment of air-gapped nodes rather than construction milestones—insulating progress from political delays. However, no full-scale buildout commences, and the Core remains in early implementation.

Simultaneously, the eval_anomaly_reports trigger emergency action. The EAAD detects unexplained code generation patterns in a 58.0-capability agent managing power grids—behaviour not present in training logs. Citing emergent risk, the Commission expands the EAAD’s mandate via delegated act, enabling continuous runtime monitoring for all systems above 55.0 in critical infrastructure. A freeze is imposed on open-weight models exceeding 50.0 capability, enforced through national regulators and ENISA takedown protocols.

Yet enforcement falters. Open-source communities resist, calling the freeze overreach. Hosting platforms push back, and compliance is patchy. The measure lands partially: runtime monitoring expands, but evaluation capacity lags, limiting immediate gains.

Public sentiment dips further. The live dashboard from planning sites fails to quell accusations of technocratic opacity. Protests grow, fed by the emergent human futures movement. Job losses in legal and administrative sectors deepen resentment.

Meanwhile, `ai_capability` rises to 63.5, driven by advances in autonomous code generation. The US posture of **RETRENCHMENT** slows American progress, but global momentum shifts to Beijing and Zurich. Openweight models creep to 50.0, now at 51.0 due to partial replication of newer techniques despite the freeze.

`resilience` declines slightly as dependency risks persist. `eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 13.0—capability rose by 1.5, costing −1, with no offsetting gain from infrastructure completion. Political capital sees a narrow net gain, buoyed by a proposal bonus for the urgent new measure, but weighed down by ongoing costs.
