### A Crisis of Control

The EU declares a strategic emergency in response to the cyberattack on health IT systems, traced to AI-generated exploit chains. Invoking Article 122 TFEU, the Commission forces immediate construction of the Sovereign AI Core in Brittany and northern Sweden, overriding national permitting delays. Fast-track contracts are awarded, and €45bn is reallocated from REACT-EU and defunct funds. Yet resistance flares: Hungary refuses participation, citing sovereignty concerns, while Italy hesitates, demanding more concessions. Germany and Sweden accept co-governance, but legal challenges from regional courts slow groundbreakings.

The new measure, though large and urgent, fails to gain full traction. No physical progress occurs this turn—only preparations for construction. The promised €30bn EIB match remains undispersed, pending clarity on revenue guarantees. Air-gapping and audit plans exist only on paper. The Core remains months from any operational capacity.

Meanwhile, the cyber_major_incident deepens public distrust. Hospitals face weeks of disrupted scheduling and record access, with no clear attribution. The automated_decision_scandal erupts in Bavaria, where an AI-driven unemployment benefits system is found to have wrongfully denied thousands of claims. A regional ombudsman confirms systemic bias, triggering outrage. Combined with labour_displacement in law and accountancy, sentiment plummets.

Verification_widens as clinical coding proves machine-verifiable, accelerating AI adoption in medical documentation despite the scandals. Offshore labs push capability forward, now at 65.0, driven by autonomous development loops. Openweight models reach 52.0 through partial replication, aided by leaked training data.

Resilience erodes further—critical systems remain exposed. The EAAD’s runtime monitoring expands in name, but lacks tools to inspect internal agent behaviour. ai_safety drops as oversight gaps widen.

### Fractures Deepen

The member_state_defection materialises: Poland signs a bilateral compute deal with a US hyperscaler, granting preferential access in exchange for green energy credits—undermining the EU’s unified front. This, combined with the failed legitimacy of top-down emergency powers, drains political capital sharply.

The Union acts with urgency—but not unity, nor effectiveness. The Core’s delay becomes a symbol of dysfunction, not resolve.
