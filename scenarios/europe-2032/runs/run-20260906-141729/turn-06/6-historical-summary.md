The EU launches the Sovereign AI Core Programme in response to a major cyberattack on public health systems, exposing critical dependencies on vulnerable open-source and commercial cloud infrastructure. Citing Article 122 TFEU, the Commission seeks to fast-track hardened, microgrid-linked data centres in Brittany, northern Sweden, and the Alps to host EAAD evaluations, ENISA resilience swarms, and sovereign AI controls, backed by an €80bn reallocation and EIB-issued output-linked bonds. Legal challenges from Germany, Austria, Italy, and Hungary delay land acquisition, budget approval, and governance ratification, stalling construction. The Core remains in planning, with no implementation this turn. Emergency Resilience Corridors are elevated in priority, repurposing stalled initiatives, but no new standalone measure is launched. Public trust erodes further amid protests over job losses in legal and administrative sectors, blackouts in peripheral regions, and perceptions of elite technocracy. The EAAD expands runtime monitoring but lacks capacity to audit the growing number of open-weight models. AI capability reaches 62.0 due to advances in autonomous code generation, while open-weight models rise to 50.0, limited by compute and evaluation bottlenecks. The US enacts strict AI restrictions under its new administration, slowing domestic progress and shifting global leadership toward Beijing and Zurich, leaving Europe to navigate a recalibrating landscape without immediate advantage.
