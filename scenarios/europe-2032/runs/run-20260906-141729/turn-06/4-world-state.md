### A Core Forged in Crisis

The EU announces the **Sovereign AI Core Programme**, a decisive bid to reclaim control after a devastating cyberattack cripples public health IT systems across three member states. The breach, enabled by poisoned open-source software with AI-generated obfuscation, spreads rapidly. Commercial cloud providers invoke force majeure, blocking automated patching tools mid-deployment—exposing fatal dependency.

In response, the Commission proposes invoking Article 122 TFEU to bypass unanimity requirements and fast-track the development of hardened microgrid-linked data centres in Brittany, northern Sweden, and the Alps. These would eventually host EAAD evaluations, ENISA resilience swarms, and sovereign agent controls. A proposal to redirect €80bn from expired or stalled initiatives is tabled, and the EIB is asked to prepare issuance frameworks for output-linked bonds. Germany and Austria demand environmental safeguards; Italy and Hungary resist mandatory audit access, threatening legal action.

However, no construction begins this turn. Legal challenges delay land acquisition, and the Council has not yet ratified the Article 122 activation. Governance disputes stall the budget reallocation. While planning advances and runtime monitoring expands, coverage remains partial. Public communication via live (sanitised) feeds begins, yet protests intensify as job losses accelerate in legal and administrative sectors. Media frames the proposed Core as “a fortress for elites” amid rolling blackouts in under-resourced regions.

Meanwhile, `ai_capability` rises to 62.0, driven by autonomous code generation. Open-weight models increase modestly to 50.0 as limited leaks of training recipes enable incremental replication—still constrained by compute access and evaluation bottlenecks. The EAAD lacks capacity to audit them all, but diffusion remains uneven and regionally patchy.

Political capital drains under sustained pressure. The portfolio charge continues without delivery: the Sovereign AI Core Programme is in planning only, not implementation, so no measure finishes to offset costs. Sentiment drops as displacement deepens and trust in state solutions wanes.

The EU does not launch a new measure this turn. Instead, it advances the **Emergency Resilience Corridors**—already active and elevated to priority—by repurposing elements of the stalled InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package into supporting roles. No new standalone initiative is initiated, respecting the limit of one new measure per turn. The Sovereign AI Core remains in formulation, with formal launch expected next turn pending legal and fiscal clearance.

### US_POSTURE: RETRENCHMENT

With the 2028 election outcome now effective, the new US administration enacts sweeping restrictions on frontier AI development, citing national stability. Progress slows domestically, but global momentum continues—now increasingly led from Beijing and Zurich. Europe watches closely, aware that American retrenchment may open space, but offers no immediate relief.
