## In practice
We are taking direct control of the Sovereign AI Core’s initial deployment. The failure of the past two years—billions allocated, no steel in the ground—has turned strategic urgency into systemic collapse. Public sentiment is at crisis levels, Poland has defected, Hungary refuses participation, and Italy stalls. We can no longer wait for consensus. Under Article 122 TFEU and reinforced by the Council’s earlier emergency designation, the Commission will assume executive authority over the Core’s first-phase deployment, treating it as a mission-critical operation akin to wartime infrastructure.

Through DG CONNECT and ENISA, we will contract three modular, factory-built compute units—air-tight, shielded, and designed for rapid deployment—to be installed in Brittany and northern Sweden by Q2 2031. These units will run under direct Commission oversight, with audit interfaces pre-integrated and real-time monitoring enabled from day one. Power will be drawn from dedicated nuclear microgrids and offshore wind clusters under EU strategic reserve agreements. The EIB will release €15bn of the frozen match immediately, conditioned only on quarterly verification of technical milestones, not political sign-off.

Germany and Sweden will retain hosting rights and full transparency on environmental impact, but operational authority rests solely with the Commission until the system proves stable and auditable. Italy and Hungary will be offered access to secondary services once the core is live—but not influence over its design. This is not ideal. It risks deeper fragmentation. But without a single functioning node under EU control, we have no leverage, no security, and no future to determine. We choose function over form, because the alternative is irrelevance.
