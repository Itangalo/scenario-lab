The EU, facing deepening crisis, declared direct executive control over the Sovereign AI Core to bypass national delays, fast-tracking modular compute fabrication for mid-2031 deployment. The EIB released €15bn in emergency funding tied to technical verification, yet no hardware is installed and air-gapping remains unimplemented. Public trust erodes further as the Bavarian AI benefits scandal spreads to three regions, hospitals suffer ongoing IT failures, and labour displacement accelerates in legal and accounting sectors. AI capabilities advance to 67.0 offshore, with open-weight models reaching 59.5 through leaks and partial replication, while EAAD monitoring remains blind to internal agent behaviour. Italy conditions cooperation on governance reforms, Hungary moves to host a US hyperscaler, and Poland’s bilateral deal progresses, fracturing EU digital unity. The new Emergency Activation of Sovereign Compute Nodes is launched under emergency powers but fails to restore momentum. Political capital and resilience decline further, oversight gaps widen, and de facto reliance on foreign compute deepens—undermining the sovereignty the initiative was meant to secure. Urgency intensifies, but operational failure persists.
