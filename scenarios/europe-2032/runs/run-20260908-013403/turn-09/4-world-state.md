### Fallbacks Enforced, Bio-Risks Emerge

The EU formally completes the Emergency Fallback Certification, achieving full compliance in France, Germany, and Italy through direct Commission intervention. Despite legal resistance from Spain and Hungary, cohesion fund suspensions are activated, and emergency task forces certify local analog systems in non-compliant regions. The move asserts central authority but deepens political fractures.

Simultaneously, a major AI-generated ransomware attack cripples municipal services across Belgium, the Netherlands, and northern Italy. The Emergency AI Incident Response Corps deploys within hours, limiting cascading failure. However, detection lag and reactive posture expose enduring fragility. The attack underscores the urgency of fallback systems now being enforced.

In response, the EU launches the Biosecurity Surveillance and Academic Compute Oversight Framework, targeting emerging bio-design activity on public research clusters. The new ENISA-Biohub mandates real-time monitoring for AI-assisted gene editing patterns, with authority to suspend access. While framed as a resilience upgrade, the measure sparks backlash from academic communities, who decry surveillance overreach.

### Capability Rises, Safety Stalls

`ai_capability` increases by +1.5, reflecting closed-lab progress. No safety improvements reach deployed systems; `ai_safety` remains stagnant at 14.0. `openweight_capability` rises to 54.5, as open models diffuse into bioengineering applications.

Resilience jumps to 60.0, boosted by the completed fallback regime (+4) and the new bio-surveillance measure’s partial early effect (+2), with additional gains from the live incident response (+1–3, set to +4). However, political capital erodes: portfolio costs mount, public sentiment drops due to the high-profile cyberattack and perceived overreach, and no legitimacy bonus applies.
