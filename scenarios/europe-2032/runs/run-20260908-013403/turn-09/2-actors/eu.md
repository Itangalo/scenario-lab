## In practice
We are closing the Emergency Fallback Certification this turn — a commitment forged in the hospital ransomware attack of turn 7, and now reaching its final implementation. ENISA has verified that France, Germany, and Italy have fully staffed and tested manual fallbacks for emergency services, power grid coordination, and border control. Spain and Hungary remain non-compliant, but the Court of Justice has fast-tracked our action under Article 260 for failure to fulfill obligations, and cohesion fund suspensions are now legally enforceable. We are not waiting: emergency grants are being redirected to regional authorities willing to bypass national resistance, and Commission task forces are on the ground certifying local capacity. This is no longer a request — it is a condition of continued access to EU funding and infrastructure.

At the same time, the cyber_major_incident has landed: a model-generated ransomware sweep, using AI to chain zero-days across municipal services in Belgium, the Netherlands, and northern Italy. The attack exploited AI-managed IT dependencies now embedded in local government — and our Incident Response Corps is deploying within hours, not days. This is the first live test of our preparedness, and it is exposing both readiness and gaps: the stress tests worked, but our detection lags offensive capability by at least 18 months. We are accelerating fallback certification not because it is convenient, but because we now know — in real time — that when AI fails, we must already have the people, the paper, and the power to run the state without it.

And we are launching a new front: the emergent_open_bio_design_collectives. Open-weight models are being used on EU academic clusters to generate gene circuits and protein binders — not yet pathogens, but moving fast. These systems are outside our regulatory perimeter, hidden in plain sight among legitimate research. Our new measure creates a joint ENISA-Biohub with authority to mandate intrusion-detection systems on all public research compute, require real-time anomaly reporting, and suspend access when bio-design patterns match high-risk profiles. We are working through ERA governance and the Horizon Europe compliance framework to bind national research bodies, and we are offering technical support and funding to ease the burden — but compliance is not optional. This is resilience in a new domain: we cannot secure AI if we do not secure the science it enables.
