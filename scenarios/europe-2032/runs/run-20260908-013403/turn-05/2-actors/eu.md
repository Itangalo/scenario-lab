## In practice  
We are acting on the principle that dependency without fallback is not sovereignty — it is tribute. This turn, we are launching the EU-Wide Resilience Stress Test Regime to subject every AI-dependent public service — from hospital triage to border control — to unannounced, adversarial red-teaming twice a year. These will simulate three scenarios: total withdrawal of foreign-hosted models, targeted sabotage of domestic systems, and silent corruption of outputs due to misaligned reasoning. The European AI Safety Authority will lead, using adversarial models developed in-house and from trusted national labs, with results reported directly to the Commission and the European Parliament. Member states that fail will be required to submit recovery plans within 60 days, with access to emergency funds under Article 122 TFEU — but also under threat of temporary federalisation of critical functions if they do not comply.

At the same time, we are pushing the Frontier Compute Co-Location Mandate through trilogue, using the InvestAI Gigafactories’ reserved capacity as leverage. France and the Netherlands are on board, but Sweden’s concerns about innovation flight are gaining ground in COREPER. We are offering a carve-out for research-only training below 55.0 capability, conditioned on full audit access, to preserve goodwill. The Sovereign Safety Audit Regime and Critical Incident Response Protocol have now formally concluded — their frameworks are law, but their implementation is patchy. Germany has not yet integrated red-teaming into its national cyber command, and Spain’s reporting system remains manual. We are redirecting €3bn from the Digital Europe Programme to close these gaps, but the real test will be whether the new stress tests expose what audits cannot see. Public sentiment remains fragile: with backlash_physical still active and the cyber_major_incident unresolved, we are walking a line between control and collapse. We are not slowing AI — we are learning how to survive it.
