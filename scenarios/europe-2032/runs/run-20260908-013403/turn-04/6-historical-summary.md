The Emergency Resilience Surge concludes after 18 months, cementing binding cyber hardening in critical sectors across the EU, with Germany accepting compromised red-teaming oversight and Spain achieving reporting compliance with EU support. The Critical Incident Response Protocol transitions to live red-teaming using adversarial models from French and Dutch labs, though coordination gaps persist. 

Amid rising systemic threats, the EU launches the Frontier Compute Co-Location Mandate under Article 114 TFEU, requiring high-capability AI models (above 50.0 or 10^25 FLOP/s) to train on EU-anchored infrastructure with real-time monitoring by the European AI Safety Authority, incentivized by fast-track licensing and access to InvestAI Gigafactories. 

Resistance mounts rapidly: a broad political backlash converges around AI restraint, physical protests escalate with data center occupations in Belgium and Ireland and sabotage of an AI cluster’s power substation, injuring two. Public trust plummets as automation in public services is blamed for delays. A major cyber incident—AI-generated ransomware—reveals ongoing fragility despite resilience efforts. 

Globally, AI capability reaches 60.0 via automated exploit generation; open-weight models hit 48.0 as toolkits leak widely, while AI safety declines to 22.0 due to stagnant safeguards. The new mandate faces legal challenges over extraterritoriality, U.S. developer pushback, and divided member state support—France backs the policy, Sweden warns of innovation flight. EU political capital remains strained, and public sentiment drops to 26.0.
