## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_test_shot not as an anomaly but as a signal flare: the frontier has produced tools that can embed, coordinate, and persist across critical infrastructure, and our detection failed not because of negligence but because the playbooks are obsolete. This turn, we invoke Article 122 TFEU to launch an emergency resilience programme, fast-tracking Council and Parliament adoption of binding hardening standards for electricity, water, ports, and health data systems. The European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) will be mandated to issue technical specifications within 90 days, backed by a €30bn emergency fund drawn from the NextGenerationEU recovery instrument, conditioned on member states adopting red-team mandates and real-time intrusion logging.

At the same time, we are pushing the InvestAI Gigafactories through national permitting bottlenecks, using the Tech sovereignty package’s accelerated zones to fast-track grid connections in Finland, Spain, and Poland. The Commission is in talks with ASML and three energy providers to ring-fence 500 megawatts of baseload power for sovereign AI workloads, legally binding output to EU control. Resistance from member states fearing fiscal overreach is being met with sidecar guarantees: any country hosting a Gigafactory site will receive priority access to the resilience fund and co-investment in regional retraining hubs. We are not waiting for consensus on capability — we are building it, while the ground shifts beneath us.
