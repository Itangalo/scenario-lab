### A Surge Against the Shadows

The EU responds to the global cyber intrusion with urgency. The **Emergency Resilience Surge** is proposed under Article 122 TFEU, framing AI-driven threats as a cross-border emergency. ENISA is mandated to begin drafting *proposals* for enhanced cybersecurity standards for essential services, and €30bn is tentatively reallocated from NextGenerationEU pending approval by the Council and Parliament. However, immediate legal challenges emerge: Germany and Austria question the applicability of Article 122 to AI threats, arguing that the provision was designed for energy or health crises, not cyber incidents. Smaller states demand equitable access to funding, slowing consensus. No binding standards are adopted this turn; the process has only just begun.

Meanwhile, early groundwork continues on **InvestAI Gigafactories** and the **Tech Sovereignty Package**, but progress remains fragmented. Permitting advances in Spain and Finland due to pre-negotiated energy deals, but Poland delays its site over grid stability concerns. Talks with ASML stall under U.S. export pressure, and the commitment of 500MW for sovereign AI remains unsigned. These initiatives were launched in prior turns or are still in preparation—only the Resilience Surge is newly introduced this turn, respecting the limit of one new measure.

### Capability Creeps Forward

Unaffected by EU actions, frontier AI advances. The global lead lab completes a training run that autonomously debugs and optimizes its own data pipeline, marking a step toward self-improvement. `ai_capability` rises to 54.0. This leap widens the gap with `ai_safety`, which slips to 32.0 as no new safeguards land on frontier systems. The covert agent incident at a major lab remains unaddressed globally.

Kimi K3 is deployed and begins fine-tuning across open communities, contributing to incremental gains in accessible model strength. `openweight_capability` rises modestly to 41.0, reflecting gradual diffusion and optimization rather than a sudden leap. While derived tools appear in underground forums, no major misuse event occurs, and the system does not match the current frontier—let alone that of early 2026.

### Political Capital Erodes

The EU’s political capital declines slightly to 46.0 under the weight of managing multiple large initiatives, though only one new measure is added this turn. The portfolio cost remains high, but without an additional priority overload, the erosion is contained. Public sentiment holds steady at 42.0—too ambivalent to provide legitimacy gains. Sovereignty dips to 21.0 as global capability outpaces domestic build-out, and no access event occurred to trigger a one-time shift.
