### A Fragile Step Forward

The EU pushes through a narrow advance in resilience, even as trust and sovereignty erode further. The **Emergency AI Incident Response Corps** is formally established, with recruitment underway and joint training scheduled for early next year. ENISA coordinates the framework, drawing personnel from national cyber commands and Europol. However, Spain and Hungary delay ratification, citing concerns over sovereignty and centralisation — a warning that deployment may face political blockades even during emergencies.

Meanwhile, the **Emergency Fallback Certification** gains technical momentum: ENISA publishes standards defining certified non-AI modes, requiring live drills and trained staff. France accelerates its civil service retraining, while Germany and Italy accept matching grants. Yet implementation remains patchy, and public messaging falters — media portray the effort as “preparing for collapse,” deepening anxiety.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme concludes with little visible impact. No frontier model training has migrated to EU soil, and private investment remains frozen. The infrastructure exists, but the capability does not.

A major AI-generated ransomware attack across hospital networks confirms the urgency of these moves. Though contained, it exposes how deeply opaque systems have become, and how unprepared most states are for manual fallback. Recovery takes weeks, undermining confidence in both AI and the state’s ability to manage it.

Despite this, open-weight models see only incremental diffusion. No major leak or release closes the gap with the frontier, which continues advancing.

### Sovereignty Slips, Capital Holds

With `ai_capability` rising by 1.5, and no safety improvements landing on deployed systems, `ai_safety` holds flat. Resilience inches up due to partial progress on preparedness measures. Political capital stabilises thanks to finishing a large measure, though new costs and falling sentiment limit gains.
