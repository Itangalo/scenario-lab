The EU establishes the Emergency AI Incident Response Corps and advances the Emergency Fallback Certification with technical standards and drills, yet Spain and Hungary delay ratification over sovereignty concerns, weakening coordinated deployment. ENISA leads integration of cyber and law enforcement personnel, while France, Germany, and Italy adopt fallback preparedness measures amid worsening public perception. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme concludes without attracting frontier model training or private investment, leaving technological dependence unchanged. A major AI-generated ransomware attack on hospital networks exposes systemic unpreparedness, prolonging recovery and eroding trust in both AI systems and state competence. Open-weight models see minimal progress, failing to close the capability gap as ai_capability rises by 1.5 and ai_safety remains stagnant; resilience improves slightly due to partial preparedness gains, but political capital plateaus amid rising costs and declining public confidence.
