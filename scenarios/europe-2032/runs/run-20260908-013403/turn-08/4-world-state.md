### Resilience Gains, Sovereignty Stalls

The EU completes the **EU-Wide Resilience Stress Test Regime**, conducting unannounced red-team exercises across critical infrastructure in six member states. ENISA identifies alarming fragilities in health, energy, and border systems, triggering binding remediation orders. While the drills improve preparedness, they also confirm systemic brittleness—especially in countries resisting fallback mandates.

The **Emergency Fallback Certification** advances technically, with France, Germany, and Italy expanding trained manual teams. However, Spain and Hungary continue to resist, citing national sovereignty, and the Commission’s use of cohesion fund leverage sparks legal challenges. Public messaging remains fraught: media frame the fallback push as preparing for AI collapse, deepening anxiety rather than reassurance.

In response, the EU launches the **EU Compute Sovereignty Accelerator**, aiming to secure 100,000 high-end accelerators on EU soil by turn 12. The plan combines emergency procurement, grid prioritization, and legal anchoring to prevent remote shutdowns. Negotiations begin with EU hyperscalers under DMA gatekeeper clauses, offering regulatory relief for binding availability commitments. Foreign providers face a new condition: single market access now requires enforceable compute sovereignty. Funding is drawn from unspent Digital Europe funds and SURE emergency borrowing.

Yet early progress is slow. Private hyperscalers resist binding commitments, fearing lock-in and liability. U.S. providers warn of compliance conflicts with home regulations. No new physical capacity comes online this turn, and the political cost is immediate.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stagnates

`ai_capability` rises by +1.5, reflecting steady progress in closed labs. No safety improvements land on deployed systems, and `ai_safety` remains at 14.0—opaque reasoning, unchecked misuse risks, and no interpretability in the field. Open-weight models see minor diffusion, pushing `openweight_capability` to 53.0, but no major leak closes the gap.

Resilience improves to 54.0, boosted by the completed stress test regime (+4). However, the new large measure and priority strain political capital. With three large measures in flight and a named priority, costs mount.
