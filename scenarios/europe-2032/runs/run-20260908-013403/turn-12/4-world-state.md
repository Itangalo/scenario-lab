### Stalled Sovereignty, Mounting Pressures

The EU Compute Sovereignty Accelerator formally concluded this turn, but its legacy is one of unmet ambition: while 38,000 accelerators were contracted, legal disputes over data jurisdiction and grid access prevented any deployment under sovereign control. No new compute capacity came online, leaving the Union critically exposed.

In response, the Emergency Sovereignty Procurement Pact was launched as a priority, aiming to secure binding access to a frontier AI system from a neutral developer in Singapore. Negotiations remain fragile—concerns over enforceability and market retaliation have stalled progress, and no breakthrough was achieved.

Simultaneously, the Commission tightened enforcement of the Mandatory Safety-Through-Fallback regime. Forty percent of Digital Europe AI funding is now contingent on verified compliance, and ENISA rapid audit teams began site visits. A public compliance dashboard is in development, set to launch next turn. Yet resistance grows: research institutions in Germany and France warn of chilling effects, and Hungary formally challenged the funding linkage before the Court of Justice.

Plans for a new initiative—tentatively named the Emergency Fallback Enforcement and Compliance Acceleration—were drafted to strengthen implementation, but due to strained political capital and institutional fatigue, the measure was not formally adopted this turn. Senior officials deferred its launch to assess feasibility and avoid overloading ongoing efforts.

Public sentiment held steady, as resentment over coercive measures was offset by continued appreciation for resilience during recent disruptions. Protests in Madrid and Budapest continued, but did not grow in scale, and media coverage remained divided.

Meanwhile, offensive capabilities advanced. The bio_uplift_findings—a contested but significant study showing AI-assisted design of human-infective organisms—raised alarm within closed biodefense circles, accelerating calls for tighter model controls. However, no corresponding leap in safety assurance occurred.

Despite rising capability, resilience held steady due to prior investments, absorbing the latest wave of disruption without systemic failure. Open-weight capability saw modest gains from incremental replication and optimization of existing public models, but no structural breakthrough offset the widening frontier gap.
