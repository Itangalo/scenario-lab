The EU declares a systemic digital emergency following a coordinated AI-powered ransomware attack across seven member states, crippling critical infrastructure. In response, the Commission invokes Article 122 TFEU to activate the Emergency Resilience Surge, unlocking €15bn in conditional grants and mandating immediate cyber hardening for healthcare, energy, and finance sectors. Legal challenges emerge from Poland over treaty overreach and Italy delays implementation, while uneven adoption persists—Germany resists centralized red-teaming and Spain struggles with reporting requirements. The new Critical Incident Response Protocol establishes 48-hour reporting and live red-teaming obligations, expands ENISA’s powers, and creates a rotating EU-wide response unit, though execution remains fragmented.

The Sovereign Safety Audit Regime is scaled back with concessions to France and Sweden, but consensus stalls as concerns grow over innovation flight. Public trust deteriorates further amid perceptions of institutional unpreparedness, amplified by job losses from automation and the prior social insurance scandal. Globally, AI capabilities advance to 58.0 due to breakthroughs in AI-generated exploit chains, with offensive tools proliferating in criminal networks. Open-weight models reach 44.0 amid partial leaks, while AI safety declines to 24.0 due to lagging safeguards. EU political capital remains low at 41.0, and public sentiment drops further to 34.0.
