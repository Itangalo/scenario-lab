## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure autonomous AI capability to prevent strategic dependency and catastrophic risk, but only through mechanisms that restore and sustain public legitimacy — control, transparency, and enforceable safety — not through deployment at any cost  
`Trigger: cyber_major_incident`  
`Grounds:` The cyber_major_incident, combined with the ongoing fallout from the automated decision scandal, confirms that capability without control is not sovereignty — it is exposure. The attack, generated by model-assisted tooling and landing across public services, reveals that our systems are not only untrusted but unready. We are being hit not by the frontier’s peak, but by its leakage — and we are failing to stop it. This changes the strategic calculus: our two-year commitment stands, but its path must now run through containment and credibility, not just compute and capacity. We cannot claim autonomy if we cannot secure what we build, nor if the public sees it as a threat. The commitment is rephrased to reflect that sovereignty now depends on legitimacy as much as on infrastructure.

## New measure  
**Critical Incident Response Protocol**  
Establish a binding, EU-wide protocol for rapid detection, classification, and coordinated response to AI-generated cyber incidents, with mandatory reporting and live red-teaming of essential service providers.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +12, ai_safety +5 (via faster learning from incidents), public_sentiment +3 (if visibly effective)  
`Applies to:` All essential services in the EU, with enforcement through ENISA and national CSIRTs

## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_major_incident has exposed that our critical services are unprepared for AI-amplified attacks, and without immediate, binding hardening, every other measure risks being overtaken by cascading failure

## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that disrupted hospital scheduling, tax filing, and local government portals across seven member states — as a wake-up call. It did not break the internet, but it broke trust in continuity. The attack exploited known vulnerabilities, but at a speed and scale that overwhelmed human response, and forensic analysis confirms AI-assisted tooling was used to adapt payloads in real time. This is no longer hypothetical. The Emergency Resilience Surge, stalled in legal debate, can no longer wait. This turn, we invoke Article 122 TFEU under the justification of systemic digital emergency, offering a compromise: the hardening requirements will apply immediately to healthcare, energy, and finance, with transport and digital infrastructure following in turn 4. We are fast-tracking adoption by offering member states co-funding from the Recovery and Resilience Facility — up to €15bn in conditional grants — in exchange for binding national implementation plans by March 2028.

At the same time, we are launching the Critical Incident Response Protocol to ensure that when the next attack comes, we learn faster. This builds on ENISA’s draft framework but adds teeth: all essential service providers must report AI-related incidents within 48 hours, and undergo quarterly live red-teaming exercises using adversarial models approved by the European AI Safety Authority. We are allocating €4.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and repurposing 800 cybersecurity FTEs from national agencies into a rotating EU incident pool. For the Sovereign Safety Audit Regime, we are narrowing the initial scope to systems above 50.0 capability or trained on EU compute exceeding 10^25 FLOP/s, to reduce legal friction. We are offering Sweden a joint research exemption for non-deployed models, and France a co-chair role in the audit authority’s technical board. The goal remains: no high-capability system runs in or on the EU without independent scrutiny. And on the industrial front, we are holding InvestAI Gigafactories to its revised terms — no model above threshold capability may operate on EU soil without audit clearance. Sovereignty is not just about who owns the chips. It is about who gets to say what runs on them.
