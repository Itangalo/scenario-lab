The EU enters a crisis-driven phase, launching the Emergency Fallback Certification for Critical AI Systems to counter growing AI illegibility and operational failures, though resistance in Spain and Hungary delays implementation amid fears of systemic collapse. A major cyber incident—model-generated ransomware—cripples hospital networks across three member states, exposing vulnerabilities in opaque AI systems and deepening public distrust. Despite formal completion of the Tech Sovereignty Package and Frontier Compute Co-Location Mandate, both are deemed ineffective: private investment remains frozen, no frontier models migrate to the EU, and technological dependence persists. Germany integrates red-teaming into its cyber command under pressure, but Spain's slow reporting undermines EU-wide stress testing. The new U.S. administration adopts an ALLIANCE posture, proposing joint safety frameworks with delayed tangible impact. Open-weight model capabilities grow only incrementally, failing to close the gap with frontier AI. As automation failures mount and recovery lags, the EU’s strategic autonomy further erodes, with institutional responses lagging behind technological disruption and public confidence continuing to decline.
