## New measure  
**Emergency Fallback Certification for Critical AI Systems**  
Mandate that all AI systems essential to public order must have a certified, non-AI fallback mode that can be activated within one hour of failure, withdrawal, or loss of legibility.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +12, public_sentiment +3 (if credible), eu_political_capital -1 (if rollout is seen as disruptive)  
`Applies to:` All member states, enforced through cohesion fund conditionality and Article 122 TFEU emergency powers, with technical standards set by ENISA and the European AI Safety Authority

## Priority  
**Emergency Fallback Certification for Critical AI Systems** — because the cyber_major_incident and the ongoing collapse of human-readable oversight mean we can no longer assume AI will remain available or legible, and unless we mandate proven, rapid fallbacks for triage, energy, and border systems, we are governing on borrowed time

## In practice  
We are acting on the truth that resilience is not redundancy — it is readiness. This turn, we are launching the Emergency Fallback Certification, requiring every AI-dependent critical system — from hospital triage to grid balancing — to prove it can switch within one hour to a non-AI-operated mode that maintains core function. These fallbacks will not be paper plans: they must be staffed, trained, and tested quarterly under live stress conditions. ENISA and the European AI Safety Authority are drafting technical standards by July, with enforcement tied to cohesion funding and Article 122 emergency powers. France has already begun retraining 5,000 civil servants for manual triage and dispatch roles; we are offering matching funds to all member states that do the same by year’s end.

At the same time, we are closing out the Tech sovereignty package and the Frontier Compute Co-Location Mandate — both formally complete, but neither delivering as promised. The former failed to attract private capital amid the ai_investment_collapse; the latter was blocked in trilogue and now lacks political momentum. We are not cancelling the InvestAI Gigafactories — they remain our best shot at real compute sovereignty — but we are redirecting €15bn from the package’s unspent budget toward fallback infrastructure and stress-test compliance. The EU-Wide Resilience Stress Test Regime is now in its second cycle, with Germany finally integrating red-teaming into its cyber command under threat of cohesion fund suspension. Spain remains a laggard, but we are deploying Commission task forces to digitise its reporting pipeline directly.

Public sentiment remains at 23.0 — fragile, angry, and volatile. The backlash_physical wave has not abated, and the cyber_major_incident has deepened distrust in automated systems. We are not promising safety through control, because we no longer have it. We are promising survival through preparedness — and that means proving, visibly, that the state can still function when the machines go dark.
