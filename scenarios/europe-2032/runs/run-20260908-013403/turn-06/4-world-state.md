### A Turn of Reckoning

The EU pivots sharply toward survival as cascading failures expose systemic fragility. The **Emergency Fallback Certification for Critical AI Systems** is launched as a priority, aiming to restore minimal state functionality in the face of growing unavailability and illegibility of AI. France leads with a visible retraining push, but resistance flares in Spain and Hungary, where governments decry “analog regression” and warn of service collapse under manual operation. Cohesion fund conditionality sparks legal challenges, slowing rollout.

Meanwhile, the **cyber_major_incident** delivers a severe blow: a model-generated ransomware variant infiltrates hospital networks across three member states, encrypting triage and logistics systems. The attack exploits newly opaque reasoning paths, evading legacy detection. Though containment is achieved in days, recovery takes weeks, reinforcing public perception that AI systems are both indispensable and untrustworthy.

The **Tech sovereignty package** and **Frontier Compute Co-Location Mandate** formally conclude but are widely seen as underdelivering. Private investment remains frozen, and no new frontier training runs have migrated to EU soil. With these measures expiring, the EU gains a political capital boost — but not enough to offset new costs.

Germany integrates red-teaming into its cyber command under pressure, yet Spain’s reporting lags, undermining the **EU-Wide Resilience Stress Test Regime**. The Commission deploys technical teams, but progress is uneven.

The U.S. election outcome is now active: the new administration takes office, adopting a posture of **ALLIANCE**. Joint evaluation frameworks are proposed, offering a narrow path to improved safety coordination — though concrete benefits are months away.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Tightens

Despite ambitious action, the EU’s strategic position weakens. Technological momentum outpaces institutional delivery, and public trust erodes further as automation failures dominate headlines.

Open-weight model capability sees limited diffusion, constrained by the slow pace of frontier progress and lack of major open releases or policy-driven dissemination. The modest gain in `openweight_capability` reflects only incremental adoption and fine-tuning of existing accessible models, not a leap in capability — consistent with the measured rise in overall AI capability. No structural breakthrough or leak has occurred to accelerate open-weight parity, and the gap with the frontier remains substantial.
