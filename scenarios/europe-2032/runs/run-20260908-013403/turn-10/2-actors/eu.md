## In practice
We are closing two major measures this turn: the Emergency Fallback Certification and the Emergency AI Incident Response Corps. The former has forced analog continuity into the core nervous system of the state — not universally, not without resistance, but in enough of France, Germany, and Italy to say the principle is now EU law, not just Commission ambition. Spain and Hungary remain defiant, but the Court of Justice rulings are now binding, and cohesion fund suspensions are no longer hypothetical. The latter — the Incident Response Corps — has passed its first live test. It deployed within hours of the ransomware sweep, contained cascading failures in Belgium and the Netherlands, and provided forensic clarity within days. That speed saved services, but also exposed how thin the margin remains: detection lag, lack of pre-positioned access, and unclear escalation thresholds nearly let the incident spiral. We are not ahead — we are just barely keeping pace.

The new measure — Mandatory Safety-Through-Fallback — builds directly on these lessons. It is not about preventing deployment, nor about banning capability. It is about ensuring that wherever high-risk AI is used — in gene editing, in grid control, in emergency dispatch — there is a known, tested, and *automatic* path back to human oversight when the system behaves unpredictably. This will be enforced through ENISA audits, integrated into the AI Act’s conformity assessments, and tied to public procurement: no contract above €10 million for AI-enabled services will clear without a certified fallback regime. We are working through the Internal Market Council and the Health and Research formations to align the scope, and offering co-funding from Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme to offset compliance costs. Resistance will come from private operators and from research institutions who see this as bureaucratic overreach — but the cyberattack and the openweight bio-risks have shifted the burden: the question is no longer whether we can afford such safeguards, but whether we can afford not to have them.
