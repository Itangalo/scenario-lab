### Crisis Response and Fragile Gains

The EU’s Emergency AI Incident Response Corps successfully contained the recent ransomware sweep, deploying within hours and preventing cascading failures in critical infrastructure across Belgium, the Netherlands, and northern Italy. Forensic analysis confirmed the attack was AI-generated, exploiting vulnerabilities in legacy municipal systems. While resilience held, the incident underscored persistent gaps in detection and coordination.

The Emergency Fallback Certification regime is now fully enforced in France, Germany, and Italy, with analog continuity protocols operational in core sectors. Spain and Hungary continue to resist, but cohesion fund suspensions are active, and the European Court of Justice has upheld the Commission’s authority. The precedent is set, but unity is fraying.

In response to emerging bio-risks and the cyber incident, the EU launches *Mandatory Safety-Through-Fallback for High-Risk AI Deployments*, a large category 6 measure requiring certified human-supervised fallbacks in AI systems used in bio-design and critical infrastructure. The rule will be tied to public procurement and enforced via ENISA audits. Early resistance emerges from private operators and research institutions, wary of compliance costs and bureaucratic burden.

Despite the urgency, political capital continues to erode. The portfolio remains heavy, and public sentiment remains low, anchored by perceptions of overreach and disruption. The new measure is named priority, but with capital below 20, this confers no acceleration or cost benefit. The academic backlash against bio-surveillance deepens, and a transnational coalition prepares legal challenges, framing oversight as disproportionate.

Meanwhile, offensive AI capability advances. The *verification_widens* development allows automated systems to self-validate in domains like clinical coding and structural engineering, accelerating capability growth. This progress is confined to closed labs; no corresponding safety improvements reach deployed systems.
