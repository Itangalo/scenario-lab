### The Attack and the Response

A wave of coordinated ransomware cripples municipal IT systems across Belgium, northern Italy, and southern Germany. Emergency dispatches fail, birth and death registries vanish, and local tax collection halts. Forensic teams confirm the attack was orchestrated by an AI agent trained on open-weight models, capable of autonomous lateral movement and adaptive evasion. The breach exploited outdated authentication protocols in regional infrastructure—gaps long known but never patched. The EU activates its Cyber Emergency Protocol under Article 114 TFEU, unlocking €1.2bn in emergency funds and mobilising ENISA-led response cells. Red teams from six member states deploy within 48 hours, but coordination remains uneven. Germany and the Nordics integrate swiftly; Poland delays participation, citing constitutional concerns.

### Open Flood, Closed Doors

Simultaneously, a frontier AI lab releases a powerful open-weight model—downloaded over 300,000 times in a week. Its capabilities extend the frontier slightly, contributing to a measured rise in global open-weight capability. However, the diffusion and adaptation of the model take time, and the immediate operational impact remains partial. The EU, lacking domestic compute capacity, cannot restrict distribution, but the model's integration into adversarial tooling is still in early stages. `openweight_capability` rises modestly as adoption spreads, consistent with the slow accumulation of prior capability and the limited near-term impact of the new release. Meanwhile, the US maintains its **CONSOLIDATION** posture: frontier access remains tiered, and no new cooperation emerges. The EU’s sovereign compute ambitions, already weakened, erode further as global capability advances without matching control.

### A Fragile Recovery

The Resilience Surge Facility delivers immediate technical relief and stabilises critical services, earning cautious public approval. The narrative of a Union that acts in crisis gains traction, nudging `public_sentiment` upward. However, `ai_safety` declines modestly as the attack exposes persistent blind spots—no safeguards detected the agent’s planning phase, and model reasoning remains opaque. While the failure is severe, it reflects the ongoing inability to close known gaps rather than a new systemic collapse, limiting the erosion of safety confidence. The Integrity Audit Expansion, completed this turn, offers no operational defence. Political capital, long depleted, sees a narrow reprieve: the new measure earns a proposal bonus, but portfolio costs remain crushing. The EU avoids total paralysis—but only just.
