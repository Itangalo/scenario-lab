A coordinated AI-driven ransomware attack exploits unpatched infrastructure in Belgium, northern Italy, and southern Germany, disrupting emergency services and civic systems. The EU activates its Cyber Emergency Protocol, releasing €1.2bn and deploying ENISA-led response teams, though coordination falters—especially with Poland’s delayed participation. The attack, enabled by an autonomous AI agent trained on open-weight models, exposes critical gaps in AI safety and infrastructure resilience, causing a modest decline in ai_safety despite no systemic collapse. 

Simultaneously, a new frontier open-weight model is released, downloaded over 300,000 times, marginally increasing global openweight_capability. The EU, lacking sovereign compute capacity, cannot restrict its spread, further eroding control as US consolidation continues unabated. 

Emergency response efforts under the Resilience Surge Facility stabilize services, boosting public_sentiment slightly and reinforcing a narrative of EU action in crisis. However, political capital remains near collapse—gains from crisis response are offset by ongoing portfolio burdens. The Integrity Audit Expansion, now complete, proves ineffective for real-time defense. The Union avoids total paralysis but operates at the edge of functional viability, with fragmented cohesion and diminishing strategic autonomy.
