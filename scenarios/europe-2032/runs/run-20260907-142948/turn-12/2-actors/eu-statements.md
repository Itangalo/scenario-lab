# Statements: The European Union (turn 12)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Shift focus from restoring trust in sovereign AI systems to ensuring the EU can absorb and recover from AI-enabled systemic shocks, with enforceable redress for affected individuals and institutions, and continuity of essential services under sustained disruption

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Shift focus from restoring trust in sovereign AI systems to ensuring the EU can absorb and recover from AI-enabled systemic shocks, with enforceable redress for affected individuals and institutions, and continuity of essential services under sustained disruption
  - Trigger: the **cyber_major_incident** and the **openweight_frontier_release**, combined with `ai_safety` at 16.0, `resilience` at 43.0, and `eu_political_capital` at 0.0
  - Grounds: The cyberattack confirms that harm is no longer hypothetical — it is operational, automated, and beyond our perimeter. The open-weight release ensures that offensive capability is now permanently distributed. Our previous commitment assumed that trust could be rebuilt through procedural integrity in sovereign systems. But those systems are neither sovereign in practice — access is rationed, compute is foreign-controlled — nor safe in effect, as `ai_safety` collapsing to 16.0 proves. We are no longer in a position to prevent harm; we are in a position to fail. The new commitment must reflect that: not control, but continuity; not audit, but absorption; not trust, but survival.
  - Evidence quoted: **cyber_major_incident:** A large, largely automated attack lands. Its form is chosen at the time – a ransomware sweep across public services, a compromised software dependency with an unknown blast radius, or disruption of infrastructure directly – and severity scales with how far offensive capability has outrun defence. Damage is real and public, defenders are visibly behind, and attribution takes months. Whoever ran it, the tooling was model-generated.
**openweight_frontier_release:** An open-weight release lands within months of the closed frontier rather than a year. It is downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in the first week, and whatever capability it carries is now on private hardware permanently and beyond recall. `openweight_capability` moves to within 5–10 points of `ai_capability` at a stroke.
  - Reason: The cyberattack proves sovereign systems are compromised; the open release makes control impossible. Both directly undermine the feasibility of restoring trust, shifting the priority to resilience and survival.
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we now recognise that our capacity to act depends not on decisions alone, but on whether the Union can still mobilise as one
  - Grounds: With `eu_political_capital` at 0 and Poland’s ECJ challenge unresolved, we have reached the point where even correct decisions cannot be implemented. The cyberattack landed not because defences were technically inadequate, but because coordination was absent. Our past actions assumed that decisive direction from the centre would suffice. It does not. We still act under uncertainty, but we now do so knowing that the greatest uncertainty is whether the Union remains a single actor at all.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
