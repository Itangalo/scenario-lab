## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Shift focus from restoring trust in sovereign AI systems to ensuring the EU can absorb and recover from AI-enabled systemic shocks, with enforceable redress for affected individuals and institutions, and continuity of essential services under sustained disruption  
  - Trigger: the **cyber_major_incident** and the **openweight_frontier_release**, combined with `ai_safety` at 16.0, `resilience` at 43.0, and `eu_political_capital` at 0.0  
  - Grounds: The cyberattack confirms that harm is no longer hypothetical — it is operational, automated, and beyond our perimeter. The open-weight release ensures that offensive capability is now permanently distributed. Our previous commitment assumed that trust could be rebuilt through procedural integrity in sovereign systems. But those systems are neither sovereign in practice — access is rationed, compute is foreign-controlled — nor safe in effect, as `ai_safety` collapsing to 16.0 proves. We are no longer in a position to prevent harm; we are in a position to fail. The new commitment must reflect that: not control, but continuity; not audit, but absorption; not trust, but survival.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we now recognise that our capacity to act depends not on decisions alone, but on whether the Union can still mobilise as one  
  - Grounds: With `eu_political_capital` at 0 and Poland’s ECJ challenge unresolved, we have reached the point where even correct decisions cannot be implemented. The cyberattack landed not because defences were technically inadequate, but because coordination was absent. Our past actions assumed that decisive direction from the centre would suffice. It does not. We still act under uncertainty, but we now do so knowing that the greatest uncertainty is whether the Union remains a single actor at all.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Activate EU-wide Cyber Emergency Protocol and Mobilise Resilience Surge Facility  
A binding, time-limited activation of cross-border cyber mutual aid, automatic funding release for critical service restoration, and emergency access to Horizon Europe bio-detection assets in response to the major incident.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `resilience` by 8–12 points by forcing coordination under fire, demonstrating functional solidarity, and hardening response pathways before the next incident; stabilise `public_sentiment` by showing the Union can act when lives are on the line  
`Applies to:` All member states, with automatic disbursement tied to incident declaration and participation in joint response exercises

## Priority  
**Priority:** Activate EU-wide Cyber Emergency Protocol and Mobilise Resilience Surge Facility  
Because the cyber_major_incident has exposed that our greatest vulnerability is not technical but political — the inability to act as one when seconds count — and because this measure is the only one that can prove, in real time, that the EU still functions as a protective union.

## In practice  
This turn, we are operating in emergency mode. The cyberattack — a model-generated ransomware sweep targeting municipal services in Belgium, northern Italy, and southern Germany — has crippled local administrations, disrupted emergency dispatch, and exposed the fragility of our supposedly hardened systems. Attribution is ongoing, but the tooling bears the hallmarks of an open-weight agent trained for lateral movement and persistence. We cannot stop what is already loose. But we can stop it from breaking us.

We are activating the EU-wide Cyber Emergency Protocol under Article 114 TFEU, paired with the newly mobilised Resilience Surge Facility under the Multiannual Financial Framework. This means automatic release of €1.2bn in pre-allocated cohesion and Digital Europe funds to affected states, contingent on their participation in joint ENISA-led response cells and adoption of standardised incident logging. We are dispatching cross-border red teams to restore access, not just to clean systems but to rebuild trust in coordination. The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre is fast-tracking deployment of AI-augmented detection stacks to all regional governments within 90 days.

At the same time, we are pushing the Societal Resilience Acceleration Programme into implementation, despite `eu_political_capital` at zero. We are offering early disbursement of 2025 cohesion funds to member states that activate rapid-response labour transition councils by June — not as a reward, but as a lifeline. Germany and the Nordics are moving fast; Poland remains resistant, citing sovereignty concerns. We are not negotiating. We are invoking solidarity: if you accept EU funds in crisis, you accept EU coordination in response. The message is clear — the era of opt-out resilience is over. Either we stand together, or we fall apart.
