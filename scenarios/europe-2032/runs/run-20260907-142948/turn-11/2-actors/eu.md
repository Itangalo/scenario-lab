## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public and institutional trust in sovereign AI systems by ensuring auditable decision-making, enforceable redress, and resilience to both technical compromise and operational misuse  
  - Trigger: the **openweight_frontier_release** and the **capability_plateau_evidence**, combined with the collapse of `ai_safety` to 16.0 and `public_sentiment` to 18.0  
  - Grounds: The frontier is no longer advancing visibly, but its capabilities are now diffusing irreversibly. The open-weight release means that systems near the closed frontier are already beyond containment, rendering our containment-first strategy obsolete. At the same time, the plateau suggests that the race is not accelerating—but the damage is compounding. Our commitment assumed a world where trust could be rebuilt through sovereign control and procedural accountability. That world is gone. We now face a landscape where harm is inevitable, diffusion is irreversible, and trust cannot be restored through audits alone. The commitment must shift from restoring trust in systems to building societal resilience to systemic failure.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as synonymous with agency, nor control as synonymous with safety  
  - Grounds: The **opaque_reasoning** shift and the failure of the Sovereign Stack Hardening Initiative have shown that even fully contained systems can produce illegible, harmful outcomes. Our past actions assumed that if we controlled the infrastructure, we controlled the risk. We were wrong. The cost of that error is now measured in public trust and political capital, both at historic lows. We still act before certainty, but we do so now with the humility that our tools are blunt, our visibility poor, and our authority fraying. Speed without legibility is noise. Control without understanding is complicity.

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch the Integrity Audit Expansion  
We are pushing this hardest this turn because it is the last active instrument capable of exposing whether our sovereign systems are still functional under the new regime of opaque reasoning — and because its completion this turn is our only chance to extract any credibility from a failed oversight strategy. Without it, we have no evidence, no accountability, and no basis for public trust.

## In practice  
This turn, we are operating in the aftermath of systemic failure. The **automated_decision_scandal** has crystallised public anger, and the **openweight_frontier_release** has made containment a fantasy. We can no longer pretend that sovereignty means safety. Our focus is now on damage control — not of systems, but of society. The Integrity Audit Expansion is our final audit of the old paradigm: EAAD and ENISA are finalising joint templates for adversarial probing of decision provenance, even in the absence of interpretable reasoning. These will rely on outcome drift detection, redress pattern analysis, and weight integrity checks. Findings will be binding: any system found to produce uncorrectable harm will be suspended pending redesign. We are negotiating with the Justice Council to embed these into a revised enforcement mechanism under the AI Act, offering member states co-governance in exchange for compliance.

At the same time, we are launching the Societal Resilience Acceleration Programme to shift the burden from prevention to absorption. This means fast-tracking wage insurance for workers displaced by AI-driven restructuring, expanding Digital Europe Programme funds for municipal cyber-response teams, and activating Horizon Europe surge capacity for bio-detection R&D. The Commission will propose a special resilience facility under the Multiannual Financial Framework, but we know the budget is tight. So we are offering member states early access to cohesion funds in exchange for rapid implementation of retraining pipelines and critical service redundancy plans. The message is clear: we cannot stop the harm, but we can stop it from becoming a crisis.

Poland’s ECJ challenge and the paralysis in Marseille and Wrocław have taught us that top-down mandates without social legitimacy fail. So we are pairing the new programme with a public listening tour — not to consult, but to acknowledge. The era of selling AI as progress is over. Now we must govern it as risk, and ourselves as stewards — not of technology, but of trust.
