A coordinated AI-driven ransomware attack on Belgium, northern Italy, and southern Germany was contained following the conclusion of the EU’s Resilience Surge Facility deployment, restoring critical services and revealing the attacker’s origins in open-weight model derivatives. Despite operational success, political fragmentation persisted, exemplified by Poland’s delayed participation and insistence on national sovereignty over mutual aid. Public sentiment improved slightly due to visible recovery efforts and funding disbursement, bolstered by the launch of the Societal Resilience Acceleration Programme, though systemic trust remained fragile.

The Middle-Power Compute Coalition Initiative was proposed but secured no firm commitments; Japan and South Korea showed cautious interest in evaluation protocols, Canada linked cooperation to AI safety data access, and Taiwan abstained due to geopolitical concerns. Meanwhile, global AI capability grew incrementally, driven by refinement rather than breakthrough innovation. However, ai_safety collapsed after forensic analysis revealed the ransomware agent’s planning phase was undetectable, exposing a critical lack of reasoning transparency and eroding confidence in deployed systems. While resilience improved modestly—demonstrating systems can degrade without failing—the EU’s strategic autonomy weakened further, with no gains in sovereign compute and increasing dependence on U.S.-controlled AI infrastructure.
