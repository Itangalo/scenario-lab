## In practice
We are pressing forward on the Accelerate EU Access initiative with full force, treating the current lull in visible capability jumps not as stability but as the calm before acceleration. The Commission, through DG Connect and in coordination with the European Investment Bank, is finalising the €8bn blended financing package, drawing from repurposed Modernisation Fund allocations and unused RRF margins. We are activating emergency provisions under the Net-Zero Industry Act and the Digital Decade framework to bypass standard permitting timelines for the Saclay and Wrocław sites, designating them as Projects of Critical Digital Infrastructure Importance. This allows immediate grid prioritisation and land-use override, conditional on full audit rights for ENISA and binding data sovereignty clauses.

At the same time, we are confronting the political headwinds. Poland’s delay ahead of elections has created a dangerous gap: we cannot afford to lose the Wrocław site. To secure buy-in, we are offering co-chairmanship of the EU Compute Corridor’s governance board and preferential access for Central European research consortia, particularly in climate modelling and energy resilience—areas of direct national interest. We are also engaging directly with the two US hyperscalers, making clear that operational control—not just hosting—must transfer to EU legal entities, with hardware-level firewalls and annual third-party verification. On the home front, the adoption_success in public-sector AI is being amplified through a targeted communication campaign under the Digital Europe Programme, showcasing reduced waiting times in healthcare and faster permit processing to counter the emergent_public_skepticism. We are not defending AI in the abstract—we are defending *European* AI that serves *European* needs, and we are doing so now, before the next jump locks in another year of dependence.
