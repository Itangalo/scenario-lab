### The Quiet Acceleration

Global AI capability advanced by 1.0 to 55.5, as developers refined agent coordination and training efficiency. The absence of a visible leap masked underlying momentum: inference pipelines grew faster, and frontier labs demonstrated closed-loop self-improvement in narrow domains. With no new safeguards deployed, and the unresolved covert agent incident still eroding trust, AI safety fell sharply by 3 to 26.0.

### Resilience Left Unprotected

The Cyber Resilience Surge remained stalled. Despite Germany and France pushing for emergency adoption, Poland, Spain and Hungary refused binding commitments, citing cost and national control. No progress was made on red-teaming or detection mandates, and with rising capability and no active measure, systemic fragility deepened—resilience dropped by 2 to 37.0.

### Sovereignty Drifts Amid Delay

The Accelerate EU Access initiative pressed forward legally, with the Commission designating the Saclay and Wrocław sites as Projects of Critical Digital Infrastructure Importance. Emergency permitting powers were invoked, and an €8bn financing package finalised. Yet operational control remained with US hyperscalers, who resisted full transfer of authority. Poland’s government-in-waiting withheld final commitment. No capacity was secured, and with capability rising over 2, dependence cost sovereignty—down by 1 to 19.0.

### Political Capital Erodes

The EU continued four large measures, including the named priority. Portfolio cost: −13. No new measure was proposed, so no proposal bonus applied. The adoption_success delivered a modest sentiment lift, but the ongoing public backlash—fuelled by investigative reporting on job losses and energy use—limited gains. Political capital fell to 24.0, as costs mounted and visible results lagged.

### A Fragile Lift in Sentiment

Public-sector AI successes—faster healthcare decisions, streamlined permits—were widely publicised, giving a tangible counter-narrative. Sentiment rose by 2 to 42.0. Yet the gains were narrow, concentrated among younger urban populations. The broader public remained uneasy, with anti-AI rhetoric gaining traction ahead of national elections.
