## New measure
**Integrate Acquired AI Assets into EU Legal and Operational Framework**

Establish binding technical, legal, and security protocols to bring all acquired AI systems — hardware, models, and talent — under enforceable EU jurisdiction, ensuring they are auditable, secure, and aligned with Union values before deployment.

## Priority
**Integrate Acquired AI Assets into EU Legal and Operational Framework** — because the Emergency Acquisition measure will fail in substance if we cannot operationalise what we buy, and because sovereignty is not ownership on paper but control in practice; this turn, the bottleneck shifts from acquisition to integration, and our credibility hinges on proving we can absorb, not just seize.

## In practice
We are shifting from pursuit to consolidation. The Office for Strategic AI Acquisition (OSAA) remains engaged with the London, Dublin, and Zurich assets, but the focus now is no longer just purchase, but integration. In London, we are renegotiating the acquisition terms to include a binding clause: the mothballed training run will only be released if the model weights are transferred to a EuroHPC site in Lyon, with full audit access granted to ENISA before any resumption. In Dublin, we are offering the US hyperscaler a phased decommissioning deal — they retain branding and some R&D access, but the cluster moves under Irish state supervision and EU legal control by turn 11, with power prioritisation tied to the National Energy Resilience Plan.

The new integration measure will be implemented through a tripartite structure: a Legal Integration Cell in DG CONNECT will draft bespoke sovereignty accords for each asset; a Technical Integration Unit at EuroHPC will handle hardware onboarding and model provenance tracking; and a Safety Assurance Board, co-chaired by ENISA and the European AI Board, will certify interpretability and alignment readiness. We are already facing resistance from the Zurich team, who fear EU bureaucracy will stifle their work. To counter this, we are offering them autonomous status under the European Research Council, with direct funding and publication rights — but only if their alignment tools are open-sourced under a GPL-like licence and their models undergo continuous monitoring.

At the same time, we are closing out Redirect Compute Access. The Bucharest and Seville sites are now fully permitted and will serve as intake hubs for the acquired hardware. Romania’s extraterritorial concession means the EMBL facility will operate under EU, not national, law — a precedent we intend to replicate. This is no longer about catching up. It is about becoming the anchor point for a sovereign stack — one we can defend, update, and trust.
