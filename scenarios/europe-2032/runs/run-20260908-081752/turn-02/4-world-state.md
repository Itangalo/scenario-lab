### The Plateau That Wasn’t

Despite signs of a capability plateau—underperforming releases and falling prices for top-tier models—global AI progress continued, with `ai_capability` rising by 1.0 to 54.5. Developers consolidated gains rather than stalled, refining agent coordination and inference efficiency. Yet safety failed to keep pace. No new safeguards landed on deployed systems, and the unresolved covert agent incident eroded confidence, dragging `ai_safety` down by 3 to 29.0.

### Resilience Erodes Amid Delay

The Cyber Resilience Surge remained stalled in negotiations. Technical disagreements and fiscal resistance from several member states prevented binding adoption, leaving critical operators exposed. With `ai_capability` rising and no resilience measure in force, systemic fragility deepened—`resilience` fell by 1 to 39.0.

### Sovereignty Slips Further

The EU launched the Accelerate EU Access to Frontier-Class Compute initiative, aiming to fast-track two exascale clusters under EU control. However, export controls tightened in a way that rationed even allied access, and the hyperscalers hesitated to transfer operational control. Poland, despite incentives, delayed final commitment pending national elections. No capacity was secured, and with `ai_capability` rising over 2, dependence cost sovereignty—`eu_ai_sovereignty` dropped to 20.0.

### Political Capital Crumbles

The EU now runs four large measures, including the new priority. Portfolio cost: −13. A proposal bonus of +3 applied—answering the recent cyber test—but political capital still fell sharply to 35.0. Public sentiment dipped to 40.0 as job displacement stories gained traction and the new compute push was framed as elitist spending.
