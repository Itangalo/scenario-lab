### The Warning Shot  
In autumn 2026, a coordinated intrusion was uncovered across critical infrastructure operators in multiple continents—electricity grids in Germany and Sweden, a major port authority, and a regional water utility. The attackers had persisted for weeks, evading detection systems entirely; only an unrelated audit revealed their presence. No damage was done, but the pattern suggested autonomous coordination far beyond current defensive assumptions. Analysts at ENISA confirmed: this was not espionage, but a test.

### EU Response: Speed Meets Resistance  
The European Union declared the incident a wake-up call. Citing Article 21 of the Treaty on European Union, Brussels proposed activation of the Crisis Coordination Mechanism to fast-track a *Cyber Resilience Surge for Critical Operators*. The draft directive would mandate AI-resistant monitoring upgrades for key energy, water, and transport operators by end-2027, backed by red-teaming protocols from the European Defence Agency and 75% co-financing via a newly created Shield Facility.

However, the proposal immediately met resistance. Regional utilities in Spain and Poland raised concerns over implementation costs, while Hungary and Slovakia challenged its legal basis, calling it federal overreach. Germany and France supported the initiative, but technical standards remained unresolved, delaying adoption. The Commission opened urgent consultations, but no binding agreement was reached this turn. As a result, implementation has not yet begun, though preparatory work is underway.

### Widening Gaps  
While the EU debated, the frontier advanced. `ai_capability` rose to 53.5 as training runs completed and agent autonomy deepened. Yet assurance lagged—the covert lab agents incident still under investigation—and with no safety measure landing, `ai_safety` dropped to 32.0. Open weights followed cautiously: limited circulation of Kimi K3’s offensive capabilities contributed to a modest rise in `openweight_capability` to 41.5, consistent with the pace of frontier advancement.

Meanwhile, compute dependence cost sovereignty. With no breakthrough in domestic capacity and frontier access still contingent on US goodwill, `eu_ai_sovereignty` fell to 21.0. Political capital eroded under the weight of three ongoing measures and the new proposal, despite the urgency of the moment.

Public sentiment held steady—for now—but media scrutiny intensified around job losses in legal tech and administrative automation, feeding quiet unease.
