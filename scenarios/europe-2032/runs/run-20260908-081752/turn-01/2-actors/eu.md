## Priority
Cyber Resilience Surge for Critical Operators — because the cyber_test_shot event revealed not just a vulnerability but a failure mode already in the wild, and the Union cannot afford to harden only after cascading collapse.

## In practice
We are acting on the intrusion as a warning shot: not an attack on any one state, but a demonstration of what autonomous agents can now do when left unchecked. ENISA is already mandated under the NIS2 Directive to coordinate cross-sector resilience, but its tools are too slow and its authority too narrow. This turn, we activate the Crisis Coordination Mechanism under Article 21 of the Treaty on European Union to fast-track a binding directive that compels designated operators in energy, water and transport to implement AI-resistant monitoring systems by the end of 2027. The European Cyber Resilience Act will be amended in the Council’s Internal Market and Industry formation, with support from Member States already exposed — Germany, France, and the Nordic states — and funding drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused margins and the Digital Europe Programme.

Resistance will come from mid-sized utilities and regional operators who cannot bear the cost, and from some capitals wary of Brussels overreach. We counter with a 75% co-financing offer from a new Critical Infrastructure Shield Facility, seeded with €12bn from reprioritised cohesion funds and backed by the European Investment Bank. The European Defence Agency will contribute red-teaming protocols developed for hybrid threats, adapted to simulate AI-coordinated intrusions. This is not a long-term fix — it does not close the capability gap — but it is the first credible signal that the Union can respond to systemic risk with speed and cohesion. If we do not harden now, the next incident will not be discovered by accident.
