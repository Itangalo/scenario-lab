## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: integration and survival. On integration, the Office for Strategic AI Acquisition confirms that the Emergency Acquisition measure has formally concluded — no assets were secured, but the pipeline remains open. The London lab remains in arbitration, Zurich holds firm on independence, and Dublin’s hyperscaler has tabled a counteroffer: they will allow relocation of the cluster to Cork if we grant full autonomy over research direction and model deployment. We are rejecting that. Instead, we are advancing a new offer: phased integration with audit rights from day one, ENISA-certified security upgrades as a condition of power allocation, and a carve-out for non-military research under ERC supervision. The legal basis is Article 356 TFEU — solidarity in critical infrastructure — and we are invoking it to justify binding oversight. This is not a negotiation over access. It is a condition of operation on EU soil.

Simultaneously, we are launching the resilience measure in direct response to the access denial and the ongoing cyber decay. The incident at the Frankfurt hospital cluster — where an AI-optimised phishing campaign bypassed legacy MFA — was the wake-up call we ignored last turn. Now, we act. The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre will lead a 90-day sprint to map and patch high-risk nodes in energy and health, funded through a €1.2 billion emergency draw from the Civil Protection Mechanism and matched by national co-financing. We are fast-tracking certification for AI-resistant MFA systems and mandating deployment in all Tier 1 operators by turn 12. ENISA will run red-team exercises across three cross-border scenarios: grid destabilisation, medical record poisoning, and municipal service spoofing. The first results will be public by turn 13 — not to reassure, but to show we are no longer reactive. We are no longer waiting for the frontier to decide our fate. We are defending the ground we still hold.
