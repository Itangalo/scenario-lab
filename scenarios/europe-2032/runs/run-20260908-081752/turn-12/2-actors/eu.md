## New measure

**Launch EU Baseline Compute Initiative: Secure, Energy-Limited, Publicly Governed AI Infrastructure**

Begin construction of EU-sovereign compute capacity anchored in renewable energy grids, starting with hardened sites in Finland, Portugal, and Romania, under joint civilian-military oversight, with initial access reserved for public research, resilience testing, and safety evaluation.

## Priority

**Accelerate Resilience of Critical Infrastructure Against AI-Enabled Threats** — because with `resilience` at 24.0 and `ai_safety` collapsing to 8.0, the system is already in freefall, and because every act of sabotage, every protest, every unpatched node confirms that we are not defending what we have — let alone building what we need. This measure is no longer about risk reduction; it is about proving that the Union can still act before the next incident becomes irreversible.

## In practice

We are acting on two tracks: one immediate, one foundational. On the immediate front, we are executing the resilience measure with emergency intensity. The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre has activated its crisis protocol, leading a 90-day sprint to patch high-risk nodes across energy and health. €1.2 billion has been drawn from the Civil Protection Mechanism, with 50% co-financing required from member states — a condition we are enforcing through Council Regulation 2021/2092, despite pushback from Hungary and Slovakia. We are fast-tracking certification for AI-resistant MFA systems, mandating deployment in all Tier 1 operators by turn 12. ENISA is running red-team exercises simulating grid destabilisation, medical record poisoning, and municipal service spoofing — scenarios chosen not for plausibility, but for contagion potential. The first results will be public by turn 13, not to reassure, but to prove we are no longer reactive.

On the strategic front, we are abandoning the fiction of sovereignty-by-agreement and beginning to build from the ground up. The new Baseline Compute Initiative launches this turn with site selection and emergency permitting under the Net-Zero Industry Act. We are allocating €3.8 billion from the Innovation Fund and EIB loans, with 60% reserved for energy integration and physical hardening. The first clusters will be governed by a joint civilian-military board under ENISA and EDA authority, with access strictly tiered: public research first, safety evaluation second, industry only after audit. We are bypassing the stalled Nordic and Irish negotiations, instead offering bilateral energy-for-access deals with Finland and Portugal in exchange for hosting rights and grid priority. This is not the scale we wanted — it will run models up to 58.0, not 61.5 — but it is the foundation we can defend. We are no longer buying our way in. We are building our way out.
