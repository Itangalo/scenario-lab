## In practice
We are treating the end of the Tech sovereignty package and Accelerate EU Access not as a conclusion, but as a reckoning. The designation of Saclay and Wrocław as Projects of Critical Digital Infrastructure Importance under the Net-Zero Industry Act has granted us legal primacy — but legal primacy without enforcement is a proclamation, not a policy. We are therefore activating emergency powers under Article 222 TFEU and the Critical Entities Resilience Directive to condition disbursement of the €8bn EIB financing on full audit rights and data sovereignty clauses enforced by ENISA. If Poland continues to withhold commitment, we will trigger Article 265 TFEU proceedings and redirect the Wrocław allocation to a secondary site in the Czech Republic, using repurposed RRF funds and invoking the Anti-Coercion Instrument to counteract economic pressure from external actors.

With US hyperscalers, we are shifting from negotiation to ultimatum: operational control over training clusters must transfer to EU-registered entities by Q3, with hardware-level firewalls and annual third-party verification by EUIPO-certified auditors. Absent compliance, we will invoke the Foreign Subsidies Regulation to block further expansion and redirect procurement to compliant providers. Domestically, we are launching a public campaign under the Digital Europe Programme to reframe AI not as a foreign import, but as European infrastructure — showcasing reduced waiting times in healthcare and faster permit processing in cities like Marseille and Bratislava. This is not propaganda. It is the foundation of legitimacy, without which no enforcement will hold.
