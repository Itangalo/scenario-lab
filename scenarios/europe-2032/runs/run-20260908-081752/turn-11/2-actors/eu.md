## In practice

The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre has activated its crisis protocol, leading a 90-day sprint to patch high-risk nodes across energy and health. €1.2 billion has been drawn from the Civil Protection Mechanism, with 50% co-financing required from member states — a condition we are enforcing to ensure ownership. We are fast-tracking certification for AI-resistant MFA systems, mandating deployment in all Tier 1 operators by turn 12. ENISA is running red-team exercises simulating grid destabilisation, medical record poisoning, and municipal service spoofing — scenarios chosen not for plausibility, but for contagion potential. The first results will be public by turn 13, not to reassure, but to prove we are no longer reactive.

On the strategic front, we are pivoting from acquisition to sovereignty-by-agreement. The failed Emergency Acquisition has shown that distressed assets are not enough — and that legal force alone cannot compel cooperation from neutral or resistant states. Instead, we are offering a new model: binding, reciprocal integration. To Switzerland, we offer energy security and access to EU research talent in exchange for audit rights and emergency inference access. To Ireland, we offer full participation in EU security architecture and grid priority in exchange for relocating and hardening the Dublin cluster under Article 356 oversight. To the Nordics, we propose a joint sovereignty framework — shared compute, shared safeguards, shared escalation protocols — anchored in EDA structures. This is not a purchase. It is a treaty-level alignment of survival interests. The frontier may be stalled, but it will move again. We will not be caught naked when it does.
