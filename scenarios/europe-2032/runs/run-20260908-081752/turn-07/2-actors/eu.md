## New measure
**Redirect Compute Access via Secondary Nodes**

Establish sovereign EU control over frontier-class compute by redirecting planned investments to alternative sites in member states with demonstrated willingness to comply, and by brokering direct access agreements with non-US hyperscalers and neutral research consortia.

## Priority
**Redirect Compute Access via Secondary Nodes** — because the failure of enforcement in Poland and the US’s tiered access posture have created an immediate sovereignty gap, and this measure is the only one that can rebuild credible EU control within two turns, using existing capital and redirecting stalled investments to willing partners.

## In practice
We are acting on the failure of compulsion by shifting to construction. The Saclay site remains on track, but Wrocław is formally abandoned as a node for EU-controlled frontier compute. We are redirecting €48bn in RRF and InvestAI funds to fast-track two alternative sites: one in Bucharest, co-hosted with the European Molecular Biology Laboratory, and one in Seville, integrated with EuroHPC’s next-generation deployment. Both will be designated under the Net-Zero Industry Act and granted emergency permitting under the Critical Entities Resilience Directive. ENISA will conduct quarterly audits, and all training clusters will operate under EU-registered legal entities with hardware-level access controls verified by EUIPO-certified auditors.

Simultaneously, we are opening direct negotiations with non-US hyperscalers: Alibaba Cloud (for access to its Singapore and Frankfurt clusters under EU jurisdiction), NEC (for joint development of post-exascale systems in Europe), and the Nordic Quantum-AI Consortium (for shared training runs on closed models). These are not equal substitutes for US capacity — but they are real, available, and politically feasible. The Foreign Subsidies Regulation will be used selectively to block non-compliant expansions, but our primary tool is now attraction, not coercion: we are offering tax stability, grid priority, and access to EU talent in exchange for binding legal anchoring. The Digital Europe Programme will launch a visibility campaign in January 2030 to showcase these nodes as proof that Europe is building its own path — not waiting for permission.
