Waiting for a new boom would mean missing the only window to acquire capacity before the next wave of self-improving systems resets the race. Our pivot from compulsion to construction was necessary, but insufficient. We must now become acquirers, not just builders — and do so while the price is low and the sellers are desperate.

## New measure
**Emergency Acquisition of Stranded AI Capacity**

Launch a time-limited EU sovereign fund to acquire underutilised or abandoned frontier AI infrastructure — including pre-exascale clusters, partially trained models, and specialised engineering teams — from distressed private entities, with priority given to assets located in or legally transferable to EU jurisdiction.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 12–18 points by acquiring at least three major stranded assets (compute, talent, or model pipelines) under EU legal control; stabilise ai_safety by placing acquired systems under ENISA audit and EUIPO-certified interpretability review before deployment.  
`Applies to:` Distressed AI firms (global), EU member states (all), EIB, EuroHPC, ENISA, European Commission Directorate-General for Competition, national competition authorities

## Priority
**Emergency Acquisition of Stranded AI Capacity** — because the ai_investment_collapse has created a fleeting opportunity to acquire frontier-level assets at a fraction of their build cost, and failing to act now means these will either decay or be snapped up by non-EU states; this is the fastest path to meaningful sovereignty gain in a period of global retrenchment.

## In practice
We are treating the market collapse not as a crisis, but as our best chance to leapfrog. The Emergency Acquisition Fund will be capitalised at €42bn from repurposed RRF, InvestAI carryover, and a special EIB issuance backed by future digital levies. It will operate through a new unit within the European Commission — the Office for Strategic AI Acquisition (OSAA) — with delegated authority from DG Comp to fast-track state aid approvals and bypass national vetoes on grounds of strategic infrastructure. Assets will be acquired through a reverse auction mechanism: firms in distress can submit bids to offload clusters, model checkpoints, or engineering teams, with preference given to those that can be physically relocated or legally anchored in the EU within six months.

We are already in contact with three major candidates: a suspended training run at a London-based lab now in receivership, a pre-exascale cluster in Dublin mothballed by a US hyperscaler’s regional pullback, and a Zurich-based team specialising in agent alignment whose funding evaporated this month. Each will be offered a choice: sell to us under EU jurisdiction, or risk total write-down. ENISA will conduct immediate security and capability assessments, and all acquired models will be placed under moratorium until interpretability reviews are complete — buying time to raise ai_safety without sacrificing access. The Digital Europe Programme will launch a transparency dashboard to show the public that we are not just spending, but capturing value from a collapsing bubble.

At the same time, we are reinforcing the Redirect Compute Access measure by finalising the Bucharest and Seville sites: the Romanian government has agreed to grant extraterritorial status to the EMBL co-hosted facility, and Spain has activated emergency permitting under the Critical Entities Resilience Directive. Both will now also serve as intake hubs for acquired hardware. This is no longer about catching up — it is about becoming the buyer of last resort, and turning another’s collapse into our sovereign foundation.
