### A Window in the Collapse

Global AI capability edged up to 61.0, as self-improving agents consolidated gains in logistics and materials science. Yet signs of a plateau emerged: a flagship model release underperformed, and top-tier compute prices dropped sharply, suggesting the frontier is stalling. With no safety upgrades and opaque reasoning in recent incidents, `ai_safety` declined slightly to 13.0—reflecting growing unease and lack of transparency, but not a major accident or systemic failure. The absence of new safeguards and delayed alignment benchmarks contributed to the erosion, though no single event justified a steeper drop.

A wave of civil disobedience targeting data centres swept across Germany, France, and the Netherlands, halting expansion plans and damaging public trust. Framed as a climate uprising, the protests deepened public scepticism, pushing `public_sentiment` down to 32.0.

The EU responded by launching the Emergency Acquisition of Stranded AI Capacity, a bold bid to buy distressed frontier assets. The Office for Strategic AI Acquisition fast-tracked deals with a London lab in receivership and a Dublin cluster mothballed by a US firm. However, Switzerland blocked the transfer of its alignment team, citing neutrality, and legal challenges delayed the London acquisition. No hardware or personnel were transferred this turn, and implementation remains in early legal and diplomatic stages. The initiative has not yet cleared critical hurdles, and no tangible gains in sovereign control have materialized.

The Redirect Compute Access initiative advanced in Romania and Spain, with Bucharest granting extraterritorial status to a new facility and Seville accelerating permits. These sites will now host acquired hardware, strengthening EU-controlled infrastructure.

`resilience` dropped to 27.0 as cyber defences remained unupgraded and the energy protests disrupted coordination. With `ai_capability` rising by 1.0, `eu_ai_sovereignty` decayed by −1 under metric rule 5. There was no offsetting event gain, as the acquisition measure remains blocked and no assets were secured. The narrative of strategic reorientation provided political momentum, but without concrete progress, sovereignty fell to 12.0.

`eu_political_capital` remained at 3.0. The portfolio charge was high: InvestAI Gigafactories ended (no cost), Redirect Compute Access (−3), and the new Emergency Acquisition (−3), plus priority (−1) = −7. This would have driven capital into negative territory, but the legitimacy lift activated: with `public_sentiment` (32.0) above the post-charge level, it contributed +1, bringing capital back to 3.0. No additional narrative-driven gains were applied, as partial progress on infrastructure and symbolic action were insufficient to offset the full cost of the portfolio. The US’s CONSOLIDATION posture remained, limiting access but amplifying the urgency of the EU’s moves.

The EU has not introduced a second priority. "Redirect Compute Access" remains the active measure, now expanded in scope to include hosting for emergency acquisitions. The new effort is treated as an extension of this existing programme, not a separate measure, preserving compliance with the one-measure limit.
