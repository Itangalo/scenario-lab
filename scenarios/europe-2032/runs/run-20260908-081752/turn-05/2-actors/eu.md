## In practice
We are treating the conclusion of the Cyber Resilience Surge as a diagnostic, not a defeat. Its failure to bind Poland, Spain, and Hungary reveals a deeper truth: resilience cannot be imposed without sovereignty. Without sovereign levers—compute, control, credibility—no mandate on critical infrastructure will hold. We are therefore doubling down on the Accelerate EU Access initiative, using the designation of Saclay and Wrocław as Projects of Critical Digital Infrastructure Importance to lock in legal primacy. Emergency powers under the Net-Zero Industry Act and Digital Decade framework are now active, enabling immediate grid prioritisation and land-use override. The European Investment Bank is finalising the €8bn blended financing package, drawing on repurposed Modernisation Fund allocations and unused RRF margins, with disbursement conditional on full audit rights for ENISA and enforceable data sovereignty clauses.

At the same time, we are preparing for a shift in tone. If Poland’s incoming government continues to withhold commitment, we will trigger Article 265 TFEU proceedings for failure to fulfil obligations under the Digital Markets Act and Critical Entities Resilience Directive. We are also preparing a public offer to Central European research consortia—especially in climate and energy—for preferential access to the Wrocław cluster, framed not as a concession but as a return on shared investment. With US hyperscalers, we are moving from negotiation to ultimatum: operational control must transfer to EU legal entities by Q3, with hardware-level firewalls and annual third-party verification by EUIPO-certified auditors. Absent compliance, we will invoke the Foreign Subsidies Regulation to block further expansion. Domestically, the adoption_success is being amplified through a targeted campaign under the Digital Europe Programme, showcasing reduced waiting times in healthcare and faster permit processing—not to sell AI, but to sell *European* AI that serves *European* needs. This is not just about technology. It is about legitimacy, leverage, and the right to decide.
