### In practice

We are closing the Emergency Resilience Reinforcement Directive with full certification from CERT-EU: Lyon, Stuttgart, and Valencia have integrated air-gapped control systems into Gaia-X’s sovereign monitoring layer, unlocking the final tranches of the Modernisation Fund. Poland and Hungary are now legally bound into the mutual aid pact under Article 8, with Cohesion Fund penalties enforceable at the Commission’s discretion. But Cyprus, Latvia, and Slovakia remain outside the Union-Wide Critical Service Failover Protocol. We are offering joint liability pooling and technical assistance to secure their buy-in, but contingency activation paths—bypassing their nodes in crisis—are now in final design.

The Cyber Incident Response Surge Capacity is mobilising under ENISA, with €900m redirected from Digital Europe and Horizon. Twelve mobile teams are being staffed with seconded national experts, but legal disputes over deployment authority in non-compliant states and conditional data access from providers are slowing readiness. A live simulation in Q3 will test response to a poisoned software dependency chain, but full operational capacity will not land before turn 14. This is not just about speed — it is about creating a feedback loop from real-world attacks to defensive training and regulation, even as ai_safety plummets.

And now, the ground shifts beneath us: Italy, Austria, and Finland have invoked emergency override clauses to suspend EU-level AI deployment in public services, citing uncontrolled risk and democratic accountability. This is not rebellion — it is a signal that the Union’s authority is no longer assumed. The Frontline Public Assurance Corps, deploying now in Barcelona, is our only instrument operating at the level of trust, not just control. We are expanding its mandate: mediators will now co-design binding reforms with local communities, including temporary suspensions of contested systems. But this is not enough. We are launching the Union-Wide Legitimacy Accord — a binding renegotiation of the Union’s role in AI governance, to begin in Q4 under the European Council, with the EDPB, national parliaments, and civil society at the table. The two mandates remain — but they can only be fulfilled together if we are still seen as legitimate.
