## Priority

## In practice
We are treating the close of the two-year cycle as a reckoning: the Emergency Sovereign Access Initiative did not deliver an operational stack, and while secure inference designs are complete and hosting agreements signed, hardware delays and national opt-outs have broken the timeline. The modular data centres in Finland and Romania remain in storage, their deployment blocked by legal challenges in Helsinki and Bucharest. We are now converting that failure into structural reform — not by retreating from sovereignty, but by making it governable.

The Sovereign AI Co-Design Pact, launched under the Digital Europe Programme and co-governed by the Social Observatory for AI, will bind all future EU AI infrastructure to three conditions: mandatory citizen assemblies at siting locations, enforceable local veto rights over deployment, and revenue-sharing mechanisms that direct 15% of operational profits to regional innovation and transition funds. This applies retroactively to the InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package, reopening permitting processes where consent was not secured. We are funding this with €300m from the unused portion of the Public-Sector AI Adoption Accelerator, which underperformed due to procurement delays.

Internally, we are facing resistance from the Competitiveness Council, which argues that binding vetoes will paralyse rollout. But the election_alliance event has changed the calculus: structured access from the US reduces immediate pressure to deliver sovereign stacks at any cost. We are using that breathing room not to slow down, but to redirect — trading speed for legitimacy, because without it, no stack, no matter how secure, will stand. We are presenting this not as a delay, but as a correction: sovereignty that cannot be defended in court or in public square is no sovereignty at all.
