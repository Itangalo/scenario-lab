### A Reckoning on Sovereignty

The Emergency Sovereign Access Initiative formally concludes but fails to deliver an operational sovereign AI stack. Modular data centres in Finland and Romania remain in storage, blocked by legal challenges in Helsinki and Bucharest over unconsulted siting. The Union acknowledges the shortfall, converting the failure into structural reform: the **Sovereign AI Co-Design Pact** launches as a small category 9 measure, binding all future EU-funded AI infrastructure — including InvestAI Gigafactories — to mandatory citizen assemblies, local veto rights, and 15% revenue sharing with host regions.

The Competitiveness Council resists, warning of paralysis, but the recent election_alliance in the United States eases immediate pressure. With structured access to frontier models now likely, the EU shifts from speed to legitimacy, betting that sovereignty must be defensible both technically and politically.

### Capability Plateaus, Controls Tighten

Global AI capability advances only modestly, rising to 58.4, as evidence mounts that the frontier is plateauing: a flagship model underperforms expectations, and prices for top-tier inference fall sharply. However, export_control_escalation follows — the US restricts chip shipments even to allied buyers, citing strategic scarcity. This undermines EU compute autonomy efforts, contributing to a −1 in eu_ai_sovereignty under rule 5’s access term.

Meanwhile, openweight_capability climbs to 46.0, fed by diffusion of last year’s breakthroughs. No safety upgrades land on deployed systems; ai_safety remains stagnant at 23.0 despite rising risks.

### Resilience Gains, Capital Holds

Cyber Shield for Critical Operators completes, boosting resilience +3 to 41.0. Joint exercises reveal gaps in municipal coordination, but detection protocols are now mandatory across energy and water sectors.

Political capital stabilizes at 31.0 despite portfolio strain. The completion of two measures — Cyber Shield (+4) and the expired Emergency Initiative (+2) — offsets ongoing costs. Public sentiment rises to 39.0, lifted by visible progress and the Co-Design Pact’s promise of inclusion.
