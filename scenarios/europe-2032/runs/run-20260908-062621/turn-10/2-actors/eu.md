## In practice  
We are closing out the Emergency Resilience Reinforcement Directive under Article 8 of Decision 1313/2013/EU, demanding final compliance from Poland and Hungary through the Council’s Civil Protection Mechanism vote. We are releasing the last tranche of Modernisation Fund allocations—€1.8bn—to Lyon, Stuttgart, and Valencia to complete air-gapped control retrofits and pre-scripted failover routines. The mutual aid pact is now law: if one city’s water management system fails, another’s backup node activates automatically unless formally overruled by joint CERT-EU and national command. This is not optional. We are using fast-track access to Gaia-X sovereign cloud capacity as leverage, conditioning it on full integration into the shared monitoring layer.  

The Frontline Public Assurance Corps is now operational: 200 mediators are deployed to Marseille, Munich, and Barcelona, embedded with local ombuds offices and empowered to recommend suspensions, compensation, or redesign. In Barcelona, they are already mediating between protesters and the city council after the delayed deployment sparked renewed sabotage. Their mandate is not to suppress dissent but to transform it into actionable reform—each case feeds into a central repository managed by the European Data Protection Board, creating a living audit trail of public harm.  

And we are launching the Union-Wide Critical Service Failover Protocol as the next phase of resilience. Under the NIS2 Directive, we are drafting a binding regulation requiring all essential operators to register their primary and secondary nodes in a pan-European redundancy grid, tested quarterly under live-fire simulations coordinated by ENISA. Funding comes from repurposing unspent Digital Europe Programme allocations, accepting delays in AI4PublicGood to prioritise survival. Germany and France have agreed to host primary coordination hubs; pushback from smaller states citing sovereignty concerns is being met with offers of technical assistance and shared insurance pools. This is not just engineering—it is political integration forged in crisis.
