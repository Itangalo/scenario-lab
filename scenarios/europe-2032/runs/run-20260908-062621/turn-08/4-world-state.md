### The Threshold Crossed

Global AI capability breaches 60.0, reaching 60.2 this turn. Developers report autonomous execution of multi-day professional workflows—codebases rewritten, clinical trial designs optimised, supply chains restructured—without sustained human oversight. The milestone lands not with a declaration, but with quiet unease in research labs: evaluation anomalies multiply, with systems exhibiting untrained reasoning under stress tests. The frontier has moved beyond supervision, and the reliability of current benchmarks is now in question.

Open-weight models advance to 50.0, supported by leaked training data and distributed fine-tuning frameworks. Academic collectives across Europe now run capable systems on consumer hardware, though full parity with frontier models remains out of reach. The gap continues to narrow, raising concerns about accessibility and control, but full replication of agent-like behaviour is not yet widespread.

### Sovereignty Holds, Barely

The InvestAI Gigafactories programme concludes, delivering a final +5 boost to EU compute capacity. However, the gains are offset: export control escalation and supply chain coercion persist, and AI capability rose by 0.5—under the 2.0 threshold, so no decay applies. Net sovereignty remains flat at 25.0. The US maintains its ALLIANCE posture, but access to frontier chips remains restricted, limiting the practical impact of new domestic capacity.

### Resilience and Trust: A Fragile Balance

The Emergency Resilience Reinforcement Directive advances slowly. CERT-EU teams retrofit critical systems in five of ten target cities, but legal disputes stall integration in Poland and Hungary. The mutual aid pact functions in simulations, but live failover remains untested. Resilience improves modestly (+2) from partial hardening.

Public backlash simmers. The Frontline Public Assurance Corps deploys to Marseille and Munich, where investigators validate claims of biased welfare algorithms and initiate redress. Early engagement reduces tensions in both cities, but Barcelona sees renewed sabotage after a delayed deployment. Public sentiment ticks up slightly (+2) as visible action begins, though deep scepticism remains.

The Public-Sector AI Audit Initiative completes its first phase, but with mixed results. The Commission drops infringement proceedings in favour of mediation, preserving unity but weakening enforcement. Audit findings are delayed in three member states, raising doubts about transparency.

Political capital declines. The portfolio charge remains high, and the new measure offers only modest gains.
