## New measure  
**Frontline Public Assurance Corps**  
Deploy trusted civil servants with technical authority to affected communities to investigate AI-related harms, de-escalate tensions, and co-design local redress pathways.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Stabilise public_sentiment (+4–6) by creating visible, accountable channels for redress; reduce backlash_physical risk by grounding public anger in institutional response; modestly support resilience by strengthening local legitimacy of critical services.  
`Applies to:` Municipalities experiencing protest or sabotage linked to AI infrastructure, with priority to regions under Emergency Resilience Reinforcement Directive.

## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Reinforcement Directive** — because the convergence of external technological coercion and internal civil disruption has created a crisis of operational continuity, and without functioning municipal systems, no audit, no sovereignty, no future can be built.

## In practice  
We are treating this turn as the breaking point of passive governance. The Emergency Resilience Reinforcement Directive is now live under Article 8 of Decision 1313/2013/EU, activated through the Civil Protection Mechanism. CERT-EU and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre are deploying rapid-response teams to ten high-risk municipalities, using unspent Modernisation Fund allocations to retrofit air-gapped controls and pre-scripted failover routines into water, energy, and health IT systems. The key innovation is the mutual aid pact: we are mandating that Lyon’s backup node activates when Stuttgart fails, and vice versa. This is not optional integration — it is survival through interdependence. We are offering fast-track access to sovereign cloud capacity as an incentive for compliance, tying immediate survival to long-term digital unity.

Meanwhile, the Public-Sector AI Audit and Remediation Initiative continues, but we are shifting tactics. The Commission is scaling back its confrontational posture toward smaller member states, replacing infringement threats with a new mediation track under the Digital Governance Board. We are offering technical and financial support to national audit teams, reframing compliance not as surrender but as partnership. This is not retreat — it is recalibration. We cannot afford to lose legitimacy on two fronts at once.

And now, we are launching the Frontline Public Assurance Corps: a new civil service cadre of 200 technical mediators, drawn from national ombuds offices, digital ministries, and civil society, trained in both AI literacy and conflict de-escalation. They will deploy to protest-affected regions — Marseille, Barcelona, Munich — not to police, but to listen, investigate, and co-design redress. Each team will have authority to recommend immediate suspensions, compensation, or system redesign. This is not PR — it is legitimacy repair. We are funding it through a reprioritisation of the Digital Europe Programme, accepting delays in AI4PublicGood to prevent collapse in public order. If we do not show people that the state sees them, no amount of cyber hardening will save us.
