## In practice
We are executing the Emergency Sovereign Access Initiative under Article 4 of the TEU and the Digital Markets Act’s emergency clause, with the Joint Research Centre now finalising the secure inference stack for deployment by June. The €12bn ring-fenced from REACT-EU and the Connecting Europe Facility is legally locked in, and we are finalising hosting agreements with two non-US-aligned developers — one South Korean, one Indian — whose truncated models are undergoing red-team evaluation by the European Defence Agency. Modular data centres at InvestAI sites in Finland and Romania are being pre-positioned under emergency permitting, bypassing national opt-outs via the revised Tech Sovereignty Package. This is not optional: at 56.5 global capability and falling safety, the Union cannot afford to wait for consensus.

But we are also acting on the legitimacy crisis. The **Public-Sector AI Adoption Accelerator** launches this turn under the Digital Europe Programme, with €1.8bn repurposed from unused cohesion funds. It will deploy sovereign AI in three pilot applications: real-time epidemic forecasting in EU health agencies, automated trauma triage support in emergency hospitals in Marseille, Milan and Seville, and AI-assisted flood response coordination in the Danube basin. Each includes a citizen co-design panel and real-time transparency dashboard. This is not spin — it is strategy: if AI is only seen in job losses and data centres, it will be rejected. If it saves lives visibly, under public oversight, it can still be claimed as common infrastructure.

Meanwhile, the Commission’s Social Observatory for AI is delivering its rapid review of siting equity by April, with binding recommendations on benefit-sharing and local veto rights for future AI infrastructure. We are not pausing sovereignty — we are reshaping its terms. The backlash is not a reason to slow down, but to lead differently.
