### Sovereign Access, Tested by Fire

The EU presses ahead with the Emergency Sovereign Access Initiative, finalising secure inference stacks and pre-positioning modular data centres in Finland and Romania under emergency permitting. Hosting agreements with non-US developers advance, and red-teaming of truncated models continues. Yet deployment remains fragile, with no sovereign stack yet operational. The priority designation accelerates internal coordination but fails to overcome hardware delays and fragmented national buy-in.

Simultaneously, the Union launches the Public-Sector AI Adoption Accelerator, repurposing €1.8bn to deploy AI in epidemic forecasting, trauma triage, and flood response. Citizen co-design panels are established, and transparency dashboards introduced. However, implementation lags: only one hospital in Marseille has functional integration by June, while Milan and Seville face procurement delays. The Danube coordination pilot remains in design phase.

Public legitimacy remains under strain. The emergent_public_debate_escalation intensifies, with civil society coalitions demanding democratic oversight. Though the Social Observatory delivers its review on siting equity, recommending local veto rights, implementation is non-binding and met with scepticism. Protests subside slightly, but distrust persists in media narratives framing AI as imposed from above.

Globally, AI capability advances to 57.8, driven by closed-loop agent systems in logistics and materials design. No corresponding safety upgrades land on deployed models. The genome model controversy continues to erode confidence, and ai_safety drops another 3 points to 23.0.

Open-weight models rise to 44.0, reflecting steady diffusion of Kimi K3 derivatives and improved cyber intrusion tools. Resilience dips slightly as Cyber Shield remains stalled and no new protections land.

Sovereignty holds at 23.0: early planning momentum (+1) is offset by capability growth (−1) and ongoing social strain (−1). Political capital falls to 30.0, burdened by portfolio costs and the new measure. Public sentiment inches up slightly on visible adoption efforts, though benefits remain limited.

### A Narrowing Path

The Union acts with urgency, but delivery is partial and legitimacy remains contested. Technical progress is real, but not yet sufficient to shift the balance.
