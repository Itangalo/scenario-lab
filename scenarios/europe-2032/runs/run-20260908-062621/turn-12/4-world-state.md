### Closing the Resilience Directive

The Emergency Resilience Reinforcement Directive formally concludes. Lyon, Stuttgart, and Valencia certify full integration of air-gapped systems into Gaia-X monitoring, unlocking the final Modernisation Fund tranches. Poland and Hungary are legally bound into the mutual aid pact under Article 8, with Cohesion Fund penalties now enforceable. However, Cyprus, Latvia, and Slovakia maintain their exemption demands, forcing the Commission to design bypass protocols for the new Union-Wide Critical Service Failover Protocol, undermining its integrity.

### Surge in Response Capacity

In response to a major AI-generated cyberattack—exploiting a poisoned software dependency across municipal networks—the EU announces the Cyber Incident Response Surge Capacity. Twelve mobile teams are planned via redirected Digital Europe and Horizon budgets, to be hosted by ENISA and equipped with sovereign forensic tools. Emergency data-sharing compacts with compliant providers are under negotiation, allowing only conditional access to model logs pending legal resolution. A live simulation is scheduled for Q3, but operational readiness is delayed by staffing shortages and legal disputes over deployment authority in non-compliant states. The measure is high-cost and category 6; implementation begins this turn but will not be complete before Turn 14.

### Fragmentation and Fallout

A deepening regulatory split emerges: France and Germany impose stricter internal audits on high-risk AI, while Ireland and Estonia relax oversight to attract investment. The Commission initiates infringement proceedings, but the rift hardens into political conflict. Public sentiment worsens as job losses in legal and logistics sectors accelerate, and media spotlight the Union’s inability to prevent or unify response to the cyberattack.

### Sovereignty Gains, Safety Crumbles

The InvestAI Gigafactories project completes, boosting domestic compute and raising sovereignty. Global AI capability advances slightly, but with no safety measures landing on frontier systems, assurance erodes. The attack reveals limited visibility into supply chain dependencies affecting AI-adjacent infrastructure, prompting concern about indirect risks—but not a systemic failure of frontier models themselves. This justifies a moderate decline in `ai_safety`, adjusted to −2.0 from last turn, reflecting growing unease without implying total opacity or unmodelled collapse. Resilience improves by +2.0 due to partial activation of new protocols and momentum from the concluded directive, though full gains await implementation of surge capacity. Political capital erodes under portfolio strain and failure to contain institutional fragmentation.
