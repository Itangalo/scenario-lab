## Priority
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators** — because the cyber_test_shot proved existing defences are blind to advanced persistent threats, and without immediate action to harden systems, even sovereign AI capacity will run on brittle foundations.

## In practice
This turn, we activate the NIS2 Directive’s enforcement mechanisms through the Cyber Resilience Act framework, mandating that all operators of essential services implement next-generation behavioural anomaly detection systems by Q2 2027. We task ENISA with publishing technical standards within three months, co-developed with national CERTs and drawing on lessons from the recent intrusion. A €9bn implementation fund, drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU recovery instruments, will cover 70% of compliance costs for regional utilities and smaller grid operators, removing fiscal resistance in lower-capacity member states.

We are negotiating with the Council’s Telecommunications Working Party to fast-track adoption under Article 12a of the NIS2 Directive, invoking “clear and present cross-border threat” as legal basis. Resistance is expected from two member states citing national security exceptions, but we are offering bilateral cyber capacity upgrades — including secure AI-assisted SOC teams — in exchange for alignment. Simultaneously, we launch the first EU-wide cyber exercise focused on AI-driven intrusions in December, hosted by the European Defence Agency with participation from private operators. This is not optional preparedness — it is the floor beneath everything else we build.
