### The Threshold Deepens

Global AI capability advances to 61.0, as frontier labs report systems autonomously restructuring multi-week workflows in legal, engineering, and logistics domains. Supervision is no longer a reliable control mechanism—developers admit they no longer fully understand how decisions are made under pressure.

Openweight capability rises to 51.0, reflecting continued diffusion and incremental improvements across distributed networks. A growing base of open models, sustained by academic collaboration and shared compute pools, enables steady progress. Some models show performance gains due to refinements in data curation and fine-tuning efficiency, but no single release dominates. The increase is consistent with ongoing accumulation under fixed global pacing—no sudden leap from a discrete event or newly mobilized training effort has occurred. Infrastructure bottlenecks and access fragmentation continue to limit reach, preserving the gap behind closed frontier systems.

### Crisis and Coordination

A major cyber incident strikes: a self-propagating ransomware strain, generated by AI, cripples hospital networks in three countries. Response is fragmented—some cities fail to activate backups, while others lack tested coordination. The Emergency Resilience Reinforcement Directive completes its run, delivering partial hardening. Five cities are now resilient; five are not. Resilience rises to 50.0, but the incident reveals dangerous gaps.

The EU forces final compliance from Poland and Hungary via Council vote, unlocking the last Modernisation Fund tranches. Mutual aid protocols are now legally binding, and Gaia-X access is leveraged to ensure integration. Yet trust frays: three smaller states request exemptions from AI Act enforcement, citing administrative strain, as the audit initiative collapses under political resistance.

In light of the crisis, the European Commission begins drafting the Union-Wide Critical Service Failover Protocol—a high-cost resilience measure that would mandate cross-border redundancy for essential services and require live-fire testing under ENISA. However, due to the existing portfolio burden, formal proposal and implementation cannot begin this turn. No resources are committed, and no timeline is set. The idea is under discussion, but it remains in early conceptual development with no formal status. It does not enter the portfolio, and no proposal bonus is awarded.

### Public Trust Erodes

The Frontline Public Assurance Corps deploys to Marseille, Munich, and Barcelona, mediating AI harm claims. In Barcelona, delayed action fuels renewed sabotage. Public sentiment dips to 30.0 as visible failures outweigh early gains.

Political capital declines. The portfolio remains heavy, gains are limited, and public confidence wavers. With no bandwidth to launch another major initiative, the new protocol stays in preparatory work only, contributing no immediate safety benefit.

AI safety erodes to 13.0, reflecting the systemic strain exposed by the cyberattack and the collapse of the audit initiative. While resilience efforts mitigate some risks, the loss of enforcement coherence and the inability to respond cohesively to emergent threats weaken the overall safety posture. The drop is modest but meaningful—consistent with accumulating fragility, not sudden collapse.
