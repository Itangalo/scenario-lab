## In practice
We are executing the Emergency Sovereign Access Initiative under Article 4 of the TEU and the Digital Markets Act’s emergency clause, with the Joint Research Centre standing up a minimal sovereign stack by December. The €12bn repurposed from REACT-EU and the Connecting Europe Facility is now legally quarantined and ring-fenced for secure inference clusters on EU soil. We have secured hosting rights from two non-US-aligned developers in exchange for mutual data access and export immunity, and their truncated models are undergoing red-team evaluation by the European Defence Agency. Modular data centres at InvestAI sites are being pre-positioned under emergency permitting, bypassing national opt-outs via the revised Tech Sovereignty Package.

But we are also confronting a new front: the **backlash_physical** events show that public tolerance is no longer passive. We are launching a rapid review of siting equity and benefit-sharing for AI infrastructure, to be delivered by the Commission’s Social Observatory for AI by Q1 2028. We are also accelerating the first phase of the public-sector adoption programme under consideration for next turn — not as a distraction, but as a stabiliser: if citizens see AI restoring diagnostics, not just disrupting jobs, the narrative can still shift. We cannot afford to lose both time and trust.
