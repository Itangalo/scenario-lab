### Winter of Fracture

The EU scrambles to stabilize its digital foundations as a cascade of coercion and unrest exposes systemic fragility. The **Emergency Resilience Reinforcement Directive** launches under emergency powers, directing rapid cyber-hardening of municipal systems in ten high-risk regions. Air-gapped controls and mutual aid pacts are mandated, but rollout is patchy—some cities lack personnel to implement protocols, and legal disputes delay cross-border automation in Germany and Poland.

Washington’s tightening of chip export controls, justified under the ALLIANCE framework, effectively restricts European access to frontier compute. A broader ASML servicing cutoff, enforced via jurisdiction over embedded American IP, halts upgrades to mid-tier fabrication lines across the bloc. This is not a blockade of China alone—it is a reordering of technological sovereignty, and the EU is on the receiving end.

Public backlash intensifies. Data centre occupations spread across France and Spain, with sabotage damaging power links in two locations. One protester is injured in a confrontation with private security in Bavaria, fueling outrage. The narrative shifts: AI is no longer just failing citizens—it is provoking them.
