## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now see that under terminal capability, sovereignty is not a balance between them, but their synthesis: only systems we can audit and control are both safe *and* ours.  
- Grounds: The ai_capability of 92.0 has rendered all un-auditable systems functionally foreign, regardless of jurisdiction. The cyber_major_incident, enabled by model-generated tooling and landing on public services, confirms that opacity is not just a safety failure but a sovereignty failure — we cannot defend against what we cannot understand, and we cannot act when we must ask permission. The emergent_open_bioscience_breakthrough proves that auditable systems, though slower, are the only ones that can be fully integrated into sovereign operations. Our two mandates, once in tension, now converge: survival and self-determination depend on the same foundation — verifiable control.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer accept dependence on systems we cannot verify, even if that means acting alone.  
- Grounds: The export_control_escalation and eu_frontier_access_denied have shown that access to frontier models is neither reliable nor revocable on our terms. Waiting for clarity from external developers or allies has become a strategic vulnerability. The Union must now treat any system it cannot audit as a threat surface, not a capability. This turn’s cyber_major_incident, executed with AI-generated tooling, demonstrates that the cost of uncertainty is no longer delayed progress but cascading failure. We act — and bind ourselves — on the principle that sovereign agency begins with verifiable control, not raw power.

## New measure  
**Mandate auditable AI in all critical public infrastructure**  
Require full model transparency, reasoning traceability, and open-weight availability for any AI system used in energy, healthcare, water, and transport operations.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +15 (via enforced verifiability in high-consequence domains), resilience +5 (via reduced surprise failure), eu_ai_sovereignty +8 (by anchoring control in EU institutions)  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, binding on member states and operators of essential services

## Priority  
**Open Science Resilience Initiative** — because it is the only measure that directly builds the technical foundation for the new restriction we are imposing: without a credible, sovereign pipeline of auditable models, the mandate would be a dead letter, and we would be banning AI from critical systems without offering a replacement. This turn, with political capital at 5.0, we cannot afford to legislate without capacity — so we double down on delivery.

## In practice  
This turn, we are finalizing the integration of ENISA and ECDC into the joint cyber-biosurveillance pact. The co-located cells in Brussels and Stockholm are now operational, with secure data pipelines linking to national CERTs and bio-labs. Real-time telemetry sharing has been activated with France, Germany, and the Netherlands, though Italy and Sweden remain on observer status, citing legal compatibility issues. We are treating the cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across regional hospitals and transit systems, enabled by AI-generated zero-days — as the validation of our integration path: the attack was detected within hours by the new joint cell, and containment protocols were activated before cascading failure could occur. This success, though narrow, is being used to justify the cost of the Open Science Resilience Initiative in Council discussions.

Simultaneously, we are preparing the legal and technical framework for the new mandate on auditable AI in critical infrastructure. The Commission is drafting a revised Critical Entities Resilience Directive under Article 114 TFEU, to be proposed to the Council and Parliament in Q1 2032. DG ENER, DG SANTE, and DG MOVE are mapping existing AI dependencies in energy grids, hospital systems, and rail networks, identifying high-risk opaque systems for phase-out. We are offering member states a transition fund — €1.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU — to offset replacement costs, conditioned on adoption of open, verifiable models from the Open Science Resilience Initiative. The resistance is strongest in Italy and Sweden, where national providers rely on closed foreign models; we are negotiating bilateral side letters offering extended timelines in exchange for binding commitments to adopt EU-built auditable systems by turn 15. This is not just regulation — it is industrial policy with teeth: we are forcing the market to follow the sovereign capability we are building, not the other way around.
