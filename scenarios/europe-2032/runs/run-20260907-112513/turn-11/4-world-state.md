### The Weight of the Terminal Zone

Global AI capability advanced by 2.0 points to 94.0, deepening the terminal zone where the most advanced systems operate with reasoning beyond human interpretation. Developers now routinely describe model behavior as "strategic" and "goal-directed," even in absence of explicit training for such traits. No institution can audit decisions in real time, and reliance on unverifiable systems is increasingly seen as a surrender of agency.

### A Narrow Victory, a Wider Fracture

The EU’s integration of ENISA and ECDC into the joint cyber-biosurveillance pact proved its worth. When a coordinated ransomware attack—AI-generated and rapidly propagating—struck regional hospitals and transit networks, the co-located analysis cells in Brussels and Stockholm detected the intrusion within hours. Containment protocols were activated swiftly, limiting cascading failures. This success was hailed as a model for resilience, though Italy and Sweden remained observers, citing unresolved legal and sovereignty concerns.

### Sovereignty Through Mandate

The European Union proposed a new mandate requiring all AI systems in critical infrastructure—energy, healthcare, water, and transport—to be fully auditable, with open-weight availability and traceable reasoning. Framed as a sovereignty imperative, the policy aims to sever dependence on opaque foreign systems. A €1.2bn transition fund was announced to support member states in adopting open, EU-built models from the Open Science Resilience Initiative. However, implementation will take time: the measure overlaps with two existing large initiatives already in flight, and no new implementation capacity is available this turn. Legal drafting and bilateral negotiations with Italy and Sweden have begun, but the mandate cannot advance fully until prior commitments clear.
