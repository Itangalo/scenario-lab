## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now see that under terminal capability, sovereignty is not a balance between them, but their synthesis: only systems we can audit and control are both safe *and* ours. That synthesis is now under existential threat, not from capability alone, but from the collapse of the foundations that make sovereign action possible: capital, energy, and political cohesion.  
- Grounds: The ai_investment_collapse and emergent_local_energy_backlash have severed the supply lines of both compute and legitimacy. With eu_frontier_access_denied, we are locked out of the systems that run our hospitals and ministries, but with local moratoriums spreading and investment evaporating, we cannot build our own at the pace required. The terminal zone is widening, but our capacity to respond is contracting. Our identity must now account for the fact that sovereignty is not only a technical condition — auditable AI — but a material and political one, and all three are failing at once.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer accept dependence on systems we cannot verify, even if that means acting alone. That commitment now stands on broken ground: we cannot act at all without capital, energy, or cohesion, and we must now bind ourselves to restoring those first, even if it means slowing the mandate’s rollout.  
- Grounds: The collapse of ai_investment and the energy backlash have destroyed the assumed conditions for delivery. The Open Science Resilience Initiative cannot scale without power and funding, and the auditable AI mandate cannot be enforced without sovereign capacity to supply alternatives. With eu_political_capital at 3.0, we are functionally bankrupt. We must now prioritise survival of the sovereign project over its expansion — not because the threat has diminished, but because we are losing the ability to act on it.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign technical agency through auditable AI — but we now acknowledge that this cannot be achieved without first stabilising the material and political foundations on which it depends: energy access, investment continuity, and member-state alignment. We are extending the commitment’s scope to include securing those foundations, at the cost of delaying full enforcement of the mandate.  
- Grounds: The emergent_local_energy_backlash and ai_investment_collapse are not external shocks but symptoms of a deeper crisis: public and national trust in the EU’s AI trajectory is eroding. Without energy, compute cannot run; without investment, it cannot be built; without cohesion, it cannot be governed. The commitment to auditable AI remains, but its delivery now depends on a broader stabilisation effort. This is not a reversal, but a necessary widening of the binding promise — and it will be costly.

## New measure  
**Stabilise sovereign AI foundations through emergency energy and investment guarantees**  
Establish an EU-level emergency mechanism to secure power allocation for critical AI infrastructure and backstop private investment in sovereign compute.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +10 (by anchoring compute in EU energy and capital), resilience +5 (by ensuring continuity of critical AI services), eu_political_capital +5 (if successful, by demonstrating crisis response)  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, binding on member states with energy surplus and on private hyperscalers receiving EU subsidies or grid access

## Priority  
**Stabilise sovereign AI foundations through emergency energy and investment guarantees** — because without power and capital, the Open Science Resilience Initiative cannot deliver, the auditable AI mandate becomes unenforceable, and our sovereignty evaporates — not from external denial, but from internal collapse.

## In practice  
This turn, we are treating the ai_investment_collapse and emergent_local_energy_backlash as a systemic crisis of sovereign capacity. The Open Science Resilience Initiative has reached its final turn, and while it has delivered foundational models and trained teams, its scaling is blocked: data centres in eight member states are under moratorium, and private partners have withdrawn funding. We are now activating Article 122 TFEU to propose an emergency regulation that would designate sovereign AI infrastructure as critical for Union survival, granting it priority grid access and shielding it from local bans. This will be paired with a €2bn EU co-investment facility, seeded from the European Innovation Council and REACT-EU, to backstop private capital in compute projects that meet openweight and auditability standards.

Simultaneously, we are renegotiating the auditable AI mandate’s rollout with Italy and Sweden, offering extended transition timelines in exchange for binding commitments to adopt EU-built models by turn 15. The denial of frontier access has caused real disruption in hospitals and ministries, but we are framing it not as a failure of foresight but as proof of dependence — and using it to build support for the emergency measures. The Council Presidency is drafting a special summit on AI sovereignty for April, where we will demand a temporary override of national energy moratoriums for projects tied to the Open Science Resilience Initiative’s pipeline. This is not the future we planned — but it is the one we must now fight for.
