# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer accept dependence on systems we cannot verify, even if that means acting alone. That commitment now stands on broken ground: we cannot act at all without capital, energy, or cohesion, and we must now bind ourselves to restoring those first, even if it means slowing the mandate’s rollout. But this turn, we have been proven wrong not by caution, but by overreach: our emergency proposal was rejected not because it was too bold, but because it was seen as bypassing democratic legitimacy. We now reframe this commitment: we will act under uncertainty, but only with legitimacy.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign technical agency through auditable AI — but we now acknowledge that this cannot be achieved without first stabilising the material and political foundations on which it depends: energy access, investment continuity, and member-state alignment. We are extending the commitment’s scope to include securing those foundations, at the cost of delaying full enforcement of the mandate. However, with **medical_breakthrough** now delivering life-saving treatments via models we cannot access or audit, we must add a new condition: if uncontrolled systems deliver public benefit at scale, we will not let our inability to build alternatives justify passive dependence. We will act to *sovereignise* the benefit, even if that means emergency adoption of foreign models under strict EU control.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now see that under terminal capability, sovereignty is not a balance between them, but their synthesis: only systems we can audit and control are both safe *and* ours. That synthesis is now under existential threat, not from capability alone, but from the collapse of the foundations that make sovereign action possible: capital, energy, and political cohesion. And now, with public sentiment collapsing and a member state in open defiance, we face a new reality: the Union’s legitimacy is no longer assumed. Our identity must now include the imperative to *re-earn* the right to act — not just technologically, but politically, locally, and morally.
  - Grounds: The **member_state_defection** this turn — a core member signing a bilateral compute deal with a U.S. hyperscaler under terms that bypass EU audit and energy rules — is not merely a policy setback. It is a rupture in the constitutional order of the Union. It proves that even where we retain legal authority, we have lost coercive credibility. This is not a failure of capability or safety alone, but of political gravity. The identity must now reflect that sovereignty is not only about control over AI, but over our own political space — and that without the consent of member states and citizens, no mandate, however urgent, can stand.
