## New measure
Secure Open-Source Frontier Equivalents through Defensive Publication  
Launch a coordinated EU programme to produce and release open-weight models that match or exceed the capability of currently restricted frontier systems, ensuring sovereign access even under denial.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`

## Priority
Secure Open-Source Frontier Equivalents through Defensive Publication — because the denial of access this turn has exposed a critical dependency, and accelerating sovereign capacity through open but secured frontier-equivalent models is the fastest path to closing the strategic gap without waiting for physical compute build-outs that remain years from completion.

## In practice  
We are acting on three fronts: legal, technical, and political. Legally, we are expanding the scope of Article 222 TFEU to include not just emergency access to foreign models, but the authority to commission and release sovereign equivalents when access is denied for strategic reasons. This is being coordinated through the European Commission’s Directorate-General for Communications Networks, Content and Technology, with input from the European Cybersecurity Agency on classification thresholds for release.

Technically, we are redirecting a portion of the InvestAI fund — originally earmarked for infrastructure — toward model development, partnering with the French-Italian research consortium MosaicML-EU and the Nordic AI Security Lab to train and validate open-weight models up to capability level 55. These models will be released under a “protected open” licence: weights publicly available, but inference restricted to EU-secured infrastructure unless waived under mutual trust agreements. Germany has agreed to host the validation suite, provided its own public health AI pipeline gets priority integration — a concession we accepted to maintain cohesion.

Politically, we are framing this not as a break from openness but as its defence: the ASML leak demonstrated that knowledge diffusion is inevitable, so our best protection is to shape that diffusion ourselves. The Council of the EU will vote on the funding reallocation in May, and we are offering Spain and Poland co-leadership roles in the model stewardship board to secure broader support. This is not a replacement for compute independence — that remains the long pole — but a necessary hedge while the bricks and wires catch up.
