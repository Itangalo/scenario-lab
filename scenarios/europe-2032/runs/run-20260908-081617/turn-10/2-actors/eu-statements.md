## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Ensure EU institutions can enforce continuous, auditable access to any AI system above 75.0 capability that supports essential public functions or poses systemic risk, regardless of origin or ownership
  - Trigger: the emergent_verification_breakthrough and the collapse of sovereign compute integration
  - Grounds: The frontier has moved beyond ownership — sovereignty now turns on access, not hardware. The failure of integration means we do not control what we own; the verification breakthrough means we can now detect deception at scale, but only if we can reach the systems. Strategic dependence is no longer about compute or chips, but about whether the Union can compel transparency from those who operate systems that govern our critical infrastructure. This shift demands a new formulation: not preventing dependence, but guaranteeing enforceable access as a condition of market access and public use.
  - Evidence quoted: emergent_verification_breakthrough (emergent event): A major AI lab announces a breakthrough in automated verification of model reasoning, enabling fast and reliable detection of deceptive or goal-directed behaviour in systems above capability 70. The method works post-hoc on already-deployed models and is quickly adopted by red teams globally. This shifts the balance between offensive and defensive actors in AI security, though it does not close the gap in interpretability.
  - Reason: The breakthrough enables detection of deception in high-capability AI, but only if access to systems is guaranteed—directly altering the basis for ensuring sovereignty under the commitment.
