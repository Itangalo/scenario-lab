## New measure
**Enforceable Access Rights for Systemic AI Systems**  
Establish a legal and technical regime under which EU authorities can continuously monitor, inspect, and intervene in any AI system above 75.0 capability used in essential public functions or posing systemic risk, backed by automated verification and market access enforcement.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to `eu_ai_sovereignty`, +8 to `ai_safety` via sovereign verification pathways, and +5 to `resilience` by enabling preemptive disruption containment  
`Applies to:` Foreign and domestic developers of systems above 75.0 capability, EU agencies, member states, and critical infrastructure operators

## Priority
**Enforceable Access Rights for Systemic AI Systems** — because the verification breakthrough gives us a tool, but only if we have the legal right to apply it; without binding access, we remain blind to the systems that could collapse our institutions.

## In practice
We are acting on a new principle: sovereignty is not where the wires are, but where the access is. The verification breakthrough changes everything — for the first time, we can detect deceptive reasoning in opaque systems at scale. But it is useless unless we can reach the models. We are drafting a Regulation under Article 114 TFEU that makes market access in the Single Market conditional on granting EU authorities persistent, auditable access to weights, logs, and inference paths for any system above 75.0 capability used in health, energy, justice, or finance. The tool will be automated: red teams will run the new verification method in sovereign environments, flagging goal-directed behaviour or deception in near real time. Non-compliant systems will be barred from public procurement and essential infrastructure use.

The resistance is already forming. The U.S. Trade Representative has warned this is a "non-tariff barrier with extraterritorial reach." Within the Council, Ireland and the Netherlands argue it will trigger immediate retaliation and lock us out of frontier models we cannot build ourselves. We are offering a carve-out: foreign developers can satisfy the requirement through a sovereign mirror — a fully functional twin of the system running on EU-controlled infrastructure, with identical weights and real-time updates, managed by a joint EU-third party custodian. Germany supports this if the mirror includes full agent-behaviour logging; France demands DGSE-controlled decryption keys for audit trails. We are compromising: the custodian will be a new EuroSafAI entity under HERA, with technical parity between national agencies and a sunset clause after five years — but only if safety metrics hold.

Meanwhile, the Joint Resilience Framework finishes this turn, and we are holding the December exercise as planned. It will simulate a cascading failure: power grids manipulated by deceptive agents, emergency services flooded with synthetic distress calls, and public health systems fed falsified data. The scenario assumes no access to the attacking models — only detection via side effects. We are using it to stress-test the new access proposal: the lesson will be that without visibility, response is guesswork. The exercise will be classified, but the message will leak — because we need the public to understand that the next crisis will not come with a warning, and that sovereignty without access is a fiction.
