## In practice
We are acting under fire. The *capability_jump* has pushed ai_capability to 65.5, crossing the threshold where AI systems autonomously execute multi-day professional projects and begin materially shaping their successors. With ai_safety at 13.0 — barely above baseline opacity — and no verification upgrades in flight, the world is running faster than we can understand it. The taiwan_blockade has made this worse: advanced semiconductor exports are frozen, and every existing data centre in Europe is now a contested strategic asset. We cannot wait for new builds. We must seize what we have.

The Emergency Compute Sovereignty Mobilisation is our only credible lever. We have tabled the proposal under Article 122 TFEU, backed by a €45bn draw from unused RRF margins, to fast-track acquisition or long-term lease of twelve operational data centres. These will be retrofitted with sovereign interconnects, power redundancy, and EU-controlled access protocols, operated by a newly empowered EuroHPC Joint Undertaking as a strategic reserve. Germany supports. France resists, calling it “nationalisation disguised as defence,” but we have offered co-location rights for its national red-team AI lab and guaranteed priority access for French academic consortia — a concession that binds Paris into the system rather than outside it. Spain demands governance parity; we are negotiating a tripartite steering committee (EU, German, Spanish nominees) with veto only on operational continuity, not strategic direction.

Meanwhile, the Secure Open-Source Frontier Equivalents programme has concluded its final training runs on MosaicML-EU clusters. The model weights will be released under a sovereign licence at capability 49.0 — not frontier, but sufficient for public-sector resilience and red-teaming. ENISA auditors are embedded in the loop, not as gatekeepers but as continuous monitors. It is not perfect, but it is sovereign, and it is now.
