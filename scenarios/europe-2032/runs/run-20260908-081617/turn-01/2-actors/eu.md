## Priority
Accelerated Compute Access for Public Institutions — because the denial of frontier access this turn proves that reliance on foreign providers for core governance functions is a strategic vulnerability that must be closed now, regardless of our longer-term industrial build-out.

## In practice
We are acting on two tracks: one legal, one technical. On the legal side, the Commission is invoking Article 222 TFEU (the “solidarity clause”) to establish a temporary emergency access regime, allowing public authorities to use non-EU frontier models under strict data sovereignty protocols — air-gapped inference, EU-appointed custodianship of prompts and outputs, and prohibitions on model fine-tuning from public queries. This is being coordinated through the European Defence Agency’s secure infrastructure unit, with pilot integrations at the European Medicines Agency and national energy regulators already underway.

On the technical track, we are fast-tracking certification of three EU-hosted high-capacity clusters — one in France, one in Finland, one in Poland — to serve as fallback nodes. These will run either licensed models under audited conditions or open-weight versions of comparable capability, secured to a state-actor standard. The Ministry of the Interior in Germany, which initially resisted central coordination, has agreed to participate after we committed to co-funding their local resilience layer. The cost is high, and some member states are pushing back on the centralisation of access, but the cyber_test_shot incident and the sudden loss of model access have shifted the balance: this is no longer about competitiveness. It is about being able to govern at all.
