# Statements: The European Union (turn 7)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor sovereignty and risk resilience

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Anchor EU sovereignty in a fully operational, sovereign AI backbone by turn 9, capable of sustaining essential public functions and red-teaming frontier models under foreign denial, disconnection, or coercion, with legally binding access rights and unified operational command
  - Grounds: The `eu_frontier_access_denied` event has evolved from a shock to a structural condition — dependence is now active vulnerability. The failure of the Emergency Compute Sovereignty Mobilisation to yield integrated control, despite legal ownership, proves that hardware alone is not sovereignty. The commitment must now specify not just capacity, but *functionality under duress*, with enforceable command structures. This turn, Germany, Spain, and France have each blocked integration on national grounds, revealing that without irreversible legal and technical integration, the Union cannot act as a single agent in AI. The revised commitment binds us to deliver not just assets, but a working sovereign system — or admit strategic irrelevance.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
