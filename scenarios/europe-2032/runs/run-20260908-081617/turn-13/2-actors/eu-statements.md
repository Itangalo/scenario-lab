# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Ensure EU institutions can enforce continuous, auditable access to any AI system above 75.0 capability that supports essential public functions or poses systemic risk, regardless of origin or ownership — and, where access cannot be compelled, establish sovereign alternatives capable of performing the same functions under full EU control and monitoring

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Ensure EU institutions can enforce continuous, auditable access to any AI system above 75.0 capability that supports essential public functions or poses systemic risk, regardless of origin or ownership — and, where access cannot be compelled, replace those systems with sovereign alternatives that perform the same functions under full EU control, monitoring, and interpretability, and which are powered by EU-anchored compute, talent, and energy
  - Grounds: The emergent_local_compute_resistance event proves that sovereignty is not just technological or legal, but territorial and social. Our ability to host compute is now contested at the municipal level, and legitimacy is fraying not only in capitals but in communities. The original commitment assumed we could build sovereign systems if we had the will and the capital — but we now face a future where we may have both and still lack the physical and political space to deploy. The revision binds the commitment to material conditions: it is no longer enough to substitute foreign models with domestic ones; we must ground them in infrastructure that is not only ours to control, but accepted as belonging. This is not a narrowing of ambition, but a deepening: sovereignty must be both enforced and earned.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that speed alone can overcome social fracture or physical constraint
  - Grounds: The collapse of public sentiment to 5.0 and the blocking of three major data centres by municipal coalitions reveal a new binding constraint: political capital is not only spent in Brussels or Washington, but in city halls and on power grids. We acted fast, but we did not act *with* the Union’s fabric — we acted *upon* it. The cost has been legitimacy, cohesion, and now, physical deployment. We still reject paralysis, but we now acknowledge that acting under uncertainty includes the risk of alienating the very populations we are meant to protect. Our urgency must henceforth carry its own consent architecture.
