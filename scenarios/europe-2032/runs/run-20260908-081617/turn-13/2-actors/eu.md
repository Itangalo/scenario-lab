## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Ensure EU institutions can enforce continuous, auditable access to any AI system above 75.0 capability that supports essential public functions or poses systemic risk, regardless of origin or ownership — and, where access cannot be compelled, replace those systems with sovereign alternatives that perform the same functions under full EU control, monitoring, and interpretability, and which are powered by EU-anchored compute, talent, and energy  
- Grounds: The emergent_local_compute_resistance event proves that sovereignty is not just technological or legal, but territorial and social. Our ability to host compute is now contested at the municipal level, and legitimacy is fraying not only in capitals but in communities. The original commitment assumed we could build sovereign systems if we had the will and the capital — but we now face a future where we may have both and still lack the physical and political space to deploy. The revision binds the commitment to material conditions: it is no longer enough to substitute foreign models with domestic ones; we must ground them in infrastructure that is not only ours to control, but accepted as belonging. This is not a narrowing of ambition, but a deepening: sovereignty must be both enforced and earned.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that speed alone can overcome social fracture or physical constraint  
- Grounds: The collapse of public sentiment to 5.0 and the blocking of three major data centres by municipal coalitions reveal a new binding constraint: political capital is not only spent in Brussels or Washington, but in city halls and on power grids. We acted fast, but we did not act *with* the Union’s fabric — we acted *upon* it. The cost has been legitimacy, cohesion, and now, physical deployment. We still reject paralysis, but we now acknowledge that acting under uncertainty includes the risk of alienating the very populations we are meant to protect. Our urgency must henceforth carry its own consent architecture.

## In practice
We are acting on a new reality: we can no longer assume that compute follows capital. The emergent_local_compute_resistance event is not a delay — it is a signal that our sovereignty project will fail unless it is rooted in local legitimacy as much as in technological autonomy. We are therefore adjusting our approach to the Sovereign AI Substitution Programme. The two new clusters — in Finland and Bulgaria — will now be subject to binding energy-impact reviews and must include local benefit packages: grid modernisation, public AI access hubs, and job guarantees for regional workers. We are instructing the European AI Agency to pause site finalisation until these agreements are in place.

We are also launching the Energy-Aware Compute Siting Framework under Article 114 TFEU, using the internal market rationale to harmonise what was becoming a patchwork of local bans. The framework will not override national sovereignty, but it will set minimum conditions for EU funding and priority grid access: any operator seeking support from the Digital Europe Programme or Connecting Europe Facility must now demonstrate local consent through a structured negotiation with municipal and energy cooperative representatives. Germany, Poland, and Spain will be offered fast-track approval for one sovereign project each if they adopt the framework by turn 14.

The U.S. has not yet cut component flows, but their silence is tightening. We are preparing reciprocal measures: a draft Commission decision under the Anti-Coercion Instrument stands ready, targeting hyperscaler access to EU cloud markets if export controls block our clusters. But our deeper vulnerability is internal. With political capital at 8.0 and public sentiment at 5.0, we are one misstep from paralysis. So we are not just building compute — we are rebuilding trust. The pivot is not away from urgency, but into a more durable form of it. We are no longer racing the frontier in isolation. We are anchoring our future in the ground beneath us.
