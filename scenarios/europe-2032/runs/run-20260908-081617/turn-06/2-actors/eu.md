## New measure
**Accelerated Sovereign Compute Integration**  
Establish a legally irreversible, operationally independent EU sovereign compute backbone by retrofitting acquired data centres with unified access controls, secure interconnects, and failover protocols, ensuring continuity of critical public AI services under foreign denial or attack.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to `eu_ai_sovereignty`, +5 to `resilience`, stabilise `ai_safety` by enabling sovereign red-teaming and deployment of EU-controlled safeguards  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, EuroHPC Joint Undertaking, and designated national operators in member states

## Priority
**Accelerated Sovereign Compute Integration** — because the `eu_frontier_access_denied` event has turned theoretical dependence into lived vulnerability, and we must now harden sovereign access not just by owning hardware, but by making it functionally resilient to disconnection, sabotage or coercion.

## In practice
We are acting in the wake of denial. The leading model — once embedded in hospital diagnostics, border systems, and research pipelines — has been cut off from EU users without explanation or appeal. This is not a market fluctuation. It is a political act with operational teeth. The Emergency Compute Sovereignty Mobilisation has now concluded: twelve data centres in Germany, Spain, and Poland are under EU legal title, acquired through emergency RRF drawdowns and national cooperation. But ownership is not control. These facilities remain fragmented, running on disparate stacks, with no unified security protocol, no sovereign interconnect, and no failover mechanism. They are assets, not a backbone.

We now move to integrate them. Under Article 346 TFEU, we are invoking the newly established EuroHPC Sovereign Integration Directorate to standardise access via a zero-trust EU AI gateway, enforce weight encryption to state-actor standards, and deploy a dedicated dark fibre mesh between nodes. This is not merely technical — it is constitutional. We are negotiating with Germany and Spain to cede operational command to a Council-confirmed directorate, not a coalition of national interests. France remains reluctant, fearing loss of strategic autonomy, so we are offering it guaranteed peacetime access for its national red-team lab and co-leadership of the audit board. The goal is not just resilience, but irrevocability: once this network is live, no external actor can disable it, and no member state can unplug it unilaterally.

At the same time, the Tech Sovereignty Package nears its final Council vote. We are trading accelerated permitting for equity: in return for fast-tracking grid connections and land use, we demand that private hyperscalers grant the EU pre-negotiated access to 15% of peak compute during emergencies. It is not ownership, but it is leverage — and in a world where `ai_capability` is at 68.5 and rising, leverage is the only currency that buys time.
