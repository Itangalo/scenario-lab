## New measure  
We must act now. The legal fog has not lifted, but the world has moved: the *capability_jump* and *taiwan_blockade* have redefined our strategic environment in one stroke. We can no longer wait for Article 222 clarity. Our dependency is now a national security vulnerability, and the semiconductor blockade makes compute sovereignty not just political but physical. We propose:

**Emergency Compute Sovereignty Mobilisation**  
Launch an emergency directive to fast-track state-backed acquisition of existing high-performance data centres across the EU, repurposing them for sovereign AI use under permanent legal title.

## In practice  
We are acting under fire. The *capability_jump* has pushed ai_capability to nearly 60, while our access remains conditional and fragile. With the taiwan_blockade cutting off advanced semiconductor exports, every watt of existing compute in Europe becomes a contested asset. We cannot build new gigafactories fast enough — not with permitting, grid upgrades, and chip shortages stretching timelines. So we are seizing what we already have.

The Commission will table an emergency proposal under Article 122 TFEU (public security) by month’s end, backed by a €45bn emergency appropriation from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused margins. This will fund direct acquisition or long-term lease of at least twelve operational data centres currently owned by hyperscalers or decommissioned financial firms, retrofitting them with sovereign interconnects and power redundancy. The sites will be managed by a newly empowered EuroHPC Joint Undertaking, now mandated to operate as a strategic reserve.

France still resists, calling this “nationalisation disguised as defence.” We are offering Paris co-location rights for its national red-team AI lab and guaranteed priority access for French academic consortia — a concession, but one that binds them into the system rather than outside it. Germany, initially cool, now supports the move as a necessary response to supply shock. Spain demands governance parity; we are drafting a tripartite steering committee (EU, German, Spanish nominees) with veto only on operational continuity, not on strategic direction.

At the same time, we are accelerating the Secure Open-Source Frontier Equivalents programme through parallel distillation runs on the redirected MosaicML-EU clusters. The Nordic Lab’s control concerns remain, so we are embedding ENISA auditors directly into training loops — not as gatekeepers, but as continuous monitors. It is not perfect assurance, but it is movement. And in this turn, movement is survival.
