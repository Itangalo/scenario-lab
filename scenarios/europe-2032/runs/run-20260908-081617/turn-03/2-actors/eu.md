## In practice  
We are operating under strain: three large category-4 measures still in flight, political capital at 37.0, and no margin for failure. The completion of *Accelerated Compute Access for Public Institutions* delivers a quiet but real win — every major health agency, civil protection directorate, and energy regulator now has verified access to models at capability level ~55 through secured gateways, reducing operational dependence on foreign providers. That access is temporary, conditional on continued goodwill, and technically fragile — but it held through the winter crisis drills, and that counts.

The core struggle now is legal and political. The European Court of Justice’s signal on Article 222 TFEU has frozen momentum: we cannot legally commission sovereign equivalents without a clear mandate that such action falls under emergency technological self-defence. The Commission’s Legal Service is drafting an urgent opinion, while we lobby informally in the Permanent Representatives Committee for a narrow interpretation that would allow the current programme to proceed under “strategic continuity” grounds. France remains sceptical, fearing mission creep; Spain demands a seat on the technical validation board as price for support. We have offered Madrid co-chairmanship with Finland on the stewardship council and additional funding for regional adaptation hubs — a move that may yet split the southern bloc if Italy feels excluded.

On the technical side, MosaicML-EU has begun pre-training on the redirected InvestAI compute allocation, but progress is slow. The distillation pipeline from leaked frontier benchmarks is working, but interpretability tools remain primitive, and we cannot verify alignment properties at scale. The Nordic Lab has flagged two potential control failures in early agent simulations — not catastrophic, but enough to justify the Court’s caution. We are responding by fast-tracking a sub-measure under category 5: limited third-party access for EASA and ENISA to audit model behaviour in high-risk domains, using the newly stood-up evaluation sandbox in Tallinn. This does not restart our public safety credibility, but it begins to close the loop between capability and assurance — quietly, and within existing budgets.
