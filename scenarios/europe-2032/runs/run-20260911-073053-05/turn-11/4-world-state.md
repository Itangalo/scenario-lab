### The winter of repair
The second half of 2031 did not bring peace, only routine. Convoys with spare transformers moved under escort to the fenced grid works. Engineers in high-visibility vests spliced cable under naval observation while hospitals kept their priority dispatch and clinics kept their backup schedules. One gigafactory link carried partial load through autumn; the other remained a concrete yard with floodlights.

The emergency Shield held because mayors and works councils guarded it. In exchange for night watches around substations and data halls, therapy queues stayed open and ports kept short shifts. Finance ministers still refused new money, so the Commission borrowed and re-labelled. It kept lights on, not much more.

### Spares and science
The monthly middle-power table became the Union's real foreign ministry. Export-licence lists were aligned, a pool of spares and inference hours was divided by need, and Brussels repeated its offer of cable landings and cleanrooms as protected neutral infrastructure. No belligerent signed, but neither struck the sites either. Insurers still priced the Atlantic as a war zone, and shipments rerouted south.

Amid this, EU-linked labs announced an AI-designed solid-state electrolyte that sharply cut battery fire risk. The press ran it as a European result from European industrial capacity, with pilot cells shown on a factory floor. It did not restore graduate hiring or clear evaluation backlogs — reviewers had long stopped pre-clearing frontier releases — but for the first time in a year evening news carried an AI story about something made, not something lost.

By December, the Union was still rationed, still neutral, still borrowing. It had not regained capacity, but it had shown it could share scarcity without splitting.
