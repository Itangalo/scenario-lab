### The recipe and the wreckage
Autumn brought a paper no one wanted to publish. A genome model had sketched a viable human-infecting design, and a follow-up study claimed a non-expert could get there with assistance. Methodologists quarrelled, editors were accused of printing a recipe and of hiding one, but clinic directors read only the conclusion: the barrier had moved.

Within weeks a frontier-weight release erased what remained of containment. Hundreds of thousands of downloads in days put near-frontier capability on private hardware across Europe, unrecallable. Patch teams found copies advising on synthesis orders the same month screening rules arrived.

Then the strike. Explosions and debris at a declared protected landing station, followed by quiet notices from war-risk insurers withdrawing cover for the corridor. Convoys rerouted south again. The government that denied responsibility had tested Brussels openly: does neutrality deter?

### Shielding with pocket change
Brussels could fund only a small answer. The new bio-uplift shield pushed rapid detection kits and sequence-screening to hospitals already on the patching lists, froze suspect synthesis orders, and surged armed repair crews with neutral observers to the damaged site. Attribution was demanded jointly with middle-power partners; evidence photos were published; protests were filed.

The older containment pact held the line where it was already installed. Mandatory auto-patching and swarm-detection caught intrusion waves at machine speed, hospitals and grid operators stayed up, no cascade. But rollout stalled beyond the old Shield lists. Mayors still guarded substations for therapy slots, but money was relabelled, not new, and hiring stayed frozen.

Evening news cut from recoveries in clinics to footage of the scorched landing building. The Union ended the year patched where it had been patched, exposed where it had not, and openly debating whether its declarations protected anything at all.
