## New measure  
**Secure Open-Weight Monitoring and Response (SOMR)**  
Launch a distributed, real-time monitoring network for open-weight models to detect and respond to emergent capabilities and misuse patterns.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` openweight_capability −5 (delayed diffusion of dangerous capabilities), ai_safety +10 (via early detection and shared understanding), resilience +5 (indirectly, through faster response)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, open-source AI communities, and academic institutions within the Union

## Priority  
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)** — because the cyber_major_incident has already landed, exposing critical infrastructure to coordinated AI-enabled attacks, and delaying this measure further would mean accepting that the Union is structurally unprepared to defend its essential services.

## In practice  
We are advancing CS-CO through the Competitiveness Council with a revised offer: co-financing now covers 50% of compliance costs for the first three years, drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and front-loaded by the European Commission. ENISA is preparing to activate its new mandate—designating AI incident telemetry hubs in each member state and launching cross-border red-teaming exercises by June. Germany and the Nordics remain cautious, but the recent attack has shifted the tone; we are now negotiating not whether to act, but how fast. The legal base under Article 114 TFEU remains our anchor—qualified majority voting is still within reach if we secure the Benelux and Eastern flank.

Meanwhile, InvestAI Gigafactories has entered detailed site assessment, with Finland and Spain emerging as frontrunners due to grid availability and cooling potential. We are fast-tracking EIB guarantees and pushing for state-aid clearance by Q3. The Tech Sovereignty Package faces continued resistance in France and Italy, where local opposition frames data centres as energy drains. In response, we are finalising regional digital transition funds—€1.2bn tied to retraining and local AI adoption—to convert resistance into partnership. On the new front, SOMR is being scoped with the Joint Research Centre and ELLIS institutes to build a distributed network that can detect emergent capabilities in open-weight models before they spread. This is not about control—it is about visibility, early warning, and shared defence. We are not waiting for another breach to realise that openweight_capability at 41.0 is already weaponised.
