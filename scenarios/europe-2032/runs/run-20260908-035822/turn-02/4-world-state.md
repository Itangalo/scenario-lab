### Frontier Creeps Forward, Safety Cracks Open  
Global AI capability advances to 54.0, driven by iterative improvements and compressed release cycles. While the pace remains steady, the gap between capability and safety widens sharply. The `safety_breakthrough` event does not materialize—despite progress in interpretability research, no new control mechanism lands on deployed systems. Instead, the `cyber_major_incident` confirms that offensive AI tools are already in circulation, generated by models at or near the open frontier. As a result, `ai_safety` plummets by 5 points to 28.0, reflecting both the absence of safeguards and the demonstrated use of AI in coordinated attacks.

### Open-Weight Under Scrutiny  
With `openweight_capability` rising to 42.5—midway between its prior value and last turn’s `ai_capability`—the EU launches the Secure Open-Weight Monitoring and Response (SOMR) initiative. A network of academic and technical nodes begins scoping across member states to detect emergent misuse patterns. However, the effort faces skepticism from open-source communities, who see it as surveillance under the guise of safety. No immediate technical gains emerge, and the distributed nature of open-weight models limits early visibility.

### Cyber Shield Pushed, But at a Cost  
The EU prioritizes the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO), revising co-financing terms to cover 50% of compliance costs. ENISA prepares to designate AI incident telemetry hubs, and red-teaming exercises are scheduled. Yet Germany and Nordic states remain hesitant, demanding more clarity on liability and implementation timelines. The political push delays other initiatives, and public sentiment sours as news of the cyberattack spreads. Local protests, amplified by the *Right to Review* movement, frame the response as reactive rather than preventative.

### Political Capital Erodes  
The portfolio now includes three large measures—InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, and CS-CO—each costing −3, plus the priority −1, for a total charge of −10. SOMR, newly added, adds another −3, bringing the total burden to −13. Despite the urgency of the cyber incident, no proposal bonus applies—SOMR is seen as complementary, not decisive. `eu_political_capital` drops to 30.0. `resilience` holds at 40.0, as no measure has yet landed. `eu_ai_sovereignty` remains unchanged—no capacity event occurred, and the rise in `ai_capability` by 1 point does not trigger rule 5’s −1 term.
