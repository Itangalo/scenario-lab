## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Ensure that any AI system operating above 45.0 capability within or against EU jurisdictions is subject to enforceable accountability mechanisms — including attribution, legal consequence, and redress — regardless of origin, ownership, or deployment method  
- Trigger: the **emergent_labour_protests_escalate** event this turn  
- Grounds: The cross-border strikes in France and Spain have revealed a fatal blind spot: our accountability doctrine, while legally robust, lacks social legitimacy. Workers on the front lines of automation see the AF-HCS not as protection but as elite retaliation, shielding systems they distrust while offering no buffer against job loss or erosion of professional autonomy. This undermines enforcement, as compliance depends on public cooperation and institutional buy-in. To hold the commitment, we must now expand it beyond technical control and financial interdiction to include visible, immediate protections for those displaced by high-capability systems. Accountability cannot be only downward — toward rogue models and foreign providers — but must also be upward, toward the societies these systems transform.

## New measure  
**Expand the Accountability Framework with Labour Transition Safeguards (AF-HCS-LTS)**  
Link access to high-capability AI systems above 45.0 capability to enforceable commitments from adopting institutions to retrain, redeploy, or compensate workers displaced by automation in public services.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` public_sentiment +12 (by addressing core grievances behind protests), resilience +4 (by reducing social shock from displacement), eu_political_capital -3 (initial cost due to resistance from efficiency-driven agencies), ai_safety +2 (indirectly, by increasing institutional oversight of deployment conditions)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, public-sector employers, and private contractors using high-capability AI in healthcare, transport, and administrative services within EU jurisdictions

## Priority  
**Accountability Framework for High-Capability Systems (AF-HCS)** — because the **emergent_labour_protests_escalate** has made clear that without social legitimacy, even the most powerful enforcement regime will fracture; this measure must now evolve from a tool of external deterrence into one of internal fairness, or risk collapse under its own weight.

## In practice  
We are treating the strikes not as obstruction, but as a diagnostic: the EU’s credibility on AI hinges not just on controlling frontier models, but on governing their consequences. The **AF-HCS**, already nearing completion, becomes the vehicle for integrating labour safeguards into accountability itself. We are amending the framework so that any public body deploying a model above 45.0 capability must submit a workforce transition plan — vetted by the newly empowered European Labour Authority — detailing how affected roles will be retrained, redeployed, or compensated. This applies equally to private vendors delivering AI-driven services under EU contract. We are routing enforcement through the same AMLD6-aligned mechanism: non-compliance triggers suspension of procurement eligibility and, where applicable, access to EU cloud gateways.

To gain traction, we are targeting the pilot group — Belgium, the Netherlands, Finland — first, offering them €3.2bn in additional cohesion funding to co-design the transition rules. We are also engaging ETUC and BUSINESSEUROPE in trilateral talks under the Social Dialogue Protocol, framing this not as restriction but as managed transformation. Behind the scenes, we are revising the delegated act on red-teaming to require audits not only of model behaviour but of organisational impact — a small shift that embeds human consequence into technical evaluation. This is no concession to protest; it is the recalibration of power. If we cannot protect both the system *and* the citizen, we protect neither.
