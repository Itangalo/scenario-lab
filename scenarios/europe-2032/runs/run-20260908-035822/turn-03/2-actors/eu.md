Our commitment must reflect that leverage is now as critical as compute. While the goal of autonomous capacity remains, it must now be pursued through coalition, not isolation.

## New measure  
**Launch the European AI Security Partnership (EASP)**  
Establish a binding coordination framework among EU member states and aligned middle powers to jointly manage access to critical AI inputs—compute, chips, talent, and data—through aligned export controls, shared evaluation capacity, and mutual leverage.

`Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_political_capital +10 (via coalition legitimacy and shared burden), ai_safety +5 (through coordinated pre-release evaluations), eu_ai_sovereignty +10 (by securing access to frontier systems on EU terms)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Canada, and Norway

## Priority  
**Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO)** — because the cyber_major_incident has already exposed systemic fragility, and with Germany and the Nordics still hesitating, delaying implementation risks rendering the entire resilience agenda a paper exercise.

## In practice  
We are finalising the legal and technical framework for CS-CO through the Competitiveness Council, with ENISA preparing to designate AI incident telemetry hubs in each member state by Q1 2028. The 50% co-financing offer remains on the table, drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and front-loaded by Commission liquidity. We are now targeting a qualified majority under Article 114 TFEU, leaning on the Benelux and Eastern member states to counterbalance Nordic caution. The cyber incident has shifted the tone: this is no longer about compliance, but about survival of essential services.

Meanwhile, InvestAI Gigafactories has entered site validation, with Finland and Spain advancing to final review. We are fast-tracking EIB guarantees and state-aid clearance, but grid integration remains a bottleneck. The Tech Sovereignty Package continues to face resistance in France and Italy, where local actors frame data centres as extractive. In response, we are finalising €1.2bn in regional digital transition funds—tied to retraining and local AI adoption—to convert opposition into partnership. SOMR is now scoped with the Joint Research Centre and ELLIS, building a distributed detection network for open-weight models; early focus is on offensive cyber patterns, with pilot nodes in Germany, France, and the Netherlands.

The new EASP initiative builds directly on the `middle_power_coalition` event: we are convening a summit in December 2027 to lock in export-licence alignment and shared evaluation protocols. This is not about ceding sovereignty—it is about multiplying it. By acting together, we gain leverage over the full stack: from TSMC’s fabs to NVIDIA’s export licences to the talent pipelines of Toronto and Zurich. The coalition is not a club; it is a switch we can flip when the frontier moves too fast. This is sovereignty through interdependence—our new line of defence.
