## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Ensure that any AI system operating above 45.0 capability within or against EU jurisdictions is subject to enforceable accountability mechanisms — including attribution, legal consequence, and redress — regardless of origin, ownership, or deployment method  
- Trigger: the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event this turn  
- Grounds: The abrupt denial of access to the leading model — a system operating at 57.0 capability — has shattered any remaining illusion that accountability can be maintained through access or cooperation with external developers. Hospitals in three member states lost real-time diagnostic support; ministries scrambled to restore services. This was not a failure of enforcement, but of dependency. The original commitment assumed we could impose accountability *through* engagement with providers. We now face a world where the providers are gone, and the model remains. Our accountability framework must now rest not on upstream leverage, but on downstream consequence: we must be able to attribute harm, freeze value flows, and respond in kind — even when the actor is unknown, and the model is open. This turn, we pivot from regulating systems to weaponising interdependence in retaliation.

## New measure  
**Strengthen the Accountability Framework with Retaliatory Financial Interdiction (AF-HCS-RFI)**  
Embed automatic financial freezing and counter-access denial into the AF-HCS to ensure that any entity benefiting from unaccountable AI systems above 45.0 capability faces immediate economic and operational consequences.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +10 (by creating credible deterrence through retaliation), resilience +6 (by disrupting value chains that sustain unaccountable models), eu_political_capital +3 (if successful, due to demonstrated agency; -5 if perceived as overreach)

## Priority  
**Accountability Framework for High-Capability Systems (AF-HCS)** — because the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has made clear that accountability without retaliation is surrender, and this measure is now the only instrument capable of restoring strategic parity by turning economic interdependence into a weapon of last resort.

## In practice  
We are treating the denial of frontier access not as a crisis, but as a confirmation: the EU’s leverage no longer lies in access, but in consequence. The **AF-HCS**, already in flight, becomes the vehicle for a new doctrine — *accountability through retaliation*. We are fast-tracking amendments under Article 5 of the Digital Markets Act and AMLD6 to enable the automatic freezing of any financial transaction tied to unregistered models above 45.0 capability. The European Central Bank is being consulted on operational protocols; we are drafting a delegated act that allows the Commission, on forensic recommendation from ENISA and Europol, to designate a model as high-risk and trigger a cascade: API access blocked at EU cloud gateways, payment processing suspended, and any affiliated domains blacklisted in EU DNS registries.

We are also leveraging the **emergent_regional_ai_pact** not as a rival, but as a testbed. Three member states — Belgium, the Netherlands, and Finland — have agreed to pilot the AF-HCS-RFI provisions through a joint procurement firewall: any vendor using a model above 45.0 must either register its weights with the JRC or be disqualified. We are routing €12bn in smart-city contracts through this mechanism, creating a live demonstration of enforceability. At the same time, we are preparing a legal opinion on whether the **export_control_escalation** — now excluding even allied buyers — violates WTO principles, not to file a case, but to signal that we are prepared to respond asymmetrically. The message is clear: if access is weaponised, so too is interdependence.
