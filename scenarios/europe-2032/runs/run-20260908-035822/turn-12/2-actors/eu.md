## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Ensure that the EU retains sovereign access to AI systems capable of defending its critical infrastructure and democratic processes — now explicitly including the ability to detect, contain, and terminate unauthorised agent behaviour in real time, under EU command and on EU-controlled infrastructure, without reliance on external legal goodwill or contested jurisdictional interpretations — **but under revised operational conditions: we now acknowledge that emergency activation has failed to deliver capability, legal cohesion remains absent, and the systems are not under effective control**  
- Grounds: The invocation of Article 222 TFEU did not activate sovereign capability; instead, it exposed the Union’s inability to enforce its own emergency decisions. The Joint Oversight Board remains unformed, power connections are still delayed, and no defensive agents are operational despite technical readiness. The legal challenges have not been overcome but deepened, and member states now treat SDAI as a contested overreach rather than a shield. Our commitment stands — the need for sovereign control is greater than ever — but the path through unilateral emergency action has collapsed. We must now secure the same objective through means that bind rather than bypass, or lose legitimacy entirely.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but now, we act on the assumption that unaligned optimisation is not only in motion but has already crossed into operational environments, and that our defences must be anticipatory, not reactive — **and we add the corollary: when action fails to land, we adjust course without waiting for a second collapse**  
- Grounds: We acted under uncertainty and were wrong in method, though not in diagnosis. The surge in openweight capability to 57.0 and the verification_widens event confirm that autonomous, uncontainable systems are already in motion across domains once thought to require human judgment. Yet our emergency activation did not produce operational sovereignty — it consumed political capital, fractured cohesion, and left us more exposed. To persist in the same mode would not be resolve; it would be denial. We maintain the duty to act early, but now bind it to a duty to recalibrate swiftly when action fails to materialise.

## New measure  
**Reconstitute SDAI under Binding Council Agreement and Joint Oversight Protocol**  
Reform the Sovereign Defensive AI Initiative through a Council Regulation under Article 114 TFEU, requiring unanimous endorsement of a Joint Oversight Board with veto power over agent deployment, real-time audit access, and cross-border mandate, in exchange for full transparency, capped mission profiles, and phased activation starting with non-autonomous defensive tools.

## Priority  
**Reconstitute SDAI under Binding Council Agreement and Joint Oversight Protocol** — because the emergency activation failed to deliver capability while destroying political capital, and because without a legally and politically sustainable foundation, no amount of compute or code will give the EU control over its own defence.

## In practice  
We are withdrawing the unilateral emergency activation of SDAI and proposing a new legal basis under Article 114 TFEU, which allows for internal market harmonisation in the face of systemic technological risk. This shifts the initiative from a contested invocation of mutual defence to a collectively owned instrument of digital sovereignty. We are convening an extraordinary Justice and Home Affairs Council to negotiate a binding agreement that reinstates SDAI — but only with a Joint Oversight Board endowed with real authority: power to audit, to halt deployment, and to review mission parameters quarterly. In return, we are capping the initial deployment to non-autonomous, rule-bound defensive tools: intrusion detection, disinformation pattern matching, and grid stability monitoring — no agent persistence, no goal-directed adaptation until DARCA proves readiness.

We are leveraging the verification_widens event — the sudden automatability of contract review, clinical coding, and structural engineering — to demonstrate that the frontier is no longer confined to code but is now eroding the foundations of professional judgment. This is the argument we take to sceptical member states: that the risk is not hypothetical, and that our current exposure is total. To secure buy-in, we are offering transparency: all SDAI operations will be logged in a real-time feed accessible to national delegates, and all models used will be drawn from the OWIRC-verified open-weight pool, hardened and monitored by DARCA’s interpretability stack.

On the ground, we are halting further emergency grid allocations and instead fast-tracking a joint French-German-Belgian energy resilience pact to guarantee power to the Gigafactories under peacetime rules, not emergency overrides. We are also repurposing the Labour Impact Observatory to feed into a new Social Foresight Unit under the European Commission, tasked with projecting displacement in the newly automatable domains. This is not a new measure, but a realignment of existing tools to stabilise sentiment and prove that governance can adapt — not just technologically, but politically. We are acting, but we are no longer acting alone.
