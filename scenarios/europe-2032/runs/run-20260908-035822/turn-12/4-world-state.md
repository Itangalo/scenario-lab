### A Shift in Strategy, Not a Turnaround

The European Union formally abandoned the emergency activation of the Sovereign Defensive AI Initiative, acknowledging its failure to deliver operational capability amid legal fragmentation. In its place, a new push began under Article 114 TFEU to reconstitute SDAI through a binding Council agreement, centred on a Joint Oversight Board with audit and veto powers. The revised plan capped initial deployments to non-autonomous tools—intrusion detection, disinformation filtering, and grid monitoring—pending DARCA’s readiness to handle adaptive agents.

Despite the more inclusive approach, consensus remained elusive. Several member states questioned the necessity of centralised control over defensive AI, while others demanded guarantees on national data sovereignty. The extraordinary Justice and Home Affairs Council ended without agreement, delaying the Oversight Board’s formation. No new compute came online, and power allocations to Luxembourg and Marseille remained stalled under national regulatory review.

### Capability Creeps Forward

Global AI capability rose to 63.0, driven by the **verification_widens** event: contract review, clinical coding, and structural engineering now admit automated validation, accelerating progress in high-stakes domains once thought immune to full automation. Open-weight models followed, reaching 58.5 as developers released increasingly capable agent frameworks under open licenses. The gap between closed and open systems narrowed further, increasing misuse risks.

DARCA continued integrating interpretability tools but still had no live agents to monitor. The Labour Impact Observatory was repurposed into a Social Foresight Unit, but public trust remained low. Strikes in legal and healthcare sectors intensified, fed by visible displacement in coding and diagnostics roles.

### Political Capital Dwindles

The EU’s political capital eroded further. The failure of the emergency SDAI activation, combined with the ongoing portfolio burden and lack of visible progress, deepened institutional fatigue. The new large measure was added, but no immediate gains followed. Sentiment held at 28.0, reflecting persistent anxiety over job losses and opaque governance.
