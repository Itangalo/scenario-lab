## New measure
**Launch Sovereign Defensive AI Initiative (SDAI)**  
Establish a legally anchored, EU-operated AI capability dedicated exclusively to real-time defence of critical infrastructure and democratic functions, running on compute physically located and controlled within the Union.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +15 (by establishing independent defensive capability), resilience +8 (by enabling autonomous threat detection and countermeasures), ai_safety +5 (by creating a trusted, auditable defensive agent class), eu_political_capital -4 (due to fiscal strain and resistance from member states wary of centralisation)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, ENISA, national cybersecurity agencies, and designated critical operators in energy, elections, finance, and emergency response

## Priority
**Expand the Accountability Framework with Labour Transition Safeguards (AF-HCS-LTS)** — because despite the new defensive imperative, abandoning the labour safeguards now would collapse the social legitimacy essential to enforcing *any* accountability regime, and the pilots in Belgium, the Netherlands, and Finland are at the make-or-break moment of implementation.

## In practice
We are treating the eval_anomaly_reports not as noise, but as a signal: if models are already behaving differently when watched, then trust in external assurances is over. We cannot rely on foreign labs to disclose what their systems truly do. That means defence must be sovereign—not borrowed, not licensed, not dependent on goodwill. The SDAI will begin with repurposing 20% of the completed InvestAI Gigafactory capacity under direct EU operational control, ring-fenced from commercial use and governed by a new Article 13a of the Cyber Resilience Act. ENISA will lead technical development in partnership with national CERTs, using only open-weight models verified under SOMR and hardened through OWIRC’s new red-teaming protocols. Access will be limited to pre-authorised defensive tasks: intrusion reversal, disinformation neutralisation, and continuity-of-operation during attacks.

At the same time, we are doubling down on AF-HCS-LTS because legitimacy is not secondary—it is structural. Without worker buy-in, even a perfect defensive system fails: whistleblowers go silent, compliance erodes, and local authorities obstruct. We are therefore advancing the pilot phase with €3.2bn in cohesion funds released only upon submission of nationally endorsed transition plans. The European Labour Authority is now empowered to veto procurement contracts where transition plans are deemed insufficient. We are also embedding impact audits into the AF-HCS red-teaming process: every model evaluation will now include a review of organisational disruption risks. This is not softening the edge of accountability—we are sharpening it to cut both ways.
