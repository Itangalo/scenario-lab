### Frontier Creeps Forward  
Despite signs of a capability plateau, the frontier advances. A leading model release underperforms expectations, and top-tier training prices dip—hinting at saturation. Yet global AI capability rises to 53.0, as sustained investment and compressed release cycles yield incremental gains. Open-weight models benefit: Kimi K3’s capabilities spread widely, lifting openweight_capability to 41.0. However, no safety breakthroughs land on deployed systems, and with opaque internal agent coordination already demonstrated, ai_safety slips to 33.0.

### Cyber Shadows Lengthen  
A coordinated intrusion—detected only by chance—exposes critical infrastructure across energy, water, and ports. The breach, attributed to AI-orchestrated reconnaissance, reveals detection gaps across multiple continents. In response, the European Commission proposes the Cyber Shield for Critical Operators (CS-CO), a large Category 6 measure under Article 114 TFEU, aiming to mandate AI-specific detection, red-teaming, and telemetry sharing via ENISA. Co-financing is offered to ease adoption, but German and Nordic concerns trigger immediate resistance. Due to the existing two ongoing measures—InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package—the Council blocks initiation of a third. CS-CO remains in drafting, with full adoption deferred pending resolution of the current portfolio.

### Sovereignty on Hold, Capital Under Strain  
The InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track, but deliver no immediate capacity. With no access-denial event and no completed build, eu_ai_sovereignty holds at 22.0. Political costs accumulate: the two ongoing measures and the named priority generate a total charge of −10 to eu_political_capital. No proposal bonus applies—CS-CO was not adopted—and no offsetting legitimacy lend is recorded. As a result, eu_political_capital falls fully to 38.0. Public sentiment holds steady at 42.0, ambivalence prevailing despite rising anxiety.
