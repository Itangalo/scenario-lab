### A spring of empty desks
The hiring freeze became the story of the spring. Law firms, accountants, software houses and customer-operation centres confirmed what graduates already knew: entry posts were not being advertised. National papers named the sectors and ran photos of empty trainee rows. Student unions marched in Brussels, Lyon and Warsaw. At the fenced factory sites, night vigils drew new faces, and police watches around two substations were extended after cables were cut and quickly repaired.

Brussels answered with a modest instrument. Employment and finance directorates stood up wage-subsidised retraining vouchers and six months of wage insurance for hires in the five named sectors, co-funded with large employers and delivered through public employment services. The promise was tracked on a public scoreboard of restored hiring pipelines. Uptake was slow at first; employers queried paperwork, regions queried co-funding.

### Loose models, tighter checklists
A new open release, close to the closed frontier, was downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in its first week. University labs and small firms installed it locally. Hospital IT teams updated passwords and isolated terminals again, folding a newly published interpretability check into routine hygiene. Health and cyber agencies stayed on passive monitoring, with no public alarm.

Showcase hospitals stayed open on maintenance funds, with shorter imaging queues where domestic triage helpers still ran. One network briefly reverted to manual triage after a data scare involving a locally run model, then resumed. Energy and legal teams continued town-by-town talks on capped water and grid sequencing, but referees asked for more metering and councils held firm. Cranes did not move.

Washington repeated continuity language without a restoration date. The separate foreign supply deal held. Survival held; rebuilding did not.
