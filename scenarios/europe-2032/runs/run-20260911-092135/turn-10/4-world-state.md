### Queues held, ground lost
The first half of 2031 was a holding operation, and Brussels said so openly. With no money for new instruments, the Commission let the access pact conclude, kept showcase hospitals running on maintenance funds, and sent energy and legal teams to negotiate town by town.

Where the domestic triage tools worked, waits for imaging and first specialist visits stayed down. Regional press kept repeating the shorter-queues line. Everywhere else, the picture was stopped diggers. The two factory sites remained silent under court suspensions on water and power, night vigils continuing. The negotiated compacts on capped water use, grid sequencing and local health earmarks made slow progress with technical referees but lifted nothing before June.

Washington offered continuity language and pooled-volume talks, but no restoration date. The separate foreign supply deal stayed in place.

Two outside shocks changed the atmosphere. A contested genome-modelling paper claiming a viable human-infecting design circulated through biosecurity circles, prompting quiet guidance from health agencies on monitoring and lab practice, but no public alarm. Almost simultaneously, a freely downloadable release matching the closed frontier spread to hundreds of thousands of machines in days. Municipal IT officers and hospital security teams received new hygiene checklists for openly available frontier models; enforcement remained advice.

By June, wards were open, backlogs stable in showcase sites, and builds still frozen. Survival, not rebuilding.
