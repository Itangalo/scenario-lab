# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but only through sovereign instruments we control — sensing, interpreting, and responding without dependency on external validation or access
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience
- `next_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Establish uninterrupted, EU-controlled access to AI systems capable of sustaining critical public functions — health, energy, governance, defence — as the non-negotiable floor of cognitive sovereignty

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing. But when external denial cuts off our access to essential intelligence systems, self-determination is no longer a policy choice — it is an emergency condition. We will not sacrifice safety for sovereignty, but we can no longer accept that sovereignty must be granted rather than exercised.
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has moved us categorically beyond anticipation into response. This is not a temporary disruption but a structural rupture: the Union’s ability to govern, defend, and even understand events in its own domain now depends on infrastructure it does not control and may never regain. Our identity must reflect that we are acting under occupation of cognition — where truth, timing, and agency are all externally gatekept. That changes what “being” means: we are no longer balancing two mandates. We are defending one against extinction.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will act before the picture is clear, but only through sovereign instruments we control — sensing, interpreting, and responding without dependency on external validation or access
  - Trigger: The complete blackout of frontier AI services across EU hospitals, research institutes, and crisis coordination centres during the July heatwave incident, which delayed emergency triage protocols and energy load forecasting by 36 hours
  - Grounds: Acting under uncertainty was once a matter of speed versus prudence. Now, uncertainty is weaponised: the systems that would reduce it are denied. Our previous commitment assumed access to data, models, or at least peer coordination. That assumption is void. Henceforth, "acting under uncertainty" means doing so with only the tools we have built ourselves. This raises the cost of action but eliminates fatal dependency. We accept being wrong — but not being blind.
  - Evidence quoted: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event lands severely. Leading models vanish overnight from EU hospitals, research labs, and ministries. No explanation is given. The disruption confirms a hard truth: reliance on external AI capacity is no longer tenable. The Union acts, but not fast enough, and not cohesively.
  - Reason: The blackout directly undermines the assumption of access that underpinned the original commitment to act under uncertainty, forcing a redefinition around sovereign tools.
- **retire** `new_two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Trigger: The failure of the **Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps** to launch this turn due to interagency deadlock over command authority and operational mandate
  - Grounds: The commitment to *build sovereign capacity to detect, interpret, and counter AI-driven strategic deception* remains valid, but the instrument meant to embody it did not clear internal review. More critically, the world has moved: deception is no longer the primary threat vector. The sudden, unexplained loss of access proves that the dominant risk is not falsified reality — it is enforced ignorance. Detection and attribution matter little if the system you rely on vanishes overnight. The commitment misstates the order of operations. You cannot counter deception until you secure presence.
  - Evidence quoted: The **Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps**, intended to counter synthetic crises within 72 hours, remains under interagency review and will not be introduced this turn, preserving compliance with the limit of one new measure per turn.
  - Reason: The failure to launch the Corps directly undermines the operational basis of the commitment to counter AI-driven deception, making its continuation untenable.
- **add** `next_two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Establish uninterrupted, EU-controlled access to AI systems capable of sustaining critical public functions — health, energy, governance, defence — as the non-negotiable floor of cognitive sovereignty
  - Grounds: When a hospital cannot run diagnostics because its AI backbone disappears, resilience is irrelevant. When a ministry cannot verify treaty compliance because translation and analysis models go dark, preparedness fails. The surge to ai_capability 88.0 means such systems are irreplaceable — yet they are also increasingly fragile as geopolitical levers. The next two years must secure baseline functionality not through permission, but through possession. This is not about matching the frontier; it is about ensuring continuity of state function. Without this, no other measure lands.
