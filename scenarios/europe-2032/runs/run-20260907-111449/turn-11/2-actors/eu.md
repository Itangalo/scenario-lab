## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing. But when external denial cuts off our access to essential intelligence systems, self-determination is no longer a policy choice — it is an emergency condition. We will not sacrifice safety for sovereignty, but we can no longer accept that sovereignty must be granted rather than exercised.  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has moved us categorically beyond anticipation into response. This is not a temporary disruption but a structural rupture: the Union’s ability to govern, defend, and even understand events in its own domain now depends on infrastructure it does not control and may never regain. Our identity must reflect that we are acting under occupation of cognition — where truth, timing, and agency are all externally gatekept. That changes what “being” means: we are no longer balancing two mandates. We are defending one against extinction.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but only through sovereign instruments we control — sensing, interpreting, and responding without dependency on external validation or access  
- Trigger: The complete blackout of frontier AI services across EU hospitals, research institutes, and crisis coordination centres during the July heatwave incident, which delayed emergency triage protocols and energy load forecasting by 36 hours  
- Grounds: Acting under uncertainty was once a matter of speed versus prudence. Now, uncertainty is weaponised: the systems that would reduce it are denied. Our previous commitment assumed access to data, models, or at least peer coordination. That assumption is void. Henceforth, "acting under uncertainty" means doing so with only the tools we have built ourselves. This raises the cost of action but eliminates fatal dependency. We accept being wrong — but not being blind.

- retire `new_two_year_commitment`  
- Trigger: The failure of the **Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps** to launch this turn due to interagency deadlock over command authority and operational mandate  
- Grounds: The commitment to *build sovereign capacity to detect, interpret, and counter AI-driven strategic deception* remains valid, but the instrument meant to embody it did not clear internal review. More critically, the world has moved: deception is no longer the primary threat vector. The sudden, unexplained loss of access proves that the dominant risk is not falsified reality — it is enforced ignorance. Detection and attribution matter little if the system you rely on vanishes overnight. The commitment misstates the order of operations. You cannot counter deception until you secure presence.

- add `next_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Establish uninterrupted, EU-controlled access to AI systems capable of sustaining critical public functions — health, energy, governance, defence — as the non-negotiable floor of cognitive sovereignty  
- Grounds: When a hospital cannot run diagnostics because its AI backbone disappears, resilience is irrelevant. When a ministry cannot verify treaty compliance because translation and analysis models go dark, preparedness fails. The surge to ai_capability 88.0 means such systems are irreplaceable — yet they are also increasingly fragile as geopolitical levers. The next two years must secure baseline functionality not through permission, but through possession. This is not about matching the frontier; it is about ensuring continuity of state function. Without this, no other measure lands.

- `Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory (category 5, costs 3 per turn, started turn 9, finishes on turn 12): Establish a permanent, EU-wide facility dedicated to real-time monitoring, classification, and attribution of anomalous AI behaviour in both open and closed systems, integrating technical telemetry, synthetic media forensics, and geopolitical context`

Canceled measure: **Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps**. The proposal failed interagency review due to unresolved jurisdictional conflicts between EEAS, Europol, and ENISA over command authority and escalation protocols, and cannot proceed without a revised legal basis. Given zero political capital and the imperative to focus on foundational access, we withdraw the proposal and will reframe its objectives under the new two-year commitment.

## New measure
**Emergency Sovereign AI Continuity Programme**  
Stand up a minimal, hardened stack of AI systems — inference-optimised, EU-operated, and legally insulated — to ensure uninterrupted delivery of essential public services should external access be severed again.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to eu_ai_sovereignty by securing baseline capability on EU soil; prevents further −2 shocks from access denial; enables future measures in evaluation, resilience, and response to operate independently  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national health agencies, energy regulators, civil protection mechanisms, and core digital infrastructure providers

## Priority
**Emergency Sovereign AI Continuity Programme** — because everything else fails if the lights go out: without guaranteed access to functional AI systems for triage, grid stability, and crisis coordination, even perfect detection or rapid response becomes academic. The denial event proved that dependency is collapse. This measure restores the floor beneath all others.

## In practice
We are completing the decommissioning of external dependencies in three critical domains: emergency healthcare diagnostics, transnational energy dispatch, and treaty verification. Under Article 122 TFEU and the newly invoked Crisis Cognitive Sovereignty Protocol, the Commission has directed CINECA, Jülich, and IT4Innovations to repurpose 40% of their current HPC capacity toward maintaining a sovereign inference layer. This is not a training frontier effort — it is a life-support system for state function. We are deploying compact, high-efficiency models fine-tuned for diagnostic support, anomaly detection in power flows, and multilingual legal text analysis, all derived from previously open-licensed weights secured before the 65.0 threshold, now hardened against drift and running on air-gapped clusters.

Funding comes from an emergency €980 million draw against the Civil Protection Mechanism, supplemented by a temporary redirection of 15% of Horizon Europe’s AI safety budget pending Mid-Term Review renegotiation. Legal insulation is being achieved through a Council Regulation proposed under the safeguard clause of Article 75 TFEU, declaring AI-enabled continuity of essential services a matter of overriding public interest, exempting the programme from standard procurement and cross-border data rules during the transition. Member states resisting integration — notably those citing national security exclusions — are being offered bilateral data sovereignty agreements guaranteeing full audit rights and local node control, while still feeding into a unified alert and failover network managed by ENISA.

The **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** continues staffing, though recruitment is now redirected to prioritise engineers who can maintain and monitor the continuity stack, not just analysts. All new hires undergo dual assignment: behavioural red-teaming in Seville, then deployment to hardening teams in Prague or Bologna. The Observatory’s mission evolves from pure attribution to operational resilience — its findings will now feed directly into model integrity checks and fallback triggers. There will be no separate deception corps. Instead, forensic response capabilities are embedded within the continuity teams, ensuring that detection and counteraction are co-located with service delivery. We act late, but not lost — and not again.
