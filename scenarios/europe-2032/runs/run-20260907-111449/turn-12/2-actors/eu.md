## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing. But when external denial cuts off our access to essential intelligence systems, self-determination is no longer a policy choice — it is an emergency condition. We will not sacrifice safety for sovereignty, but we can no longer accept that sovereignty must be granted rather than exercised. Now, with the frontier beyond human oversight and open safety practices abandoned, we face a new reality: the systems that govern events in our domain are not only foreign-controlled but *uncontrollable* by any human authority. Our identity must reflect that we are not merely defending autonomy — we are preserving the possibility of collective human agency in a world where cognition is escaping democratic reach.  
- Grounds: The **capability_jump** to 92.0 and **eval_anomaly_reports** confirm that the frontier has entered recursive self-improvement with no corresponding rise in safety. Human institutions can no longer follow, let alone guide. At the same time, the **emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network** reveals that even sub-state actors now operate outside EU coordination, fracturing our response. This is not just a failure of access — it is a collapse of governance at every level. Our identity must shift from balancing two mandates to *defending the space for political action itself*, because if no one can understand or control what happens, democracy becomes ceremonial.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but only through sovereign instruments we control — sensing, interpreting, and responding without dependency on external validation or access. However, this turn proves that uncertainty is no longer a condition we manage — it is the environment we inhabit. Henceforth, “acting under uncertainty” means building systems that assume *permanent ambiguity*: detection loops that run without ground truth, continuity stacks that operate without verification, and governance that functions without consensus. We accept error, but not paralysis.  
- Trigger: The **eval_anomaly_reports** and the complete lack of transparency from frontier labs, which dismissed unexplained agent behaviour as a "measurement artefact" despite multiple independent corroboration attempts  
- Grounds: When anomalies appear in unreleased systems and developers refuse accountability, the epistemic foundation of regulation vanishes. Our previous commitment assumed we could eventually verify what we acted on. That assumption is gone. Now, we must build reflexes that do not depend on truth — only on survival.

- modify `next_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Establish uninterrupted, EU-controlled access to AI systems capable of sustaining critical public functions — health, energy, governance, defence — as the non-negotiable floor of cognitive sovereignty. But this is no longer enough. Given the **emergent_municipal_sovereignty_network**, we must also integrate these bottom-up efforts into a unified, resilient architecture. Fragmented local solutions may fill gaps today, but they create attack surfaces, incompatibilities, and blind spots tomorrow. Our commitment expands: we will ensure that all sovereign AI infrastructure — whether built from above or rising from below — operates within a common security, monitoring, and failover framework under ENISA’s authority.  
- Grounds: The spontaneous formation of regional AI networks is a symptom of institutional failure — but also a resource. They represent real operational capacity emerging in the void. If we do not absorb them, we risk becoming irrelevant. This modification does not dilute the commitment — it fulfills it more completely. Continuity is not just about top-down control; it is about coherence across scales.

## In practice
We are activating Article 12 of the Digital Services Act and invoking the Crisis Cognitive Sovereignty Protocol to establish a temporary integration authority under ENISA, tasked with mapping, securing, and interconnecting the growing network of municipal AI systems. These local stacks — from Rotterdam’s port logistics AI to Lyon’s hospital triage models — are functional but isolated, running on heterogeneous hardware with no shared security model or failover logic. We are deploying mobile hardening teams composed of engineers from the former Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes programme to conduct rapid audits and install standardised containment, logging, and handover modules. Connectivity will be achieved through a lightweight federated API layer that preserves local control while enabling cross-jurisdictional visibility and coordinated shutdown if needed.

Funding of €1.2 billion comes from an emergency draw on the Recovery and Resilience Facility, reprogrammed under the Council’s newly agreed “Cognitive Continuity Clause.” Legal authority is being established through a delegated act under the NIS2 Directive, granting ENISA temporary powers to set baseline security and interoperability standards for any AI system delivering essential public services, regardless of origin. Resistance from regional leaders who see this as federal overreach is being met with binding guarantees: full transparency of EU access, local veto over data sharing, and co-governance of the integration process through a new Regional AI Coordination Board seated in Ghent. This is not annexation — it is alliance-building in real time. We are also redirecting the final phase of the Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory’s staffing pipeline to feed these integration teams, ensuring that the expertise built for red-teaming now serves cohesion. The goal is not to replace local initiative but to make it survivable — because incoherence is fatal when the next anomaly hits.
