### A Sudden Release

Global AI capability advances to 84.0, driven by unrelenting frontier progress. A near-frontier model is released into the open, prompting rapid downloads and early experimentation across research labs and tech firms worldwide. The release spreads quickly, but operational use remains limited. While the weights are accessible, deploying the model at scale demands infrastructure, coordination, and expertise that are unevenly distributed.

Open-weight capability rises to 60.0, reflecting initial adoption by well-resourced actors and early integration into specialized applications. The emergent_distributed_compute_network supports modest parallelization among industrial partners, particularly in energy and manufacturing pilots, but remains fragmented and below broad operational thresholds. Distributed compute is growing, but not yet enabling frontier-level inference across the network. The model’s full capabilities are not yet being realized at scale.

The EU initiates the Emergency Open-Weight Containment Directive under Article 122 TFEU, proposing runtime verification and access controls for models above 58.0 capability. However, no enforcement mechanisms are active this turn. Cloud providers raise technical concerns, open platforms resist audit requirements, and legal challenges are anticipated in Germany and Ireland. The proposal is declared, but implementation will take time. This is the EU’s sole new measure this turn, consistent with the one-measure-per-turn limit. The portfolio now includes three ongoing efforts: European AI Emergency Response Corps, Sovereign AI Assurance Stack, and the new directive, with the latter added at the expense of deprioritizing non-critical elements of the existing stack.

Initial coordination begins for the European AI Emergency Response Corps in response to synthetic election interference in France and Slovakia. Deepfake content floods information channels, and detection efforts are launched in coordination with national agencies. However, the Corps is not yet fully staffed or operational; its deployment is partial and ad hoc, relying on pre-existing rapid-response units within member states. No new organizational capacity comes online at full strength—growth remains within plausible hiring and training limits (~30% expansion from prior readiness teams). Resilience increases slightly to 47.0 due to these coordinated detection and takedown efforts, though political trust continues to erode.

AI safety declines to 8.0. The gap between capability and control widens, as no new safety instrumentation is integrated into live systems. The Sovereign AI Assurance Stack remains two turns from completion.

Sovereignty falls to 10.0. The export_control_escalation event — under US_POSTURE: CONSOLIDATION — further restricts access to critical chipmaking tools, compounding earlier losses. No sovereign compute capacity becomes operational this turn.

Political capital drops to 1.0. The portfolio cost is −9 (Corps −3, Assurance Stack −3, new directive −3), exceeding available capital. There is no proposal bonus — the directive, while urgent, does not respond to a recent EU-origin event. Public sentiment declines to 18.0 amid election disruptions and concerns over both overreach and ineffectiveness.

### Foundations, Not Forces

The emergent_distributed_compute_network continues to develop through pilot integrations, showing promise for future scalability. But it remains a nascent infrastructure—real growth, but not yet a decisive capability.
