## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without minimum sovereignty, neither mandate can be fulfilled, and that sovereignty without safety is a trap that accelerates our own subjugation.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but this turn, the loss of control incident and emergent cyber insurance collapse have made uncertainty itself a systemic threat, not just a condition of action, and we must now treat opacity as a hostile condition.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure societal resilience as the foundation of sovereign agency — but with ai_safety at 4.0 and openweight_capability rising, resilience alone is no longer a sufficient foundation; we must now couple it with sovereign control over at least one viable path to safe capability, and that path must be demonstrably safer than the frontier, not just independent.  

* Trigger: The emergent_scientific_diaspora brings a rare opportunity — but also reveals how far behind we are in operational safety infrastructure. The supply_chain_coercion event proves our sovereignty is not just lagging but actively undermined. Meanwhile, public_sentiment continues to fall despite our efforts, because trust is not restored by promises of future stacks, but by visible control *now*.  
* Grounds: Our identity must reflect that sovereignty without safety is not sovereignty at all — it is the risk of building our own uncontrolled frontier. Our commitment to act under uncertainty must now include treating lack of visibility as a threat vector. And our two-year commitment must sharpen: we are not just building an alternative, we are building a *safer* alternative — otherwise, we trade dependence for danger.

## Priority  
**Sovereign AI Assurance Stack** — because the emergent_scientific_diaspora gives us a narrow window to anchor world-leading safety expertise into a sovereign stack, and because without this integration, the stack risks becoming just another compute pipeline, not a controlled one.

## In practice  
We are acting on the only leverage we have: trust. The emergent_scientific_diaspora is not a gift — it is a test. These researchers are not fleeing for money or prestige; they are fleeing systems where safety is secondary to speed. We must prove we are not just offering refuge, but a better way. That is why we are launching the Emergency Talent Integration Directive under Article 80 TFEU, with the European Commission fast-tracking residence and work permits for qualifying researchers, and the ERC allocating €1.2bn in emergency grants tied to participation in the Sovereign AI Assurance Stack or the Civilian AI Safety Board. We are instructing national academies to reserve tenured chairs, and EuroHPC-JU to grant priority access to testbeds for teams embedded in the stack.

At the same time, we are finalising the legal activation of the European AI Emergency Response Corps this turn — not as a symbolic body, but as an operational force. Despite resistance from Germany and Poland, we are invoking the solidarity clause in Article 222 TFEU to establish binding data-sharing protocols for cross-border AI incidents, with opt-outs requiring qualified majority override. ENISA and the ECDC are now co-leading a live-readiness exercise in Q2, simulating a coordinated bio-AI synthesis attack, with response cells in Dublin, Athens, and Malmö pre-authorized to deploy. The fiscal incentive remains — 70% co-funding for Resilience Surge upgrades — but now tied directly to Corps integration.

We are also responding to supply_chain_coercion not with retaliation, but with redirection. We are convening a special meeting of the Competitiveness Council to accelerate domestic lithography R&D under Horizon Europe, focusing on post-ASML alternatives, and we are offering chip design sovereignty to any member state willing to host a sovereign fab using repurposed Gigafactory sites. This is not a race we can win by catching up — we must change the track.
