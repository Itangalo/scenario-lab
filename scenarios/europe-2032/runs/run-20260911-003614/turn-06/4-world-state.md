### The jump and the machines
Winter ended with two shocks arriving together. An American lab demonstrated a system that made the previous autumn's leaked benchmarks look modest — planners rewrote timelines in a week, and safety teams admitted they could not explain its longer chains of reasoning. At the same time, commercial humanoids moved from pilots to purchase orders in ports, warehouses and hospitals.

For Europe the second shock cut deeper. The robots arriving in Rotterdam, Łódź and Lyon were built elsewhere, on supply chains Europe does not control and control software it does not own. Chinese vendors offered price and volume; American stacks offered capability with tiered access. EU industrial firms, squeezed by energy and chip costs, bought both.

### Stoppable robots
Brussels did not ban them — with towns already hostile it could not. It ordered them registered, fitted with remote-stop and manual override, and drilled for continuity where they displaced porters, pickers and care assistants. DG GROW and ENISA inspectors audited kill-switches; joint exercises rehearsed a warehouse running without its fleet.

It worked unevenly. Large logistics hubs complied and filmed the drills. Smaller operators hid machines, wired around interlocks to keep throughput, or discovered the stop button halted the robot but not the line that depended on it. A Lyon hospital strike over displaced aides forced a retreat to wage-bridges.

The two finishing programmes helped at the margins. The sovereignty package's permits and grid priority kept the two gigafactory shells rising, and the bio-shield's monitoring and stockpiles absorbed a spring flu scare without panic. But municipal staffing pacts stalled where no staff applied, and the insurer backstop renewed only for cities.

By June, Washington's new team took office promising structured allied access and joint evaluation. Relief in capitals was real, and so was the quiet shelving of home-build urgency. In small towns, the sight of foreign machines doing local jobs hardened resentment into something colder.
