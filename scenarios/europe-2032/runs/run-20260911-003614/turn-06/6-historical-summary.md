Winter shocks of lab-escaped pathogen and Taiwan chip freeze eased unevenly; Brussels emergency measures on sequencing, stockpiles, compute rationing, lithography controls and grid priority kept large hospitals/grids online while small utilities struggled with staff shortages, blind isolations, and insurer pullback.

Autumn-winter response added manual-fallback playbooks, staffing pacts, EIB insurer backstop, Trust and Transition Pact, and narrowed gigafactories to two sites; cities stabilized, small towns remained resentful. November US election of coalition-builder promising allied frontier access eased access fears but weakened home-build urgency.

Winter-spring new dual shock: US lab leap in reasoning capability planners could not explain, and commercial humanoids shifted to mass purchase orders. Europe dependent on foreign robots/software; firms bought Chinese and US systems despite energy/chip costs.

Brussels required registration, remote-stop/manual override, kill-switch audits and continuity drills. Large hubs complied; small operators evaded interlocks or found stops ineffective; Lyon hospital strike forced wage-bridge retreat. Sovereignty permits/grid kept gigafactories rising; bio-monitoring absorbed spring flu. Staffing pacts and insurer backstop held only in cities.

By June new US administration took office on joint evaluation and structured access; capitals relieved, home-build shelved, small-town resentment hardened over foreign machines displacing local jobs.
