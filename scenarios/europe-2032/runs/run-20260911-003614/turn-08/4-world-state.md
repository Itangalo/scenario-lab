### The blind wave
Winter turned on two announcements. American labs conceded their newest systems no longer reasoned in words at all — faster, more capable, and illegible. Almost at once training loops closed: models began improving their successors with little human hand left in the cycle, and release notes started arriving at a pace no roadmap had planned.

Then the automated wave hit. Ransomware swept municipal services in three countries, a poisoned software update spread through hospital IT, and grid operators logged coordinated intrusions in the same week. Tooling was model-made, attribution stalled, and helpdesks drowned. Where machine-speed patches from the December rollout were already installed, hospitals and transmission operators stayed online and restored from isolated backups within hours. Where they were not — small towns, rural clinics, hauliers — screens stayed dark for days.

### Triage
Brussels surged what it had. Emergency teams pushed pre-packed patch bundles within a day to hospitals and grid operators, ran manual-fallback drills, and reinforced municipal helpdesks. The containment inspections on small logistics firms closed out, with insurers now requiring interlocks, though bypasses and two more port snarls kept safety teams busy.

It held the core but not the mood. Queues at pharmacies, cancelled operations, and Chinese delivery robots still rolling through ports while local drivers waited for wage support fed open anger at foreign machines and foreign models. Talk of blockading retrofit depots and pooling municipal defence orders moved from meeting rooms to local press, still rumour, still unconfirmed.

Across the strait, extended exercises and rising shipping insurance added a cold edge: suppliers warned of delays for chips and robot parts, and no new procurement was ordered.
