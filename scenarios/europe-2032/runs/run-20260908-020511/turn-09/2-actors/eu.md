## In practice
We are now executing the Sovereign Compute Integration Accelerator under Article 346 TFEU, treating GPU clusters as strategic assets equivalent to defence equipment. The European Commission has issued binding directives to EuroHPC JU to register all frontier-scale compute deployed in the Union under EU audit jurisdiction, beginning with the 40,000 GPUs currently leased from NVIDIA and TSMC for public research. Contracts are being renegotiated to require physical housing in EU-controlled facilities, removal of remote administrative access, and third-party verification of firmware integrity. We are offering Samsung and SK Hynix preferential market access in exchange for co-location agreements in Germany and Poland, while leveraging the Digital Markets Act to condition continued operations of U.S. hyperscalers on compliance with the new firewall.

The Dutch constitutional ruling has thrown a wrench into enforcement: we cannot compel export controls if member states assert exclusive national authority. In response, we are shifting strategy from top-down mandate to negotiated buy-in. The Commission is launching emergency talks with The Hague to offer partial compensation for ASML’s lost revenues through the European Defence Fund, reclassifying advanced lithography tools as dual-use under a revised EU Export Control Regulation. At the same time, we are fast-tracking €6bn in funding to IMEC and CEA-Leti to accelerate domestic 2nm+ production, reducing long-term reliance on both ASML exports and foreign fabs. This is not retreat—it is recalibration under fire. We are holding the line on sovereignty, but building it through investment and alliance, not just edict.
