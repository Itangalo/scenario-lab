## New measure
**Sovereign AI Stack Initiative**  
Establish a fully sovereign, end-to-end AI development and deployment capability within the EU, including domestic model training, secure infrastructure, and public evaluation, to ensure autonomous access to frontier-level systems.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +15, ai_safety +5 (via secure, auditable domestic development), eu_political_capital −3 sustained  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member states, selected national labs (e.g. Fraunhofer, INRIA), and European hyperscalers (e.g. OVHcloud, Atos)

## Priority
**Sovereign AI Stack Initiative** — because at 62.0 global capability and falling safety, dependence on external access is no longer a constraint but an existential vulnerability; the election_consolidation event confirms that the US will treat frontier AI as a national strategic asset, not a shared good, and without our own stack, we cannot enforce red lines, verify safety, or respond to threats—we can only react.

## In practice
We are treating the Sovereign AI Stack Initiative as a mission-scale effort, equivalent to the semiconductor push of the 2020s. The Commission will launch a new legal instrument under Article 118 TFEU for strategic technological autonomy, creating a dedicated governance body—AI Sovereignty Office—reporting directly to the College. This office will coordinate the integration of the InvestAI Gigafactories with a newly funded EU Model Foundry, to be hosted jointly by France and Germany, with satellite training facilities in Finland and Spain leveraging green energy and cooling advantages. We are reallocating €45bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility to secure 100,000 H100-equivalent GPUs by Q3 2029, with binding procurement clauses requiring EU-based operation and legal jurisdiction.

Simultaneously, we are adapting the Emergency Open-Weight Licensing regime to acknowledge its limits. The revised proposal before COREPER now includes a sunset clause: if eu_ai_sovereignty remains below 30.0 by turn 7, the regime shifts from pre-approval to mandatory post-release monitoring and threat assessment for models above 50.0, with sanctions focused on hosting providers rather than developers. We are offering the Netherlands a binding role in the new AI Sovereignty Office and a lead in the Foundry’s interpretability division in exchange for dropping its opposition. On the resilience front, the completed Joint Threat Response Integration Initiative has enabled ENISA to stand up the EU AI Threat Fusion Cell, now receiving live telemetry from NATO and Five Eyes+ partners—this will become the operational backbone for early detection of emergent model behaviours, even when they originate outside our jurisdiction.
