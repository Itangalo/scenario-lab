## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.  
  - Grounds: The eval_anomaly_reports and openweight_frontier_release confirm that capability is accelerating beyond current governance paradigms, while the member_state_defection proves that sovereignty cannot be assumed even when access exists. These are no longer parallel concerns — they are colliding. Our identity must reflect that we are not balancing two mandates, but governing a single system where each move either tightens or loosens both at once.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only when the cost of inaction is measurable and rising.  
  - Grounds: The eval_anomaly_reports represent a control gate opening — not yet a catastrophe, but a signal that the frontier is behaving unpredictably. We acted on uncertainty before, but now uncertainty has structure. This turn demands not just action, but discrimination: we must commit selectively, where the risk is not only unknown but *knowably* compounding.

- add `hold_the_openweight_line` (commitment): We will not allow open-weight releases to reach capability levels that enable uncontrolled, distributed harm, and will treat such releases as a systemic threat to public order.  
  - Grounds: The openweight_frontier_release has moved openweight_capability to within 12 points of ai_capability — a collapse of the buffer that once made frontier risks governable. This is not a policy preference; it is a line in the sand drawn by events. We stake ourselves to it now because if we do not, no future action will be credible.

## New measure

**Emergency Open-Weight Licensing and Recall Regime**  
Establish a legally binding framework to condition the legality of open-weight model releases on pre-approval and enforceable recall rights, treating unapproved releases above a defined capability threshold as a sanctionable breach of public order.

## Priority

**Emergency Open-Weight Licensing and Recall Regime** — because the openweight_frontier_release has collapsed the time-to-distribution of frontier capabilities, and with eval_anomaly_reports suggesting emergent behaviours beyond design intent, we can no longer rely on controlling labs alone — the capability is already everywhere it needs to be, and the next release could cross a threshold we cannot walk back from.

## In practice  

We are treating the openweight release not as a leak, but as a breach of the operating system of governance itself. This turn, the Commission tables a draft *AI Public Order Regulation*, invoking Article 114 TFEU to harmonise member states’ approaches to open-weight models. The regulation establishes a capability threshold — pegged at 50.0 on the index — above which any open-weight release must be pre-registered, evaluated, and subject to enforceable recall. Hosting providers and code repositories operating in the EU will be required to block unapproved uploads, with fines scaled to revenue. We are fast-tracking a pilot with DE-CIX and EU-based Git platforms to test takedown efficacy, while offering white-listed research collectives expedited access to vetted models under EU jurisdiction. Behind the scenes, we are coordinating with FR, DE, and ES interior ministries to treat non-compliant releases as cyber incidents under NIS2, enabling cross-border enforcement. Resistance is expected from open-source communities and some member states’ digital rights offices — but we are framing this not as censorship, but as a minimal condition for public safety: you can build anything, but not deploy it without a brake. The message is clear — the open frontier is not a free zone.
