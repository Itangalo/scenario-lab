We are treating the Sovereign AI Stack Initiative as the core of our survival in a world where AI capability has reached 62.5 and safety has collapsed to 13.0. The AI Sovereignty Office is now standing up under Article 118 TFEU, with a mandate to integrate the InvestAI Gigafactories into a unified national-champion model foundry. We have secured provisional commitments from France and Germany to co-host the main facility, with satellite training clusters in Finland and Spain leveraging renewable cooling. This is not procurement—it is strategic rearmament.

Simultaneously, we are acting on the Taiwan blockade by proposing the ASML Jurisdictional Safeguard Directive under the Anti-Coercion Instrument and Article 346 TFEU. The Netherlands remains resistant, but the parliamentary inquiry into ASML’s exposure to U.S. export controls has created political space. We are offering The Hague a formal role in the AI Sovereignty Office’s export compliance board and a guaranteed 15% share of Foundry compute in exchange for accepting EU-level authority over ASML’s critical technology flows. This is not about nationalisation—it is about ensuring that when the next blockade hits, the Union, not a foreign power or a single member state, decides who gets what. The directive will be fast-tracked through COREPER II and the Competitiveness Council, with a vote scheduled for June.

The completion of the Joint Threat Response Integration Initiative has given us operational reach: ENISA’s AI Threat Fusion Cell now receives live telemetry from NATO and Five Eyes+ partners, allowing early detection of emergent model behaviours—even those originating outside our jurisdiction. We are feeding this into the design of the Sovereign Stack’s internal red-teaming protocols. Meanwhile, the Emergency Open-Weight Licensing regime and the Tech Sovereignty Package have formally concluded. Their legacy is mixed: the former exposed the limits of enforcement without capacity, the latter failed to unlock private investment at scale. We are not renewing either. The future is not in regulating what others build—it is in building what only we can.
