## In practice  

We are treating the ASML crisis not as a diplomatic incident, but as a structural warning: the EU’s dependence on foreign-aligned industrial champions risks becoming a dependence on foreign-aligned futures. This turn, we convene an extraordinary meeting of the Competitiveness Council under Article 114 TFEU, invoking the strategic autonomy clause to coordinate a unified position on semiconductor equipment exports. The Commission tables a legislative proposal — the *Strategic Technology Facility Act* — that reclassifies ASML’s most advanced systems as dual-use critical infrastructure, subject to EU-level export licensing and oversight. We offer the Dutch government a binding share of future licensing revenues and co-governance on the oversight board in exchange for ceding unilateral control. Behind the scenes, we are fast-tracking €30bn in conditional investment through the European Defence Fund and InvestEU to re-shore key materials and subsystems into the EU, with binding siting agreements in Germany, France, and Italy. Resistance from industry is expected — ASML’s board has long operated under Washington’s informal guidance — but we are countering with a clear offer: autonomy with investment, or isolation with scrutiny. The message is that no single member state can outsource the Union’s strategic future.
