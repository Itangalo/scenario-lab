### Cyber Shock and Political Resolve

A coordinated ransomware campaign, powered by AI-generated exploits, cripples hospital networks in Belgium, Portugal, and Slovakia. Emergency systems falter, surgeries are delayed, and public trust frays. Attribution is slow, but forensic analysis confirms the attack tools were autonomously designed—offensive capability has decisively outpaced defence.

In response, the EU launches the **Emergency Cyber Shield**, a large-scale resilience initiative targeting healthcare, energy, and emergency infrastructure. Mandated under Article 114 TFEU, it compels deployment of AI-driven intrusion detection and failover protocols. ENISA establishes a 24/7 Threat Simulation Cell, feeding adversarial models into defensive training loops. Implementation has begun, but operational maturity will depend on integration timelines, resource allocation, and member state coordination.

Simultaneously, the **ASML Jurisdictional Safeguard Directive** clears COREPER II—but faces immediate legal and political headwinds. Dutch negotiators secured veto rights over export decisions, weakening centralized EU control. While the proposal asserts a claim to legal authority over a critical technology node, the domestic backlash continues to grow: a formal parliamentary inquiry in the Netherlands is now drafting constitutional challenges to the directive’s enforceability. The Union may claim jurisdiction, but actual control remains unproven and contested.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme officially completes, delivering foundational compute capacity. However, U.S. export bottlenecks delay GPU deliveries, slowing integration. Parallel procurement from South Korea advances, but under tighter verification terms due to **export_control_escalation**, which now restricts even allied access to advanced chips.

Despite completion of a major initiative—Gigafactories—the political toll is steep. The cyberattack fuels anxiety, and no immediate public benefit offsets the disruption. Public sentiment holds at 37.0.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 13.0: despite progress, `ai_capability` rose by 3.5, triggering −1, and no new sovereign compute came fully online. The ASML directive does not qualify as secured access under rule 5, as compliance is not established and enforcement is actively disputed.

`eu_political_capital` increases to 11.0. Resilience rises to 59.0: +3 from the completed Joint Threat Response, and +3 more from the Cyber Shield’s early implementation under rule 4.

AI safety drops to 3.0: opaque reasoning spreads, and no safety upgrade landed on deployed systems.

`openweight_capability` remains at 57.0. While general AI capability advanced, no open-weight model releases, sovereign stack deployment, or targeted infrastructure boost occurred to specifically advance open models. The proposed increase lacked narrative grounding and is therefore reverted to the prior level.
