## In practice
The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep targeting hospital networks in three member states — has confirmed our worst fears: offensive AI is already weaponised, and our defences are reactive, not anticipatory. While the Joint Threat Response Integration Initiative gave us eyes, it did not give us reflexes. We are now launching the Emergency Cyber Shield under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the NIS2 directive’s enforcement powers to mandate AI-driven intrusion detection systems across all critical public services by Q1 2030. ENISA will lead a 24/7 Threat Simulation Cell, feeding real-time adversarial model outputs into defensive training loops, while national agencies are required to conduct biweekly failover drills. Funding of €18bn will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme’s emergency clause and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with strict conditionality: no disbursement without verified deployment.

At the same time, we are treating the completion of the ASML Jurisdictional Safeguard Directive as a partial victory — the vote passed in COREPER II, but only after the Netherlands secured veto rights over individual export decisions, diluting the directive’s leverage. Still, it marks the first time the Union has asserted legal jurisdiction over a strategically vital firm, setting a precedent. We are now turning that foothold into operational control: the AI Sovereignty Office is integrating ASML’s compliance board into the Sovereign AI Stack’s supply chain firewall, ensuring that any future compute procurement — including the 100,000 GPUs under negotiation — is legally bound to EU jurisdiction and audit. The €45bn reallocation remains on track, though delivery timelines are slipping due to U.S. export bottlenecks. We are not waiting. Parallel procurement from South Korea and Taiwan is being explored under new bilateral tech security accords, brokered through the European External Action Service.

Public sentiment remains fragile. The cyberattack has amplified anxiety, but also opened space for decisive action — we are framing the Cyber Shield not as restriction, but as protection. Still, political capital is at rock bottom. We cannot afford another misstep. Every instrument we now deploy must land, and land hard.
