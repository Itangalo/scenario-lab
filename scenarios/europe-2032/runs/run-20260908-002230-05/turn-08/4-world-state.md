### Fractured Response

The EU launched the **Emergency Resilience Surge**, mobilising civil protection resources to harden critical public services against AI failures. ENISA deployed fallback architectures to high-risk systems in Germany, France, and the Netherlands, but Poland and Hungary refused participation, citing sovereignty concerns. Conditional access to sovereign compute was invoked — a first — but legal challenges loom. The surge began yielding partial protections, though full implementation awaits turn 9.

The **Middle-Power Access Compact** reached its finishing turn but faltered. Japan and Canada published red-team findings, but South Korea abstained, and Washington dismissed the EU’s semiconductor leverage as coercive. No new evaluation rights were secured. The compact’s failure, combined with the Dutch audit fallout, deepened fragmentation.

### Open Frontier, Uncertain Diffusion

An open-weight model was released weeks after the closed frontier’s latest advance, drawing significant attention and uptake across research and activist networks. While the release demonstrated improved reasoning and modularity, its actual capabilities remain below the frontier. The diffusion is notable but not transformative — early analysis suggests it enables automation of narrow research tasks, but not autonomous self-improvement or widespread replication in high-stakes domains.

This progress contributes to a measured rise in `openweight_capability` to 51.0, reflecting incremental gains from distributed development and tooling improvements. The 2.0-point increase aligns with sustained open-source momentum seen over prior turns, rather than a sudden leap. No systemic breakthrough in training efficiency or decentralised compute access has been confirmed, and the gap to `ai_capability` remains structurally intact.

A biosecurity controversy erupted when a genome model produced a plausible human-infective organism design. Though contested, the `bio_uplift_findings` intensified fears of misuse. No major breach occurred, but confidence in oversight collapsed further.

Physical protests escalated into sabotage at data centres in Bavaria and Wallonia, injuring two workers — a `backlash_physical` event that underscored institutional erosion. Public sentiment dipped as media framed the state as defending infrastructure over citizens.

### Sovereignty Erodes

Despite finishing the Sovereign Compute Buildout, `eu_ai_sovereignty` fell to 11.0: `ai_capability` rose 0.5, and the `member_state_defection` from turn 7 continued to cost access. No capacity event offset it.

Political capital rose slightly to 19.0, buoyed by the proposal bonus for the large, urgent resilience measure. The portfolio charge held at −7, but the −1 priority cost did not apply — capital remained below 20, nullifying priority effects per rule 10.

Resilience improved slightly due to early surge effects, but `ai_safety` plummeted 5 points from the capability jump and lack of deployed safeguards.
