A six-month acceleration programme to harden essential administrative, healthcare, and social service systems against AI-enabled cyber and decision failures, combining rapid audits, fallback protocols, and real-time monitoring.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`

## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Public Services** — because backlash_physical and the automated_decision_scandal have shown that the next failure may not be technical but systemic, and if public services cannot hold during an incident, no amount of sovereign compute or coalition leverage will matter.

## In practice  
We are treating the current moment as one of operational emergency. The completion of the Sovereign Compute Buildout gives us infrastructure, but not stability. With openweight_capability now nearing 49.0 and an uncontrolled model circulating that approaches autonomous research execution, and with physical attacks on data centres in Bavaria and Wallonia, we can no longer assume that our systems will remain intact — or trusted — long enough to function. The Audit and Accountability Framework proposed last turn remains necessary, but too slow: it lands in turn 9, and harm arrives now.

So we are launching the Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 122 TFEU, mobilising the Union Civil Protection Mechanism to support national cyber rapid-response teams. The European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) will deploy pre-tested fallback architectures to ten high-risk public service systems — including unemployment benefits, medical triage, and border controls — with manual override, log integrity checks, and live red-teaming. Funding comes from the unused portion of the InvestEU contingency reserve (€18.3bn), redirected via Commission delegated act. Member states must opt in, but access to sovereign compute and emergency funding for AI-related disruptions will be conditional on participation. Germany, France, and the Netherlands have signalled support; Poland and Hungary have not.

Simultaneously, we are making the final push on the Middle-Power Access Compact. The Dutch audit revealing the secret U.S. data deal has created leverage: we are informing Washington that continued access to EU semiconductor exports depends on verifiable adherence to the compact’s evaluation protocols by turn’s end. Japan and Canada have agreed to co-publish their first joint red-team findings by June. South Korea remains hesitant, but we are offering reciprocal access to EU audit tools in exchange for alignment. This is not ideal — it is damage control. But without shared visibility into what the frontier is doing, even our best defences will face threats we cannot see.
