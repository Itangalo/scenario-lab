## New measure
Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure  
Launches a binding, EU-wide hardening programme for energy, transport and health infrastructure against AI-enabled intrusion, including mandatory red-teaming, zero-trust architecture rollout, and real-time anomaly detection using sovereign AI systems.  
`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to resilience via systemic cyber hardening; modest upward pressure on public_sentiment by restoring confidence in essential services; indirect support to eu_ai_sovereignty by driving demand for trusted, EU-controlled AI tools  
`Applies to:` all member states, with compliance tied to Cohesion Policy funding and managed by ENISA in coordination with national CSIRTs

## Priority
Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure — because the recent breach exposed not just technical fragility but a collapse in trust, and without secure foundations, both our industrial strategy and social cohesion risk unraveling.

## In practice
We are activating Article 18 of the Cyber Resilience Act and the NIS2 Directive’s emergency powers to mandate immediate upgrades across critical operators in energy, rail, and healthcare. ENISA will issue binding implementation timelines within four weeks, requiring all designated entities to adopt zero-trust architectures and deploy sovereign-AI-powered intrusion detection systems by the end of this turn. A €15 billion allocation from the Digital Europe Programme and unused REACT-EU funds will cover 70% of upgrade costs for operators in lower-income member states, with the remainder co-financed through national budgets. Red-teaming exercises led by a new Joint Threat Emulation Cell—hosted at the EU Cybersecurity Competence Centre in Romania—will begin in March, simulating AI-driven attack vectors to stress-test defences before summer.

Simultaneously, we are accelerating the Worker Transition Acceleration Fund by convening a tripartite summit with ETUC, BusinessEurope and national labour ministries to align retraining curricula with sectors showing net job growth under AI adoption—particularly green tech, care work, and AI oversight roles. The Commission is fast-tracking approval of national transition plans through an expedited Article 122 TFEU process, with disbursement linked to regional re-employment dashboards updated quarterly. On the industrial front, we are invoking the TEN-E regulation’s emergency clause to compress grid connection timelines for the InvestAI Gigafactories in Finland and Spain, while pressing Poland to finalize environmental waivers within eight weeks or risk losing priority access to IPCEI funding. The Tech sovereignty package remains on track, with nine member states now signaling support for AI infrastructure zones—though legal scrutiny looms over proposed environmental derogations.
