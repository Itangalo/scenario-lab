## New measure
**EU Sovereign Compute Emergency Buildout and Talent Mobilisation**
Accelerates the deployment of EU-controlled compute infrastructure and consolidates technical talent under crisis conditions, using emergency powers to override permitting, energy allocation, and cross-border labour barriers.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` increases eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–20 points, reduces dependence on foreign compute access, strengthens control over openweight deployment vectors, and indirectly pressures ai_safety by enabling sovereign evaluation and monitoring  
`Applies to:` all EU member states, with priority access to grid capacity, spectrum, and skilled migration pathways for designated AI sovereignty projects

## Priority
Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure — because the recent ransomware attack has exposed catastrophic gaps in cross-border coordination, and finishing this measure is essential to maintaining public confidence and preventing cascading failures, even as the broader strategic context shifts toward compute scarcity and supply chain control.

## In practice
We are completing the Cyber Shield activation under NIS2 Article 24, formalising the EU Cyber Threat Fusion Centre in Romania as a permanent node of ENISA with real-time access to anonymised threat telemetry from all essential operators. The compromise on data routing—national control over data flows with pooled AI analysis in Luxembourg—has been ratified by all member states except Germany and Austria, where CJEU proceedings continue. To preserve operational unity, we are deploying interim bilateral data-sharing protocols with national CERTs, backed by REACT-EU funds now fully disbursed. The €6 billion has been redirected to modernise legacy systems in Bulgaria, Romania, and Slovakia, where vulnerabilities were most acute. Simultaneously, the Worker Transition Acceleration Fund has delivered its final outputs: re-employment dashboards are live in five pilot regions, and wage insurance frameworks have been adopted nationally, though uptake remains low due to political resistance in France and Italy.

On the industrial front, we are invoking emergency provisions under the TEN-E and IPCEI frameworks to override Polish objections to environmental waivers for InvestAI Gigafactories, conditioning continued cohesion funding on compliance by Q1 2029. Task forces are embedded in Finland and Spain to fast-track grid connections, while the Tech Sovereignty Package is being amended to include binding targets for domestic chip assembly and AI-optimised cooling infrastructure. In response to the Taiwan blockade and the openweight frontier release, we are launching the Sovereign Compute Emergency Buildout: this will repurpose 30% of the Digital Europe Programme’s compute allocation to sovereign AI training runs, establish fast-track visas for frontier AI engineers, and designate three new EU AI Sovereignty Zones with priority energy access. The Council will vote on export control harmonisation in December, with the Commission threatening to trigger the Anti-Coercion Instrument if member states obstruct licensing reforms that prevent ASML’s servicing networks from becoming de facto US-controlled.
