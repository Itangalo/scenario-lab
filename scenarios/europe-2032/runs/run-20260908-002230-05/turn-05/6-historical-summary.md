The EU launched the Sovereign Compute Emergency Buildout in response to a Taiwan blockade that disrupted global semiconductor supplies, overriding Polish environmental objections through cohesion fund leverage and fast-tracking energy access for AI zones, though Finland and Spain missed grid deadlines and Dutch-Belgian restructuring under Luxembourg raised concerns over transparency. The Cyber Shield was formally completed with the Romanian Fusion Centre operational and zero-trust mandated, but Germany and Austria continued to limit data sharing, relying on bilateral CERT agreements, while public trust declined due to delayed rollout and a prior ransomware attack exploiting legacy systems. The Worker Transition Fund achieved full deployment with re-employment dashboards and wage insurance, yet low uptake in France and Italy weakened its impact, exacerbating perceptions of EU inefficacy. Industrial momentum stalled as the Tech Sovereignty Package's private investment goals appeared unattainable and export control harmonisation failed in the Council due to French and Dutch opposition. Political capital eroded further amid four overlapping initiatives, growing public anxiety over jobs and infrastructure, and anticipation of tighter U.S. technology restrictions following the U.S. election.
