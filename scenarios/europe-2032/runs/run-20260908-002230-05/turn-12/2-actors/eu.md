## In practice  
This turn, we are executing on two fronts: one in triologue, one in the field. The EU-Wide Civic AI Accountability Framework is in its final legislative stretch, but its survival depends on concessions that risk hollowing it out. We are now routing the implementing acts through a temporary co-governance body—national ombudsmen and civil society observers hosted by the European Economic and Social Committee—not as a dilution, but as a bridge. If it passes, it will be the last time we can claim to be rebuilding legitimacy from within the system. But legitimacy means nothing if the system fails.  

Simultaneously, we are activating emergency procedures under Article 122 TFEU and the Union Civil Protection Mechanism to fast-track the Emergency Resilience Mobilisation. ENISA and HERA are being resourced to conduct live-fire cyber defence drills across ten member states by turn’s end, with a focus on ransomware resistance in public health systems. We are also deploying mobile bio-detection units to five major ports and research hubs, pre-positioned to respond to any signal from the uplift findings. This is not precaution—it is triage. The cyber_major_incident exploited a model-generated dependency chain; we were behind before it began. Now, we are racing to ensure that the next attack does not cascade. The cost is high, the capital thinner still—but without this, there will be no sovereign future to defend.
