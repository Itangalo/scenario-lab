The Emergency Resilience Surge ended amid escalating crises, with ENISA deploying fallback systems featuring manual override and red-teaming protocols to ten critical platforms in Germany, France, and the Netherlands. These mitigated a major AI-powered ransomware attack on hospital records, preventing total collapse but exposing fragmented response capabilities. A parallel biosecurity breach—triggered by the accidental release of a model-designed pathogen in northern Europe—confirmed AI's emerging role in biological threats, eroding public trust and sparking violent protests. The Middle-Power Access Compact expired, leading the EU to develop a unilateral audit framework for bilateral AI evaluation, though no agreements were signed. The Commission launched the EU-Wide Civic AI Accountability Framework, mandating algorithmic impact assessments and citizen oversight tied to sovereign compute access, but faced strong resistance from Eastern member states. Despite completing key initiatives, political capital declined as public sentiment worsened.
