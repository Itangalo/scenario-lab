A binding directive establishing independent, transparent review of all AI systems used in public services, with mandatory public hearings, algorithmic impact assessments, and citizen-led oversight panels.

## In practice
This turn, we are closing out the Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 122 TFEU, with ENISA delivering final fallback architectures to ten critical systems across Germany, France, and the Netherlands. These include medical triage platforms, unemployment benefits automation, and border control decision engines — all now equipped with manual override, immutable logging, and red-teaming protocols. The systems are operational, but the human cost of the cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep that locked hospital records in three countries — confirms that technical hardening alone is not enough. We are treating the incident as a wake-up call: the tooling was AI-generated, the response was fragmented, and the public sees only chaos.

Simultaneously, the bio_incident — a laboratory release involving a model-designed agent — has shattered what little trust remained. We are now launching the EU-Wide Civic AI Accountability Framework as the cornerstone of our new two-year commitment. This is not a technical fix but a political one: the directive will require every public AI deployment to undergo algorithmic impact assessment, publish its risk profile, and submit to citizen-led oversight panels with real powers to halt deployment. We are proposing it through the ordinary legislative procedure under Article 114 TFEU, with the Commission drafting by turn’s end. The Council’s Internal Market and General Affairs formation will lead negotiations, but we are already meeting resistance from member states wary of slowing digital transformation. To overcome this, we are linking access to next phase sovereign compute funding to compliance — a leverage we now have, barely, and must use.

The Middle-Power Access Compact has collapsed in practice, though not yet in name. With Washington dismissive and Seoul absent, we are converting its evaluation mechanisms into a standalone EU instrument — one we will offer to partners bilaterally. This is not the coalition we wanted, but it is the accountability we can build now.
