## New measure
Worker Transition Acceleration Fund  
Establishes an EU-level rapid-response mechanism to support displaced workers in administrative, legal support and junior software roles, with wage insurance, retraining pathways and incentives for firms to redeploy talent.  
Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 5  
Targeted effect: +10 to resilience via faster absorption of workforce shocks; stabilize public sentiment against backlash; modest upward pressure on eu_political_capital through visible solidarity  
Applies to: all member states, with conditionality tied to national implementation speed and equity benchmarks

## Priority
Worker Transition Acceleration Fund — because emergent labour displacement has already begun, and without a credible response public sentiment will collapse, undermining both our ability to act and the legitimacy of our industrial strategy.

## In practice
We are activating emergency flexicurity instruments under the European Social Fund Plus and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, repurposing €45 billion in unused allocations toward a new Worker Transition Acceleration Fund. The Commission is drafting a Council Regulation under Article 122 TFEU to fast-track approval, leveraging the economic hardship clause triggered by widespread job losses across Germany, France and Italy. Member states must submit national transition plans within six weeks to access funding, with priority given to regions where AI-driven displacement exceeds 15% in targeted sectors.

Resistance is expected from fiscally conservative states wary of open-ended commitments, so we are pairing the proposal with a sunset clause after two years and binding performance metrics: at least 60% of supported workers must be retrained or re-employed within 18 months. To gain buy-in from employer groups, we are introducing tax credits for companies that retain and redeploy affected staff rather than lay them off. The European Centre for the Development of Vocational Training (Cedefop) will coordinate curricula development for AI-augmented legal and administrative roles, ensuring training aligns with evolving demand.

Simultaneously, we continue advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories programme, finalizing site selections in Finland, Spain and Poland with grid integration timelines now compressed by invoking the TEN-E regulation’s emergency provisions. The Tech sovereignty package is entering its second phase: we have secured agreement from seven member states to designate AI infrastructure zones with automatic permitting and priority grid access, though delays in environmental impact waivers threaten to slow rollout. These efforts remain central to our long-term aim — but this turn, the immediate test is whether the Union can absorb disruption without fracturing.
