### A Fragile Opening

A rare interpretability advance—allowing real-time tracing of high-stakes decisions in deployed models—spreads rapidly across leading labs, lifting global ai_safety. The breakthrough, born from a shared need for control, is quickly integrated into evaluation pipelines. Simultaneously, the US and China announce a limited pact on biological design tools, including basic verification. While the EU was neither consulted nor included, the agreement slows frontier progress slightly, contributing to a modest +0.5 in ai_capability.

Encouraged, the EU activates a joint biosurveillance response mechanism, gaining access to early-warning data and coordinated containment protocols. This collective shield boosts resilience, helping systems absorb ongoing cyber stress and bio-risk signals.

### Sovereignty on Shaky Ground

The Commission launches the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive, aiming to build five sovereign data centres by mid-2033. Fast-tracked permitting and EIB funding are announced, but immediate progress stalls: Poland pushes back on hosting terms, ENTSO-E warns of grid strain, and hyperscalers demand stronger legal guarantees before repatriating workloads. No steel is laid this turn.

Meanwhile, the EU-Wide Civic AI Accountability Framework advances in trilogue, but Eastern resistance hardens. Offers of flexible timelines tied to cohesion funds are seen as coercion. The priority status yields no acceleration—political capital is too low for pull-in effects to function.

### Capital Erodes, Foundations Tremble

With ai_capability rising and no category 4 completion, eu_ai_sovereignty slips further. The portfolio charge overwhelms limited gains: two large measures in flight drain political capital, and public sentiment remains stagnant. The legitimacy feedback loop fails to trigger. The EU acts, but the ground keeps moving.
