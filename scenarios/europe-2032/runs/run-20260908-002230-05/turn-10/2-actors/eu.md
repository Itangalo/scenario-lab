## In practice
This turn, we are advancing the EU-Wide Civic AI Accountability Framework through co-decision under Article 114 TFEU, with the Commission publishing its draft directive by April and opening trilogue consultations with the Parliament’s Committee on Legal Affairs and the Council’s Internal Market formation. We are deploying high-level envoys to Warsaw, Budapest, and Bucharest to counter resistance from Eastern member states, offering differentiated implementation timelines tied to cohesion funding adjustments — not as punishment, but as recognition that institutional readiness varies. The linkage between compliance and access to next-phase sovereign compute funding remains our strongest lever: no country will be excluded outright, but priority allocation will go to those who ratify early and implement fully.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive as a response to the widening gap between global capability and EU control. The proposal will designate five sites — two in France, two in Germany, one in Poland — for immediate permitting override under a new emergency clause in the TEN-Digital Regulation, with grid connections fast-tracked by ENTSO-E and co-financed through a reprogrammed portion of the Digital Europe Programme and EIB loans backed by the Multiannual Financial Framework. Each facility will operate under a dual-use mandate: commercial workloads permitted only if public safety, evaluation, and emergency access take absolute precedence. We are already in talks with three European hyperscalers to repatriate training runs currently hosted overseas, offering tax incentives conditional on full audit rights and secure weight-handling.

The world has shifted: the **safety_breakthrough** gives us a rare opening to embed new interpretability tools into both incoming systems and our own evaluators, but only if we have the compute and jurisdictional control to apply them. The **us_china_agreement**, while excluding us, confirms that great powers are beginning to constrain themselves — which makes our dependence on their grace more dangerous, not less. And the **joint_threat_response** in biosurveillance offers a seat at the table, but only if we can demonstrate credible domestic capacity. That credibility does not come from declarations. It comes from steel in the ground, code under audit, and power under sovereign switch. We are building all three — but first, we must prove we still govern.
