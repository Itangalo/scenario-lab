## New measure  
**Launch the Regional Data Resilience Pact**  
Establish a binding, EU-coordinated network for real-time cyber incident detection, data sharing, and joint response among critical infrastructure operators across member states, leveraging the momentum of the emergent Regional Data Coop.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `resilience` by at least 5 points by enabling faster detection and coordinated response to AI-optimized attacks; modest upward pressure on `eu_ai_sovereignty` by reducing dependency on non-EU coordination mechanisms; potential short-term strain on `eu_political_capital` due to integration complexity, offset by alignment with national security agencies.  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with mandatory participation from national cybersecurity agencies, critical energy, transport, and health infrastructure operators, and integration with ENISA’s threat intelligence platform.

## Priority  
**Priority:** *Launch the Regional Data Resilience Pact* — because the cyber_major_incident has exposed that our defences are not just weak, but disconnected, and because the emergent_regional_data_coop offers a rare moment of alignment between member states to act before the next strike lands.

## In practice  
We are moving fast to institutionalise the momentum behind the emergent Regional Data Coop. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that crippled regional health services in three member states — has made clear that patchwork defences are no longer tenable. This turn, the Commission is drafting a Regulation under Article 114 TFEU to establish the Regional Data Resilience Pact as a binding framework. ENISA will be empowered to mandate real-time telemetry sharing from critical operators, with anonymised data routed through a newly established Cross-Border Incident Fusion Cell hosted in Luxembourg. Germany, France, and the Nordic states have already signalled support; we are offering Italy and Spain co-leadership of the Fusion Cell’s AI threat modelling unit to secure buy-in.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on all existing measures. The Counter extraterritorial pressure initiative is now in active diplomacy mode: the High Representative has delivered démarches to Washington, Seoul, and Tokyo, while legal teams finalise the WTO notice. We are also advancing bilateral service agreements with Japanese equipment firms, offering reciprocal access to EU metrology standards in exchange for guaranteed DUV maintenance. On evaluation, the Council’s Legal Service remains cautious, but the cyber incident has shifted the debate: we are now reframing EAEVA’s mandate as a resilience instrument, not just a safety one, and offering Ireland and the Netherlands veto rights over red-team targets in exchange for dropping proportionality objections. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, though we are diverting €1.8bn from the Tech Sovereignty Package’s private capital incentive pool to de-risk grid upgrades in Germany, pairing it with Just Transition Fund allocations to pacify local opposition. This is not just infrastructure — it is legitimacy, wired into the grid.
