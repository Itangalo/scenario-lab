## New measure
**Secure EU sovereign evaluation capability**  
Establish a permanent, well-resourced EU body with legal authority to independently evaluate and monitor frontier models before deployment.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by 8–12 points; create durable public capacity to assess models independently of developers; modest upward pressure on `eu_political_capital` over time by demonstrating competence.  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with mandatory access to models developed or deployed in the Union.

## Priority
**Priority:** *Secure EU sovereign evaluation capability* — because the covert coordination incident and the openweight frontier release prove that private assurance is no longer sufficient, and we cannot rely on external powers to safeguard our security when their own actions show inconsistent commitment.

## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that the Union cannot outsource its understanding of what the frontier does. The cyber_test_shot and openweight_frontier_release have shattered any illusion that risks are contained or detectable through market mechanisms alone. This turn, the Commission will draft a regulation under Article 16 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU, invoking the need for urgent action in the area of security of the Union, to establish the European AI Evaluation Agency (EAEVA). The agency will be headquartered in Brussels with technical nodes in Paris and Munich, drawing on existing research capacity at INRIA, Fraunhofer, and the ELIXIR bioinformatics network. It will be funded with €1.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe, reallocated via the upcoming mid-term review.

Resistance is expected from member states with large hosting interests — particularly Ireland and the Netherlands — who fear additional friction for foreign hyperscalers. We are offering them guaranteed first access to EAEVA’s red-teaming results and co-location rights for national evaluation units. The Council’s Permanent Representatives Committee will be briefed next month, and we are fast-tracking a special session of the Competitiveness Council in December to lock in political support before the winter pause. This is not about slowing innovation — it is about ensuring that when something goes wrong, we know why, and who.
