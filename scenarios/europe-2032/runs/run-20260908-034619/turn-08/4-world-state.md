### The Fracture Deepens

A major AI-powered cyberattack sweeps across hospital networks and transport systems, overwhelming response teams. Defenders are blind until media reports break—no early warning systems triggered. The EU’s proposed telemetry mandate stalls as Germany and Poland invoke national security to block data sharing. Italy’s new digital minister derides the plan as a “data pipeline to Brussels.” In response, the Commission shifts from imposition to co-governance: the Cyber Threat Fusion Cell will be jointly managed by national CISOs with veto power over data use.

### A Strike at the Core

Civil servants in France, Germany, and Italy launch coordinated strike actions, halting digital service upgrades and audit preparations. Unions demand a moratorium on AI in public services until redress mechanisms are fully implemented and trusted. The strikes expose a deeper crisis: automation is seen not as support, but as a tool of managerial overreach. Public sentiment dips further, now framing AI as both dangerous and dehumanizing.

### Closing One Chapter, Opening Another

The *public right to explanation* directive formally completes, becoming law in five member states. France integrates its pilot; Germany remains out, citing federal complexity. Enforcement is patchy—national DPAs are understaffed and lack technical capacity. Still, the finish delivers a political capital boost for delivering amid crisis.

Simultaneously, the EU launches a new small measure: a tripartite crisis protocol linking ENISA, EAEVA, and civil service unions. Funded through Horizon Europe to bypass national resistance, it begins with monthly tabletop exercises. The move is symbolic but necessary—sovereignty cannot hold if public institutions withdraw consent.

### Capability Marches On

Global AI capability advances to 64.5, driven by autonomous research agents. Open-weight models reach 58.1, widening misuse risks. No safety gains land—assurance lags. Resilience inches up slightly from partial telemetry progress and the new coordination effort. But political capital erodes under dual pressures: high portfolio cost and internal dissent. Sovereignty slips as dependence deepens and no domestic compute comes online.
