## New measure
**Launch sovereign compute access for public technical institutions**  
Provide guaranteed, high-priority access to EU-controlled frontier compute clusters for public evaluators, red teams, and safety researchers to strengthen operational oversight and independent analysis.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` Strengthen `ai_safety` (+3 to +5) by enabling continuous, independent evaluation of frontier systems; support `eu_ai_sovereignty` (+2) by anchoring technical scrutiny within EU-controlled infrastructure; mitigate risks from rising `ai_capability` (now 58.5) by ensuring public actors can keep pace. Risk of friction with private developers over resource allocation.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, EAEVA, ENISA, and designated national public research and evaluation bodies.

## Priority
**Priority:** *Launch sovereign compute access for public technical institutions* — because as private frontier systems advance and the US offers structured access under alignment conditions, we must ensure our own evaluators are not dependent on goodwill or foreign infrastructure to do their job, and because without sovereign compute for public scrutiny, sovereignty is hollow.

## In practice  
We are closing the first two-year cycle under the banner of securing foundational capacity, and the results are mixed but real. The Regional Data Resilience Pact is now in its final negotiation phase: Spain and Italy have accepted rotating co-chairmanship of the threat modelling unit, and Poland’s legal concerns were addressed through a recital affirming member state control over operational response. ENISA has integrated live telemetry from Finland and the Netherlands, and the first cross-border alert protocol will be tested in September. However, full activation is delayed by unresolved data-sharing protocols with private telecoms, and we are now pushing for a delegated act under Article 114 TFEU to override holdouts.

On industrial sovereignty, the InvestAI Gigafactories are entering site finalisation, with Bavaria and Wallonia selected for phase one. The €1.8bn redirection to substation upgrades has secured grid commitments, and Just Transition Fund co-financing has eased local opposition. The Tech Sovereignty Package is now tied to national permitting reforms: countries that establish fast-track zones for AI infrastructure will receive priority access to the incentive pool. Meanwhile, the Counter extraterritorial pressure initiative concludes this turn — the WTO notice is filed, and the Japanese DUV maintenance agreement is signed, securing critical lithography support outside US jurisdiction.

The European AI Talent Shield is now live, with France and Germany committing €3bn each. We are offering smaller states embedded fellowships and regional compute nodes to ensure equity. But the election_alliance event shifts the ground: the US is offering structured access to frontier models for aligned partners. This is a relief — but also a trap. If we accept foreign capability as a substitute for our own, sovereignty erodes. That is why this turn we launch sovereign compute access for public institutions: EAEVA, ENISA, and national red teams must run on EU-controlled clusters, with priority scheduling above commercial users. We are reallocating 15% of Gigafactory phase-one capacity for this purpose, funded through a reprioritisation of the InvestAI capital stack. This is not a new programme — it is the operational core of sovereignty: the ability to see, test, and challenge without asking permission.
