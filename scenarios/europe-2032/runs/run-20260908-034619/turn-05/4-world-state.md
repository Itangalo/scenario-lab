### The Alliance and the Scandal

The United States elects a president committed to transatlantic technological cooperation. The result is met with cautious optimism in diplomatic circles, though details remain sparse. While early signals suggest a willingness to discuss structured access to frontier AI systems for aligned partners, no agreements have been concluded, and conditions around export controls and standards are still under negotiation. For now, EU access remains dependent on goodwill, with no concrete changes to capacity or control.

At the same time, a damning ombudsman report reveals that an AI-driven benefits assessment tool in a major member state denied thousands of legitimate claims, some for over two years. The system, trained on biased historical data and shielded from review, becomes a symbol of unchecked automation. Protests erupt, and public trust in AI plummets.

### Sovereignty Through Public Access

The EU launches *Sovereign Compute Access for Public Technical Institutions*, redirecting 15% of InvestAI Gigafactory capacity to EAEVA, ENISA, and national red teams. The policy is formally adopted, but implementation will take time: compute allocation begins at a reduced scale and ramps up over the next turn. This strengthens long-term oversight capacity and anchors scrutiny in EU-controlled infrastructure. The move is technically sound but sparks tension with private developers, who protest the prioritisation of public over commercial workloads.

The *Regional Data Resilience Pact* and *Counter Extraterritorial Pressure* initiative both finish this turn. The pact achieves partial integration, with live telemetry now flowing across five member states, earning a +3 boost to resilience. The legal override for telecoms remains pending. The semiconductor defence package secures Japan’s cooperation, mitigating immediate supply risks.

### Capital Erodes, Sentiment Falls

Political capital declines despite two completions (+4 total). The portfolio charge of −13 (four large measures, priority) outweighs gains. The scandal, of internal origin, costs −8. The new measure earns no proposal bonus — it answers no recent external shock. Public sentiment drops sharply on the scandal, falling to 33.0.

Sovereignty rises by 1.0: +2 from the ongoing InvestAI Gigafactories, −1 from ai_capability crossing 60, and no capacity event. The US alliance offers no immediate sovereignty gain, as no binding commitments or transfers of capability have materialized. Openweight capability increases modestly to 47.5, reflecting continued incremental progress in open model deployment and tuning, consistent with the underlying growth of ai_capability but not exceeding it disproportionately. No sudden leap occurs, as no major open model release or infrastructure activation is realized this turn.
