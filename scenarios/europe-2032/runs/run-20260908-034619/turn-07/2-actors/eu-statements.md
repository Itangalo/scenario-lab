## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now accept that without public legitimacy, neither can hold.
  - Grounds: The welfare scandal and the cyber_major_incident have converged into a crisis of institutional credibility. Capability without accountability is coercion; safety without sovereignty is dependence. But legitimacy without protection is performance. The Union cannot sustain its dual mandate if one half constantly undermines the other. This revision does not weaken the original identity — it sharpens it with the lesson of this turn: political agency in AI is not just about control over technology, but about earned authority over its use.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as an excuse for opacity.
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident exploited tools generated by models we cannot inspect, and the welfare scandal was enabled by systems we did not understand. We acted fast, but our actions were blind. This turn, we saw that acting under uncertainty is not enough — we must act in ways that reduce uncertainty for others. The commitment stands, but its execution must now include mandatory transparency in public-sector AI, not just as a restriction, but as a condition of legitimacy. Speed without scrutiny is not leadership — it is drift with momentum.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
