## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now accept that without public legitimacy, neither can hold.  
- Grounds: The welfare scandal and the cyber_major_incident have converged into a crisis of institutional credibility. Capability without accountability is coercion; safety without sovereignty is dependence. But legitimacy without protection is performance. The Union cannot sustain its dual mandate if one half constantly undermines the other. This revision does not weaken the original identity — it sharpens it with the lesson of this turn: political agency in AI is not just about control over technology, but about earned authority over its use.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as an excuse for opacity.  
- Grounds: The cyber_major_incident exploited tools generated by models we cannot inspect, and the welfare scandal was enabled by systems we did not understand. We acted fast, but our actions were blind. This turn, we saw that acting under uncertainty is not enough — we must act in ways that reduce uncertainty for others. The commitment stands, but its execution must now include mandatory transparency in public-sector AI, not just as a restriction, but as a condition of legitimacy. Speed without scrutiny is not leadership — it is drift with momentum.

## New measure  
**Mandate real-time incident telemetry from all critical infrastructure operators**  
Require all operators of essential services to stream anonymized, real-time cyber incident telemetry to a centralized EU monitoring hub, enabling early detection and coordinated response.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 9  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `resilience` (+5 to +7) by enabling faster detection and containment of AI-driven attacks; improve `ai_safety` (+2) by revealing emergent threat patterns from deployed systems; signal to member states that fragmentation is no longer acceptable. Risk of resistance from national regulators and operators citing sovereignty and operational secrecy.  
`Applies to:` All EU member states, for energy, water, transport, healthcare, and digital infrastructure operators designated under NIS2.  

## Priority  
**Priority:** *Mandate real-time incident telemetry from all critical infrastructure operators* — because the cyber_major_incident revealed that we are blind to the very threats we are supposed to defend against, and no amount of sovereign compute or public redress will matter if we cannot see the next attack coming.  

## In practice  
We are finishing the first cycle of our two-year commitment under fire. The Gigafactories are online, the Talent Shield has placed 187 researchers in public technical roles, and sovereign compute access is now live at ENISA and EAEVA — but none of it matters if we cannot see. The cyber_major_incident was not just an attack; it was a mirror. We have resilience on paper, but in practice, we learned about the breach from a hospital ransomware notice, not from our own sensors. The Regional Data Resilience Pact delivered integration across five states, but three others ran parallel red-team exercises outside the EU framework — a dangerous divergence. We cannot have sovereignty in compute if we lack visibility in crisis.  

So we are acting on the blind spot. We are drafting a regulation under Article 114 TFEU to mandate real-time, anonymized telemetry from all critical infrastructure operators, feeding into a new EU Cyber Threat Fusion Cell hosted at ENISA. The data will be encrypted, access strictly controlled, and used only for threat detection and response coordination. Germany and Poland have already signaled resistance, citing national security and data sovereignty — so we are offering co-location: the raw data stays in national hubs, but metadata streams to the Fusion Cell under a new Schengen-style protocol. We will fund 70% of the integration through the Digital Europe Programme, with penalties for non-compliance redirected to cross-border response drills. This is not surveillance — it is shared survival.  

And we are holding the line on transparency in public AI. The right to explanation directive is now in trilogue, and we are pushing for audit logs to be machine-readable and publicly queryable — not just for appeals, but for pattern detection. France has agreed to pilot it in social services by turn 8; Germany remains hesitant, but we are offering full Just Transition Fund coverage for their federal-state integration costs. We are not trading sovereignty for speed, nor safety for trust. We are trying, for once, to build all three at once — because this turn has shown that we cannot afford to build any of them alone.
