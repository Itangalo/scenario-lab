### The Weight of Crisis

A sweeping AI-powered ransomware campaign cripples regional hospitals and transit authorities across southern and central Europe. The attack exploits zero-day vulnerabilities in legacy medical and logistics software, with malicious code auto-generated and tailored at scale. Defenders are blindsided—no telemetry flows from key operators, and early warnings fail. The Cyber Threat Fusion Cell stands up in name only, its data pool incomplete due to national opt-outs. Germany and Poland maintain strict data sovereignty, while Italy’s participation remains symbolic.

### Closing Measures, Mixed Results

The *public right to explanation* directive formally concludes, but enforcement lags—only five member states have operational redress mechanisms, and DPAs lack resources to audit AI systems effectively. The telemetry mandate completes on paper, yet real-time data sharing remains patchy. The tripartite crisis protocol, however, delivers tangible progress: monthly tabletop exercises build trust between ENISA, EAEVA, and civil service unions, and joint threat assessments improve response coordination. Though symbolic, the protocol stabilizes morale in key agencies.

### A New Path: Co-Design Over Mandate

In response to the civil servant strikes and legitimacy crisis, the EU launches the Civil Service AI Co-Design Fellowship. France and the Netherlands commit participants; Germany delays, citing federal complexity. The programme embeds frontline operators into AI governance, aiming to transform resistance into ownership. Early design workshops yield practical improvements to redress workflows, but systemic distrust persists.

### Capability Creeps Forward

Global AI capability advances to 66.0, driven by autonomous research agents and closed-loop cyber tools. Open-weight models reach 59.6, widening the gap between frontier and safety. No safety gains land—assurance remains reactive. Resilience rises slightly, buoyed by the tripartite protocol’s partial success. Sovereignty dips as foreign dependence deepens and no domestic compute comes online. Political capital sees a modest rebound from finishing two measures, offset by high portfolio costs and low sentiment.
