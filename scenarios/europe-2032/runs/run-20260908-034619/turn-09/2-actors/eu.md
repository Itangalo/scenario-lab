## New measure
**Launch EU Civil Service AI Co-Design Fellowship Programme**  
Embed seconded civil servants from national administrations into EU-level AI governance bodies and pilot deployment teams to co-develop tools, redress workflows, and incident response playbooks.

`Size:` small

## Priority
**Priority:** *Launch EU Civil Service AI Co-Design Fellowship Programme* — because the strike revealed that the crisis is not only about safety or control, but legitimacy: public institutions will not defend systems they did not help build, and no mandate can override that refusal. This measure directly addresses the fracture by making civil servants co-authors of the tools they must operate, turning resistance into ownership.

## In practice
We are closing two major measures this turn: the *public right to explanation*, now law in five states but still unevenly enforced, and the telemetry mandate, which formally completes but faces active resistance in Germany, Poland, and Italy. The Cyber Threat Fusion Cell will stand up under co-governance rules—national CISOs hold veto power, and redaction protocols are being co-drafted with operators through the CSIRT network. This is not ideal, but it is functional: better shared control than no data at all. The tripartite crisis protocol also concludes its first cycle, with tabletop exercises held in Luxembourg and Berlin, union representatives participating directly, and anonymized threat feeds now routed to the forum. Early feedback is cautiously positive—operators feel heard, and threat assessments are richer for it.

Now we go further: the new Co-Design Fellowship will second 50 civil servants over the next 18 months—ten per wave—into rotating placements at ENISA, EAEVA, and EU digital innovation hubs. They will work on live redress cases, stress-test telemetry dashboards, and help draft the next version of the incident playbook. Funding comes from the Digital Europe Programme, with participation incentivized through staff development credits and union-nominated selection. France and the Netherlands have already signaled interest; Germany remains hesitant, but we are offering federal-state parity in cohort allocation to ease concerns. This is not consultation as afterthought, but integration by design. If sovereignty is to survive the next incident, it must be lived in the daily work of those who keep the state running—not imposed from above, but built from within.
