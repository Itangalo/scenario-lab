## New measure
**Launch EU-Wide AI Harm Reckoning and Redress Initiative**  
Establish binding accountability mechanisms and visible compensation pathways for individuals and sectors harmed by AI systems, starting with automated decision scandals and displaced entry-level workers.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 15  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `public_sentiment` (+15) and `resilience` (+8) by delivering tangible, visible redress to those wronged by AI systems and creating a transition pathway for displaced workers; signal that the EU governs for people, not just systems.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member states, national labour ministries, social insurance bodies, and public-sector employers using AI in hiring or decision-making.

## Priority
**Priority:** *Launch EU-Wide AI Harm Reckoning and Redress Initiative* — because while the compute buildout remains urgent, the **automated_decision_scandal** and **labour_displacement** have made clear that without immediate action to restore public trust, no amount of sovereign infrastructure will be politically sustainable; this turn, legitimacy is the scarcer resource.

## In practice
We are finishing the Civil Service Co-Design Fellowship, which delivered modest but real integration: 12 operators embedded, joint playbooks drafted, trust built in pockets. But Germany’s continued non-participation and the telemetry blockage in Italy and Poland show that legal integration cannot outpace political will. The tripartite protocol exercises worked—when systems went down, the right people picked up the phone—but too many systems were not ours to run.

Now we are forcing the pace on compute: invoking Article 122 TFEU to override national permitting delays, directing ENTSO-E to prioritise grid access for qualifying AI data centres, and conditioning access to the Single Market on 20% crisis capacity allocation. We are using REPowerEU’s digital resilience window and MFF flexibility to fund it. But resistance is hard: Germany’s constitutional challenge, Spain’s grid refusal, and the energy spikes in Bavaria and Andalusia have made this a battle not of technology, but of legitimacy.

And now the ground has shifted beneath us. The **automated_decision_scandal**—a social insurance algorithm denying benefits to thousands—has made restriction politically cheap, but also made adoption impossible without accountability. The graduate job market has collapsed in law, accountancy, and administration, not because of poor policy, but because the systems work too well. We cannot rebuild consent on the promise of future control. We must repair harm now. So we are launching the Reckoning Initiative: a pan-EU taskforce under the European Labour Authority and the EDPS to audit and compensate wronged individuals, paired with a retraining compact for displaced graduates funded through the Social Climate Fund. This is not optional. It is the price of remaining a Union.
