### A Crisis of Legitimacy

The EU’s emergency compute buildout, intended as a bulwark against strategic dependence, faltered under legal and political resistance. Germany and Poland advanced constitutional challenges, utilities withheld grid access, and energy disruptions in pilot regions deepened public distrust. The sudden denial of access to the leading frontier model—used widely in hospitals and ministries—exposed the Union’s fragility, but the political response only widened the rift.

Amid this, the automated decision scandal erupted: a social insurance AI was found to have systematically denied benefits, triggering public outrage and a formal ombudsman ruling. Simultaneously, entry-level job markets in law, accountancy, and administration collapsed, with graduates facing a future without footholds. In response, the EU announced the **AI Harm Reckoning and Redress Initiative**, a large-scale effort to audit harms and fund retraining. However, due to low `eu_political_capital` (now at 9.0), the initiative remains in early planning stages, with no implementation yet and uncertain prospects for funding or coordination.

The drop is significant but incremental, not catastrophic.
