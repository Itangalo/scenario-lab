## Priority
**Priority:** *Accelerate EU Sovereign Compute Buildout via Emergency Grid Access and Siting Waivers* — because the cyber_major_incident exposed not just operational fragility, but strategic dependence: we cannot command systems we do not control, and we cannot defend what we cannot run when the outside world cuts us off. ## In practice
This turn, we are finishing three major measures: the *public right to explanation*, the telemetry mandate, and the tripartite crisis protocol. The first two are laws on paper but patchy in practice—only five member states have functional redress mechanisms, and real-time telemetry remains blocked in Germany, Poland, and Italy over data sovereignty concerns. Still, the tripartite protocol has delivered something real: monthly tabletop exercises between ENISA, EAEVA, and civil service unions have built trust and improved joint threat assessment. That trust is now our only working capital.

We are carrying forward the Civil Service AI Co-Design Fellowship, now in its first cohort phase. France and the Netherlands have seconded 12 operators; Germany has agreed to observe, not participate. The programme is small but symbolic—embedding frontline workers into EU AI governance teams to co-design redress workflows and incident playbooks. It runs on Digital Europe Programme funds, with staff credits and union-nominated selection as incentives. This is not consultation. It is integration.

Now we act on the deeper fracture: the absence of sovereign compute. The eval_anomaly_reports and the ransomware sweep prove that capability is outpacing control, and that openweight models at 59.6 are already near the threshold of autonomous harm. We cannot regulate what we cannot run, nor defend what we cannot replace. So we invoke Article 122 TFEU to justify emergency grid access for AI data centres that commit to sovereign service. National regulators must fast-track permits; transmission operators must prioritize connections. In return, private hyperscalers gain market certainty—but only if they agree to allocate 20% of their capacity to EU-critical workloads under crisis conditions. We will fund this through REPowerEU’s digital resilience window and the MFF’s margin, pulling every lever to make it binding. This is not a subsidy. It is a condition of doing business at scale in the Single Market. Without it, we are not sovereign. We are tenants.
