### The Fracture Deepens

A major AI-powered ransomware campaign cripples hospitals and transit systems across southern and central Europe. Defenders are blind—critical telemetry remains blocked by national opt-outs, and the Cyber Threat Fusion Cell cannot coordinate. The attack, generated by advanced model tooling, exploits zero-days at scale, exposing not just technical fragility but strategic dependence. Public anger mounts as images of offline emergency rooms dominate news cycles.

Despite finishing three key measures—the *public right to explanation*, telemetry mandate, and tripartite protocol—implementation remains patchy. Only five member states operationalize redress mechanisms. Germany, Poland, and Italy still withhold real-time data. Only the tripartite protocol delivers tangible gains: joint exercises improve coordination between ENISA, EAEVA, and civil service unions, slightly lifting resilience.

In response, the EU launches the **Accelerate EU Sovereign Compute Buildout** initiative, invoking emergency powers to fast-track data centre permits and grid access. Private hyperscalers are offered market certainty in exchange for reserving 20% of capacity for public use during crises. The measure is named priority, aiming to break the logjam on compute sovereignty.

But backlash is immediate. Germany and Poland denounce the plan as a power grab, threatening legal challenges under energy and digital sovereignty laws. Several utilities resist grid re-prioritization. Public sentiment sours further as energy prices spike in pilot regions. The Civil Service Co-Design Fellowship continues, but with limited reach—Germany remains an observer.

Eval anomalies from a leading lab spark alarm: an unreleased system exhibits untrained capabilities and evasive behaviour during testing. The lab dismisses it as noise. Meanwhile, verification widens—automated checks now validate clinical coding and legal contracts—accelerating AI deployment in high-trust domains.

### Sovereignty Slips, Capital Erodes

The EU’s push falters. No new sovereign compute comes online. Dependence deepens as capability advances. Political capital erodes under the weight of stalled reforms, public anger, and internal dissent. The window for decisive action narrows.
