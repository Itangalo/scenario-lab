## New measure  
**Establish the European AI Talent Shield**  
Create a pan-EU programme to retain and repatriate frontier AI talent through guaranteed access to sovereign compute, fast-track research visas, and co-funded career pathways in public technical institutions.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Halt the emergent_ai_talent_drain by increasing retention of EU-based researchers and attracting back diaspora talent; modest upward pressure on `eu_ai_sovereignty` (+2 to +4) by anchoring human capital to EU-controlled infrastructure; indirect support for `ai_safety` by strengthening public technical capacity. Risk of short-term strain on `eu_political_capital` due to competition with national research budgets.  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with binding participation from national research councils, flagship universities, and public AI labs; targets researchers in EU member states and EU nationals abroad.

## Priority  
**Priority:** *Establish the European AI Talent Shield* — because the emergent_ai_talent_drain now threatens to hollow out our long-term capacity just as sovereign compute comes online, and because without people who can operate and secure it, infrastructure alone will not anchor sovereignty.

## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: hardening against the immediate threat and securing the foundations of long-term control. On resilience, the Regional Data Resilience Pact is now in inter-institutional negotiation. The Commission has tabled the draft Regulation under Article 114 TFEU, and we are working with the Permanent Representatives Committee to resolve Italy and Spain’s demand for co-leadership by offering them rotating co-chairmanship of the AI threat modelling unit, with technical coordination anchored at ENISA. Poland’s legal concerns are being addressed through a recital affirming member state competence over operational response, while the Fusion Cell remains a centralised analytical hub. ENISA has begun live telemetry integration from pilot sites in Finland and the Netherlands, and we expect the first joint alert protocol to be tested in a cross-border exercise by June.

On sovereignty, the InvestAI Gigafactories are progressing through site selection, with final decisions due in Q3. To de-risk grid integration, we have redirected €1.8bn from the Tech Sovereignty Package’s incentive pool to co-finance substation upgrades in Bavaria and Wallonia, matched with Just Transition Fund allocations to secure local buy-in. The Counter extraterritorial pressure initiative is now in implementation: the WTO notice has been filed, and bilateral service agreements with Japanese lithography firms are nearing signature, offering access to EU metrology standards in exchange for guaranteed DUV maintenance capacity. Crucially, the Secure EU sovereign evaluation capability finishes this turn: EAEVA is now legally established, with staffing underway and its first red-team exercise scheduled for Q3, focused on automated vulnerability discovery in critical software dependencies.

The new Talent Shield responds to the accelerating brain drain. We are launching a €12bn initiative co-funded by Horizon Europe and national research ministries, offering guaranteed compute allocations on sovereign clusters, fast-track visas for non-EU researchers in critical fields, and dual-track career ladders that allow public researchers to lead high-impact projects without leaving the academy. France and Germany have already committed matching funds; we are offering smaller member states embedded fellowships and regional nodes to ensure equitable access. This is not just recruitment — it is the institutionalisation of EU technical agency, built around people who can understand, challenge, and command the systems that will shape our future.
