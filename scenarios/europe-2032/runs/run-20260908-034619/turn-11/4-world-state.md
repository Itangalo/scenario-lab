### The Weight of Dependence

Despite the completion of three foundational AI governance measures — the *right to explanation*, the telemetry mandate, and the tripartite crisis protocol — their impact remains fragmented. Only five member states have implemented functional redress systems, and real-time data sharing is still blocked by Germany, Poland, and Italy. While joint exercises between ENISA, EAEVA, and civil service unions have improved coordination, trust is thin and operational cohesion remains elusive.

The EU launched the **Accelerate EU Sovereign Compute Buildout**, invoking emergency powers to fast-track data centre development and secure 20% of private hyperscaler capacity for public use during crises. Intended as a decisive step toward strategic autonomy, the initiative immediately triggered resistance. Germany and Poland challenged its legality under energy and digital sovereignty laws, while utilities in France and Spain refused to re-prioritize grid access. Pilot regions saw energy prices spike, fueling public backlash.

The Civil Service Co-Design Fellowship continued with limited participation. France and the Netherlands embedded operators, but Germany remained an observer, underscoring the political divide. Meanwhile, openweight models advanced, now capable of executing multi-day professional projects — a threshold crossed without corresponding safeguards.

Eval anomalies in a leading lab’s unreleased system deepened concerns about uncontrolled capability growth, yet no coordinated response emerged. Verification systems expanded into clinical and legal domains, but these gains were overshadowed by eroding public trust. Images of offline hospitals and disrupted transit networks dominated media coverage, reinforcing perceptions of AI as a source of fragility.

With no sovereign compute coming online, dependence on foreign providers deepened. Political capital dwindled under the strain of internal dissent, stalled implementation, and rising costs. The window for unified action narrowed further as member states retreated into national positions.

### A System Under Strain

The EU’s attempt to force structural change collided with the reality of fragmented sovereignty. Ambition outpaced cohesion, and the political cost of integration rose faster than capacity could grow.
