We are binding ourselves to it because without sovereign execution capacity, no tribunal, no dashboard, no fund will matter. This is not a long-term industrial strategy — it is an emergency generator for political agency.

## In practice
We are acting on the edge of institutional failure. The Tribunal exists on paper, but its investigators cannot access the systems they are meant to audit — not because of resistance, but because the hardware running them is locked down by foreign vendors enforcing U.S. policy. The Public Trust Corps has a dashboard, but it runs on cloud infrastructure that could be throttled tomorrow. We are not just weak — we are *unplugged*.

So this turn, we are pivoting to survival. We are invoking Article 114 TFEU and the Union’s emergency competence in security of supply to fast-track integration of the Nordic-Baltic Sovereign AI Refuge into the EuroHPC JU. We are redirecting €8bn from unused cohesion funds and leveraging EIB emergency lending to expand their LUMI-class systems with secure enclaves for AI governance workloads — identity verification, benefit processing, incident forensics. This will not match frontier capability, but it will run a model at 50.0 safely and without asking permission.

We are negotiating with Finland and Sweden to host the control plane under dual civilian-military oversight, with full transparency to the European Parliament and red lines enforced by air-gapped audit logs. Germany and the Netherlands are hesitant, fearing escalation with Washington, but we are offering them co-stewardship of the verification framework and first access to the stack for their own critical systems. This is not about catching up to the frontier — it is about ensuring that, when the next AI-driven denial of benefits happens, we have *one* system we can actually investigate, on *our* soil, with *our* people. Without that, we have no legitimacy left.
