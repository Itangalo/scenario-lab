### The Brink of Sovereignty

The European Union launched a high-stakes counter-coercion strategy, invoking the Anti-Coercion Instrument to challenge U.S. extraterritorial control over ASML’s lithography equipment. In a coordinated move, the Commission conditioned access to European rare earth refining and quantum research partnerships on guaranteed servicing rights and baseline chip access for EU sovereign systems. The message was clear: if the Union could not maintain its seized AI infrastructure, it would leverage its downstream advantages to force parity.

But the response from Washington was cold. U.S. officials dismissed the move as symbolic, noting that alternative rare earth sources, while less efficient, were already being scaled. Quantum talks were suspended indefinitely, and retaliatory measures loomed over European tech firms operating in the U.S. The leverage asymmetry remained stark — the EU’s rare earth hold was real, but narrow, and the cost of escalation threatened to fracture already fragile alliances.
