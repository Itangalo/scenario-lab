- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore political agency and public trust through demonstrable control over AI incidents, transparent accountability, and sovereign response capacity — but this turn, survival comes before transformation  
- Grounds: The commitment’s original intent — to rebuild trust through visible, accountable action — has been overtaken by institutional collapse. The emergency seizure of EuroHPC systems has not restored credibility; it has deepened legal fragmentation and exposed the Union’s inability to enforce even its own emergency decisions. The AI Incident Dashboard remains offline, the Accountability Tribunal cannot operate without access, and public sentiment is frozen at rock bottom. We are no longer transforming governance or even stabilizing it — we are attempting to preserve a minimal technical foothold. The reversal costs us legitimacy: the world sees a Union that acts unilaterally not from strength, but from desperation.

- modify `emergency_sovereignty` (commitment): We will secure at least one operational AI stack — compute, model, and control — under full EU jurisdiction and physical control, capable of running critical sovereign functions without external permission  
- Grounds: The commitment stands, but its meaning has narrowed. What began as a strategic goal has become a tactical emergency: we are no longer building a stack for governance, but seizing infrastructure to prevent total blackout. The systems at LUMI, Leonardo, and MareNostrum are under partial control, but legal challenges from Germany and the Netherlands threaten to invalidate the action, and the model running is minimal — useful only for administrative triage. The reversal costs us cohesion: the Union is now acting as a coalition of the willing, not a unified body, and the Court may later strike down the entire measure.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (position): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late  
- Grounds: The position remains valid in spirit, but its application is now conditional. We still reject paralysis by analysis — but we can no longer act without *some* enforcement vector. The Trust Corps is defunct, the Tribunal is blind, and the Sovereign Stack is contested. Acting without means is not courage — it is performance. We will continue to move before full clarity, but only where physical control, legal authority, or public mandate still exist. Until those are restored, this remains a position, not a commitment.

## In practice  
We are out of illusions. The emergency seizure of LUMI, Leonardo, and MareNostrum gave us one auditable system — but it runs on hardware we cannot repair, with chips we cannot replace. Washington has turned our one chokepoint against us, cutting not just frontier tools but the very machines that sustain ordinary compute. We cannot build sovereignty on borrowed foundations. So this turn, we shift from internal triage to external confrontation.  

We invoke the Anti-Coercion Instrument in Council formation, formally declaring that the extraterritorial enforcement of US export controls on ASML — now extended to equipment critical for non-Chinese EU industry — constitutes economic coercion. We do not seek rupture, but we will not accept unilateral disarmament. In parallel, we condition access to European rare earth refining capacity — currently essential for US defence and AI hardware — on a renegotiated trilateral agreement that guarantees baseline access for EU sovereign systems. We also freeze ongoing negotiations on quantum research collaboration with the US until servicing rights for existing EU lithography tools are restored.  

This is not a trade war — it is a bid for parity. We are offering a deal: mutual restraint on extraterritorial leverage, in exchange for continued cooperation on AI safety and supply chain resilience. But if that fails, we will weaponize our own downstream bottlenecks. The Commission is preparing implementing acts to restrict exports of processed rare earths to the US defence sector by 40% starting next quarter, unless a framework is agreed. We are coordinating with France and Poland on domestic stockpiling, and with Finland on alternative processing routes. The cost will be high — retaliation is likely — but without it, our sovereignty is a fiction. We have one stack. Now we must fight for the means to keep it alive.
