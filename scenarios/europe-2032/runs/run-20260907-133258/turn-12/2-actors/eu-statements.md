# Statements: The European Union (turn 12)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore political agency and public trust through demonstrable control over AI incidents, transparent accountability, and sovereign response capacity — but this turn, survival comes before transformation
- `emergency_sovereignty` (commitment): We will secure at least one operational AI stack — compute, model, and control — under full EU jurisdiction and physical control, capable of running critical sovereign functions without external permission

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Restore political agency and public trust through demonstrable control over AI incidents, transparent accountability, and sovereign response capacity — but this turn, survival comes before transformation
  - Grounds: The commitment remains valid in intent, but its execution has failed. The Emergency Sovereign AI Stack remains unprovisioned due to legal blockage and member-state hesitation. The Public Trust Corps dashboard failed during the last incident, and the Accountability Tribunal cannot compel evidence because the systems it needs to audit are inaccessible. Political capital is at 0.0, public sentiment at 5.0, and the Union is effectively powerless to enforce its own laws. We are not transforming governance — we are trying to prevent total institutional blackout. The reversal costs us legitimacy: the world sees a Union that promised control but cannot even run its own systems.
  - Reason: modified text is identical to the current text
- **modify** `emergency_sovereignty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will secure at least one operational AI stack — compute, model, and control — under full EU jurisdiction and physical control, capable of running critical sovereign functions without external permission
  - Grounds: The original commitment assumed a path through legal integration and funding redirection. That path is now blocked. We are still bound to the goal, but we must now pursue it through alternative means: emergency commandeering of existing EuroHPC infrastructure, unilateral action by willing member states, and potential bypass of contested legal frameworks. The commitment stands, but its method has shifted from coordinated expansion to crisis improvisation. The reversal costs us cohesion: Germany and the Netherlands may formally disassociate, and the Court may later rule such action ultra vires.
  - Reason: modified text is identical to the current text
- **reclassify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-relevance
  - Tier: position
  - Trigger: **capability_plateau_evidence**, **backlash_physical**, and **taiwan_tension_rise**
  - Grounds: We can no longer afford to act without means. The commitment to act under uncertainty required that we had *some* enforcement vector — now we do not. The Trust Corps cannot operate its dashboard, the Tribunal cannot compel access, and the Sovereign Stack is stillborn. Acting without traceable accountability or enforcement is no longer policy — it is theatre. We remain willing to commit before clarity, but only where we still have the physical and legal means to follow through. Until those means exist, this is a position, not a binding pledge.
  - Evidence quoted: **capability_plateau_evidence:** A major release underdelivers against its own briefing, and – the stronger signal – the price of top-tier capability falls sharply rather than staying flat, which is what happens when the frontier stops moving and last year's ceiling becomes this year's commodity. It is disputed at once, and the dispute is not resolvable from outside the laboratories.

**backlash_physical:** Protest against AI infrastructure moves from petitions and hearings to direct action: occupations at data centre sites, sabotage of grid connections, and in the harder cases injury. Policing it costs the Union more than the damage does.

**taiwan_tension_rise:** Extended military exercises, shipping insurance premiums rising, a diplomatic expulsion. Nothing that has not happened before, at a scale that is slightly harder to dismiss. This is a precursor: it opens the Taiwan gate for the next 3 turns.
  - Reason: The developments are real but do not alter the EU's reasons for acting under uncertainty; they neither change the cost of keeping the commitment nor its strategic rationale.
