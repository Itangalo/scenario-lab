## In practice
We are out of time and out of capital. The Emergency Sovereign AI Stack is stalled at the Court, the Trust Corps has collapsed with its dashboard, and the Tribunal cannot investigate what it cannot access. So this turn, we act unilaterally. Invoking Article 4 of the Treaty on European Union — solidarity in the face of systemic threat — we direct the European Defence Agency, in coordination with Finland, Italy, and Spain, to activate emergency control protocols over LUMI, Leonardo, and MareNostrum. These systems will be reconfigured to run a minimal sovereign stack: a model at capability 50.0, trained on public administration tasks, air-gapped from external cloud services, and auditable by EU personnel only.

We are not waiting for the Court. We are not waiting for Germany. We are using existing crisis powers to take physical control of infrastructure already funded by the Union. The systems will be hardened against remote access, with firmware-level locks on external updates. We are offering co-stewardship to willing member states, but we are proceeding with or without them. This is not a long-term solution — it is a triage measure to ensure that when the next AI-driven denial of benefits occurs, we have one system we can actually investigate, on our soil, with our people.

The cost will be high: legal challenges, accusations of overreach, potential fragmentation. But the alternative — total passivity — is the end of the Union as a political actor. We are not saving the future this turn. We are trying to keep the lights on.
