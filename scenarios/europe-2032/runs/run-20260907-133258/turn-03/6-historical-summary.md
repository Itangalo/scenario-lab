The EU accelerates the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme through emergency powers, designating national champions and overriding grid objections, but faces mounting legal challenges from Germany and ENTSO-E over energy planning and lacks binding infrastructure commitments. A €50bn EIB funding package is politically endorsed but frozen pending audit review. Amid growing public backlash over job losses and an AI-driven cyberattack exploiting open-weight models, the EU launches an Emergency AI Accountability and Redress Mechanism, though key states like Italy and Hungary block audits, limiting its reach. A breakthrough in model interpretability enables pre-deployment certification, improving safety oversight, but rapid advances in autonomous code generation push overall AI capability up by 2.0, outpacing control measures. Trust erodes further as systemic vulnerabilities persist, political capital declines sharply, and perceptions of elite overreach undermine legitimacy, deepening the sovereignty gap despite partial technical progress.
