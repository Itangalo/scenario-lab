We cannot afford to lose solidarity in the name of enforcing unity. Our commitment must evolve: compliance is not submission, and sovereignty must be built with, not over, the member states.

* ``- modify `no_time_for_perfection` (commitment): We will accept imperfect tools, incomplete readiness, and partial compliance — but only if they serve a functioning whole. We will prioritize systems that work over systems that are pure. But we now add: we will not accept fragility disguised as function. A system that works today but collapses under pressure is not a system — it is a trap. Our tolerance is for imperfection, not for illusion.``

We acted to repair reality, but we must not mistake activity for resilience. Unity is not a precondition; it is the outcome we build.``  
- Grounds: The emergent_eu_aid_strike and the prior resistance to the Continuity Assurance Framework show that top-down enforcement fails when legitimacy is thin. We are staking ourselves on a new principle: sovereignty in AI cannot be imposed — it must be co-constructed. This is not retreat; it is strategy.

## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from emergency response to sovereign foundation. The cyber_major_incident and the emergent_eu_aid_strike have made one thing clear: we cannot command continuity we do not control. The Continuity Assurance Framework is now law, but its nodes depend on foreign models and foreign access — and when those fail, the EU has no fallback. We are launching the Sovereign AI Core Programme under Article 122 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act’s systemic provider provisions, mobilizing €4.2bn from the Crisis Solidarity Mechanism and the Digital Europe Programme.

The European AI Assurance Directorate will lead, in partnership with national agencies, to deploy hardened, EU-operated AI stacks on domestic compute clusters in five member states. We are using open-weight models at 52.0 capability — not frontier, but sufficient for critical functions — and applying rigorous, auditable hardening: weight signing, runtime monitoring, and split-control execution. Each node will be co-managed by a joint EU-national cell, with full cost coverage offered to states that integrate. In return, we demand full transparency and interoperability.

We know the capability plateau evidence is disputed. But if it holds, this is our moment: the frontier slows, and we can close the gap. If it doesn’t, we still gain — because this time, when the next jump comes, we will not be starting from zero. We are not waiting for permission. The lights will stay on — and this time, they will be powered by the Union.
