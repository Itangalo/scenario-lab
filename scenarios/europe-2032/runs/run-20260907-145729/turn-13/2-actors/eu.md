## Statement changes
* - modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement will now be conditional on the operational readiness of the Sovereign AI Core Programme, and we will not cut off a member state from critical capabilities unless we can offer a viable, secure alternative within 90 days. However, in light of the emergency activation of sovereign stacks at 52.0 capability across five member states, we now add: where a member state seeks external access *because* the Union’s sovereign stack is not yet available to it, the Union bears part of the responsibility. We will accelerate deployment to all member states on an equitable, needs-based schedule, and any state left without access after 120 days from the cutoff will not be penalised for seeking alternatives — but will be required to share full technical and operational data with the EU to prevent fragmentation of assurance standards.  
- Grounds: The emergency stacks have proven technically viable, but their uneven rollout has created a two-tier Union: those who were ready, and those left behind. The principle of collective security cannot tolerate that gap. If we demand loyalty to the sovereign framework, we must deliver the framework — and deliver it fast. Punishing states for filling the void we left is not enforcement. It is abandonment.

* - modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but now we add: we will also institutionalise retrospective validation. Every action taken under uncertainty will be reviewed within six months by an independent panel with access to full operational data, and findings will be published to refine doctrine, correct errors, and restore trust where overreach occurred.  
- Grounds: The bypassing of national governments, while necessary, has deepened political fractures. Acting under uncertainty cannot become a licence for unilateralism. We must balance speed with accountability — not after years, but within one turn. The panel will not block action, but it will ensure that acting early does not mean learning late.

## In practice
This turn, we are moving from emergency response to sovereign foundation. The lights stayed on — barely — because five member states had pre-vetted stacks and trusted command channels. But three others did not. That is not resilience. It is luck. We are now launching the Sovereign Stack Acceleration Programme under the Digital Markets Act’s interoperability powers and the Crisis Solidarity Mechanism, directing the European AI Assurance Directorate to standardise, certify, and deploy hardened open-weight AI systems (52.0+) across all member states within 120 days.

We are mobilising €4.2 billion from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility to fund compute upgrades, secure model distribution, and fast-track legal agreements with national operators. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking will coordinate deployment, with binding delivery milestones set for turns 14 and 15. Where national governments resist, we are offering full cost coverage, shared command protocols, and liability protection — but we are also activating bilateral agreements with regional health authorities and grid operators who have already signed up. No member state will be left without sovereign stack access by turn 15 — and no state will be punished for seeking alternatives until it has been offered one.

At the same time, we are establishing the Retrospective Validation Panel under Article 16 of the AI Act, with mandate to review all emergency actions taken under Article 122 TFEU, including command cell overreach and bypassed national authorities. Its findings will inform the next phase of the Sovereign AI Core Programme — not as a post-mortem, but as a course correction. We acted under uncertainty. Now we learn under scrutiny.
