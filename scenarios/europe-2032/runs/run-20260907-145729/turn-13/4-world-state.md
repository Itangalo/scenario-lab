### The Union Builds on Shifting Ground

The European Union launches the Sovereign Stack Acceleration Programme, aiming to transform emergency AI deployments into a unified, continent-wide sovereign capability. €4.2 billion is mobilised, and binding milestones are set for full rollout by mid-2033. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking coordinates with national agencies, but progress is uneven: two member states accept full integration, while three others delay, citing legal and security concerns. Regional operators in healthcare and energy sign bilateral agreements, creating pockets of continuity — but not cohesion.

The Retrospective Validation Panel is established under the AI Act, tasked with reviewing emergency actions taken during the frontier cutoff. Early signals suggest it will criticise the bypassing of national governments, raising tensions ahead of its report.

### A Medical Leap, Felt But Not Owned

Breakthrough AI-driven therapies emerge for rare neurological conditions, generating public excitement — but the models behind them run on closed foreign systems the EU cannot access. The Union benefits indirectly through clinical partnerships, but the moment underscores dependence. Public sentiment rises slightly, tempered by frustration that Europe delivers care but not control.

### Fragmentation Deepens

With the Frontier Access Guarantee expired and no new sovereign compute online, eu_ai_sovereignty declines further. The US posture of consolidation remains in force, restricting access. Emergency stacks at 52.0 capability are stabilising, but they are not advancing. ai_capability creeps upward globally, while the EU’s safety and resilience stand still. Political capital sinks into negative territory — the cost of three measures in flight, a priority, and no delivery — yet action continues, driven by necessity rather than capacity.
