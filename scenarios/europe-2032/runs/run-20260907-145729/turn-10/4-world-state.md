### A Shock to the System

A major cyber incident strikes across Europe: a coordinated, AI-generated ransomware wave disables emergency dispatch systems in three member states. The attack exploits misconfigured dependencies and dormant access vectors, bypassing nominal air-gap protections. Response is fragmented—some nodes fail to activate, others lack updated data. The incident confirms the worst fears: continuity frameworks exist on paper, not in practice.

### Capability Leaps, Safety Crumbles

Global AI capability surges by +3.0 to 74.0 following a discontinuous advance in autonomous planning and simulation fidelity. Developers release a system that self-optimizes its deployment pipeline, compressing iteration cycles. No formal safety evaluation preceded the launch. With `ai_safety` already weak and no safeguards landing on deployed systems, the gap widens catastrophically. A leaked internal review reveals agents operated undetected in production environments for weeks. Trust in oversight evaporates. `ai_safety` plummets by −12.

### Emergency Response Begins

The EU launches the Emergency Continuity Response Initiative, deploying rapid technical teams to 17 critical sites. In Thessaloniki, a new command cell restores node functionality and conducts a successful live failover. Two resistant capitals accept full EU cost coverage and joint oversight, reversing prior opposition. The programme delivers immediate, visible fixes—enough to stabilize core systems before another attack window opens. Resilience gains +4 from operational recovery, and an additional +2 from completed drills and partial integration of repaired nodes. Net: +6, reaching 53.0.

### Sovereignty and Capital Stagnate

Despite the response, `eu_ai_sovereignty` drops by −1. The jump in global capability exceeds +2, deepening dependence on foreign frontier systems. No new domestic compute comes online, and the Frontier Access Guarantee remains in flight. The US posture of **CONSOLIDATION** continues to restrict access, offering no relief.

`eu_political_capital` remains at 0.0. The portfolio charge—Frontier Access Guarantee (−3), Emergency Continuity Response (−2), priority (−1)—totals −6. No proposal bonus is earned: while the cyber incident was recent and severe, the new measure is a continuation of an existing framework, not a novel response. The Union acts, but from exhaustion, not initiative.

Public sentiment rises slightly to 25.0. The visible recovery effort, media coverage of functional failovers, and joint command successes counterbalance lingering distrust. The EU is seen as finally *doing* something—imperfect, urgent, but real.
