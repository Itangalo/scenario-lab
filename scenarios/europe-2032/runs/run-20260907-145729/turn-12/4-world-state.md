### The Lights Stay On — Just Barely

The Union acts under denial. With frontier access severed without explanation, hospitals lose diagnostic support, emergency dispatch systems falter, and grid operators report instability. The EU invokes Article 122 TFEU, launching emergency deployment of hardened open-weight AI stacks across five member states. Pre-vetted models at 52.0 capability activate on domestic compute, restoring basic functions in health, energy, and crisis coordination within 72 hours — just in time to prevent cascading failures.

Joint command cells, long stalled by political resistance, finally coordinate action — but only by bypassing national capitals. Regional health authorities in three states accept direct EU deployment, while two others delay, citing sovereignty concerns. The lights stay on, but the cost is political fragmentation.

### A Guarantee Broken

The Frontier Access Guarantee, meant to secure reliable access to frontier models, collapses under real pressure. No audit, no appeal, no recourse: the cutoff exposes total dependence. The EU’s credibility in strategic autonomy takes a direct hit. The Sovereign AI Core Programme, already delayed, is now reframed as damage control.

### Unity Under Strain

The emergency action draws fierce criticism. Two member states condemn the bypassing of national governments as institutional overreach. Talks on the Core Programme freeze entirely. Public sentiment holds steady — relief at restored services balances anger at foreign dependence and internal disunity. Resilience does not improve: the emergency stacks are functional, but not robust. Safety remains critically low — no time for assurance, only activation.
