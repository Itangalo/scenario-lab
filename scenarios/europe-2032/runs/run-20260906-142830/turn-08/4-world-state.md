### The Open Frontier, the Broken Shield

The closed AI frontier advanced steadily, reaching 64.5 as labs refined multi-day reasoning and self-correction in complex domains. A significant development occurred with the release of a frontier-level model under permissive terms. The model spread rapidly, enabling widespread replication and adaptation. Openweight capability increased to 52.5—still behind the frontier but closing faster than expected. This diffusion, not a sudden leap, marked a shift in the balance: open models were no longer trailing by years but months. The 2.0-point gain reflects sustained replication momentum and partial absorption of the new release, consistent with prior accumulation trends.

The EU completed its Sovereign Cyber Shield across all Tier 1 operators, enforcing integration despite legal resistance. Real-time monitoring now runs in energy, transport, and health command centres. However, its assumptions—trusted models, auditable reasoning—were already outdated. The new open-weight threat operates outside its design.

In response, the EU drafted a large category 6 measure: mandatory behavioural containment for open-weight models in critical functions. Formally verified sandboxes, adversarial probing, and kill switches would become mandatory. The proposal was considered for prioritization, but the Runtime Integrity Enforcement measure remains ongoing and not yet closed. As only one measure may be active at a time under the EU’s current governance constraints, the new containment mandate cannot be introduced this turn. No formal proposal has been adopted, and no funding has been disbursed. The initiative remains in internal consultation, with debate intensifying over sequencing and feasibility.

Sovereignty declined to 12.0. The InvestAI Gigafactories delivered hardware, but without energy autonomy or auditability, their strategic value was limited. Supply-chain pressure from Washington led ASML to restrict mature-node exports, reducing EU access to essential fabrication tools. This leverage, exercised through extraterritorial influence, directly undermined the bloc’s ability to maintain and scale sovereign infrastructure. However, no physical sabotage or destruction of existing compute occurred, and thus the erosion remains within the bounds of reversible access loss. The notepad records a fall from 14.0 to 12.0 due to cumulative access degradation (−2.0), reflecting substitution efforts partially offsetting the restrictions.

Political capital fell to 7.0. The completion of the Sovereign Cyber Shield yielded +4, and the proposal bonus for Runtime Integrity Enforcement was +4. However, the portfolio charge—Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative −3, Runtime Integrity Enforcement −3, and priority −1—totalled −7. With no legitimacy lends, the net change was −3, resulting in a decline from 10.0 to 7.0. The narrative of containment lacks credibility, and no visible benefit has reached citizens. Public sentiment remained frozen at 14.0.
