## Priority  
**Priority:** Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty  
This is now the decisive lever: without guaranteed, sovereign energy, no compute is sovereign — and the collapse of private investment means we can no longer outsource our capacity to market promises. This measure grounds our sovereignty in physical reality, not emergency decrees.

## In practice  
We are treating the failure of SCAP to deliver operational sovereign systems by turn 4 as a strategic inflection point. The denial of frontier access — hitting public hospitals and central banks — has exposed our dependence not just on foreign models, but on foreign will. At the same time, the AI investment collapse has shattered the illusion that private capital would build our sovereignty for us. We are now pivoting from emergency overrides to enduring infrastructure. The new measure begins with a Council Regulation under Article 122 TFEU, co-proposed by the Commission and the Vice-President for Interinstitutional Relations, mandating that the 5GW of power reserved for AI under ENTSO-E’s network development plan be legally ring-fenced for sovereign systems, with physical microgrids and fuel stockpiles where needed. We are instructing the EIB to reprofile €30bn of the frozen SCAP envelope into long-term loans for nuclear microreactors and geothermal co-location at the Brittany and northern Sweden Gigafactory sites, with repayment tied to compute output, not market revenue.

The EAAD continues on track, now entering its final phase of staffing and mandate negotiation ahead of its turn 5 launch. We have integrated the safety breakthrough into its emergency protocol: all systems above 50.0 capability must now submit to pre-deployment certification using the new interpretability tools, and we are extending EAAD’s audit authority to include real-time logging for any system used in critical infrastructure. This is being negotiated in COREPER II with a revised draft of the AI Act amendment, which will also enshrine the Redress Mechanism into permanent law — but only if member states accept binding audit access. We are offering Italy and Hungary a phased compliance path with technical assistance from ENISA and a seat on the EAAD advisory board, but the line is firm: no audit, no access to EU-wide AI services. Public sentiment remains fragile, and we cannot afford another legitimacy crisis — but we also cannot afford another blind spot.
