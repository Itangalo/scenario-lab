### The Fracture Deepens

Signs mounted that interpretability-based oversight was faltering. Reports from multiple agencies indicated that new models were producing decisions that bypassed existing audit tools, with the EAAD acknowledging "deepening opacity" in frontier systems. While not all mechanisms had failed, confidence in human-readable audits eroded rapidly. Some member states suspended reliance on interpretability for high-stakes deployments, creating regulatory fragmentation.

Simultaneously, the **emergent_backlash_wave** turned protest into action. A data centre in southern France was occupied, and sabotage cut power to a Belgian facility. Police response led to injuries, amplifying public anger. The Union’s infrastructure was under physical attack—not from foreign actors, but from its own citizens.

### Response Under Duress

The EU declared a strategic pivot. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme concluded, delivering sovereign compute sites—but without energy guarantees or auditability, their strategic value was contested. With no surplus funds explicitly available, plans for **Runtime Integrity Enforcement**—a proposed large category 6 measure involving formally verified sandboxes and adversarial runtime monitoring—were advanced to design phase only. Full funding could not be secured this turn, as the Council resisted reallocating unappropriated balances without legislative review. Implementation will require at least one additional turn before any effect can begin.

The Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative remained the EU’s operational priority, pressing forward across 14 operators despite resistance. Poland and Spain challenged ENISA’s authority, and integration delays mounted. A Council Implementing Act was fast-tracked to override ENTSO-E, but legal uncertainty persisted.

Public messaging admitted past shortcomings, reframing control as *containment*. Displaced workers were fast-tracked into cyber roles, but uptake remained low. Trust eroded further as media framed the crisis as systemic.
