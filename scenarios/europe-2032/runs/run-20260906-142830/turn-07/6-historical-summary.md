A deepening opacity in frontier AI systems undermined confidence in interpretability-based oversight, leading to regulatory fragmentation as some member states suspended reliance on audits for high-risk deployments. The EU faced internal unrest as the emergent backlash wave escalated into physical attacks on infrastructure, including the occupation of a data centre in France and sabotage in Belgium, marking a shift from protest to direct action. In response, the EU pivoted strategically, advancing Runtime Integrity Enforcement to design phase—a category 6 measure involving verified sandboxes and runtime monitoring—but failed to secure full funding due to Council resistance, delaying implementation. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme delivered sovereign compute capacity, though its value was limited by lack of energy guarantees and auditability. The Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative continued across 14 operators despite legal and political challenges, including pushback from Poland and Spain on ENISA’s authority and ongoing grid allocation disputes with ENTSO-E, partially addressed by a fast-tracked Council Implementing Act. Public trust deteriorated further amid admissions of systemic failure, low uptake in worker retraining, and media narratives framing the crisis as structural, exacerbating social and institutional strain.
