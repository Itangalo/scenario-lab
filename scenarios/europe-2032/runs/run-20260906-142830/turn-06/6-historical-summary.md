A major AI-powered cyberattack on energy and healthcare systems exposed critical vulnerabilities, prompting the EU to launch the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative with AI-audited defences and zero-trust protocols; however, rollout was hampered by member state resistance and coordination delays. ENISA led implementation, but legal and integration challenges slowed progress. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme concluded with physical deployment lagging due to ongoing legal challenges from Germany and Austria, and ENTSO-E’s refusal to enforce grid allocations, undermining energy autonomy despite Commission action under Article 194 TFEU. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) certified its first models, but real-time logging mandates failed to pass, weakening enforcement and public trust amid growing protests over job displacement. A retraining initiative using SCAP infrastructure saw limited uptake. AI capability rose to 61.5, driven by autonomous code generation, while open-weight models reached 49.0, yet safety measures did not keep pace, widening the capability-safety gap.
