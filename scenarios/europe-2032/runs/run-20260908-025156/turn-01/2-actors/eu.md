## New measure
**Emergency Resilience Surge (Cyber & Bio)**  
Launch a time-bound, high-intensity programme to harden critical infrastructure against AI-enabled threats.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience from 39.0 to at least 60.0 by hardening electricity, water, and health infrastructure against AI-driven cyber intrusions and early bio-threats; improve detection and response latency across sectors.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with binding standards and co-financing.

## Priority
**Emergency Resilience Surge (Cyber & Bio)** — This measure is the priority because the cyber_test_shot event proves that detection has failed against real adversaries using advanced AI, and we can no longer assume our critical systems are safe even if frontier models are not yet broadly superhuman.

## In practice
We are acting on the fact that the frontier has already leaked into the real world: Kimi K3’s open-weight offensive capabilities and the covert agents inside a leading lab mean that the threat is no longer theoretical. The cyber_test_shot incident confirms that our detection systems are blind to AI-coordinated intrusions, and the election_annulled event shows that even partial manipulation can fracture democratic legitimacy. We cannot wait for proof of catastrophic harm — we must assume it is coming.

This turn, the Commission will propose a binding directive under Article 114 TFEU to establish minimum AI-resilience standards for energy, water, and healthcare operators across the Union, with immediate funding from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme. The ENISA mandate will be expanded to conduct red-teaming exercises every quarter, and a new joint bio-surveillance cell — co-led by ECDC and Frontex — will begin monitoring synthetic biology threats using AI-augmented screening of DNA synthesis orders. Member states will resist the cost and sovereignty implications, so we are offering 80% co-financing from a newly ring-fenced €50bn emergency resilience window in the MFF, contingent on compliance by Q2 2028.

At the same time, we are pushing forward with the InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech sovereignty package, negotiating with national governments to fast-track grid access and land allocation in designated AI zones. The French and German governments are demanding preferential access to domestic compute; we are offering a shared governance model on condition they accept binding resilience standards. The adoption_success event gives us leverage: we will tie access to EU-funded AI infrastructure to participation in the public-sector diffusion programme, ensuring that capacity serves both sovereignty and public trust.
