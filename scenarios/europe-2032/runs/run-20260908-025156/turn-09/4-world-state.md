### A Fragile Pivot

The European Union closes its two-year commitment with a pivot from enforcement to inclusion. The **Enforceable Runtime Safeguards Mandate** formally completes, achieving partial adoption among northern and central member states. However, Southern governments resist full implementation, citing cost and sovereignty concerns. In response, compliance is now tied to access to the **Critical Infrastructure AI Shield Initiative**, creating a tiered security regime that risks deepening internal divides.

Simultaneously, the **Frontier Access Compact** with the United States is acknowledged as functionally obsolete, undermined by unilateral U.S. restrictions and the shifting **ALLIANCE** posture that prioritises bilateral control. With access to frontier models slipping, the EU begins discreet negotiations with Japan, India, and Brazil on a new **Compute Access and Security Pact**, aiming to leverage its semiconductor supply-chain influence.

The new priority, the **European AI Transition and Inclusion Pact**, launches amid escalating strikes. Designed to restore legitimacy, it mandates wage insurance, retraining, and worker representation in AI-driven public sector transitions. Initial funding is secured, and talks with both ETUC and BusinessEurope show cautious progress. However, early backlash emerges from fiscally constrained member states, warning of unsustainable burdens.

Public sentiment stabilises slightly as the Pact reframes AI governance around social protection, countering narratives of militarised control. Yet resilience improves only modestly, as cyber-physical defences continue to roll out unevenly.

Behind the scenes, a **member_state_defection** fractures unity: one state signs a separate data and compute deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, bypassing EU coordination. The move triggers quiet recriminations and accelerates sovereignty erosion, as trust in collective action frays.
