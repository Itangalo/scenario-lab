## New measure  
**Enforceable Sovereignty Framework**  
Establish binding pre-approval requirements for member state agreements with foreign AI infrastructure providers, backed by suspension of cohesion and digital transition funding for non-compliance.

`Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by preventing further member_state_defection; strengthen eu_political_capital by demonstrating the Union can enforce its own rules; stabilise public_sentiment by showing coherence in the face of external pressure.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with enforcement through the European Semester and conditional access to EU funds.

## Priority  
**Enforceable Sovereignty Framework** — This measure is the priority because the Hungary precedent has opened a fissure in our strategic autonomy that no amount of compute or retraining can close: if member states can unilaterally bargain away our collective leverage, the Union cannot act as a sovereign entity, and our two-year commitment becomes hollow.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that sovereignty is not just about infrastructure or jobs — it is about the Union’s authority to speak and bind as one. The Hungary deal was not just a policy divergence; it was a breach of the compact that enables collective action. This turn, we invoke Article 122 TFEU and the conditionality mechanism under Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2020/2092 to establish a binding pre-approval regime for any member state negotiation with foreign hyperscalers on data, compute, or AI infrastructure. The Commission will issue implementing acts by Q1 2028, and the Council must adopt them under the Economic and Financial Affairs configuration, where we have secured early backing from France and the Nordics. We are offering Germany and Poland a carve-out: they may pursue bilateral technical partnerships, but only if they submit them for prior review and accept joint negotiation terms that protect EU-wide access and data governance. Non-compliance will trigger automatic suspension of digital transition and cohesion funds, with reinstatement tied to corrective action plans.

Simultaneously, we are accelerating the Emergency Resilience Surge by integrating the cyber_defence_breakthrough into the draft directive: automated patching and swarm-detection tooling will be mandated for all critical operators by turn 5, with ENISA conducting live-fire exercises in Q3. The Labour Transition Shield is being operationalised through the ESF+, with €30bn unlocked for wage insurance in legal, financial, and public administration sectors — but access is now tied to participation in the Enforceable Sovereignty Framework, creating a leverage point. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain bottlenecked by grid capacity, so we are fast-tracking the TEN-E revision through the Energy Council, linking approval to national compliance with the sovereignty framework. The emergent_global_ai_governance_initiative offers a diplomatic opening: we will engage as an observer, but only to steer it toward mutual recognition of safety evaluations — not to outsource our own authority. Sovereignty must be enforced, not negotiated away.
