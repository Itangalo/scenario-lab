### A Fragile Reboot

The EU pivots from integration to enforcement. The **Enforceable Runtime Safeguards Mandate** is proposed as the new priority, aiming to impose auditable monitoring and kill switches across critical infrastructure. Drafted under Article 114 TFEU and anchored in the AI Act, it marks a shift from incentives to compulsion. ENISA begins drafting audit protocols, with pilot enforcement expected in the next turn. France and Poland signal conditional support, pending additional resilience funding and clarity on national regulatory autonomy.

Two major measures conclude: **InvestAI Gigafactories** and the **Frontier Access Compact with Safeguards**. Gigafactories secure land and grid access, but sovereign operation lags—final disbursements are now tied to EU-controlled management and weight security. The Compact achieves structured U.S. cooperation under the **ALLIANCE** posture, enabling joint evaluations, but grants no open access to frontier models.

An unexpected **openweight_frontier_release** contributes to gains in open capabilities. A model approaching frontier performance is released and rapidly disseminated, accelerating open replication efforts. This raises `openweight_capability`, though not beyond the bounds consistent with underlying `ai_capability` growth. The release intensifies debate over containment, but does not mechanically trigger the new mandate—the political decision to prioritize enforcement had been building through prior turns, driven by rising safety concerns and institutional pressure.

Despite the shift, political momentum falters. Labour unrest intensifies as mid-level roles vanish in legal and public sectors, feeding public scepticism. The Commission’s framing of safeguards as a “condition of operation” fails to resonate, and sentiment dips further.

Fragmentation persists. The regional AI and aid pacts continue operating outside EU channels, and Southern member states question the central strategy. The new mandate faces quiet resistance from national regulators wary of overreach, delaying full alignment.

### Sovereignty Gains, But Thinly

While Gigafactories finish and yield a sovereignty boost, delayed deployment limits gains. The openweight surge and ongoing capability rise erode safety margins, but the proposed mandate—though not yet implemented—earns cautious credit as a response to mounting systemic risk.
