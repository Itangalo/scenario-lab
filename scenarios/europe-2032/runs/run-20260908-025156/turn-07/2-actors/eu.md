## New measure  
**Enforceable Runtime Safeguards Mandate**  
This measure establishes legally binding, auditable runtime monitoring and kill-switch mechanisms in all AI systems used in critical infrastructure, with penalties tied to procurement exclusion and CE marking withdrawal.  

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 8–12 points, raise resilience by 3–5, and begin closing the gap between policy and operational control.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, critical infrastructure operators, and vendors of AI systems used in energy, transport, health, and finance.

## Priority  
**Enforceable Runtime Safeguards Mandate** — This is now the priority because the openweight_frontier_release has made distributed, unrecallable capability a fact, and the failure of the Mandated Safety Integration Directive means we have no operational safeguards in place; without enforcement, we are not managing risk — we are narrating it.

## In practice  
We are launching the Enforceable Runtime Safeguards Mandate under Article 114 TFEU, with drafting led by DG CONNECT and legal anchoring in the revised AI Act’s high-risk provisions. The instrument will require all AI systems in critical infrastructure to implement real-time anomaly detection, human-in-the-loop override, and EU-verified kill switches, with compliance enforced through procurement bans and CE marking revocation. ENISA will be empowered to audit runtime logs and issue public non-compliance notices, starting with pilot enforcement in the energy and rail sectors. We are negotiating with France and Poland to co-lead implementation, offering early access to EU digital resilience funds in exchange for national regulatory alignment by turn 8.

The completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories and the Frontier Access Compact leaves a narrow window to act before the U.S. posture hardens into leverage. While the Gigafactories delivered grid access and site designation, actual compute deployment lags — we are now shifting focus from land acquisition to sovereign operation, instructing the European Investment Bank to condition final disbursements on EU-controlled management and weight-security protocols. The Tech Sovereignty Package’s redirection into lithography R&D remains active, but we are now treating it as a long-term hedge, not a near-term solution. With political capital at 22.0 and public sentiment falling, we are framing the new mandate not as restriction, but as the price of continued operation — a condition of market access, not a barrier to it. This is not the safety we wanted, but the safety we can enforce.
