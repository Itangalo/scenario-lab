## New measure  
**Critical Infrastructure AI Shield Initiative**  
This measure establishes a standing EU-level cyber-physical protection force for AI infrastructure, with authority to secure, monitor, and if necessary, physically isolate systems in critical sectors during incidents.  

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 8–12 points, reduce incident cascade risk, and signal state-level control over AI infrastructure to counter backlash and deter sabotage.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, critical infrastructure operators, and designated AI deployment sites.

## Priority  
**Critical Infrastructure AI Shield Initiative** — This is now the priority because **backlash_physical** has crossed a threshold: our infrastructure is under direct attack, and without visible, credible protection, no mandate or audit will restore legitimacy; we are losing not just control, but the perception of control, and that is fatal to both sovereignty and political capital.

## In practice  
We are launching the Critical Infrastructure AI Shield Initiative under the authority of the Cybersecurity Act and the NIS2 framework, with operational command split between ENISA and Frontex under a new Joint AI Infrastructure Protection Cell. This is not merely a technical upgrade — it is a political signal that the Union will defend its digital spine with the same resolve as its borders. The initiative will deploy rapid-response cyber-physical units to safeguard data centres, grid nodes, and transport AI hubs, with authority to enforce access controls, conduct live intrusion sweeps, and, in extremis, physically disconnect compromised systems. Funding will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with €12bn allocated over three turns.

We are also accelerating implementation of the Enforceable Runtime Safeguards Mandate in parallel, now tying compliance to access to Shield protection — a carrot of security, not just a stick of exclusion. France and Poland are being offered co-command roles in regional Shield cells in exchange for fast-tracking national adoption of the mandate. Meanwhile, the completion of the Gigafactories and the Frontier Access Compact leaves us with a narrow sovereignty window: we are now instructing the European Investment Bank to withhold final disbursements unless operators sign binding agreements on EU-controlled access and real-time monitoring. The U.S. has noted our move toward enforcement, but the **us_china_agreement** shows they will not wait. We are not part of that deal, but we can still shape the periphery — by being the only power that can credibly protect its stack, we may yet become indispensable to it.
