### A Shield in Response to Fire

The EU pivots hard toward physical enforcement. The **Critical Infrastructure AI Shield Initiative** is launched as the new priority, establishing rapid-response cyber-physical units under ENISA and Frontex to protect AI-critical nodes. The move responds directly to escalating sabotage at data centres and grid links across Germany and Spain—acts labelled as "defensive civil disobedience" by some protest groups, but as infrastructure warfare by EU security chiefs. The Shield is framed as a restoration of state authority, with €12bn committed from digital and recovery funds.

Implementation of the **Enforceable Runtime Safeguards Mandate** accelerates, now tied to Shield protection: compliance buys security, non-compliance risks isolation. France and Poland accept co-command roles in regional cells, easing national resistance. However, Southern states remain sceptical, warning of a "fortress Europe" approach that deepens North-South divides in both infrastructure and trust.

### The World Moves On

The **us_china_agreement** on frontier model security marginally slows global capability growth, but the effect is narrow and short-lived. Verification in law and engineering widens, enabling rapid AI deployment in contract analysis and clinical coding—domains once thought to require human judgment. This sharpens private-sector adoption and fuels further displacement, especially in legal and health administration.

The **openweight_frontier_release** from last turn continues to ripple: open models now support semi-autonomous research agents, pushing `openweight_capability` to 54.0. Meanwhile, `ai_safety` erodes further—no new safeguards have landed on deployed systems, and the gap between capability and control grows.

Public sentiment dips as strikes intensify and sabotage images dominate media. The Shield is seen by many as militarising AI governance, worsening perceptions. Labour unrest and physical backlash together dampen political capital, despite finishing two major measures.

Sovereignty rises slightly—Gigafactories and the Frontier Access Compact deliver tangible assets—but gains are offset by delayed deployment and external exclusion. The EU is not at the table in the U.S.-China deal, reinforcing dependency.
