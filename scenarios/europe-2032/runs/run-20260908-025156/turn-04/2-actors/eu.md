## New measure  
**Mandated Safety Integration Directive**  
Require all AI systems used in critical infrastructure to incorporate verified safety mechanisms, including runtime monitoring, kill switches, and third-party red-teaming, with enforcement through CE marking and ETSI standards.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 15–20 points by turn 6, reduce cyber_major_incident recurrence, and stabilise public_sentiment by demonstrating concrete action on control.  
`Applies to:` AI providers and operators in energy, transport, health, and digital infrastructure across the EU.

## Priority  
**Mandated Safety Integration Directive** — This measure is the priority because the cyber_major_incident has exposed that we are already in the era of autonomous attacks, and our oversight regime is functionally absent; without enforceable safety in deployed systems, no amount of sovereignty or compute will protect us.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that capability has outpaced control, and the Union’s credibility now hinges on its ability to protect, not just to govern. The **cyber_major_incident** — a model-generated ransomware sweep that crippled hospital scheduling and municipal services in Belgium and northern Italy — has shattered the illusion that safety can be deferred. Attribution remains unclear, but the tooling was AI-synthesised: polymorphic, adaptive, and evasive. The incident lasted 72 hours before containment, with patching overwhelmed by mutation speed. This is not a failure of vigilance — it is a failure of architecture. We are now in a world where **ai_capability** is at 58.0 and rising, yet **ai_safety** is at 28.0: we are flying faster with fewer instruments.

In response, we are launching the **Mandated Safety Integration Directive** under the AI Act’s conformity assessment framework, leveraging Article 23a and the New Legislative Framework. By turn 6, all AI systems in critical sectors must demonstrate integration of runtime anomaly detection, human-in-the-loop kill switches, and red-team validation from ENISA-accredited labs. We are tasking ETSI with fast-tracking technical standards by Q3, and the Commission will issue delegated acts to align CE marking with these requirements. Enforcement will be tied to market access and funding — no AI system without verified safety integration will be eligible for public procurement or EU infrastructure contracts.

Simultaneously, we are winding down the **Emergency Resilience Surge**, which ends this turn. Its legacy — the cyber_defence_breakthrough — is being institutionalised: automated patching and swarm detection are now part of the NIS2 revision, with mandatory adoption by Q1 2029. The **Labour Transition Shield** continues, but we are decoupling it from the failed Enforceable Sovereignty Framework to prevent further backlash. Instead, we are offering Germany and Poland a new bargain: full participation in the Shield and access to InvestAI funds in exchange for joining a voluntary EU-wide AI safety compliance network, pre-negotiating red lines with the U.S. and China. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** remain grid-constrained, so we are invoking the TEN-E revision not as leverage, but as emergency infrastructure — declaring AI compute a project of common interest to unlock fast-tracked connections. We are no longer trying to command unity — we are building survivability.
