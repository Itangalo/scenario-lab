## New measure  
**Labour Transition Shield**  
Establish a pan-EU rapid-response framework to support displaced mid-level professionals with wage insurance, retraining pathways, and sectoral transition incentives.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Stabilise public_sentiment by mitigating social backlash from emergent_labour_displacement_wave; slow the decline in political capital by demonstrating responsive governance; support resilience by preventing urban unrest and service degradation in affected regions.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with conditional access to cohesion and digital transition funds tied to participation.

## Priority  
**Labour Transition Shield** — This measure is the priority because the emergent_labour_displacement_wave is fracturing the social foundation of AI adoption, and without public legitimacy, no technical or defensive measure can hold; the two-year commitment to sovereignty now depends on maintaining political cohesion at home.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that AI is no longer a future risk but a present disruptor. The emergent_labour_displacement_wave in legal, financial, and public administration roles — driven by agents that execute multi-step workflows — has triggered visible unemployment in key urban centres and mobilised unions across France, Germany, and the Benelux. This is not cyclical job loss; it is structural displacement arriving ahead of our social architecture. We cannot secure AI capacity if the public turns against it, nor can we harden infrastructure if cities burn from economic despair. The Labour Transition Shield will be proposed under the European Social Fund Plus, with a new emergency window of €30bn co-financed at 75% for member states that implement rapid retraining pipelines and wage insurance for displaced workers in AI-affected sectors. We will coordinate through the Employment, Social Policy, Health and Consumer Affairs Council (EPSCO), offering early access to EU compute allocations under the InvestAI Gigafactories to countries that stand up sectoral transition councils within 90 days.

At the same time, we are advancing the Emergency Resilience Surge by finalising the draft directive on AI-resilience standards for critical operators. ENISA will conduct its first quarterly red-teaming exercise in May, focusing on energy grid operators in the Nordic and Central European regions. We are offering Poland and Hungary a compromise: opt into the resilience framework with a lighter initial compliance burden, but in return accept binding audit rights and participation in the joint bio-surveillance cell. The French and German governments are still demanding preferential compute access; we are holding firm on shared governance but offering them leadership roles in the red-teaming programme and early deployment of the public-sector AI models that powered the adoption_success in healthcare. The Tech Sovereignty Package continues to face grid bottlenecks in Germany; we are convening a special Energy Council session to fast-track permitting for AI zones under the revised TEN-E regulation, linking progress to the broader resilience agenda. The member_state_defection — widely seen as a surrender to external powers — has weakened our leverage, so we are doubling down on tangible delivery: if we cannot command unity, we must earn it through results.
