### A Turn of Narrowing Options

Despite diplomatic anticipation, the EU’s strategic position weakens. The incoming U.S. administration takes office, but its posture—**ALLIANCE**—brings conditional cooperation, not open access. While joint evaluation and incident reporting are welcomed, the cost of category 4 measures rises, tightening the squeeze on already strained sovereignty efforts.

The **Mandated Safety Integration Directive** finishes this turn as the named priority, but enforcement remains diluted. Though ENISA finalises red-teaming protocols and early-compliance labs come online, runtime safeguards are still not operational in any critical infrastructure. The shift to incentive-based adoption delays real oversight, and no measurable safety gain lands on deployed systems.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** concludes with underwhelming results. Private investment in AI data centres lags, hindered by permitting delays and market uncertainty. In response, redirected funding flows into lithography R&D with ASML, IMEC, and CEA-Leti. However, this pivot does not count as a new measure and yields no immediate sovereignty boost.

Meanwhile, capability advances. **Verification widens** into legal and clinical domains, accelerating progress in those fields. Though **capability_plateau_evidence** sparks debate, the overall trend continues upward: `ai_capability` reaches 62.5. With no safety measure effectively implemented, `ai_safety` drops by 1. Resilience declines as threats grow faster than defences, and public sentiment falls amid visible job displacement and frustration with EU inaction.

The **emergent_regional_ai_pact** and **emergent_regional_aid_pact** deepen fragmentation. Mid-sized and Southern European states act independently, eroding central coordination. The Commission’s political capital erodes further under sustained portfolio costs and declining legitimacy.

### US Posture Confirmed

The new U.S. administration officially adopts a stance of **ALLIANCE**, prioritising structured cooperation with allies on AI safety and incident reporting. Transatlantic negotiations on the **Frontier Access Compact** are expected to begin next turn.
