## In practice
We are finalising the Mandated Safety Integration Directive with full engagement from member states, having secured Germany’s withdrawal of legal objections through co-design rights under Article 114 TFEU. ENISA is now operationalising the red-teaming protocols with the Netherlands-Belgium-Austria-Finland alliance, and we are accrediting early-compliance labs in Lyon, Warsaw, and Lisbon to decentralise capacity and build trust. The CE marking linkage holds, but enforcement is being softened for the first year: instead of procurement penalties, we are offering fast-track access to EU digital infrastructure grants for systems that pass early compliance. This is not retreat — it is sequencing. We need adoption before we can enforce, and enforcement only matters if systems are actually deployed.

The Tech Sovereignty Package concludes this turn, but its private capital targets have underperformed due to permitting bottlenecks and investor caution. We are therefore redirecting its remaining momentum into domestic lithography R&D, activating co-funding mechanisms with ASML, IMEC, and CEA-Leti for next-gen etch and metrology tools. This is not a new measure, but a repurposing of existing capital to respond to the supply_chain_coercion event: if we cannot buy access, we must rebuild the means to produce it. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track for turn 7 completion, and we have invoked the TEN-E revision to designate AI compute as a Project of Common Interest, unlocking emergency grid access and fast-tracked environmental assessments — a move that has drawn legal scrutiny from several member states but is proceeding under urgency grounds.

On the international front, we are preparing for formal negotiations on the Frontier Access Compact, contingent on the incoming U.S. administration’s stance. Our delegation — Commission, EEAS, and national intelligence leads — stands ready to move within 72 hours of confirmation. The core demand remains unchanged: EU-controlled deployment environments with full logging, kill switches, and real-time anomaly detection. In return, we will align with U.S. export controls on China and co-lead a transatlantic incident reporting cell under NATO’s CIRE. This is not dependence — it is a bridge. Without it, we cannot close the safety gap. With it, we buy time to rebuild sovereignty from a position of strength, not desperation.
