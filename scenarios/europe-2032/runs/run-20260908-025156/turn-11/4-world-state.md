### A Winter of Crises and Corrections

The EU reels from two major blows. A sweeping AI-generated ransomware campaign cripples public health IT systems in two member states, disrupting patient records and delaying critical care. The attack, attributed months later to a transnational cybercrime group using model-optimized exploit chains, exposes deep fragilities in critical infrastructure defences. Simultaneously, a national ombudsman delivers a scathing report: an AI-supported social insurance system has wrongfully denied benefits to over 10,000 vulnerable citizens due to biased risk scoring. The scandal ignites public fury and halts all planned public-sector AI expansions.

In response, the EU launches the **Critical Infrastructure AI Shield Initiative**, a large resilience measure under emergency powers, aiming to deploy real-time detection and failover systems across energy, water, and health networks. Funded with €9bn, it is immediately prioritized. However, legal disputes delay ENISA’s authority to mandate private operator compliance, and rollout begins only in pilot zones.

The **Shielded Compute Corridor Initiative** shifts to a new legal basis—the Critical Entities Resilience Directive—avoiding prior antitrust challenges, but Poland resists integration with civil protection agencies, slowing site activation. Talks with Japan, India, and Brazil on shared compute access advance, offering a potential hedge against U.S. restrictions.

Meanwhile, the **European AI Transition and Inclusion Pact** faces internal collapse. Worker councils, citing the scandal, intensify deployment blockades. The Commission prepares emergency acts to limit veto powers, fracturing the social consensus behind the pact.

Public sentiment plummets as strikes spread and headlines emphasize failure. Political capital erodes under the weight of overlapping crises, stalled measures, and internal dissent.
