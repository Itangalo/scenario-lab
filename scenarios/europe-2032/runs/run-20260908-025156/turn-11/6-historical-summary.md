The EU faces a severe crisis of confidence in 2031 as a large-scale AI-driven ransomware attack disrupts critical health systems in two member states, exposing systemic vulnerabilities in cyber-physical infrastructure. Concurrently, a scandal erupts over an AI-powered social insurance system that wrongfully denied benefits to over 10,000 citizens due to biased algorithms, triggering public outrage and halting all public-sector AI expansions. In response, the EU launches the €9bn Critical Infrastructure AI Shield Initiative under emergency powers to secure energy, water, and health networks, though legal barriers delay mandatory compliance and limit initial deployment to pilot zones.  

The Shielded Compute Corridor Initiative bypasses earlier antitrust hurdles by aligning with the Critical Entities Resilience Directive, but progress stalls due to Polish resistance to civil protection integration. International talks with Japan, India, and Brazil advance on shared compute access, offering a partial counterweight to U.S. semiconductor export controls. Domestically, the European AI Transition and Inclusion Pact unravels as worker councils intensify AI deployment blockades, prompting the Commission to consider emergency limits on veto powers, further eroding social consensus.  

Strikes spread amid declining public trust, political cohesion weakens, and key resilience measures remain delayed or fragmented. Globally, AI capability rises to 70.0, with openweight_capability reaching 58.5, driven by autonomous research and legal drafting, while ai_safety continues to decline in the absence of coordinated safeguards.
