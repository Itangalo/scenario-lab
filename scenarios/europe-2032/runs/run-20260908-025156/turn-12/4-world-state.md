### A Fractured Response

The EU’s emergency measures falter under the weight of cascading crises. The **cyber_major_incident**—a coordinated ransomware sweep across public health systems—exposes profound defensive gaps. Response systems, still in pilot phase, fail to contain the spread, and recovery drags on for weeks. The **backlash_physical** escalates: data centre occupations in Germany and France turn violent, with infrastructure sabotage disrupting power feeds. Police intervention inflames tensions, deepening public distrust.

In response, the EU declares the **Critical Infrastructure AI Shield Initiative** a priority, mobilising €9bn to accelerate deployment. Legal disputes stall enforcement—ENISA lacks authority to compel private operators, and Poland blocks expanded oversight under sovereignty concerns. The Shielded Compute Corridor, meant to anchor EU-controlled compute, stalls at integration talks. Austria and Nordic states express interest in hosting, but coordination remains fragile.

The **Emergency Compute Hardening Directive** is proposed, mandating strict physical and cyber controls for large data centres. Enforcement would tie electricity supply to compliance—a bold move, but met with resistance from member states wary of central overreach. However, due to the constraint of introducing at most one new measure per turn, the directive is not formally entered into the portfolio. Internal debates delay its advancement, and it remains in drafting, pending resolution of jurisdictional disputes. No implementation begins this turn.

Meanwhile, the **European AI Transition and Inclusion Pact** expires without renewal. Worker councils denounce the abandonment, and strikes intensify. Funding is redirected to retraining hubs, but without guarantees, participation lags. The social contract frays further.

Globally, the **openweight_frontier_release** shocks the landscape. A powerful open model, functionally near the frontier, spreads rapidly. Offensive cyber tools are reverse-engineered within days. Resilience efforts are outpaced—defenders are reactive, not adaptive.

The American posture remains **ALLIANCE**, but with limited leverage. Joint safety frameworks exist on paper, but EU access to advanced models remains constrained. Tokyo, New Delhi, and Brasília advance their compute diplomacy initiative, offering alternatives to US-EU dependency—but the EU’s weakened capital limits its bargaining power.
