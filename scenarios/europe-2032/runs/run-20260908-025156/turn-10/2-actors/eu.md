## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor social legitimacy through inclusion and worker agency while rebuilding sovereign capacity to act independently in AI, ensuring that autonomy is not only technological but politically sustainable  
- Grounds: The **emergent_worker_representation_backlash** reveals that inclusion mechanisms, if not carefully bounded, can become veto points that paralyse deployment and erode public trust in governance itself. The European AI Transition and Inclusion Pact was intended to stabilise legitimacy, but its worker representation clause is now being weaponised not to enable responsible adoption, but to block it — undermining the very institutions it was meant to protect. At the same time, the **cyber_major_incident** and **supply_chain_coercion** expose that sovereignty without operational capacity is hollow. We must recalibrate: inclusion remains essential, but it cannot come at the cost of paralysis. The commitment now reflects that legitimacy must be earned not only through protection, but through agency — and that sovereignty requires both infrastructure and the political freedom to use it.

## New measure
**Shielded Compute Corridor Initiative**  
Establish a legally protected, physically hardened network of EU-anchored compute facilities with guaranteed access for public institutions and vetted researchers, using conditional electricity and infrastructure rights to enforce control.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 10–15 points, create a non-recallable base for future capability development, and signal credible autonomy to coalition partners in the Compute Access and Security Pact negotiations.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, public research institutions, and selected private operators under binding security and access terms.

## Priority
**Shielded Compute Corridor Initiative** — Because the **supply_chain_coercion** and **member_state_defection** together prove that external actors now treat EU coordination as optional, and because the **cyber_major_incident** shows our critical systems are exposed not just to attack but to cascading failure, we must act now to anchor irreducible compute capacity under EU jurisdiction — not as a future aspiration, but as a condition of survival.

## In practice
We are launching the Shielded Compute Corridor Initiative under the legal basis of the Digital Markets Act and the Critical Entities Resilience Directive, with implementation led by a new Joint Compute Authority co-chaired by DG CONNECT and the European Defence Agency. The Corridor will consist of three core nodes — in Finland, France, and Poland — each with hardened physical security, sovereign grid access, and legally enforceable non-compliance clauses for any entity attempting to override EU control. We are reallocating €14bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Innovation Fund to fast-track construction, with a binding requirement that all hardware be subject to EU export control and that firmware be open to audit by ENISA. Access will be granted first to national AI safety institutes, public health modelling teams, and energy grid operators — demonstrating utility while building political cover.

At the same time, we are managing the fallout from the emergent_worker_representation_backlash by amending the European AI Transition and Inclusion Pact through delegated acts: worker representatives on AI Deployment Councils will retain advisory power, but final deployment decisions will rest with elected oversight boards, ensuring that safety concerns are heard without granting veto rights. We are negotiating this change directly with the ETUC, offering expanded funding for union-led AI literacy programmes in exchange for de-escalation. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep across municipal services in two member states — is being treated as a wake-up call: we are accelerating the rollout of the Critical Infrastructure AI Shield Initiative’s detection layer, using emergency provisions under Article 122 TFEU to bypass stalled procurement. This is not the future we wanted, but it is the one we are building — one where sovereignty is not begged for, and legitimacy is not surrendered to paralysis.
