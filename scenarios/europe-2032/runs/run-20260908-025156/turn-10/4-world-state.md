### A Winter of Compounding Pressures

The EU enters 2031 under siege on multiple fronts. A sweeping, AI-generated ransomware campaign cripples municipal services in two member states, exposing critical gaps in cyber-physical defences. Recovery is slow, and public trust erodes further as officials admit they were unprepared for the speed and coordination of the attack.

Simultaneously, Washington tightens export controls, restricting not only advanced chips but also older ASML equipment vital to global semiconductor supply chains. The move, enforced through extraterritorial jurisdiction, underscores the EU’s strategic vulnerability. The Netherlands complies under duress, triggering outrage in Brussels and confirming fears of dependency.

In response, the EU launches the **Shielded Compute Corridor Initiative**, a large sovereignty measure aiming to build a hardened, EU-controlled compute backbone. Nodes in Finland, France, and Poland are designated, and €14bn is redirected from existing funds. However, early implementation is rocky — legal challenges arise over the use of the Digital Markets Act as a basis, and Poland voices concerns over military oversight.

Worse, the **European AI Transition and Inclusion Pact** backfires. Worker representatives embedded in public AI projects begin halting deployments over unresolved safety concerns, turning inclusion into obstruction. The European Trade Union Confederation (ETUC) resists proposed reforms that would limit veto power, demanding more, not less, control.

The **Enforceable Runtime Safeguards Mandate** formally completes, but its impact is muted — southern states still lag in adoption, and the tiered enforcement model weakens collective security. No resilience gains materialise.

Public sentiment dips as strikes spread and headlines focus on paralysis and cyber chaos. Political capital, already thin, plummets under the weight of multiple crises and an overstretched agenda.
