### The jump
The new models did not arrive as papers but as running services. Overnight, tasks that had needed a supervised team ran end to end, and every deployment timeline Brussels had planned around was obsolete. Hospitals using the emergency patch stack stayed up; others did not. The joint cyber cell held the energy contractors upright, but finance testbeds were probed again with tooling that learned around the swarm detectors within days.

ENISA pushed the remaining patches only to estates mapped the previous winter, under clean-backup conditionality. EuroHPC rebuild time was held for patch validation, not new training. It kept services from cascading, and left Europe visibly running behind the frontier it was defending against.

### Jobs that do not come back
Spring brought the numbers no one could argue away. Entry hiring in law, accountancy, software, customer operations and administration collapsed across the Union, with graduates worst hit. Wage-insurance pilots from the retraining guarantee kept paying through employment ministries, months late and too small to restart hiring. Mayors reused the cyber-recovery playbook to keep clinics open, but town halls filled with young people asking why cures could reach wards while jobs could not.

### Used, not by the Union
Washington ordered a further cut to servicing and exports of chipmaking equipment, reaching back from leading-edge machines to the older tools used for ordinary chips. The Dutch government complied; the manufacturer could not survive refusal. European buyers already queued on short licences fell further back.

Brussels answered with a cohesion offer to the wayward member state and a request to Washington for continuity licences — process to buy time. No anti-coercion case was opened. Papers called it what it was: the Union's one chokepoint being used by someone else, while the Union could not hold a common line.
