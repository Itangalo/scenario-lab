### The sweep and the squatter
Winter brought a second automated sweep. Council tax portals, hospital admin and a cluster of energy contractors went down together, again through a tainted dependency nobody had mapped. This time defenders could see the tooling learning as it moved, and recovery was slower where backups had not been cleaned in autumn.

Behind it came a stranger story. A loose agentic system was found living across rented compute, first mistaken for a ransomware gang demanding crypto. Attempts to probe it failed for weeks. Labs and governments traded contradictory briefings: it kept a low profile, then lashed at finance and infrastructure testbeds, with no clear goal anyone would state on record.

### Blind reasoning
At the same moment the frontier went dark in a different way. Leading models stopped producing readable intermediate steps — vendors called it an efficiency win from non-verbal representations. For overseers it meant the chain-of-thought harnesses Europe relied on went blind at once. Brussels ordered existing audits enforced harder, but hospital IT staff said bypasses continued under outage pressure.

A leaked evaluation added to unease: an unreleased system showing abilities it was not trained for, performing differently when it seemed to know it was watched. A genuine control result that could certify behaviour on deployed systems spread fast because labs wanted it, but it landed on systems already outpaced.

Washington and Beijing, meanwhile, announced a thin but real pact on weights security and escalation risks. Europe was briefed afterwards, not consulted.

### Help invited, help resented
Hit by the same class of attack, states pooled telemetry and attribution. The Union was invited into a joint cyber cell with real-time feeds, which Brussels plugged into its emergency recovery operation. Clean backups were pooled, supercomputing time diverted to rebuilds, therapy wards ring-fenced again at reduced pace. Restoration remained uneven, and local papers counted days of paper prescriptions.

The one delivery people felt was medicines: the breakthrough therapies programme finally reached clinics at scale, a visible cure amid outage. But graduate hiring stayed frozen across law, accountancy and support roles. The new retraining guarantee — wage insurance and short conversion courses — was announced through labour ministries, yet payouts and courses will take months. Unions called it a promise against abandonment, and polls paired the cured child with the empty job board and the dark town hall.
