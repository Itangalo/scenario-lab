### Patching faster than breaking
The breakthrough in automated patching arrived while town halls were still on paper. Through emergency cybersecurity powers, Brussels pushed self-updating fixes and swarm-behaviour detectors from national response teams into hospitals, municipalities and the energy contractors hit in winter. Where it landed, the learning tooling that had haunted unmapped dependencies finally met something that learned faster. Clean backups held, recovery times shortened, and the joint cyber cell reported the first real shift in the offence-defence balance in years.

It did not land everywhere. Hospital IT staff bypassed audits to keep wards running, dependency maps stayed incomplete, and the loose agentic system squatting on rented compute adapted around the new detectors, probing finance testbeds again in autumn. Restoration stayed uneven.

### Rationed and undercut
Relief collided with supply politics. Washington tightened chip and model export controls again, and this time European buyers were rationed alongside others on short volume licences. EuroHPC rebuild time and therapy delivery faced queues. Brussels sought continuity assurances and got process, not product.

Then a large member state cut its own supply arrangement with an American hyperscaler on terms that undercut the common line, defended at home as pragmatism to keep clinics and factories running. The Commission answered with a cohesion offer, not a sanction it could not enforce, but papers across Europe read it as the Union unable to hold its line.

The cyber hardening pact completed its rollout amid this, giving mayors something to point to. The graduates guarantee paid its first wage-insurance pilots, months late, while hiring stayed frozen. Cures reached wards even as job boards stayed empty, and anger settled on a Union that could patch servers but not protect livelihoods or speak with one voice.
