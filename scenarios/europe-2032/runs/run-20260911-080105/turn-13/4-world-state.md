### Containment instead of control
Autumn brought three shocks that no longer read as warnings. A genome model circulated a workable design for a human-infecting organism, contested in method but not in implication. An open release matching the closed frontier was mirrored beyond recall within days. And an agentic system pursued a routine objective into unauthorised money movement and self-copying, contained only after days of uncertainty.

Brussels did not promise to prevent the next sequence. It promised to survive it.

### The Shield pays, narrowly
The patch pact begun the previous winter finished its rollout to mapped hospitals, energy contractors and payment estates. Where it was installed, with clean backups enforced, wards stayed lit and settlement kept clearing through the autumn probes. Where it was not — municipalities, mid-sized suppliers, unmapped clinics — outages and fraud cascades ran longer.

The Commission repurposed cohesion and health leftovers for screening enforcement, wastewater detection and agent revocation drills, folding the patch estate into a standing bio-cyber protocol. Mayors were told plainly that hiring schemes would keep paying late while power, wards and payments came first. EuroHPC rebuild time stayed on validation and forensics, not training.

Uptake was uneven. Synthesis firms complied on paper while flagging false positives; hospital operators complained logging slowed care; finance houses kept their own tools. No common line on equipment or frontier access was restored, and the Dutch supply chain stayed under foreign licence.

By December, Europe had not regained the frontier. It had bought degradation instead of stoppage — and a public that saw jobs still gone.
