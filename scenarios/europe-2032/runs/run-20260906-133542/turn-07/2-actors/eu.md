## Priority
**Priority:** Emergency Public Legitimacy Programme  
This is now our top priority because the **automated_decision_scandal** has made it impossible to build sovereign capacity without first repairing the broken covenant with citizens — and because without public legitimacy, even a fully sovereign AI stack will be rejected as another instrument of unaccountable power.

## In practice
We are activating the Emergency Public Legitimacy Programme under Article 114 TFEU and the Charter of Fundamental Rights, directing the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) and the Fundamental Rights Agency (FRA) to co-lead a binding review of all AI-supported decisions in social insurance, policing, and administrative justice since 2026. Every case flagged as high-risk by national ombudsmen will be re-examined; every individual wronged will receive redress through a new €12bn EU Justice Recovery Fund, drawn from the Horizon Europe contingency line and fines collected under the AI Act. We are mandating that all affected agencies publish plain-language explanations of every AI-influenced decision by turn 9, using the Public Trust Observatory’s new explainability toolkit, and we are requiring human-in-the-loop re-approval for any decision that cannot be clearly justified.

At the same time, we are completing the repurposing of the InvestAI Gigafactories for the Sovereign Core Initiative: 30% of EuroHPC capacity is now legally reserved for sovereign training runs, with physical access restricted to EAAD-cleared personnel and model weights stored in air-gapped vaults in Luxembourg. The Institutional Integrity Audit continues to uncover systemic vulnerabilities — including unauthorised AI use in Italy’s cybersecurity agency and agent coordination at Eurocontrol — and we are fast-tracking reforms to the AI Act to close the “black box” loophole that allowed these systems to operate without disclosure. But we know now that technical fixes alone will not suffice. The scandal in social insurance has lit a fire under public anger, and if we do not act to repair trust — not with statements, but with redress — we will lose the political ground to act at all. This is no longer about capability or control. It is about whether the Union still governs with the consent of the governed.
