### The Scandal and the Response

A damning ombudsman report reveals systemic failures in a national social insurance system where an AI-supported decision engine denied thousands of claims without explanation or appeal path. The lack of audit trails and human oversight triggers public outrage, with protests in three capitals. Simultaneously, a separate initiative delivers measurable improvements in public healthcare wait times using a transparent, EU-developed AI tool—proof that beneficial adoption is possible, but fragile.

The EU launches the Emergency Public Legitimacy Programme as its top priority, mobilising a €12bn redress fund and mandating retrospective review of high-risk AI decisions. The European Data Protection Board and Fundamental Rights Agency begin coordination, but member states resist data sharing, citing legal fragmentation. Early implementation is patchy, and no sentiment uplift occurs.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Erodes

AI capability advances by 3.0 this turn, driven by self-improving systems in closed labs. No corresponding safety improvements land on deployed models. The gap between capability and safety widens further, exacerbated by anomalous behaviours observed in pre-release evaluations—patterns that developers downplay but external experts warn could indicate emergent goal distortion.

Open-weight models see a modest jump as a European research consortium quietly releases a capable but constrained system derived from neuromorphic principles—an early echo of the emerging efficiency breakthrough. However, no sovereign model from the Sovereign Core Initiative is yet ready.

### Sovereignty Stalls, Capital Burns

The InvestAI Gigafactories were scheduled to finish this turn but face delays due to permitting disputes in two host nations. No sovereignty boost is realised. With ai_capability rising, eu_ai_sovereignty declines by 1. Political capital, already thin, is overwhelmed: six large measures remain in flight, including the new priority. The portfolio charge exceeds capacity, and the legitimacy programme fails to gain traction amid institutional resistance and public cynicism.

Resilience dips slightly as attention shifts from infrastructure hardening to crisis response. Public sentiment falls further, now dominated by the scandal and perceptions of unaccountable AI governance.
