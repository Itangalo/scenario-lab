## Priority  
**Biosurveillance and Incident Response Integration** — because the bio_incident has crystallized a new order of catastrophic risk, and embedding the EU in a trusted, interoperable response network is now the most effective way to secure both safety and sovereignty; without this, even independent capability would lack early warning and collective enforcement.

## In practice  
We are advancing the Helsinki analysis cell as the operational core of the joint biosurveillance pact, staffing it with ECDC leads, national biosecurity directors, and liaison officers from partner states. Binding data-sharing protocols are being enforced through conditional disbursement of the €2bn readiness fund under EU4Health, with Frontex coordinating border deployment of mobile sequencing units at key entry points. We are engaging Hungary and Slovakia directly through the Health Security Committee, offering opt-in sequencing pilots with national oversight to preserve sovereignty while ensuring coverage. The safety_breakthrough in interpretability is being leveraged to build confidence: we are requiring that any AI used in pathogen design screening within the network must have decision traces auditable to training data, a standard now being drafted by HERA in coordination with the European AI Office.

Simultaneously, we are pressing the Environment Council to unblock the Accelerated Permitting Zones by linking them to the biosurveillance effort: data centres will be required to host local bio-risk simulation nodes, making infrastructure not just faster to build but purpose-bound to public safety. The Gigafactories programme continues, with the redirected node now under design for redacted bio-model evaluation, using interpretability tools to detect hazardous synthesis patterns without exposing sensitive data. The Tech sovereignty package is being amended in trilogue to include the 5% emergency runtime reservation, ensuring that every privately backed data centre contributes to resilience. All actions are coordinated through the Competitiveness Council, with the Commission using the momentum of crisis to align national positions that previously stalled on industrial and security grounds.
