## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Transition European AI sovereignty from infrastructure anchoring to operational control, ensuring EU institutions can direct, monitor, and benefit from sovereign AI systems in real time  
- Trigger: the **ai_investment_collapse** and **supply_chain_coercion** events  
- Grounds: The collapse in private investment has frozen frontier advancement, creating a rare window where the pace of capability growth is not accelerating — but coercion over supply chains proves that strategic vulnerability persists even when compute is built. Our sovereignty remains passive: we have hardware, but no command leverage over its use or protection against external control. The Gigafactories are complete, yet we cannot defend their inputs. Operational control must now extend beyond software interfaces to include material resilience and political insulation from foreign jurisdictional overreach. This turn demands we harden what we built, not just connect to it.

## New measure  
**Resilient Material Supply Chains for Strategic AI Infrastructure**  
Establish EU-controlled stockpiles, alternative routing, and dual-use conversion capacity for critical semiconductor inputs, insulating sovereign AI infrastructure from extraterritorial coercion.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `eu_ai_sovereignty` (+6–9) by reducing exposure to foreign chokepoints in lithography chemicals, precision optics, and helium supply; indirect boost to `resilience` (+2–4) through hardened input streams for essential compute.  
`Applies to:` EU-funded data centres, chip fabs receiving state aid, and critical suppliers designated under the Critical Entities Resilience Directive.

## Priority  
**Open-Weight Accountability Framework** — because despite the investment collapse, open-weight models remain at 50.0 capability with no safety floor, and without liability enforcement, misuse incidents will still emerge from within the EU’s own developer communities; this measure closes the last legal gap before widespread autonomous harm becomes inevitable, and it expires this turn — failure to adopt it now means abandoning the principle entirely.

## In practice  
We are finalizing adoption of the **Open-Weight Accountability Framework** in the Council, where resistance from Germany and France over open-source innovation has been tempered by the recent doxxing incident traced to an unmodified Llama-derived model trained locally. We are offering exemptions for academic and non-commercial releases below 48.0 effective capability, verified via standardized benchmarks administered by the European AI Office. Legal Services has confirmed Article 114 TFEU covers harmonization of civil liability for digital products posing systemic risk, and we are linking market access to registration in a new EU Developer Integrity Registry. Enforcement will begin with takedown notices and escalate to bandwidth throttling for repeat offenders — a step we know risks backlash, but one justified by the absence of any other containment mechanism now that models at 50.0 are globally distributed and self-hosted.

The **European AI Command Interface** enters its second phase this turn, with pilot deployments now live in DG Health’s drug approval tracking unit and Eurojust’s cross-border fraud analysis cell. Early findings confirm severe logging gaps: models optimize for efficiency, not auditability. We are mandating transformer attention-layer tagging and energy-proportional logging across all public AI workloads on sovereign infrastructure, funded through repurposed InvestAI operations budgets. ENISA is drafting baseline standards for interface interoperability, to be adopted under the Cyber Resilience Act. National CIOs are being convened to align APIs, but Italy and Spain warn that retrofitting legacy systems could delay full integration until after turn 11.

Simultaneously, we are launching the **Resilient Material Supply Chains** initiative in response to Washington’s coercion of ASML and the sudden fragility of precursor gas deliveries. Joint Action under the Single Market Emergency Instrument will designate helium-3, deuterated polymers, and deep-UV optical coatings as strategic materials. We are activating Horizon Europe emergency funding to fast-track recycling R&D and signing bilateral stockpile-sharing agreements with Japan and Taiwan — politically delicate, but necessary. The Commission is preparing a proposal under Article 173 TFEU to fund regional micro-fabs for non-leading-edge components, ensuring that even if advanced nodes are blocked, maintenance and mid-tier compute production can continue. This is sovereignty not as aspiration, but as redundancy — and it begins now, while capital is cheap and the frontier is paused.
