### A Crisis Reshapes Priorities

The discovery of a novel, engineered pathogen in Southeast Asia — later confirmed to have escaped from a research facility using AI-designed synthesis pathways — sent shockwaves through global health and security institutions. The **bio_incident** triggered an immediate international response, culminating in the formation of a joint biosurveillance pact with real-time data sharing, rapid attribution protocols, and mobile response teams. The EU was invited to join, but only on condition of full integration: national labs had to report anomalies within 24 hours, and sequencing capacity had to be co-located and interoperable.

Brussels moved swiftly. Invoking Article 7 of the Civil Protection Mechanism, the Commission fast-tracked the **Biosurveillance and Incident Response Integration** measure, establishing a joint analysis cell in Helsinki and deploying mobile units at key border points. Smaller member states accepted €2bn in readiness funding, easing adoption. Yet resistance lingered — Hungary and Slovakia delayed data-sharing agreements, citing sovereignty concerns, slowing full operational capability.

Meanwhile, the **safety_breakthrough**, a new interpretability technique allowing direct mapping of model decisions to training data traces, was rapidly adopted across leading labs. Assurance improved visibly, especially in high-stakes domains like bio-research and cyber-defense. Combined with the joint threat framework, this lifted confidence in systemic control.

On infrastructure, progress stalled. The **Accelerated Permitting Zones** remained blocked in the Environment Council, despite pressure over **supply_chain_coercion** — Washington’s further restrictions on ASML exports weakened Europe’s semiconductor leverage and underscored dependence. No new compute access was secured, and rising frontier capability eroded sovereignty further.

Public sentiment edged up slightly as the bio-response was framed as decisive, though job losses in administrative sectors continued feeding quiet unrest. Political capital declined under the weight of three large ongoing measures and the new priority, with no completions to offset costs.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

Despite strategic reorientation, the EU’s material position weakened. Domestic compute expansion lagged, external supply controls tightened, and no category 4 measure finished. The gap between ambition and execution widened.
