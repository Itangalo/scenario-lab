Biosurveillance and Incident Response Integration  
Integrate EU health security agencies into the newly formed joint biosurveillance pact, enabling real-time pathogen data sharing, binding sample access, and co-located analysis cells.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by at least +15 points through integration into a standing, exercised response framework; improve early-warning coverage across member states and reduce attribution lag in biological incidents.  
`Applies to:` EU-level health and security agencies (ECDC, HERA, Frontex), with binding participation requirements and shared funding from the Union Civil Protection Mechanism.

## Priority
**Biosurveillance and Incident Response Integration** — because the bio_incident has reset the risk landscape, and the joint_threat_response offers a rare window to embed the EU in a functional, high-trust defense network; without this, our sovereignty is performative, not protective.

## In practice
We are activating Article 7 of the Union Civil Protection Mechanism and the HERA mandate to fast-track integration into the joint biosurveillance pact. This means binding commitments from national health institutes to forward anomalous pathogen sequences within 24 hours, co-funding mobile sequencing units stationed at major ports and border crossings, and establishing a joint analysis cell in Helsinki — jointly staffed by ECDC, national labs, and allied partners. We are leveraging the political momentum of the bio_incident to override national hesitations on data sharing, pairing the measure with a €2bn emergency readiness fund under the EU4Health programme to offset implementation costs in smaller member states.

Simultaneously, we are pressing the Competitiveness Council to finally adopt the Accelerated Permitting Zones under qualified majority, using the supply_chain_coercion event as leverage: without domestic compute resilience, we cannot secure the infrastructure this biosurveillance system will depend on. The Gigafactories remain on track, but we are redirecting one node — originally slated for general-purpose AI training — toward secure, redacted bio-model evaluation under public oversight. This shift answers both the safety_breakthrough and the bio_incident, turning raw compute into a sovereign assurance function. The Tech sovereignty package now includes a clause requiring all supported data centres to reserve 5% of runtime for public health and emergency response simulations — not just profit or research. This is how we make capability answerable: not only to Brussels, but to the next crisis.
