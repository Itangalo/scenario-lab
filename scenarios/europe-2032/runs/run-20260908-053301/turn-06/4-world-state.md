### A Turn of Enforcement and Exposure

The EU shifted from building to enforcing, launching the **Open-Weight Accountability Framework** as its new priority amid rising alarm over automated harassment campaigns exploiting powerful open models. Though framed as a defence of democratic discourse, the move sparked fierce backlash from open-source communities, who denounced it as overreach that would stifle innovation. The European AI Office began consultations, but early signals suggest compliance will be patchy, especially from decentralized developer collectives beyond direct jurisdiction.

Meanwhile, the **Sovereign Compute Access and Resilience Corridors** advanced slowly through the Digital Ministers Council. Germany and France agreed to binding runtime thresholds for energy and emergency services, but Poland hesitated, citing cost, and Hungary formally challenged the legal basis under Article 114 TFEU, arguing it exceeds internal market harmonization. Conditional access to the Just Transition Fund kept negotiations alive, but implementation delays pushed the effective rollout into 2030.

The completion of the **Tech Sovereignty Package** freed modest political bandwidth. Accelerated permitting is now law, and private investment in EU data centres has stabilized — though ASML export controls continue to bottleneck chip production in Dresden and Grenoble, undermining sovereignty gains.

### US Posture Takes Hold

With the 2028 election concluded, the new administration’s **RETRENCHMENT** stance became operational: American AI progress slowed as infrastructure moratoriums took effect, reducing pressure on the EU frontier. While this eased competitive strain, it also deepened uncertainty over future access to US-developed models and components.

### Resilience Holds, Safety Erodes

Resilience rose slightly as biosurveillance integration delivered measurable improvements in cross-border detection. However, **ai_safety** dipped as capability advanced without corresponding safeguards in shipped systems. The open-weight misuse incident underscored systemic gaps — and with no safety-focused measure completed, confidence eroded.

Public sentiment fell slightly, reflecting unease over both the harassment campaigns and the EU’s restrictive response. Political capital, however, saw a modest rebound from recent completions and the legitimacy of acting decisively on emerging threats.

The rise in **openweight_capability** was limited, consistent with the broader stagnation in global compute availability and the constrained pace of model development under the US RETRENCHMENT posture. While some open models saw incremental improvements through efficiency gains, there was no structural leap in their performance relative to frontier systems, and no evidence of mass leakage or uncontrolled release of advanced capabilities.
