## New measure
**Sovereign Compute Access and Resilience Corridors**  
A binding directive establishes minimum compute thresholds for essential public functions across energy, health, and emergency response, reserving priority runtime on EU-controlled infrastructure for these services during disruptions.  

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `eu_ai_sovereignty` by +8–12, modestly raise `resilience` (+5–7) by hardening public services against disruption, and lock in long-term demand for domestic compute buildout.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with implementation through national digital ministries and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking.

## Priority  
**Sovereign Compute Access and Resilience Corridors** — because the failed US frontier release and inward turn leave a strategic opening, but also expose that our own compute sovereignty remains hollow without guaranteed public access; this measure turns infrastructure into service continuity, directly answering the erosion of `eu_ai_sovereignty` while anchoring future capacity in mission-critical functions.

## In practice  
We are finalizing the integration of the Helsinki analysis cell into the global biosurveillance pact, with binding data-sharing agreements now active across 18 member states. The EU4Health readiness fund has been conditionally released, unlocking full data flows from frontline sequencing units at border points, while Frontex coordinates logistics for rapid deployment of mobile units during alerts. Hungary and Slovakia remain partial participants, but opt-in pilots for local bio-risk simulation nodes—offering national oversight of AI decision traces—are gaining traction as a sovereignty-preserving model. HERA’s auditable AI standard is now embedded in procurement rules for all AI used in pathogen screening, enforced through the European AI Office’s new inspection mandate.

Domestically, we are pivoting from infrastructure promises to guaranteed public utility. The **Sovereign Compute Access and Resilience Corridors** directive will bind national digital strategies to minimum runtime allocations for essential services—energy grid management, pandemic response, and emergency communications—on EU-controlled compute clusters. This is not merely about access; it is about continuity under stress. We are negotiating with national energy regulators and civil protection agencies to define baseline thresholds, using the Just Transition Fund to offset compliance costs in lower-capacity member states. The measure builds on the **Tech Sovereignty Package** by converting private investment pledges into enforceable public service obligations, ensuring that compute built with accelerated permitting delivers resilience by design.

The **Public Compute Access Programme**, proposed last turn, is being implemented through regional innovation agencies in Spain and Poland, linking 15% of future Gigafactory runtime to local hubs in exchange for siting approval. Retraining funds are being redirected through cohesion mechanisms to support displaced administrative workers, softening resistance. However, political capital remains strained at 23.0, and we are holding firm against expanding the portfolio further without completion or cancellation. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** remain on track for completion in turn 7, but delays in semiconductor supply chains—exacerbated by ASML export controls—continue to threaten milestones. The window opened by the US election retrenchment is real, but it will not stay open long. We are acting now to anchor sovereignty not in aspiration, but in obligation.
