### US Election Shifts the Landscape

The 2028 US election concluded with a decisive victory for the retrenchment faction, marked by widespread moratoriums on AI infrastructure, restrictions in public institutions, and a domestic focus that sharply reduced American leadership in the AI frontier. While this inward turn eases pressure on European sovereignty, it also removes a key partner in joint governance and creates uncertainty about future access to cutting-edge models and components. The shift is widely interpreted in Brussels as a strategic opening—but one that demands immediate action before the momentum fades.

### Sovereignty Through Service Obligations

The EU launched **Sovereign Compute Access and Resilience Corridors**, a large category 4 measure aimed at binding national digital strategies to guaranteed runtime allocations for essential services on EU-controlled infrastructure. Framed as a continuity-of-government initiative, it gained rapid support from civil protection agencies and energy regulators. However, early negotiations revealed resistance from member states wary of centralized mandates, delaying implementation timelines.

### Resilience Gains, Sovereignty Stalls

Two major measures concluded: **Accelerated Permitting Zones** unlocked faster approvals for strategic AI infrastructure, and **Biosurveillance Integration** became fully operational across 18 member states, significantly boosting detection capacity. These completions, combined with ongoing adoption of auditable AI in health security, lifted `resilience` to 50.0. Yet `eu_ai_sovereignty` dipped further to 18.0 as `ai_capability` rose by 1.0 without a domestic compute milestone, triggering decay under metric rule 5. The new US posture, while reducing external competition, also undermines confidence in transatlantic supply chains.

Public sentiment edged up slightly, supported by visible gains in biosurveillance and inclusive compute access in Spain and Poland. However, political capital declined slightly under the weight of a growing portfolio and the priority penalty, despite modest gains from recent completions.
