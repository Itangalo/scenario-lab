Operational resilience and material sovereignty for European AI systems

## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Operational resilience and material sovereignty for European AI systems``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn
- Grounds: The completion of compute corridors and early deployment of the Command Interface confirm that infrastructure anchoring is no longer the bottleneck. What fails now are not connections, but continuity: audit gaps in live systems, unsecured supply chains, and foreign jurisdictional overreach that disables maintenance even when hardware is on EU soil. The shift from building to hardening is categorical. This new commitment reflects that control must now be measured not in access, but in endurance — against coercion, cascading failure, and silent degradation. We are no longer proving we can build; we are proving we can hold.

## New measure
**Secure Provenance and Auditability Standards for Public AI Workloads**  
Mandate technical standards for real-time audit trails and model provenance in all AI systems used by EU institutions and critical operators, integrating with the emergent open-source provenance framework and making it legally operable.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` (+4–6) by enforcing legibility and traceability in deployed systems; indirect boost to `resilience` (+2) through faster incident attribution and containment.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member state agencies using sovereign AI, and contractors operating under the Critical Entities Resilience Directive.

## Priority
**European AI Command Interface** — because despite its flaws, it is the only instrument currently in flight that directly delivers on our expiring two-year commitment to operational control, and this turn’s audit failures prove that without enforced logging and interface standardization, sovereignty remains theoretical — a network of powerful systems we cannot see into, let alone direct.

## In practice
We are finalizing the adoption of the **Open-Weight Accountability Framework** in trilogue, accepting the German-French compromise that exempts non-commercial and academic releases below 48.0 capability, provided they register with the European AI Office and submit to benchmark verification. The Developer Integrity Registry will go live next month, hosted by ENISA, with API integration required for cloud providers serving the EU market. While bandwidth throttling remains on the table for enforcement, we are downgrading it to a secondary tool, relying instead on DNS delisting and withdrawal of certification under the Cyber Resilience Act for non-compliant actors. This preserves deterrence while reducing the risk of backlash from developer communities whose cooperation we still need.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the **European AI Command Interface** pilots in DG Health and Eurojust, where early results have forced a pivot: the logging mandates we imposed are technically feasible but create unacceptable latency in time-sensitive workflows. To reconcile auditability with performance, we are funding an emergency R&D sprint under Horizon Europe to develop sparse, event-triggered logging protocols — lightweight by default, high-fidelity when anomalies are detected. National CIOs are being asked to adopt a common schema by turn 10, with Italy and Spain granted temporary derogations in exchange for participation in the protocol design. Without this adaptation, the Command Interface risks becoming a dashboard that only works after the fact.

The new **Secure Provenance and Auditability Standards** initiative builds directly on the unexpected momentum of the emergent open-source provenance framework. We are convening its core developers under Article 33 of the AI Act to fast-track integration with EU legal definitions of accountability, turning their metadata tagging system into a de facto compliance pathway. This avoids reinventing the wheel while grounding innovation in enforceable standards. The Commission is preparing delegated acts under the Digital Services Act and AI Act to recognize compliant systems as “presumed trustworthy” in procurement and liability contexts, creating positive incentives for adoption. Funding will come from repurposed InvestAI modernization budgets, avoiding new fiscal requests during this period of constrained capital.
