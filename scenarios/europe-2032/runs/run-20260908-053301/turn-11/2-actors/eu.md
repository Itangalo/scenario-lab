## Statement changes
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not mistake persistence for correctness. When a measure fails to land as intended, we will adapt the strategy, not just the tactics.  
- Grounds: The Enforceable Legibility Standards, launched this turn in response to opaque_reasoning, already face technical unprovenness and industry resistance, while the European AI Command Interface — our priority — is being downgraded from continuous to anomaly-triggered logging due to operational failure. This is not just uncertainty; it is evidence that our current path to oversight is breaking. We must now treat adaptation as part of commitment, not a betrayal of it.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Enduring control over European AI systems — ensuring continuity of operation, auditability of decisions, and immunity from external coercion — even when reasoning is no longer legible, infrastructure is under strain, and trust is thin  
- Grounds: The opaque_reasoning event and cyber_major_incident confirm that control is no longer a function of access or logging, but of resilience under breakdown. The commitment must now explicitly encompass failure modes: when systems are inscrutable, when attacks land, and when public trust frays. This is not a shift in direction, but a deepening of resolve — control must persist not only in ideal conditions, but in the winter of the black box.

## New measure
**Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Public Services**  
Accelerate cyber hardening, incident response capacity, and fallback procedures for essential public functions in health, justice, and energy, with mandatory breach simulation exercises and AI-disruption contingency plans.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `resilience` (+6–8) by ensuring essential services can degrade gracefully under AI-enabled attack; indirect boost to `public_sentiment` (+3–5) if visible preparedness reduces perceived vulnerability.  
`Applies to:` Member states, operators of essential services under NIS2, and EU agencies with public-facing digital infrastructure.

## Priority
**Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Public Services** — because the cyber_major_incident proves that defensive readiness has fallen behind offensive capability, and with ai_safety at 28.0 and openweight_capability rising, we can no longer assume attacks will be caught before they land; this turn, survival matters more than sovereignty.

## In practice
We are pivoting hard from prevention to preparedness. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that crippled municipal health portals in three member states — has shattered the assumption that detection and logging alone are enough. The European AI Command Interface, while still in final integration, is now clearly insufficient: its shift to anomaly-triggered logging means we may not see the breakdown until it’s too late. We are therefore launching the Emergency Resilience Surge under the Critical Entities Resilience Directive, co-funded by the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme, with a €4.2 billion allocation redirected from stalled adoption projects. This will mandate that all NIS2-covered entities complete AI-disruption tabletop exercises by turn 12, deploy AI-resistant authentication layers, and designate human-operated fallback protocols for core services.

At the same time, we are acknowledging that the Open-Weight Accountability Framework has failed to land: with openweight_capability now at 51.0 and the emergent_open_provenance trend offering real technical pathways, legal liability without technical feasibility is political theatre. We are therefore pausing enforcement actions under the framework and redirecting the European AI Office to fast-track recognition of the provenance standard as a compliance pathway under the AI Act, using delegated acts under Article 33. This is not retreat, but realignment: we will reward verifiable accountability, not punish its absence where tools are lacking.

Public sentiment remains fragile at 37.0, and the labour_displacement trend — especially in entry-level law and software — is feeding a narrative of AI as a job destroyer, not a public good. We cannot afford another automated_decision_scandal. That is why the Resilience Surge includes a public communications component: member states will report quarterly on preparedness milestones, and the Commission will publish a live dashboard of AI incident response capacity. Trust must be earned not through promises of control, but through proof of readiness.
