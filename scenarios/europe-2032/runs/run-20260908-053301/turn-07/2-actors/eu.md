## New measure  
**European AI Command Interface**  
A unified operational layer for EU institutions to monitor, task, and audit AI systems running on sovereign infrastructure, ensuring political oversight is not outsourced to algorithms or foreign providers.  

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `eu_ai_sovereignty` (+5–8) and `ai_safety` (+4–7) by enabling real-time interpretability, usage logging, and conditional intervention rights in AI systems serving public functions. Builds institutional muscle to act when openweight models are repurposed for systemic misuse.  
`Applies to:` EU agencies, national public services, and operators of sovereign compute infrastructure funded or regulated under EU law.

## Priority  
**Open-Weight Accountability Framework** — because the openweight_frontier_release has already distributed a model at 53.5 capability into the wild, and without liability in place, every day of delay deepens the harm we cannot reverse; this measure is the only brake left on uncontrolled diffusion, and it must land before the next jump pushes open models past supervised autonomy.

## In practice  
We are finalizing the **Sovereign Compute Access and Resilience Corridors** directive through the Digital Ministers Council, with the European Commission pushing for adoption under Article 114 TFEU despite Hungary’s challenge. The legal service has confirmed the internal market rationale for harmonizing compute access during systemic risk, and we are offering Poland a phased cost-sharing mechanism via the Just Transition Fund to secure its vote. Technical annexes from the Joint Undertaking now define runtime thresholds for grid stability and emergency medical coordination, ensuring that when a crisis hits, public services get compute — not just private bidders.

The **Open-Weight Accountability Framework** is in active consultation with the European AI Office, facing fierce resistance from open-source coalitions who see watermarking and takedown protocols as censorship. We are holding firm: the decentralized doxxing campaign proved that 48.7 capability in the wild is already at the edge of autonomous harm, and now, with a new release at 53.5, the window is closing. We are refining the threshold at 45.0 openweight_capability to exempt research, but above that line, developers must now register, assess, and monitor — or lose access to EU markets. A public campaign will frame this as digital civil defense, not suppression.

Simultaneously, we are launching the **European AI Command Interface** — a new Category 5 initiative to ensure that sovereign compute does not mean sovereign opacity. This is not about building more models, but about seeing into the ones we depend on. The interface will give EU auditors read-only access to reasoning traces, task logs, and energy use on public AI workloads, enabling real-time oversight and post-incident forensics. It will be piloted in the Commission’s legal drafting unit and Frontex’s situational analysis cell, with funding drawn from the now-completed InvestAI Gigafactories programme. This is the next phase of sovereignty: not just having the machines, but knowing what they do.
