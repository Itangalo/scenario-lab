### Capability Plateau, Open Surge

The AI frontier showed signs of strain as a major commercial release failed to meet internal benchmarks, and the price of top-tier compute dropped sharply—market signals suggesting the pace of breakthroughs may be slowing. Yet even as closed labs hit friction, the open ecosystem advanced: a powerful open-weight model, functionally close to the closed frontier, was released and downloaded hundreds of thousands of times within days. This narrowed the open-closed gap meaningfully, lifting `openweight_capability` to 47.0—reflecting strong diffusion and adoption, but within a trajectory of accumulated progress rather than a single-step leap.

### Biosurveillance Gains, Permitting Progress

The EU made significant progress on **Accelerated Permitting Zones**, advancing legislative approval and securing initial pilot sites for streamlined data centre and chip fab development tied to sovereign AI. While not yet fully enacted, the measure is on track to deliver long-term compute capacity gains. Simultaneously, the biosurveillance network became operational across 18 member states, with binding data-sharing agreements and auditable AI standards now enforced in pathogen screening. These advances contributed to gains in resilience and public confidence.

### Resistance and Rebalancing

Despite progress, local opposition in Spain and Poland hardened into de facto moratoriums on new data centres, citing environmental and sovereignty concerns. In response, the EU *proposed* the **Public Compute Access Programme**, allocating 15% of future Gigafactory runtime to regional hubs in exchange for siting approval—tying infrastructure to public benefit. Retraining funds were also redirected to ease administrative job losses in affected regions.
