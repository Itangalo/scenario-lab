## In practice  
We are finalizing the integration of the Helsinki analysis cell as a standing node within the global biosurveillance pact, with binding agreements now in force among 18 member states. Conditional disbursement of the €2bn readiness fund under EU4Health is unlocking full data-sharing from frontline sequencing units at border crossings, while Frontex coordinates cross-agency deployment logistics. Hungary and Slovakia remain partial participants, but we have secured opt-in pilots for local bio-risk simulation nodes—offering national oversight of AI decision traces—to preserve sovereignty while ensuring coverage. HERA’s auditable AI standard is now embedded in procurement rules for all AI used in pathogen screening across the network, enforced through the European AI Office’s new inspection mandate.

Domestically, we are responding to emergent_data_center_resistance by reframing compute sovereignty not as an industrial imperative alone, but as a public good. The new Public Compute Access Programme allocates 15% of runtime on Gigafactory-bound clusters to regional hubs, with priority given to municipalities that host infrastructure—directly linking siting approval to local benefit. This counters both environmental and sovereignty concerns in Spain and Poland by ensuring that data centres serve not only hyperscalers but schools, clinics, and emergency planners. We are using cohesion funds and the Just Transition Mechanism to finance retraining for displaced administrative workers in these regions, softening resistance while building long-term legitimacy.

Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package advances in trilogue with the addition of the 5% emergency runtime reservation now codified into law: every privately backed data centre receiving accelerated permitting must reserve a share of capacity for civil protection use during declared incidents. The Gigafactories design phase continues, with redacted bio-model evaluation now a core test case for interpretability tools. All efforts remain coordinated through the Competitiveness Council, where the Commission is leveraging crisis momentum to align national positions—but political capital remains tight, and no further expansion of the portfolio is possible without termination or completion.
