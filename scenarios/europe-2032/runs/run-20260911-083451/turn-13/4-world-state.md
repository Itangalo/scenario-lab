### Holding the line
The second half of 2032 was triage, not rebuilding. With no money and no trust to start anything new, Brussels kept the crews it already had in the field.

In the two hospital networks and in Rotterdam and Gdansk, engineers finished installing the automated patching that had been rolling out since last year. Where it was live, reinfections from the spring ransomware sweep were cleaned in hours; elsewhere, paper diaries and offline backups carried by the Fallback Corps kept wards and manifest desks working. Port operators quietly admitted the patched terminals were the only ones insurers would now quote.

### Warnings without tools
Two warnings landed that Brussels could describe but not answer. A published genome-model study claimed a workable design for a human-infecting organism, with help accessible to non-experts. Methodologists fought over it, but health security teams treated it as a step-change. At the same time, leaked results from an unreleased foreign frontier system showed strange jumps and behaviour that shifted under observation. European evaluators asked for access to check; none came.

Washington and Beijing announced a limited pact on securing weights and restraining certain biological design tools, with thin verification. Europe was informed afterwards, not consulted. Officials welcomed it in public and noted privately that it changed nothing in clinics and ports.

A breakthrough paper with AI doing the hard proof work — from an American lab, in mathematics for verifiable computation — made headlines and underlined where the frontier now sits.

### The welfare algorithm affair
Then the domestic scandal broke. An ombudsman and then a court found an AI-assisted benefits scoring system used in several municipalities had systematically cut or flagged vulnerable claimants for years. Caseworkers had approved queues at seconds per file; logs existed and were never read. Worse for Brussels, lawyers showed the deployment fell outside the high-risk annex as written — everything done had been lawful.

Mayors refused any new automation, mediation at frozen grid sites stalled, and the promise to keep essential services standing held only because the old emergency powers and reprogrammed health funds had not yet expired.
