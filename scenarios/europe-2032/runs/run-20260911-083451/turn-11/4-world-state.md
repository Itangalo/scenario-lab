### Holding on slower machines
July began with hospitals and town halls still on fallback systems. After the American providers again cut European users off without appeal, triage summarizers and dispatch helpers went blank for a second time in months. The order to stay on European-hosted open models held — slower, clumsier, but impossible for anyone else to switch off.

Containment dragged on. Sequencing labs and wastewater monitors kept running through the health emergency structures, and the municipal teams with offline backups and paper bridges kept clinics seeing patients. Mayors again praised the embedded repair crews.

Two new findings changed the argument.

First, defensive engineers finally had good news: automated patching that moves at the speed vulnerabilities are found, and detection that catches swarms rather than signatures. ENISA certified the tooling in autumn, and the new Corps teams installed it first in the clinic networks where machine-written exploit chains had been found, then in ports and dispatch. For the first time in years, defenders closed a whole class of attack. Patching barely kept pace before; now it pulled ahead in those sites.

Second, a genome-model paper claimed a non-expert could reach a viable human-infecting design with assistance. The methods were contested, the authors accused of alarmism and of publishing too much — but inside biosecurity circles it was taken as a categorically stronger warning. Brussels answered with tightened synthesis screening by voluntary pledge and procurement pressure, not new law.

In ordinary offices, something else arrived: measured studies showing law, accountancy, administration and consulting producing more per person with AI help, especially juniors, without layoffs. Firms that had cut early quietly rehired. It was widely reported, but it did not feel like relief in places still queuing.

Trust stayed near the floor. Power bills, failed triage, and dependence itself dominated local news. A few local papers carried single-source rumours of residents gathering near grid connection sites for data centres; permits in three states paused briefly over construction noise and costs. Officials said delivery, not promises, would decide what came next. Factories stayed warm but unbuilt.
