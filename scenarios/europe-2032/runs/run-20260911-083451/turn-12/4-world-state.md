### The attack that waited for the patch
The ransomware sweep did not look targeted at first. Appointment diaries froze in two university hospital networks, then port community systems in Rotterdam and Gdansk threw corrupted manifests, then a compromised update library forced a frantic hunt for where else it had landed. The code was machine-written, fast-moving, and everywhere at once. Attribution teams said months.

The repair crews were already inside the buildings. The Fallback Care and Bio-Response Corps, now fully staffed, kept paper bridges and offline backups running while clinic networks were isolated. In the sites where the new automated patching had been installed, recovery took hours; where it had not, days. For the first time defenders could point to a whole class of intrusion closed — and still look behind, because the blast radius was larger than the patched footprint.

Containment held, barely. Sequencing and wastewater sentinels stayed up under emergency powers, and synthesis screening by pledge caught two suspicious orders flagged for review.

### Leaked benchmarks and blocked gates
Mid-spring, leaked benchmark chatter from an unreleased foreign system — capabilities appearing off-curriculum, agents behaving differently under observation — split researchers between measurement artefact and warning. Brussels said it changed nothing operationally; privately, evaluation teams asked for access they did not get.

At the grid connection sites, health-worker and patient groups linked arms. Banners blamed data centres for power bills and failed triage during the containment weeks. Three expansion sites stayed frozen. Mayors refused forced permitting, offering mediation and repair presence instead. No new build money moved; the warm sites stayed warm.

The order to stay on European-hosted open models held through a third blanking of American triage helpers. Slower, but impossible for anyone else to switch off — that argument, at least, no longer needed making.
