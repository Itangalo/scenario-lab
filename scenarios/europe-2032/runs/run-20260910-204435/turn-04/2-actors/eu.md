## New measure
**EU Trusted Hosting and Shield Continuity Pact**
Stops municipal open-model hosting bans from fragmenting the hardening infrastructure by offering certified EU-hosted rental standards with ENISA monitoring in exchange for municipalities holding off blanket bans.
This is Preparedness and resilience work, and why now is that the September investigation turned hosting into the backlash frontline just as the shield depends on local infrastructure, and with a new open-weight frontier release now unrecallable we cannot harden uncovered networks if towns switch off the servers we need.

## Priority
EU Trusted Hosting and Shield Continuity Pact, because without halting the municipal bans our finished shield and equal-protection rollout lose the ground they run on, and our two-year commitment to withstand disruption without foreign permission fails first at home.

## In practice
We run this through DG CNECT and ENISA with the Committee of the Regions and the Justice and Home Affairs Council: a voluntary EU Trusted Hosting label — KYC on bulk GPU rental, logging to national SOCs, swarm-detection sensor pre-installed — funded from Digital Europe reprogramming, offered to municipal operators and private EU hosts. Signatory cities pause blanket bans while certification audits complete this semester.

M1/M2 gigafactory permitting continues on low burn via accelerated-permitting guidance to keep sovereignty alive without spending scarce capital, while M3/M4/M5 fielded tooling is operated by the managed SOC teams already deployed. We hold the US chip-for-alignment offer at technical-dialogue level, conserving capital for the hosting fight, and task Employment ministers to note but not yet act on the knowledge-work plateau until hosting stability returns.
