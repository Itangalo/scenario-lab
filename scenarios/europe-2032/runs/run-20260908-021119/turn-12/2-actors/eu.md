## In practice
We are acting under Article 222 TFEU and the existing framework of the Crisis Coordination Protocol to stand up an Emergency Democratic Integrity Protocol, mobilising €900 million from the Internal Security Fund and the European Peace Facility to equip national election bodies with real-time AI forensic tools and cross-border incident response teams. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) is tasked with coordinating a standing alert system, while Europol’s European Cybercrime Centre (EC3) activates joint cells with national intelligence services to trace disinformation vectors back to their source. We are fast-tracking mutual legal assistance agreements to enable rapid takedowns and counter-narrative deployment in affected member states.

This is not merely technical resilience—it is political survival. The defection of a member state to a foreign hyperscaler on terms that bypass EU law undermines our very claim to collective sovereignty. In the Political and Security Committee and the Justice and Home Affairs Council, we are arguing that democratic integrity is now a matter of critical infrastructure, no less than the power grid. We are offering participating states priority access to sovereign compute and AI auditing capacity in return for full cooperation. But we know this is a moment of maximum fragility: if we cannot show that the Union can protect not just jobs, but votes, then neither the reskilling compact nor the compute directive will survive the next election cycle. We are holding both mandates by refusing to let the first act of war in this new era go unanswered.
