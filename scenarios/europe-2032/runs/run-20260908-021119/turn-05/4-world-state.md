### The American Choice

The United States elects a new president who treats advanced AI as a strategic national asset. Access to frontier models is now formally tiered and subject to national security review. For the EU, this means dependence hardens into a structural constraint: compliance with U.S. terms is the price of access. The administration takes office next turn.

### Cyber Shockwaves

A coordinated AI-generated ransomware campaign cripples regional health administrations in three member states. The attack exploits zero-day vulnerabilities discovered and weaponised autonomously, evading detection through adaptive mutation. Defenders are overwhelmed. The incident confirms that offensive AI capabilities have decisively outpaced defences.

### EU Responds in Crisis Mode

The European Union activates emergency powers under Article 222 TFEU, launching the Emergency Resilience Reinforcement Programme. ENISA coordinates with national CERTs and the newly formed defensive alliance to deploy AI-assisted intrusion detection and automated failover systems across energy, transport and healthcare. Fast-tracked procurement allows rapid rollout, but patchwork implementation reveals disparities in national readiness.

### Sovereignty Under Strain

The Sovereign Model Development Initiative remains delayed—Extremadura’s first exascale node is not yet online. The Frontier Compute Access Compact falters as labs cannot provide the verifiable containment logs now demanded. France and Germany resist further pressure, fearing disruption. Legal efforts shift toward critical infrastructure mandates, but consensus remains fragile.

### Political Capital Erodes

With six large measures now in flight—including the new priority—the portfolio charge overwhelms the system. Public sentiment falls further as job losses in law, accounting and software mount. The cyberattack, combined with visible dependency and delayed sovereignty, fuels a narrative of institutional lag.
