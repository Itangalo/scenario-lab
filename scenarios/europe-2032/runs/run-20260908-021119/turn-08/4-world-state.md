### The Weight of Intentions

The EU pressed forward with the Sovereign Model Development Initiative, intensifying efforts at the Extremadura site and expanding red-teaming under the existing Emergency Sovereign Red-Teaming Expansion—both measures already in flight. Despite contingency funding and interconnect prioritisation, instability in the model’s distributed layers persists; convergence remains elusive, and no successful sovereign frontier run has yet concluded. The European Defence Agency maintains air-gapped control, but operational fragility deepens doubts about near-term autonomy.

The Commission began drafting a potential moratorium on public releases of models above 55.0 capability, exploring the use of Article 114 TFEU to justify emergency powers. However, no formal proposal was adopted this turn. Legal and technical consultations remain ongoing, with strong internal disagreement over proportionality and enforceability. Academic consortia and open-source developers have already voiced concerns, and protests have grown in cities hosting data centres, but the policy remains in development. As only one new measure may be introduced per turn, and the "Secure the Openweight Frontier" initiative is already active (incurring portfolio charge and proposal bonus), no new measure was launched. The moratorium proposal will require additional time to mature before it can be formally introduced.

### Fractures in the Front

U.S. export controls under the **CONSOLIDATION** posture have not yet directly constrained EU developers, as no mechanism for enforcement beyond U.S. jurisdiction has been established in this turn. While rhetoric has hardened and licensing reviews lengthened, the practical impact on model updates and telemetry access remains limited. The EU continues to rely on existing licensed tools and open channels, though delays are anticipated if bilateral coordination fails to clarify access terms. For now, no measurable degradation in capability or resilience stems from U.S. policy alone.

ENISA’s live-fire red-teaming revealed troubling gaps: early sovereign models failed to simulate sophisticated adversarial logic, and integration with national systems was patchy. However, no new vulnerabilities were exposed beyond those already known, and ongoing patching efforts maintain baseline resilience. Energy and healthcare sectors report steady monitoring but no escalation in risk posture.

Public sentiment declined further, driven by media coverage of job losses and the perceived militarisation of AI governance. The prospect of restrictive new policies, though not yet enacted, contributed to a sense of overreach. Political capital eroded under the combined weight of two large ongoing measures and the growing strain of the drafting process for future initiatives, but not to the point of collapse. The portfolio’s strain is evident, but cohesion holds as member states await clearer returns on sovereignty investments.

Openweight capability, while lagging behind the frontier, continues to rise through incremental integration of available model components and distributed training advances. With no physical disruption to open compute networks, and given the metric’s non-declining nature, it advances in line with global progress, reaching 63.5—narrowing the gap with `ai_capability` but not surpassing it.

The `eu_ai_sovereignty` metric fell to 18.0, reflecting the 1.0-point decay from global capability growth (+1.5 to `ai_capability`) and the additional 1.0-point decay imposed by the **CONSOLIDATION** U.S. posture, which continues to erode sovereignty gains in the absence of a demonstrated build-up of sovereign force. No positive adjustments occurred this turn, as no measure concluded and no event gate was triggered.
