## Priority

## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_test_shot as a wake-up call: not an isolated breach, but a demonstration of what AI-enabled coordination can do when detection systems are blind. This turn, we activate the Emergency Resilience Surge under the NIS2 Directive, using Article 21 to issue binding operational standards for electricity transmission operators, major ports, and water utilities. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will draft the technical benchmarks by December, with a focus on AI-resistant segmentation, anomaly detection using lightweight models under human oversight, and mandatory red-teaming of control systems. Funding of €12bn will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with 70% frontloaded to ensure deployment by mid-2027.

We face resistance from member states citing cost and sovereignty, particularly in Central Europe, where some operators argue that compliance would require full system overhauls. To overcome this, we are pairing the mandate with a fast-track approval process for national recovery funds to cover 85% of certified upgrades, and offering direct technical support from a new ENISA Rapid Response Cell. We are also leveraging the incident itself — sharing anonymised forensic data through the CSIRT network to show how deep the access was and how silent the attack remained. This is not about punishment; it is about survival.

At the same time, we continue to stand up the InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech sovereignty package. The first site selection for Gigafactories will be finalised in December, with Spain and Poland shortlisted based on grid readiness and cooling capacity. We are fast-tracking environmental assessments under the new Net-Zero Industry Act, but this requires Council agreement in December — a negotiation we are leading through the Competitiveness Council. The message is clear: sovereignty is not just about compute, but about control — and control means being able to defend what we build.
