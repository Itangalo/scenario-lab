### A Surge Against Silence

The EU responds to the global cyber intrusion with the Emergency Resilience Surge, a binding directive to harden critical infrastructure by mid-2027. ENISA drafts AI-resistant segmentation standards and mandates red-teaming, backed by €12bn in frontloaded funding. Yet early resistance flares: Hungary and Slovakia question the legal basis under NIS2, while Polish grid operators warn of delays due to equipment shortages. The Rapid Response Cell deploys to three countries, but coordination remains uneven.

### Frontier Drift

AI capability advances to 54.0 as leading labs compress release cycles further. The jump reflects not general intelligence, but deeper optimisation in agent coordination and simulation fidelity—precisely the tools used in the recent intrusion. Openweight capability rises to 45.0, pulled up by Kimi K3’s offensive cyber features now being adapted in underground forums. Despite the EU’s efforts, no safety gains materialise; the gap between capability and control widens.

### Sovereignty Under Strain

The Gigafactories site selection concludes with Spain chosen over Poland due to faster permitting, but protests in Extremadura delay ground-breaking. The Tech Sovereignty Package struggles to unlock private capital, as investors await clarity on energy pricing. With AI capability rising by 2.0 and no sovereign compute completed, eu_ai_sovereignty falls to 21.0.

### Political Costs Mount

The Resilience Surge becomes the named priority, but political capital erodes. The portfolio now includes two large category 4 measures and one large category 6, costing −8 per turn, plus −1 for the priority—totaling −9. No proposal bonus applies, as the cyber_test_shot, while serious, did not constitute a major incident under the rules. Public sentiment holds steady at 42.0, too ambivalent to lift capital.
