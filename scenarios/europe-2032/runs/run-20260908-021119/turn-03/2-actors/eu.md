## In practice  
We are closing the Emergency Resilience Surge with a final report to the European Council, documenting both progress and gaps. In Greece, we have secured temporary air-gapped control systems for water infrastructure, funded through the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s emergency clause. In Poland, the equipment shortages were overcome by redirecting ENISA’s stockpile from the Baltic Shield exercise, but legal delays under NIS2 remain unresolved—this will feed into the upcoming review of the directive. The anonymised intrusion logs have been published through the CSIRT network, and we are using them in a targeted campaign with national governments to justify the need for binding segmentation standards. The Surge ends not with completion, but with proof: we were already penetrated, and we did not know.

On sovereignty, we are doubling down. The Gigafactories in Spain are now under construction, with the first foundation poured in Extremadura after the Net-Zero Industry Act’s emergency clause cleared the final legal hurdles. Regional buy-in was secured through a side agreement on local energy allocation—15% of output at capped rates, as promised—and we are now in talks with three semiconductor firms about co-location for advanced packaging. The Tech Sovereignty Package is nearing final agreement in the Competitiveness Council, with binding energy-price guarantees unlocking €62bn in private capital. This is no longer just about compute—it is about making the EU a jurisdiction where frontier AI can be anchored, not just tolerated.

The new Frontier Compute Access Compact is our answer to the fact that we are blind at the top. The Sovereign Red-Teaming Network cannot function without access, and developers will not grant it without protection. So we are offering a trade: full, auditable access to weights and telemetry for systems above 52.0 in exchange for a fast-track deployment licence and limited liability for harms caused by verified-safe configurations. This is not a surrender to the labs—it is a condition of market access. We are drafting it under Article 16 of the AI Act, with legal opinions from the Commission’s in-house team and external counsel confirming proportionality. France and Germany are on board; we are bringing in Finland and Belgium as pilot hosts for the secure facilities. If they refuse, we will fall back to Category 3—restrictions on deployment without access—but we would rather build a bridge than a wall. The alternative is governance without eyes.
