## In practice  
We are pressing forward on three fronts, but the Emergency Resilience Surge remains our focus. ENISA has finalised the first draft of AI-resistant segmentation standards, now under review by the NIS Cooperation Group. The Rapid Response Cell is embedded in Poland, Hungary, and Greece, helping national operators conduct red-teaming exercises using lightweight adversarial models. We are releasing anonymised logs from the recent intrusion through the CSIRT network—not to assign blame, but to show how long the attacker remained undetected, how deeply access was established, and how conventional IDS failed at every stage. This is the evidence we are using to counter legal resistance: this is not a hypothetical threat, but one that has already succeeded.

On sovereignty, we are advancing the Gigafactories timeline despite protests in Extremadura. We have activated the Net-Zero Industry Act’s emergency clause to fast-track permitting, and are offering regional governments 15% of local energy output from the facility at capped rates to secure buy-in. The first construction contracts will be signed in April. Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package is being renegotiated in the Competitiveness Council to include binding energy-price guarantees for anchor tenants, unlocking €42bn in committed private capital. This is not just about compute—it is about making EU infrastructure *irreversible*.

The new Sovereign Red-Teaming Network responds directly to the stagnation in ai_safety. With capability at 54.0 and no independent verification of control claims, we can no longer rely on developer self-assessment. This measure builds on the European AI Office’s mandate, establishing red teams in four EU member states (France, Germany, Finland, and Belgium) with cross-border access to test systems in secure environments. They will operate under strict legal safeguards, with model weights never leaving sovereign-controlled facilities. We are funding this from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe, and will require any developer seeking EU deployment approval for systems above 50.0 to undergo red-team evaluation. This is not a moratorium—but it is a line: you do not operate here unless we can test what you build.
