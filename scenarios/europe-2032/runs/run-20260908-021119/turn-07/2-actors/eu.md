## In practice
The cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across public health records in Belgium and northern Italy, propagated via poisoned dependencies in open-source medical billing software — has confirmed our worst assessment: we are defending with tools we do not control against threats generated by systems we cannot inspect. The attack exploited a model-generated exploit chain that bypassed static analysis, and attribution remains uncertain six weeks later. This is not a failure of vigilance — it is a failure of architecture. We are acting accordingly.

We are treating the conclusion of the InvestAI Gigafactories programme not as a victory but as a warning: compute without control is not sovereignty. The Extremadura node is now live, but its first training run is fragile. We have placed the European Defence Agency in operational command, with binding authority over ETP-Cloud and EuroHPC JU resources, to ensure the training environment remains air-gapped and auditable. The first sovereign weights will be red-teamed by a joint unit from the BSI, ANSSI, and BfV, with results fed into a new sovereign incident response protocol. We are also fast-tracking a derogation under Article 114 TFEU to require that any AI system above 55.0 capability used in critical infrastructure must undergo sovereign red-teaming — a legal backstop if the AI Act continues to lag implementation.

At the same time, we are launching the Emergency Sovereign Red-Teaming Expansion to close the gap between our defensive posture and the threat. ENISA will coordinate live-fire exercises on live (but isolated) infrastructure in three countries, using attack models derived from the Extremadura run. This is not simulation for show — it is preparation for failure. We are allocating €8.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme’s contingency reserve to fund the expansion, and we are offering Poland a seat on the Sovereign Model Evaluation Board to secure Council alignment. The U.S. posture of consolidation has made one thing clear: no one will secure us but ourselves. We are building that capacity now — not for parity, but for survival.
