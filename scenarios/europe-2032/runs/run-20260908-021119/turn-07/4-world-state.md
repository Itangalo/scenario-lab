### The Shock of Autonomy

A major AI-generated cyberattack strikes public health systems in Belgium and northern Italy, exploiting a poisoned open-source dependency with a model-crafted exploit chain. Static defences fail to detect the intrusion, and patient records are encrypted in a coordinated ransomware sweep. Recovery takes weeks, and attribution remains unclear. The incident underscores a dangerous asymmetry: attackers wield autonomous tools the EU cannot inspect, let alone replicate.

### Sovereignty in Motion, Not Yet Realised

The InvestAI Gigafactories programme officially concludes, delivering exascale compute capacity at Extremadura. However, the node remains fragile—its first sovereign training run is still in early stages, and operational control is tenuous. The European Defence Agency assumes oversight, enforcing air-gapped protocols, but integration with red-teaming infrastructure lags. The new Emergency Sovereign Red-Teaming Expansion is launched, targeting energy, healthcare, and transport systems in Germany, France, and Poland. ENISA coordinates live-fire simulations using early sovereign models, aiming to uncover emergent failure modes.

### Political Strain Deepens

Despite the urgency, political capital erodes. The portfolio remains heavy: two large measures and a priority strain coordination. Public sentiment worsens as media coverage focuses on the cyberattack and job losses in administrative sectors. Protests intensify in response to the red-teaming expansion, with critics calling it a militarisation of AI oversight. The derogation under Article 114 TFEU faces legal challenges from two member states, delaying implementation.

### American Consolidation Tightens

The U.S. posture of **CONSOLIDATION** hardens, with frontier access now formally tiered. Telemetry and model updates from American labs are delayed for EU partners, slowing defensive adaptation. This structural dependency compounds the Union’s vulnerability, even as it races to build independent capability.
