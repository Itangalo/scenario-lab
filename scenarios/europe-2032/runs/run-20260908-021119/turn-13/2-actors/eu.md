## In practice  
We are acting under Article 114 TFEU (internal market) and Article 222 TFEU (solidarity clause) to establish the Sovereign Compute Anchoring and Democratic Enforcement Pact, a legally binding framework that conditions access to EU energy priority, public funding, and cross-border data flows on compliance with enforceable sovereignty requirements. The Pact will be negotiated in the Competitiveness Council and co-decided with the European Parliament under urgent procedure, with the Commission empowered to suspend national projects that outsource control to foreign entities without reciprocal oversight. We are leveraging the current investment collapse to renegotiate stalled siting agreements—offering fast-tracked state aid and grid access in exchange for legal anchoring of model weights and operational control within EU jurisdiction.

The annulled election has shifted the Overton window: even sceptical member states now acknowledge that uncontrolled AI infrastructure poses a systemic risk to governance. We are using this moment to close the loophole exposed by the bilateral hyperscaler deal, declaring that any compute facility receiving public support or operating at strategic scale must submit to EU-level audit, incident reporting, and restrictions on foreign data access. ENISA will be expanded to include sovereignty verification, and the Anti-Coercion Instrument will be updated to treat unilateral compute outsourcing as a form of strategic dependency that triggers collective response. We are offering early access to the first sovereign training runs from the Compute Mobilisation Directive as an incentive for buy-in, but the message is clear: we will not fund, power, or protect infrastructure we cannot control. This is not just about chips and watts—it is about who decides.
