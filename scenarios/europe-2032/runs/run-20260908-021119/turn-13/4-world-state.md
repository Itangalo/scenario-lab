### Strategic Reorientation Amid Collapse and Crisis

The global AI investment collapse sends shockwaves through the ecosystem. Frontier labs scale back training runs, and several Western compute expansions are scrapped, briefly slowing the pace of closed-lab advancement. AI capability creeps up only slightly, to 73.5, as existing trajectories complete but no major new ones begin.

In response, the EU pivots decisively. The Emergency Democratic Integrity Protocol is formally wound down—its cross-border detection cells deemed insufficient after an election was annulled due to information manipulation. Trust in coordination alone has eroded; now, control is paramount.

The Sovereign Compute Emergency Mobilisation Directive concludes this turn. Three exascale clusters are now legally anchored in EU jurisdiction, with secured energy supply and binding oversight protocols. This delivers a tangible boost to sovereignty, countering earlier fragmentation.

Seizing the moment, the Commission launches the Sovereign Compute Anchoring and Democratic Enforcement Pact. Framed as a defence of democratic legitimacy, it demands joint oversight for all strategic AI infrastructure, tying grid access and public funding to enforceable red lines on foreign partnerships. The measure passes the Competitiveness Council under urgency, though only after Hungary and Poland extract concessions on national data sovereignty. Full implementation remains uncertain.

Public sentiment holds at 18.0—deep hostility persists, with protests framing the new pact as technocratic overreach. Yet political capital rises to 15.0. The completion of the compute directive yields +3, outweighing ongoing portfolio costs (−9) and the priority penalty (−1). No legitimacy lending occurs, as sentiment remains below capital.

Resilience holds at 68.0, with no new defensive upgrades. AI safety remains stagnant at 14.0—no shipped systems reflect improved assurance. Open-weight capability stays at 71.0, as model proliferation plateaus amid the investment freeze.
