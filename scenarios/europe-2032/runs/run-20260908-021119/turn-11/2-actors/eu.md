## New measure  
**Accelerate EU Labour Transition and Reskilling Compact**  
To prevent social fracture from accelerating job displacement in cognitive professions, we launch a targeted reskilling and wage insurance programme for workers in law, accountancy, software, and administrative services, prioritising recent graduates and mid-career professionals at highest risk.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +5 (by reducing societal shock from structural job losses), public_sentiment +6 (by demonstrating responsive support), eu_political_capital +2 (if perceived as effective)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, national employment agencies, higher education institutions, and firms above 250 employees in affected sectors

## Priority  
**Accelerate EU Labour Transition and Reskilling Compact** — because the **labour_displacement** wave has crossed from forecast to fact, and with public sentiment at 18.0, the social contract is fraying faster than compute can be built; if we do not act now to cushion the fall, the political capital needed for sovereignty will vanish entirely.

## In practice  
The denial of frontier access has laid bare not only our technological dependence but the fragility of our social foundations. Hospitals may have reverted to legacy diagnostics, but graduates in Madrid, Berlin, and Vilnius are finding no diagnostics to enter at all. The legal and administrative sectors report 40% fewer entry-level hires than two years ago; software firms automate onboarding, not just coding. We are building sovereign compute, but if the people have no trust in what we are building *for*, it will not hold.  

We act through the European Social Fund Plus and the new Just Transition Fund for Digital, redirecting €1.8bn to fast-track national reskilling programmes in the hardest-hit sectors. The Commission issues guidelines for wage insurance covering 60% of lost income for up to two years, conditioned on enrolment in certified reskilling tracks in AI auditing, cybersecurity, and public-sector data stewardship — roles we are expanding under the Cyber Resilience Act and the AI Act’s monitoring mandates. We negotiate with member states through the Employment, Social Policy, Health and Consumer Affairs Council (EPSCO) to harmonise eligibility, and with the European University Association to accredit micro-credentials that stack into full qualifications. Resistance is thin but real: some member states see this as fiscal overreach, others as insufficient. But we have no choice — if the next election annulled is blamed not on disinformation but on despair, we will have failed both mandates.
