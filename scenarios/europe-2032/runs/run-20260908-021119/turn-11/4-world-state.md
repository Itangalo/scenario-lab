### Labour in Freefall, Response in Motion

Job losses in cognitive professions have crystallized into a visible crisis. Law firms report near-zero junior hiring; accounting departments run lean with AI auditors; software onboarding is fully automated. The absence of entry points for graduates fuels unrest, particularly in university towns. In response, the EU launches the Labour Transition and Reskilling Compact, a large-scale programme funneling €1.8bn through national agencies to fund retraining in AI oversight, cybersecurity, and public data roles. Wage insurance covering 60% of lost income is introduced, conditional on enrolment in certified tracks.

Despite broad agreement on necessity, implementation stumbles. Two member states resist harmonization, citing fiscal strain and sovereignty. Accreditation of micro-credentials drags as universities debate standards. Still, early sign-ups exceed projections, and public messaging frames the effort as a shield against structural collapse.

Meanwhile, sovereign compute efforts advance slowly. Legal challenges to the Emergency Mobilisation Directive persist, delaying site approvals. The Extremadura model’s limitations grow more apparent, with internal reports questioning its long-term viability. Yet resilience improves: red-teaming from the now-completed Autonomous Threat Simulation Corps enables preemptive patching of industrial systems, absorbing emerging threats.

AI capability climbs to 71.5, driven by closed-lab progress now nearing the terminal zone. Open weights follow, reaching 69.0, as prior frontier models leak into distributed networks. With no safety gains, `ai_safety` remains stagnant at 14.0. Sovereignty erodes further—capability growth outpaces domestic build-out, and U.S. exclusion under **CONSOLIDATION** persists.

Political capital dips to 12.0 under the weight of three large ongoing measures. Public sentiment inches up slightly, as the reskilling push signals responsiveness, but distrust remains deep.
