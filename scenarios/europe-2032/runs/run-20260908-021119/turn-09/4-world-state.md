### Sovereign Breakthrough, Fragile Gains

The EU celebrates a milestone: the Extremadura exascale node completes its first stable frontier-scale training run, producing a sovereign model audited at 61.0 capability. Full interpretability logging and air-gapped integrity checks mark a shift from dependency to autonomy. The model is transferred to ENISA and the newly launched Autonomous Threat Simulation Corps, which begins live adversarial simulations targeting energy, transport, and healthcare systems.

This operationalisation delivers tangible gains. The Emergency Sovereign Red-Teaming Expansion identifies critical vulnerabilities in grid protocols, while the new Corps integrates with an expanding defensive alliance now spanning ten national cyber agencies. Their coordinated response halts a live ransomware strain before it reaches hospitals, showcasing real-time telemetry and automated playbooks. Resilience improves as detection and response harden against self-evolving threats.

Yet progress is strained. The cyber_major_incident—a ransomware sweep across Belgian and Italian health systems—reveals how far offensive AI has outpaced static defences. The attack exploited poisoned software dependencies generated by a 64.0-capability model, adapting in real time. Public sentiment remains low, unswayed by technical victories. Open-source and academic communities denounce the proposed moratorium on high-capability model releases, warning of innovation suppression.

Political capital erodes under the weight of three large measures now in flight. The U.S. **CONSOLIDATION** posture continues to constrain access, though the EU’s new sovereign capacity mitigates some dependence. A member state quietly negotiates a bilateral data and compute deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, undermining the Union’s unified stance—a quiet defection that draws sharp rebukes but no enforcement.

AI capability advances to 68.0, nearing the terminal zone. Openweight capability follows at 65.0, narrowing the gap but remaining behind. The sovereign model’s success delivers a safety boost through early attack pattern detection, but systemic risks persist.

### Limits of Control

The Autonomous Threat Simulation Corps becomes operational, symbolising a shift to proactive defence. Yet staffing delays, legal challenges to its authority in two member states, and patchy integration with national systems reveal the limits of rapid centralisation. The EU moves forward—not decisively, but with enough momentum to hold the line.
