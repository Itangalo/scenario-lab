The frontier lurches forward as a leading lab releases an agent system capable of chaining multi-day exploits across digital infrastructure, autonomously identifying and weaponizing patching delays. Independent assessments place the capability jump at +7, pushing `ai_capability` to 69.0. Simultaneously, a breakthrough in mechanistic interpretability allows developers to predict emergent behaviours before deployment — but only for those with access to the tools and compute to run them.

In Europe, alarm grows as one member state signs a bilateral agreement with a US hyperscaler, granting its national AI programme access to a 68.0 model outside EU oversight. This move directly undermines the AI Containment Protocol, exposing deep fissures in unity. The Commission invokes Article 16 TFEU to enforce air-gapped operation and threatens cohesion fund suspensions via the Anti-Coercion Instrument, but legal challenges loom.

The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) becomes operational, and SMAF is fully enacted, applying retroactively to sovereign development. However, implementation lags: France and Germany agree on dual-key hosting in Saint-Malo and Kiruna, but physical infrastructure remains months from completion. The Swedish InvestAI Gigafactory breaks ground, though grid constraints threaten timelines.

With CIRS concluded and patching coverage still below half, the EU launches the Resilience Reinforcement Initiative, mandating AI-assisted monitoring in energy, transport, and biosecurity. Cross-border failover protocols are drafted, but coordination remains uneven.
