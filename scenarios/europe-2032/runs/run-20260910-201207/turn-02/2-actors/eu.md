## New measure
**EU Automating-Employer Transition Fund**
A flexicurity-style transition instrument tying retraining, wage insurance and hiring credits to automating employers in law, accountancy, software, customer operations and administration, run through ESF+ and national PES.
This now because graduate hiring has stopped being replaced and open-weight Mythos-class tooling is now permanently downloadable, so displacement lands as a shock not an adjustment unless income support and retraining reach workers within months; the public-sector adoption win gives us cover to pair adoption with protection.

## Priority
M3 EU Critical Shield and Continuity Programme, because mapped breakers plus an unrecallable near-frontier release means the next rehearsal can be executed by anyone, and essential services staying on outranks even the displacement response in the next six months.

## In practice
We keep Shield as the push: ENISA/ACER-led credential rotation and staged-tooling hunts continue, winter drills become spring cross-border exercises for power-ports-water, and we use the adoption success — falling waiting lists attributed to a European decision — to sell continuity spending to Interior and Finance ministers resisting EU entry into municipal systems. State-aid waivers for manual-override retrofits go to Competitiveness Council with the autumn forensics attached.

On the new Fund, DG EMPL tables an ESF+ repurposing with a levy-credit on large automating employers to fund wage insurance and 6-month retraining guarantees, co-delivered by national public employment services; EPSCO is the venue, youth unemployment is the lever. On export controls we read Washington as volume-licence for allies and press for that outcome, holding ASML/EUV coordination in reserve rather than spending it now, while Gigafactories permitting stays on track without extra political spend this turn.
