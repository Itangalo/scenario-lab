### Cut off
In February the notices arrived with no reason and no appeal. American frontier models that ran triage support, referral planning and grid-anomaly summaries simply stopped answering European institutional users. Hospitals that had built routines around them fell back to older models and paper in a day. Ministries called it a technical issue for a week before admitting it was a decision.

The humiliation landed harder than the outage. Evening bulletins ran ward interviews beside empty prompt windows. Opposition papers called it foreseeable dependence.

### The pact and the depots
Brussels moved where a door was open. Trade and space-industry officials signed into a coordination framework of middle powers holding lithography servicing, evaluation labs and spare compute — offering servicing slots and testing capacity in exchange for pooled inference quotas for health, justice and grid operators. At home, the Commission stopped negotiating with frightened towns: fuel, transformers and breaker-log stocks were ordered into military depots under existing manual restart procedures, with reimbursement continued and written guarantees on who pays if a depot generator burns.

Neither fixed the spring. Quotas were promised for late in the year, not delivered. Convoys moving guarded pools out of municipal utilities at night confirmed what mayors had whispered — civilian cover was gone. Showcase dashboards stayed live, but tours thinned and two more clinics asked to leave the scheme.

Defenders kept patching at machine speed, which prevented a worse winter. Capability abroad crept forward; assurance at home slipped again.
