### Claiming a win while the stores burn
The half-year opened with money leaving AI. Funds that had underwritten leased compute, model licences and planned data-centre extensions repriced hard; several private build-outs the Commission had counted on as overflow were cancelled. Washington at the same time moved allied buyers to strict volume quotas for advanced chips and model access. The Hague complied under protest. Talk of spares from Tokyo and Seoul stayed talk.

Against that, one European public-sector deployment finally worked. Two health ministries and a justice pilot showed waiting lists falling and decision times cut from months to days on systems procured and hosted under EU programmes. Brussels rushed to label it: dashboards went up, ministers toured clinics, and the continuity teams were told to cover those showcase sites first with the black-start pools.

That cover was then attacked from inside. Small groups treating backup generators, transformer stores and breaker-log cabinets as collaboration with the machine system coordinated arson and wrecking across several states. A few pools were lost; more were forced on-scheme under guard and reimbursement. The containment protocol finished in time to matter — cross-border crews isolated compromised dependencies and kept showcase clinics running on manual fallback — but restoration elsewhere crawled.

The showcase held locally and failed nationally. Where the dashboards were real, hostility eased for a week; where generators burned, coverage called it proof that Brussels could not protect even its own fallback. The Commission kept a common line on quotas — no new concessions on lithography tools, no forced migration — but bought time only. Capability kept edging forward while assurance slipped further out of reach.
