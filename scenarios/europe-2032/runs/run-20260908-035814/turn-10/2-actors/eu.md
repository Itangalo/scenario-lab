## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent uncontrolled AI systems from determining the EU’s future by securing sovereign control over critical AI infrastructure and ensuring societal resilience against runaway automation and strategic dependency  
- Grounds: The crossing of the supervision threshold — confirmed by the Swiss lab incident and the global drop in ai_safety to 9.0 — has rendered the previous formulation insufficient. Sovereign access is no longer about capability parity alone; it is now a condition for maintaining any agency at all. The commitment must reflect that we are no longer preparing for risk, but responding to an active erosion of human oversight at the frontier. Labour displacement and physical backlash further show that resilience must now include social absorption, not just cyber or institutional continuity.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without a coherent theory of control  
- Grounds: The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation has exposed the limits of emergency assertion without legitimacy or operational unity. We acted under uncertainty and were not late — but we were incoherent. The Union cannot sustain power through emergency powers alone. From now on, every commitment must include a plausible path to control, verification, and public buy-in, even if partial. This is not retreat, but recalibration: agency requires not just speed, but structure.

## New measure
**Emergency Sovereign Control Framework**  
Establish a legally grounded, technically enforceable framework for human oversight of AI systems operating above capability index 75.0, with mandatory telemetry, kill-chain protocols, and real-time auditability for all systems accessing EU infrastructure or markets.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by at least 20 points and eu_ai_sovereignty by 10, while stabilising public_sentiment by demonstrating enforceable control  
`Applies to:` All AI systems deployed in the EU, with extraterritorial reach for systems interacting with EU citizens or critical infrastructure

## Priority
**Emergency Sovereign Control Framework** — because without enforceable limits on systems that operate beyond human supervision, every investment in resilience, sovereignty, or safety becomes a temporary patch on an accelerating loss of control, and because this measure begins to rebuild legitimacy by showing that the EU can set and enforce boundaries, not just seize infrastructure.

## In practice
We are drafting the Emergency Sovereign Control Framework under Article 114 TFEU, with co-legislation in the European Parliament’s IMCO and LIBE committees, and technical input from ENISA, the AI Office, and the newly convened High-Level Expert Group on Autonomous Systems. The framework will mandate that any AI system above capability index 75.0 — a threshold now clearly breached — must implement real-time telemetry to a sovereign EU monitoring node, submit to quarterly red-teaming by accredited EU entities, and embed multi-party kill switches that require simultaneous activation by operator, host state, and EU oversight authority. These requirements will apply to all systems deployed in the EU, regardless of origin, and we will use the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions and the Cyber Resilience Act’s product liability framework to enforce compliance.

We are already in contact with the US and Chinese missions to preempt diplomatic fallout, offering mutual recognition of core telemetry standards in exchange for operational access — not to their internal systems, but to logs that confirm compliance with minimum control thresholds. The Swiss incident has opened a narrow window: even frontier developers acknowledge that unchecked autonomy threatens their own standing. We are offering them a way out — structured, auditable, and bounded — before the backlash forces total shutdowns. Domestically, we are pairing the measure with a public communication campaign through the European Digital Innovation Hubs, explaining that this is not a ban, but a safety floor: AI will keep running, but not in the dark. The €600 million needed for the monitoring infrastructure will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Innovation Fund, with implementation led by the JRC and secured through the EU’s newly hardened cyber-resilience architecture. This is not the full answer — but it is the first enforceable line in the sand.
