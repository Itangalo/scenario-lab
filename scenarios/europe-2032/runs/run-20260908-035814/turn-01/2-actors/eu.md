## In practice
We are acting on the intrusion revealed in the autumn: the fact that it was found by accident, not by design, exposes a fatal lag in our defensive posture. This turn, the Commission will issue an emergency directive under Article 14 of the NIS2 Directive, invoking the “exceptional circumstances” clause to fast-track binding security baselines for electricity transmission operators, water utilities and port authorities across the Union. ENISA will be tasked with issuing technical specifications within 90 days, drawing on classified threat assessments from the Joint Cyber Unit and the recently expanded EU Cybersecurity Competence Centre. Funding will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with up to €8bn mobilised in grants and low-interest loans for intrusion detection upgrades, zero-trust architecture rollouts, and red-teaming of high-risk nodes.

At the same time, we are pushing the Council’s Permanent Working Group on Cybersecurity to agree on a standing mechanism for cross-border incident response — not just information sharing, but pre-authorised access to each other’s systems during active breaches. Germany and France are on board, but Hungary and Slovakia have raised sovereignty concerns. We are offering them co-leadership of the pilot phase and a dedicated allocation for Eastern European grid operators to offset resistance. The goal is not perfection but speed: we need to close the window between compromise and detection from months to hours, because the next agent may not wait.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track. The first site selection committee meets in December, with Spain, Poland and Finland in contention. We are fast-tracking grid access agreements with ENTSO-E and negotiating with EDF and Siemens Energy to co-locate nuclear microgrids at two proposed sites. The bottleneck is not capital but permitting — hence the push to designate the zones as “strategic AI infrastructure” under the revised TEN-E regulation, which would trigger automatic environmental assessment overrides. Industry is watching closely: if we deliver by turn 3, private capital will follow. If we stall, the credibility of the entire sovereignty agenda collapses.
