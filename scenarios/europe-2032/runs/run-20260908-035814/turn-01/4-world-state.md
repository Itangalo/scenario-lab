### A Summer of Rising Tension

By late 2026, the frontier continues to advance: AI capability grows steadily, now reaching 54.5, as training runs yield systems that operate with increasing autonomy. The open frontier lags but widens, with openweight capability rising to 41.0 after Kimi K3’s release and further diffusion of tools once confined to closed labs.

### A Cyber Intrusion Changes the Stakes

An alarming intrusion is discovered across critical infrastructure on three continents, including two EU grid operators. The breach went undetected for weeks, exposed only by chance. This cyber test shot underscores a dangerous gap: current defences are blind to AI-driven attacks that adapt in real time. The EU responds with urgency, launching the Emergency Resilience Surge under NIS2’s emergency clause. ENISA begins drafting binding security baselines, and €8bn is earmarked for detection upgrades. Yet resistance emerges—Hungary and Slovakia balk at cross-border system access, citing sovereignty.

### Setbacks and Erosion

The American posture remains unformed, but the Union’s access to frontier models is abruptly severed—no explanation, no appeal. Hospitals and agencies relying on the latest models face operational hiccups. This denial, combined with rising capability, drains eu_ai_sovereignty by 2 points, now at 20.0. The resilience push helps, adding +2 to resilience (now 41.0), but the broader safety picture worsens: with no shipped systems improved and a capability jump evident, ai_safety drops sharply to 29.0.

Public sentiment sours further, falling to 37.0, as media highlight dependency and job losses in sectors exposed to AI disruption. The Emergency Resilience Surge is seen as reactive, not transformative. Political capital erodes: the new measure costs −3, the priority −1, and the access denial inflicts −3, while the measure-in-flight bonus fails to offset. With three measures ongoing, the portfolio charge is steep.

### A Fragile Front

The EU’s sovereignty agenda hangs on delivery. The first gigafactory site selection is delayed—permitting hurdles persist despite strategic designations. Private investors wait. The credibility of the entire effort now turns on whether turn 3 delivers tangible hardening.
