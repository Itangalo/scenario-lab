## Priority  
**Sovereign Access Acceleration Initiative** — because the unexplained denial of access to frontier models has exposed a strategic vulnerability that no resilience measure can compensate for, and without sovereign capability, all other defences are downstream of someone else’s choice.

## In practice  
This turn, we are acting on the hard truth exposed by the access denial: sovereignty is not a long-term ambition—it is a condition of agency, and we are operating without it. The **Sovereign Access Acceleration Initiative** will cut through normal procurement cycles by establishing a fast-track legal and financial vehicle under the Digital Europe Programme and the European Defence Fund, enabling the Commission to contract directly with a consortium of EU-based developers, cloud providers, and security agencies to deploy a sovereign frontier-capable system by mid-2028. We are authorising €12bn in emergency funding—drawn from unallocated RRF reserves and the EU Innovation Fund—with 70% committed to building sovereign training infrastructure on EU soil, 20% to securing model weights under EU legal jurisdiction, and 10% to establishing a red-teamable deployment environment with real-time monitoring. The initiative will be led by a new task force reporting directly to the Vice-President for Values and Transparency, with technical oversight from ENISA and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking.

We are also advancing the **Emergency Resilience Surge** with urgency. ENISA has issued the first draft of binding security baselines, and we are pressuring member states to adopt them by April. Germany and France have agreed to mutual cross-border access protocols during active incidents, but Hungary and Slovakia remain resistant. In response, we are offering a new formula: full opt-in grants covering 90% of detection upgrade costs in exchange for pre-authorised access during declared emergencies. We are also launching a pilot with the European Cyber Rapid Response Teams to conduct live red-teaming on two major grid operators in Q2, with results to be shared across the network. The credibility of our resilience agenda depends on visible progress by turn 3—without it, public and private confidence will erode further.

Meanwhile, the **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech Sovereignty Package** remain on track, though permitting delays in Spain and Poland threaten the first site selection. We are invoking the newly passed Strategic AI Infrastructure designation to override local objections, but legal challenges are expected. We are also in advanced talks with ASML, Intel, and EDF to co-locate EU-controlled lithography and power infrastructure at the selected sites, ensuring that the compute built here cannot be remotely disabled. If we do not demonstrate tangible progress on sovereign capacity by turn 3, the entire industrial agenda risks becoming a symbol of delay rather than deterrence.
