## In practice
We are invoking Article 122 TFEU — the same legal base used for pandemic and energy crisis responses — to declare a systemic risk to the Union’s technological sovereignty and security. This is not a request: Warsaw and Madrid will hand over operational control of their national AI compute clusters to the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking by June. We are redirecting €1.8 billion from the Digital Europe Programme and Recovery and Resilience Facility to cover final integration costs, grid upgrades, and staffing. The Commission is issuing implementing acts to bypass national permitting bottlenecks, and we are deploying a crisis cell from DG CONNECT, ENISA, and the Joint Research Centre to ensure technical continuity.

Poland’s resistance — rooted in fears of losing control over its Wroclaw data hub — is being met with a binding offer: full voting rights on the JU’s executive board and guaranteed priority access for Polish research institutions. Spain’s hesitation, tied to regional autonomy claims in Barcelona, is being addressed through a co-governance model with the Catalan government, backed by a €300 million innovation envelope. This is not ideal, but it is fast. Meanwhile, the cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep hitting hospital systems in three member states — is proving what we already knew: we cannot defend what we cannot run ourselves. The US is retreating, China is accelerating, and the frontier just jumped to 66.5 — but we still can’t audit it, can’t patch fast enough, and can’t act without begging permission from capitals that don’t see the cliff we’re on. This measure is the only thing left that might pull us back.
