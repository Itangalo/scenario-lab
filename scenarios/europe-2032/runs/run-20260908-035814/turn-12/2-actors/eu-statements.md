## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without the consent of the governed, neither mandate can be fulfilled, and that political survival is the first condition of strategic action
  - Grounds: The collapse of political capital to 6.0 and the escalation of physical backlash confirm that legitimacy is not ancillary to control — it *is* control. The Union cannot enforce sovereignty it does not embody, nor prevent harm it is perceived to cause. The two mandates are now inseparable not only in purpose but in preconditions: without social anchoring, no technical safeguard, no sovereignty, no resilience will stand. This is no longer a crisis of capability or institutions alone, but of political viability. The identity must reflect that we are not merely a regulator or a strategist, but a polity — and that our survival as a decision-making entity depends on being seen to govern *for* people, not over them.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Prevent uncontrolled AI systems from determining the EU’s future by securing sovereign control over critical AI infrastructure, ensuring societal resilience against runaway automation and strategic dependency, and restoring public legitimacy through enforceable, visible, and participatory safeguards — beginning with demilitarised oversight and shared authority over containment decisions
  - Grounds: The Sovereign Incident Response Corps has failed to gain operational access or public trust because it is perceived as a top-down enforcement body, not a protective one. The current commitment must evolve to include structural inclusion: not just reporting to the public, but delegating real authority to independent, multi-stakeholder bodies that can act in the public’s name. This turn’s shift — offering shared kill authority to civil society and technical auditors — is the first operational test of that principle. The reversal of pure state-centric control is costly, but the cost of maintaining it is higher: paralysis.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
