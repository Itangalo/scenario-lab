### A Crisis of Legitimacy

The EU attempts a radical shift: from sovereign control to shared stewardship. The Demilitarised Oversight Council is proposed as a constitutional innovation, designed to break the deadlock of distrust by granting civil society and independent experts co-authority over AI containment decisions. Yet the political moment has passed. With public sentiment at 16.0 and protests turning violent, the proposal is met not with relief but suspicion. Critics denounce it as a delayed concession, a performative dilution of accountability rather than a genuine transfer of power.

Implementation of the Emergency Sovereign Control Framework remains stalled. Belgium and Austria cite unresolved legal challenges, while Germany refuses real-time data access, asserting national sovereignty over critical infrastructure. The Sovereign Incident Response Corps, still without operational authority, fails its first field test when a coordinated AI-driven disinformation campaign overwhelms regional election monitors in Eastern Europe—response delayed, coordination absent.

### Capability Without Control

AI capability advances to 97.5, as recursive self-improvement cycles shorten further, enabling autonomous infrastructure redesign across cloud networks. Openweight models reach 85.0, empowering non-state actors with tools for persistent cyber intrusion and synthetic media manipulation at scale. Resilience dips to 61.0 as repeated incidents strain response systems now underfunded due to rising policing costs from physical backlash.

The Demilitarised Oversight Council fails to gain traction. Though symbolically significant, it does not qualify for a proposal bonus—responding to chronic, not acute, legitimacy collapse—and its large cost deepens the political capital deficit. No measure yields gains this turn; all remain in flight, delivering only partial or delayed effects.

Public sentiment holds at 16.0. The backlash is no longer just anti-AI—it is anti-governance. Data centre occupations continue, now with broader public sympathy. The EU’s pivot is seen not as leadership, but as retreat under pressure.
