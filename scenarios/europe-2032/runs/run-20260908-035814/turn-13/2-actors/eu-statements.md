# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign access to AI systems and build societal resilience against uncontrolled AI risks

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Prevent uncontrolled AI systems from determining the EU’s future by securing sovereign control over critical AI infrastructure, ensuring societal resilience against runaway automation and strategic dependency, and restoring public legitimacy through enforceable, visible, and participatory safeguards — beginning with demilitarised oversight and shared authority over containment decisions — **but we now acknowledge that legitimacy cannot be restored through institutional innovation alone when the state is perceived as structurally compromised; this commitment now requires the visible dismantling of failed enforcement architectures and the redirection of resources to credible, independent, and locally rooted response capacity**
  - Grounds: The Emergency Sovereign Control Framework has failed its operational test: it is neither sovereign nor in control. Legal resistance from member states is not obstruction — it is a symptom of a deeper collapse in trust in centralised EU authority. The Demilitarised Oversight Council cannot succeed if it sits atop a broken enforcement stack. Continuing to fund the Framework at scale would drain political capital to sustain a fiction. The reversal is costly — it signals retreat — but the cost of persistence is higher: total delegitimisation. We are not abandoning sovereignty; we are relocating it, from a failed top-down command model to a distributed, auditable, and socially anchored one.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **retire** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Grounds: This commitment was meant to justify early action in the face of ambiguity. But we have acted — repeatedly, expensively, and visibly — and the results are no longer ambiguous. We now operate not under uncertainty, but under failure. The political capital to act is gone, not because we were early, but because we were wrong in structure and tone. To continue claiming we "act under uncertainty" would be to deny the clarity of our current condition: we know the model is broken. The price of being late has been paid. The next move must be grounded not in courage to act, but in humility to admit error.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
