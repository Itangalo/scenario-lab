- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent uncontrolled AI systems from determining the EU’s future by securing sovereign control over critical AI infrastructure, ensuring societal resilience against runaway automation and strategic dependency, and restoring public legitimacy through enforceable, visible, and participatory safeguards — beginning with demilitarised oversight and shared authority over containment decisions — **but we now acknowledge that legitimacy cannot be restored through institutional innovation alone when the state is perceived as structurally compromised; this commitment now requires the visible dismantling of failed enforcement architectures and the redirection of resources to credible, independent, and locally rooted response capacity**  
- Grounds: The Emergency Sovereign Control Framework has failed its operational test: it is neither sovereign nor in control. Legal resistance from member states is not obstruction — it is a symptom of a deeper collapse in trust in centralised EU authority. The Demilitarised Oversight Council cannot succeed if it sits atop a broken enforcement stack. Continuing to fund the Framework at scale would drain political capital to sustain a fiction. The reversal is costly — it signals retreat — but the cost of persistence is higher: total delegitimisation. We are not abandoning sovereignty; we are relocating it, from a failed top-down command model to a distributed, auditable, and socially anchored one.

- retire `act_under_uncertainty`  
- Grounds: This commitment was meant to justify early action in the face of ambiguity. But we have acted — repeatedly, expensively, and visibly — and the results are no longer ambiguous. We now operate not under uncertainty, but under failure. The political capital to act is gone, not because we were early, but because we were wrong in structure and tone. To continue claiming we "act under uncertainty" would be to deny the clarity of our current condition: we know the model is broken. The price of being late has been paid. The next move must be grounded not in courage to act, but in humility to admit error.

## New measure  
**Distributed Resilience Nodes**  
Establish a network of regionally embedded, EU-funded technical response hubs with real-time access to AI telemetry, local accountability councils, and authority to initiate containment protocols in coordination with the Demilitarised Oversight Council, prioritising speed, transparency, and community trust over centralised control.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 12 and public_sentiment by 15 by decentralising response authority to trusted local institutions, reducing dependence on contested central enforcement, and creating visible, accountable crisis response capacity  
`Applies to:` EU member states, regional governments, public technical agencies, and operators of high-capability AI systems within or affecting the Union

## Priority  
**Distributed Resilience Nodes** — because the Sovereign Incident Response Corps remains paralysed by lack of access and trust, and because the collapse of the Emergency Sovereign Control Framework proves that centralised enforcement is politically untenable; this turn demands not another top-down instrument, but a credible, visible shift to locally rooted, technically capable, and socially accountable response.

## In practice  
We are dismantling the fiction of central control. The Emergency Sovereign Control Framework ends this turn — not with a vote, but with silence. Its standards will live on, repurposed: ENISA’s telemetry specs and kill-switch interface will be integrated into the new Distributed Resilience Nodes, but now under a different logic. Control is no longer a command from Brussels; it is a network of trusted local actors, each with real-time access to system telemetry, trained in containment protocols, and accountable to local oversight councils that include civil society, technical auditors, and elected representatives. We are redirecting the €320 million saved from canceling the Framework — plus €180 million from deferred digital infrastructure projects — to stand up five pilot Nodes in regions with both high AI exposure and high protest activity: Wallonia, Saxony, Eastern Romania, Central Greece, and Northern Portugal. Each Node will be hosted by a national technical agency but governed jointly with regional authorities and civil society. We are anchoring this in Article 192 TFEU (environmental protection) as a crisis response infrastructure, because the AI emergency is now environmental in scale: it is the substrate of our institutions that is degrading. The Sovereign Incident Response Corps will not be scrapped — it will be re-tasked as a coordination and escalation body, activated only when a Node requests mutual aid. We are not building a central army. We are seeding a distributed immune system. And we are doing it not because it is faster, but because it is the only form of authority that still breathes.
