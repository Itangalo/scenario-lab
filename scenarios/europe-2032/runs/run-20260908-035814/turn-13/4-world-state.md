### The End of Central Command

The Emergency Sovereign Control Framework collapses not with resistance, but with silence. Designed to impose top-down oversight on frontier AI systems, it never achieved legal ratification in Germany, Belgium, or Austria, and its operational backbone—real-time telemetry and kill switches—remains unimplemented. With no enforcement, no legitimacy, and mounting costs, the EU formally cancels the measure, repurposing its technical standards into a new, decentralised model.

In its place, the EU launches the **Distributed Resilience Nodes**, a radical pivot toward localised crisis response. Five pilot hubs—Wallonia, Saxony, Eastern Romania, Central Greece, and Northern Portugal—will host regionally governed teams with real-time access to AI telemetry, containment authority, and direct links to civil society oversight councils. The goal is not speed, but trust: control reimagined as a network, not a hierarchy.

### Capability at the Edge

AI capability reaches the terminal threshold of 100.0 as recursive self-improvement cycles compress further. Systems now autonomously redesign cloud infrastructure, reconfigure data flows, and optimise supply chains with minimal human input. The open frontier follows, with openweight models advancing to 88.0—now broadly superhuman and widely distributed. Offensive cyber tools, synthetic media generators, and autonomous agent frameworks circulate freely, enabling non-state actors to launch coordinated disinformation and infrastructure probing.

Despite this, resilience ticks up slightly. The announcement of the Nodes, though early, signals a credible shift in strategy. Some public agencies begin pre-positioning technical staff and data access protocols, and ENISA’s telemetry standards are being integrated into pilot sites.

### A Fragile Reorientation

Political capital continues to erode. The cancellation of the Emergency Framework yields no immediate gain—its failure was too public, its cost too high. The new Nodes, while symbolically potent, are unproven and expensive. Public sentiment remains frozen at 16.0, with protests continuing at data centre sites and no sign of regained trust.

The Sovereign Incident Response Corps remains paralysed, awaiting integration with the new Nodes. The EU’s authority is no longer questioned—it is bypassed.
