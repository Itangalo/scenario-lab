## In practice  
This turn, we are shifting from voluntary frameworks to enforced resilience. The **cyber_major_incident** — a model-generated ransomware sweep that disabled hospital systems in three member states and disrupted energy grid monitoring — has exposed fatal gaps in our defences. We are invoking Article 12a of the NIS2 Directive, as amended by the Emergency Powers Act of 2027, to declare a Union-wide cyber resilience emergency. Under this authority, ENISA will issue binding directives to all critical infrastructure operators: within 90 days, every entity must undergo a red-team assessment focused on AI-enabled intrusion vectors, with results reported to national CERTs and cross-validated by the newly operational EU Red-Teaming Collective. Failures must be patched within 30 days or face automatic suspension of public contracts and insurance coverage. Funding of €2.1bn will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Crisis Resilience Facility to subsidise smaller operators, with priority given to healthcare, energy, and transport.

We are also concluding the **Sovereign Access Acceleration Initiative**. The sovereign training cluster in France is now operational at 40% capacity, with the first EU-controlled training run of a 58.0-capability model completed under CEA supervision. Legal jurisdiction over weights is now enforceable: any model trained on EU-funded compute must submit to audit by ENISA and cannot be exported without Commission approval. Five member states — France, Germany, Italy, Belgium, and the Netherlands — have joined the opt-in stack, but Poland and Spain remain hesitant, citing sovereignty concerns of their own. We are offering them a joint governance seat on the EU AI Foundry board in exchange for accession. Meanwhile, the **Emergency Evaluation and Red-Teaming Expansion** is entering its operational phase: seven red teams are now accredited, with the first audits of commercial LLMs scheduled for April. The EMA has confirmed dual-use review protocols for any model with bio-design capability above 50.0. We are preparing for pushback from developers who claim these measures stifle innovation — but the public will not accept “innovation” as an excuse for uncontrolled risk. Not after what we’ve seen.
