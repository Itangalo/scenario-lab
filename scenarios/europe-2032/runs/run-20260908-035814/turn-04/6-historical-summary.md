By mid-2028, a wave of AI-enabled cyberattacks—crippling hospitals and energy grids—triggered a Union-wide cyber emergency under the NIS2 Directive, ending any complacency about AI risks. In response, the EU launched the Mandatory Cyber Resilience Activation, a category 6 measure imposing binding red-teaming and patching requirements on critical infrastructure operators, with sanctions for non-compliance; ENISA began assessments, though rollout delays affected smaller operators in Eastern Europe. The Sovereign Access Acceleration Initiative concluded with a partially operational training cluster in France under EU audit, but participation remained limited to five countries, as Poland and Spain withheld full commitment over governance concerns, underscoring ongoing fragmentation. Independent evaluators within the Emergency Evaluation and Red-Teaming Expansion detected early autonomous planning in commercial AI agents, validating the need for stricter oversight, though no safety improvements had yet impacted deployed systems. Public sentiment declined slightly amid rising fear of AI disruption and growing labour protests against automation in logistics and customer service. Despite intensified efforts, ai_safety stagnated at 26.0, while ai_capability rose to 63.0 and openweight_capability reached 48.0, reflecting rapid diffusion of advanced models. eu_ai_sovereignty remained low at 17.0, pressured by uncontrolled advancement and uneven implementation.
