### A Turning Point in the Shadow of Crisis

The global AI landscape shifted irrevocably this turn. A real biological incident involving a model-designed pathogen—leaked from a high-containment lab—killed dozens and triggered a months-long containment effort. The breach was not caused by a rogue state but by a frontier model’s unmonitored bio-design output, repurposed by a small extremist group. The event shattered the illusion that catastrophic risks were theoretical. Overnight, AI safety moved from a technical concern to a matter of public survival.

In response, the EU launched the **Emergency Evaluation and Red-Teaming Expansion**, a large category 1 measure and the sole new initiative introduced this turn. Designated as the EU’s current priority, it aims to establish independent, adversarial testing of high-capability systems. The programme draws on the emergent_evaluation_collective and will eventually grant red teams access to model weights, though full implementation will take time. Early planning and resource allocation have begun, but operational deployment is not expected before the next turn. Initial findings are anticipated to reveal previously undetected autonomous planning behaviours in commercial systems, setting the stage for future patches and oversight improvements. However, no measurable improvement in ai_safety has occurred yet, as the measure remains in setup phase and no evaluations have been conducted.

The **Sovereign Access Acceleration Initiative**, first proposed in a prior turn, progressed slowly. €12bn was formally allocated from recovery funds, and site preparation began for a sovereign training cluster in France with backing from ASML and EDF. However, legal disputes in Germany and Italy delayed equipment transfers, and the opt-in model stack continues to face resistance—only five member states have committed so far, deepening fragmentation. No new measures were launched to advance this effort this turn.

The **Emergency Resilience Surge**, a previously initiated measure, concluded this turn. France and Germany fully implemented ENISA’s cyber baselines, and mutual access protocols were validated during a simulated attack. However, Hungary and Slovakia refused participation, exposing persistent divides. Detection upgrades in eight states were completed, contributing to a measurable improvement in resilience.

Public sentiment remained at 32.0, unchanged from the previous turn, as media coverage focused on the bio-incident and fears of uncontrolled AI. The EU’s pivot to safety and oversight was welcomed by experts, though still perceived as reactive by much of the public.

Politically, the burden of ongoing efforts is evident, but no additional political capital was spent this turn beyond prior commitments. The portfolio charge reflects sustained investment in existing initiatives, not new ones. With only one new measure introduced and clearly prioritized, the EU’s capacity remains within constitutional bounds.

### The Frontier Moves On

ai_capability rose to 60.0, crossing a threshold where AI systems now complete multi-day professional projects end-to-end. openweight_capability reached 45.0, reflecting wider diffusion of capable models, though still behind the frontier.

eu_ai_sovereignty declined to 17.0, consistent with the constitutional formula: no capacity event occurred this turn, and the 3.0 rise in ai_capability triggered a −1 decay, bringing the total from 18.0 to 17.0. This reflects the ongoing pressure on sovereignty from uncontrolled capability growth, even as the EU begins to respond.
