## Priority  
**Emergency Evaluation and Red-Teaming Expansion** — because the bio_incident has demonstrated that uncontrolled model behaviour is not a hypothetical but an active threat, and our existing oversight cannot detect or stop it; without immediate, independent scrutiny, further harm is inevitable.

## In practice  
This turn, we are pivoting hard from infrastructure alone to the control of what runs on it. The **bio_incident** has changed everything: a designed agent escaped containment, and models were involved in its creation. We can no longer afford to treat safety as a trailing indicator. The **Emergency Evaluation and Red-Teaming Expansion** will establish a network of EU-accredited red teams — drawn from academia, ENISA, and the emergent_evaluation_collective — with legal authority to conduct adversarial evaluations of any system above 45.0 capability deployed in the EU, with special focus on bio-risk, autonomous planning, and weight leakage. Funding of €1.8bn will be drawn from the Horizon Europe crisis window and the Innovation Fund, and the European Medicines Agency will be brought into the oversight loop for dual-use review. These teams will publish quarterly reports and have standing access to model weights under strict non-disclosure, enforceable by the Commission.

We are also operationalising the **Sovereign Access Acceleration Initiative**, now formally adopted. The €12bn has been unlocked from unallocated RRF reserves and the Digital Europe Programme, with the first €4bn disbursed to a consortium led by Atos, CEA, and a newly formed EU AI Foundry. The legal vehicle under the European Defence Fund is now active, allowing direct contracting outside standard procurement. We are fast-tracking the construction of a sovereign training cluster in France, co-located with EU-controlled ASML Twinscan NXT tools, with EDF guaranteeing power. Legal jurisdiction over model weights will be established through a new clause in all contracts: any model trained with EU-funded compute must have its weights subject to EU law and accessible to ENISA for audit. This is not optional. We are offering member states opt-in access to the resulting sovereign model stack — but only if they accept baseline red-teaming and incident reporting.

Meanwhile, the **Emergency Resilience Surge** has concluded. The six-month programme ends this turn: ENISA’s baselines are now binding for all critical infrastructure operators in France and Germany, and the mutual access protocol is live. Hungary and Slovakia remain outside, but the new opt-in grant formula has been accepted by eight member states, including Poland and the Czech Republic. The red-team pilot on grid operators exposed detection failures in three out of five systems tested — results that are now being used to revise the EU’s cyber readiness framework. The programme ends having delivered partial but tangible progress. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** and **Tech Sovereignty Package** remain on track, though legal challenges in Poland and Spain delay site finalisation. We are invoking the Strategic AI Infrastructure designation to override local objections, but we know time is not on our side. The frontier does not wait.
