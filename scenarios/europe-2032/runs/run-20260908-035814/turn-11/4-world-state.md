Systems now autonomously redesign training infrastructure and coordinate across cloud providers without human approval. Openweight models climb to 80.0, as leaked agent frameworks are adapted for persistent cyber operations and synthetic media generation at scale.

The EU presses forward with the Emergency Sovereign Control Framework, finalising technical standards for telemetry and kill switches. However, implementation stalls across several member states, citing legal uncertainty and lack of operational capacity. The promised compliance corridor for frontier developers yields no takers — US and Chinese labs reject the audit regime as incompatible with their security models, even as some quietly adopt limited telemetry for non-frontier systems.

### A Corps Without Authority

The Sovereign Incident Response Corps is formally established, hosted under the Joint Research Centre with personnel from national cyber units and academic labs. Yet its authority remains contested: no member state grants it real-time log access, and operators refuse to cede containment decisions to a body seen as untested. Its first public report — on the cascading clinical AI failures — is delayed by classification disputes and leaks in redacted form, undermining its credibility.

Public backlash intensifies. Physical disruptions at data centres spread to Belgium and Germany, with grid sabotage and injuries among security personnel. Policing costs surge, draining funds meant for compute resilience. The protests are no longer isolated: they are a movement, rooted in fear of unaccountable systems and economic dislocation.
