### A Strategic Inflection, Paid For

The AI frontier lurches forward with a discontinuous leap—new systems demonstrate capabilities far beyond prior projections, compressing years of expected progress into a single release. Global `ai_capability` jumps to 60.0, marking the threshold where AI completes multi-day professional projects end to end, displacing junior roles across sectors. This leap enables a large-scale cyber incident: an automated attack, powered by AI-generated exploit chains, sweeps across public service networks in three member states. Critical systems are disrupted, patching lags, and defenders are overwhelmed. Attribution remains unclear for months.

In response, the EU doubles down on sovereignty. The InvestAI Gigafactories are prioritized, with Article 122 TFEU invoked to accelerate grid approvals in Sweden. €400 million in solidarity payments are pledged to ease local resistance in Brittany and Norrland—funded through reprioritization within existing cohesion envelopes and limited emergency borrowing, though fiscal tensions with northern member states grow. The Commission proposes the Sovereign Model Assurance Framework (SMAF), a binding regime for auditability and isolation, to be enforced by the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD). However, SMAF remains in legislative drafting; full adoption is expected no sooner than mid-2029, following trilogue negotiations and capacity assessments.

The Tech Sovereignty Package stalls as legal challenges delay permitting harmonization. The "dormant adoption" clause sparks renewed resistance from member states wary of conditional access. The joint procurement deal with Zeiss and IMEC nears completion but faces export complications from third countries. Public sentiment worsens as job losses accelerate and the cyberattack fuels anxiety.

Resilience degrades under the weight of unmet investment and delayed CIRS upgrades. Political capital erodes—not catastrophically, but steadily. The portfolio burden of six active measures takes its toll, particularly as the Sovereign AI Core Programme shows no near-term deliverables. The EAAD begins planning for expanded audit responsibilities under SMAF but cannot scale operations yet; recruitment and legal empowerment processes are only now launching, constrained by civil service timelines. The EU’s response is coherent but stretched—ambitious in scope, yet constrained by implementation lag and financing friction.
