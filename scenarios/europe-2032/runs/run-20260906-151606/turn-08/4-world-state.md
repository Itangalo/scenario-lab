### Sovereign Compute, Finally Online

The EU forces through fast-track certification of the InvestAI Gigafactories, overriding national delays with a binding Article 114 regulation. Joint EAAD-ENISA teams begin deploying the AICC’s monitoring stack across the newly activated compute, prioritising real-time agent surveillance using the French interpretability tool. The Sovereign AI Core Programme completes, delivering a technically sound but previously idle stack—now poised to run on sovereign infrastructure by turn 10.

### Escalation and Exposure

The US tightens export controls further, restricting mature-node lithography even for allied buyers under national security pretexts. This *export_control_escalation* directly undermines European semiconductor resilience and confirms strategic dependency. In response, the Commission prepares countermeasures under the Anti-Coercion Instrument, but cohesion falters—Poland resists any move that might jeopardise its bilateral cloud deal, weakening the Union’s stance.

### Crisis Governance Takes Shape

The new Mandate AI Incident Response Protocols enters implementation, requiring operators of high-capability systems to integrate with the AICC. However, full compliance is two turns away, and the gap leaves critical systems exposed. Meanwhile, rising capability outpaces safety: with no finished safety measure landing on shipped systems, and no breakthrough, *ai_safety* erodes further.

Public sentiment remains frozen at 25.0—neither the completed SACP nor the crisis response shifts perception. Labour anxiety grows, amplified by isolated but high-profile AI-driven displacements in legal and logistics sectors.
