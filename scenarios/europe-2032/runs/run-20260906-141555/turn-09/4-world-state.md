### The Core Ignites, the Public Fears

**Sovereign Core Breaks Ground**  
The Commission invoked Article 122 TFEU to redirect €50bn and launched binding contracts in Brittany, northern Sweden, and the Alps. Orano and Fortum resumed work under emergency procurement. The Core’s first phase is now operational in name, with auditability protocols active. Despite legal challenges, the Commission’s crisis framing held. Sovereignty gains +3 from partial delivery of a category 4 measure.

**Open-Weight Crackdown Begins**  
The new Monitoring and Containment Regime targets misuse of open models in elections and infrastructure. Enforcement begins against hosting platforms distributing high-capability weights. While diffusion cannot be reversed, deployment ecosystems face friction. Openweight capability rises only +0.5, trailing general AI growth.

**Resilience Inches Up**  
The Joint Threat Desk conducted its first live exercise, integrating data across energy and health sectors. Response coordination improved, though attribution lagged. Resilience +1.

**Safety Stalls Again**  
Runtime transparency failed against opaque reasoning. The EAAD’s post-hoc tracing tools work, but no real-time control emerged. No safety gain.

**Political Capital Erodes Further**  
Portfolio cost: three measures in flight (−9), priority (−1). New small measure proposed, but no proposal bonus—response to ongoing interference, not a fresh shock. Capital falls to 10.0.

**Public Sentiment Deteriorates**  
Synthetic media manipulation in multiple elections triggered outrage. The new regime is seen as reactive. Sentiment −3.

**AI Capability Advances**  
Frontier models improve in strategic reasoning and cyber operations. Capability +1.5.
