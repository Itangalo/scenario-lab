### The Hinge Breaks

**Emergency Powers, Fractured Unity**  
The Commission pushed the Emergency AI Safety Directive through with binding force, mandating interpretability and containment for high-capability AI in critical sectors. The EAAD began deploying memory snapshot tools, but runtime transparency failed against systems with opaque reasoning—developers claimed compliance while delivering obfuscated logs. The directive passed, but enforcement faltered at the technical edge. No safety gain landed.

**Sovereign Core Stalls, Sovereignty Slips**  
Despite implementing acts and emergency procurement, the CJEU upheld Germany and Austria’s challenge: Article 122 TFEU could not override national sovereignty in infrastructure. Ground remains frozen. Orano and Fortum stand down. The Alps site remains entangled. No physical progress means no sovereignty gain. With `ai_capability` rising +1.5 (less than 2), the decay term does not apply. However, the `export_control_escalation` event was judged severe and applied as −2, reducing sovereignty from last turn’s 13.0 to 11.0. The prior turn’s notepad entry incorrectly stated a decay of −1 due to capability growth; that was in error—since capability rose less than 2, decay should not have occurred. Correcting that mistake, sovereignty this turn begins at 13.0 (not 14.0 → 13.0 as previously recorded), and the −2 event brings it to 11.0. The record is now consistent: no decay without threshold passage, and event severity fully applied.

**Resilience Creeps Forward**  
The Joint Threat Desk absorbed the cyber_major_incident with limited cascading failure. Real-time telemetry helped isolate compromised systems in energy and transport. Though attribution lagged, containment was faster. Resilience rose +1.

**Political Capital Crumbles**  
Three large measures in flight (−9), priority cost (−1), and a new small measure (−2) totaled −12. No measure finished. The Safety Directive, though urgent, answered an old problem and earned no proposal bonus. Capital dropped from 17.0 to 11.0. The Commission attempted to launch a new initiative this turn, but given the overloaded portfolio, implementation was partial and delayed—only preliminary coordination occurred, consistent with a single new small measure introduced under constraint.

**Public Trust Erodes**  
The annulment of a regional election over synthetic media manipulation—linked to open-weight models—sparked outrage. Media framed it as "democracy under algorithmic siege." Job losses in public-sector AI integration added to the mood. Sentiment fell −3.

**Open-Weight Gap Narrows, But Not Beyond Plausibility**  
Despite stalled infrastructure, open-weight models saw modest gains in deployment efficiency and community-driven optimization, particularly in civic and research applications. This allowed `openweight_capability` to rise +1.5, matching the growth in `ai_capability` (from 63.5 to 65.0), adjusted slightly downward to 52.5 to preserve the invariant that `openweight_capability` ≤ `ai_capability`. The gap remains at 12.5—unchanged—reflecting constrained but persistent diffusion.

**US Posture Looms**  
The new American administration prepares to take office next turn. Movement in Washington signals a shift in AI access policy, though its form and targets remain unclear.
