## New measure  
**Resilience Shield Programme**  
A coordinated EU-wide hardening of critical infrastructure against AI-enabled cyber intrusions, with binding minimum standards and joint threat simulation exercises.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to +15 to resilience by turn 5; begins reversing the downward trend in detection and response capability exposed by the recent intrusion; establishes a standing EU-level cyber defence coordination cell for AI-driven threats  
`Applies to:` All member states, with enforcement through Article 114 TFEU and conditionality on cohesion and recovery fund disbursements

## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because without binding control over compute deployed on our soil, all other measures depend on permission from others, and the Kimi K3 release proves that openweight capability is already weaponising at scale beyond governance reach.

## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that compute is power, and that power cannot be outsourced if sovereignty is to mean anything. This turn, the Commission advances the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive through trilogue negotiations with the Council and Parliament. The legal basis remains Article 114 TFEU, and we are holding firm on the 30% reservation threshold. To break the deadlock with Germany and Ireland, we are offering a phased rollout: operators have six months from entry into force to comply, with priority access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund unlocked upon submission of a binding compliance plan. The liability shield is now tied to third-party certification of security practices, administered by ENISA, to prevent abuse. The European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) is being staffed with civil servants seconded from member states and technical experts from publicly funded research institutes; its first mandate is to publish the auction framework for reserved capacity by the end of this turn.

At the same time, we are responding to the intrusion that slipped through existing defences by launching the Resilience Shield Programme. This is not a reaction in panic — it is a necessary correction to our earlier imbalance. We have prioritised sovereignty, but the breach revealed that even sovereign systems are fragile if the environment around them is soft. The programme will impose mandatory AI-enabled intrusion detection systems on all operators of essential services, require quarterly cross-border red-teaming exercises coordinated by ENISA, and establish a rapid-response cyber reserve drawn from public-sector AI talent and vetted private experts. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with non-compliant member states facing suspension of cohesion transfers. This is how we close the loop: sovereignty without resilience is just a more secure way to fail when the attack comes. We are not retreating from our two-year commitment — we are making it durable.
