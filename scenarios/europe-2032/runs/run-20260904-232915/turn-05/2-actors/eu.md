## New measure
**Public Compute Obligation Expansion**  
Expand the 5% public-interest compute reservation to require all EU-backed data centres to run audited, open-protocol AI agents for civic services, with real-time transparency and third-party verification.

## In practice
This turn, we are closing the Emergency Resilience Activation for Democratic Infrastructure with urgency: ENISA has delivered the detection toolkits, and we are now enforcing integration through the Digital Europe Programme and cohesion conditionality. Member states are required to report full deployment by December, with non-compliance triggering immediate review of cohesion fund access. We are working through the Justice and Home Affairs Council to establish legal interoperability, and through the European Centre of Excellence for Countering Hybrid Threats to train national teams. The goal is not just detection, but deterrence: a signal that AI-enabled attacks on democracy will be met with binding, material consequences.

At the same time, we are advancing the Sovereignty through Legitimacy Initiative with new teeth. The 5% public compute obligation is no longer symbolic: we are requiring that reserved runtime be used for audited civic AI agents—climate risk forecasting, public health triage, regulatory compliance automation—running on open protocols with verifiable logs. The EDIC will audit usage quarterly, and any operator found circumventing the obligation will face disbursement suspension. In Ireland, we are negotiating directly with the grid operator EirGrid to co-design load-balancing solutions, offering additional green energy incentives in exchange for project continuity. This is not about building faster than others, but about building differently—sovereignty as service, not just speed.
