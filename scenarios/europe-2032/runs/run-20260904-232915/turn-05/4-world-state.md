### The Weight of Finishing
The Resilience Shield Programme and the Emergency Resilience Activation for Democratic Infrastructure both conclude on schedule. ENISA certifies deployment of detection systems across all electoral commissions, public broadcasters, and high courts, marking a rare on-time delivery. Joint threat simulations reveal gaps in judicial response protocols, but the baseline is now in place. The activation’s completion delivers a tangible boost to institutional confidence, even as public debate over the annulled election remains polarised.

### Building Legitimacy, Not Just Compute
The Public Compute Obligation Expansion is launched, mandating audited civic AI agents on reserved compute. While symbolically powerful, implementation is patchy: only two of five Gigafactory sites commit to the new standard, and EDIC audits are delayed by legal disputes over verification protocols. The initiative strengthens the narrative of AI in public service, but tangible sovereignty gains are deferred.

### A Fragile Alliance
The U.S. election concludes with a victory for the candidate favouring transatlantic cooperation. Though the new administration takes office next turn, early signals suggest structured access to frontier models for allied institutions. This eases immediate pressure, but deepens internal EU debate: should sovereignty investments continue at pace if external access is reliable?

### Capability Creeps, Safety Slips
AI capability advances to 59.0 on sustained efficiency gains. No new safety frameworks land on deployed systems, and reports of unmonitored agent behaviour in hybrid threat environments trigger internal alarms. With no finished safety measure, ai_safety drops to 21.0. openweight_capability rises to 48.0, nearing the current closed frontier.

### Political Capital Holds—Barely
Despite four large measures in flight, political capital stabilises. The completion of two resilience measures provides a morale boost, partially offsetting the strain.
