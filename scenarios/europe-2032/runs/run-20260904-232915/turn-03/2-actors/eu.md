## New measure  
**Sovereignty through Legitimacy Initiative**  
A package of local benefit-sharing agreements, environmental safeguards, and democratic oversight mechanisms tied to the permitting of AI infrastructure, designed to defuse public opposition and accelerate stalled projects.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Restores momentum to sovereign compute deployment by addressing the root causes of local resistance; increases public sentiment by +8 to +12 by turn 6 if implemented credibly; enables faster rollout of InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package by reducing permitting delays  
`Applies to:` Member states hosting proposed or approved AI data centres, with funding and permitting conditionality tied to compliance

## Priority  
**Sovereignty through Legitimacy Initiative** — because the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive has failed to pass and public infrastructure is now being physically blocked, we cannot secure sovereignty by fiat; we must rebuild the social licence to build, or our industrial ambitions will remain grounded.

## In practice  
We are acting on the recognition that sovereignty is not just about compute on paper — it is about what can actually be built, operated, and defended within the EU’s political and social fabric. This turn, with the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive failing to pass, we are shifting from compulsion to co-optation. The Sovereignty through Legitimacy Initiative will tie access to InvestAI funding and fast-track permitting to binding local agreements: data centres must provide community energy rebates, water recycling plans, job pipelines for local workers, and real-time environmental monitoring dashboards accessible to the public. The European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) will administer these conditions, with enforcement linked to disbursement of cohesion and recovery funds. We are piloting this in three regions where projects are stalled — eastern Germany, western Poland, and southern Ireland — offering national governments a way to say yes without appearing to override local will.

At the same time, we are maintaining pressure on resilience. The Resilience Shield Programme is now in its second turn: ENISA has published draft detection standards and is coordinating the first cross-border red-teaming exercise between French, Dutch, and Finnish critical infrastructure operators. We are using the Digital Europe Programme to co-fund AI intrusion detection systems in healthcare and energy grids, with deployment tied to quarterly audits. Member states resisting the mandate are being reminded that cohesion transfers are conditional on compliance — but we are also listening. The backlash against data centres has taught us that coercion without consent fails twice: first in the streets, then in the systems. We are not abandoning our two-year commitment — we are grounding it in reality.
