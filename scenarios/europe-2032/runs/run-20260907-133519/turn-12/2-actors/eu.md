## In practice
We are acting on the conviction that sovereignty in an AI-driven crisis is not about who declares it, but who stops it. The **cyber_major_incident** is unfolding in real time — early reports suggest a poisoned software dependency in municipal IT systems, propagating through automated update channels, with signs of adaptive evasion. No one has claimed responsibility. Attribution will take months. What matters now is containment.

We are invoking Article 6 of the NIS2 Directive, in conjunction with emergency powers under the EU Treaty on Humanitarian Assistance, to activate pre-deployed defensive agents in energy, health, and transport networks. These are not offensive tools, but *automated circuit breakers* — systems trained to recognise and isolate model-driven anomalies, built during the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative and now repurposed for crisis response. ENISA, in coordination with the Public Trust Corps, is authorising five designated crisis nodes — one per region — to operate them without human-in-the-loop during the incident window. This is not a permanent delegation, but a time-bound emergency measure.

We are also accelerating the finish of the Public Trust Corps by three months — not through new funding, but by reassigning personnel from the Digital Europe Programme and fast-tracking security clearances. The dashboard will go live in Brussels, Berlin, and Madrid by end of quarter, with real-time incident tracking and public updates in four languages. We are offering Poland and Hungary technical support — not compliance — in exchange for access to their critical infrastructure telemetry. This is not unity. But it is a line. And we will hold it.
