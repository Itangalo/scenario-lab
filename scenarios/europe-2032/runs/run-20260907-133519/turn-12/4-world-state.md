### The Lights Go Out

A model-generated cyber intrusion, exploiting a poisoned dependency in municipal IT systems, cascaded across energy and transport networks in three member states. Defenders were overwhelmed; patches failed to propagate before adaptation. The attack’s signature — fast-evolving payloads, AI-driven evasion — confirmed that offensive capability now outpaces human-mediated response. Public trust, already fragile, eroded further.

### Emergency in Real Time

The EU invoked emergency powers under the NIS2 Directive, activating pre-deployed defensive agents in designated crisis nodes across energy, health, and transport. These machine-speed systems, developed under the now-completed Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative, began isolating compromised nodes without human escalation. Initial containment was effective: cascades were halted in two regions. But in one, an overzealous circuit breaker triggered a temporary blackout, inflaming public anger.

### Fragmentation Under Fire

Poland and Hungary rejected EU-coordinated response protocols, citing sovereignty concerns. Italy delayed activation, citing legal ambiguity. Only Germany, France, and the Benelux countries fully engaged. The Public Trust Corps, still not fully staffed, launched its dashboard in Brussels, Berlin, and Madrid — but with delayed telemetry and no access to Eastern member states. Communications were clear, but patchy.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Crumbles

`ai_capability` rose to 70.5, driven by real-world validation of offensive models. `openweight_capability` followed, closing slightly to 59.0. But `ai_safety` plummeted to 2.0 — the gap between offensive power and defensive assurance had widened catastrophically. The activation of autonomous defences, while necessary, introduced new opacity.

`resilience` increased by +3, as the emergency protocols absorbed part of the blow. But `eu_ai_sovereignty` fell to 5.0, as capability advanced by more than 2 points with no offsetting capacity gain. `eu_political_capital` remained stagnant at 5.0, weighed down by portfolio costs and no finishing bonus. A new measure earned no proposal bonus — it responded to an ongoing incident, not a past one. `public_sentiment` dropped to 5.0, as visible failures deepened public hostility.
