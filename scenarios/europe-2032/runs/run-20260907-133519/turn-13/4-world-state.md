### A Surge in Capability, a Silence in Trust

The open-weight release of a near-frontier model—within months of the closed frontier—sent shockwaves through the global AI landscape. Defensive and offensive capabilities alike were now permanently distributed, accelerating the spread of machine-speed threats. `ai_capability` rose to 72.0, driven by rapid iteration in real-world cyber operations, while `openweight_capability` increased to 61.5, reflecting early but incomplete diffusion of the new model. The gap between open and closed frontier narrowed, but did not close abruptly—capabilities are spreading, not leaping.

Despite the EU’s emergency activation of autonomous defences, the blackout caused by an overzealous system eroded confidence further. Public sentiment remained at rock bottom—5.0—as visible failures mounted and no meaningful recovery narrative emerged. The newly operational Public Trust Corps struggled to gain traction, limited by incomplete data sharing and political resistance.

### Fragmentation and Response

The EU launched the Real-Time Assurance Layer for Autonomous Defences, a large category 5 measure aimed at embedding interpretability and human oversight into all sovereign AI defences. However, with `eu_political_capital` at just 5.0, the initiative strained an already overloaded portfolio. The measure was officially prioritised, but implementation lagged, especially in member states that rejected central coordination.

Poland and Hungary declined to adopt the assurance framework, framing it as an infringement on national security autonomy. Italy delayed integration pending legal review. Only core states moved quickly, creating a patchwork of compliance.

### Coalition and Cost

The formation of a middle-power coalition on supply-chain coordination bolstered diplomatic leverage and counted as securing EU access to critical compute. This provided a modest political capital boost. However, the rise in `ai_capability` by 1.5 points, with no offsetting sovereignty gain, triggered a −1 decay. Worse, the lack of sovereign compute or frontier access meant continued structural dependence, dragging `eu_ai_sovereignty` down to 3.0.

`ai_safety` remained stagnant at 2.0—no improvement had landed on deployed systems. Resilience held at 44.0, absorbing some harm but unable to offset deeper systemic fragilities.
