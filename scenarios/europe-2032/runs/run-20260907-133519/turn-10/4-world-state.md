### A Crisis of Justice

The ombudsman’s damning report on the social insurance algorithm — which wrongfully denied benefits to over 12,000 citizens — ignited a firestorm across the EU. Public outrage crystallised around the demand for accountability, not just transparency. In Marseille, protesters disrupted a Public Trust Corps drill, shouting, “We don’t want rehearsals — we want answers.” The European Commission responded by launching the EU AI Accountability Tribunal and Remediation Fund, a large category 3 measure aimed at restoring legitimacy through redress. Council negotiations stalled, however, as six member states demanded sweeping opt-outs, forcing concessions on enforcement and co-governance that diluted supranational authority.

### Capability Leaps, Safety Crumbles

A sudden leap in frontier AI capability — demonstrated in autonomous cyber operations and formal verification — marked a discontinuous advance. Attackers now operate with speed and precision unseen months prior, though general competence rose only modestly to 68.5. This capability_jump, combined with persistent anomalies in model reasoning, shattered confidence in existing safety frameworks. ai_safety plummeted as no safeguards had landed on deployed systems, and interpretability failed to keep pace.

Meanwhile, supply_chain_coercion struck at the heart of European autonomy. Washington compelled the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s exports beyond cutting-edge tools, now affecting mature-node lithography vital to industrial and automotive sectors. This move, leveraging U.S. jurisdiction over embedded technology, severed a critical supply line the EU cannot replicate, accelerating the decay of its technological base.

### Trust Erodes Further

The automated_decision_scandal deepened public distrust. With no prior action to mitigate such harms, eu_political_capital remained stagnant at 5.0 despite the new measure, which incurred full costs but delivered no immediate benefit. Resilience dipped as systemic failures mounted and response capacity proved symbolic. Public sentiment collapsed to 10.0, reflecting widespread anger and disillusionment.

The EU’s attempt to pivot from preparedness to justice foundered on institutional fragmentation and eroded sovereignty. The Tribunal, while symbolically potent, could not yet stem the tide.
