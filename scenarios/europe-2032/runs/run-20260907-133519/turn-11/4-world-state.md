### A Verification Floor, Not a Moratorium

The EU moved decisively to halt AI deployments in clinical coding, structural certification, and public contract review where automated verification is absent. Invoking Article 5 of the AI Act and CJEU precedent on algorithmic opacity, the Commission issued an implementing act mandating human-in-the-loop for these domains until technical standards are ready. The EIB aligned lending with compliance, and the Accountability Tribunal was empowered to treat bypasses as obstruction. The move was framed as preventive sovereignty: if it can’t be verified, it can’t be governed.

Yet enforcement fractured. Germany and France accepted the framework, but Poland and Hungary declared it an overreach, continuing AI-assisted clinical coding under national exemptions. Italy delayed implementation, citing workforce shortages. The Health and Justice Councils avoided a formal veto, but consensus eroded. The Public Trust Corps expanded its dashboard to ten member states and conducted drills in Lisbon and Vilnius, but its authority remained advisory.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

`ai_capability` rose by +1.0, driven by **verification_widens** — automated checks in clinical coding and contract review enabled rapid fine-tuning of high-stakes systems, accelerating deployment cycles. This was not a jump in raw intelligence, but in deployable reliability. `openweight_capability` followed, closing the gap slightly to 58.0.

No safety improvements landed on deployed systems. The new verification floor is prospective and not yet operational; existing opaque systems remain in use where enforcement lags. `ai_safety` held at 7.0, still reflecting systemic blind spots.

### Resilience Gains, Sovereignty Slips

The completion of the **Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative** boosted `resilience` by +3, hardening Tier 1 infrastructure against autonomous threats. However, `eu_ai_sovereignty` fell to 6.0, as `ai_capability` rose by more than 2 since turn 9, costing −1, with no offsetting capacity event. The US posture of **RETRENCHMENT** persists, limiting spillover gains.

`eu_political_capital` remained at 5.0. The portfolio charge was −7 (two large measures, one priority), with no finishing bonus (only Cyber Shield finished, but its effect was already priced in previous turns). The new measure earned no proposal bonus — it responds to a capability shift, not a past incident. Public sentiment held at rock bottom.
