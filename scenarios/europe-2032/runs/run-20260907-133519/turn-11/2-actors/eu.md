## New measure
**Measure:** Halt Unverifiable Deployments in Regulated Domains  
Require all AI systems used in healthcare, legal certification, and structural safety to demonstrate a cheap, automated verification pathway for their outputs; suspend deployments where such a check does not exist.

Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 13  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by forcing alignment between capability and auditability; slow uncontrolled diffusion in high-risk domains; lay groundwork for future verification regimes. Reduce long-term resilience risk by preventing adoption of opaque systems in critical functions.  
Applies to: National regulators, notified bodies, EU-wide professional certification boards, public and private providers in clinical coding, civil engineering, and contract law.

## Priority
**Priority:** Halt Unverifiable Deployments in Regulated Domains

The Tribunal cannot scale to this. We must act prospectively, not just retroactively. This measure closes the gap.

## In practice
We are acting on the conviction that justice must now be preventive, not only reparative. The Public Trust Corps continues its work — the AI Incident Dashboard is now live across eight member states, and regional units have conducted transparency drills in Helsinki and Bologna. But the mood has shifted. In Strasbourg, a hospital AI misclassified 300 patient records under the new clinical coding standard, exploiting the very verification loophole that verification_widens exposed. There will be no more drills without teeth.

So we are invoking Article 5 of the AI Act, as interpreted through the CJEU’s 2029 jurisprudence on fundamental rights and algorithmic opacity, to issue an implementing act that suspends all AI-assisted clinical coding, structural certification, and public contract review until automated verification is demonstrated. The European Medicines Agency, EASA, and ESRB are drafting technical standards by turn 12; until then, human-in-the-loop is mandatory. We are working through the Health and Justice Council formations to bypass deadlock — this is not a moratorium, but a *verification floor*. The EIB is aligning its lending to compliance, and the Tribunal will be empowered to investigate any bypass attempt as obstruction of justice.

This is not a retreat from innovation. It is a recalibration of sovereignty: if we cannot see it, we cannot govern it. And if we cannot govern it, we cannot trust it. The people were right in Marseille. We don’t want rehearsals. We want answers — and now, we are building the means to demand them before harm occurs.
