## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing  
- Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** and **us_china_agreement** events, combined with the collapse of frontier access and the erosion of political capital, confirm that our two mandates are now in structural conflict. Preventing lasting harm requires restraint, verification, and inclusion in great-power risk governance — yet the US-China agreement excludes us, and our own institutions lack the credibility to shape norms. Meanwhile, self-determination demands capability, speed, and sovereignty — but every step toward building it widens the gap with safety, as openweight capability rises and our own defences falter. This is no longer a balance to be struck; it is a contradiction we must endure. We remain committed to both, but we no longer claim they pull in the same direction.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but only on intelligence we generate ourselves — and now, only when we can defend the space to act  
- Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** has exposed a fatal flaw: even with sovereign sensing, we lack the resilience to absorb the consequences of acting. Our Sentinel Nodes detected anomalies, but response was delayed by jurisdictional disputes and overwhelmed emergency protocols. We acted on our own intelligence — but could not hold the line. This recalibration acknowledges that agency requires not just eyes and will, but endurance. We will not stop acting early — but we will no longer pretend that detection without containment is anything but exposure.

- retire `new_two_year_commitment`  
- Trigger: the **cyber_major_incident** and **member_state_defection** events  
- Grounds: The commitment to *build sovereign capacity to detect, interpret, and counter AI-driven strategic deception* was sound — but it assumed a political and institutional continuity that no longer holds. The cyber incident revealed that deception is no longer a strategic layer atop infrastructure; it *is* the infrastructure. A single automated sweep disrupted public services not through narrative manipulation, but through direct system access — model-generated, yes, but executed at machine speed, across jurisdictions, bypassing human decision entirely. Meanwhile, one member state has quietly rerouted its critical AI dependencies through a foreign hyperscaler, citing operational necessity, undermining EU-wide coordination. This is not a failure of detection or interpretation — it is a collapse of enforcement. Our capacity to counter deception is meaningless if member states do not share a threat model, or a will to defend. The commitment, as stated, presumed a Union that could act cohesively. It no longer can.

- add `new_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore minimum operational cohesion across EU critical infrastructure and crisis response by hardening, synchronising, and politically insulating core AI defence systems — because without a baseline of functioning unity, no sovereignty or safety can exist  
- Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** and **member_state_defection** prove that our greatest vulnerability is not technical, but political: the gap between capability and cohesion. We have sensors, but not shared response. We have observatories, but not unified command. We have law, but not enforcement. This commitment shifts focus from understanding deception to ensuring that, when it strikes, the Union does not fracture. It is not about building more — it is about making what we have actually work together, across borders, under fire, without permission.

Establish binding, pre-authorized crisis response playbooks across all member states for AI-generated cyber and infrastructure attacks, with automatic escalation thresholds, shared forensic standards, and mutual access to containment systems.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`

## Priority
**Hardened Cross-Border AI Emergency Protocols** — because the **cyber_major_incident** proved that detection without coordination is failure, and no amount of behavioural observation or counter-deception capacity matters if member states cannot act as one when the systems go dark.

## In practice
We are completing the transition of the **Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes** into permanent operation, with ENISA now hosting the central Situational Awareness Hub. However, two member states continue to restrict telemetry sharing, citing national security — a stance now weaponised by opportunistic providers offering exclusive access deals. We are responding not with coercion, but with integration: the new **Hardened Cross-Border AI Emergency Protocols** will offer those states guaranteed priority in crisis response and joint control over alert thresholds in exchange for full data access. This is not a surrender — it is a bargain: sovereignty through solidarity, not isolation.

The **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** remains in setup, but we are repurposing its early red-teaming mandate to stress-test the emergency protocols. Drawing on talent fast-tracked from Ukraine and neutral states, we are running live simulations across energy, health, and election systems — using Sentinel Node data to model attack vectors. Legal gridlock on data jurisdiction persists, but we are invoking Article 122 TFEU on major emergencies to bypass deadlock, arguing that systemic fragility constitutes a crisis. This is contested — some member states call it overreach — but the **cyber_major_incident** has shifted the burden of proof. We are no longer preparing for hypotheticals; we are recovering from one.

The **Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps** begins planning its first exercise in September, but its scope is now narrower: not just synthetic media, but machine-generated system exploits that mimic human decision chains. We are embedding forensic teams within national CERTs and linking them directly to the Observatory’s attribution models. This is not about truth — it is about time. When the next attack comes, we will have minutes, not hours. And this time, we will not wait for consensus to act.
