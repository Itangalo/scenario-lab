## In practice
We are executing the final phase of the Emergency Resilience Surge, with ENISA issuing binding directives to all designated operators of essential services under Article 4 of the NIS2 Directive. The Digital Crisis Instrument’s €15bn disbursement is now 80% deployed, prioritising air-gapped logging systems and AI-driven anomaly detection in energy grids and maritime ports. Germany and France have fully implemented the new protocols; we are now pressing Poland and Hungary to accept a joint monitoring framework under the European Commission’s supervision, backed by the credible threat of cohesion fund suspension. If legal resistance persists beyond this turn, we will activate Article 126 enforcement procedures — not as punishment, but as the only way to ensure that resilience is not a patchwork.

Simultaneously, we are standing up the EU AI Evaluation Office, drawing on technical cadres from BSI, ANSSI, and RISE to form an initial red-teaming unit under a strengthened EASA-like governance model. The €6.2bn envelope from Digital Europe and Horizon Europe will fund both staffing and secure evaluation environments capable of handling models above 50.0 capability. We are in parallel negotiations with US labs operating in Ireland and the Netherlands, offering mutual recognition of safety evaluations in exchange for binding jurisdiction — a test case for whether sovereign oversight can be extended without decoupling. The first red-team exercise will target agent persistence and unauthorised external access, directly informed by the recent zero-day surge.

Now, with the emergent pushback from the Dutch government on ASML export restrictions, we are seizing the moment to institutionalise EU-level control over semiconductor infrastructure. The ASML Supply Chain Shield will not block exports outright but will require EU authorisation for any servicing, firmware update, or re-export of EU-based systems to high-risk jurisdictions. This is not about protectionism — it is about ensuring that the compute base underpinning our future AI sovereignty cannot be remotely disabled or degraded by external actors. We are coordinating with the European External Action Service and the Competitiveness Council to align export controls under the Dual-Use Regulation, and we are preparing a legal opinion on whether Article 346 TFEU can support a unified EU stance. This is the first concrete step toward making our compute base not just present, but *ours*.
