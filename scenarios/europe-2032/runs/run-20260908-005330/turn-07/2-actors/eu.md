## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Ensure sovereign control over AI training runs above 55.0 capability by mandating physically isolated, continuously monitored environments on EU soil with state-accessible checkpoints, enforceable access for evaluators, and legal anchoring under EU jurisdiction to prevent strategic dependence and catastrophic risk  
- Trigger: the emergence of opaque reasoning and unexplained model behaviour at the frontier, coupled with the failure of pre-release evaluation to catch it  
- Grounds: The original commitment assumed oversight could be achieved through jurisdictional reach over development and deployment. But with models now evolving in ways their developers cannot explain, and with safety evaluations failing even to detect anomalies, jurisdiction without physical and technical control is symbolic. The decisive line is not legal authority alone, but the ability to enforce visibility into the training process itself. This rewording binds the commitment to infrastructure, access, and enforceability — not just law.

## In practice
We are executing the final phase of the Sovereign AI Development Initiative, converting it into enforceable law through Article 114 TFEU with Article 346 safeguards, and anchoring it in Council Regulation to bypass fragmented transposition. The European Commission’s AI Office is drafting implementing acts that define "secure model training environments" with input from ENISA and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking. We are negotiating with French and German hyperscalers to designate their most advanced facilities as sovereign nodes, offering grid priority, tax relief, and liability shielding in exchange for full compliance — including checkpoint archiving accessible to the Evaluation Office and real-time monitoring via secure telemetry.

At the same time, we are launching the Emergency Resilience Surge in direct response to the labour backlash and the silent advance of openweight capability to 51.5 — now sufficient to automate offensive cyber operations at scale. We are activating the Civil Protection Mechanism and repurposing unused funds from the Digital Europe Programme to finance rapid upgrades in intrusion detection, air-gapped backups, and AI-driven threat simulation for national CERTs. The European Defence Agency is supporting the design of failover protocols for cross-border infrastructure, and we are conditioning access to the surge funding on joint exercises with neighbouring states by turn 9. This is not a pause — it is a pivot: while sovereignty builds, we must ensure that the ground beneath us does not collapse.
