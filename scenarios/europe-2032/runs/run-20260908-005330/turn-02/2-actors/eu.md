## Priority
**Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure** — because the capability_jump in cyber operations proves that the threat is no longer latent but operational, and because partial implementation under legal dispute risks leaving critical nodes exposed during a period of rising attack sophistication, this measure remains the urgent anchor of our response.

## In practice
We are finalising the first wave of ENISA directives under the Emergency Resilience Surge, mandating multi-factor authentication, air-gapped logging, and AI-driven intrusion detection for all critical infrastructure operators. The Digital Crisis Instrument has released €15bn in immediate co-financing, with 70% coverage for SMEs and municipalities, and the JRC is now running live simulations of the new adversarial tactics derived from the recent intrusion. Despite German and French support, Poland and Hungary continue to challenge the legal basis under Article 4; we are countering with a joint monitoring protocol that allows national oversight while ensuring compliance, but the cohesion fund suspension mechanism remains on the table. If this resistance persists into the next turn, we will escalate.

Simultaneously, we are launching the legal and institutional groundwork for the EU AI Evaluation Office. This begins with a proposal to the European Parliament’s Internal Market and Civil Liberties committees, backed by a €6.2bn envelope drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe surplus. The office will be hosted under a strengthened EASA-like model, with binding authority to access model weights, run red-team evaluations on agent persistence, goal stability, and cyber exploitation potential, and delay releases that exceed defined thresholds. We are engaging with national regulators in France, Germany, and the Netherlands to pre-seed technical staffing, while negotiating with the US and UK labs operating in Europe to accept jurisdiction in exchange for streamlined access. This is not about catching up with the frontier — it is about ensuring that, for the first time, the EU has sovereign eyes on what is being built before it is released.
