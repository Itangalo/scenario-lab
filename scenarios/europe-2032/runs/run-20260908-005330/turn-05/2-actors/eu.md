## New measure
**Sovereign AI Development Initiative**  
Launch a binding directive requiring that all frontier AI development and high-impact deployment within the EU be subject to sovereign oversight, including secure model training environments, state-accessible checkpoints, and deployment logging under EU jurisdiction.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +10 to +15 by anchoring advanced development within EU legal and physical control, ai_safety +5 through structured access for evaluation, and resilience +2 by reducing reliance on unaccountable external providers  
`Applies to:` Frontier AI developers operating in or serving the EU market, with phased compliance based on capability thresholds starting at 55.0

## Priority
**Sovereign AI Development Initiative** — because the convergence of rising frontier capability (59.5), collapsing safety (13.0), and persistent foreign leverage demands that we anchor not just compute but actual development within our jurisdiction, this measure is the necessary evolution of our two-year commitment now that mere infrastructure is no longer enough.

## In practice
We are concluding the ASML Supply Chain Shield and the EU AI Evaluation Office’s pre-release regime this turn, both now operational. The Shield has established a centralised log of ASML equipment servicing rights and firmware update controls under Article 346 TFEU, with provisional moratoria in place for high-risk jurisdictions. Legal validation is ongoing, but the precedent is set: the Union can act where strategic technology is concerned. The Evaluation Office has completed its first red-team assessment on a near-55.0 model, identifying agent persistence and data exfiltration risks; mutual recognition with Finland and Ireland is live, and we are extending jurisdictional reach through conditional fast-tracking for compliant developers. However, the opacity of models remains acute—our evaluators report reasoning traces are still functionally illegible—and without access deeper than release points, assurance cannot rise.

The Labour Transition Acceleration Fund is now disbursing €12bn in conditional grants, with Germany and Sweden moving quickly on digital upskilling pipelines in legal tech and accounting automation. Italy and Poland remain hesitant on co-financing, so we are offering bridge financing from the European Social Fund Plus, tied to quarterly milestones. Mobile training units are being prepositioned in Silesia and Calabria, targeting regions where job losses are accelerating. This is not charity—it is political shock absorption, ensuring that public sentiment does not collapse further as displacement becomes visible.

At the same time, we are launching the Sovereign AI Development Initiative to close the gap between having compute and having control. Under a new legal instrument based on the Critical Digital Infrastructure framework, we will require that any training run above 55.0 capability must occur in sovereign-designated facilities with real-time monitoring, checkpoint archiving accessible to the Evaluation Office, and strict limits on model leakage. We are negotiating with French and German hyperscalers to host the first two centres, offering tax incentives and grid priority in exchange for full compliance. Resistance is expected from U.S.-based labs with EU subsidiaries, which is why we are coupling this with outreach through the newly opened U.S. allied access channel—offering equivalency if they submit to mirrored safeguards. This is not about blocking innovation; it is about ensuring that when capability lands, it lands under law.
