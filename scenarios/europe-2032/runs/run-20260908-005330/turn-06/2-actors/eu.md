## New measure
**Secure Training Environment Mandate**  
Require that all AI training runs above 55.0 capability occur in physically and legally secured facilities with continuous monitoring, checkpoint archiving, and no external connectivity during training.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +12 to +18 by anchoring actual training within EU jurisdiction, ai_safety +6 through persistent access for evaluators, resilience +3 by reducing reliance on unaccountable external providers  
`Applies to:` Frontier AI developers operating in or serving the EU market, with phased compliance based on capability thresholds starting at 55.0

## Priority
**Secure Training Environment Mandate** — because the eval_anomaly_reports and opaque_reasoning events confirm that frontier models are now evolving in ways we cannot read or replicate, and because safety_breakthrough offers a narrow window to mandate access before capabilities race further ahead, this measure is the only way to ensure that sovereign oversight means something when the systems themselves are no longer legible.

## In practice
We are finalising the Sovereign AI Development Initiative this turn, converting the political momentum from the cyber incident and safety collapse into binding legal form. We are drafting under Article 114 TFEU, with Article 346 as a safeguard, to ensure that any AI system trained above 55.0 within the EU — or serving the EU market at that level — must operate under sovereign oversight. This means real-time monitoring, checkpoint archiving accessible to the Evaluation Office, and strict limits on model leakage. We are negotiating with French and German hyperscalers to host the first two sovereign-designated facilities, offering tax incentives and grid priority in exchange for full compliance. Resistance is expected from U.S.-based labs with EU subsidiaries, which is why we are coupling this with outreach through the newly opened U.S. allied access channel — offering equivalency if they submit to mirrored safeguards.

At the same time, we are launching the Secure Training Environment Mandate as a direct response to the eval_anomaly_reports and opaque_reasoning developments. The breakthrough in interpretability gives us a fleeting chance to act: if we can mandate access to training checkpoints now, we may be able to apply the new tools before they become obsolete. We are working with the European Defence Agency to adapt secure facility standards from military AI programmes, and with the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking to repurpose existing infrastructure. The first two sites will be operational by turn 8, co-located with Gigafactory nodes in France and Germany. This is not about slowing innovation — it is about ensuring that when the systems become too powerful to understand, we still have a way to hold them.
