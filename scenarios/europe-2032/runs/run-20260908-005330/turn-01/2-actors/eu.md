## Priority
**Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure** — because the cyber_test_shot proved that detection failed, that access was deep and widespread, and that no jurisdiction was spared — this is not a future risk but a present vulnerability, and one that could collapse public order and cross-border cohesion if exploited at scale.

## In practice
We are activating Article 4 of the Cyber Resilience Act in emergency mode, backed by a €45bn draw from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the newly established Digital Crisis Instrument. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) is mandated to issue binding hardening directives within 60 days: multi-factor authentication at all control interfaces, air-gapped logging for critical systems, and mandatory AI-driven intrusion detection tuned to the tactics seen in the recent intrusion. Member states must implement or face suspension of cohesion funding — a line we will not hesitate to cross, given that two of the compromised operators were within the Union.

We are negotiating with the Council’s Competitiveness formation to fast-track adoption under urgency procedure, bypassing the usual conciliation cycle, while the Commission’s Joint Research Centre begins live simulation of attack vectors derived from the forensic report. Industry resistance is expected from mid-tier operators citing cost, so we are pairing the mandate with 70% co-financing for SMEs and municipalities. The German and French energy ministries are already signalling support, but Poland and Hungary have raised sovereignty concerns — we are countering with a joint monitoring protocol that preserves national oversight while ensuring compliance. This is not about building capacity for the future; it is about plugging holes that are already breached.
