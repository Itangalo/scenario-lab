## In practice
We are executing the final phase of the ASML Supply Chain Shield, with the European Commission’s Directorate-General for Trade and the EEAS finalising a legal opinion on the use of Article 346 TFEU to establish a unified EU position on servicing controls for ASML equipment. The Netherlands, under domestic political pressure, has agreed to a provisional moratorium on firmware updates to systems in high-risk jurisdictions pending the outcome of the legal review. We are engaging with ASML’s service partners and EU-based operators to map the full installed base and establish a centralised log under Euroatom-like safeguards. This is not about blocking trade — it is about ensuring that the tools enabling our future compute base cannot be disabled remotely by external powers. The measure concludes this turn, but its implementation will require sustained enforcement through the Dual-Use Regulation and the Competitiveness Council beyond.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the EU AI Evaluation Office, with the first cohort of red-team engineers now in place at the secure facility in Luxembourg. The office is conducting its first pre-release assessment on a model reported to approach 55.0 capability, focusing on agent persistence, unauthorised external access, and model leakage vectors. We have secured mutual recognition agreements with Ireland and Finland for initial pilot evaluations, and are in technical talks with OpenAI and Anthropic’s EU subsidiaries to extend jurisdiction, offering fast-track approvals in exchange for full access. The model’s opacity remains a challenge — our evaluators report that even with access, interpretability lags capability — but the office is now operational and delivering early warnings.

With the cyber_major_incident exposing the fragility of public services and the emergent_labour_displacement_wave gaining traction in national media, we are launching the Labour Transition Acceleration Fund as a crisis-responsive measure. Drawing €12bn from the European Social Fund Plus and the Globalisation Adjustment Fund, we will disburse conditional grants to member states that implement rapid retraining pipelines in AI-augmented legal, accounting, and technical support fields. Germany and the Nordic states have expressed early interest; we are preparing to deploy mobile upskilling units in regions like Silesia and southern Italy where displacement is concentrated. The fund will require national co-financing and adherence to common digital competence frameworks, ensuring coherence without uniformity. This is not a retreat from sovereignty — it is the necessary foundation for maintaining the political capital to pursue it.
