### Shelter Under Fire
July to December 2031 kept Europe out of the American-Chinese war but not untouched by it. Freight insurance stayed tripled, southern air corridors faced rolling delays when navigation signals dropped, and backup power for hospitals and registries was rationed week to week. The Council held its non-belligerency line: no basing, no targeting work from European fabs, cables or data centres. Reserve engineers shuttled clean images between clinics to keep triage assistants alive.

The late-paying Transition Shield finished disbursing in autumn. Wage top-ups and hospital placements reached payrolls, backlogs shortened, and local press noted it — briefly.

### Two Blows To Trust
Then came the genome-model paper. A team showed a model helping produce a viable design for a human-infecting organism. Methodologists quarrelled, editors were accused of printing a recipe, but health ministries treated it as real. Brussels froze reagent sharing outside pooled EU labs, doubled down on European hosting and public-clinic delivery for the new immune therapies, and pushed detection work through the bio-care network. Queues grew; a few thousand more were treated, most waited.

At the same time, ombudsmen in two member states found an AI-supported benefits and policing support system had systematically wronged claimants for months. Conformity papers were in order, logs existed and were never read, human oversight meant a caseworker clearing a recommendation every forty seconds. The Commission conceded enforcement had failed and ordered manual review queues and redress through national ombudsmen.

The combination — fear of what biology models could do, anger at what welfare software already had — crushed acceptance. Protests targeted data-centre sites, unions called the top-ups hush money, and talk of rewriting the rulebook died for lack of votes.
