### The Shelter Holds, The Ground Moves
January to June 2032 was the half-year Europe kept the lights on by rationing them. Hospitals and civil registries stayed on week-to-week backup power. Reserve engineers carried clean system images between clinics to keep triage assistants running. Freight insurance stayed tripled, southern air corridors slipped when satellite navigation wavered, and the continuity cell in the Council managed delays rather than clearing them.

The pooled bio-labs delivered the one bright spot. Validation work there turned last year's frightening genome-model finding into a usable detection signature, now pushed out through the care network. Health ministries presented it as proof the pooling decision had produced something real. Queues for the new immune therapies still moved by the thousand, not the hundred thousand, and reagent sharing stayed locked inside European labs.

Then Washington changed the terms of everything. The United States placed its leading AI laboratories under direct state control — security officers inside training runs, weights treated as defence articles, foreign customers chosen politically. Brussels understood at once that conformity paperwork and market access no longer reached the provider. Access stopped being a purchase and became a negotiation between states. DG Trade opened a state-to-state channel on service continuity and incident notice, while operations were quietly shifted to whatever could actually run on European hardware.

Almost immediately, a member state broke ranks and signed its own hosting arrangement with a large American provider on cheaper, looser terms. At home it was sold as pragmatism — power, jobs, guaranteed service. In Brussels and other capitals it was read as hollowing the common line on no military use and European hosting. With almost no political room to enforce, the presidency offered shelter power, pooled reagents and arrears payments in exchange for restraint, and avoided a public fight it would have lost.

Trust kept falling. Manual review of the wrongful benefits and policing cases crawled, data-centre protests continued, and dependency felt humiliating in plain view.
