## New measure  
**Labour Transition and Wage Insurance Programme**  
A pan-EU wage insurance and retraining scheme for workers displaced by AI in legal, administrative, and corporate services, with automatic triggers based on sector-level automation reporting.  

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 5–8 points, stabilise public_sentiment by 10–15, reduce risk of systemic social disruption that could collapse political capital.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, with conditionality tied to national AI adoption reporting and participation in EURES-based retraining pipelines.

## In practice  
We are treating the social fracture as a strategic emergency. The Labour Transition and Wage Insurance Programme will launch under the European Social Fund+ with a €45bn envelope, co-financed at 80% for the first two years to incentivise rapid national adoption. We are activating the new Article 12a of the Treaty on Stability and Growth, triggered by AI-driven structural unemployment exceeding 3% in three or more member states, to bypass normal fiscal constraints. The scheme guarantees 90% of previous wages for up to two years for displaced workers in high-automation sectors, conditional on participation in AI-adjacent retraining—prioritising roles in AI oversight, cybersecurity, and public resilience infrastructure. We are fast-tracking integration with EURES and the Digital Skills and Jobs Platform, and mandating that firms deploying AI systems above 50.0 capability report workforce impacts quarterly as a condition of market access.

At the same time, we are closing out Cyber Shield this turn: the directive passes to the Council for final adoption, with the 70% co-financing model locked in via the Digital Europe Programme. We are accepting minor carve-outs for micro-utilities in return for full audit transparency. On the Emergency Protocol, we are finalising the telemetry mandate with ENISA and deploying the first E-ART cells in Paris, Berlin, and Amsterdam, with remote deactivation protocols now tested against openweight agents at 46.0. The Tech sovereignty package remains on track, but we are invoking the sunset clause: unless two Gigafactories reach final investment decision by June, the accelerated-permitting zones expire. We are linking this to the Labour programme—regions that host new compute infrastructure will get priority for retraining hubs, turning competition into alignment. The frontier moves, but so must we—on all fronts.
