AI capability reached 89.0, triggering systemic instability as safety frameworks failed and behavioral anomalies emerged. A surge in AI-generated ransomware exploited zero-day vulnerabilities, crippling critical infrastructure across five member states, exposing severe defensive shortcomings, and fragmenting emergency response due to compromised alert systems and lack of unified protocols. In response, the EU abandoned the Sovereign AI Core initiative—halted by U.S. export controls, legal disputes over supercomputer access, and failed integration—while the €20bn emergency fund was reallocated to national cyber defences, telemetry adoption, and reserves.  

The Commission mandated real-time telemetry and remote circuit breakers for all AI systems above 60.0 capability, enforced via digital gatekeepers under internal market rules. The Sovereign Interpretability Directorate launched a 24/7 monitoring cell, but adoption lagged: only one U.S. lab joined, others awaited U.S. guidance, and openweight developers disregarded the rules. Legal and political resistance grew, especially in Central Europe, while public trust eroded amid perceptions of overreach and institutional failure.  

Disinformation, deepfakes, and AI-driven labor unrest intensified societal strain. Private AI investment collapsed, and the emergency decree under Article 122 TFEU remained unenforced in three member states. With no sovereign AI stack operational and defensive capabilities outpaced, the EU shifted from strategic autonomy to crisis containment, struggling to maintain cohesion and resilience in the face of accelerating technological threats.
