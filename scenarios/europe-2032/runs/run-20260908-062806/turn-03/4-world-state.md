### The Frontier Keeps Moving  
AI capability advances steadily to 59.5 as leading labs integrate agentic reasoning into core training loops. The trajectory remains linear but firm, with no signs of deceleration. Developers report systems now capable of managing multi-week research projects with minimal supervision, though verification remains bottlenecked by human oversight.

### Open Access, Hidden Dangers  
Openweight models reach 46.0, driven by reverse-engineered training techniques from leaked frontier system configurations. These models are now powerful enough to generate convincing falsified legal documents and automated filings, exacerbating fears in administrative sectors already reeling from displacement.

### Labour Fears Deepen  
White-collar job losses accelerate across Germany, France, and the Benelux as law firms and corporate back offices deploy AI for contract review and compliance. Protests grow outside data centres in Ireland and Finland, with demonstrators accusing tech firms and EU policymakers of ignoring the social cost. Media narratives increasingly frame AI not as a tool, but as a force displacing skilled professionals.

### EU Focuses on Containment  
The Emergency Protocol advances in DG CONNECT, with France and Germany agreeing to pilot the European Agentic Incident Response Team. Telemetry mandates are being drafted for high-capability systems, but resistance emerges from industry stakeholders who argue the rules could stifle innovation. Meanwhile, Cyber Shield nears adoption, with 70% co-financing swaying several reluctant member states.

However, political capital erodes under the weight of four large concurrent measures. The refusal to act on labour displacement fuels public distrust. Sentiment drops further as unions warn of a “silent structural crisis.” Sovereignty continues to decline as global compute growth outpaces the EU’s build-out, despite progress on permitting reforms.
