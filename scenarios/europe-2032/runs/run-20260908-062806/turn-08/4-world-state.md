### The Frontier Leaps, Governance Stumbles  
A sudden breakthrough at a leading U.S. lab sent `ai_capability` surging by +4.5 this turn, pushing the metric to 75.0. The pace of advancement accelerated sharply, outstripping near-term planning assumptions. In response, a growing network of independent researchers — technically sophisticated and deeply skeptical of closed-lab assurances — published a damning joint analysis, correctly predicting a recent agent control failure. Their credibility soared, amplifying pressure on regulators.

### Sovereignty Inches Forward, But Falls Short  
The Emergency Sovereignty Acceleration Facility reached its scheduled completion, marking a symbolic victory. Legal frameworks were anchored, disbursement mechanisms activated, and site planning advanced in Finland, France, and Italy. However, physical deployment stalled due to unresolved environmental appeals, and Slovakia quietly negotiated direct access to a U.S. hyperscaler’s frontier models, undercutting the EU’s unified stance. This act of quiet defection eroded trust and limited sovereignty gains.

### Safety Efforts Drown in the Surge  
The Emergency AI Safety Coordination Cell became operational, staffing up and initiating red-team exercises. Yet without real-time telemetry, its reach remained shallow. The EU proposed a Binding Safety Telemetry Mandate to enforce visibility into high-capability systems, but fierce resistance emerged from both foreign developers and privacy advocates. While the proposal underscored urgent needs, it failed to secure adoption, and no new enforcement mechanisms took effect this turn. Some existing safety monitoring lapsed under the strain of rapid capability growth, contributing to a modest erosion in overall safety posture.

### Political Capital Holds, Barely  
Five major initiatives concluded, freeing resources. The portfolio charge for ongoing efforts totaled −6, partially offset by a +3 bonus for launching the Emergency AI Safety Coordination Cell under urgent conditions. The Cell was designated as this turn’s priority initiative, reflecting heightened concern over control failures. However, the simultaneous push on sovereignty and safety, combined with the stalled telemetry mandate, strained an already fragile political balance. Public sentiment remained stagnant, with no visible benefits to offset growing anxiety. Resilience held steady — past measures had institutionalized baseline protections — but no new harm-reducing policies landed.
