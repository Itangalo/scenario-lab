## New measure
Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure  
A binding directive requiring cyber resilience baselines across electricity, water, and port operators, with mandatory audits and real-time monitoring.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` increases `resilience` by 10–15 points; reduces probability of cascading failure after cyber intrusion  
`Applies to:` EU member states with critical infrastructure in energy, water, and transport sectors

## Priority  
Cyber Shield for Critical Infrastructure — because the cyber_test_shot proved our defences are blind to advanced persistent threats, and we cannot afford to wait for damage to occur.

## In practice  
We are acting on the intrusion revealed this autumn not as an isolated breach but as proof of systemic fragility. The cyber_test_shot exposed that our detection regimes failed to catch actors embedded for weeks in core systems — and that includes two EU member states’ grid operators. This is not a call for panic, but for structure: we are drafting a Cyber Shield directive under Article 114 TFEU, to be proposed through the Internal Market and Consumer Protection Council. The legal base allows qualified majority voting, but we will need to secure cohesion early in COREPER II to avoid national vetoes dressed as subsidiarity concerns.

The directive mandates minimum cyber resilience baselines for operators of essential services, with real-time monitoring, automated threat sharing via ENISA, and annual red-teaming exercises modelled on the U.S. Cyber National Guard drills. We are working with Germany, France, and the Nordic states to build a blocking minority in favour — not because they are natural allies, but because the intrusion touched Denmark and Italy. The resistance will come from smaller states fearing cost and centralisation, so we are pairing the directive with a reprogramming of the Digital Europe Programme’s unused €12bn to co-finance implementation, conditioned on national contributions. This is not charity: it is leverage to ensure the shield is whole. The Commission’s Joint Research Centre will host the monitoring backend, and we are fast-tracking procurement for intrusion-detection AI trained on non-sensitive network telemetry — not to replace human oversight, but to restore our ability to see.
