## New measure  
**Sovereign AI Core: Build and Operate EU-Controlled Frontier Systems**  
Establish and operate EU-owned, EU-staffed AI systems at or above 75.0 capability, trained and run on sovereign infrastructure, with full interpretability and telemetry, to serve as both operational backbone and control reference.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 25–30 points and ai_safety by 10–15 by creating a sovereign capability stack that is physically, legally, and technically independent. This will anchor resilience, enable real oversight, and restore leverage over foreign providers.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, EuroHPC, ENISA, EIB, and selected national research labs; binding on all member states receiving EU digital resilience funding.

## In practice  
We are shifting from oversight to ownership. The denial of access to the leading model — cutting off hospitals, ministries, and critical infrastructure — was not just a disruption; it was a regime change. Combined with opaque_reasoning, it has ended the era in which we could rely on transparency from others. Our safety institutions are blind, our sovereignty is conditional, and our credibility is evaporating. This turn, we stop asking for access and start building it.

We are launching the Sovereign AI Core under Article 122 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act’s ancillary powers, with the EIB committing €45bn in initial funding, matched by €30bn from national co-investment and redirected Gigafactory contingency. The core will be built on the physical sites secured under the Emergency Sovereignty Acceleration Facility in Finland, France, and Italy, but now with full EU ownership and operation. We are instructing EuroHPC to repurpose LUMI, JUWELS, and Leonardo for sovereign training runs, and ENISA will lead a new Interpretability Directorate to develop non-linguistic monitoring tools compatible with opaque internal representations.

We are offering member states a clear ultimatum: integrate your national AI strategy into the Core, or lose access to future EU digital funding. Slovakia will be addressed directly in the Council’s internal market configuration — their bilateral deal is now a breach of collective security. We are seconding top researchers from INRIA, Fraunhofer, and Aalto to form the first training team, and we are invoking emergency procurement rules to fast-track chip imports from ASML and TSMC under EU-level export licensing. This is not a parallel effort — it is the new centre of gravity. The Emergency AI Safety Coordination Cell will be re-tasked to monitor and evaluate our own systems first, others only after. We are no longer waiting for the frontier to slow down. We are becoming it.
