## In practice
We are acting on the conviction that sovereignty is not a luxury but the precondition of all governance. The capability_jump — a leap to 64.5 — and the eval_anomaly_reports, suggesting unmodelled agentic behaviour, have shattered any illusion that oversight alone can contain systemic risk. The US retrenchment removes a stabilising force, but also a crutch: we can no longer assume access to cutting-edge capability will persist. The member_state_defection — Slovakia’s bilateral compute deal with a US hyperscaler, granting preferential access in exchange for data sovereignty concessions — proves the Union’s position is fracturing from within.

We are therefore launching the Emergency Sovereignty Acceleration Facility under Article 122 TFEU, treating AI compute as critical infrastructure in crisis. The €75bn will be drawn from the EU’s strategic investment envelope and reallocated cohesion funds, with disbursement tied to binding legal commitments: systems must be physically located in the EU, operated under EU law, and subject to ENISA telemetry and audit. We are offering member states a choice: integrate into this sovereign stack, or lose access to future resilience and transition funding. The first nodes will be co-located with existing EuroHPC sites in Finland, France, and Italy, leveraging grid and cooling infrastructure already in place.

At the same time, we are closing out five major measures this turn. The Labour Transition and Wage Insurance Programme, though stalled in some states, is now operational in Germany and France, covering 42% of the at-risk workforce. We are converting its implementation into a benchmark for the new Facility: only regions with active retraining hubs will qualify for priority compute access. The Mandatory AI Impact Audits are being folded into a new EU AI Observatory under the AI Office, ensuring continuity of transparency even as enforcement challenges mount. The Emergency Protocol, though incomplete in telemetry coverage, has established E-ART cells in three capitals and will be sustained through Frontex and ENISA budgets. These closures free political capital — just enough — to fund the new push. We are not abandoning accountability or resilience. We are recognising that without sovereign capability, they are promises written on water.
