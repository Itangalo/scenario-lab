### The Stack That Wasn’t

The EU doubled down on sovereignty, launching the Sovereign Interpretability Directorate with binding access rights and accelerating the Sovereign AI Core across EuroHPC sites. Emergency funds flowed, chips were ordered, and a unified stack was declared non-negotiable. Yet execution faltered. Slovakia refused to dismantle its bilateral deal, citing national continuity, and other states withheld full access to infrastructure. The EU AI Infrastructure Board met for the first time but failed to agree on operational protocols, leaving LUMI, JUWELS, and Leonardo in legal limbo — national ownership intact, but no clear path to unified control.

Meanwhile, the frontier advanced. A U.S. lab refined its non-linguistic reasoning architecture, pushing global AI capability to 82.0. This leap further eroded human-legible oversight, deepening the opacity that the new Directorate was meant to solve — too late, and without the systems to test on. No sovereign model had yet been trained; the first architecture design was still in draft.

The access denial worsened public trust. Hospitals faced disruptions, and false AI-generated emergency orders — mimicking regional officials — spread across platforms, stoking panic about energy rationing. The disinformation wave, coordinated and multilingual, exploited institutional fragility. A transnational tribunal issued a second transparency ruling, backed by courts in Finland and Belgium, but it remained symbolic — unenforced and ignored by the targeted provider.

Resilience dipped as adaptive capacity was overwhelmed. Political capital eroded further: the portfolio charge hit hard, and the new large measure offered no immediate relief. Public sentiment fell as dependency and disarray became visible. The EU acted with ambition, but the stack remained unbuilt, the tools unproven, and the unity unsecured.

### A Sovereignty of Paper

The Sovereign AI Core and the new Directorate were real on paper, but not yet in operation. Without access to functioning systems, interpretability remained theoretical. Sovereignty, measured in actual capacity, declined further.
