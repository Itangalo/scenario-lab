  - Grounds: The simultaneous collapse of oversight (due to opaque reasoning) and denial of access has revealed that regulatory authority is meaningless without the ability to run, observe, and replicate frontier systems. Our two mandates converge: sovereignty is no longer a means but the condition of both safety and self-determination.

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer tolerate dependence on systems we cannot observe, control, or replicate. This turn, we act not on uncertainty, but on the certainty of our own irrelevance if we remain external to the stack.
  - Grounds: The event `eu_frontier_access_denied` was not a shock — it was the logical endpoint of a decade of deferred investment. We now treat sovereign capability as the baseline for any action, not a secondary ambition. The cost of uncertainty is no longer delay — it is institutional nullity.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Deliver a sovereign AI stack — trained, operated, and interpreted within EU jurisdiction — that can serve as the operational backbone of critical public services and as a control reference for safety, achieving minimum capability of 75.0 by turn 12.
  - Grounds: The original commitment to “act as a sovereign power” was aspirational; it is now operational. The denial of access and the obsolescence of monitoring tools have made clear that sovereignty is not about influence or negotiation — it is about having working systems under our own roof, on our own terms. This reframing reflects not a change in direction, but a sharpening of focus under fire.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
