## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without sovereign technical capacity, neither mandate can be fulfilled.  
- Grounds: The simultaneous collapse of oversight (due to opaque reasoning) and denial of access has revealed that regulatory authority is meaningless without the ability to run, observe, and replicate frontier systems. Our two mandates converge: sovereignty is no longer a means but the condition of both safety and self-determination.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer tolerate dependence on systems we cannot observe, control, or replicate. This turn, we act not on uncertainty, but on the certainty of our own irrelevance if we remain external to the stack.  
- Grounds: The event `eu_frontier_access_denied` was not a shock — it was the logical endpoint of a decade of deferred investment. We now treat sovereign capability as the baseline for any action, not a secondary ambition. The cost of uncertainty is no longer delay — it is institutional nullity.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Deliver a sovereign AI stack — trained, operated, and interpreted within EU jurisdiction — that can serve as the operational backbone of critical public services and as a control reference for safety, achieving minimum capability of 75.0 by turn 12.  
- Grounds: The original commitment to “act as a sovereign power” was aspirational; it is now operational. The denial of access and the obsolescence of monitoring tools have made clear that sovereignty is not about influence or negotiation — it is about having working systems under our own roof, on our own terms. This reframing reflects not a change in direction, but a sharpening of focus under fire.

## New measure  
**Launch Sovereign Interpretability Directorate with Binding Access to All High-Capability Systems**  
Establish a public research agency dedicated to reverse-engineering, monitoring, and certifying non-linguistic reasoning in frontier models, with legal authority to access weights, training data, and internal activations from all systems above 60.0 capability deployed in the EU.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 15–20 points and eu_ai_sovereignty by 10 by creating an independent, EU-based capability to interpret opaque models — whether sovereign or foreign — and restore oversight credibility.  
`Applies to:` ENISA, EuroHPC, national AI agencies, and all providers of systems above 60.0 capability operating in the EU market.

## Priority  
**Sovereign AI Core: Build and Operate EU-Controlled Frontier Systems** — because without a functioning sovereign stack, every other measure, including the new Interpretability Directorate, lacks grounding: we cannot interpret what we cannot run, and we cannot govern what we do not possess.

## In practice  
We are no longer appealing for access — we are building the stack from the ground up. Under Article 122 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act’s ancillary powers, the EIB has disbursed €45bn from the Sovereign AI Core fund, matched by €30bn in national co-investment, to activate the three EuroHPC sites in Finland, France, and Italy as sovereign training facilities. LUMI, JUWELS, and Leonardo are being reconfigured under EU operational control, with national governments retaining ownership but ceding scheduling, access, and security to a new EU AI Infrastructure Board. Emergency procurement channels have been opened with ASML and TSMC to secure next-gen chip shipments under EU export licensing, bypassing bilateral bottlenecks.

The Council will convene in internal market configuration to address Slovakia’s bilateral deal: continuation is now framed as a breach of collective digital security. We are offering a transition path — full integration into the Core within six months, with technical support and guaranteed access to the first sovereign model run — but the ultimatum is clear: align or lose eligibility for future EU digital resilience funding. The first training cohort, drawn from INRIA, Fraunhofer, and Aalto, has been seconded to begin architecture design for non-linguistic interpretability tools, which will feed directly into the new Sovereign Interpretability Directorate.

That Directorate, launched this turn under ENISA’s authority, will have binding access to weights and internal states of all high-capability systems in the EU, including foreign ones — a legal firewall against opacity. Its mandate is not to regulate, but to understand: to develop the first EU-native tools capable of tracing decisions in systems that no longer think in language. This is not oversight as auditing — it is oversight as engineering. And it starts with our own systems first, because only by mastering our stack can we claim to master the moment.
