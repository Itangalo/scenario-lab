### The Denial and the Response

In January 2031, the EU’s access to the world’s most advanced AI model was abruptly suspended without explanation. Hospitals relying on diagnostic support, ministries running crisis simulations, and research consortia building on its reasoning layer were left blind. The cut-off was total and unilateral—no appeals, no grace period. In Brussels, it was not framed as a technical failure but as a strategic revocation: Europe’s agency had been outsourced, and now it was being reclaimed by others.

The Union responded with institutional speed—but not unity. The **Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes**, deployed across Jülich, CINECA, Prague, and Bologna, achieved partial operational status this spring, with integration succeeding in three of four nodes. National reservations in two member states delayed full telemetry sharing, though side agreements preserved alert interoperability. The system provides early detection capability, but its coverage remains incomplete.

Work also began on the **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory**, with fast-tracked recruitment filling 85 specialist roles across Seville, Stockholm, and Bucharest. Security clearances were expedited under emergency provisions, though legal challenges loom over data sovereignty clauses. The facility is in setup phase and will not begin red-teaming public services until next turn at the earliest.

The **Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps** was formally proposed under EEAS-Europol coordination, aiming to counter synthetic crises within 72 hours. A draft legal basis was advanced under a novel interpretation of NIS2 and Article 4(2) TEU, enabling future emergency broadcast coordination and takedown requests. However, inter-institutional negotiations are ongoing, and no training exercises have yet occurred. Implementation is expected to take at least two turns, with operational capacity not expected before mid-2032.

Political capital remains fragile. The partial deployment of the Sentinel Nodes delivered modest resilience gains, but the denial of frontier access triggered a sovereignty penalty. Public sentiment holds at rock bottom—no visible benefit has offset the trauma of manipulated elections and silenced institutions.

Europe is building sovereign eyes and ears. But whether it can think—and act—for itself remains unproven.
