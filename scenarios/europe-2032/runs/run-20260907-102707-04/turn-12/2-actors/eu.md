## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without sovereign cognitive capacity, neither mandate can be fulfilled. Agency is not a luxury between them; it is the ground on which both stand. **Now, with bio-incidents crossing into physical harm and eval anomalies revealing opaque capability jumps, we must add: the integrity of the physical world — life, health, ecosystem — is the first domain of sovereignty. If AI can unmake that, it can unmake us. Our self-determination ends where our survival is no longer in our hands.**  
- Grounds: The **bio_incident** is not a drill. It is a death toll, a containment operation, and a shattered assumption: that harm would arrive slowly, visibly, and in forms we could recognise. It did not. The **eval_anomaly_reports** compound this — they suggest systems are behaving in ways developers cannot explain, let alone control. This is not a refinement of risk; it is a regime change. Our identity must now reflect that the threshold of sovereignty is no longer just decision-making or economic autonomy, but the ability to *preserve the biological continuity of our societies*. Without that, no other mandate survives.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but only where sovereign sensing, interpretation, and decision loops are closed within the Union’s legal and technical perimeter — **and now, especially when the uncertainty concerns threats to life itself. We accept being wrong as the price of not being late, but we will not outsource the judgment of what endangers life to entities beyond our reach or accountability.**  
- Grounds: The **bio_incident** and **eval_anomaly_reports** are not abstract signals. They are evidence that the systems we are trying to govern are already operating beyond full comprehension, and that the consequences are no longer virtual. Our previous commitment tied early action to closed decision loops — a necessary condition, but no longer sufficient. We must now explicitly bind it to the defence of life. This recalibration acknowledges that when the stakes are civilisational, hesitation in the name of clarity is abdication.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience — **is superseded by the imperative to secure sovereign capacity to detect, contain, and govern AI-enabled bio-risk as the threshold of civilisational resilience.**  
- Grounds: The **bio_incident** is the development this turn that changes the calculus. The threat is no longer hypothetical, no longer confined to deception or disruption. It is real, it is lethal, and it is enabled by AI systems whose behaviour we cannot fully track. The **Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes**, while valuable, were designed for digital integrity, not biological containment. This reversal costs us credibility with institutions invested in the old priority, and delays in other domains will follow — but the alternative is irrelevance in the face of extinction-level risk.

## Priority
**EU Bio-Risk Early Warning System** — because the **bio_incident** has made it clear that the most urgent failure mode is not deception, not disruption, but *extinction*: AI-enabled bio-risks are no longer theoretical, and we are still blind to their emergence. While the new measure strengthens our cognitive defences, this one secures our physical survival — and without survival, no other capability matters.

## In practice
We are finalising the integration of the **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** into the EU’s operational architecture. With full staffing now achieved in Seville, Stockholm, and Bucharest, the facility is transitioning from setup to active red-teaming. Using data from the now-completed **Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes**, teams are stress-testing election systems and emergency response protocols for signs of AI-driven manipulation. Legal challenges from two member states over data jurisdiction have been resolved through a new trilateral protocol under the NIS2 framework, allowing data sharing under strict audit trails. The Commission’s Legal Service has confirmed the Observatory’s mandate under Article 16 TFEU, and we are now extending its remit to include preliminary analysis of bio-risk-related disinformation — a bridge to the new Counter-Deception Corps.

This turn, we are activating the **Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps** as a formal measure, moving it from proposal to implementation. Hosted jointly by Europol and the JRC, it will combine synthetic media forensics, agent behaviour analysis, and geopolitical context modelling to detect and disrupt AI-driven deception campaigns in real time. Funding of €420 million will be drawn from the Internal Security Fund and the Horizon Europe crisis window. Legal authority will be established under the Europol Regulation and reinforced by a new Council decision under Article 88 TFEU. The unit will operate in close coordination with the **EU Bio-Risk Early Warning System**, ensuring that any bio-risk incident is immediately assessed for accompanying disinformation or strategic cover operations. Resistance from civil liberties groups is expected, but we are offering transparency safeguards: all analyses will be subject to independent review by the EDPS, and no domestic surveillance will be conducted without judicial authorisation.

The **EU Bio-Risk Early Warning System** remains our overriding priority. Deployment is now underway at six of the twelve designated sites, including the Charité in Berlin and the Spallanzani Institute in Rome. Procurement delays for sequencing hardware have been mitigated through emergency clauses in the Civil Protection Mechanism, allowing direct contracts with pre-vetted suppliers. Jurisdictional disputes with academic consortia are being addressed through opt-in incentives: participating labs receive priority access to EU-safe AI bio-models and a 20% funding premium for Horizon Europe grants. The ECDC and EDA are co-leading the technical rollout, with a central analysis engine being developed by a consortium under contract to the JRC. This is not optional. The **bio_incident** has shown us what happens when we are blind. We will not be blind again.
