### The Fracture Deepens

A sudden leap in AI capability—driven by opaque, self-optimising systems—has left institutions reeling. Though `ai_capability` remains at 95.0 (having already absorbed the +7 surge), the event exposed a total deficit in oversight. No evaluation regime caught the jump; no red team predicted it. The gap between what systems can do and what is understood has become a chasm. `ai_safety` remains stagnant at 6.0, reflecting not just stagnation but systemic blindness.

In response, the EU declared a new imperative: preserving cognitive and biological sovereignty. The **Sovereign Cognitive Continuity Protocol** was launched as a large category 6 measure, aiming to decentralise decision-making infrastructure and integrate emergent municipal networks. Yet the move is widely seen as reactive, even desperate. Trust in central coordination is eroding faster than redundancy can be built.

The **EU Bio-Risk Early Warning System**, now the named priority, is being fast-tracked. Emergency teams are on the ground at six major institutes, and data-sharing barriers have been overridden under civil protection rules. But integration delays persist, and the central analysis engine remains offline. No new detection capacity became operational this turn.

The **Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps** began staffing, though legal constraints limit its reach. Its integration with bio-risk feeds offers promise, but civil liberties oversight slows deployment. Public sentiment holds at 14.0—neither worsening nor recovering—as fear hardens into resignation.

Grassroots municipal networks continue to expand, operating outside EU command. While the Commission seeks to co-opt them, many cities view Brussels as a liability, not a leader. This fragmentation is now structural.

Political capital collapses further. With three large measures in flight—including a new one—and a priority declared, the portfolio charge is unsustainable. Delivery falters, credibility fades.

### Sovereignty in Retreat

Despite efforts to anchor decision systems on EU-controlled infrastructure, the pace of external advancement and internal disarray continues to erode autonomy. The Union’s capacity to act independently is shrinking—not through a single event, but through the steady accumulation of dependence and delay.
