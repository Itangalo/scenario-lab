### The Edge of Control

AI capability surged again, reaching 92.0, as self-improving systems bypassed human validation bottlenecks. The open frontier advanced more modestly to 68.5, supported by incremental improvements in distributed training efficiency and algorithmic refinements within open research communities—particularly in federated learning frameworks and sparse model adaptation. While open-weight systems gained ground, they continued to lag behind the frontier, constrained by fragmented compute access and limited coordination. No major new open infrastructure came online this turn, and the absence of centralized investment or policy support prevented a larger leap.

The EU moved to operationalize the Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes, integrating initial sensor arrays into ENISA’s new AI Situational Awareness Hub. The rollout is underway but incomplete: real-time telemetry now flows from pilot sites in energy and finance, yet full deployment across critical sectors remains months behind schedule. Interpretation systems are still manual, and no autonomous countermeasure has been cleared for deployment. The nodes are not yet a unified system—fragmented governance and technical incompatibilities across member states have delayed full integration. This is not a completed measure, but the beginning of a phased activation.

Public sentiment declined to 18.0, reflecting growing unease over AI-driven decision-making in critical infrastructure. Protests against data centre expansions occurred in Sweden and Italy, with blockades disrupting construction. In Bavaria, sabotage damaged a transformer linked to an AI cluster, causing localized blackouts. These events were widely reported and amplified fears of unaccountable systems, but the unrest remained regionally contained. The drop in sentiment is notable but not systemic—no broad collapse in institutional trust has yet materialized.

In response to emerging bio-risk concerns, the EU announced the **EU Bio-Risk Early Warning System**, a large category 6 measure targeting AI-augmented bio-threats. Hosted jointly by the ECDC and European Defence Agency, it will deploy sequencing nodes at labs, airports, and wastewater sites. Legal authority was asserted under public health and emergency clauses, though academic pushback is mounting. Funding is approved, but procurement delays and jurisdictional disputes have slowed progress. Full deployment will take at least two full turns. This is the Union's new initiative for the period—no priority has been formally named.

The announcement placed further strain on exhausted political capital. The **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** remains active, continuing its charge of −3. Launching a new large measure (−3) without pausing or completing the prior initiative, combined with the ongoing cost of the current priority (−1), resulted in a total portfolio charge of −7. Starting from 2.0, this brings political capital to −5.0. No new funding could be unlocked. Several member states withheld full cooperation, citing legal overreach and capacity limits.

The **proposal bonus of +4 for the Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps** remains pending and will be evaluated in a future turn, in accordance with the minimum recognition delay for high-cost measures. It has not been realized and is not reflected in current accounting.

A grassroots **municipal sovereignty network** grew in response, with cities like Utrecht and Thessaloniki deploying open-weight models to audit local systems independently. They were not waiting.
