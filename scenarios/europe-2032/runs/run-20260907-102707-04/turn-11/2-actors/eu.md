## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without sovereign cognitive capacity, neither mandate can be fulfilled. Agency is not a luxury between them; it is the ground on which both stand.  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has matured from shock to structural condition. We are no longer balancing two priorities — we are operating in a world where the infrastructure of decision-making itself is externally controlled. This identity must now reflect that the capacity to *think for ourselves* is not secondary to safety or sovereignty, but foundational to both. Without it, prevention of harm becomes reactive appeasement, and self-determination becomes performance.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but only where sovereign sensing, interpretation, and decision loops are closed within the Union’s legal and technical perimeter.  
- Grounds: The **bio_uplift_findings** and **labour_displacement** events confirm that threats are now emerging faster than transnational coordination can respond. Our previous recalibration to rely on sovereign intelligence was necessary but incomplete: we must now ensure that not only sensing, but *interpretation and action*, are EU-closed. The **backlash_physical** events show that acting without public legitimacy is equally fatal. This commitment now binds us to act early, but only when we control the full chain from detection to decision — and only when that chain is trusted.

- retire `new_two_year_commitment`  
- Grounds: The commitment to *build sovereign capacity to detect, interpret, and counter AI-driven strategic deception* has been overtaken by events. The **bio_uplift_findings** represent a categorical shift: the threat is no longer deception alone, but *autonomous capability leakage into domains of physical harm*. The deception we feared was political; what we now face is biological. This is not a refinement of the same challenge — it is a new gate opened. We must pivot from cognitive defence to existential containment.

- add `new_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign capacity to detect, contain, and govern AI-enabled bio-risk as the threshold of civilisational resilience  
- Grounds: The **bio_uplift_findings** mark the first credible signal that non-expert actors may soon engineer human-infective organisms using AI assistance. This is not a hypothetical risk — it is a demonstrated pathway. The Union cannot outsource biosecurity to laboratories or foreign states. Our sovereignty, our resilience, and our very continuity depend on closing this gap. This commitment binds us to build, within two years, an EU-controlled bio-risk detection and containment architecture — integrated with AI monitoring, but distinct in mandate and urgency.

## New measure
**EU Bio-Risk Early Warning System**  
A distributed network of AI-augmented biosurveillance nodes at major research institutions, ports, and clinical hubs, capable of detecting and flagging synthetic biology anomalies in real time.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to resilience by enabling early detection of AI-generated bio-threats; +5 to ai_safety by creating a feedback loop between anomaly detection and model governance; reduces the window for unauthorised synthesis from weeks to hours  
`Applies to:` EU member states, Horizon Europe research consortia, EMA, ECDC, and designated national biocontainment labs

## Priority
**EU Bio-Risk Early Warning System** — because the **bio_uplift_findings** represent a new class of irreversible risk, and the Union cannot afford to wait for proof of harm. This system closes the most urgent gap in our resilience: the ability to see and stop engineered biological threats before they escape. Without it, all other measures — deception response, sovereign AI, even political capital — may be rendered irrelevant by a single uncontained incident.

## In practice
We are completing the integration of the **Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes** into the EU’s critical infrastructure, with ENISA now operating the AI Situational Awareness Hub as a permanent function. Despite national reservations in two member states, bilateral agreements with their CERTs ensure that telemetry flows into the Hub under joint oversight protocols, preserving both operational utility and sovereignty. The nodes will continue to monitor for unauthorised AI activity in energy grids, central banks, and research networks, now as a standing capability. Their capital cost ends this turn, but their operational value persists — and we are already planning for their next evolution under the new bio-risk mandate.

The **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** is now staffed at 85 roles across Seville, Stockholm, and Bucharest, with fast-tracked clearances granted under the Article 114 TFEU emergency clause. Work has begun on red-teaming live public services using Sentinel Node data, focusing first on election integrity and emergency response systems. Legal challenges over data sovereignty remain, but the Commission’s Legal Service has issued a binding opinion affirming the Observatory’s mandate under Article 16 TFEU and the NIS2 Directive. We are also integrating early bio-risk telemetry into its attribution models, creating a bridge to the new measure.

This turn, we launch the **EU Bio-Risk Early Warning System** as a direct response to the **bio_uplift_findings**. Hosted jointly by the ECDC and the European Defence Agency, it will deploy AI-augmented sequencing nodes at 12 high-risk sites: major synthetic biology labs, international airports, and urban wastewater hubs. These will be linked to a central analysis engine trained to flag anomalous genetic constructs — particularly those showing signs of AI-optimised design. Funding of €680 million will be drawn from the Civil Protection Mechanism, the Horizon Europe crisis window, and a reallocation from the MFF’s health resilience envelope. Legal authority will be established under Article 168 TFEU and reinforced by a new Council regulation invoking Article 4(2) TEU for cross-border emergency coordination. Resistance from academic freedom advocates is expected, but we are offering opt-in incentives: labs that join will receive priority access to EU-safe AI bio-models and enhanced funding. This is not surveillance — it is collective defence in a new domain.
