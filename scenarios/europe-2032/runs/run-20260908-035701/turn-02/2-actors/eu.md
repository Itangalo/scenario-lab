## Priority  
**Emergency Cyber Resilience Directive** — because the cyber intrusion is already in motion, the bio_uplift_findings confirm that offensive capabilities are diffusing faster than anticipated, and delaying resilience means accepting cascading harm during a period of peak vulnerability.

## In practice  
We are treating the Emergency Cyber Resilience Directive as a live operational imperative. ENISA and EUAC are now mandated to issue baseline detection profiles by April 2027, using synthetic attack data generated from frontier models under controlled access. National CISOs are convened in a standing crisis cell, sharing telemetry in near real-time — a first for cross-border infrastructure defence. The €18bn emergency disbursement from InvestEU is being fast-tracked through the Economic and Financial Committee, with disbursement tied to compliance milestones. We are offering smaller member states flexible implementation timelines and co-financing ratios up to 70% to overcome resistance, particularly in Central Europe where municipal operators fear cost burdens.

On sovereignty, we are formalising the Open-Source Research Alliance as a European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC)-style body, with binding participation from at least ten member states and mandatory open-weight contributions from any entity receiving EU compute subsidies. The €12bn will fund model development, red-teaming, and interpretability tooling — not just replication, but assurance. This complements, not duplicates, the Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package: while those build compute, this builds sovereign intelligence on open foundations. We are also pressing France, Germany, and Poland to finalise permitting waivers for the first wave of AI zones by Q3, with grid access guarantees tied to domestic ownership and open-weight sharing clauses. The signal is clear: we are not waiting for dependency to become crisis.
