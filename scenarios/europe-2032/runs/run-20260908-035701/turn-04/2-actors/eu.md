## New measure  
### Strengthen Open-Source Research Alliance with Binding Safety and Access Clauses  
We are transforming the Open-Source Research Alliance into a conditionality mechanism: any entity receiving EU compute subsidies or grid access must contribute model weights, training data logs, and interpretability tooling to the Alliance, creating a sovereign, auditable, and safety-by-design stack under EU jurisdiction.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +8 (by anchoring open-weight development to EU infrastructure), openweight_capability +5 (by accelerating open replication of frontier techniques), ai_safety +3 (by mandating interpretability and red-teaming in funded models)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, private developers receiving public support, Horizon Europe and EIB grantees

## Priority  
**Strengthen Open-Source Research Alliance with Binding Safety and Access Clauses** — because the supply_chain_coercion event proves the Union cannot rely on external chokepoints, and the only leverage we can trust is one we build into our own infrastructure: open, auditable, and sovereign capacity that cannot be switched off or withheld.

## In practice  
We are treating the completion of the Emergency Cyber Resilience Directive as a milestone, not an endpoint. ENISA has now operationalised baseline detection profiles using synthetic attack data, and the cross-border CISO cell is sharing real-time telemetry across nine member states. The final disbursements from the €18bn InvestEU emergency fund are being released, tied to verified compliance with detection upgrades and red-teaming participation. Municipal resistance in Central Europe has been partially overcome through 70% co-financing offers and direct engagement via the European Committee of the Regions, though full harmonisation remains uneven. With the measure now finished, we are shifting focus to embedding its protocols into the NIS2 framework for long-term resilience.

On sovereignty, we are no longer treating open-source development as a parallel track — it is now the core condition of access to EU infrastructure. The Commission is finalising legal clauses that will bind all recipients of InvestAI Gigafactory subsidies, Tech Sovereignty Package incentives, and fast-tracked grid access to contribute model weights, training logs, and interpretability outputs to the Open-Source Research Alliance. This is being structured through Horizon Europe and EIB funding conditions, with compliance enforced by a new Digital Infrastructure Compliance Office under DG CONNECT. We are negotiating with French, German, and Polish regulators to align these clauses with the sovereign compute zone permitting process, ensuring that domestic ownership is paired with open contribution. The goal is clear: sovereignty without openness is dependency in disguise, and we are closing that loophole now.
