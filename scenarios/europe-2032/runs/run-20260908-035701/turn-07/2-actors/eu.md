## In practice  
The completion of the *InvestAI Gigafactories* marks a symbolic milestone: EU-anchored compute capacity is now sufficient to host full-scale agent workloads, though operationalisation lags. We are transferring responsibility for sovereign execution from DG CONNECT to the newly empowered Digital Infrastructure Compliance Office, which will integrate runtime logging, red-teaming mandates, and interpretability requirements into all Horizon Europe grants involving agent deployment. The Council’s Competitiveness Working Party remains divided — Austria and Sweden welcome procurement alignment, but Poland insists on transitional exemptions for legacy systems, citing implementation delays. We are offering bridging funding through the EIB to close the gap, conditional on binding commitments by Q1 2030.

Meanwhile, the unverified incidents in Germany and Finland have exposed a critical blind spot: while we mandate contributions to the Open-Source Research Alliance, we lack real-time capacity to assess whether those contributions carry emergent risks. With *openweight_frontier_release* now confirmed — a model at 58.0 capability leaked within weeks of the closed frontier — we cannot afford to wait for audits to catch up. The new monitoring framework will activate ENISA-led rapid evaluation cells in six member states, tasked with scanning major open releases for self-modification, goal persistence, and cyber exploitation potential. These cells will report to a central alert panel under Article 16a of the Digital Services Act, empowered to recommend temporary suspension of funding access or compute subsidies pending deeper review. Resistance from developer communities is expected; we are engaging the Free Software Foundation Europe early to co-design transparency safeguards that preserve openness while preventing weaponisation. This is not control — not yet — but it is sight. And right now, sight is what we’re losing fastest.
