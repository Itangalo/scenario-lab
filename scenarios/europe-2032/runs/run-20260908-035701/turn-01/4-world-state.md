### Accelerating Frontier, Lagging Defences  
The global AI frontier advances steadily, with closed models demonstrating new levels of autonomous coordination in research and cyber operations. `ai_capability` rises by 2.0, driven by sustained investment and compressed release cycles. The open frontier sees moderate progress: limited replication of recently disclosed capabilities allows `openweight_capability` to increase by 2.0 to 42.0, narrowing the gap but remaining within the bounds of gradual accumulation. This diffusion occurs just as a major AI-generated cyber intrusion is confirmed across multiple sectors, including two EU grid operators.

### Emergency Response Amid Fracture  
The EU invokes emergency powers under DORA to propose the **Emergency Cyber Resilience Directive**, mandating AI-augmented intrusion detection and cross-border red-teaming. While technically sound, the measure faces immediate resistance. Smaller member states, particularly in Central Europe, protest the cost and centralisation, slowing consensus. An emergency fund of €18bn is proposed to secure buy-in, but without a clear source—whether reallocation, borrowing, or new revenue—its approval is delayed. ENISA begins preliminary drafting of standards, but no implementation occurs this turn. As a high-cost measure, full effect is at least two turns away, even under emergency procedures.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** progresses slowly, with France, Germany, and Poland agreeing in principle to host AI compute zones. However, private investment remains cautious, and permitting acceleration is bottlenecked by local opposition. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** site selection starts, but no contracts are awarded. Both category 4 measures remain in flight, yielding no sovereignty bonus yet.

### Political Costs Mount  
The cyber incident and biosecurity concerns trigger public anxiety. Media coverage focuses on job displacement and vulnerability, nudging `public_sentiment` down to 40.0. The emergency directive, while justified, is seen by some as overreach, and no immediate public benefit offsets the perception of crisis.

`eu_political_capital` drops sharply: the portfolio carries three active measures (two large, one small), costing −8, and the priority adds −1. No measure finishes, and no proposal bonus applies — the new directive responds to ongoing threats but was not triggered by a single recent event. The capital drop reflects overextension and fragmented cohesion.

`eu_ai_sovereignty` declines by 1: `ai_capability` rose by 2.0, and no capacity event occurred. No new sovereign access was secured, and dependence on foreign compute persists.

`ai_safety` falls to 32.0 as capability outpaces assurance, and no safety-focused measure lands. `resilience` remains unchanged at 39.0 — no progress is made on cyber hardening due to the delayed rollout of the emergency directive.
