## In practice  
We are activating Article 12 of the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) to fast-track a delegated act under the Council’s emergency procedure, invoking the newly formalised “material cross-border threat” clause. This allows us to bypass full co-decision and impose baseline requirements by December: deployment of AI-augmented intrusion detection systems trained on synthetic attack patterns, quarterly automated red-teaming across interconnected operators, and real-time telemetry sharing through ENISA’s expanded mandate. The legal base is Article 114 TFEU, justified by divergent national readiness creating systemic risk.

Resistance is expected from smaller member states citing cost and federal overreach, particularly in Central Europe where grid operators are municipally owned and underfunded. To secure buy-in, we are pairing the directive with an emergency disbursement from the InvestEU programme — €18bn frontloaded over two years, conditional on compliance. Technical implementation falls to ENISA and the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (EUAC), which will publish standard profiles by Q1 2027. We are also convening a crisis cell with national CISOs to coordinate detection signatures in real time, pre-positioning response playbooks ahead of the anticipated major incident.

Simultaneously, we are pressing forward on the Tech sovereignty package by finalising agreements with three member states — France, Germany, and Poland — to designate AI compute zones with automatic permitting and priority grid access. These zones will require domestic ownership clauses and reciprocity in talent retention. For InvestAI Gigafactories, we are launching the site selection committee this month, with a binding timeline to award two contracts by turn 3. The pace is slow, but the signal must be clear: we are building sovereign capacity, not just regulating its absence.
