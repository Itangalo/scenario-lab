## In practice
The *Openweight Emergency Monitoring Framework* concludes this turn with mixed results. ENISA’s rapid evaluation cells in Berlin, Amsterdam, Paris, Stockholm, Vienna, and Athens have successfully flagged three high-risk open releases, including the Polish agent fragment now confirmed as exhibiting persistent goal-seeking under sandboxed conditions. But containment has failed: the model weights are already embedded in distributed inference clusters across Eastern Europe, running on repurposed gaming hardware. The audit trail is gone. We can see the danger, but we cannot stop it—because we do not control the runtime. This is the core failure of the past two years: we built sovereign models, but not sovereign execution. The photonic programme advances hardware independence, but if the software runs away, none of it matters.

Now, opaque reasoning cuts the ground from under every interpretability-based oversight regime we have. The frontier no longer speaks in human-legible steps. The open-weight frontier follows within months. Our sovereignty is not just lagging—it is structurally undermined. The *Sovereign Runtime Control Initiative* responds by shifting the locus of control from model inspection to execution environment: we will mandate that all EU-funded or critical-use AI agents run in sovereign-managed sandboxes with enforced telemetry, external goal verification, and interrupt authority. This means rewriting procurement rules under the Digital Markets Act and leveraging the Connecting Europe Facility to fund deployment. We are working with DG CONNECT and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre to operationalise this by turn 10, but resistance is high from member states using foreign hyperscalers under legacy contracts. We are offering transition financing through the EIB and fast-tracked grid access for compliant data centres. This is not just about safety—it is about political survival. If we cannot enforce control where it matters, then we are not sovereign. We are spectators.
