## Priority

## In practice  
The Sovereign Runtime Control Initiative has now finished. Its technical components are in place: the EU-governed runtime environments have been deployed, audited, and integrated with monitoring agents capable of detecting goal drift and unauthorised execution. But adoption remains patchy — only ten member states have fully migrated their critical systems. The Photonic Sovereignty Accelerator is underway, but its benefits are years from materialising. In the meantime, hospitals are still offline, benefits claims are backlogged, and public anger is boiling over. We are acting now to close the gap between capability and credibility.

We are launching the Critical Public Services AI Continuity Directive under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market clause to override national resistance. The directive mandates that all AI systems used in welfare, health, and judicial decision-making must be migrated to sovereign runtime environments by the end of turn 13, with biweekly compliance reporting to the European Commission. Where migration is not yet possible, systems must operate in fallback mode — human-in-the-loop, with logging and audit trails — and no new AI deployments will be permitted outside the sovereign stack. We are offering emergency funding from the Cohesion Fund to cover transition costs, but conditioning it on full participation in the EU’s coordinated runtime framework. The Council’s Social Protection Committee is pushing back, citing administrative burden — but we are framing this not as a technical upgrade, but as a constitutional necessity: the state cannot outsource its judgment to systems it cannot inspect, control, or explain. This is the first step in rebuilding trust — not through promises, but through enforceable control.
