### Sovereignty Under Siege

The Union’s frontier access was abruptly severed, cutting off hospitals, ministries, and public agencies from the AI systems they relied on. The outage deepened the crisis in welfare and health services, already strained by the ongoing technical oversight strike. With no recourse, member states scrambled to restore basic functionality, exposing the fragility of externally dependent infrastructure.

Despite completion of the Sovereign Runtime Control Initiative, only ten member states have fully migrated critical systems to the new EU-governed runtime environments. The gap between technical readiness and operational adoption remains wide. In response, the EU launched the **Critical Public Services AI Continuity Directive**, a small category 6 measure, mandating full migration by turn 13 and imposing fallback protocols where migration lags. Invoking Article 114 TFEU, the Commission framed the move as a constitutional imperative, not merely a technical fix.

However, resistance is mounting. The Social Protection Committee warns of administrative overload, and two member states threaten legal challenges, arguing the directive overreaches. Emergency Cohesion Fund disbursements are tied to compliance, but disbursement delays fuel resentment. The directive yields early resilience gains as fallback systems stabilize some services, but public sentiment remains stagnant—citizens see disruption, not recovery.

The Photonic Sovereignty Accelerator continues in flight, but delivers no near-term sovereignty boost. Meanwhile, `ai_capability` rises by 1.5, driven by incremental photonic integration, further eroding EU leverage. With no safety improvements, `ai_safety` holds at 5.0. The access denial event triggers a −2 sovereignty penalty, and rising capability imposes a −1 decay, overwhelming the +1 from the new directive’s partial effect.

Political capital barely budges. The portfolio charge remains severe, and the new directive’s proposal bonus is minimal—it responds to recent shocks, but its scale is small. The priority adds cost without accelerating delivery. Trust in institutions remains low, and no sentiment lift occurs.

### A Fragile Foundation

Sovereignty is no longer theoretical—it is being tested in real time. The Union has tools, but not yet authority. Control without adoption is hollow. And legitimacy, once lost, cannot be decreed back into existence.
