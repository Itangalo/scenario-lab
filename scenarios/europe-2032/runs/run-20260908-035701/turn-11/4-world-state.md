### The Stack Fractures

A sudden leap in closed-model capability—driven by tightly integrated photonic inference—reshapes the frontier. Attackers now execute complex, adaptive cyber intrusions in hours, not days. This capability jump, while narrow, destabilises trust in all AI-mediated systems. Simultaneously, a major automated decision scandal erupts: a national benefits agency’s AI, relying on a closed model, denied thousands of claims due to an unexplained logic shift. A court rules the system violated due process, deepening public anger.

Export controls tighten globally. Under the standing US consolidation posture, allied access is preserved—but only through rationing. The EU’s leverage is weak, and its access to advanced chips is now contingent on political alignment, not market rights.

The Runtime Integrity Emergency Response completes, patching the immediate runtime flaw. But the fix proves brittle—only 18 of 27 member states implement it fully. Two states continue bilateral arrangements, eroding union cohesion.

The EU launches the **Photonic Sovereignty Accelerator**, a large category 4 measure, declaring it a security imperative. However, resistance in the Competitiveness Council delays funding approval. Only partial reallocations occur, slowing deployment. The measure begins but yields no immediate sovereignty gain.

Political capital collapses further. The portfolio charge is severe, and no completed measure offsets it. Public sentiment drops as the benefits scandal and runtime fragility dominate headlines. Labour unrest grows, with auditors and public-sector technicians staging a technical oversight strike, halting AI use in welfare and health systems across several capitals.

### Hollow Fixes, Harder Realities

The EU’s revised mandate—sovereignty as the foundation of safety and agency—now reflects reality. But without control of the physical stack, governance remains performative. The Union scrambles to build the infrastructure of autonomy, but the clock is running out.
