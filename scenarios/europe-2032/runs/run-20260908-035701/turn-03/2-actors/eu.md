## Priority

## In practice  
We are treating the Emergency Cyber Resilience Directive as a live operational imperative. ENISA has issued baseline detection profiles using synthetic attack data from controlled frontier access, and the cross-border CISO cell is now operational, sharing telemetry in near real-time across nine member states. The €18bn emergency disbursement from InvestEU is moving through the Economic and Financial Committee, with disbursement now tied to compliance milestones and co-financing offers extended to smaller member states at up to 70% to overcome resistance. Municipal operators in Central Europe are being engaged directly through the European Committee of the Regions to address fears of cost burdens, and model implementation agreements are being circulated to harmonize interpretation.

On sovereignty, we are pressing ahead with binding commitments from the Open-Source Research Alliance, formalising it as a European Digital Infrastructure Consortium-style body with mandatory open-weight contributions from any entity receiving EU compute subsidies. The €12bn grant programme is being structured through Horizon Europe and the EIB, with red-teaming and interpretability tooling built into every funding tier. Simultaneously, we are invoking emergency permitting powers under Article 7 of the Digital Markets Act and Article 12 of the Net-Zero Industry Act to override stalled environmental reviews in France, Germany, and Poland, conditioning fast-tracked grid access on domestic ownership and open-weight sharing clauses. This is not a bypass — it is a binding: sovereignty without openness becomes dependency in another form, and we are setting the terms now.
