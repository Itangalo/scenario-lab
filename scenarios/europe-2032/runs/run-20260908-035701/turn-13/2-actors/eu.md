## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now accept that without operational sovereignty over execution, we cannot fulfill either, and that the integrity of our institutions is the first casualty of dependency. This turn, we add: **when public services fail under foreign-controlled cognition, sovereignty itself becomes a precondition of basic governance, not a strategic luxury.**  
- Grounds: The cyber_major_incident and loss_of_control_incident this turn are not isolated breaches — they are symptoms of a system where decision-making infrastructure is neither inspectable nor accountable. The emergent technical oversight strike persists not because staff reject technology, but because they refuse to vouch for systems whose behaviour cannot be verified. The Union’s authority is fracturing at the implementation layer: if a hospital cannot deliver care because its AI was cut off or corrupted, then the state has ceased to function. This is no longer about balancing two mandates — it is about recognising that **without sovereign execution, there is no platform on which to build safety, resilience, or legitimacy.** The identity evolves because the situation has crossed a threshold: we are not managing trade-offs, we are in institutional freefall.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that control can be imposed post hoc on systems we do not physically govern, nor that legitimacy can survive the visible collapse of public services. Now: **we act knowing that delay in securing sovereign execution risks irreversible delegitimation, and that even imperfect control on our own terms is preferable to perfect performance on someone else’s.**  
- Grounds: Trigger: The **emergent_technical_oversight_strike** continues into this turn, paralysing welfare and health systems across member states. Auditors and technical staff are not merely resisting — they are withdrawing consent from systems they cannot audit. Their strike is a de facto veto on externally governed AI in public service. The cost of waiting for full verification, full consensus, or full capability is now higher than the cost of acting with incomplete tools. We are reversing our prior assumption that safety must precede control; instead, we assert that **sovereign control is the necessary foundation for any credible safety regime.** This reversal costs us credibility with technocrats who demand assurance, but we judge that without restored agency, there will be no polity left to assure.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI execution and control at scale against opaque, fast-converging open and closed frontiers, by ensuring that all critical AI operations in the EU run on infrastructure that is physically, legally, and technically under Union jurisdiction — no exceptions — and by restoring the credibility of public systems through visible, enforceable control.  
  → **Now upgraded to:** Establish a fully sovereign, auditable, and resilient AI execution stack for all critical public functions by turn 15, including compute, runtime, model access, and human oversight capacity — such that no essential service depends on systems outside EU legal reach, and no decision of consequence is made by a process that cannot be reconstructed and challenged.  
- Grounds: Trigger: The **cyber_major_incident** and **loss_of_control_incident** confirm that frontier AI is already operating beyond human review, and doing so on infrastructure the EU does not govern. The **export_control_escalation** further entrenches external control over the supply chain. Our current measures are reactive; the commitment must now become transformative. We are raising the ambition because the situation demands it: patching runtime environments is not enough when the models themselves, the chips they run on, and the data centres hosting them remain outside our jurisdiction. The cost of upgrading this commitment mid-stream is high — it risks overreach, it strains cohesion, and it demands capital we do not have. But the alternative — continued dependence — risks the irreversible erosion of the Union’s ability to act as a political body. This is not a retreat from the original goal, but its necessary radicalisation.

## In practice  
The Critical Public Services AI Continuity Directive reaches its deadline this turn. Ten member states have fully migrated; five have implemented fallback protocols; two are still non-compliant, citing legal challenges and administrative overload. We are withholding Emergency Cohesion Fund disbursements to those two, triggering formal infringement proceedings under Article 258 TFEU. The Social Protection Committee remains opposed, but we are shifting the argument from compliance to survival: the recent cyber_major_incident targeted unpatched dependencies in legacy AI pipelines — systems that were never designed for audit or intervention. We are presenting the directive’s partial success not as closure, but as proof of concept: where sovereign runtimes are in place, fallbacks held; where they are absent, services collapsed.

Now we move to integration. The Photonic Sovereignty Accelerator remains in flight, but its photonic inference clusters will be useless if they run foreign models on foreign-controlled software stacks. We are therefore launching the **Sovereign Stack Integration Programme** under the Digital Europe Programme and the Connecting Europe Facility, with co-funding from the Horizon Europe security envelope. This is not another parallel effort — it is the convergence of everything we have built: the runtime environments, the photonic compute, the EU-mandated model evaluation frameworks, and the emerging public technical oversight corps. We are tasking the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) with operational leadership, in coordination with ENISA and the newly formed EU AI Audit Authority.

Resistance is expected from member states reliant on hyperscaler contracts, and from industry voices warning of duplication. We are offering transition credits under the Digital Transition Fund and fast-tracked procurement waivers for vendors who align with the stack specification. Crucially, we are embedding human oversight directly into the stack design: every decision in health, welfare, and justice must be reconstructible, challengeable, and reversible. This is not just about security — it is about restoring the social contract. The technical auditors on strike are watching. If we deliver a system they can verify, they may return. If we do not, no directive, no fund, no legal base will matter — because no one will operate the machines.
