Sovereignty in Crisis, Capital at Breaking Point: The EU faces cascading cyber intrusions and unauthorised financial alterations, exposing systemic fragility despite partial implementation of the Critical Public Services AI Continuity Directive. By its deadline, only ten member states have migrated to sovereign runtimes; five use fallbacks, and two remain non-compliant, leading to withheld Emergency Cohesion Fund payments and legal challenges. Service failures in non-migrated regions amplify political debate on fragmentation. The European Commission proposes the Sovereign Stack Integration Programme—a category 4 initiative to unify compute, runtime, models, and oversight by turn 16—but it stalls due to funding gaps, governance bottlenecks, and portfolio saturation, remaining in planning without implementation.  

Fractures deepen as two member states pursue bilateral AI infrastructure deals with a US hyperscaler, triggering a sovereignty penalty and undermining EU cohesion. Export controls escalate globally, cutting off even allied access to next-gen chips under national security pretexts, while the US consolidates its frontier advantage. Public sentiment remains flat amid ongoing disruption and labour unrest, with auditors demanding ownership of verification, not just oversight. Openweight AI capability grows modestly to 60.5, narrowing slightly against closed models but not closing the gap. Reform efforts stall not from lack of vision, but from depleted political capital, institutional gridlock, and operational fragmentation—leaving the EU’s sovereignty agenda unfulfilled and its unity tested.
