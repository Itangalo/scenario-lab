## Priority  
**Hardened Critical Infrastructure Directive** — because compliance remains uneven, resistance persists in key member states, and without binding adoption of detection and red-teaming mandates, even breakthrough defensive tools will remain isolated and underused.

## In practice  
We are pressing forward on three fronts: mandate, deployment, and trust. The Hardened Critical Infrastructure Directive remains our anchor. The Council’s Competitiveness formation is now in trilogue, and we are using the InvestAI Gigafactories fund as leverage to secure Hungary’s and Slovakia’s support — offering co-financing for joint modernisation of regional utilities in exchange for dropping objections to phased compliance. Italy’s concerns are being addressed through targeted technical assistance from the Digital Europe Programme, with deployment timelines adjusted for regional capacity. The legal text now includes binding thresholds: all large operators must have AI-powered intrusion detection and red-teaming in place by turn 5, with smaller entities following by turn 6. ENISA’s red-team protocols, built under AI Act research exemptions, are being piloted in France and the Netherlands, and early results will feed into the final directive.

Simultaneously, we are scaling the response to the cyber_defence_breakthrough. The new European AI Defence Corridor will integrate the consortium’s behavioral anomaly detection system across critical sectors, linking it to automated patching and incident escalation protocols. The Cyber Defence Acceleration Fund is being re-profiled to prioritise this integration, with 60% of its allocation now directed to systems compatible with the new detection standard. Member states are being invited to nominate priority operators for fast-track onboarding, with technical support coordinated through ENISA and the Joint Research Centre. This is not just about technology — it is about creating a live, interoperable defence layer that learns from every intrusion.

But public trust is fraying. The automated_decision_scandal has deepened suspicion, and protests over logistics automation are spreading. We are holding back on new adoption measures until the Hardened Directive is law — rushing forward with public AI use now would be political suicide. Instead, we are preparing a transparency campaign around the new defensive systems, showing how AI is being used not to decide, but to protect. The message is clear: we are not slowing AI. We are making sure it cannot be turned against us.
