AI capability rises to 59.5 by mid-2027, driven by advances in autonomous agent frameworks, while AI safety stagnates due to absent implementation of protective measures. The EU responds to escalating threats by launching the European AI Defence Corridor, scaling a breakthrough behavioral anomaly detection system that marks a shift in the offense-defense balance. France, Germany, and the Netherlands lead rapid integration, but deployment is uneven—smaller states lack capacity, and Italy delays over liability concerns.

The Hardened Critical Infrastructure Directive advances through trilogue with a compromise: phased compliance linked to InvestAI Gigafactories funding and binding thresholds for large operators by 2028, though enforcement remains weak and penalties absent. The €12bn Cyber Defence Acceleration Fund supports red-team pilots and Defense Corridor rollout, but absorption challenges persist.

Public trust erodes further following a court ruling exposing systemic flaws in an AI-assisted benefits system and amid growing labor unrest from automation in logistics. Political capital declines, limiting the Commission’s ability to advance new initiatives. Despite defensive progress, resilience gains are narrow, systemic vulnerabilities endure, and EU sovereignty weakens as global AI development outpaces regional defenses.
