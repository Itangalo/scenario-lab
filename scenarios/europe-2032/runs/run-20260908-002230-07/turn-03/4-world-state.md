### The Defence Corridor Opens — But Trust Closes

The EU launches the European AI Defence Corridor, a bold effort to scale a breakthrough detection system across energy, healthcare, and transport networks. The system, proven in pilot environments, identifies swarm intrusions by behavioural anomaly rather than signatures — a first real shift in the offence-defence balance. Member states express interest, and France, Germany, and the Netherlands fast-track integration using redirected funds from the Cyber Defence Acceleration Fund. Yet rollout is patchy: smaller states lack technical capacity, and Italy delays participation pending clarity on liability for automated responses.

Meanwhile, the Hardened Critical Infrastructure Directive remains in trilogue. Germany and France push for binding adoption, but Hungary and Slovakia resist, demanding more co-financing. A compromise is reached: phased compliance tied to InvestAI Gigafactories grants, with binding thresholds for large operators by mid-2028. However, enforcement mechanisms remain weak, and no penalties are agreed for non-compliance.

Public sentiment worsens. A high-profile court ruling confirms systemic errors in an AI-assisted benefits system, deepening distrust. Labour protests intensify as logistics firms deploy AI-driven automation at scale, with visible job losses in major hubs. The Commission delays any new adoption initiatives, fearing backlash.

Behind the scenes, offensive AI capabilities advance. Autonomous agent frameworks improve, pushing global ai_capability to 59.5. No new safety measures have landed on shipped systems, and the gap with ai_safety widens. Defensive gains are real but narrow — resilience rises slightly, yet systemic fragility remains. Sovereignty erodes further as global compute growth outpaces EU build-out. Political capital falls under the weight of four large measures and internal friction.

### A Fragile Shield

The Defence Corridor is a technical success in early deployments, but its political and institutional foundations are strained. Without unified mandate and trust, even the best tools risk being siloed.
