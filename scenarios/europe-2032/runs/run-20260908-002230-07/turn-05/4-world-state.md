### A Shift in Strategy

The EU doubles down on anticipatory defence, launching the EU Threat Anticipation Facility — a centralised AI system designed to simulate novel cyber threats using red-team data, infrastructure logs, and open-weight model behaviour. Housed under ENISA and backed by €1.8bn from unspent cyber funds, it aims to close the cognitive gap exposed by the recent ransomware breach and the sudden collapse of human-readable reasoning in frontier models.

Despite high ambitions, implementation stumbles. The Sovereign Red-Teaming Network, now the priority, faces resistance from two leading AI labs that refuse full access, citing research autonomy. Legal enforcement under Article 12 drags into preliminary hearings, delaying the first simulations. Only partial data sharing is achieved, undermining the Facility’s training base.

### The World Moves Faster

Global AI capability leaps to 67.5, driven by systems that no longer expose their reasoning — a shift that blinds most oversight regimes. With no safety measure yet landing on shipped models, and the opaque_reasoning event erasing prior interpretability gains, ai_safety plummets by 7 points. The gap between capability and control widens dangerously.

Openweight capability rises to 55.0, as leaked model variants circulate with enhanced cyber exploitation functions, now capable of generating zero-day logic flaws without supervision.

The Hardened Critical Infrastructure Directive and Cyber Defence Acceleration Fund formally conclude, but their impact is undercut by patchy enforcement and reactive tooling. Resilience dips slightly as new threats evolve faster than defences can adapt.
