## In practice  
We are executing under the shadow of opaque_reasoning: the moment human-readable chain-of-thought stopped working, our primary control lever snapped. The Sovereign Red-Teaming Network is our answer — not just red teams, but AI-augmented red teams, running continuous adversarial simulations against both infrastructure and models. ENISA is standing up operational nodes in Paris, Berlin, and Helsinki, with secure access channels to the largest EU-hosted models. We are invoking Article 12 of the AI Act to override lab objections, but we are also offering liability shielding and joint publication rights to secure cooperation. The message is clear: you can resist, but you will be tested — or you can collaborate, and shape how it’s done.

The Defence Corridor continues its rollout, now live in French energy grids and Dutch transport systems, but the ransomware breach showed it sees only what it has already learned. That is why we are launching the EU Threat Anticipation Facility — a next-generation system that learns not from past attacks, but from synthetic futures. It will ingest red-team results, open-weight model outputs, and real-time infrastructure telemetry to project novel exploit paths before they are used. This is not an advisory body. It will have authority to issue binding hardening directives to essential operators and to mandate model modifications when emergent risks are detected. The legal basis is Article 12 and the NIS2 Directive, and the first €1.8bn is drawn from unspent Cyber Defence Acceleration Fund reserves.

Public sentiment remains at 30.0 — anxious, sceptical, and increasingly mobilised. The adoption_success event gives us a foothold: we are preparing a communication campaign around the fall in hospital waiting lists in three member states, explicitly crediting EU-coordinated AI deployment. But we cannot ignore the job losses. We are not launching a new labour measure this turn, but we are preparing a pilot wage-insurance scheme with the European Labour Authority, to be announced alongside the Threat Anticipation Facility. The narrative must hold: we are not slowing AI. We are taking control of it — for security, for sovereignty, and for society.
