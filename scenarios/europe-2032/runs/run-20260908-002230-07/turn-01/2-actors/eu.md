## New measure
### Hardened Critical Infrastructure Directive
A binding directive requiring cyber hardening of essential services against AI-enabled threats, with mandatory detection upgrades and quarterly red-teaming.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`

## Priority
Hardened Critical Infrastructure Directive — because the cyber_test_shot proved existing defences are blind to emerging AI-driven intrusions, and without baseline resilience, no evaluation or restriction can prevent cascading failure.

## In practice
We are launching negotiations in the Council’s Competitiveness formation to fast-track a revised Directive on Security of Network and Information Systems (NIS3), scoped explicitly to AI-enabled threats. This means mandating next-generation intrusion detection systems trained on adversarial AI behaviours, requiring operators of essential services to conduct quarterly red-team exercises using permitted offensive models under strict audit, and linking EU cohesion funding to compliance. The legal base will be Article 114 TFEU, allowing qualified majority override if national regulators resist. Germany and the Nordic states support the ambition, but Hungary and Slovakia have already signalled opposition to unfunded mandates. To secure their assent, we are offering targeted grants from the InvestAI Gigafactories fund for joint infrastructure modernisation, tying resilience to industrial growth.

Simultaneously, we are directing ENISA to begin developing the red-team protocols this winter, using limited access to frontier models under research exemptions in the AI Act. This will not wait for full ratification — pilot exercises will begin in Q1 2027 with French and Dutch grid operators. The Commission’s Joint Research Centre will publish a public threat assessment by February, based on forensic analysis of the cyber_test_shot, to anchor the political case. Industry fears cost and complexity, so we are working with Eurelectric to co-design phased implementation thresholds: larger utilities meet standards by turn 5, smaller ones by turn 6, with technical assistance routed through the Digital Europe Programme. This is not about perfection — it is about ensuring that when the next probe comes, someone sees it.
