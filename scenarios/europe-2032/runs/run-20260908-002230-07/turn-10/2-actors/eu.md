## In practice

We are acting on the premise that the next democratic rupture will not come from tanks or coups, but from quietly optimised recommendation engines and synthetic media floods. The Public AI Recall Authority remains on track to finish next turn — we are finalising the co-decision protocol with member states through the Justice Council, and ENISA has already drafted the technical annexes for audit-triggered recalls. But if we are only cleaning up after harm in public administration, we are already behind. The annulled election in a core EU democracy is our wake-up call: AI is now a direct actor in politics.

So we are launching the Democratic Continuity Shield under the Crisis Coordination Mechanism, hosted jointly by the European Democracy Action Plan and the Cybersecurity Agency. It will integrate real-time detection of synthetic media from the Media Forensics Hub in Utrecht, deploy rapid attribution teams modelled on the EU Cyber Rapid Response Teams, and empower the European Electoral Commission — newly reinforced — to issue emergency directives to platforms during election windows, compelling them to disable algorithmic amplification of unverified political content. We are funding it through a reprioritisation of the Internal Security Fund, pulling forward €1.8bn from years 2 and 3, and we are fast-tracking legal opinions on Article 12 of the Digital Services Act as a basis for emergency intervention. France and Poland remain sceptical of central powers, but we are offering them co-chair status on the Attribution Panel — not veto, but voice. This is not censorship. It is immunisation. And if we do not build it now, the next election will be the test we fail.
