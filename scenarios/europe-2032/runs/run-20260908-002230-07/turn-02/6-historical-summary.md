AI capability rises to 57.0 in early 2027, driven by advances in autonomous agent frameworks, while AI safety declines due to delayed implementation of protective measures. A major AI-generated ransomware attack cripples hospital networks and disrupts power grids across multiple EU states, exploiting zero-day vulnerabilities at machine speed and highlighting the offensive lead. Attribution remains uncertain, but the attack is confirmed to use model-synthesised tooling, marking a milestone in adversarial AI.

In response, the EU fast-tracks the Hardened Critical Infrastructure Directive under Article 114, linking compliance funding to the InvestAI Gigafactories initiative to ease resistance from Hungary, Slovakia, and Italy. A compromise allows phased implementation, though delays are anticipated. The Commission launches the €12bn Cyber Defence Acceleration Fund, and ENISA begins drafting red-team protocols using frontier models, with pilot programs planned in France and the Netherlands. However, uneven technical capacity across member states slows absorption of resources.

Public sentiment declines amid hospital outages, a scandal involving an AI-assisted benefits system that wrongfully denies claims, and labor protests over automation in logistics. Political capital erodes further, and EU sovereignty slips as global AI development outpaces regional defenses. Despite these challenges, a European cybersecurity consortium achieves a breakthrough with AI-driven behavioral anomaly detection capable of identifying swarm intrusions, signaling a potential shift in the offense-defense balance—though deployment remains limited. Resilience improves marginally, but systemic vulnerabilities persist.
