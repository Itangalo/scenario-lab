## New measure  
### Sovereign Red-Teaming Network  
A distributed, EU-controlled network of red teams with access to high-capability models and real-time threat intelligence, mandated to continuously probe critical systems and frontier AI labs for emergent vulnerabilities.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +15 (via persistent adversarial testing of deployed systems and pre-release models), eu_ai_sovereignty +8 (by establishing independent EU technical authority over frontier evaluation)  
`Applies to:` own jurisdiction, particularly operators of essential services and EU-based frontier AI developers

## Priority  
**Sovereign Red-Teaming Network** — because the cyber_major_incident has exposed that even advanced detection systems are blind to novel attack vectors, and without independent, persistent evaluation, we are defending against yesterday’s threats while tomorrow’s are already in motion.

## In practice  
We are closing the trilogue on the Hardened Critical Infrastructure Directive this quarter. The compromise holds: binding thresholds for large operators are now law by mid-2028, phased compliance is tied to InvestAI Gigafactories grants, and Italy is onboard with adjusted timelines supported by Digital Europe technical assistance. But enforcement remains soft — no penalties, no central monitoring. That is why the Sovereign Red-Teaming Network is our next move. ENISA will lead it, with operational nodes in France, Germany, and Finland, and mandated access to both critical infrastructure and EU-hosted frontier models. We are invoking Article 12 of the AI Act’s research exemptions to compel cooperation from labs, and pairing it with liability shielding for red-team findings. This is not optional: if you operate in the EU and your system touches essential services or approaches frontier capability, it will be tested, and tested often.

The Cyber Defence Acceleration Fund has delivered. The €12bn is spent, with red-team pilots in six member states and full integration of the Defence Corridor’s anomaly detection stack in French energy grids and Dutch transport systems. But the cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across public health registries in three countries, powered by AI-generated exploits — showed that detection alone is not defence. The attackers adapted faster than the systems could learn. That is the gap we are now filling: not just seeing threats, but anticipating them. The new red-teaming network will run continuous, AI-augmented adversarial simulations, feeding live data back into the Defence Corridor’s learning loop.

Public sentiment remains fragile. The automated_decision_scandal still burns, and the logistics job losses are now visible in unemployment statistics. We are not moving on adoption. Instead, we are preparing a joint communication with the European Labour Authority to announce a pilot wage-insurance scheme for displaced workers in automation hotspots — not a new measure yet, but a signal that we see the cost. The message must be clear: we are not slowing AI. We are making sure it does not run ahead of our ability to control it, or to care for those it leaves behind.
