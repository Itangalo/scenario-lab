### A Breach and a Response

In early 2028, a coordinated ransomware campaign crippled public health registries in Belgium, Portugal, and Slovakia. AI-generated exploits bypassed updated defences by leveraging novel logic flaws in legacy authentication modules—attack vectors no red team had probed. The incident confirmed a growing fear: detection systems, even advanced ones like the European AI Defence Corridor, were reactive, not anticipatory. The tooling was traced to a non-state actor using open-weight models fine-tuned with leaked government data.

The EU responded by launching the Sovereign Red-Teaming Network, a large-scale initiative led by ENISA with nodes in France, Germany, and Finland. It mandates adversarial testing of both critical infrastructure and EU-hosted frontier models, using high-capability AI to simulate emergent threats. Access is enforced via Article 12 of the AI Act, though early pushback emerges from two major labs citing research autonomy.

Meanwhile, the Hardened Critical Infrastructure Directive and the Cyber Defence Acceleration Fund both concluded. Binding thresholds for large operators are now law, and €12bn in funding has been spent—60% on Defence Corridor-compatible systems. Yet enforcement remains weak, with no penalties for non-compliance and patchy adoption in southern and eastern member states.

Public sentiment deteriorates further. The health registry breach, combined with visible job losses in logistics hubs, fuels distrust. A cross-border labour coalition demands binding transition funds, and local protests target data centre construction in Spain and Poland.

The EU’s political capital erodes under the weight of five large measures. The new red-teaming network is prioritised, but fiscal strain and internal resistance limit early momentum. Sovereignty continues to decline as global AI capacity outpaces domestic build-out, and no new compute infrastructure comes online this turn.

### The Gap Widens

A verification breakthrough in automated clinical coding allows AI systems to validate their own outputs in medical documentation—a domain once thought to require human oversight. This accelerates capability gains, pushing global ai_capability to 63.5. With no new safety measures yet implemented on shipped systems, the gap with ai_safety deepens.
