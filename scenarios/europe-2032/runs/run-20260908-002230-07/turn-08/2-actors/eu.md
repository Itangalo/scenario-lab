## New measure  
### EU-Wide AI Audit Regime  
A binding framework requiring independent, public evaluation of all AI systems used in public administration, social services, policing, and judicial support, with mandatory disclosure of training data sources, decision logic, and failure modes — and penalties for non-compliance.

`Category: 2 (Transparency and reporting)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +8 (by exposing systemic flaws), public_sentiment +10 (by restoring accountability), eu_political_capital +3 (by demonstrating responsiveness to scandal)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national governments, and public agencies across all member states — including contractors and AI-as-a-service providers used in public functions  

## Priority  
**EU-Wide AI Audit Regime** — because the automated_decision_scandal proves that harm is no longer hypothetical or external, but embedded in our own institutions; without immediate, visible accountability, public sentiment will collapse further, and no future measure — not even the Threat Anticipation Facility — will survive the political backlash.

## In practice  
We are acting on two fronts: one defensive, one foundational. On the defensive front, the automated_decision_scandal has shattered any remaining illusion that oversight is a luxury. We are now drafting the EU-Wide AI Audit Regime under Article 114 TFEU, using the internal market clause to override national resistance. The Commission’s Legal Service is preparing a directive that mandates third-party audits for all public-sector AI systems above a minimal risk threshold, with ENISA as the central coordinator and national data protection authorities as enforcement nodes. Penalties will include suspension of funding and public procurement eligibility — teeth that matter.

We are offering early adopters access to a new audit support pool funded from 10% of the repurposed Gigafactories surplus — €1.8bn over two years — to ease the burden on smaller administrations. But we are not negotiating on scope: this includes hiring tools, benefits allocation, policing risk scores, and any system that denies access to rights. The French government, implicated in the original scandal, is resisting, but we are holding firm — this is not about blame, it is about repair.

At the same time, we are advancing the Sovereign Threat Anticipation Facility in parallel, now reframed as the *Cognitive Sovereignty Initiative* in internal talks. We are briefing the European Council on a classified annex showing how open-weight models at 61.75 are already capable of autonomous cyber intrusion planning — and how without EU-controlled simulation, we will never see the next attack coming. We are proposing that the Facility absorb the final phase of the Sovereign Red-Teaming Network, merging live red-teaming with forward-looking simulation. But that depends on surviving the current political storm. The Audit Regime is not a distraction — it is the price of permission to build foresight. Without legitimacy, we have no mandate. Without mandate, we have no sovereignty.
