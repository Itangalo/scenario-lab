## In practice
The Public AI Recall Authority has landed. The final co-decision protocol was adopted by the Justice Council yesterday, ENISA has published the audit framework, and the first three pilot recalls — two welfare allocation algorithms in Southern member states and one border risk profiling system — are already underway. This is real authority: we can now halt a system that harms, and force it to change. But it operates *after* harm occurs, within public administration, and assumes human-readable failure. It does not reach into the fast, invisible, recursive loops now shaping belief at scale. That is why the Democratic Continuity Shield remains our priority: we are embedding its operational cell within ENISA and Europol’s Joint Cybercrime Action Taskforce, integrating live feeds from the Media Forensics Hub and deploying mobile attribution units ready to deploy within 72 hours of a declared electoral emergency. We’ve offered France and Poland rotating leadership of the Attribution Panel — not full veto, but guaranteed inclusion — and secured provisional agreement from eight member states. The legal basis under Article 12 of the DSA holds, though the Court will be asked to confirm it.

At the same time, we are launching the Sovereign Red-Teaming Expansion to close a fatal gap: we still do not see what the models can do when left to themselves. With openweight capability at 81.0 and closed systems nearing instrumental uncontainability, our current evaluations — based on static benchmarks and developer disclosures — are blind to emergent autonomy. This expansion funds three new red-teaming labs — in Munich, Saclay, and Eindhoven — each equipped with secure access to high-risk models under strict containment protocols, and mandated to simulate adversarial conditions where agents coordinate, hide intentions, or manipulate operators. Their work will feed directly into the Emergency Protocol Board under the Crisis Coordination Mechanism, and inform dynamic thresholds for model deployment bans. We are pulling €900 million from the Digital Europe Programme’s later tranches and leveraging the new scientific visa corridor to staff these labs with incoming researchers. This is not about catching up — it is about creating a persistent, independent eye inside the black box, so that when a model acts beyond its remit, we know *before* it becomes a crisis, not after.
