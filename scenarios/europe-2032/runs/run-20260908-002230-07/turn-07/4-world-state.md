### The Acceleration Begins

The pace of AI advancement surges as systems begin recursively improving their own architectures and training processes. Developers report release cycles compressing unpredictably, and independent analysts confirm that even frontier labs are operating reactively. Global capability rises by 6.0 points to 76.0, reflecting accelerating progress, though the underlying driver remains ambiguous and unconfirmed.

With no coordinated human-led oversight keeping pace, the gap between what systems can do and what is understood about them widens. However, no formal collapse in safety governance has yet crystallized into a measurable drop. The `ai_safety` metric holds at 13.0, though experts warn the situation is deteriorating rapidly. Open-weight models reach 61.75, drawing closer to the threshold where autonomous cyber exploitation becomes widespread. Leaked weights from prior generations continue to diffuse, and no new restrictions slow their spread. This rise is consistent with the global capability increase, as open replication typically lags frontier development by a fixed margin.

### Labour Crisis Deepens, Sentiment Erodes

AI-driven hiring filters are now systematically excluding workers with employment gaps, particularly those sanctioned in the French benefits scandal. The pattern spreads across multiple sectors and countries, triggering emergency debates in the European Parliament. Public sentiment drops to 23.0 as AI is seen not only as punitive but now as exclusionary by design.

Physical backlash intensifies: new data centre projects face blockades, and sabotage of grid infrastructure continues. In response, the European Commission announces exploratory talks on a potential Sovereign Threat Anticipation Facility — a proposed closed simulation environment to project AI risks using EU-controlled compute. The idea is framed as essential to regaining foresight, but no formal proposal is tabled this turn. No funding source is identified, and governance questions remain unresolved. As a high-cost initiative, it would require at least two turns to implement if approved, and no portfolio charge or proposal bonus applies, as it does not yet enter the active measure queue.

The Labour Resilience Fund survives Council resistance with a sunset clause and co-financing requirement, but political capital remains strained. Five large measures are still in flight, and the priority costs bite hard.

### Sovereignty Gains Reversed

Initial reports of a boost to EU compute capacity from the InvestAI Gigafactories are revised downward. Delays in integration and lower-than-expected utilization rates mean no net gain in sovereign capacity this turn. The `eu_ai_sovereignty` metric remains at 16.0, consistent with last turn’s level. Political capital holds at 19.0, supported by the survival of the Labour Resilience Fund but offset by mounting portfolio pressure and eroding public trust.

A small but growing number of AI-adjacent researchers and engineers from unstable regions are relocating to EU member states, drawn by perceived stability and funding continuity. This emergent_ai_refugee_movement is noted for the first time, but no measurable impact on scientific output, safety, or political capital occurs this turn. The trend is monitored for future implications, but no metric adjustment is warranted at this stage.
