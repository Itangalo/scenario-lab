# Statements: The European Union (turn 7)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI evaluation and threat anticipation capacity
- `labour_pact_response` (position): Bind AI-driven job displacement to structural transition mechanisms that include wage insurance, retraining, and sectoral transformation, co-governed with social partners — and condition future public AI procurement on compliance with these mechanisms

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without sovereign cognitive capacity to assess our own assumptions, and we will treat dependence on opaque external systems as a strategic failure in itself
  - Grounds: The rsi_onset event confirms that the pace of capability growth is now decoupled from human oversight, and with ai_safety at 13.0, even the developers no longer understand their systems. Our previous formulation treated uncertainty as a condition to be endured; we now recognise it as a vulnerability to be closed. Continuing to act without independent evaluation capacity — one that does not rely on borrowed models or unverifiable data — means governing blind. This turn, we are not merely accepting risk; we are institutionalising it. The commitment evolves to reflect that foresight is no longer optional: it is the core of sovereignty.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `labour_pact_response` — applied
  - Tier: position
  - Text: Bind AI-driven job displacement to structural transition mechanisms that include wage insurance, retraining, and sectoral transformation, co-governed with social partners — and condition future public AI procurement on compliance with these mechanisms
  - Grounds: The emergent_labour_crisis_escapes_welfare event reveals that displacement is no longer confined to public systems but is now embedded in private hiring, reinforcing exclusion. Our current position treats transition as a social stabiliser; it must now also function as a regulatory lever. By linking procurement — a major source of market power — to compliance with transition rules, we turn passive relief into active governance. This aligns with the Labour Resilience Fund’s design but raises the stakes: adoption is no longer just permitted, it is earned.
