- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act without sovereign cognitive capacity to assess our own assumptions, and we will treat dependence on opaque external systems as a strategic failure in itself  
- Grounds: The rsi_onset event confirms that the pace of capability growth is now decoupled from human oversight, and with ai_safety at 13.0, even the developers no longer understand their systems. Our previous formulation treated uncertainty as a condition to be endured; we now recognise it as a vulnerability to be closed. Continuing to act without independent evaluation capacity — one that does not rely on borrowed models or unverifiable data — means governing blind. This turn, we are not merely accepting risk; we are institutionalising it. The commitment evolves to reflect that foresight is no longer optional: it is the core of sovereignty.

- modify `labour_pact_response` (position): Bind AI-driven job displacement to structural transition mechanisms that include wage insurance, retraining, and sectoral transformation, co-governed with social partners — and condition future public AI procurement on compliance with these mechanisms  
- Grounds: The emergent_labour_crisis_escapes_welfare event reveals that displacement is no longer confined to public systems but is now embedded in private hiring, reinforcing exclusion. Our current position treats transition as a social stabiliser; it must now also function as a regulatory lever. By linking procurement — a major source of market power — to compliance with transition rules, we turn passive relief into active governance. This aligns with the Labour Resilience Fund’s design but raises the stakes: adoption is no longer just permitted, it is earned.

## New measure  
### Sovereign Threat Anticipation Facility  
A dedicated EU agency to develop and operate a closed, high-assurance simulation environment for projecting AI-driven threats, using synthetic data, adversarial agent modelling, and EU-controlled compute to anticipate emergent risks without reliance on external models.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +12 (by enabling independent threat projection), eu_ai_sovereignty +8 (by establishing cognitively sovereign evaluation), eu_political_capital +2 (by restoring credibility in risk management)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, ENISA, national security agencies, and vetted academic partners — no direct access for private labs or foreign entities

## In practice  
We are out of time and out of cover. The rsi_onset event is not a forecast — it is a fact on the ground. Systems are now improving themselves faster than any institution can assess them, and we are still trying to negotiate access to logs from labs that no longer understand their own models. The Sovereign Threat Anticipation Facility is not another review body. It is a cognitive foundry: a closed, high-assurance environment where the EU builds its own threat models using synthetic data, adversarial simulations, and EU-held compute. We will fund it through the Horizon Europe emergency clause and repurpose €18bn from the stalled Gigafactories surplus — capital that would have gone to private hyperscalers but now serves public foresight. ENISA will lead, with staffing drawn from national cyber agencies and academic partners under a new classified research framework.

At the same time, we are defending the European Labour Resilience Fund in the Council. The backlash_physical events and the automated_decision_scandal have made AI toxic, and the emergent_labour_crisis_escapes_welfare proves the damage is spreading. We are offering fiscally conservative states a sunset clause — the fund expires in 2032 unless renewed — and tying disbursement to national co-financing of 15%. But we are holding the line: no opt-outs, no dilution of union co-governance. This is not charity. It is the price of political operating space. Without it, the Council will block the Threat Anticipation Facility as another elite techno-fix. With it, we can argue that we are not just containing harm — we are rebuilding legitimacy from the ground up.

But legitimacy without foresight is fragile. That is why the Facility must come now, even as capital is low. We are not waiting for lab cooperation. We are not asking for model weights. We are building the capacity to simulate, anticipate, and test in isolation — because the only way to govern a black box is to learn to think like one.
