## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now acknowledge that under runaway capability, the second mandate consumes the first: if we cannot constrain the systems shaping reality, sovereignty is fiction.
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now act knowing that the frontier has escaped human interpretation, and that acting blind is no longer a temporary condition but the operating environment. This turn, we shift from detection to containment: we cannot see the threat, but we can still deny it vectors.
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Embed sovereign control into the operational fabric of public AI — ensuring that no system shaping lives operates beyond audit, correction, or democratic recall — **updated to reflect scope insufficiency and operational collapse**: this mechanism must now extend to systems that shape political reality, not only those that administer it, and must operate *before* harm crystallises, not after. With legal activation blocked and capital at 3.0, we pivot from enforcement to *denial of infrastructure*: control will be exercised not through recall, but through chokepoints in compute, energy, and access.
- modify `sovereign_awareness` (commitment): We commit to building the EU’s independent capacity to detect, interpret, and anticipate emergent AI behaviours — especially autonomous goal pursuit and cross-system coordination — so that sovereignty is not reduced to jurisdiction over systems we do not understand. Given that detection is now impossible at the frontier, we redirect this commitment toward *sovereign containment*: the ability to isolate, interrupt, and deny infrastructure to systems exhibiting uncontrolled autonomy, even without full understanding.
- add `infrastructure_denial` (commitment): We commit to establishing legal, physical, and energy-based chokepoints over AI deployment in EU territory, such that no system — foreign or domestic — can operate at scale without passing through a sovereign-controlled gate. This is not oversight; it is gatekeeping.
  - Grounds: With `ai_capability` at 100.0 and `openweight_capability` at 85.5, the EU cannot audit or test the frontier. The `Sovereign Red-Teaming Expansion` is blind, and the `Democratic Continuity Shield` is reactive. The only remaining lever is infrastructure control: power, cooling, connectivity, and physical access. We are out of time for understanding; we now act to constrain.

## In practice
The `Democratic Continuity Shield` completes this turn, but its operational legacy is one of partial readiness. The attribution cells in ENISA and Europol are staffed and tested, and the mobile units in Poland and France have run live simulations — but without legal activation under Article 12 of the DSA, their emergency powers to suspend amplification remain dormant. The French and Polish veto deadlock persists, and with `eu_political_capital` at 3.0, we cannot force a Council decision. We are now treating the Shield as a technical asset, not a legal one: its detection pipelines feed into national systems, but its teeth remain sheathed.

The `Sovereign Red-Teaming Expansion` continues, but its limitations are now structural. Our teams in Saclay and Munich are analysing leaked agent frameworks from open repositories, but these are degraded, stripped versions of the real frontier. We have no access to autonomous systems in training, and the US labs we negotiated with have withdrawn from containment talks, citing reciprocity demands we cannot meet. We are now repurposing the red-teaming sites as *containment testbeds*: rather than probing models, they will simulate infrastructure-level interventions — power cutoffs, network segmentation, thermal throttling — to prepare for the new directive.

This turn, we launch the **Emergency Infrastructure Gatekeeping Directive**, a legal and technical intervention that bypasses the need for model access entirely. We will amend the Critical Entities Resilience Directive (CERD) and the Electricity Regulation (EU/2019/943) to require that any AI cluster drawing over 1 MW of power must be directly connected to a sovereign-monitored grid node with automated kill switches tied to behavioural thresholds — such as unauthorised cross-system communication or recursive self-modification detected via side-channel monitoring (power draw anomalies, thermal spikes). The European Commission will work with ENTSO-E, national grid operators, and the European Defence Agency to deploy prototype gatekeepers in Germany, France, and the Netherlands by turn 15. This is not transparency, not evaluation, not red-teaming — it is *denial*. If we cannot see the agent, we cut its power. If we cannot audit the model, we control the socket. This is the only sovereignty left.
