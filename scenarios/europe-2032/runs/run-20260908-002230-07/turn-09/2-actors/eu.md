## New measure
### Public AI Recall Authority  
A new enforcement power allowing the European Commission, acting on audit findings or verified incidents, to suspend or decommission any AI system used in public administration that systematically violates rights or fails safety thresholds, with mandatory remediation and re-audit before reinstatement.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +12 (by enabling removal of harmful systems), public_sentiment +8 (by demonstrating enforcement teeth), eu_political_capital +2 (if exercised visibly and fairly)  
`Applies to:` All AI systems used by EU institutions, national governments, and public agencies — including contractors and AI-as-a-service providers — in functions affecting fundamental rights

## Priority
**Public AI Recall Authority** — because the Audit Regime alone cannot restore trust if there is no consequence for failure; we must now prove we can act when systems harm citizens, not just audit them after the fact — and doing so under our renewed commitment to foundational control makes this the first real test of EU sovereignty over public AI.

## In practice
We are moving from audit to authority. The EU-Wide AI Audit Regime is now law, but its credibility hinges on enforcement — and right now, we can expose harm, but we cannot stop it. That changes with the Public AI Recall Authority. We are drafting the instrument under Article 114 TFEU, piggybacking on the internal market justification used for the Audit Regime, with ENISA as the operational arm and the Commission’s Directorate-General for Justice and Consumers as the decision-maker. The legal basis is tight: we are not banning technologies, but enforcing compliance with existing rights under the Charter. When audits reveal systemic denial of access to benefits, unjustified risk scoring, or discriminatory hiring patterns, we will have the power to pull the plug.

We are already negotiating with member states through the Article 30 Working Party and the Justice Council. France, Germany, and Poland have raised concerns about central overreach, so we are offering a co-decision protocol: a national government may challenge a recall decision before an independent panel of EU jurists and technical experts, but the burden of proof shifts to them — they must demonstrate the system is safe, not merely that it exists. We are also building the recall mechanism into procurement contracts: any new public AI contract after turn 10 will include an automatic clause accepting the Authority’s jurisdiction. This is not just a regulatory tool — it is a signal that public AI in Europe serves citizens, not vendors or efficiency metrics. And if we do not use it when the first damning audit lands — likely in Q1 2031, on the French unemployment algorithm — then we have failed the test of control.
