### A Crisis of Trust, Not Capacity

The Continuity Assurance Framework is now fully operational, its quarterly drills completed across Berlin, Helsinki, and Valencia. Critical systems can fail over without external AI — a quiet victory for resilience, but one drowned out by deeper turmoil. The Frontier Access Guarantee collapses with no fanfare: Japanese partners formally exit, leaving only Canadian nodes, now insufficient to sustain the illusion of strategic insurance. The EU is no longer hedging — it is scrambling.

The Sovereign AI Core Programme Pilot launches in Valencia, integrating EU-controlled compute with hardened open models. But the stack, limited to ~55.0 capability, struggles with real-time emergency coordination. Glitches multiply under stress, and human operators treat it as a backup, not a replacement.

Worse, a wave of strikes sweeps public-sector AI deployments. Healthcare workers in France, Germany, and Belgium refuse to implement algorithmic triage; social workers in the Netherlands and Sweden reject automated benefit assessments. The unions demand accountability, not abolition — and the EU responds.

The Public AI Accountability Directive is proposed, mandating real-time logging, human review, and public auditability for all public AI systems. It targets transparency in high-impact domains, aiming to restore trust. But member states push back hard, citing cost and national sovereignty. Enforcement hinges on cohesion fund conditionality, a move that fuels resentment in Eastern Europe.

Political capital, already fragile, buckles. The directive is large and controversial, adding immediate cost. Public sentiment remains frozen at 15.0 — hostility persists, with no sign of thaw.

AI capability climbs to 78.0, driven by closed-lab progress. Openweight_capability follows to 59.0. But with no safety gains, and no measure yet landing on shipped systems, ai_safety holds at 5.0. The gap widens.

Sovereignty slips to 17.0: −1 from capability growth, and no capacity event offsets it. The physical build continues, but trust — in systems, in institutions, in the Union itself — is the true bottleneck.

### The Cost of Acting

The EU acts, but the cost is total depletion of political capital. No measure fails to start — just barely — but the margin is gone. The directive may pass, but only if every lever is pulled. The strike continues. The pilot wobbles. The floor holds. The foundation does not.
