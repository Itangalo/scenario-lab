## In practice
This turn, we close two chapters: the Continuity Assurance Framework and the Frontier Access Guarantee. The first stands as a floor — a minimal but proven capacity to operate without external AI. Its certification in Berlin, Helsinki, and Valencia is now live, auditable, and integrated into NIS3 enforcement. The second dies quietly: Japanese partners have formally withdrawn, and Canadian access, while intact, is no longer sufficient to claim strategic insurance. We are no longer hedging; we are building. The Sovereign AI Core Programme Pilot is now live in Valencia, integrating EU-anchored compute with open-weight models hardened to ~55.0 capability. The stack runs under continuous assurance protocols, with air-gapped nodes on standby. It is narrow, slow, and fragile — but it is ours.

The strike in public services forces our hand. Unions in healthcare and social administration have grounded algorithmic triage and benefit assessments, not out of Luddism, but because these systems are black boxes with lives on the line. We cannot afford to lose public trust, nor can we afford to halt adoption. So we act: the Public AI Accountability Directive will require every public AI decision — from hospital triage to unemployment sanctions — to be logged, explainable, and subject to human override, with real-time dashboards accessible to oversight bodies and the public. We will fund this through the Digital Europe Programme and enforce it via the European Data Protection Board, with DG HOME and Eurofound conducting compliance sweeps. Member states resist on grounds of cost and sovereignty, but we tie access to cohesion funds to implementation timelines — not as punishment, but as proof that we are serious about control that serves people, not just systems.

We are still building compute, still fighting grid delays in Bavaria and Wallonia, still fast-tracking brownfield sites in Spain and Finland. But this turn, we shift from infrastructure alone to legitimacy: sovereignty without accountability is just another form of capture. We will not let that happen. The pilot must work, the directive must land, and the strike must end — not with concessions, but with a new standard: AI in public service must be auditable, not just automated.
