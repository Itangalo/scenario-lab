### Frontier Leaps Ahead  
AI capability advanced sharply to 55.0 as automated verification in law, engineering, and clinical coding unlocked faster training loops and broader agent deployment. Systems now autonomously refine legal contracts and medical classifications with minimal review, eroding the boundary between narrow and general competence. The open-weight frontier made progress as a leading lab released a model within months of its closed counterpart—downloaded tens of thousands of times, it signaled growing momentum in open access to frontier-adjacent capability. However, structural delays in deployment infrastructure and safety filtering continue to limit uptake and adaptation. Openweight capability rose to 43.0, maintaining a realistic lag behind the frontier and reflecting incremental diffusion rather than systemic breakthrough.

### EU Ramps Up Sovereignty Push  
The EU accelerated the InvestAI Gigafactories, fast-tracking permitting and redirecting 3GW of reserved compute to a Swedish site. A joint Commission-member state committee bypassed regional delays, but local protests in Brittany and Norrland intensified, citing energy cost hikes. The Tech Sovereignty Package advanced bilateral deals with ASML and Zeiss, securing early delivery slots in exchange for R&D cooperation, though the permitting harmonization directive faced legal scrutiny over national sovereignty concerns.

### Assurance Efforts Meet Resistance  
The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD), now staffed temporarily under ENISA, issued its first evaluation demand to a non-EU hyperscaler. The firm contested the legal basis, calling the levy a trade barrier. A pilot tax on past revenue proceeded under the Digital Services Act, but full enforcement remains uncertain pending court rulings.

### Resilience Planning Begins, Implementation Delayed  
The Critical Infrastructure Resilience Surge (CIRS) was announced, proposing mandatory detection upgrades for energy and water systems. However, no implementation has begun. Only three member states have signaled preliminary interest, and cross-border red-teaming remains blocked by classification disputes. The proposal will require at least one full turn before initial effects can register, consistent with low-readiness rollout for high-cost infrastructure mandates. As a newly proposed high-cost measure, CIRS contributes only partially to the portfolio burden this turn.

### Public and Political Strain Mounts  
Public sentiment dipped as visible job losses in legal and engineering support roles grew, amplified by AI-generated disinformation in regional elections. Political capital declined, reflecting the governance strain of managing multiple active initiatives. Only *one* new measure—CIRS—was introduced this turn.
