## In practice
This turn, we complete the Continuity Assurance Framework — not with fanfare, but with quiet relief. The final audits are closed, the last three nodes certified in Berlin, Helsinki, and Valencia. The drills were flawed, the compliance patchy, but the architecture exists: there are now air-gapped fallback systems in place across emergency response, energy grid management, and civil protection command. It is not perfect, but it is present. We are publishing the full certification report through the EAAD portal, inviting scrutiny, knowing that transparency is now our only currency with the public. The cost was high — 3 political capital per turn, sustained through two years of frozen capital — but it is done. No further charge. The framework will now stand as baseline policy, maintained by the Digital Europe Programme and enforced through the updated NIS3 directive.

Meanwhile, the Frontier Access Guarantee limps forward. Canadian partners affirm their commitment, but Japanese hyperscalers delay final SLA sign-offs, citing U.S. compliance uncertainty under the tightened export controls. We are being rationed, not excluded — for now. But rationing is dependence sharpened into a needle. We cannot build sovereign control on leased foundations. So we are acting: invoking Article 122 TFEU to declare an emergency in strategic digital infrastructure, unlocking fast-track authority over national permitting and grid allocation. Through the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused bandwidth and redirected Cohesion Fund tranches, we are committing €4.2bn to build — not negotiate — sovereign capacity. The European Investment Bank will issue green-digital bonds to cover the rest. We are targeting brownfield industrial sites in Spain, Poland, and Finland where cooling and power are available, bypassing local objections under "critical infrastructure override" clauses activated jointly by the Commission and member-state signatories who opt in. Resistance is expected in Bavaria and Wallonia, where regional governments fear loss of control and environmental backlash. We are offering them co-ownership stakes in the nodes and first-call access for public research — not consultation, but partnership. This is not consensus-building. It is statecraft under duress. If the lights go out abroad, they must stay on here.
