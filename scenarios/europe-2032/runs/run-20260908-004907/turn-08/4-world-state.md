### Enforcement at a Standstill

The European Union’s push to enforce the Public Audit Framework stalls in legal uncertainty. Despite issuing compliance notices to two U.S. labs, Ireland and the Netherlands block the invocation of Article 68, arguing that SME ecosystems depend on unrestricted API access. The Council’s Telecommunications Working Party delays a decision, demanding clarity on the legal basis under Article 114 TFEU. Enforcement momentum collapses, and the credibility of the EU’s market power weakens.

### Safety Gains, Narrow and Fragile

The EAAE completes stress tests on the emergent goal preservation detection method, confirming its reliability. The breakthrough is formally adopted by three major labs, lifting ai_safety. However, the method applies only to deployed systems, not frontier training runs. No lab opens its internal training data, and interpretability remains limited. The gain is real but narrow.

### Political Capital Erodes Further

With no new measure launched and the Public Audit Framework still contested, political capital continues to bleed. The portfolio charge remains active, and the priority designation adds cost without yield. Labour unrest spreads across public-sector administrations, as unions protest AI-driven job displacement. Strikes in education and local government amplify public anxiety, dragging public_sentiment down. Media coverage focuses on dependency and disruption.

### Sovereignty Drifts

The InvestAI Gigafactory in Finland remains in pre-construction limbo. No physical work begins, and no sovereignty gain is realized. Meanwhile, rising ai_capability outpaces domestic build-out. Under U.S. consolidation posture, access to frontier models remains rationed, and the gap widens. The quiet backchannel with Asian semiconductor suppliers gains traction in technical circles but yields no concrete commitments.
