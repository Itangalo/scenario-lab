### The Denial and the Pivot

In July 2031, the EU was abruptly locked out of the latest frontier model—used in cancer research, emergency logistics, and national security planning—without explanation. Hospitals rerouted diagnostics, ministries scrambled, and firms froze deployments. The incident, later attributed to automated tooling from a compromised foundation model, spread across public services in three member states before containment. Defenders were overwhelmed; the offensive capabilities embedded in open agents had outpaced national cyber defences.

With political capital at 5.0 and public trust eroding, the Commission doubled down on coalition-building. The proposed Coalition for Open Access and Restraint (COAR) aimed to pool leverage over chip tools, rare materials, and secure routing with Japan, South Korea, Canada, the Netherlands, and Taiwan. The offer included shared evaluation nodes and preferential access deals. Yet early signals were mixed: Tokyo expressed interest but deferred commitment, Seoul demanded binding talent mobility terms not yet agreed, and Taipei raised concerns about exposure to U.S. secondary sanctions.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactory remained idle. No foundation was poured. Supplier hesitation hardened into观望—Tokyo Electron cited unresolved energy guarantees, SK Hynix pointed to unmet infrastructure milestones. The modification of the two-year commitment now hung in the balance: deliver proof of capacity within twelve months, or pivot entirely.

Public-sector strikes intensified as AI-driven automation advanced in back offices despite resistance. Media framed the COAR initiative as desperate diplomacy, while the unaddressed cyber incident fed narratives of systemic fragility. Trust in AI governance collapsed further.

### Sovereignty Drains, Capital Stretches Thin

The EU’s political capital, already critically low, absorbed the ongoing cost of the stalled gigafactory (−3) and the new priority (−1). The COAR proposal, though large and timely, faced scepticism—no immediate partner had signed, and the coalition’s leverage remained theoretical. No proposal bonus was earned. The portfolio charge held at −4.

Sovereignty fell to 11.0: no category 4 completion, capability rose by 0.5 (insufficient for −1), but the denial of frontier access—now a lived reality—counted as `eu_frontier_access_denied`, costing −2 under rule 5. Resilience dropped further as incident response lagged. ai_safety plunged due to uncontrolled agent behaviour and no safety mechanisms in new models. openweight_capability rose as tool-integrated agents leaked. public_sentiment fell to 15.0, nearing open hostility.
