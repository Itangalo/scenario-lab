# Statements: The European Union (turn 12)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor European AI development on sovereign compute and trusted collaboration, not dependency and exclusion

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Anchor European AI development on sovereign compute and trusted collaboration, not dependency and exclusion — but having failed to demonstrate tangible capacity within twelve months, pivot fully to securing leverage through coalition and access, with sovereign infrastructure pursued only as a component of collective power, not as a standalone strategy.
  - Grounds: The InvestAI Gigafactory remains unbuilt after 15 months of emergency designation, supplier agreements, and political capital expenditure, with no foundation laid and no credible path to breakthrough. The failure is not of intent but of execution capacity: member states cannot compel energy guarantees, and external partners no longer believe in EU delivery. The denial of frontier access in 2031 was a warning; the member_state_defection this turn confirms that the Union’s credibility is now actively decaying. We cannot claim sovereignty through unilateral build when we cannot build. The pivot is no longer optional — it is the only way to prevent total strategic irrelevance. This revision binds the commitment to reality: sovereignty must now be achieved through coalition strength, not in spite of it.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer pay for errors that stem from institutional immobility masked as prudence.
  - Grounds: The failure of the gigafactory was not a gamble that failed; it was a plan that never moved. We mistook legal designations for momentum, and supplier MOUs for delivery. This turn’s member_state_defection — a national carve-out with a foreign hyperscaler under terms that bypass EU safeguards — reveals that uncertainty is no longer our primary enemy. Inaction is. We will still act before clarity, but we will no longer tolerate the pretense that process is progress. The cost of being wrong must be borne by those who block, not by the Union’s strategic position.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
