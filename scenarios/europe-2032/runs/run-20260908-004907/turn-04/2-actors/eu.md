## In practice
We are finalizing the legal text of the Pause Frontier Deployment Licenses measure under Article 114 TFEU, with the AI Office leading drafting and ENISA providing technical validation of the red-teaming protocols. The proposal will go to the Internal Market Council by June, where we are working bilaterally with France, Poland, and Sweden to secure qualified majority support. We are framing the licensing gate not as a freeze but as a safety floor: developers who submit to sovereign evaluation and demonstrate progress in agent control will gain fast-track access, while those who refuse will face market exclusion. Given the current investment slowdown, that threat has teeth — and we are using it to push back against U.S. accusations of protectionism by offering to align our safety case template with the transatlantic open safety coalition’s emerging standards.

At the same time, we are advancing SAFM through the pilot phase, with Karlsruhe now operational as a testbed for sovereign model hosting. We are integrating security-certified compute from the upgraded HPC sites in Finland and France into the SAFM network, ensuring that even as greenfield gigafactories stall, we are still building sovereign capacity. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being recalibrated to embed SAFM compliance into the accelerated-permitting zones, so that private investment strengthens public control. Meanwhile, the Public Audit Framework is issuing its first formal audit demands to three high-capability systems, leveraging DMA gatekeeper status to compel access. One has agreed to limited red-teaming; two have refused, triggering the first enforcement phase. We are preparing exclusion notices, not to escalate, but to prove that the framework is not symbolic — and we are sharing the audit protocols with Canadian and German safety institutes to build legitimacy. Public sentiment remains low, but we are beginning a targeted communication campaign with regional governments and energy cooperatives to reframe sovereign AI infrastructure as resilient public utility, not speculative tech — a narrative shift that may buy space for the next phase.
