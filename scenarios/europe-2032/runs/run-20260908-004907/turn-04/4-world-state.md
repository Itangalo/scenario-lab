### Capability Creeps Forward

AI capability advances by 1.5 points to 58.0, as coordination efficiencies and distributed research sustain momentum despite market skepticism. Behind closed doors, labs refine agent persistence and cross-task generalization, edging toward autonomous execution of multi-day workflows. Open-weight models benefit from leaked training data and improved fine-tuning frameworks, pushing `openweight_capability` to 47.0—still trailing, but narrowing the gap as safety researchers gain early access.

### Biosecurity Alarm Rises, EU Holds Course

The publication of the *bio_uplift_findings* sparks intense debate within scientific and security circles. While the study is methodologically contested, its implications are not ignored: for the first time, a plausible pathway from AI-assisted design to a functional human-pathogenic organism has been documented. The EU, already advancing its Pause Frontier Deployment Licenses proposal, uses the moment to frame its licensing regime as a necessary firewall. Legal drafting nears completion, with the AI Office aligning red-teaming protocols with transatlantic partners to counter accusations of unilateralism.

However, progress stalls in the Council. France supports the measure, but Poland and Sweden demand exemptions for defense-related AI development, weakening the consensus. The proposal remains in negotiation, its fate uncertain by June.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Talent Drains

Despite operational progress at Karlsruhe and integration of upgraded HPC resources, broader sovereign capacity falters. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain delayed, and private investment under the Tech Sovereignty Package slows as developers question long-term access to frontier models. A quiet but accelerating exodus of AI talent to North America and neutral hubs undermines domestic expertise, with several EU labs reporting critical vacancies.

The Public Audit Framework moves forward, but resistance from two major developers persists. Enforcement actions loom, yet no resolution is reached, eroding confidence in the EU’s ability to compel compliance.

Public sentiment dips to 31.0 as protests against data centers merge with growing anxiety over bio-risk and job displacement. The EU’s communication campaign gains little traction, perceived as spin amid stalled infrastructure and elite-driven regulation.

Political capital erodes under the weight of five large measures, including the priority, with no major delivery in sight.
