### Stalled Momentum, Mounting Pressures

The EU enters 2029 with its agenda strained and its leverage thin. The U.S. administration’s shift toward strategic consolidation hardens into policy: frontier AI is now treated as critical infrastructure, with access rationed by alliance. The EU, lacking independent frontier capacity, is downgraded to a secondary tier. Developers quietly suspend cooperation with the Public Audit Framework, citing compliance risks under new American rules.

Brussels presses ahead with enforcement, threatening gatekeeper suspensions under the AI Act for non-compliant labs. But the legal basis — Article 114 TFEU — faces immediate challenges. A coalition of member states, led by Ireland and the Netherlands, warns that unilateral audit mandates could fracture the single market and provoke retaliatory tech restrictions. The European AI Evaluation Agency (EAAE) begins operations in Karlsruhe, but with only partial model access, its authority remains symbolic.

Meanwhile, the so-called "capability plateau" sparks debate. While benchmark stagnation and falling model prices suggest a slowdown, internal U.S. lab reports remain classified, and some investors quietly double down on long-horizon research. The pause, if real, may be temporary — but it offers no relief to the EU’s eroding sovereignty.

The Tech Sovereignty Package formally concludes, having failed to attract promised private investment. In response, Brussels repurposes elements into emergency permitting for sovereign compute sites, piggybacking on the InvestAI Gigafactories initiative. However, legal hurdles persist, and no new site breaks ground this turn.

Public sentiment holds at 28.0, sustained by regional heat-recovery projects linking data centres to district heating. Yet protests against AI-driven job losses in media and design sectors grow in Paris and Berlin, reinforcing a narrative of uneven burden.

### Strategic Drift

With no new measure launched and five initiatives still in flight, political capital continues to bleed. The named priority — the Public Audit Framework — yields no tangible progress, underscoring the limits of soft enforcement in a hard-power landscape.
