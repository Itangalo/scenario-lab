The EU’s AI sovereignty continues to erode in 2029 amid stalled initiatives, legal challenges, and deepening dependency on U.S. frontier models. Strategic consolidation in the U.S. locks critical AI infrastructure behind alliance barriers, downgrading EU access and prompting developer non-cooperation with the Public Audit Framework. Enforcement efforts under the AI Act face constitutional pushback from member states, weakening Brussels’ authority and leaving the European AI Evaluation Agency under-resourced and ineffective. The Tech Sovereignty Package fails to attract investment, forcing a pivot to emergency permitting for sovereign compute—though no new infrastructure emerges. Public sentiment stagnates at 28.0, sustained only by localized benefits like heat-recovery projects, while protests grow over job displacement. With no progress on key fronts and political capital depleting, the EU drifts strategically, unable to bridge regulatory ambition with technological autonomy.
