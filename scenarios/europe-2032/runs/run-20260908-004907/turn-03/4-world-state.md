### Capability Stalls, Open Weights Gain Ground

AI capability advances by 1.5 points to 56.5, driven by incremental coordination gains, though signs of a plateau emerge: a flagship model underperforms expectations and top-tier access prices drop, suggesting market doubt. Despite this, progress continues. Meanwhile, speculation grows around an upcoming open-weight release—rumored to approach frontier capabilities in agent coordination—sparking debate among developers and regulators. No major dissemination has yet occurred, but testing frameworks based on leaked prototypes circulate in safety circles. `openweight_capability` rises modestly to 45.5, reflecting early access and anticipation, not full deployment.

### EU Proposes Pause Amid Fracture

The EU introduces one new measure—temporary licensing for systems above 55.0 capability—framed as a safety pause, making it the new priority. This category 3 proposal leverages market access to pressure developers, citing the recent investment slowdown and invoking emergency procedures. Legal drafting begins, but consensus among member states remains fragile. U.S. labs denounce the move as protectionist, and some EU governments question its proportionality, slowing adoption.

The existing portfolio—InvestAI Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, EU SAFM Directive, and Public Audit Framework—continues, but with constrained momentum. Local opposition halts new data center construction under InvestAI, forcing reliance on upgrades to existing HPC sites in France and Finland, which proceed at a slower pace. SAFM pilots advance in Germany, the Netherlands, and Spain; Karlsruhe is designated for the first audit node.

The Public Audit Framework issues preliminary scrutiny demands using DMA leverage. One major developer complies partially; two others resist, setting up enforcement clashes. Outreach to the transatlantic open safety coalition builds goodwill but yields no joint standards yet.

### Sentiment Sours, Capital Strains

Public anger over data centers intensifies, merging with creative sector job-loss narratives. Protests spread, and local moratoriums harden. The new licensing proposal, despite its safety framing, is seen by many as elite technocracy ignoring public concerns—further eroding trust. `public_sentiment` drops to 33.0.

`resilience` falls slightly as no protective measure lands and capability rises. `ai_safety` declines again due to widening control gaps. The EU’s political capital is strained by the new priority and ongoing portfolio load. The proposal bonus for the Public Audit Framework (+3) offsets some cost, but the net burden reduces `eu_political_capital` to 33.0—down, but not collapsed.
