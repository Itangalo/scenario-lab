## New measure
**EU Sovereign Access to Frontier Models (SAFM) Directive**

Establishes a legal right for EU public institutions to run and audit frontier AI systems on sovereign infrastructure, with full weight access under strict security, for evaluation, crisis response and essential public services.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases eu_ai_sovereignty by enabling independent control of capable systems; indirectly pressures ai_safety upward by enabling public scrutiny; may reduce public_sentiment if perceived as overreach, but builds long-term trust in autonomy.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member states, and frontier AI developers with significant presence in the EU.

## In practice
We are acting on the conviction that sovereignty is not a policy option but a condition of action. This turn, we begin the legal drafting of the SAFM Directive under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market clause to override national fragmentation, with the Commission’s AI Office leading and Frontex, Europol, and the Joint Research Centre designated as anchor users. We will require that any provider placing a model above 50.0 capability on the EU market must provide a sovereign instance — identical in weights and training data — hosted on EU-controlled infrastructure, with access logs and security protocols subject to ENISA audit. This is not about copying weights; it is about ensuring that when a crisis hits, the EU can run its own diagnostics, respond without asking permission, and maintain continuity of essential functions.

At the same time, we are pushing the InvestAI Gigafactories into site selection and grid integration planning, using the Recovery and Resilience Facility as a bridge for early disbursements, while negotiating with EIB and national development banks on risk-sharing for the first two sites — one in Finland for energy availability, one in France for talent and research linkage. Resistance is coming from member states wary of centralisation and from energy regulators concerned about grid strain, but we are offering co-ownership models and guaranteed grid upgrades funded from the same envelope. The Tech sovereignty package is being reoriented to include SAFM-compatible infrastructure in its accelerated-permitting zones, aligning private and public build-out.

The cyber_test_shot event has made clear that detection is no longer enough — we must assume compromise and plan for sovereign recovery. That requires not just resilience, but the ability to verify and rebuild from within. We are not waiting for the next incident to prove whether the frontier labs can secure their own systems. We are building the capacity to act when they cannot.
