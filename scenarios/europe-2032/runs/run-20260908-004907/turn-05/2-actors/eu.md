## In practice
We are finalizing the Pause Frontier Deployment Licenses proposal under Article 114 TFEU, with the AI Office completing red-teaming protocols validated by ENISA and aligned with the transatlantic open safety coalition. The text will be presented to the Internal Market Council in June, where we are negotiating with France, Poland, and Sweden to secure qualified majority support—offering Poland a time-limited defence exemption in exchange for backing the core regime, and Sweden access to joint Nordic red-teaming infrastructure. The threat of market exclusion remains credible due to the stalled private investment under the Tech Sovereignty Package, and we are using that leverage to resist U.S. pressure to dilute the safety gate.

At the same time, we are operationalizing the SAFM Directive as a finished measure, standing up Karlsruhe as a permanent sovereign model hosting facility with certified compute from Finland and France integrated into the network. This infrastructure will now serve as the enforcement backbone: any developer seeking to operate above 55.0 capability in the EU must allow their model to be mirrored and audited within the SAFM environment. We are linking this requirement to the Public Audit Framework, which is escalating enforcement against the two developers who refused red-teaming—preparing exclusion notices under the Digital Markets Act and coordinating with national regulators to suspend gatekeeper status. The audit protocols are being shared with Canadian and German institutes to build legitimacy, but the real signal is sovereign capacity: without it, audits are theatre.

Public sentiment remains at 31.0, and we are launching a targeted campaign with regional governments and energy cooperatives to reframe sovereign AI infrastructure as resilient public utility—linking data centres to district heating, renewable microgrids, and local job pipelines. This narrative shift is not about enthusiasm, but about tolerance: we need just enough public breathing room to survive the next incident without backlash collapsing our capital entirely. The talent exodus continues, but we are fast-tracking residency and security clearance for critical hires under the EU AI Talent Shield pilot, using SAFM access as a recruitment tool. Sovereignty is not yet secure, but it is no longer theoretical.
