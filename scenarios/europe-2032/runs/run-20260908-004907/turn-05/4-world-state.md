### U.S. Election Shifts the Balance

The 2028 U.S. presidential election concludes with a decisive victory for the candidate advocating strategic consolidation of AI. The new administration treats frontier models as national security assets, tightening export controls and rationing access based on geopolitical alignment. For the EU, this transforms dependency into vulnerability: what was once market friction becomes a lever held firmly in Washington. The AI Act’s enforcement power weakens overnight, as developers now weigh compliance against the risk of losing American access altogether.

### Pause Licenses Collapse, SAFM Stands Alone

The Council rejects the Pause Frontier Deployment Licenses, unable to reconcile Poland’s demand for broad defense exemptions with France’s push for universal application. Sweden abstains, citing insufficient Nordic coordination. The failure marks a symbolic defeat for the EU’s safety-first strategy. Meanwhile, the SAFM Directive achieves formal implementation, with Karlsruhe now hosting certified sovereign infrastructure and integrating French and Finnish compute. However, no major developer has agreed to mirror models under the framework, citing legal uncertainty and U.S. objections.

### Talent Drain Accelerates, Sentiment Sours

The exodus of AI researchers intensifies, with key labs in Germany and the Netherlands reporting critical vacancies. Recruitment from neutral hubs yields little. Public opposition to data centres grows, merging with fears over bio-risk and job losses. A new campaign reframing AI infrastructure as public utility gains limited traction in Scandinavia but fails elsewhere. Trust in EU institutions continues to erode.

### Political Capital Erodes, Sovereignty Slips

With five large measures in flight and no major delivery, political capital declines sharply. The failure of the Pause measure triggers a confidence penalty. Resilience drops as capability advances without protective measures. Safety erodes due to the widening gap between capability and oversight. Sovereignty falls further, reflecting both the U.S. posture shift and ongoing talent loss.
