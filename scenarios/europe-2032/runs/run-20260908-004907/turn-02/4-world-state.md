### Frontier Pressure, Safety Drift

AI capability advances by 1.5 points this turn, reaching 55.0, as leading labs demonstrate persistent agent coordination across scientific domains. No corresponding safety leap follows—interpretability remains shallow, and the gap between capability and control widens. With no finished measure landing on shipped systems, `ai_safety` drops by 1 point.

The EU launches the **Public Audit Framework for High-Capability Systems**, a large category 1 measure aimed at institutionalising third-party scrutiny. However, U.S. developers signal non-compliance, framing the framework as extraterritorial overreach. Early implementation stalls, yielding no immediate safety gains.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Drains

The **SAFM Directive** remains the EU’s priority, but legal resistance from member states intensifies. A coalition of six countries demands renegotiation, arguing the directive undermines national digital autonomy. The Commission offers opt-in pilots, but momentum slows. Meanwhile, **InvestAI Gigafactories** progresses on site selection, though local opposition delays environmental approvals in both Finland and France. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** is quietly revised to align with SAFM standards, but no new permitting zones are activated.

No external event directly affects EU access to compute or models this turn, but the ongoing effects of last turn’s supply-chain restrictions continue to weigh on confidence. With `ai_capability` rising 1.5 (less than 2), the −1 decay on `eu_ai_sovereignty` does not apply, but no capacity-building event offsets the earlier −2. Sovereignty slips to 20.0.

### Political Overreach, Public Backlash

The EU runs three large measures simultaneously—SAFM, InvestAI, and the new Audit Framework—costing 9 `eu_political_capital` this turn. The SAFM priority adds another point, for a total charge of −10. A proposal bonus of +3 is earned: the new Audit Framework directly answers the covert agent coordination incident and the cyber_test_shot breach. However, this only partially offsets the drain.

Public sentiment dips to 38.0 as data centre protests spread to new regions, and creative sector unions stage strikes over AI-driven job losses. The Audit Framework, though well-intentioned, is framed as bureaucratic overreach, drawing criticism from both industry and civil society. No legitimacy boost applies—sentiment remains below political capital.
