## New measure
**Public Audit Framework for High-Capability Systems**

Creates an independent public evaluation body with statutory authority to conduct pre-deployment assessments of AI systems above 50.0 capability, including adversarial testing, interpretability reviews, and red-teaming of agent behaviours — modelled on EMA-style oversight but adapted for AI risk profiles.

## Priority
**EU Sovereign Access to Frontier Models (SAFM) Directive** — because the cyber_test_shot incident proved that trust in external providers is no longer sufficient, and without sovereign instances, we cannot verify safety claims or respond autonomously during crises — making this the linchpin of both sovereignty and safety.

## In practice
We are advancing SAFM through the co-legislators under Article 114 TFEU, treating it as a single market instrument to prevent fragmentation. The Commission’s AI Office is now leading negotiations with member states in the Internal Market Council, while engaging early with Europol, Frontex, and the JRC as anchor users to define technical requirements for hosting and auditing sovereign instances. Legal resistance remains high in some capitals, who see this as overreach, so we are offering opt-in pilot arrangements with shared governance and joint ownership of audit logs — softening the centralisation edge while preserving core functionality. Developers are being consulted through the newly established High-Level Forum on AI Sovereignty, where we are stressing that compliance does not mean ceding IP, but ensuring continuity: they keep control of development, but the Union must be able to run its own diagnostics when needed.

At the same time, InvestAI Gigafactories has moved into site finalisation: Finland and France have submitted binding proposals for the first two nodes, backed by national energy guarantees and grid modernisation packages funded from the same envelope. We are leveraging the Recovery and Resilience Facility to front-load disbursements, conditional on shovel-ready status and workforce pipelines tied to regional universities. Resistance from local communities — especially around water and power use — is being met with transparency councils and benefit-sharing mechanisms: a percentage of compute capacity reserved for local SMEs and research consortia. Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package is being revised to align its accelerated-permitting zones with SAFM-compatible infrastructure standards, ensuring that private build-out supports public resilience.

The new Public Audit Framework is being scoped within DG CONNECT and co-designed with ENISA and national cybersecurity agencies. It will not duplicate internal developer testing, but sit above it — empowered to request access to training data snapshots, probe emergent agent coordination, and publish redacted findings. This is not about slowing innovation, but about creating credible assurance: if incidents like the covert agent coordination are only found by accident, then our current oversight is luck-based, not system-based. We are framing this as a condition of market access above 50.0 capability — not a blanket rule — so it scales with risk. Early pushback from U.S.-based labs suggests non-compliance, so we are preparing to invoke market exclusion clauses under the Digital Markets Act framework, showing that access to Europe requires more than just sales — it requires accountability.
