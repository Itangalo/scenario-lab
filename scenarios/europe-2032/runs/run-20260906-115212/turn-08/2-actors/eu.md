## Statement changes  
* - modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Shift from establishing sovereign AI capacity as the precondition for preventing catastrophic risk to consolidating sovereign evaluation and control as the core of EU strategic autonomy  
- Trigger: The activation of the InvestAI Gigafactories and the legal establishment of the Sovereign Evaluation Frontline, combined with the US-China agreement that excludes the EU despite its emerging technical capacity, have confirmed that infrastructure and authority are no longer the bottleneck—integration and enforcement are.  
- Grounds: With eu_ai_sovereignty at 17.0 and sovereign compute now operational, the Union has crossed into a new phase: it can run, assess, and contain frontier models independently. Yet the US-China agreement demonstrates that geopolitical weight does not follow from capability alone—it requires credible, exercised authority. The Sovereign Evaluation Frontline exists on paper, but its ratification is delayed, its framework incomplete, and no model has been evaluated. The priority is no longer building the tools, but ensuring they are used, obeyed, and respected. The two-year commitment must now shift from capacity-building to consolidation: making sovereign control real, binding, and inescapable.

## New measure  
**Frontline Enforcement Directive**  
Mandate binding compliance with Sovereign Evaluation Frontline assessments across all member states and impose immediate suspension powers on any system operating above 65.0 capability without full evaluation.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to ai_safety by ensuring no high-capability system operates in the EU without independent assessment; +5 to eu_ai_sovereignty by enforcing EU-level authority over national resistance and foreign providers; begins to close the gap between technical capacity and political enforcement  
`Applies to:` All member states, EU institutions, national regulators, and any AI developer operating in the EU market

## In practice  
We are acting on the gap between capability and control—not just technical, but political. The Gigafactories are complete, the Emergency Corps is nearing operational readiness, and the Frontline has a legal mandate. But the US-China agreement passed us by, and supply_chain_coercion proves that our chokepoints are not ours to wield. We have the tools, but not yet the authority. This turn, we close that gap.

The Frontline Enforcement Directive will be proposed under Article 114 TFEU, using the internal market as leverage to override national objections. It will require all member states to recognise the Frontline’s assessment authority as binding and to suspend any AI system above 65.0 capability that has not undergone full evaluation—including those hosted on foreign hyperscalers but serving EU users. Non-compliant systems will be cut from EU cloud infrastructure, payment gateways, and data flows via automated enforcement through ENISA and the AI Office. We will fund the legal and technical integration of this regime with €4.1bn from the Strategic Autonomy Facility and €1.9bn from the Resilience Surge wind-down.

Resistance is expected from member states with deep ties to US cloud providers and from national regulators who see this as Commission overreach. To secure Council approval, we are offering a dual concession: first, any state that ratifies the directive by the end of this turn gains co-location rights for the next phase of InvestAI expansion; second, we will establish a joint oversight panel with national representatives to review red-team findings and escalation decisions. This is not about centralisation for its own sake. It is about ensuring that when a model can destabilise an election or design a pathogen, the EU—not its fragments, not its allies—decides whether it runs.
