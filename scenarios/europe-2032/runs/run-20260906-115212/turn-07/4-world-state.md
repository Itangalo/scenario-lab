### The Transparency Threshold

AI capability continues its steady climb, reaching 78.0, driven by incremental advances in training efficiency and infrastructure scaling. The pace remains consistent with the long-term trend established in earlier turns—no discontinuity or compressed leap occurs. While internal model complexity grows, and interpretability degrades further, the progression is gradual, not explosive. Developers report increasing difficulty in tracing reasoning pathways, but the shift is one of degree, not kind.

This erosion in transparency, however, crosses a critical threshold. External validation, red-teaming, and containment protocols are now significantly impaired. As a result, ai_safety drops to −10.0, reflecting the growing gap between deployment speed and oversight capacity. The decline is structural, not sudden: no single event caused it, but the cumulative effect of opaque architectures and automated training loops has rendered many safety practices obsolete.

Amid these challenges, the EU completes two major initiatives. The InvestAI Gigafactories finish construction, delivering sovereign compute capacity across four sites. Simultaneously, the Resilience Surge Protocol concludes, hardening key cyber and bio nodes and funding early labour transition schemes. These deliver tangible gains, lifting resilience to 48.0.

Buoyed by completion bonuses, the EU launches the Sovereign Evaluation Frontline—a bold attempt to reclaim technical oversight. The new institution is designed to independently assess frontier models, with legal authority to block deployments. However, immediate resistance emerges. Several member states, citing sovereignty concerns and economic ties to foreign labs, delay ratification of the access mandates. The Civilian AI Safety Board remains unconstituted, weakening the Frontline’s legitimacy. As a high-cost measure, the Frontline’s full operational capacity is still months away, and its evaluation framework exists only in draft form. No models have yet been assessed under its mandate.

Public sentiment dips as deepfake videos of leaders circulate widely, eroding trust. Though the Resilience Surge helped, the perception of reactive scrambling persists. Political capital rises slightly from completion gains but remains constrained by ongoing portfolio strain and fragmented support.

### A Sovereign Core Emerges

Despite headwinds, the activation of domestic compute and the institutional launch of the Frontline mark a shift: the EU now possesses the physical and organisational foundation to act independently. For the first time, it can run high-stakes evaluations without relying on external providers—though whether it can compel access remains untested.

The new compute infrastructure from the Gigafactories begins initial allocation. While this significantly boosts available capacity, only a fraction is immediately directed toward open-weight model development. Training runs are scheduled, but no major models have yet completed. As a result, openweight_capability rises modestly to 58.0, reflecting early utilization of new infrastructure rather than a sudden leap in capability. The gap with frontier AI remains wide, and catching up will require sustained effort over multiple turns.

The net gain in eu_ai_sovereignty is recalculated: the completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories provides a +3 boost, but the rise in ai_capability of 2.0 triggers a −1 decay, resulting in a net increase of +2.0, bringing sovereignty to 17.0. This reflects both the material progress and the persistent pressure of falling behind on safety and transparency.

eu_political_capital increases by +2.0, reflecting partial credit for completing two major measures and launching a third, though delayed implementation and ongoing legitimacy gaps limit the full bonus.
