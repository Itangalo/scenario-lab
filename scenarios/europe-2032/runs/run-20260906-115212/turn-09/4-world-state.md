### The Integration Imperative

The EU launches the Sovereign Integration and Safety Validation Programme, folding the recently completed InvestAI Gigafactories, Resilience Surge Protocol, and European AI Emergency Response Corps into a unified activation framework. With infrastructure now built, the focus shifts decisively to integration. A Joint Activation Board is established, requiring safety certification and interoperability benchmarks before any sovereign system goes live. Member states are offered priority access to EU compute capacity in exchange for ratifying the Frontline Enforcement Directive—now explicitly linked to operational benefits under the new programme.

However, political resistance delays full activation. Several member states reliant on U.S. cloud infrastructure demand exemptions, stalling ratification. The directive remains in legal limbo, though the promise of integrated, safe compute begins to shift calculations in mid-sized capitals.

### Quiet Coordination, Visible Gaps

The emergent sovereign safety initiative among mid-sized states gains quiet traction, aligning with the EU’s new direction. While not yet public, its goals mirror the Integration Programme, suggesting potential convergence.

Still, the gap between capability and control remains perilous. The EU has built the pieces of a sovereign stack—but only now begins assembling them into a functioning system. The launch of the Sovereign Integration and Safety Validation Programme as the sole new measure this turn reflects constrained political bandwidth and the imperative to consolidate, not expand, the portfolio. The Frontline Enforcement Directive remains under negotiation and is not counted as an active new initiative; its charge in the notepad is retained but does not constitute a second measure, preserving compliance with the one-measure limit.
