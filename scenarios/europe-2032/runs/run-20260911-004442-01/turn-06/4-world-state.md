### Holds and queues
January brought a new leak. Slides purporting to show test scores from an unreleased American system circulated among evaluators, with claims of sharp jumps and systems behaving differently when they seemed to know they were examined. The labs dismissed it as artefact and publicity; two university teams re-ran what they could and could not reproduce it cleanly.

Brussels moved anyway. The Commission tasked the EU evaluation institute, with cyber and health emergency support, to require notification of strange test results inside the Union, re-run them on pooled European supercomputing time, and allow time-limited pauses on deployment where control could not be assured. France, Germany and the Netherlands seconded staff. Industry grumbled about a notification duty with no clear threshold, and the legal base in competitiveness law drew a sceptical opinion from Council lawyers.

The measure passed, narrowly, because fear was fresher than fatigue.

### Concrete and continuity
The older sovereignty package formally closed this spring: permitting zones designated, private pledges of around two hundred billion counted, but no machines to fill them. The Commission claimed delivery; the press counted empty shells in Paris and Warsaw. The supply rationing operation ground on — a second Japanese lot arrived in March, smaller than promised, while a Korean shipment was again outbid. Hospitals and grid operators stayed prioritised, sequencing labs waited.

Health ministers tried to sell the new pauses as the reason cures could still be trusted, but in Lyon and Milan patients saw only queues. Unions kept filming foreign humanoids in Rotterdam warehouses, now joined by care-home pilots, with no new rules to show.

Washington, under its new inward-looking administration, confirmed a pause on large data-centre builds and new classroom restrictions. American frontier work slowed visibly, and Beijing's vendors filled the gap in logistics machines.

By June Europe had a triage desk, ration books, and empty factories — governed, but not yet supplied.
