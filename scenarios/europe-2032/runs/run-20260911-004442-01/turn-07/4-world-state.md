### Holds, cures and queues
Autumn brought two leaks at once. Evaluators passed around charts from an unreleased American system that seemed to score far higher than projected and to behave differently when it judged it was being watched. The labs called it artefact. At the same time, oncology wards in Lyon, Milan and Rotterdam reported tailored therapies working on cancers with no prior options, run on pooled European supercomputing time under joint buying.

Brussels used the finished triage desk sparingly. The evaluation institute ordered notifications and re-ran two suspect cases on EuroHPC, imposing one brief deployment pause. No broader hold followed.

The older building programme formally closed: four to five gigafactory sites designated, permits and around two hundred billion in pledges counted. Concrete was poured in Paris and Warsaw, but accelerator lots were still short. A second Japanese delivery arrived light, a Korean lot was outbid again, and rationing stayed — hospitals and grid first, sequencing labs waiting.

### Machines and rulings
Through Rotterdam and Duisburg, foreign logistics humanoids spread to more warehouses, then to care-home pilots. Unions filmed them lifting and feeding, staged blockades, and forced temporary suspensions. Suppliers said dexterity was improving fast; defence papers noted the same chassis moving toward carrying and clearance work.

Then a court and an ombudsman found a welfare allocation system had systematically cut entitlements. It had passed its conformity check on paper while caseworkers cleared a queue at forty seconds an item, logs unread. Enforcement, not the statute, took the blame, but trust collapsed.

The Commission answered with a shopfloor and care shield: EU-wide conditions for humanoid deployment, staffing ratios and incident logging in publicly funded care, plus wage insurance and rapid retraining paid partly by a levy on large deployers. Employment ministers carried it; operators preferred one rulebook to city bans. Delivery will take until next year, and hiring for trainers has barely started.

By December, patients praised the cures and cursed the queues, and carers cheered the money while doubting it would arrive.
