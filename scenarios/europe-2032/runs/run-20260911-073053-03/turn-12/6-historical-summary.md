US-China war degraded orbital, undersea, fab, cable and datacentre infrastructure; Europe suffered hits to Atlantic cable station, Low Countries cleanroom supplier and southern interconnectors, with queues tightening and medical supply stalled.

Union imposed emergency protection for fabs, cables, grid and hospital compute with guarded repairs and failover, keeping triage/command nets alive without restoring supply. Arson halted gigafactory grid works under military guard; graduate/logistics guarantee paid thinning bridge pay amid white-collar hiring freeze. An open near-frontier model spread unrecallably; Washington placed leading labs under direct federal control, replacing export licences with classification and turning European access into intergovernmental favour.

In February Washington and Beijing announced a limited pact on frontier risk — weight security, autonomous-escalation restraints, controls on biological design tools — with thin verification. Europe remained outside: dose rationing continued, grid connections stayed fenced and idle, open model unrecalled. The wartime continuity shield finished, stabilising landing station and interconnectors, formalising hospital power priority and preventing cascading blackouts, but not restoring supply as transformers queued on military need. Commission pressed US channel for evaluation observers and landing-rights/cleanroom neutrality for sparing, and bid for observer then party status in the pact offering JRC evaluators and inspections. Washington accepted a second observer talk and released headline-level medical slots and spares but no construction restart, and with Beijing deferred European accession. By June Europe was alive and hardened at edges but excluded from rule-setting.
