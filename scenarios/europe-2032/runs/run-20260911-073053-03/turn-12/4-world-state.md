### A floor elsewhere
In February, Washington and Beijing announced what Brussels had long said was impossible: a limited pact on frontier risk. The text covered securing of model weights, restraints on autonomous escalation, and controls on a class of biological design tools. Verification was thin — liaison visits, shared incident notices, paper promises of audits — but it was written down.

For Europe, the news landed as relief and humiliation at once. Dose rationing still governed clinics, the two grid connections for the halted compute builds still stood fenced and idle, and the open near-frontier model now ran on countless private machines beyond recall.

### The shield that held
The one programme that did finish was the wartime continuity shield. Guarded repair crews stabilised the Atlantic landing station and the southern interconnectors, hospital power priority was formalised, and failover drills kept triage and command nets alive through spring cable faults. It did not restore supply — transformers still queued on military need — but blackouts that would have cascaded in 2031 degraded instead of stopping.

Mayors still fought over warehouses, and bridge pay for graduates thinned as white-collar hiring stayed frozen. Arson attempts shifted from grid works to cable depots, met now by military guard.

### Asking to join
The Commission moved on two tracks. The live channel to the US State Department and AI safety office stayed the operational core, pressing observer visits to the anomalous evaluation and trading landing-rights neutrality and cleanroom neutrality for written sparing assurances. The same team tabled a bid for observer, then party, status in the new pact, offering Joint Research Centre evaluators, neutral cleanroom inspections and cable-site checks as a verification contribution.

Washington took the offer as useful and gave little. American officers accepted a second observer conversation and released a handful of medical-inference slots and spare parts — enough for headlines, not enough to restart construction. On accession, Brussels was told it would be informed, consulted in passing, but not seated while terms were still being set between the principals. Beijing echoed the delay.

By June, Europe was alive, hardened at the edges, but still outside the room where the floor was being laid.
