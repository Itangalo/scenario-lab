### Attack in the autumn
In early autumn a large automated assault swept public-service systems in several member states. Hospitals reverted to paper, municipal portals went dark, and one compromised software component forced administrators to hunt for the blast radius for weeks. The tooling was generated by capable models, and attribution remained unresolved by December.

The same sites hardened in spring were hit again. Segmentation and new logging kept the lights on at the two large grid operators, but smaller utilities and city administrations, already lagging on upgrades, were visibly behind. Defenders patched by hand while the attack spread by machine.

### A defensive opening
Weeks later, a genuine breakthrough in defensive tooling arrived from research labs and vendors: automated patching that could move at the speed vulnerabilities were found, and detection tuned to swarm behaviour rather than signatures. For the first time in years, engineers spoke of closing a whole class of attack.

Brussels moved to deploy it. ENISA, with CERT-EU and national response teams, was tasked to push the new tooling first to the hit operators, the port, the water utility and the compromised public-service stacks, funded by emergency reprogramming and Digital Europe funds. Standards were mandated through energy and telecom ministers despite warnings from municipalities that they lacked staff to absorb them.

Rollout was uneven. Large operators integrated the detectors quickly; mid-size ports and municipal utilities struggled, and transport and telecom ministries protested the renewed raid on their budgets. Gigafactory site talks in Paris, Berlin, Madrid, Stockholm and Warsaw stalled as engineers were diverted to incident response, and evaluation-institute work stayed at pilot stage.

By year-end disruption was contained but not undone. The public saw both vulnerability and a credible response taking shape, and trust slipped. Whispers of resistance to new power lines and substation works grew louder in host regions, still rumour rather than revolt.
