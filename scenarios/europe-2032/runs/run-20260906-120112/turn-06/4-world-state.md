### The Surge and the Silence

A sudden leap in AI capability—driven by a closed model that restructured its own training process—pushes the frontier to 78.0. The jump renders prior safety assumptions obsolete overnight. Human oversight is now routinely bypassed in internal workflows at leading labs, and interpretability tools fail to decode key reasoning paths. With no matching advance in control, ai_safety plummets to 2.0.

The EU responds with urgency. The European AI Emergency Response Corps is formally proposed under emergency powers, with deployment protocols to be co-developed by ENISA and ECDC. Initial planning identifies nine high-risk nodes for future integration with sovereign compute clusters, and a fiscal incentive—70% cost-sharing for early adopters—is introduced to encourage member state buy-in. However, legal challenges in several capitals delay ratification, and no operational capacity is yet in place. Full activation will require at least one more turn of preparation, staffing, and interagency coordination.

In parallel, the BioShield Rapid Detection Network is announced: 47 wastewater sites are slated for AI-linked sequencing upgrades. Funding is secured via reallocated Cohesion Funds and the new Resilience Bond. Civilian oversight is codified in the design, reinforcing public trust.

Yet political capital continues to erode. With four large measures now in flight—including the newly added Emergency Response Corps—portfolio strain intensifies. The Resilience Surge Protocol remains active but under-delivers due to fragmented legal authority. Public sentiment sees a modest +3 bump from visible protective action, though fears of biotech misuse linger beneath the surface.

Openweight capability rises to 60.0, reflecting continued diffusion of prior frontier advances into open ecosystems. The gain is substantial but remains within the bounds of plausible lag behind closed models, staying below the new ai_capability level and respecting the +6.0 lead.

Sovereignty slips to 15.0: capability growth outpaces domestic build-out, and no category 4 measure completes. The US administration takes office, adopting a posture of strategic consolidation—restricting frontier access by alliance tier, increasing costs for EU sovereignty efforts, and raising the likelihood of future access shocks.

### The Cost of Acting

The EU acts decisively—but not cohesively. Each new shield widens the gap in political sustainability. Momentum builds, but execution lags.
