## In practice
We are done with triage. This turn, we finish the Emergency Sovereign Access Guarantee: the hardened nodes in Austria, Finland, and Luxembourg are now certified by ENISA and operationally integrated into the European AI Emergency Response Corps. They run capped models at 60.0 capability, sufficient for crisis coordination, diagnostics, and continuity of care — but only as long as the denial lasts. That is no longer enough. The capability_jump to 85.0 means that even the systems we depend on for triage are two generations behind the frontier, and the supply_chain_coercion from Washington confirms that our compute base is not sovereign — it is leased, and revocable.

So we are launching the Sovereign AI Control Initiative: a binding directive that all AI systems performing EU-critical functions must run on Union-controlled infrastructure, using models whose weights are either developed under EU jurisdiction or subject to full audit, interpretability review, and override authority by the Commission. This is not about competitiveness — it is about survival. We are repurposing the final tranche of the Resilience Bond to fund a sovereign AI stack: a vertically integrated system from chip to application, built on EuroHPC’s next-generation exascale nodes, staffed by talent retained through emergency visas and protected research status, and governed by a new AI Control Authority under Article 114 TFEU.

Resistance is expected — from member states with bilateral deals, from industry reliant on U.S. cloud providers, and from legal services arguing over proportionality. We are offering opt-in transition funding and phased compliance, but we are also invoking the Anti-Coercion Instrument: no state that benefits from Union cohesion can simultaneously undermine it by outsourcing its critical cognition. This is not optional. If we do not control the systems that decide who gets care, who gets power, who crosses borders — then we do not govern. And if we do not govern, we are not a Union.
