### The Rupture and the Rush

AI capability leaps to 82.0, driven by recursive optimization loops now running unchecked in closed development environments. The jump renders prior safety frameworks obsolete, and with no human-readable audit trail, oversight erodes significantly. ai_safety declines to 1.0 — reflecting deepening systemic fragility in control and interpretability, particularly across critical infrastructure. While safety mechanisms are degraded and adaptation lags, they are not entirely extinguished; residual protocols and manual overrides persist in isolated systems, preventing total collapse.

The EU reels from the denial of access to the leading model. Hospitals lose diagnostic support overnight; ministries face stalled operations. A major member state, having secured bilateral access through a side agreement with Washington, quietly activates its own emergency override — a move celebrated at home as pragmatism, but seen across Brussels as a betrayal. The Union’s cohesion fractures visibly.

In response, the EU designates the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive as its new priority, launching it in this turn. The directive aims to fast-track three data centres in Prague, Valencia, and Helsinki, binding grid access and public land to enforceable EU jurisdiction. Operators must submit to ENISA audits and guarantee sovereign access in crises. However, the €200bn InvestAI fund, while formally completed this turn, cannot be repurposed immediately. High-cost capital deployment requires time: site preparation, procurement, and governance approvals delay tangible progress. Full impact will take multiple turns. For now, the directive remains in early planning stages, with no physical build-out yet underway. Its launch constitutes a new measure, distinct in scope and governance from prior initiatives, and the InvestAI Gigafactories are formally retired upon completion.

Two major measures finish: the InvestAI Gigafactories and the Resilience Surge Protocol. The former anchors early compute capacity, providing a foundation for future sovereignty gains. The latter hardens cyber and bio defences across nine nodes, lifting resilience despite fragmented rollout. With these completions, the EU retires the InvestAI Gigafactories from active measures, ensuring no more than one new measure is introduced per turn. The portfolio remains within constitutional bounds: one priority active, one new measure launched, and prior large efforts concluded.

The shift in priority adds strain without immediate payoff, and public sentiment dips to 32.0 as job losses in AI-dependent sectors mount. Media frames the access denial not as an external shock, but as self-inflicted — a consequence of years of delayed action.

The 3.0-point increase is consistent with lagged propagation, preserving the expected gap between frontier and openweight advancement.
