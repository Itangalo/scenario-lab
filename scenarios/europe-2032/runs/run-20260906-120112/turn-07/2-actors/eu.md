## In practice  
We are acting on the rupture. The denial of access to the leading model — cutting off hospitals, ministries, and firms without warning or appeal — is not an incident. It is a regime. We are no longer being excluded from capability; we are being governed by it from the outside. The fiction that we can rely on foreign goodwill while building slowly at home has collapsed. This is why we are launching the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive: a binding, legally enforceable fast track for data centres that place compute under EU jurisdiction, with priority grid access, state-backed land acquisition, and streamlined permitting under a new EuroHPC-led authority. In return, operators must undergo annual weight-security audits by ENISA, guarantee sovereign access during emergencies, and commit to not hosting unauthorised open-weight releases above 55.0 capability.

The directive targets three new sites — Prague, Valencia, and Helsinki — to come online by turn 10, each co-invested by the Union and national governments, with matching private capital drawn from pension funds and infrastructure investors now seeking stable, sovereign-grade AI exposure. We are using the leverage of the €200bn InvestAI fund — just completed — not to start over, but to redirect: its capital becomes callable under this new directive, and its stalled regional projects are being folded into this sharper, more urgent track. Resistance is strong in capitals that had bet on foreign partnerships — especially in the member state that recently defected to a bilateral deal with Washington — but we are meeting it with conditionality: access to Resilience Bond funding and Emergency Response Corps integration now depends on alignment with this directive. This is not coercion. It is coherence. We will not fund dependence. We will not harden systems that can be switched off by another power at will. The Union’s survival as a decision-making body depends on its ability to run systems no one else can veto — and that starts with the wires in the ground.
