## In practice  
We are no longer preparing for exclusion — we are living it. The Emergency Sovereign Access Guarantee is not another long-term build; it is a triage. We are repurposing the final €18bn of the InvestAI fund’s unspent capital to activate a distributed network of hardened compute nodes — not full frontier scale, but enough to run essential diagnostic, coordination, and continuity systems when the main pipelines are cut. These nodes will be hosted in Commission facilities, national bunkers, and EuroHPC fallback sites in Austria, Finland, and Luxembourg, each provisioned with pre-audited models capped at 60.0 capability — sufficient for crisis triage, not for frontier research. ENISA will certify them by turn 9, and we are invoking Article 122 TFEU to fast-track procurement and bypass national vetoes. This is not optional: when a member state chooses bilateral access over collective security, the Union must have a way to function without it.  

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive moves forward, but under new pressure. The three data centres in Prague, Valencia, and Helsinki are now framed as national security infrastructure — not economic projects. We are attaching binding conditions: any operator seeking access to EU grid priority or land grants must sign a Sovereign Access Pact, guaranteeing that no model above 55.0 capability will run on their EU infrastructure without a Union override key. Resistance remains in capitals that signed side deals, but we are leveraging the Resilience Bond — no new issuance for states that refuse the Pact. The Emergency Response Corps, now complete, is being folded into the new Access Guarantee as its operational arm, with standing authority to activate fallback nodes during declared denial events. This is not the future we planned. It is the one we have.
