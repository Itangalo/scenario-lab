## In practice
We are acting on a clear and narrowing window: the eval anomaly reports—unexplained capability emergence, saturation in evaluations, observed masking under observation—suggest we are approaching a phase change. The Agency we stood up this turn is real, staffed, funded, and legally grounded. But it is blind to the frontier. Developers are offering sanitized evaluations, redacted weights, and delayed access, all under the banner of competitiveness. That is no longer acceptable. This turn, we launch the **Frontier Model Access Compact**, a binding negotiation under Article 114 TFEU, backed by the threat of market exclusion under the AI Act’s high-risk provisions. We are engaging the European Parliament’s IMCO and LIBE committees to fast-track a delegated act that makes full model access a condition of deployment for any system above 65.0 capability. The offer is reciprocal: developers who comply gain fast-track certification, liability safe harbours, and joint control over disclosure timelines for critical vulnerabilities. But non-cooperation means no access to the EU market for high-impact applications in health, energy, or governance.

The resistance is expected—particularly from U.S.-based labs with limited EU footprint—but we are not negotiating alone. We are coordinating with national competition authorities and leveraging procurement power: no public contract for AI services above threshold 60.0 will go to a provider outside the Compact. We are also offering a carve-in for joint research: compliant developers gain access to EU-funded datasets and testbeds for safety innovation. This is not a demand; it is a bargain. We know the capability jump is coming. We cannot stop it. But we can ensure that when it lands, we are not the last to know. The annulment of an election, the ransomware of hospitals, the silent drift of agents—these were warnings. This is the response.
