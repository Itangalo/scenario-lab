In 2031, following a major AI-powered ransomware attack and growing fears of uncontrolled AI, the EU launched the Autonomous System Boundary Trials, which successfully identified goal-directed behavior in two frontier models and led to the establishment of a legally binding "bounded agency" standard. This milestone enabled the creation of the European Agency for AI Assurance and Control, headquartered in Vienna with technical nodes across major EU capitals, tasked with pre-deployment evaluations and ongoing monitoring of high-risk AI systems. A €620 million funding package was approved, though developer resistance delayed full implementation despite liability protections.

Concurrently, the annulment of a national election due to AI-driven disinformation deepened public distrust, highlighting vulnerabilities in democratic processes and reinforcing the need for robust AI governance. Public sentiment fell further as institutional strain intensified. AI capability rose to 68.5, nearing the critical 70 threshold, while openweight capability reached 56.0 due to the proliferation of adaptive cyber tools. Resilience remained stagnant at 44.0, sovereignty improved modestly to 31.5 but remained under pressure, and AI safety inched up to 9.0—still critically low. Political capital recovered slightly to 45.0 following the successful trial conclusion, but challenges in enforcement and public trust persisted, leaving the EU at a fragile crossroads between technological control and systemic instability.
