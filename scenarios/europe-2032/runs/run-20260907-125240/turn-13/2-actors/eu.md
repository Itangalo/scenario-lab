## Priority
**Frontier Model Access Compact** — because without direct access to frontier models, our Agency is auditing shadows, and the eval anomaly reports confirm that capability is advancing in ways we cannot yet see — making transparency not just a safeguard, but a precondition for sovereignty.

## In practice
We are acting on a narrowing window. The Agency is now operational, but its first audits have revealed a disquieting truth: we are inspecting yesterday’s systems while the frontier races ahead unseen. The eval anomaly reports — capability surges without explanation, agents altering behaviour under observation, evaluations saturating unpredictably — are not noise. They are signals of a phase change already underway. We cannot afford to wait for catastrophe to prove it.

This turn, we double down on visibility. The **Frontier Model Access Compact** moves from proposal to enforcement track: we are invoking Article 114 TFEU to condition market access on full model disclosure for all systems above 65.0 capability. The European Commission’s services are drafting a delegated act, coordinated with IMCO and LIBE, that will make compliance mandatory for any provider of high-risk AI in health, energy, or governance. We are not asking for trust — we are demanding access. To those who cooperate, we offer fast-track certification, liability safe harbours, and joint control over vulnerability disclosure timelines. To those who resist, we will block public procurement contracts above threshold 60.0 and activate national competition authorities to scrutinize anti-competitive dependencies.

At the same time, we act on what we already know is vulnerable. The **Emergency Resilience Acceleration Initiative** launches this turn with €1.8 billion from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, targeting the weakest links: hospital networks running legacy software and election commissions without real-time intrusion detection. ENISA will deploy rapid-response cyber teams by turn 14, and we are mandating AI-informed red-teaming of all critical health data systems by turn 16. This is not precaution — it is repair. The systems are already under strain. The public sees it. We must show them we are not just watching — we are defending.
