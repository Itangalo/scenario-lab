## Priority  
**Emergency Compute Resilience Directive** — This turn demands a signal that the EU can act when its infrastructure is under systemic pressure, and with supply_chain_coercion proving that external powers can weaponise chokepoints, sovereignty must be operationalised now — not just funded.

## In practice  
We are activating Article 122 TFEU and the crisis clause of the Digital Markets Act to draft a directive that allows the Commission, in concert with ENISA and the European Defence Agency, to designate critical compute assets and ensure their availability to essential public services during a disruption. This is not nationalisation, but crisis-access: a legal framework to prevent a repeat of Washington’s unilateral control over frontier models, or a future where China or another power can freeze EU access to systems embedded in its infrastructure. We will negotiate with the ECOFIN and INDIA Councils to secure qualified majority support, offering member states co-governance through a new Compute Resilience Board. Resistance is expected from states hosting major hyperscaler facilities, who fear investor backlash — we will counter with guarantees of compensation and a firewall against peacetime use.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by convening a task force under the European Investment Bank and national development banks to fast-track site selection in Finland, Spain, and Poland, where grid capacity and cooling infrastructure are viable. We are also pressing the European Parliament to accelerate the Tech sovereignty package’s permitting reforms, using the cyber_test_shot as evidence that delay is a security risk. The message is consistent: the EU will not outspend the US on AI, but it will out-secure its foundations.
