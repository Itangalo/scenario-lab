### Frontier Pressure and Fractured Leverage  
The global AI frontier advances relentlessly, with capability rising to 54.0 as two leading labs complete accelerated training runs. Offensive cyber tools derived from open models are now in circulation, and the first AI-optimised industrial control exploits appear in the wild. The EU’s public stance hardens, but its tools remain strained.

### Emergency Powers, Divided Consensus  
The European Commission proposes the Emergency Compute Resilience Directive, invoking crisis powers to secure state access to compute during disruptions. While legally grounded in Article 122 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act, the proposal triggers fierce resistance. Germany and Ireland, hosting major hyperscaler data centres, warn of capital flight and legal challenges under bilateral investment treaties. The ECOFIN Council delays a vote, demanding stronger compensation guarantees and sunset clauses. The directive passes in principle but is delayed by one turn due to protracted negotiations.

### Compute Sovereignty in Motion  
Despite friction, progress emerges. The InvestAI Gigafactories task force selects two initial sites — one in Finland, one in Spain — with grid integration studies underway. The Tech Sovereignty Package advances in the European Parliament, where the cyber_test_shot has become a focal point in debates over permitting reform. Lawmakers from several member states cite the incident as evidence of systemic vulnerability, but no final decision on streamlining approvals is reached this turn.

### Sentiment and Safety Under Strain  
Public sentiment dips as media frames the compute directive as a “digital seizure,” amplifying fears of state overreach. Simultaneously, the cyber_test_shot — though not catastrophic — exposes critical detection gaps, undermining confidence. AI safety declines as no new safeguards land on frontier systems, and the gap between capability and control widens.
