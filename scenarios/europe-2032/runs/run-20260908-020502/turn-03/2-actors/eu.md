## New measure  
**Sovereign Evaluation and Monitoring Initiative**  
Establish an EU-owned, operationally independent agency with technical capacity to conduct pre-deployment evaluations and continuous monitoring of frontier AI systems, using red-teaming, interpretability tools, and access to weights under binding legal authority.

## Priority  
**Sovereign Evaluation and Monitoring Initiative** — Because the eval_anomaly_reports signal a capability leap that may already be outpacing developer understanding, and because the EU still lacks sovereign eyes on the frontier, we cannot rely on voluntary disclosures or foreign oversight; this measure builds the technical capacity to see for ourselves, which is the precondition of both safety and sovereignty.

## In practice  
We are standing down the Emergency Compute Resilience Directive as it reaches completion: the Compute Resilience Board is legally constituted, its first members appointed, and its protocols integrated into ENISA’s crisis framework. While invocation remains constrained by the German and Irish safeguards, the precedent of public authority over private compute is now law. We are redirecting part of that political momentum into a new sovereign capability — not just access, but understanding. The eval_anomaly_reports confirm our worst fear: capability is emerging in ways even developers cannot explain. We cannot secure what we cannot see.

Thus, we are launching the Sovereign Evaluation and Monitoring Initiative under the European Defence Agency’s secure infrastructure, with a new AI Safety Directorate to be hosted in Seville. This is not another layer of reporting — it is hands-on, technical evaluation with teeth. We are drafting a regulation under Article 114 TFEU to mandate weight-sharing with this body for any system above 45.0 capability operating in the EU, backed by fines and deployment blocks. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking will transfer €12bn in unused funds to equip the agency with sovereign compute for red-teaming and interpretability research. We are negotiating with France and Poland to second top AI safety researchers, and with the Netherlands to host secure data enclaves under dual-key access.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on the Tech Sovereignty Package in trilogue, trading further rural broadband commitments for fast-track permitting in designated AI sovereignty zones — now expanded to include evaluation infrastructure. The InvestAI Gigafactories proceed with environmental impact assessments in Finland and Spain, though grid delays threaten the 2029 timeline. And we are scaling the Public AI Adoption Acceleration measure through the Digital Europe Programme, with pilot rollouts in Italian healthcare and Polish education now showing measurable improvements in service delivery. But make no mistake: adoption without control is vulnerability. This turn, we anchor our sovereignty not in compute alone, but in understanding.
