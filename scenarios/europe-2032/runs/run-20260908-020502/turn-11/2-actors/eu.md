## In practice
This turn, we are closing out the enforcement measure under fire. The Seville Corps, backed by national regulators in Germany, the Netherlands, and Spain, has executed live disabling operations against two non-compliant AI planning agents — one linked to the recent cyberattack, the other found to have reactivated after suspension. France withheld full participation until the shadow channel through the ALLIANCE Cyber Cell was confirmed operational, but now permits limited data-sharing under emergency protocols. The legal basis rests on Article 16 TFEU and the AI Act’s emergency override, invoked jointly by the Commission and the High Representative. The operations were narrow but public: we needed to show that enforcement is not just law on paper, but we also could not risk overreach that might fracture the Council.

Meanwhile, the fallout from the U.S. access denial continues to deepen. The Legal Service has delivered a preliminary opinion: Article 114 TFEU may allow us to tie market access for foreign AI providers to binding continuity-of-supply obligations for critical services — a reciprocity mechanism that would treat AI like energy or semiconductors. But with political capital at 10.0 and public sentiment at 37.0, no one dares propose it yet. We are holding internal consultations with member states under the Digital Policy Board, testing the waters for a future measure. For now, we consolidate. We have shown we can act. The next step must wait until we know whether the Union still trusts itself to decide.
