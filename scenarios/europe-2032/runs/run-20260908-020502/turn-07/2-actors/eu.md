## In practice
We are treating the end of the InvestAI Gigafactories and the Sovereign Evaluation and Monitoring Initiative not as closures but as foundations. The Gigafactories’ completion frees up political bandwidth, but not capital: we are redirecting attention, not money, toward enforcement and integration. The Sovereign Evaluation and Monitoring Initiative is now operational, and its first red-team cohort in Seville is conducting live assessments on two systems flagged for anomalous planning behaviour. We are leveraging the delegated act under Article 114 TFEU to demand full model weight access for all systems above 55.0 capability operating in or affecting the EU, with retroactive application justified by systemic risk. Germany’s legal concerns are being met with a sunset clause: retroactive audits will be reviewed by the CJEU in 18 months, but they proceed now under emergency powers invoked for systemic AI risk.

At the same time, we are acting on the emergent automotive disruption wave and the eval anomaly reports by launching the Emergency Resilience Acceleration Programme. This is not a new Gigafactory-style build, but a targeted surge: ENISA is issuing binding guidelines by turn’s end for AI-hardened control systems in automotive supply chains, energy grids, and medical device networks, with funding drawn from the European Cyber Resilience Act’s contingency reserve and co-financed by member states under a new rapid-disbursement protocol. We are fast-tracking procurement waivers for intrusion detection systems trained to spot AI-generated adversarial inputs, and we are expanding the Seville red-teaming mandate to include real-time monitoring of open-weight model deployments in industrial settings. Slovakia and Germany are on board because the disruption is hitting their core industries; France is holding out, demanding that resilience funds also cover military AI systems, which we are resisting to keep focus on civilian continuity.

The U.S. ALLIANCE offer remains on the table, but we are conditioning participation on prior EU certification. In COREPER II, we are finalising a Council decision that any transatlantic data-sharing or joint evaluation initiative requires that U.S. systems first pass EU-led audits. This turns dependency into leverage: we are not blocking cooperation, but we are making it flow through our institutions. Meanwhile, public sentiment is fraying, and we know that resilience without visible benefit will not hold. So we are pairing the resilience push with a targeted communication campaign—under the existing Public AI Adoption Acceleration framework—showcasing AI-assisted retraining programmes in Bavaria and Wallonia, where displaced engineers are being redeployed into safety oversight roles. It is not a fix, but it is a signal: we are not just defending, we are transitioning.
