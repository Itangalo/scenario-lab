Global AI capabilities advance with widespread deployment of automated planning in cyber and industrial systems, while open-weight models narrow the performance gap, intensifying competition. The EU faces mounting pressure in early 2031: a sophisticated AI-driven cyberattack disrupts public services in two capitals, exposing persistent defensive shortcomings despite prior hardening efforts. Simultaneously, the bloc is cut off from a leading frontier AI model without explanation, crippling critical systems in healthcare, transport, and education across three member states and revealing deep vulnerabilities in external dependencies.

In response, the EU activates emergency enforcement powers under the AI Act, enabling national regulators and the Seville Corps to disable non-compliant systems. Germany backs swift action, while France demands transatlantic coordination, delaying unified implementation. A shadow channel is established via the ALLIANCE Cyber Cell to preserve cooperation without sacrificing autonomy. Legal efforts explore leveraging Article 114 TFEU to tie market access to guaranteed AI service continuity, signaling a potential shift in digital reciprocity, though no new policies are enacted amid depleted political capital.

Public trust erodes further as job losses in legal and transport sectors—driven by autonomous AI planning—dominate media narratives, and enforcement actions are perceived as reactive and legally overreaching. Despite completed frontier model audits and the suspension of a major U.S. model, diplomatic tensions persist. The capability-safety gap widens globally, with no breakthroughs in alignment or control, increasing systemic risk as offensive AI tools become operational and outpace defensive readiness. Sovereignty remains strained, enforcement credibility is tested, and the EU struggles to project resilience amid cascading technological and political challenges.
