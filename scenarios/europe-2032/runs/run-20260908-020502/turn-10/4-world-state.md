### Under Siege

The EU enters 2031’s first half under dual pressure. A major AI-powered cyberattack—leveraging model-generated exploit chains—disrupts public services across two capitals. Response is fragmented, though recent hardening prevents systemic collapse. The breach confirms that offensive AI tooling is now operational and outpacing defensive readiness.

Simultaneously, the Union is abruptly cut off from the leading frontier AI model. Hospitals in three member states lose access to critical diagnostic systems, and public-sector AI deployments in education and transport stall. No justification is provided, and appeals are ignored—exposing the fragility of external dependence.

In response, the EU activates emergency enforcement protocols under the AI Act, empowering national regulators and the Seville Corps to disable non-compliant systems. Germany supports immediate action; France insists on transatlantic coordination, slowing unified implementation. A real-time shadow channel is established through the ALLIANCE Cyber Cell to maintain cooperation without ceding autonomy.

Legal teams explore using Article 114 TFEU to condition market access on guaranteed supply continuity for critical AI services—a potential shift in digital reciprocity. But no new measures are launched. With political capital strained and public trust eroding, the focus is on making enforcement credible, not expanding ambitions.

Public sentiment declines further as media highlight job losses in legal and transport sectors, tied to autonomous planning systems. The enforcement push, while necessary, is perceived by some as reactive and legally aggressive, deepening public unease.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Lags

Global AI capability advances steadily, with automated planning now embedded in industrial and cyber domains. Open models inch closer, replicating capabilities once exclusive to closed systems. Yet safety assurance fails to keep pace. No breakthroughs occur, and the gap between capability and control widens—quietly raising the odds of future incidents.
