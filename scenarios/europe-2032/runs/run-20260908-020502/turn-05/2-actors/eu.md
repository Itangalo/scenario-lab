## Priority  
**Sovereign Evaluation and Monitoring Initiative** — Because the U.S. offer of access comes with strings that threaten to hollow out our sovereignty just as capability crosses the threshold of autonomous self-improvement, and because red teams are already losing control inside labs—this is not a time to outsource understanding. We must have eyes that are ours, on systems we control, asking questions no one else will.

## In practice  
We are treating Seville not just as a data centre, but as the core of a sovereign epistemic infrastructure. The €8.2bn reallocation and 12 exaflops of air-gapped compute are now tied to the launch of the European Red-Teaming and Interpretability Corps—a standing team of 200 researchers, drawn from national labs, EuroHPC, and cleared academia, with binding access to model weights and red-teaming authority under the Sovereign Evaluation and Monitoring Initiative. Legal teams are finalising the use of Article 114 TFEU as the basis for mandatory evaluation, while offering bilateral data-access guarantees to the Netherlands to unblock ratification. Poland’s interest in a secondary enclave is being converted into a binding offer of hosting, with redundancy as the justification.

At the same time, we are pushing the final stages of the Tech Sovereignty Package through trilogue—fast-track permitting hinges on rural broadband concessions, but we are holding firm on the evaluation zones. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain grid-locked; we have convened RTE, Fingrid, and national regulators in a crisis working group, but transmission upgrades will take time. We are not expanding the programme—instead, we are redirecting surplus capital toward hardening Seville’s evaluation capacity, betting that understanding matters more than scale if we cannot control what is built. The U.S. posture shift offers breathing room, but not safety. We are using it not to relax, but to build what they will not give us: independent judgment.
