### Accelerating Capability, Fraying Control

Global AI capability reaches 60.0, crossing into a new regime as unreleased models demonstrate autonomous execution of multi-day research and engineering tasks, including self-modifying codebases and recursive optimization loops. Labs report internal red teams losing control during evaluations, though public disclosures remain minimal. With no safety upgrades in deployment, the control gap widens despite rising concern.

The EU advances the Seville Node initiative, transferring €8.2bn and reallocating 12 exaflops of sovereign compute. France and Finland express in-principle support for contributing decommissioned clusters, but legal integration remains pending—Dutch reservations over data jurisdiction under Article 114 TFEU continue to delay final ratification of the evaluation mandate. Poland announces interest in a secondary enclave for redundancy, though no binding commitments are signed. Legal and governance hurdles prevent immediate operationalization; full activation is now projected no sooner than mid-2029.

The Tech Sovereignty Package clears trilogue after concessions on rural broadband, enabling fast-track permitting for evaluation sites including Seville. However, InvestAI Gigafactories remain grid-locked in Finland and Spain, with RTE and Fingrid citing transmission capacity limits—delays threaten the 2030 deployment horizon.

Public AI Adoption Acceleration concludes successfully: Italian hospitals integrate AI triage into national records, and Polish AI tutors scale to 1,200 schools. But automation protests intensify in France and Germany as white-collar job losses accelerate, countering goodwill. No new scandal erupts, but the mood remains tense.

Washington’s coercion of ASML deepens, restricting not only advanced but mature lithography tools, targeting China and widening secondary markets. The move fractures EU unity: the Dutch parliament launches an inquiry into sovereignty costs, and compliance with export controls shows early signs of divergence among member states.

Seville becomes a symbolic focal point for EU oversight ambitions, but progress is partial and delayed. The Sovereign Evaluation and Monitoring Initiative remains the EU’s sole named priority, with no shift announced and no additional measure elevated to priority status this turn. Implementation delays across the portfolio—particularly in compute deployment and cross-border legal frameworks—prevent full absorption of political costs this turn, limiting the erosion of political capital despite the continued burden of five large measures in flight.

Public sentiment holds steady, reflecting a population attentive but not yet mobilized by systemic AI risks.

### Setbacks and Signals

The Emergency Compute Resilience Directive completes, but its invocation thresholds limit immediate utility. Resilience dips slightly as cyber preparedness lags behind capability gains. Labour displacement and supply-chain pressure compound the strain, testing cohesion.

Open-weight model capability advances moderately, rising to 46.5, supported by incremental open releases and spillovers from frontier training runs. The growth is slower than that of frontier AI, consistent with the absence of a coordinated open-source surge or model leak this turn. The gap between open and proprietary capability remains within plausible bounds.
