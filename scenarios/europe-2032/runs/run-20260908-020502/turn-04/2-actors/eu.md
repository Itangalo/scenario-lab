## In practice  
We are treating Seville as the keystone of our sovereign understanding. The Sovereign Evaluation and Monitoring Initiative is no longer just a legal proposal — it is a physical build. We are transferring €8.2bn from the unused portion of the Digital Europe Programme and reallocating 12 exaflops of EuroHPC’s next-generation deployment to the Seville node, with full air-gapped architecture and EU-only personnel vetting. This is not a shared cloud; it is a sovereign redoubt. We are negotiating with France and Finland to contribute decommissioned national security compute clusters, repurposed under EU flag, and with Poland to host a secondary enclave as a backup. Legal teams are finalising the use of Article 114 TFEU for the evaluation mandate, while working around Dutch hesitations by offering bilateral data-access guarantees with joint oversight.

At the same time, we are completing the Public AI Adoption Acceleration measure: the Italian healthcare pilots are now integrated into national digital health records, and the Polish education AI tutors are being scaled to 1,200 schools. But we are not renewing the programme — public sentiment is rising, but so are automation protests, and legitimacy now hinges on control, not convenience. The Tech Sovereignty Package is in final trilogue: we have conceded 15% of the rural broadband expansion to secure fast-track permitting for evaluation infrastructure, including Seville. And the InvestAI Gigafactories are still delayed — we are convening RTE, Fingrid, and national regulators in a crisis working group to unblock grid connections in Finland and Spain by Q3.

We are acting on the truth the world has given us: capability is accelerating beyond understanding, and our leverage is being exercised *against* us. We cannot build compute faster than others unless we control the meaning of it. So this turn, we anchor sovereignty not in promises, but in metal, law, and independent eyes. Seville is that anchor. If we do not hold it, we do not hold anything.
