## Priority  
**Emergency Compute Resilience Directive** — Despite the positive signal from adoption_success, the unresolved crisis-access gap remains an existential vulnerability: if external powers or market failures cut off compute at a moment of strategic stress, no amount of public trust or domestic innovation will matter. This directive operationalises sovereignty now, when the precedent is still being set.

## In practice  
We are finalising negotiations on the Emergency Compute Resilience Directive with the ECOFIN Council, offering Germany and Ireland a binding compensation mechanism and a tripartite governance model via a newly established Compute Resilience Board — co-chaired by the Commission, ENISA, and rotating member-state representatives — to depoliticise access decisions. Legal scrubbing is underway using the crisis clause of the Digital Markets Act and Article 122 TFEU as joint bases, with the European Legal Service confirming proportionality and redress pathways. The goal is a qualified majority vote by the end of Q2, ahead of summer recess.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by launching grid integration and cooling feasibility studies in the selected Finnish and Spanish sites, with the European Investment Bank structuring blended finance packages to crowd in private capital. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being pushed through the Parliament’s INDIA and ITRE committees, where we are trading support for rural broadband investments in exchange for accelerated permitting for AI infrastructure in designated sovereignty zones. The cyber_test_shot remains central to our argument: delay is not prudence, it is exposure.

Now, with adoption_success proving that European-led AI deployment can deliver real public value — shorter waiting lists, faster decisions, better teaching — we are launching the Public AI Adoption Acceleration measure to scale these wins. Through the Digital Europe Programme and reinforced cohesion funds, we will identify high-impact use cases and fast-track their replication across member states, with a central repository managed by the European Commission’s AI Office. This is not just about efficiency; it is about legitimacy. We must show citizens that AI serves them — not just capitals, not just corporations — and that the EU can deliver where others only promise.
