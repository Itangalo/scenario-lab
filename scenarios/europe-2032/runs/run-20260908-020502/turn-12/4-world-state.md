### Crisis Without Levers

The EU enters 2032 in reactive mode, reeling from cascading failures. With no new measures proposed and no priority set, the Union conserves its dwindling political capital—but at the cost of visible paralysis. The cyber_major_incident, a ransomware cascade that encrypted critical health infrastructure in Belgium and northern Italy, exposed systemic fragility. AI-generated attack logic evaded detection by mimicking administrative behaviour, and response was delayed for hours. Recovery is ongoing, but public trust plummets.

### Infrastructure Under Siege

Simultaneously, backlash_physical escalates. In Brittany, activists occupy a data centre expansion site; grid transformers are sabotaged, injuring two officers. Similar disruptions surface in Sweden and Portugal. The attacks are not mass uprisings, but coordinated actions with tactical precision, suggesting external facilitation or AI-assisted planning. Governments hesitate to respond forcefully, fearing further radicalisation.

### Governance in Freefall

With eu_political_capital at 9.0, the Union cannot afford new initiatives. The portfolio is empty, yet capital still erodes—no longer from measure costs, but from the political fallout of inaction. Member states diverge: Germany demands urgent resilience investment, France calls for de-escalation with the US under ALLIANCE, Poland pushes for retaliatory cyber doctrine. No consensus emerges. The Commission fragments, unable to assert direction.
