### The Plateau That Wasn’t

Despite signs of a capability plateau, the AI frontier remains restless. While one major release underperformed and prices for top-tier models dipped—hinting at stagnation—the underlying trajectory holds. `ai_capability` remains at 62.0, as gains in recursive self-improvement are offset by market signals of saturation. Open-weight models advance to 52.0, supported by incremental spillovers from leaked training data and improved fine-tuning methods now widely accessible. This modest rise reflects diffusion of existing frontier knowledge rather than a breakthrough, consistent with flat frontier capability.

A rare safety breakthrough lifts confidence: new interpretability tools allow developers to predict emergent planning behaviour before deployment. The result is rapidly adopted across leading labs, marking a shift from reactive red-teaming to proactive verification. This strengthens `ai_safety` despite no regulatory enforcement yet being in place.

In Brussels, the EU advances a single new measure: binding evaluation requirements for frontier AI developers, leveraging the completed Tech Sovereignty Package to demand compliance as a condition for fast-track permitting. A delegated act under Article 114 TFEU proposes mandatory pre-deployment audits for systems above 55.0 capability. Germany questions retroactive application, while France warns of legal overreach. The U.S., unveiling its **ALLIANCE** posture, offers structured access—but on terms that prioritize American-led evaluations, not European ones.

Poland formalizes its secondary enclave agreement, boosting redundancy, but ASML export restrictions deepen the sovereignty dilemma. Washington compels Dutch curbs on mid-node lithography tools, hitting European semiconductor projects reliant on non-frontier kit. This supply-chain coercion costs the Union leverage, triggering a −2 penalty on `eu_ai_sovereignty`. However, the Sovereign Evaluation and Monitoring Initiative finishes its build, delivering a +3 boost. With no new capacity event and flat capability growth, the base calculation reaches 30, but delayed ratification limits immediate impact—final `eu_ai_sovereignty` settles at 28.0.

The proposal of the new measure earns a +3 bonus to `eu_political_capital`, justified by emergent labour displacement and control concerns. The completion of the Sovereign Evaluation and Monitoring Initiative adds another +3. These gains are partially offset by the ongoing portfolio charge of −10, reflecting the cost of maintaining four large measures and a named priority. Net change is −4, bringing `eu_political_capital` to 21.0.

Public sentiment sags further as automotive automation disrupts engineering jobs, feeding narratives of uncontrolled displacement. Resilience dips slightly as no new infrastructure lands, and rising open capabilities outpace defences. Discussions about integrating the Seville interpretability breakthrough into broader resilience protocols are underway, but no decision has been made, and no event gate has been triggered. The possibility remains under technical review, pending further validation.
