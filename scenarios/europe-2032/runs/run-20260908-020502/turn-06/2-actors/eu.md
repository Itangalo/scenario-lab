## Priority
**Sovereign Evaluation and Monitoring Initiative** — Because the U.S. offer of access risks eroding political will, and because the capability plateau evidence suggests we may be entering a window where control—not just compute—determines who leads, we must lock in our right to understand and verify. This measure is not about catching up; it is about refusing to fall behind in the one domain that matters most: judgment.

## In practice
We are treating the end of the Tech Sovereignty Package not as closure but as leverage. With fast-track permitting now law, we are conditioning its full deployment on compliance with binding evaluation requirements. The Commission’s Legal Service is drafting a delegated act under Article 114 TFEU to establish mandatory pre-deployment evaluations for systems exceeding 55.0 on the capability index, with retroactive application to those already fielded. The Netherlands’ ratification of Seville is being tied to reciprocal data-access guarantees, and Poland’s secondary enclave offer is being converted into a binding redundancy agreement, with joint funding from the European Defence Fund.

At the same time, we are confronting the political risk of the Atlantic Offer head-on. The U.S. shift is real, but so is its conditionality: alignment on export controls and standards in exchange for access. We are not refusing the offer—we are reframing it. In COREPER, we are advancing a Council decision that participation in transatlantic AI coordination requires prior certification by the Sovereign Evaluation and Monitoring Initiative. This turns dependency into reciprocity: access to U.S. frontier models is granted only after EU evaluators have verified their safety and control properties. The message is clear—sovereignty is not a barrier to alliance; it is its foundation. Meanwhile, the Red-Teaming Corps in Seville is being staffed ahead of schedule, with 140 of 200 positions filled, drawing from national cybersecurity agencies and academic partners under a new security-clearance framework negotiated with Europol.
