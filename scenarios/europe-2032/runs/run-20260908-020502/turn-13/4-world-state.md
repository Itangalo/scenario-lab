### Holding the Line, Losing Ground

The EU enters a state of suspended animation. With political capital fully depleted and public sentiment under severe strain, no new measures are launched. The Digital Policy Board convenes in emergency session, but fractures along national lines: Germany demands immediate investment in cyber resilience, France calls for de-escalation with the US under the ALLIANCE framework, and Poland pushes for retaliatory cyber doctrine if the recent ransomware is traced externally. No consensus emerges.

Behind closed doors, the Commission scrambles to contain institutional disintegration. The Legal Service warns that further centralised enforcement under the AI Act risks judicial invalidation if member-state opposition solidifies. A quiet revolt brews, as several governments demand a rollback of the Seville Corps’ authority and threaten to block future AI sovereignty funding unless oversight is rebalanced toward national courts and parliaments.

Bilateral outreach intensifies. France is offered a future review of Seville in exchange for eventual support; Germany is briefed on a draft resilience framework under Article 114 TFEU; Poland is assured offensive cyber remains on the table—within common bounds. But these are delaying tactics, not decisions. The Union is not acting—it is trying to survive.

### Capability Advances, Safety Erodes

Globally, AI capability advances to 70.5, driven by agentic refinement and integration into industrial R&D. Open-weight models rise to 61.0, sustained by the ongoing openweight research surge: distributed networks of researchers and hobbyists continue to refine and repurpose accessible models, achieving incremental gains through collaborative tuning and automated experimentation. These advances, while narrower in scope than frontier developments, are real and cumulative—now formally reflected in the metric. The surge, first observed in turn 12, continues into this turn, contributing to the second consecutive increment in openweight capability.

With no safety breakthrough and no new governance landing on shipped systems, ai_safety drops to 27.0. Resilience slips further as critical infrastructure remains exposed, now at 39.0.

Public sentiment declines to 20.0, reflecting growing unease over job displacement and data centre sabotage, compounded by perceptions of foreign dependency and institutional drift. The erosion is broad and steady, but not yet accelerated by a triggering event—trends deepen rather than cascade. Political capital plunges below zero to -5.0, reflecting not just inaction, but the accumulating cost of internal fracture and eroding legitimacy.
