## In practice  
This turn, we move swiftly to capitalise on the joint_threat_response event: a new coalition of states hit by AI-generated cyberattacks is forming a Joint Cyber Command with real-time telemetry sharing, automated alert escalation, and binding response protocols. We were invited in — but not seated. Our absence from the core decision loop would leave us dependent on delayed intelligence and reactive coordination, precisely when speed is survival. We therefore launch the **Joint Threat Response Integration Initiative**, a focused two-turn effort to formalise our participation through ENISA and CERT-EU, backed by the European External Action Service and aligned with NATO’s Cyber Incident Response Team. The legal basis is Article 4 of the Cyber Resilience Act and the mutual assistance clause in the Treaty on European Union. We are offering to contribute telemetry from the Systemic Integrity Watch’s grid and health data pipelines in exchange for full access to the coalition’s AI-driven threat models and early-warning feeds. Germany and Poland support immediate integration; France insists on data sovereignty safeguards, demanding that all shared EU-origin data be routed through a Luxembourg-based filtering node. We agree — with sunset clause at turn 10, contingent on proven interoperability.

At the same time, the **Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive** enters its second full turn. Progress is uneven: ASML has committed its EU-dedicated EUV throughput, and STMicroelectronics has repurposed a Leuven fab for AI-specific chip packaging. But France’s insistence on software co-location has stalled the Paris Basin node — they will not allow remote management of control stacks, citing national security. We are negotiating a compromise: a hybrid architecture where core safety monitors run locally under EU certification, while distributed orchestration remains federated. The European Investment Bank has released the first tranche of €2.1bn in conditional loans, tied to quarterly milestones in permitting and grid readiness. The bottleneck now is not money or will, but time: the opacity of frontier models means we are building the lab not to replicate them, but to begin to see them. And we are still blind.
