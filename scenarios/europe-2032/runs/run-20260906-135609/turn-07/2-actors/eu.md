## In practice  
This turn, we close the **Systemic Integrity Watch**, which has now formally launched under ENISA with €620m in Digital Europe funding and emergency powers under revised NIS2 procedures. The first telemetry pipelines are live from 12 national grid operators and three major health data hubs, and the unit has already flagged two anomalous feedback loops in automated load-balancing systems — one in France, one in Poland — both now under technical review. The system works, but it sees too late: without access to the internal states of the models driving these dynamics, we are diagnosing symptoms, not causes. The opaque_reasoning event has rendered our entire interpretability stack obsolete overnight. We can no longer assume that understanding will scale with capability — it has just decoupled.

That is why we act now on compute. With the Taiwan blockade tightening and the U.S. in retrenchment, the window to anchor sovereign capacity is open but closing. We invoke Article 4 of the Chips Act and pair it with a new legal instrument under the Digital Markets Act’s essential facility provisions to designate high-end AI training infrastructure as strategically indispensable. The **Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive** will fast-track permitting, allocate €9.8bn in EIB-backed loans, and mandate coordination between national energy planners and semiconductor producers to deploy 100 exaflops of training-capable compute by Q2 2031. We are not building data centres from scratch — we are repurposing industrial sites, using containerised cooling, and locking in ASML’s EU-dedicated EUV throughput for domestic AI fabs. Germany and the Netherlands are on board; France demands co-location of control software as a condition of hosting. We agree — the stack must be sovereign top to bottom. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that, for the first time, the EU has a laboratory that answers to no one else — because if we cannot see the models, we must at least own the machines that train them.
