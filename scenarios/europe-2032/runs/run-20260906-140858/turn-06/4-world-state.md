### A Crisis of Access

The Taiwan blockade sends shockwaves through the global AI ecosystem, abruptly halting advanced semiconductor exports and threatening compute availability for every actor outside China’s domestic supply chain. The EU’s upstream strength in lithography equipment becomes both a strategic asset and a geopolitical liability, as pressure mounts to restrict shipments.

AI capability advances to 62.5, driven by self-improving agents accelerating training pipelines in closed labs. Openweight capability rises modestly to 50.0, reflecting replication of older architectures on newly scarce hardware — but no major leak bridges the frontier gap. With capability rising sharply and no safety breakthrough, ai_safety drops to 21.0 due to growing opacity in agent behaviour and unverified coordination loops.

The **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** concludes successfully. Binding cyber mandates are now embedded in NIS2 enforcement, red-teaming protocols cover seven major grids, and fallback systems operate in nearly half of Tier 1 energy and health providers. Resilience improves to 48.0 (+3 from completion), absorbing some of the risk posed by evolving underground cyber toolkits.

In response to the blockade, the EU launches the **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative**, a small category 4 measure aimed at securing existing compute clusters under enforceable jurisdiction. It is named priority, beginning immediately with €1.8bn in emergency funding. Legal anchoring begins at EuroHPC sites and major cloud nodes, though U.S.-linked hyperscalers push back against audit demands, citing conflicting obligations.

Sovereignty increases to 27.0: +3 from the new measure (in flight, minor momentum), +3 from the capacity-securing event `taiwan_blockade`, and −1 from ai_capability growth over 2.0.

Political capital reaches 43.0. Portfolio charge is −7 (Tech sovereignty package −3, Emergency Compute Assurance −2, priority −1). The resilience sprint finishes, granting +5. No proposal bonus applies — the initiative responds to an external shock, but was not proposed in reaction to a prior EU incident. Public sentiment holds at 34.0: no visible benefit yet, and anxiety persists around infrastructure fragility.
