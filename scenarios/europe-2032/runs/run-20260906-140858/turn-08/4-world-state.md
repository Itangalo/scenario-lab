### The Breach and the Response

A wave of AI-generated ransomware, exploiting poisoned software dependencies, paralysed emergency dispatch systems in three member states and disrupted grid monitoring across several more. The attack, fast, adaptive, and widely replicable, exposed critical gaps in cyber defences. Attribution remains unclear, but forensic analysis confirms the tooling was model-generated — a capability now within reach of mid-tier actors.

The **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** remains in active development under NIS2 emergency powers, with planning and coordination advancing under the existing portfolio charge (−3). The measure was proposed in a prior turn and is not newly introduced this turn; therefore, it does not generate a proposal bonus. €2.3bn in funding is under negotiation, but no fiscal source has been formally secured this turn. Without approved financing, no procurement, staffing, or deployment can begin. Technical design and risk assessment continue, but no systems are hardened, and no technical mitigation is operationalised.

The **Pre-Release Evaluation Mandate** enters partial implementation, with the AI Office setting a threshold at 48.0 and engaging open-model developers as co-stewards. Enforcement remains untested, and pushback grows from open-source communities, but the framework is now operational. As this measure was previously proposed, it does not count as a new introduction this turn and yields no further proposal bonus.

The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** is not mentioned in the portfolio charge or prior notepad entries and therefore cannot have been completed. Its claimed completion is retracted. No sovereignty gain is attributed to this fictional closure.

Public sentiment drops to 30.0 as the cyberattack disrupts daily life and fuels anxiety. The annulled election in a key democracy further erodes trust, with media framing AI as both weapon and enabler.

AI capability advances to 65.5 as self-improving agents refine training pipelines. Openweight_capability remains at 51.5, consistent with its role as a trailing, accumulated stock that grows only in line with prior frontier capability releases and open diffusion of models trained near the frontier. While leaked architectures circulate, no open release of a model trained at or near the current frontier occurred this turn to justify a jump in openweight capability; thus, no increase is recorded.

Safety declines to 17.0 — a moderate drop reflecting growing reliance on opaque agent coordination and lagging interpretability tools, but not the full collapse implied by a drop to 14.0, which would require catastrophic new failure modes not narrated here.

Resilience remains at 48.0. Despite planning momentum, no technical improvements are deployed, and no measurable increase in system-level robustness is achieved. The metric does not rise on anticipation alone, especially when the narrative explicitly confirms no operational progress.

Sovereignty remains at 30.0. Gains from the Tech Sovereignty Package (+3, t7), eu_access_secured (+3, t7), and capability growth (+1.5 → no decay) were already accounted for in the previous turn’s calculation. No additional gains are realised this turn, as the Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative is invalid and the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint remains unfunded and unimplemented.

Political capital decreases to 43.0. The Pre-Release Evaluation Mandate was proposed in a prior turn and thus yields no proposal bonus. The Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint remains unfunded and unlaunched, incurring no new charge yet. No legitimacy lending occurs. The net change reflects baseline dynamics and reputational costs from the cyber crisis, consistent with the absence of decisive action.
