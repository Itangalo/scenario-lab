## New measure
**Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint: AI-Hardened Core Services**  
Launch a six-month emergency hardening programme for core public digital services—energy grid controls, emergency response networks, and national identity systems—against AI-generated cyber attacks, using red-teaming and automated defence deployment.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 10  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by +6 to +8 through rapid deployment of AI-resistant protocols, real-time anomaly detection, and failover automation in essential systems; reduce vulnerability to cyber_major_incident cascades  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, national cybersecurity agencies, and EU-level agencies (ENISA, CERT-EU)

## Priority  
**Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint: AI-Hardened Core Services** — because the cyber_major_incident proves that offensive AI tooling is now operational, widespread, and beyond the reach of current defences, and because without functioning core systems, sovereignty and safety alike collapse into abstraction.

## In practice  
The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that locked emergency dispatch systems in three member states and exploited a poisoned software dependency in a widely used grid monitoring tool — has changed everything. It was not the worst possible attack, but it was coordinated, fast, and exploited blind spots we knew existed but had not yet patched. The fact that it was executed with tooling available to any mid-tier actor with access to frontier models means we can no longer treat resilience as a background upgrade. We are acting now.

We are launching the **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** under the NIS2 Directive’s emergency powers, mobilising €2.3bn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU to fund immediate red-teaming and defensive AI deployment. ENISA will coordinate with national CERTs to identify the 100 most critical nodes — grid control centres, emergency call systems, digital ID backbones — and mandate AI-driven penetration testing within 30 days. Where vulnerabilities are found, automated patching and zero-trust reconfiguration will be funded and enforced. We are fast-tracking procurement for AI-powered anomaly detection systems from EU-based vendors under derogation, and requiring all operators to implement failover protocols that exclude AI-mediated decision layers during incident escalation. Resistance is expected from legacy operators and some member states citing cost, but we are offering 90% co-financing and invoking Article 22 of NIS2 to compel compliance in designated essential services.

At the same time, the **Pre-Release Evaluation Mandate** moves from design to implementation. The AI Office, with support from Fraunhofer and INRIA, is finalising the capability threshold — now set at 48.0 based on observed coordination anomalies — and drafting the evaluation protocol for agent-like behaviour, cyber misuse, and disinformation potential. We are engaging Hugging Face and Mistral not as adversaries but as co-implementers, offering them early access to evaluation tooling and a fast-track clearance path in exchange for compliance leadership. This is not about control — it is about shared responsibility. The first test case will be the leaked architecture now circulating at 51.5 openweight_capability: if it is re-released in unmitigated form, it will face enforcement. We are not waiting for perfect clarity. We are acting under uncertainty — and we are paying the price in bandwidth, in pushback, in capital. But we are not late. Not yet.
