## In practice
We are executing the **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** under the emergency powers of the NIS2 Directive, with ENISA leading a task force that includes CERT-EU, national cybersecurity agencies, and selected EU-based AI defence vendors. The €2.3bn funding remains in negotiation, but we have unlocked €600m in REACT-EU emergency reserves to begin procurement and red-teaming at the 30 highest-priority nodes — primarily grid control centres and emergency dispatch hubs. We are fast-tracking derogations for AI-powered anomaly detection systems from EU vendors like TuxAI and SecurifAI, mandating zero-trust architectures and failover protocols that exclude AI from decision loops during incident escalation. Resistance persists in two member states citing cost and sovereignty concerns, but we are offering 90% co-financing and invoking Article 22 to designate non-compliance as a cross-border risk.

The **Pre-Release Evaluation Mandate** is entering its implementation phase. The AI Office, supported by INRIA and Fraunhofer, has set the capability threshold at 48.0 and is finalising the evaluation protocol for agent-like behaviour, cyber misuse, and disinformation potential. We are engaging Hugging Face and Mistral as co-stewards, offering fast-track clearance and early access to EU-developed evaluation tooling in exchange for leadership in community compliance. The leaked 51.5-capability architecture now circulating in the compute black market will be the first test case: any unmitigated public release will trigger enforcement under the Digital Services Act. We are not waiting for perfect clarity. We are acting under uncertainty — and we are paying the price in bandwidth, in pushback, in political capital. But we are not late. Not yet.
