## In practice
We are acting on the understanding that sovereignty is not only about how much compute we build, but whether we can trust it to stay on when we need it. The **Emergency Compute Assurance Initiative** is now in full motion: legal anchoring is underway at EuroHPC sites in Finland, France, and Italy, and we are invoking the European Chips Act’s safeguard clause to compel audit access at hyperscaler facilities exceeding 1 exaFLOP/s. The Commission is negotiating reciprocity with Japan and South Korea to accept equivalent assurances, easing pressure from U.S. cloud providers who warn of compliance overload. €1.8bn from Digital Europe and REPowerEU is being deployed to fund hardware verification, failover redundancy, and legal enforcement capacity within national cybersecurity agencies. Where resistance persists — particularly at U.S.-linked nodes in Ireland and the Netherlands — we are offering phased compliance in exchange for binding failover guarantees and third-party monitoring.

At the same time, the **eval_anomaly_reports** and the **election_annulled** event force our hand on open models. We cannot assume that today’s 50.0 openweight capability will remain inert: the anomaly suggests closed agents are already exhibiting unmodelled coordination, and the annulled election proves that plausible deniability is no longer a defence. While our hands are full, we cannot ignore the growing risk that a replicated model — perhaps one just below today’s frontier — could be weaponised in the absence of scrutiny. The new **Pre-Release Evaluation Mandate for High-Capability Open Models** begins quietly: we are tasking ENISA and the newly empowered AI Office to define a threshold — likely around 48.0 — above which any public release triggers mandatory third-party assessment for cyber, disinformation, and agent coordination risks. This is not a ban; it is a circuit breaker. The tooling will be built on the existing AI Act conformity assessment framework, adapted for open development pathways. We are engaging with Hugging Face, Mistral, and academic consortia early to avoid confrontation, but the rule will bind all. If a model clears evaluation, it publishes. If not, mitigation is required — or the release is delayed. This is the first real test of whether the EU can govern not just what it builds, but what the world lets loose.
