## Priority

## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_test_shot as a wake-up call: an intrusion that bypassed all known detection mechanisms and persisted across continents demands a response that is both immediate and structural. Under Article 114 TFEU, we are drafting a Council Regulation to establish binding resilience benchmarks for electricity transmission, water, and port operators — sectors confirmed as penetrated or at highest risk. The Cyber Resilience Office within ENISA will be temporarily empowered to mandate audits, require air-gapped control systems, and deploy AI-driven anomaly detection pilots co-developed with the EU’s academic security community. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with 70% co-financing for member states that adopt the new standards by Q2 2027.

The pushback is predictable: some member states argue this infringes on national competence over infrastructure, while operators warn of cost and complexity. We are countering with a carrot — fast-tracked access to the InvestAI Gigafactories’ energy allocation for compliant sites — and a stick — exclusion from future EU energy solidarity mechanisms for those who opt out. The German and Dutch governments are on board; Poland and Hungary are hesitant, but the recent blackout in Silesia has shifted the tone. We are also quietly coordinating with NATO’s CIRE to align technical baselines, ensuring our standards don’t fragment from collective defence protocols.

Meanwhile, the Tech sovereignty package is advancing through the Council’s Competitiveness formation, where the bottleneck remains grid access and local opposition to data centre sprawl. We are fast-tracking three pilot zones — in Finland, Spain, and Romania — where permitting will be capped at six months and grid upgrades pre-approved. The ASML leverage is being used discreetly: we’ve informed Washington that export controls on EUV tools to China will be matched with EU-level restrictions on hyperscaler expansion unless joint access to next-gen fabs is guaranteed. This is not yet a formal quid pro quo, but the signal is clear: sovereignty is not just about building, but about bargaining from strength.
