## In practice  
We are treating the emergent_ai_research_consortium as a strategic asset: a spontaneous alignment of European academic and public-sector expertise that can be rapidly scaled into a formal capability. Under the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe, we are launching the EU Open-Weight Threat Response Initiative as a permanent node under ENISA’s oversight, co-located with leading cybersecurity institutes in Darmstadt, Toulouse, and Prague. It will receive €1.2bn over two years to institutionalise red-teaming of open models, reverse-engineer attack vectors from Kimi K3 derivatives, and publish open defensive tooling. Crucially, it will have legal authority to access and test any open-weight model above 40.0 capability index — a threshold now reached — and to publish findings without developer consent. This closes a critical gap: we cannot regulate open weights directly, but we can ensure the Union understands them better than those who would misuse them.

At the same time, the Emergency Resilience Surge is entering its decisive phase. The Council Regulation under Article 114 TFEU has cleared the JHA formation, but Poland and Hungary have triggered the safeguard clause, arguing that air-gapping mandates violate national control over infrastructure. We are countering with a revised compromise: full co-financing (85%) for audit and detection upgrades, and a phased air-gap requirement tied to threat levels issued by ENISA — not Brussels. The German and Dutch are urging solidarity, and the recent blackout in Silesia, while not confirmed as AI-linked, has shifted domestic politics in Warsaw. We are also embedding the emergent_cyber_defence_coalition’s new swarm-detection protocols into the mandated toolset, giving member states access to NATO-grade defences in exchange for compliance. This is not just resilience — it is integration.

Meanwhile, the Tech sovereignty package advances slowly. The three pilot zones are operational, but grid bottlenecks in Spain and Romania threaten delays. We are leveraging the ASML channel again: in bilateral talks with the US, we have proposed a joint EU-US sovereign compute corridor, where next-gen fabs in Europe would feed both markets under mutual access guarantees. It is not a quid pro quo, but a vision — and one that may be needed if the current pace leaves us dependent not just on foreign models, but on foreign silicon. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, but their impact is still years away. We are not falling behind yet — but we are not catching up either.
