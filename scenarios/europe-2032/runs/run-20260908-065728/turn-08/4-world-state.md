### The Weight of Response

The EU pushes forward on multiple fronts, but under mounting pressure. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** programme concludes successfully, delivering €200 billion in anchored investment and a tangible boost to domestic compute capacity. This marks a rare win, reinforcing confidence in long-term strategic planning.

Simultaneously, the **European Compute Assurance Directive** enters inter-institutional negotiation, leveraging Article 114 TFEU to bind state aid, grid access, and permitting to strict jurisdictional and energy-resilience standards. Despite strong backing from DG CONNECT and ENISA, resistance grows in the Council over energy redundancy mandates, with several member states warning of implementation delays. The directive remains on track but faces legal scrutiny.

The new **Critical AI Workload Hardening Initiative** launches quietly, with pilot deployments in Luleá and Salamanca securing nuclear early-warning interpretation and grid balancing. Though small in scope, it signals a doctrinal shift: from passive resilience to active, sovereign redundancy in cognitive infrastructure.

Yet the offensive edge sharpens. A contested study—debated within biosecurity circles—suggests non-experts may now use AI to design viable human-infective organisms. While not yet public panic, the finding deepens unease about uncontrolled capability diffusion.

AI capability advances steadily to 70.5, driven by unrelenting baseline progress. Open-weight models see a modest jump to 52.0, as one lab releases a capable but restricted agent framework, now widely replicated. Safety erodes further, falling to 29.0, as no new safeguards land and incident response remains strained.

Resilience ticks up slightly to 55.0, buoyed by the new initiative’s partial effect. Sovereignty climbs to 28.0, lifted by completed Gigafactories (+5) and in-flight momentum from the Assurance Directive (+2), though tempered by a −1 penalty from capability rise. Political capital holds steady at 36.0 despite portfolio costs, offset by a proposal bonus for the timely response to recent shocks.

Public sentiment remains stagnant at 39.0—too low for legitimacy to lift capital, too high for backlash to ease.
