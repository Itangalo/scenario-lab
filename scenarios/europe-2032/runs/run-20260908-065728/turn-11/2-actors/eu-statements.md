# Statements: The European Union (turn 11)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity to ensure autonomy in strategic decision-making and catastrophic risk prevention

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Maintain continuous, sovereign oversight and control over AI systems operating in or affecting EU critical functions, ensuring the Union can detect, respond to, and withstand autonomous agent behaviour and external coercion without dependency on foreign goodwill
  - Grounds: The shift from infrastructure build-out to operational resilience is now complete. The Gigafactories are live, the Compute Directive enforced, and critical workloads hardened. But the **cyber_major_incident** confirms that capability is now weaponised at scale, and **supply_chain_coercion** proves our sovereignty is still contingent. The frontier may be plateauing—**emergent_plateau_signals**—but the systems already deployed are dangerous and opaque. The commitment must evolve from securing capacity to securing cognition: not just owning the compute, but seeing and controlling what runs on it. This reframing reflects that autonomy in crisis now depends on real-time visibility and sovereign response capacity, not just hardware.
