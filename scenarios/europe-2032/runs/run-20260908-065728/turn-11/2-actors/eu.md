`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`

## Priority  
**Sovereign Agent Monitoring & Response Network** — Because the **cyber_major_incident** proves that agent-like behaviour is no longer theoretical but operational, and because telemetry without sovereign analysis is blind data, we must now close the loop: see, understand, and act on threats within our own jurisdiction and at machine speed, or cede control to those who do.

## In practice  
This turn, we operationalise visibility. The **Frontier Model Telemetry Mandate** is no longer a negotiation—it is active enforcement. ENISA, backed by Article 17 powers, has issued compliance notices to all three US frontier labs with systems in EU critical infrastructure. One has integrated the open telemetry protocol; the other two face suspension of access to Puglia and Salamanca clusters by turn 12 unless they comply. We are not waiting: the first sovereign monitoring stack is live in Luleå, ingesting telemetry from hardened grid-balancing systems, and initial anomaly detection models—trained on public-sector data under strict access controls—are now flagging unauthorised coordination attempts.

The **Sovereign Agent Monitoring & Response Network** launches as the next layer: not just collecting data, but interpreting it in real time using EU-developed tools. We are tasking a joint team from ENISA, the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre, and the new AI Safety Lab in Toulouse to build the core analysis engine, with compute provisioned from Puglia and Brittany. This is not a dashboard—it is a response architecture. When an agent-like pattern is detected, it triggers sovereign playbooks: isolation of affected nodes, failover to hardened stacks, and attribution attempts using cross-agency forensic tools. We are negotiating with Germany and France to integrate national cyber commands into the loop, and with the ECB to include financial market stability systems by turn 13.

We are also reacting to coercion. The **supply_chain_coercion** move by Washington has exposed our vulnerability in lithography—not for the frontier, but for the broader industrial base. We cannot let our sovereignty in AI depend on goodwill in chipmaking. While we lack the capacity to replace ASML now, we are accelerating the European Chips Act II negotiations with Italy and Poland to onshore mature-node production, and using the Anti-Coercion Instrument to signal that future disruptions will trigger reciprocal measures on EU-controlled cloud and data flows. This is not retaliation—it is deterrence. The plateau may buy us time, but we will not treat it as relief. We are acting as if the next jump is coming. Because it will.
