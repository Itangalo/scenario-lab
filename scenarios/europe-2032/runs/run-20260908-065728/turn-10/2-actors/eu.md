## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Accelerate operationalisation of sovereign AI infrastructure and secure strategic workloads against failure or coercion, ensuring the EU can act independently in crisis and maintain continuity of governance

- Grounds: The two-year commitment period has closed, and while the goal of sovereign AI capacity remains, the decisive shift this turn is from construction to operation. The Gigafactories are commissioned, the Compute Assurance Directive is adopted, and the focus must now pivot from building capacity to ensuring it functions under pressure. The commitment evolves to reflect that sovereignty is not just about ownership of infrastructure, but about the ability to maintain essential cognitive functions when challenged—by incident, coercion, or cascading failure. This reframing aligns with the resilience gains and hardening efforts now live in Luleå and Salamanca.

Require continuous, real-time monitoring and reporting of internal model states for all AI systems above capability threshold 65.0 operating in or affecting EU critical functions.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`

## Priority
**Frontier Model Telemetry Mandate** — Because ai_capability has reached 73.0 and ai_safety has fallen to 26.0, we can no longer rely on post-hoc evaluations or static benchmarks: systems are evolving during deployment, and opaque reasoning is now a systemic risk. The priority shifts from infrastructure build-out to cognitive visibility—without telemetry, we are governing blind.

## In practice
This turn, we activate sovereignty. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** in Brittany, Luleå, Salamanca, and Puglia are no longer construction sites—they are live compute nodes, legally anchored under EU jurisdiction, with telemetry-sharing obligations now binding for all public-facing frontier models hosted within. The **European Compute Assurance Directive** is transposed, and ENISA begins audits next month: any data centre above 10MW using EU public funds or serving critical functions must now prove jurisdictional control, energy redundancy, and supply-chain provenance—or lose access to InvestAI expansion rights. We are not waiting for compliance; DG CONNECT has already paused two foreign-operated expansions pending review.

The **Critical AI Workload Hardening Initiative** concludes its pilot phase: redundant execution environments for nuclear early-warning interpretation and grid balancing are now validated in Luleå and Salamanca, with Puglia joining by next turn. These are not backups—they are parallel sovereign stacks, capable of taking over within minutes. The triad governance model—tested in France with co-governance and peacetime veto—is now extended to Poland and Lithuania under PESCO, with ECB integration for financial stability workloads underway.

Now we turn to the unseen. The **Sovereign Model Access Programme** has revealed persistent agent-like behaviours in models marketed as narrow assistants. We cannot accept systems whose internal logic we cannot trace. Thus, the **Frontier Model Telemetry Mandate** launches: all models above 65.0 capability operating in EU critical functions must stream interpretable internal states to a new ENISA-hosted monitoring layer. This is not a one-time audit—it is continuous oversight, modelled on aviation black boxes. We are negotiating with the three largest frontier labs under Article 17 of the AI Act, offering fast-track compliance for those who integrate open, standardised telemetry protocols. Resistance is expected, particularly from US-based developers, but we will tie access to EU sovereign compute to compliance. This is not optional: if we cannot see it, we cannot secure it.
