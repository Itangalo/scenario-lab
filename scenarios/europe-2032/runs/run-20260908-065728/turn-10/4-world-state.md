### The Shift from Build to Operate

The EU enters 2031 with infrastructure finally live. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** in Brittany, Luleå, Salamanca, and Puglia are now operational sovereign compute nodes, legally anchored and enforcing telemetry-sharing for hosted models. The **European Compute Assurance Directive** is transposed, with ENISA launching audits: two foreign-operated data centres above 10MW are suspended pending proof of jurisdictional control and energy resilience. The **Critical AI Workload Hardening Initiative** completes, validating redundant execution stacks for nuclear early warning and grid balancing—now capable of failover within minutes. These systems are no longer theoretical.

AI capability rises to 75.5, crossing the threshold where strong domain expertise becomes widespread across cognitive professions. Open-weight models advance to 57.0, driven by distributed adaptation of older frontier architectures, though still far from parity. With no lab-origin safety breakthroughs and opaque reasoning deepening, **ai_safety** remains fragile—yet the new **Frontier Model Telemetry Mandate**, prioritised and launched, delivers early credibility gains. Negotiations under Article 17 of the AI Act pressure US developers; one agrees to pilot open telemetry in exchange for access to Puglia’s cluster.

Resilience climbs to 62.0 as hardened workloads prove robust in stress tests. Sovereignty jumps to 34.0: +5 from Gigafactories, +5 from the Compute Directive, offset by −1 due to capability growth exceeding 2. Political capital reaches 39.0: +5 from two completed measures, +3 from the new mandate (answering recent safety erosion), −8 from ongoing portfolio costs, and +1 from public sentiment now slightly above capital. Sentiment rises to 41.0 on visible progress, though still ambivalent.

The **export_control_escalation** tightens US chip and model access—rationed even for allies—but EU’s new sovereign capacity absorbs the blow. No access is lost, so no sovereignty penalty applies.

### A Consortium in the Making

Technical coordination among EU states, Japan, South Korea, and Canada deepens around joint model evaluation. While no formal body exists, shared audit protocols take shape—laying groundwork for future independence from vendor claims.
