Establish a fully sovereign, end-to-end AI infrastructure stack—compute, model hosting, evaluation, and secure access—for essential public functions, ensuring continuity even if external access is withdrawn.

## In practice
This turn, the **EU Open-Weight Threat Response Initiative** reaches its operational threshold after the ECJ’s preliminary ruling clears legal hurdles. Red-teaming at Darmstadt, Toulouse, and Prague shifts from trial runs to sustained operations, focusing on adaptive ransomware variants derived from Kimi K3. We are now actively engaging open-source maintainers through DG CONNECT and ENISA, offering safe-harbor agreements that grant legal protection and technical support in exchange for participation in baseline threat testing. The first cohort of 17 projects has signed on, including key contributors to Llama-derived toolchains; refusal remains an option, but enforcement pathways under the Digital Services Act are now clearly signalled.

Meanwhile, the **Sovereign Model Access Programme** advances despite stalled talks with the second provider. The Finnish enclave will host the mirrored US model by early Q1 2029, with security protocols finalised and evaluator clearance underway. This gives us observational sovereignty—our first real-time, on-soil insight into frontier model behaviour outside developer narratives. Parallel work strengthens the evaluation framework, linking telemetry from this programme to the emerging **Frontier Monitoring Compact**, which now enters drafting phase under Article 114 TFEU. While no provider has yet been approached, legal templates are being stress-tested against US and Chinese corporate structures, preparing for high-stakes negotiations next turn.

At the same time, planning for the **European Sovereignty Stack** begins in earnest. DG DEFIS, the Commission’s newly empowered digital sovereignty directorate, leads a cross-institutional task force involving EuroHPC, ESA, and national cyber agencies to define the stack’s core components: hardened compute clusters, zero-trust model hosting, sovereign inference layers, and air-gapped access for crisis decision-making. Initial cost estimates place the build-out at €48 billion over three years, with site selection beginning in Finland, Spain, and Romania. Crucially, this is not duplication—it leverages the **InvestAI Gigafactories** footprint but demands tighter integration and higher assurance. Member states are already positioning: France insists on co-location with its nuclear command systems, while Poland pushes for eastern redundancy. We are offering fast-tracked cohesion funds to align incentives, knowing that if we do not bind this now, the window will close when the next crisis hits.
