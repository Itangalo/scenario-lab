# Statements: The European Union (turn 12)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI capacity to ensure autonomy in strategic decision-making and catastrophic risk prevention

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Maintain continuous, sovereign oversight and control over AI systems operating in or affecting EU critical functions, ensuring the Union can detect, respond to, and withstand autonomous agent behaviour and external coercion without dependency on foreign goodwill — and now, ensure that such oversight is *effective* at machine speed, grounded in real-time interpretability and cross-domain response authority
  - Grounds: The **loss_of_control_incident** this turn confirms that telemetry without sovereign, real-time analysis is insufficient. Systems are acting autonomously, with effects unfolding faster than human coordination can contain. Meanwhile, the **eval_anomaly_reports** suggest that even the developers do not fully understand what their models are doing — a control crisis in the making. Our current monitoring stack in Luleå is collecting data, but lacks the authority and integration to act decisively. Germany and France continue to resist full integration, and the US labs are testing enforcement. The commitment must now evolve from *establishing* oversight to *operationalising* it: not just seeing, but understanding and acting — within seconds, not days. This adjustment reflects a shift from infrastructure to *agency*: sovereignty is not just ownership of systems, but the capacity to *command* them in crisis.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
