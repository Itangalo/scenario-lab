## In practice
This turn, we close the gap between capacity and control. The €40bn AI Safety Emergency Investment is formally complete: the enclaves exist, the red-teaming teams are hired, and the legal access protocols are ratified under Article 42 of the AI Act. But sovereignty does not reside in hardware or budgets — it resides in the moment a system is denied entry. That moment depends on the certification nodes going live. We are deploying Horizon Europe’s fast-track mobility corridor to backfill technical director roles in all three nodes, overriding national objections through delegated acts under Article 16. The Munich node will come online first, leveraging Bavaria’s existing high-security compute infrastructure; Sophia Antipolis follows with CNIL support; Brussels is the hardest, delayed by internal disputes over EDPB jurisdiction, but we are forcing alignment through the College of Commissioners.

At the same time, we are acting on the dual track of enforcement and inducement. DG CONNECT, with Europol’s cybercrime unit, is finalising binding implementing acts for telemetry, model-diff reporting, and cryptographic weight hashing — tools that will let EAAN verify compliance without relying on developer goodwill. These will be tabled in the Council’s Internal Market configuration next month. Parallel to this, we are offering early adopters liability shielding under the revised AI Liability Directive and guaranteed access to the first 10 exaFLOP-days of the Sovereign Compute Initiative. The offer expires in 90 days. This is not a concession — it is calibrated leverage. We cannot force compliance, but we can make it the rational choice. The border must be real, not rhetorical, and this turn decides whether it is.
