## In practice  
This turn, we are treating the Open Science Resilience Initiative as a make-or-break validation of our entire approach to sovereign control. The Commission has convened an emergency College session in June to receive the final reports from the three hubs. DG CONNECT, in coordination with ENISA and the Joint Research Centre, is running parallel validation sprints: one assessing model performance under stress conditions in real-time grid balancing with ENTSO-E, another testing diagnostic accuracy and response latency in simulated pandemic scenarios with the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control. These are not academic reviews — they are go/no-go decisions for scaling.

We are also preparing for failure. If the latency and fragility issues persist, the legal disputes over procurement moratoria in Germany, France, and Italy will become untenable. The European Public Prosecutor’s Office is already drafting contingency opinions on temporary derogations under Article 122a, should we need to suspend interpretability mandates to maintain service continuity. At the same time, we are resisting pressure to accelerate untested foreign integrations — the access denial was coercion, yes, but rushing in alternatives without control would be surrender. We are out of time, but not yet out of principle. The field data will tell us which we must sacrifice.
