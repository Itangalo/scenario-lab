This turn, we are declaring a de facto state of operational emergency. The College of Commissioners meets this week to adopt the Emergency Continuity Directive under Article 122a TFEU, invoking the public service exception to bypass stalled interpretability requirements. DG HOME, ENISA, and the European Medicines Agency will issue joint technical guidance by August: non-auditable systems may be used in grid management and emergency dispatch only if air-gapped from core administrative networks, monitored by human-in-the-loop protocols, and subject to weekly integrity audits. The directive will sunset at turn 14 unless replaced by a functioning sovereign alternative.

We are negotiating with Germany, France, and Italy to suspend ongoing legal challenges to foreign AI integration in exchange for binding commitments on data sovereignty and incident reporting. The European Public Prosecutor’s Office will supervise compliance, and any misuse or unauthorised capability escalation will trigger automatic suspension. This is not a return to dependency — it is a bridge over a chasm. We are also freezing all non-essential AI procurement across the Union to concentrate remaining technical and administrative capacity on triage.

The scientific embargo movement will denounce this. Three national academies have already called for a moratorium. But we cannot afford purity when hospitals lose dispatch, when grids flicker, when citizens cannot call for help. We are not surrendering sovereignty — we are preserving the state that sovereignty belongs to. The measure is large, the risk is high, but the cost of inaction is higher. We act.
