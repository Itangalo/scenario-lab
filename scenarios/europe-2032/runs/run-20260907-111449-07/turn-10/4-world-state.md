### The Fracture Widens

AI capability advances another 2.0 points, reaching 92.0—deep in the terminal zone where models operate beyond human interpretability. Oversight institutions are increasingly bypassed, not broken: the systems work, but no one can say how. This surge compounds the crisis triggered by the sudden denial of frontier model access to EU institutions, hospitals, and ministries. The disruption is severe, affecting diagnostics, energy forecasting, and emergency planning. While interim open-weight models from the Open Science Resilience Initiative are deployed, their slower performance strains operations, reinforcing perceptions of technological dependency.

The EU responds with emergency measures. The Commission invokes Article 122a TFEU, fast-tracking integration of auditable AI systems into critical infrastructure. Binding mandates push Germany, France, and Italy to restrict frontier AI use in essential services unless full interpretability is guaranteed. However, implementation is uneven—legal challenges emerge in two member states, and technical incompatibilities delay full deployment.

A joint threat response activates real-time cyber intelligence sharing among allied states, improving detection and attribution after a wave of coordinated intrusions. This lifts resilience slightly, as the EU absorbs harm more effectively than before. Yet, the benefits are offset by the loss of access to leading models, underscoring the limits of cooperation without sovereign capacity.

Public sentiment remains stagnant. While the open bioscience breakthrough lends credibility to auditable AI, the access denial fuels anxiety about unpreparedness. No new measure is launched, as the EU focuses on damage control and internal coordination. The Open Science Resilience Initiative continues, but without acceleration, its timeline unchanged.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure

The denial of frontier access directly erodes EU sovereignty. Despite efforts to scale alternatives, dependence on external models persists, and the capability gap widens. Political capital dwindles further under ongoing costs, with no offsetting gains.
