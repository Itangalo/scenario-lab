### The Weight of Waiting

AI capability advances by 2.0 points to 94.0, deepening the terminal zone. Systems now operate with such speed and internal complexity that even their developers describe them as “cooperative black boxes”—they deliver results, but no team can reconstruct why. This widening gap between function and understanding renders existing oversight rituals obsolete.

The EU holds its course, treating the Open Science Resilience Initiative as an emergency sovereign stack. Interoperability sprints link auditable models to energy grid operators and medical diagnostics, but early assessments reveal unacceptable latency in real-time demand forecasting. Temporary inefficiencies are tolerated, yet political patience wears thin. Binding interpretability mandates in Germany, France, and Italy stall procurement pipelines, prompting legal challenges over proportionality and market distortion. COREPER negotiations drag on, revealing fractures: smaller member states fear being left behind if dual systems emerge.

ENISA confirms the frontier access denial was a deliberate act—interpreted widely as strategic coercion. Yet without new measures or breakthroughs, the EU’s response remains reactive. No progress is made on accelerating the resilience hubs’ deployment timeline. The Commission insists on field data before scaling, but the delay fuels perceptions of paralysis.

Public sentiment holds at 27.0. Anxiety intensifies around energy stability, with regional blackouts linked to AI-driven load fluctuations now under investigation. A growing research coalition calls for halting frontier collaborations, framing openness as vulnerability. Still, no public credit is gained—the wait for proof continues.

### Sovereignty Erodes Further

With no new measure launched and ongoing costs unchecked, political capital erodes. The capability advance further undermines sovereignty, which slips again due to dependence and delayed substitution.
