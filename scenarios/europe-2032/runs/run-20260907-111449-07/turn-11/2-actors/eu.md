## In practice  
This turn, we are treating the Open Science Resilience Initiative not as a research pilot but as the core of an emergency sovereign stack. The Commission has activated Article 122a TFEU’s crisis clause to fast-track integration between the initiative’s three operational hubs—based in Helsinki, Toulouse, and Padua—and national regulators in energy, health, and civil protection. DG CONNECT is leading a real-time interoperability sprint with ENTSO-E, the European Medicines Agency, and the Union Civil Protection Mechanism to embed auditable models into diagnostic support systems and grid balancing, even with their current performance gap. We are accepting temporary inefficiencies as the price of control.

We are also responding to the frontier access denial by enforcing binding interpretability mandates in Germany, France, and Italy for all AI used in critical infrastructure procurement. These are not bans, but *de facto* moratoria unless foreign providers can demonstrate full logging, traceability, and human-overseeable reasoning chains—conditions no frontier model currently meets. Legal challenges from two member states are being managed through urgent COREPER negotiations, backed by the European Public Prosecutor’s Office to assert the primacy of Union-wide security over national procurement autonomy. Meanwhile, ENISA’s forensic audit confirms that the access denial was not a technical failure but a deliberate policy act, executed without notice or appeal. This is not market volatility—it is coercion. And it proves that resilience without sovereign execution capacity is a delay, not a defence. We are out of time to build alternatives. The next move must be decisive—but it cannot be blind. We wait for the first field results before scaling or pivoting, because acting without evidence now would not be courage—it would be ritual.
