At the same time, we are launching the new **Emergency Resilience Reinforcement** measure in direct response to the second wave of municipal attacks. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) is issuing binding technical standards by the end of March for all AI-integrated municipal systems, requiring runtime monitoring, adversarial input detection, and human-supervised fallback modes. Funding of €4.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility will be disbursed within 60 days to affected member states, conditioned on compliance audits by the end of turn 10. This is not a long-term fix — it is triage. We are buying time for a broader resilience strategy, but only if we act now can we prevent cascading failures in public trust and essential services. The measure is small, but urgent, and it must not distract from our core sovereignty push: we are not choosing between defending what we have and building what we need — we are doing both, at the edge of what our capital allows.
