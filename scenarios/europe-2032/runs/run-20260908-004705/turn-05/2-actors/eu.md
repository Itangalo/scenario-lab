## In practice
We are pressing ahead with the InvestAI Gigafactories, now in their fifth turn, as the cornerstone of our industrial response to strategic dependence. The Compute Siting Task Force has secured grid priority for two of the five planned zones under the revised TEN-E corridor, and we are finalising binding agreements with ASML and STMicroelectronics to anchor advanced packaging and chiplet integration within the EU. Despite delays in cross-border transmission planning, we have unlocked €42bn in phase-one disbursements through InvestEU and the Innovation Fund, with co-investment agreements signed by four of the six lead consortia. The legal basis rests on Article 118 TFEU for industrial policy coordination, with state aid approvals fast-tracked through DG COMP’s crisis mechanism.

At the same time, we are conducting a full post-implementation review of the Emergency Resilience Surge, whose binding SBOM mandates and mobile cyber teams have been operationally deployed across ten high-risk jurisdictions. ENISA has verified SBOM adoption in 68% of targeted critical entities, though patching automation lags at 41%, particularly in municipal systems in Bulgaria, Hungary, and Slovakia. The €18bn disbursement is 82% released, with 70% tied to compliance benchmarks. While no major incident has recurred, red-team exercises reveal persistent fragility in healthcare supply chains, feeding into the next phase of resilience planning. The Tech Sovereignty Package has formally concluded: it succeeded in mobilising regulatory momentum but fell short of its capital target, with only €89bn in private commitments recorded—less than half the goal. We are now assessing whether to consolidate its remaining elements into the Gigafactories initiative or launch a new category 8 measure to coordinate supply-chain leverage with like-minded partners.
