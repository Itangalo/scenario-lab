### Frontier Surge and Open Release
AI capability advances by +2.5 to 62.0, driven by longer-horizon agent training and improved world modeling. An open-weight release follows within months of the frontier, distributing a model near the current closed frontier’s strength. With offensive tooling now widely accessible, `openweight_capability` jumps to 54.0. No coordinated safety evaluation occurs, and anomalies in agent behavior grow more frequent. With capability rising and no safety measure completed, `ai_safety` plummets to 15.0—now at a level where incidents are discovered only by their victims.

### Cyber Shock and Political Response
A major AI-generated cyberattack exploits a poisoned software dependency, triggering cascading ransomware across public services in five member states. Response is fragmented, attribution unclear. In reaction, the EU launches the **Emergency Resilience Surge**, a large Category 6 measure under Article 222 TFEU, mandating SBOMs, automated patching, and AI-assisted detection. Mobile cyber teams deploy to high-risk states, and €18bn is fast-tracked via Digital Europe and Cohesion funds, tied to compliance.

### Sovereignty Erodes Further
Despite progress on ASML and packaging agreements, no category 4 measure finishes. `ai_capability` rises by 2.5, costing −1. With no access-securing event, sovereignty falls to 15.0.

### Political Capital Under Pressure
Three large measures remain in flight: Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty, Labour Transition (now complete). Portfolio charge: −10. The new **Emergency Resilience Surge** (large, −3) replaces Labour Transition, but the priority remains, costing −1. Total charge: −13. A proposal bonus of +3 applies—this large measure directly answers a recent, severe incident—softening the fall. Still, `eu_political_capital` drops to 19.0.

### Public Backlash Mounts
The cyberattack, visible job losses, and backlash against data centres push `public_sentiment` down to 30.0—now in the "anxious and sceptical" range, with organised opposition growing.
