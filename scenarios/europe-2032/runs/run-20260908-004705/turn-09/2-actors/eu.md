## Priority
**Sovereign Access to Self-Verifying Agent Stacks** — because without replicating the frontier’s self-validation logic into EU-controlled infrastructure, we cannot see how decisions are made in systems now shaping lives and infrastructure, and thus cannot contest, correct, or govern them — making sovereignty not just about access, but about sight.

## In practice
We are concluding the Secure EU Access measure this turn, with the Joint Research Centre finalising agreements with two EU-based labs holding verification stacks above 65.0. The €500m buyout offer has been accepted by one lab; the second remains under negotiation, with expropriation proceedings under Article 4 of the AI Act now formally initiated. DG COMP has cleared the transaction from a competition standpoint, while DG TRADE has activated countermeasures in response to U.S. objections, leveraging restrictions on ASML servicing exports as bargaining leverage. The verification tools will be transferred to EU-operated secure facilities in Karlsruhe and Brussels by the end of Q4, with full operational handover to the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) by turn 10.

Simultaneously, we are launching the new measure to activate public auditing nodes — a direct operational follow-on from the verification access now being secured. ENISA, in coordination with the European Data Protection Board, will stand up three pilot nodes in Helsinki, Lyon, and Bologna by the end of March, each integrated with national health and social service AI deployments. These will ingest anonymised reasoning traces from self-validating agents, using the newly acquired verification logic to flag unauthorised inference pathways, circular justification loops, and unauthorised goal drift. Funding of €1.8bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe will be disbursed to national authorities conditioned on full data access and audit rights. This is not a research exercise — it is the first act of sovereign oversight in a world where AI systems now validate themselves beyond human scrutiny.

The emergency resilience reinforcement continues, but the automated decision scandal has shifted the ground: public trust in AI-mediated outcomes is now legally contested, not just politically fragile. We are adapting the resilience measure to include mandatory disclosure of agent reasoning in all audited domains, not just runtime monitoring. This does not reduce the urgency of hardening municipal systems — it raises the stakes. We are doing both, but the price is steep, and our political capital is near collapse. The emergent middle-power coalition gives us leverage, but not time. We are acting on the belief that without visibility, there is no sovereignty — and without sovereignty, there is no future we can determine for ourselves.
