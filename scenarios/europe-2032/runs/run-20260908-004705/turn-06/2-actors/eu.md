## New measure
**Secure EU Access to Foundational Verification Tools**  
Establish an emergency programme to capture, secure, and sovereignly operate the core AI verification infrastructure now emerging as a strategic bottleneck.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `eu_ai_sovereignty` by +8–12, prevent further erosion of `ai_safety` by securing access to self-verification tooling, and create leverage over frontier deployment via control of verification infrastructure.  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with mandatory integration for any system above 55.0 capability operating in EU markets.

## Priority
**InvestAI Gigafactories** — because with ASML access now under direct U.S. leverage and the frontier advancing into self-verifying systems, sovereign physical compute is the only remaining foundation on which all other capabilities — including verification — must run, and delaying its completion risks permanent strategic dependency.

## In practice
We are entering the final phase of the InvestAI Gigafactories, with construction now active at three of the five planned sites — in Wallonia, Central Hungary, and Northern Finland — and grid integration agreements finalised for two more. The European Investment Bank has activated its crisis-disbursement protocol under Article 122 TFEU, accelerating €14bn in tranches tied to quarterly construction milestones. DG ENER is coordinating with ENTSO-E to prioritise transmission upgrades through the revised TEN-E corridors, though delays in cross-border capacity remain acute, particularly between Poland and Lithuania. We are pressing national regulators to invoke the Energy Security Regulation (EU) 2019/941’s emergency provisions to override local permitting bottlenecks, offering compensation packages to municipalities hosting facilities in exchange for accelerated zoning decisions. Resistance persists in Bavaria and eastern Romania, where concerns over water use and baseload displacement are being amplified by anti-AI movements; we are responding with binding commitments to co-locate district heating and freshwater recycling infrastructure.

The post-mortem on the Emergency Resilience Surge confirms partial success: SBOM adoption has created a new baseline for software accountability, but patching automation remains below 50% in public health and transport systems. ENISA is now drafting a Category 6 follow-up measure focused on autonomous patch deployment and fallback logic, to be proposed next turn if `cyber_major_incident` triggers cascading failures. The Tech Sovereignty Package has formally lapsed, having mobilised only €89bn in private investment — a signal failure of the voluntary model. We are consolidating its remaining elements — particularly the accelerated-permitting zones — into the Gigafactories framework, converting them from incentives to mandates under the same legal basis.

Simultaneously, we are launching a new strategic initiative in response to the `verification_widens` event: the self-checking capabilities now emerging in legal and engineering domains are not just a safety feature — they are becoming the gatekeepers of trust, and thus of market access. We cannot afford to outsource their control. The new measure will task a newly formed EU AI Verification Authority, hosted jointly by the Joint Research Centre and EUIPO, with acquiring and operating sovereign instances of the core verification models, training them on EU legal and technical standards, and requiring all high-capability systems to be validated against them before deployment. This is not a transparency or oversight play — it is a sovereignty move disguised as safety infrastructure. We will fund it through a reallocation of unused Innovation Fund reserves and invoke Article 355 TFEU to treat verification capacity as a matter of Union interest. The first procurement will go out this quarter, targeting model weights, training data, and inference infrastructure from the three leading European labs — with an option to expropriate under Article 4 of the AI Act if access is denied. This is not popular in Dublin or Stockholm, but we are framing it as the price of remaining a jurisdiction that can still say no.
