## New measure
Labour Transition Accelerator  
A targeted retraining and wage insurance programme for public- and private-sector workers displaced by AI-driven automation in administrative, legal and mid-level software roles.

## Priority
**Labour Transition Accelerator** — because the emergent labour displacement wave is already fracturing public sentiment and risks turning AI into a political rupture before we can build the capacity to control it; without social stability, sovereignty is meaningless.

## In practice
We are launching the Labour Transition Accelerator under the European Social Fund+ and the new AI Adjustment Instrument, co-financed at 85% for the first two years to incentivise rapid uptake. The Commission will issue guidelines by January 2027 for eligible sectors, prioritising regions where AI-driven job losses exceed 5% of professional employment. We are negotiating with member states through the Employment, Social Policy, Health and Consumer Affairs Council (EPSCO) to fast-track national implementation plans, offering bridge funding from the Recovery and Resilience Facility for those that align with EU digital transition benchmarks.

At the same time, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by convening the first EU Compute Siting Task Force, hosted by the European Commission’s DG CONNECT and co-chaired by national energy and digital ministers. The task force will resolve grid access and environmental permitting bottlenecks in real time, using the emergency powers granted under the revised TEN-E Regulation. We are also finalising agreements with three semiconductor firms and two cloud providers to anchor compute capacity under EU legal jurisdiction, with data sovereignty clauses modelled on the Gaia-X framework.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is facing resistance in the Council over state-aid concerns and fears of regulatory overreach, particularly from smaller member states worried about being bypassed. To secure consensus, we are offering a regional equity clause: any accelerated-permitting zone must reserve 20% of capacity for SMEs and public research institutions, and host at least one cross-border compute hub. This trade-off preserves speed while reinforcing cohesion — a necessary balance when building sovereignty under fire.
