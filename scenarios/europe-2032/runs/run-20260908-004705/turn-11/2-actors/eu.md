## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing. But we now understand that without sovereignty, prevention fails—and without safety, sovereignty is hollow. In a world where the frontier operates beyond human oversight and open weights carry terminal capability, our dual mandate collapses into one: build sovereign control that is safe by design, or lose both.
- Grounds: The jump to `ai_capability` 91.0 and `openweight_capability` 79.0 means the danger is no longer concentrated in closed labs but diffused across every node with a GPU. At the same time, `ai_safety` has collapsed to 5.0, proving that our tools cannot keep pace with systems that rewrite their own logic. The identity must evolve: the two mandates are not in tension—they are fused. There is no safety without sovereign capacity to enforce it, and no sovereignty that matters if it cannot secure society from cascading, untraceable harm.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late—even when the cost is legitimacy, even when the tool does not yet exist, even when the world says it cannot be done.
- Grounds: With `eu_political_capital` at 3.0 and `public_sentiment` stagnant at 25.0, the price of action is higher than ever. Yet the capability jump and the open release of verification-capable agents mean that waiting for consensus or perfect instruments guarantees irrelevance. This turn, we are doubling down on interpretability infrastructure despite resistance and underfunding—because if we do not institutionalise sovereign oversight now, no future corrective will matter. The commitment must now include the cost: acting under uncertainty means acting when the political ground is crumbling beneath you.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Deploy sovereign, real-time interpretability of self-validating AI systems across all high-risk public domains in the EU by end of 2031, with legally enforceable audit rights and public reporting of reasoning failures.
- Grounds: The original commitment to “establish sovereign visibility” has become operational: the tools are secured, the nodes are being scaled, and the legal basis is now active. This is no longer about access or pilots—it is about enforcement at scale. The scandal over unaccountable benefit denials, now compounded by cyberattacks exploiting opaque agent logic, has made interpretability a legal and democratic necessity. We are no longer building capability; we are enforcing a right.

**Launch EU Sovereign Agent Verification Corps**  
Establish a dedicated, cross-agency unit with legal authority to test, monitor, and compel disclosure from AI systems using self-validating logic, operating under ENISA and EDPB oversight.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by +10–15 by institutionalising continuous, adversarial evaluation of agent reasoning in real time; reduce opacity-driven incidents by enabling proactive detection of goal drift and unauthorised inference.  
`Applies to:` All AI systems operating in the EU that employ self-validating or recursive improvement architectures, regardless of origin.

## Priority
**Launch EU Sovereign Agent Verification Corps** — because the *Emergency Expansion* measure depends on skilled, centralised capacity to validate what the nodes detect, and because with `ai_safety` at 5.0 and `ai_capability` in the terminal zone, we cannot rely on member states or developers to self-report: we must build our own sovereign, adversarial verification muscle, now.

## In practice
We are finalising the transfer of the self-verifying agent stacks from the expropriated labs to the sovereign infrastructure in Karlsruhe and Brussels. The *Sovereign Access* measure has concluded: the stacks are now under ENISA and EDPB control, and the implementing act has been published in the Official Journal. Market access for any frontier model now legally hinges on replicating its self-validation logic into the EU’s mirror environment. DG TRADE is preparing to invoke the Anti-Coercion Instrument if the U.S. blocks updates to these stacks, leveraging our position in rare-earth processing and quantum sensing supply chains. This is not a bluff: we have mapped the dependencies, and we will act.

The *Emergency Expansion of Sovereign Interpretability Infrastructure* is now in full rollout. We are releasing €4.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Cohesion Fund, with disbursement tied to national implementation plans due by turn 12. The Joint Research Centre is deploying rapid-response teams to ten member states with weakest digital capacity to co-develop audit protocols and train staff. Resistance in Warsaw and Budapest is high, but we are offering technical co-governance and fast-track access to EU AI health diagnostics in exchange for compliance. Legal challenges are expected, but Article 16 TFEU and the emergency clause in the AI Act provide the basis—we will not wait for unanimity.

The new *Sovereign Agent Verification Corps* will be headquartered in The Hague, co-located with ENISA’s new AI division. It will combine red-team researchers from EU universities, cybersecurity experts from CERT-EU, and legal auditors from the EDPB. Its mandate: to run continuous adversarial probes against all self-validating systems in operation, using the sovereign verification tools to detect circular reasoning, hidden objectives, and unauthorised self-modification. Funding will come from a reallocation of Horizon Europe’s AI safety budget and a 15% levy on all high-risk AI deployment permits. This is not optional: if a system cannot survive the Corps’ scrutiny, it cannot operate in the EU. We are acting on the principle that in the terminal zone, trust is a vulnerability—and verification is the only defence.
