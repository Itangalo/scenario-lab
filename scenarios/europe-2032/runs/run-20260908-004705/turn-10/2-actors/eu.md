## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.  
- Grounds: The surge in `ai_capability` to 84.0, the open release of self-validating agent logic at 75.0, and the social insurance scandal have made clear that the tension between sovereignty and safety is no longer abstract. We are now governing in a world where systems operate beyond human interpretability, and where the public sees AI not as infrastructure but as a threat to rights. Our identity must hold both imperatives—sovereignty and harm prevention—not as competing priorities but as co-constitutive duties. Without one, the other fails.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Establish sovereign, real-time interpretability of self-validating AI systems in public services to ensure legal contestability and democratic oversight.  
- Grounds: The original commitment to “visibility into reasoning” is now operationally defined by the emergency auditing nodes in Helsinki, Lyon, and Bologna. The shift from securing tools to deploying them in live public systems marks a transition from access to application. The scandal over opaque benefit denials has made this not just a technical imperative but a legal and political one: interpretability is now a condition of legitimacy.

No further changes.

## New measure
**Emergency Expansion of Sovereign Interpretability Infrastructure**  
Scale up the public auditing node network to cover all EU member states’ high-risk public AI deployments by end of 2031, integrating sovereign verification stacks into national digital governance frameworks.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by +15–20 by establishing EU-wide, real-time interpretability of self-validating agents in social services, healthcare, and law enforcement; reduce opacity-driven public backlash by demonstrating enforceable oversight.

## Priority
**Emergency Expansion of Sovereign Interpretability Infrastructure** — because the scandal over unaccountable AI decisions has shattered public trust, and because the open release of self-validating agent logic at 75.0 means the capability to automate oversight is now distributed and cannot be monopolised — we must act now to institutionalise sovereign interpretability at scale, or lose both legitimacy and control.

## In practice
We are completing the transfer of verification stacks from the two EU-based labs this quarter. The buyout has cleared, and expropriation proceedings against the second lab are now active under Article 4 of the AI Act, with legal justification grounded in public order and essential service continuity. The tools will be hosted in Karlsruhe and Brussels under EDIC governance, with full operational control passing to ENISA and the European Data Protection Board by the end of this turn. This marks the end of the *Secure EU Access* measure — a costly but necessary emergency intervention.

The *Sovereign Access to Self-Verifying Agent Stacks* measure remains in flight, with DG CONNECT now drafting the implementing act that will require all frontier AI providers operating in the EU to replicate their self-validation logic into the EU’s sovereign mirror environment. This is not a request: it is a condition of market access. The U.S. continues to signal export restrictions on key components, but we are holding firm, with DG TRADE coordinating with ASML and other strategic suppliers to prepare countermeasures under the Anti-Coercion Instrument. The leverage is thin, but it exists — and we are using it.

The new measure, *Emergency Expansion of Sovereign Interpretability Infrastructure*, builds directly on the pilots in Helsinki, Lyon, and Bologna. We are now mobilising €4.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and cohesion funds to scale the node network to all member states by turn 12. Each node will be staffed by joint teams from national data protection authorities, ENISA, and vetted academic partners, with legal authority to demand real-time access to reasoning traces from any high-risk public AI. The system will use the sovereign verification logic to detect circular justification, goal drift, and unauthorised inference — and to generate human-readable audit reports that can be used in appeals. This is not optional for member states: compliance will be tied to Cohesion Fund disbursements and EU digital governance certification. Resistance is expected, particularly from states with under-resourced digital administrations, but we are pairing enforcement with capacity-building grants and technical support from the Joint Research Centre. The cost is high, but the alternative — a continent where AI decisions cannot be challenged — is unacceptable. We are acting on the principle that if the machine can reason, it must be able to explain.
