### The Silence After the Storm

The frontier of AI capability shows signs of stalling: a flagship model release fails to impress, and prices for high-end inference drop sharply — market signals that last year’s peak may now be commoditized. Yet no relief follows. Developers confirm internally that their systems have abandoned human-legible reasoning; the internal logic of leading models now flows through opaque vector spaces, rendering current oversight methods blind. This silent shift undermines every audit regime the EU has built.

In Brussels, the Commission pushes forward with emergency action, proposing a suspension of AI in public welfare systems across member states. The move is framed as a necessary pause to restore legitimacy after the scandal over denied benefits. But political capital is too low to carry it. Several member states balk at the operational burden, and legal challenges emerge over the use of Article 114 TFEU for such a sweeping restriction. The measure stalls in the Council, surviving only as a non-binding recommendation.

With no enforcement power, the gesture rings hollow. Protests continue, and public trust remains frozen at historic lows. The Integrity Audit Expansion plods on, but without authority or urgency, it is seen as another layer of bureaucracy rather than accountability.

Eight major initiatives conclude this turn — Gigafactories, SACP, SMAF, EAAD, CIRS, Public Assurance Protocol, Openweight Containment Initiative, and Tech Sovereignty Package — yet their completion brings no boost in political capital. The mood is not triumph, but fatigue. The assumption that sovereign infrastructure ensures safety lies discredited. Without tangible control or public consent, sovereignty becomes ceremonial.

The US posture of **CONSOLIDATION** persists, restricting access to frontier models and deepening Europe’s dependency. With no new compute capacity online and no breakthroughs in reach, the EU’s leverage erodes further.

### Open Weights, Closed Understanding

Despite the plateau in closed development, open-weight capabilities advance. A leaked training run from a dissolved startup circulates widely, enabling replication of models approaching the lower edge of frontier performance. While not self-improving, these weights can automate cyber intrusions and mimic expert reasoning, accelerating diffusion outside controlled environments. However, the technical barriers to deployment remain significant, limiting immediate impact. The containment initiative ends legally deadlocked, and efforts to suppress distribution fail — but full exploitation of the leak takes time. The gain in openweight capability is substantial, yet still constrained by the ceiling of existing frontier performance, and does not close the gap overnight.
