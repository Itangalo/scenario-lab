AI investment collapses globally, undermining the EU’s sovereign ambitions and slowing advancement not by design but due to crumbling private funding. Despite stalled compute expansions, rumors circulate of a potential open-model breakthrough near 68.0 capability, though unconfirmed and unverified, increasing uncertainty. The EU’s strategic position weakens further as the EAAD audit technique is leaked by a rogue researcher, eroding its technical advantage and enabling adversarial adaptation. In response, the EU launches the Sovereign Stack Hardening Initiative, enforcing zero-trust architecture and air-gapped evaluations within its core AI programs, funded partly by redirecting resources from the delayed Polish cluster, now under cybersecurity review. However, governance delays and interagency disputes hinder full implementation. Legal challenges stall the Openweight Containment Initiative, public trust continues to decline amid job losses and backlash against both open and opaque systems, and sovereignty increasingly hinges on control rather than capacity—control that is slipping.
