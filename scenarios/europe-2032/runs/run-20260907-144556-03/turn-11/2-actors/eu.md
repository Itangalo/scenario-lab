## In practice
This turn, we govern from weakness — but not from surrender. The emergency suspension of AI in welfare systems was proposed last turn, but it has stalled in the Council, blocked by legal uncertainty and operational resistance. We now shift ground: instead of demanding withdrawal, we demand replacement. The suspension is no longer the end, but the beginning — a pause to make space for people. We are launching the EU Civilian AI Redeployment Corps as a direct response to the hollowing out of public trust. It will take former caseworkers, benefits administrators, and local civil servants whose roles were automated — often poorly — and redeploy them as algorithmic redress officers, empowered to investigate denials, restore access, and serve as trusted intermediaries between citizens and automated systems. They will be paid 90% of their previous salary, funded jointly by the European Social Fund and a new Emergency Transition Facility under the Commission’s delegated powers.

We are negotiating with the Employment, Social Policy, and Justice Council formations to adopt the programme under Article 162 TFEU, offering member states full co-design of local implementation and front-loaded grants to cover wage guarantees. The goal is not just to cushion displacement, but to weaponise dignity: every redress officer becomes proof that the EU sees its people as more than data points. This is how we rebuild legitimacy — not by promising better audits, but by putting humans back in the loop where harm was done. The Integrity Audit Expansion has collapsed, yes, and our oversight is blind — but we still have hands. And this turn, we use them to lift people up, not just tear systems down.
