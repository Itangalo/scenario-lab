## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the Emergency Compute Access Pact  
A binding agreement among EU member states to pool access to high-end inference capacity — currently leased from US providers — and to reserve a sovereign slice for essential public functions, including crisis response, red teaming, and continuity of government services. The pact includes a mutual defence clause: any state whose access is cut off by an external actor will be served from the pooled reserve, with routing guaranteed through EU-operated infrastructure.

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Emergency Compute Access Pact  
We are pushing this hardest because the **governance vacuum** and **sovereignty at 10.0** mean we cannot enforce safety, contain openweight diffusion, or even audit our own systems — not because we lack will, but because we lack the most basic leverage: control over the compute our institutions depend on. This turn, we act on the only layer we can still coordinate: access. Without this, the Redeployment Corps will run on foreign servers, the CIRS will be blind to its own assumptions, and every assurance effort remains a performance. We are not building capacity yet — but we are staking a claim on its future.

## In practice
This turn, we govern from the edge of collapse — not with the tools we wanted, but with the ones still within reach. The Civilian AI Redeployment Corps moves forward under Article 162 TFEU, with the Commission activating the Emergency Transition Facility to disburse front-loaded grants to member states that sign on by June. We are negotiating with the Employment and Social Affairs Council to co-design local deployment frameworks, offering full wage guarantees in exchange for data-sharing on redress outcomes. The goal is not just re-employment, but the creation of a living feedback loop: every case reviewed by a redress officer becomes a data point in rebuilding public trust.

Simultaneously, we are convening an emergency session of the Competitiveness Council to secure political agreement on the Emergency Compute Access Pact. The proposal is simple: every member state currently leasing high-end inference from US providers will contribute 15% of its contracted capacity to a shared reserve, managed by a new Joint Compute Coordination Cell under DG CONNECT. In return, they gain guaranteed access during blackouts and priority routing through EU-operated gateways. We are offering early-access states a 10% bonus allocation and fast-tracked permitting for future data centres. Resistance is high — particularly from smaller states reliant on bilateral deals — but the recent ransomware incident has made the risk of dependency impossible to ignore. This is not sovereignty yet, but it is the first line in the sand: we will not be switched off without a fight.
