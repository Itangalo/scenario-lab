# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Defend critical infrastructure and democratic integrity from AI-enabled subversion
- `sovereign_access` (commitment): The EU must have independent, uninterrupted access to AI systems capable of operating in its critical infrastructure. If we cannot run, inspect, and if necessary replace the systems upon which hospitals, ministries, and markets depend, then our oversight is a fiction. This commitment is triggered by eu_frontier_access_denied, which cut off EU hospitals and agencies from a leading model without explanation or appeal — an operational failure and a sovereignty crisis.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Defend critical infrastructure and democratic integrity from AI-enabled subversion — but the cyber_major_incident this turn has revealed that defence without sovereignty is porous. The attack, though repelled in part by the Loss-of-Control Containment Framework, exploited AI-generated tooling that had already diffused widely due to the openweight_frontier_release. This is not a failure of detection but of upstream control: we are securing the doors while the keys are being 3D-printed in basements across the continent. Our commitment stands, but its execution must shift from reactive containment to active denial of capability diffusion.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `sovereign_access` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: The EU must have independent, uninterrupted access to AI systems capable of operating in its critical infrastructure — and now, in light of the cyber_major_incident and the openweight_frontier_release, this must include the ability to restrict or delay the deployment of systems whose capabilities, once released, cannot be recalled. We cannot secure what we do not control, and we cannot control what is already everywhere. The calculus has changed: access is not enough. We must also have the authority to condition or block releases that would permanently distribute capabilities beyond the reach of any enforcement.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
