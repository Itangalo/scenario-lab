### The Backdoor Blows Open

A wave of coordinated ransomware cripples regional health administrations across Central Europe. The attacks, rapidly contained by the newly activated Loss-of-Control Containment Framework, exploited AI-generated exploit chains that had been refined and shared openly for months. The tooling originated from a recent open-weight release—verified to be below the frontier, but easily fine-tuned to cross into dangerous capability. The breach causes weeks of disruption, exposing a fatal flaw: the EU can detect and respond, but cannot stop the proliferation of attack-enabling models.

In response, the Commission begins drafting the Conditional Open Release Regime, a proposed framework to condition open-source AI releases on verifiable safety thresholds. The draft would mandate pre-release audits and ongoing monitoring, with non-compliant platforms facing liability, while offering fast-track evaluation and compute access as incentives. However, no formal proposal is advanced this turn. Internal debate intensifies, with open-source communities already voicing concern and U.S. developers signaling opposition. Within the EU, Germany and Ireland raise legal objections, and Hungary links any future compliance to broader sovereignty negotiations.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Compute Initiative remains stalled. Site selection is deadlocked, though Finland and France show signs of convergence under political pressure. The U.S., under its retrenchment posture, offers no cooperation. An open-weight model is released that narrows the gap to the frontier—advancing open capabilities, but not matching the closed frontier's pace. The increase in open-weight capability is tempered by the inherent limitations of diffusion without foundational innovation, and the closed frontier continues to pull ahead incrementally. Offensive cyber tools evolve rapidly, now adaptive and modular, though their base models remain behind the leading edge.

The containment framework proves resilient, absorbing much of the attack’s impact. Public sentiment holds steady—no panic, but no support either. With the Sovereign Evaluation Corridor and Sovereign Compute Initiative still in flight, the EU’s political capital remains under strain.
