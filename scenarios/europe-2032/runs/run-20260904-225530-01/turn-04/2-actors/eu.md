## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without resilience in people and systems, sovereignty is hollow and safety is unenforceable.  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor sovereignty and risk resilience → Expand and harden EU-controlled AI capacity and social absorption infrastructure in parallel, so that technological autonomy does not outpace societal stability.  
- Grounds: The emergent labour displacement wave and open-source bio-toolkit, combined with collapsing safety and rising open capability, prove that technical sovereignty alone cannot contain systemic risk. Our commitment must now reflect that resilience — in jobs, in institutions, in public trust — is not a complement to capability but a condition of its legitimacy. We are acting on this shift in emphasis this turn, redirecting capital toward absorption capacity while maintaining compute buildout. The reversal costs us cohesion and delays in the sovereignty agenda, but failing to act would cost us the mandate itself.

## New measure  
**Measure:** Launch EU Reskilling and Transition Acceleration Facility  
Establish a pan-European rapid-response retraining and wage-insurance programme for workers displaced by AI in legal, accounting, public administration, and technical support roles, with modular digital curricula, employer co-investment incentives, and regional transition hubs.

Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 7  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 10–15 points; stabilise public sentiment by addressing visible job losses; reduce political backlash that threatens cohesion and capital  
Applies to: Member states, trade unions, displaced workers, and employers automating roles above 50.0 capability systems

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch EU Reskilling and Transition Acceleration Facility  
This is now our top priority because the emergent labour displacement wave — especially in mid-level technical roles — is eroding public trust and political capital faster than we can build compute, and without societal absorption, even sovereign AI becomes a source of instability, not strength.

## In practice  
We are activating the Reskilling and Transition Acceleration Facility under the European Social Fund+ with a €22bn envelope: €12bn in new allocations from the Just Transition Mechanism (reclassified to include AI-driven dislocation), €6bn from unused Youth Employment Initiative funds, and €4bn in matched employer contributions via tax credits. The facility will launch in Q2 2028 with pilot hubs in Berlin, Marseille, and Valencia — cities reporting the sharpest drop in mid-tier hiring — co-managed by national labour ministries, trade unions, and sectoral chambers. We are fast-tracking digital credentialing through the European Digital Credentials framework so that retraining outcomes are immediately recognised across borders. To secure Council approval, we are pairing this with a temporary suspension of state-aid limits for firms that commit to retraining 70% of displaced staff within 12 months.

At the same time, we are advancing the Sovereign AI Compute Initiative: procurement of the first 30,000 GPUs is finalised with AMD under a non-exportable firmware agreement, and site preparation begins in Oulu and Sines this quarter. The Commission’s Special Purpose Vehicle is now operational, insulated from third-country jurisdiction. We are also completing EAAD’s staffing and mandate finalisation — its first audit will target the newly released autonomous reasoning model, focusing on goal persistence and self-modification risks. The Cyber Shield’s red-teaming in Hungary has revealed three critical vulnerabilities in energy SCADA systems, now patched; we are scaling the exercise to Poland and Romania in June. The Tech Sovereignty Package clears the Council’s Internal Market formation this month, with ASML provisions deferred but grid access and permitting reforms locked in. We are not slowing sovereignty — but we are no longer building it blind to the society it must serve.
