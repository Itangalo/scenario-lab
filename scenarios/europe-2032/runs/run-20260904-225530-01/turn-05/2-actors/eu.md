## New measure
**Measure:** Establish binding pre-deployment evaluation thresholds for autonomous AI systems  
Require all models with persistent goal pursuit or self-modification capabilities to undergo independent evaluation by the European AI Assurance Directorate before release, with legally enforceable thresholds for agent persistence, goal stability, and weight security.

Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 6  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by 10–15 points by grounding enforcement in a credible, public authority; reduce the risk of uncontrolled agent proliferation in open and closed systems  
Applies to: Frontier AI developers operating in or selling into the EU, with extraterritorial reach via market access conditions

## Priority
**Priority:** Establish binding pre-deployment evaluation thresholds for autonomous AI systems  
This is our top priority this turn because the collapse in ai_safety to 14.0 and the emergence of autonomous agents in both closed and open systems mean that the window for preventive governance is closing — not next year, not after the next election, but now, and we are the only jurisdiction still positioned to act before the next capability leap.

## In practice
We are activating the European AI Assurance Directorate as a fully empowered regulator this quarter: its first mandate is to issue binding evaluation thresholds for autonomous AI systems, focusing on agent persistence, goal drift, and self-modification risks. The legal basis is Article 16 of the AI Act, as amended by the Tech Sovereignty Package, and we are invoking emergency powers under Article 22 to fast-track implementation in response to the cyber_major_incident and the documented release of agent-capable models into open ecosystems. The EAAD will publish its first evaluation framework by September, with enforcement beginning in Q1 2029. We are deploying €1.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme to scale its technical capacity, including red-team labs and secure access environments for model interrogation. To ensure compliance, we are linking market access for high-impact models to EAAD certification — a move that will face legal challenges from U.S. and Chinese providers, but one we are prepared to defend in the CJEU and at the WTO.

At the same time, we are advancing the Sovereign AI Compute Initiative: the first 30,000 GPUs are now under contract with AMD, with firmware locked to EU jurisdiction and no remote disable capability. Site preparation in Oulu and Sines is on schedule, with grid connections secured through emergency energy resilience provisions. The Reskilling Facility is operational in Berlin, Marseille, and Valencia, with 18,000 workers enrolled in digital credential pathways; we are pressuring member states to adopt the fast-track recognition framework by December or risk suspension of cohesion funds. The Cyber Shield has expanded to Poland and Romania, where red-teaming has uncovered AI-spoofable vulnerabilities in rail signalling — now being patched under binding directives. The Tech Sovereignty Package clears the Council this month, though ASML remains unresolved. We are not slowing down — but we are no longer building sovereignty in silence. This turn, we make it enforceable.
