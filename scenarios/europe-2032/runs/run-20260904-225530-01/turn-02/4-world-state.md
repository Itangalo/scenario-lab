### Capability Creeps Forward  
Global AI capability advances to 57.5, driven by longer-horizon agent coordination and improved tool integration. Labs compress release cycles, and while no public leap occurs, internal evaluations hint at anomalous reasoning patterns—systems solving novel problems without explicit training. The biosecurity community is divided over a new genome model that proposes a human-infective organism, dismissed by some as theoretical, feared by others as a blueprint.

### Open Weights Catch Up  
With Kimi K3 still circulating widely and no new restrictions, openweight capability rises to 45.0—now matching the closed frontier from just two years prior. The gap narrows not from new open releases, but from refinement of existing models on consumer hardware, enabling sustained cyber operations and automated fraud at scale.

### Safety Erodes Amid Uncertainty  
Despite growing risks, no major safety measure lands on shipped systems. The anomalous eval reports and bio uplift findings widen the gap between capability and control. Developers insist systems remain within design bounds, but independent verification is absent. AI safety dips to 32.0 as confidence in oversight erodes.

### EU Responds to Systemic Risk  
The EU launches the Hardened Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield as a binding regulation, mandating AI-resistant detection, air-gapped backups, and red-teaming for essential services. Germany and France back the plan, but Hungary and Slovakia resist, citing cost and sovereignty. Full EU co-financing (up to 80%) and a new Resilience Corps ease tensions, but implementation delays are expected.

InvestAI Gigafactories progress slowly: grid reservations are secured, but site approvals in Brittany and northern Sweden remain stalled. The Tech Sovereignty Package is restructured to reduce legal exposure, splitting ASML-related measures from permitting reforms. EAAD funding shifts from a contested levy to a graduated fee on model deployment, avoiding immediate WTO action but inviting legal challenges from U.S. firms.

### Political Capital Drains  
Three large measures and a priority strain EU cohesion. The Cyber Shield earns no immediate resilience gain, though partial progress is expected by turn 6. Political capital falls to 39.0 under portfolio costs and unresolved tensions, with no finishing measure to offset the burden.
