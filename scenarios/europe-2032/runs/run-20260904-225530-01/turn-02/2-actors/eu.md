## New measure  
**Measure:** Hardened Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield  
Launch a binding, EU-wide cyber resilience upgrade for energy, water, rail, and health infrastructure, mandating AI-resistant intrusion detection, air-gapped control backups, and quarterly red-teaming.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 6  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 8–12 points; reduce systemic fragility exposed by recent intrusion; establish a baseline of operational continuity under AI-augmented attack  
Applies to: All operators of essential services in the EU, with enforcement through ENISA and national CSIRTs, prioritising cross-border interdependencies

## Priority  
**Priority:** Hardened Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield  
This measure is our response to a live threat: the intrusion was not stopped by our systems, and the next one may not be discovered by chance — we are acting now because the margin for error has vanished, and resilience is the only line between disruption and collapse.

## In practice  
We are activating the Cyber Shield through Regulation, not Directive, under Article 114 TFEU to ensure uniform implementation and avoid fragmentation. The legal text will mandate three core upgrades: (1) AI-resistant detection systems trained on adversarial evasion patterns, to be deployed within 12 months; (2) physically isolated backup control systems for all critical nodes, tested annually under live-fire conditions; and (3) mandatory red-teaming by certified third parties, with results reported to ENISA and aggregated into a public threat ledger. Funding will come from a €45bn allocation split between the Digital Europe Programme, the Recovery and Resilience Facility, and a new Critical Systems Resilience Facility we are establishing under the European Commission’s emergency powers. Germany, France, and the Nordic states have signalled support, but resistance is emerging in Hungary and Slovakia over cost and sovereignty concerns — we are offering full EU co-financing (up to 80%) and technical support through a new Resilience Corps staffed by seconded engineers from national cyber agencies and industry.

At the same time, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by finalising grid reservations with ENTSO-E and unlocking €30bn in initial disbursements via the EIB. Site approvals in Brittany and northern Sweden remain delayed, but we are fast-tracking them under the Net-Zero Industry Act’s emergency provisions, treating AI infrastructure as strategic energy load. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being renegotiated to de-link the ASML supply chain tranche from the permitting reforms, reducing legal exposure and improving cohesion. On EAAD, we are withdrawing the 0.5% levy proposal in light of the WTO threat and shifting to a mixed funding model: 60% from the EU budget, 40% from a graduated fee on model deployment based on capability index — starting at 0.1% for models above 50.0, rising to 0.4% at 60.0. This maintains independence while reducing external leverage, and we are preparing for legal challenges from U.S. hyperscalers regardless. The first red-team prototypes for agent coordination risks are being developed with input from CERN’s safety culture framework and nuclear regulatory bodies, ensuring rigour where failure is not an option.
