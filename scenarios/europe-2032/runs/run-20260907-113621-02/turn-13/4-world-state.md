### The Fracture Deepens

The EU’s frontier access is abruptly severed. Hospitals lose real-time diagnostic AI, energy grids face instability, and ministries scramble as core systems go dark. The cause remains unexplained, and the duration is unknown—member states interpret the crisis as confirmation of central fragility. Five nations initiate talks on a data and compute pact outside EU structures, pooling resources under national sovereignty. Fragmentation looms, but formal agreements are not yet sealed.

### Emergency Response, Not Reform

With political capital critically low, the EU initiates an Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 122 TFEU, mobilising cyber-response teams from Frontex and ENISA into health and energy sectors. Implementation is partial: only six states commit immediately, while others delay pending national approvals or await terms from the emerging regional pact. Air-gapped controls and fallback protocols are in deployment, but full rollout will extend into the next period.

The Legitimacy Recovery Initiative is adjusted to focus on urgent resilience standards, repurposing co-design councils for rapid consultation. This is not a new measure, but a narrowing of scope within the existing mandate, consistent with the single-measure limit. The European Committee of the Regions facilitates early-stage intelligence sharing with the emerging regional group, though structural integration remains distant.

Their use in cyber and synthetic biology domains remains experimental and limited to small-scale demonstrations.
