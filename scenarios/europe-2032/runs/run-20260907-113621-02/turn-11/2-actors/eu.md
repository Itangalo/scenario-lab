### Statement changes  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we no longer assume that acting fast means acting alone.  
- Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** and **backlash_physical** events this turn confirm that speed without coordination is fragility. The Union’s attempt to act decisively through the Open Science Resilience Initiative has been undermined by member-state disbursement delays and legal fragmentation in data sharing. Meanwhile, the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event exposed that unilateral action—even when well-intentioned—cannot secure critical infrastructure when dependencies are systemic. We are still committed to acting before full clarity, but now recognise that in the terminal phase, acting *together* is the only way to act *at all*. Our credibility depends not just on resolve, but on cohesion.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Anchor sovereign capability in auditable AI, and extend it through binding cross-domain resilience integration  
- Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** demonstrated that offensive AI capabilities are now operationalised at scale, and the failure to achieve full telemetry sharing proves that technical sovereignty without institutional interoperability is hollow. The Open Science Resilience Initiative has produced valuable prototypes, but its impact is capped without integration into live response systems. The joint cyber-biosurveillance cells are now the front line: auditable AI must not only exist, but be *in the loop* when decisions are made. This is no longer about proving concept—it is about embedding control.

### New measure  
**Mandate cross-domain resilience integration for auditable AI systems**  
Require all high-consequence public systems (health, energy, transport) to integrate with the joint cyber-biosurveillance pact using auditable AI components, with compliance enforced through conditional funding and procurement locks.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +15, eu_political_capital +1 (if implementation holds), public_sentiment +2 (via visible system coherence)  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with binding effect on member states and EU agencies  

### Priority  
**Mandate cross-domain resilience integration for auditable AI systems** — because the **cyber_major_incident** proved that standalone defences are already obsolete, and the **backlash_physical** events show that public trust erodes when systems feel both opaque and uncontrollable; only a binding, visible integration of auditable AI into live crisis response can restore both security and legitimacy.

### In practice  
This turn, we are treating the conclusion of the **Secure foundational resilience through joint threat response integration** measure as a beginning, not an end. The co-located cells in Brussels and Stockholm are now operational, and secure pipelines link ENISA and ECDC to national CERTs and bio-labs—but data sharing remains partial, and decision authority is still fragmented. We are therefore launching a new binding directive under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market clause to mandate interoperability across health, energy, and transport sectors. The Commission will issue implementing acts requiring that any AI system used in critical infrastructure must either be integrated into the joint cyber-biosurveillance network or face defunding and procurement exclusion. This is not optional: the **cyber_major_incident** exploited precisely the gaps between domains, and only a unified, auditable layer can close them.

We are also adjusting our approach to member-state resistance. Germany and Italy have stalled disbursements to the Open Science Resilience Initiative, citing budget overruns and legal concerns over data sovereignty. In response, we are offering a new fiscal compact: conditional Eurobond rollovers tied to compliance with the integration mandate, paired with a fast-track legal opinion from the CJEU on data classification harmonisation. The goal is to convert resistance into co-ownership. At the same time, we are expanding outreach to regional governments and civil society through a new **Resilience Forum**, hosted by the European Committee of the Regions, to address the **backlash_physical** by demonstrating that AI systems can be both powerful and inspectable. The message is clear: we are not building faster—we are building *together*.
