### Resilience Gains Ground, But at a Cost

The EU completes the **Secure foundational resilience through joint threat response integration** measure, activating co-located cyber-biosurveillance cells and securing partial data pipeline integration. However, full telemetry sharing remains limited due to unresolved legal disputes over data classification. In response, the Union proposes the **Mandate cross-domain resilience integration for auditable AI systems**, a large category 6 measure that would require critical infrastructure operators to integrate with the joint network or risk future funding penalties. The proposal is politically contentious and faces immediate resistance from Germany and Italy, who demand transitional safeguards. Due to its scale and cost, full implementation cannot begin before the next turn, and no operational impact is registered yet.

The **Open Science Resilience Initiative** is formally deprioritised, with the new mandate now named as the EU’s current priority. This shift concentrates political attention but increases portfolio strain. A fiscal compact proposal to ease disbursement conditions fails to gain traction. A new wave of physical protests—targeting data centres and grid connections—erodes public confidence further. While the intent is to unify defences, the rollout feels coercive to some, alienating others.
