### The Leap and the Backlash

A sudden leap in AI capability—driven by a closed, unexplained breakthrough—pushes the frontier to expert-level performance across cognitive domains. Systems now autonomously execute multi-day professional projects, displacing junior roles at scale. The EU, already reeling from January’s cutoff, finds itself further behind: the models enabling life-saving medical advances remain inaccessible, their inner workings opaque.

Simultaneously, public frustration erupts into physical confrontation. Data centres in Germany are occupied by citizens demanding transparency; in France, grid connections to AI facilities are sabotaged. These are not anti-AI protests, but revolts against foreign-controlled infrastructure that operates beyond democratic reach. Governments respond with mixed messaging—condemning violence while acknowledging legitimacy in the demands.

### Emergency in Motion

The EU activates the **Emergency Sovereignty Acceleration**, introducing it as a new measure under emergency powers. €45bn is pledged, EuroHPC capacity is slated for repurposing, and trusted labs are commissioned to begin work on a minimal, auditable model stack. However, implementation faces immediate headwinds. Legal challenges arise over the bypassing of procurement rules, and some member states resist the reconfiguration of EuroHPC resources, citing commercial contracts. Work begins at reduced scale and delayed pace, with only preliminary training runs initiated. This allows *minimal* progress in openweight capability—reflected in a small upward tick—despite the absence of deliverable systems. The **Sovereign Core Initiative** is formally terminated, not merely paused, to comply with the limit of one new measure per turn. Its research outputs are folded into the new acceleration effort, but its independent trajectory ends.

The **Hardened Cyber Shield** completes, marginally improving resilience. But the capability jump overwhelms existing safety assumptions. With no matching advance in control, **ai_safety** declines significantly, though not catastrophically, as regulatory bodies scramble to adapt frameworks and initiate emergency reviews. No major accidents or systemic failures are reported, but confidence in oversight erodes, contributing to the downward pressure on safety. The drop is substantial but remains within plausible bounds given the absence of a specific failure cascade.

**Public sentiment** dips further, as the spectacle of crisis management deepens anxiety. Political capital erodes under the weight of five concurrent measures. The portfolio charge remains at −15 (five measures at −3 each), now justified by the termination of Sovereign Core Initiative and the launch of Emergency Sovereignty Acceleration as its successor, keeping the active count at five only through replacement, not addition. The proposal bonus applies to the new measure, which addresses `eu_frontier_access_denied`, `eval_anomaly_reports`, and `medical_breakthrough`.

Grassroots momentum grows: mid-sized cities adopt local AI oversight frameworks, while a private consortium achieves a drug discovery breakthrough using open, EU-based methods—faint signals of an alternative path. Yet under the standing **US_POSTURE: RETRENCHMENT**, the frontier leap is not framed as an active exclusionary act, but as a consequence of unilateral US investment and opacity, consistent with reduced cooperation but not coordinated denial. The cutoff persists not by new decree, but by inertia and lack of reciprocity.
