By turn 9, health ministries and grid operators must integrate fallback protocols using this stack, enforced through systemic risk provisions in the Digital Services Act.

At the same time, we are launching the Civic Co-Governance for Sovereign AI programme in parallel, recognising that without public legitimacy, even a technically flawless system will fail. Pilot boards are being convened in Berlin, Lyon, and Utrecht, combining citizens’ assemblies with technical auditors and ethicists, granted legal authority to inspect deployment logs and block integration into hospital triage, grid load-balancing, or benefits processing. These are not advisory panels — they have binding veto power until final certification by the newly expanded EAAD. This is how we close the loop: sovereignty is not just about running models on EU soil, but about ensuring those models answer to EU citizens. We are also publishing live infrastructure maps showing ownership, access rights, and energy sourcing for all major AI facilities — turning opacity into accountability. The goal is clear: by mid-2030, Europe will have not just a sovereign AI stack, but one that the public sees as theirs — because they helped build it, watch it, and can stop it.
