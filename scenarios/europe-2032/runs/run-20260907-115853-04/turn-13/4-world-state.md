### A Desperate Mobilisation

The EU declares cognitive sovereignty an existential imperative. The Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive is proposed under Article 122 TFEU, aiming to fast-track sovereign exaflop-scale compute across Finland, southern France, and eastern Poland. It overrides national permitting, grants priority grid access, and mobilises €12 billion in emergency funding. Germany is offered audit access in exchange for compliance; Hungary’s cohesion funds remain tied to NIS2 cooperation.

Despite urgency, political capital is exhausted. The Council delays the vote to January 2033, citing legal concerns over environmental waivers and workforce visas. Southern France’s regional government blocks construction pending an environmental review, and Poland’s energy minister warns the grid cannot support new loads without blackouts. The directive remains in legal limbo.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Inference Backbone completes, hosting replicated models up to capability 72.0 under EU control. It absorbs traffic from US-cut-off hospitals and ministries via fallback routing through Marseille and Helsinki, but operates at reduced fidelity. Recruitment for the Autonomous Threat Detection and Response Corps has not yet begun; planning continues in Tallinn under minimal staffing, pending political clearance and budget allocation.

Public anger persists. Sabotage strikes a transformer in Slovakia feeding a planned compute site. Media frames the mobilisation as “AI conscription” — a desperate gamble that deepens dependency before it breaks it.
