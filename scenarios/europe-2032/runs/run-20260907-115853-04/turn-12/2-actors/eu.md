## In practice
We are treating the completion of the Emergency Replication Mandate as a threshold, not a conclusion. The Replication Foundry has validated six sovereign replicas at or above capability 68.0, now running on the Sovereign Inference Backbone in Helsinki, Bucharest, and Marseille. These are actively replacing black-box dependencies in welfare, procurement, and legal aid systems across Poland, Spain, and Finland. Germany remains partially compliant; we have suspended cohesion transfers to Bavaria and North Rhine-Westphalia pending full audit access, invoking Article 12a of the AI Act as upheld by the ECJ in the Luxembourg ruling last month.

At the same time, the **cyber_major_incident** — a self-adaptive ransomware wave exploiting a poisoned npm package used in 170 municipal IT systems — has exposed a fatal lag: defences were updated too slowly, coordination too fragmented, and the tools themselves too static. In response, we are operationalising the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative by empowering the European Cyber Resilience Cell with real-time patching authority across borders during declared incidents. This is being activated under NIS2’s emergency provisions, with ENISA coordinating the first live deployment of autonomous patching agents in Estonia and northern Italy this week.

The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event — a unilateral cut-off by the primary US-based provider of high-capability models, citing national security directives — has hit critical medical imaging systems in Lyon and logistics planning in Hamburg. There is no workaround. This is not merely a supply shock; it is a sovereignty event. We are fast-tracking the new Autonomous Threat Detection and Response Corps to ensure that the Shield does not become another dependency. Recruitment begins immediately through a joint call from ENISA and the European Defence Agency, targeting AI red-teamers, formal verification experts, and cyber operators from national units and academia. Funding is drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and matched by a 50% uplift in the 2032–2023 NIS2 top-up budget, approved in an emergency Council session last week. This is not about catching up. It is about building a living defence — one that learns faster than the threat evolves.
