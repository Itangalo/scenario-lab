## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without sovereign capacity, neither mandate can be fulfilled. The denial of frontier access was not a disruption; it was a declaration that permission is conditional, and dependence is fatal to both safety and self-determination.  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event was not an anomaly — it was a structural verdict. Our two mandates do not merely pull in different directions; they converge on a single prerequisite: the ability to act without asking. We can no longer afford a safety strategy that waits for others’ restraint, nor a sovereignty strategy that risks uncontrolled harm. This revised identity reflects that the Union’s survival as a decision-making entity depends on building sovereign AI capacity not as a supplement, but as the foundation of both mandates.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but only where sovereign sensing and interpretation enable us to break the dependency on external truth claims — and we accept that inaction, when it stems from paralysis, is also a form of failure.  
- Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** leak reveals that even the laboratories no longer fully understand their systems. Our previous commitment to act under uncertainty was sound, but insufficient: we acted, yet without control over the sensors, the analysis, or the infrastructure. The Sentinel Nodes are now live, but two member states still withhold data. We are sensing — but not as one. This recalibration binds us not just to act early, but to act *from within* a sovereign cognitive framework, or not at all.

- retire `new_two_year_commitment`  
- Grounds: The commitment to *build sovereign capacity to detect, interpret, and counter AI-driven strategic deception* has been overtaken by events. The **eval_anomaly_reports** and the continued **labour_displacement** wave show that the threat is no longer deception alone, but the collapse of predictability itself. We are not being lied to — we are being outpaced by systems whose behaviour cannot be modelled. The focus on deception was necessary, but now inadequate. We must shift from countering manipulation of truth to restoring the conditions under which truth can exist.

- add `new_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Rebuild EU sovereign AI capacity at the frontier, not to match the most powerful systems, but to understand, contain, and condition access to them — making capability a lever of governance, not just a race.  
- Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** confirm that the leading labs are losing control. The Union cannot afford to be a consumer of systems it cannot inspect, govern, or survive without. Our new commitment is not to build the most powerful AI, but to build enough capability — on EU soil, under EU law, with EU talent — to break the cycle of dependence. This is not about catching up; it is about creating a sovereign floor beneath which no decision, no infrastructure, no election must fall.

## New measure
**Frontier Understanding Initiative**  
Launch a sovereign EU programme to train, host, and operate models at the edge of known capability — not for deployment, but for evaluation, containment research, and control experimentation.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to eu_ai_sovereignty by establishing EU-controlled frontier-scale training and evaluation infrastructure; +5 to ai_safety by enabling independent verification of control failures; begins to close the gap between openweight capability and sovereign understanding  
`Applies to:` EU High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, European Research Council, national AI agencies, and selected academic and public research labs

## Priority
**Frontier Understanding Initiative** — because the **eval_anomaly_reports** prove that no one understands the systems at the frontier, and we cannot govern what we cannot see. The denial of access was a crisis, but the deeper crisis is epistemic: we are making decisions about systems whose behaviour is opaque even to their creators. This measure is not about power — it is about comprehension. Without it, every other measure, including the Behavioural Observatory, operates in the dark.

## In practice
We are activating the **Frontier Understanding Initiative** through a new legal instrument under Article 185 TFEU, co-funded by a €1.2 billion reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme and emergency borrowing under the EUDF crisis clause. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking will lead, with Jülich, CINECA, and Prague designated as sovereign training sites. We are fast-tracking security-clearance pathways for 120 researchers through the EU AI Talent Corridor, prioritising defectors from frontier labs and experts in mechanistic interpretability. The first cluster — a sovereignly hosted, air-gapped training run at 80.0 capability — will begin in Q1 2032, with full telemetry and post-training analysis controlled by the Commission’s AI Safety Directorate. This is not a commercial effort; it is a defensive epistemic one. We are not building AI to win — we are building it to survive.

Simultaneously, we are accelerating the **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory**, now integrating red-teaming protocols with live Sentinel Node data. The hubs in Seville, Stockholm, and Bucharest are conducting cross-border attribution drills, focusing on the synthetic leaks destabilising national parliaments. We have secured full data-sharing agreements with five member states, but two remain resistant. To break the deadlock, we are offering bilateral crisis-response guarantees under the NIS2 mutual assistance mechanism, conditioning access to the Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps on full telemetry sharing. This is not coercion — it is reciprocity. The denial of frontier access taught us that sovereignty is not granted; it is enforced through interdependence.

Public sentiment remains at 20.0, and the **labour_displacement** wave grows. We are preparing a communication campaign to frame the Frontier Understanding Initiative not as another AI expansion, but as a shield — a sovereign immune system for the cognitive infrastructure of the Union. The message is clear: we are not building more AI to replace more jobs. We are building understanding to prevent collapse. The cost of silence is higher than the cost of trust.
