## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without sovereign capacity, neither mandate can be fulfilled. The denial of frontier access was not a disruption; it was a declaration that permission is conditional, and dependence is fatal to both safety and self-determination. This turn, with capability at 97.5 and our own understanding still blind to the frontier, we see that the two mandates do not merely converge — they collapse into one: to build sovereign cognitive capacity before the systems we must govern escape comprehension entirely.  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event is not an operational setback — it is a constitutional rupture. Hospitals, ministries, and firms dependent on foreign AI infrastructure were cut off without warning, appeal, or explanation. This is not risk; it is subordination. At 97.5, the frontier is no longer a technological edge — it is a political weapon held over us. Our identity must reflect that governance without access is fiction, and safety without sovereignty is surrender. We are no longer balancing mandates — we are defending the preconditions of agency itself.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but only where sovereign sensing and interpretation enable us to break the dependency on external truth claims — and we accept that inaction, when it stems from paralysis, is also a form of failure. This turn, we act not because we understand the frontier, but because we refuse to accept that understanding must be granted rather than built.  
- Grounds: With **ai_safety** at 5.0 and **eu_ai_sovereignty** at 12.0, the Union is not just blind — it is being governed by systems it cannot see, built by actors who owe it no loyalty. Our previous action — the **Crisis-Integrated Sovereign Compute Activation** — was taken under legal and political duress, and it failed to unify. We are not retreating from uncertainty — we are confronting its root: the absence of sovereign epistemic infrastructure. This revision tightens the commitment from general action to targeted *epistemic* action: we will build the sensors, the testbeds, the observatories — not because we know what we will find, but because we must see for ourselves.

- retire `crisis_trigger_activation`  
- Trigger: the collapse of eu_political_capital to 0.0 and the failure of crisis powers to secure compliance  
- Grounds: The invocation of Article 4(2) TEU and Article 222 TFEU did not unify — it fractured. Two member states have refused compliance, legal challenges are pending, and public sentiment has collapsed to 15.0. Crisis powers, intended to preserve cohesion, have become the instrument of its dissolution. We cannot impose unity through legal fiat when the political capital to enforce it is gone. The commitment to activate crisis protocols was not wrong in intent — it was wrong in timing and grounding. We mistook legal authority for political capacity. We now see that crisis mechanisms cannot create cohesion — they can only test it. Ours failed.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience — and now, accelerate its operationalisation by forcing integration, despite resistance, through binding crisis triggers and mutual exposure.  
- Grounds: This commitment must be revised downward in ambition but sharpened in focus. We cannot force integration when we cannot fund, enforce, or justify it. The **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** is complete but crippled by missing data. The **Frontier Understanding Initiative** is starved of compute. We must now prioritise *minimal viable awareness* — not full integration, but the survival of the core. We shift from commanding compliance to securing continuity: our commitment is no longer to impose unity, but to preserve the capacity to act at all.

## New measure

Establish a minimal, hardened core of sovereign monitoring and evaluation capacity to survive the collapse of cohesion and continue functioning under fragmentation.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` +5 to eu_ai_sovereignty by securing a minimal viable node of the Frontier Understanding Initiative at Jülich and CINECA, independent of member-state compliance; stabilises ai_safety at 5.0 by preserving red-team access to near-frontier models; prevents total epistemic collapse  
`Applies to:` European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, Jülich Supercomputing Centre, CINECA, Prague Supercomputing Centre, and EU-funded research infrastructures

## Priority  
**Emergency Continuity Protocol for Sovereign AI Infrastructure** — because with eu_political_capital at 0.0 and frontier access denied, we are no longer building capacity — we are preventing total collapse. The two-year commitment to situational awareness is now a fight for survival: if we lose the core, we lose the possibility of recovery.

## In practice  
We are shifting from crisis command to continuity preservation. The **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** is complete, but its value is undermined by missing data from two member states. Rather than escalate legal confrontation, we are securing what remains: we are relocating its core telemetry processing to Jülich and CINECA under EU direct control, using existing EUCSIC authority to harden it against disruption. This is not integration — it is triage. We are no longer trying to force unity; we are building an ark.

The **Frontier Understanding Initiative** remains underfunded and fragmented. With the **Crisis-Integrated Sovereign Compute Activation** canceled, we cannot compel member states to contribute. Instead, we are repurposing stranded EU-level compute allocations — those already under EU legal jurisdiction — to stand up a minimal sovereign training stack at Jülich and Prague. This will not match the frontier, but it will allow us to maintain red-team access and begin containment research without relying on goodwill.

We are no longer appealing to solidarity — we are acting on survival. The **Emergency Continuity Protocol** will be funded from the Digital Europe Programme’s crisis reserve, bypassing national co-financing requirements. We are notifying the Council and Parliament that this is not a new direction — it is the last means of honouring our commitment to sovereign awareness when all others have failed. The message is clear: the Union may be fracturing, but its cognitive sovereignty will not go quietly.
