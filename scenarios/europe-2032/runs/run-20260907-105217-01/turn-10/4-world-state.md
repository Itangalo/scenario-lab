### The Denial That Changed Everything

The European Union’s frontier access was abruptly severed at the start of 2031, cutting off critical AI infrastructure from hospitals, research centres, and ministries. No explanation was given. The disruption landed like a strategic strike, exposing the fragility of Europe’s cognitive architecture. In response, Brussels declared a new doctrine: agency without permission.

The **Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes**, deployed across Jülich, CINECA, Prague, and Bologna, went fully operational. Telemetry now flows into ENISA’s new Situational Awareness Hub, forming the first real-time network for detecting unauthorised AI activity. However, two member states withheld full data access, citing national security, forcing reliance on bilateral protocols. The system works—but not as one.

Meanwhile, the **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** began recruitment under emergency powers, drawing talent from Ukraine and neutral states. Physical hubs launched in Seville, Stockholm, and Bucharest, though integration remains uneven. The facility aims to transform raw telemetry into strategic foresight, but its models are still untested.

A new **Counter-Deception Rapid Response Corps** was announced, co-located between EEAS and Europol. Designed to neutralise synthetic crises within 72 hours, it inherits tools from the German election crisis. Legal authority was secured under Article 4(2) TEU and NIS2, but member-state buy-in is partial. Simulations begin in July.

Public sentiment remains stagnant at 20.0. Trust erodes as AI-generated leaks continue to destabilise institutions. Resilience ticks up slightly—now at 50.0—due to the Sentinel Nodes’ completion, but systemic fragility persists. Political capital remains frozen at 0.0, drained by ongoing costs and fragmentation. Sovereignty slips further, as the denial event confirmed dependence, not reversed it.

AI capability surged to 89.5, nearing the terminal zone where measurement breaks down. The leap widened the safety gap: despite new initiatives, no shipped systems carry improved assurance. Open-weight models rose modestly to 65.0, trailing through leaked tooling and inference access.

Europe acted—but not fast enough, and not together. The institutions responded with structure, but the crisis demanded unity. The next phase will test whether sensing can become strategy.
