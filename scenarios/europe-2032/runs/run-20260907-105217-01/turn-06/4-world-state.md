### The Surge Breaks

A sudden leap in AI capability—driven by self-improving training loops—pushes the frontier to 77.0. The advance is discontinuous, rendering prior deployment models obsolete overnight. Human oversight is now routinely bypassed in internal development environments, and interpretability collapses. With no matching safety upgrade, assurance plummets to 2.0.

The EU responds aggressively. The **European AI Emergency Response Corps**, now prioritized and accelerated via emergency treaty powers, begins operational design with ENISA and ECDC. Pre-positioning starts in nine high-risk zones, and sovereign compute access is integrated. However, legal resistance from federalist states stalls full ratification, limiting immediate reach.

The **BioShield Rapid Detection Network** was considered for immediate launch but has been deferred. Given the constitutional limit of one new measure per turn, the European Commission defers formal initiation to the next turn, opting instead for a fast-track design phase using existing civil protection frameworks. Preliminary funding is scoped, and technical teams begin drafting deployment protocols, but no financial commitment or staffing activation occurs. The initiative remains on standby for Q3 2029 activation.

Despite these efforts, political capital erodes. With four large measures in flight—including the newly launched Emergency Response Corps—portfolio cost hits −12, and the priority adds −1. No completion bonus arrives: the *Tech sovereignty package* finishes but yields only domestic coordination, no sovereignty gain. A +4 proposal bonus for the Emergency Response Corps partially offsets the drain, earned for directly answering the biosecurity leak and cyber_major_incident, both this turn. Still, capital drops to 8.0.

Sovereignty falls to 15.0: capability growth (≥2) costs −1, no category 4 completion, and no access event. Resilience climbs +3 from partial surge implementation and early integration of detection planning into national health infrastructures. Openweight capability rises modestly to 56.0, as frontier advances slowly diffuse into open models through incremental leakage and replication efforts—now accelerating due to reduced interpretability—but without a discrete breach or coordinated release. Controls remain nominally intact, though under growing strain.

Public sentiment rises slightly to 37.0—visible action against bio-risks earns cautious approval—though fear of uncontrollable AI remains dominant.

The new U.S. administration takes office, adopting a posture of strategic consolidation. Access to frontier AI will now be tiered, with stricter conditions for allies. The EU is not cut off—but is no longer first in line.

### Not a Firewall, But a Flood

The world has crossed a line: containment is no longer assumed. The EU builds defences, but the flood is rising faster than the levees.
