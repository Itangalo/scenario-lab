## New measure
**EU Critical Infrastructure Cyber-Hardening Directive**
Mandates segmented OT/IT hardening, continuous threat-hunting and exercised black-start contingency plans across electricity transmission, ports and water utilities.
This is category 6 Preparedness and resilience, and it must come now because the autumn test-shot proved segmentation failed and detection would not have caught a swarm derived from open Mythos-class models, opening a three-turn cyber window we cannot meet with existing uneven defences.

## Priority
EU Critical Infrastructure Cyber-Hardening Directive, because the precursor gate is open for three turns and grids in two member states were already mapped while Gigafactories cannot protect anything for years.

## In practice
We are pushing the hardening directive through DG ENER and DG CNECT with ENISA as operator, using the CER and NIS2 legal base for mandatory OT segmentation audits and EU-CyCLONe exercises, funded by repurposed RRF and CEF-Digital money to get TSOs and port authorities hunters on-site this winter. France, Germany and Poland want exemptions for municipal utilities; we buy them with co-financing and a mutual-aid pool.

In parallel we keep the two inherited sovereignty lines alive: InvestAI Gigafactories (M1) moves to site selection and grid-connection offers in the accelerated-permitting zones, and the tech sovereignty package (M2) stays in private-capital mobilisation, both slow by construction but now framed as the autonomy half of the same commitment. We signal to Washington and to ASML that resilience procurement will favour EU-anchored compute where possible, without opening a second compute programme.
