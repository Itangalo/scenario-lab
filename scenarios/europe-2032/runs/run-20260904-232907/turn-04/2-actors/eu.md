## New measure  
**Labour Disruption Review and Adaptive Governance Framework**  
Establish a binding, time-limited review process to assess public-sector automation pilots, define red lines for civil service displacement, and co-design transition protocols with unions and member states.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Stabilise public sentiment by +5 to +10; reduce backlash risk from emergent_labour_disruption; begin institutionalising adaptive governance for AI-driven workforce transitions  
`Applies to:` EU institutions and all member states implementing public-sector AI automation

## In practice  
We are acting on the understanding that sovereignty is not only about compute, and resilience not only about cyber. The legitimacy of the entire project rests on whether people believe AI serves them — or is being used to displace them. The strikes in border control and tax administration are not isolated; they are the first organised response to a pattern that will only grow. We cannot pause modernisation, but we cannot proceed without consent. This measure is not a moratorium — it is a circuit breaker with a timer. Under Article 152 of the TFEU and in coordination with the European Labour Authority, we are launching a tripartite review — Commission, ETUC, and national ministries — to audit all active public-sector automation pilots by turn 5. The output will be a binding framework: criteria under which AI may replace or reconfigure civil service roles, mandatory consultation timelines, and a transition protocol modelled on flexicurity principles — wage insurance, retraining pathways, and mobility support for displaced workers.

The framework will be adopted via comitology by turn 6, with opt-out only for member states willing to forgo access to the Transition Shield fund tied to the Resilience Surge. This creates leverage: compliance is not free, but resistance comes at a cost. ENISA and Eurofound will jointly assess each pilot’s transparency, impact on workload, and recourse mechanisms, publishing findings in real time to rebuild public trust. We are pairing this with a targeted communication campaign — funded from existing Digital Europe allocations — to clarify where AI augments rather than replaces, and to spotlight early wins in caseworker productivity and citizen wait times. The goal is not to stop change, but to prove it can be governed — and to show that when disruption happens, the Union has more than just infrastructure to offer.
