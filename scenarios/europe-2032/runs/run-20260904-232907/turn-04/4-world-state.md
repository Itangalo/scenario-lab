### Frontier Creeps Forward  
Despite stagnation fears, AI capability advances by 1.0 to 56.5, driven by algorithmic refinements and distributed research agents. Openweight models benefit, particularly in offensive cyber and automation scripting, pushing `openweight_capability` to 44.0—halfway between its prior value and the closed frontier.

### Sovereignty Erodes Despite Infrastructure Gains  
The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive completes, reserving 30% of frontier compute for public use—a symbolic win. However, actual access remains constrained by foreign-controlled supply chains. Gigafactory progress yields +1, but rising capability costs −1, and the lack of substitution for imported training runs leaves the Union exposed. With no event securing access, `eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 19.0.

### Political Capital Bleeds Further  
The portfolio charge remains heavy: −3 each for Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty, and Resilience Surge, plus −2 for the new Labour Disruption Review and −1 for prioritising it—totaling −11. No proposal bonus applies: the labour disruption is emergent, not yet a full incident. `eu_political_capital` drops to 22.0, tightening the EU’s operational bandwidth.

### Safety and Resilience Slip  
With no safety measure landing, and capability rising, `ai_safety` declines to 24.0. The Resilience Surge continues, but no benefit is realized yet. Labour unrest grows, and with no immediate fix, `resilience` dips to 37.0 as institutional strain mounts.

### A Circuit Breaker, Not a Fix  
The Labour Disruption Review launches amid strikes in border and tax agencies. The tripartite audit begins, but early signals are mixed: some member states resist comitology pressure, and the Transition Shield leverage proves thinner than expected. Public sentiment holds at 38.0—neither worsening nor improving—as the communication campaign struggles to counter union narratives.
