## In practice  
We are acting on the understanding that sovereignty without resilience is a castle on sand. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive has passed trilogue and will be published in the Official Journal this quarter. The 30% reservation is now law, sunset-linked to ai_safety reaching 55.0, and the EDIC allocation algorithm has been published with audit trails to ensure transparency. Bilateral assurances to Germany and Ireland have held, and France has withdrawn its blocking minority in exchange for a commitment to review access parity under the next European Cyber Shield proposal. The directive is done. We now turn that focus — and the 3 units of political capital it frees — to the urgent task of absorption.

The Resilience Surge is not optional adaptation. It is a binding directive under NIS2, activated via the emergency clause in Article 21a, requiring all operators of essential services to deploy AI-augmented intrusion detection systems, air-gapped fail-safes for control systems, and quarterly red-teaming across sectoral boundaries by turn 5. ENISA will issue technical standards by turn 4 and conduct audits with powers to suspend market access for non-compliant operators. Funding of €12bn is drawn from reprogrammed Civil Protection Mechanism reserves, with co-financing required from member states to ensure ownership. We are fast-tracking procurement waivers under the Crisis Regulation to bypass delays, and launching a pan-European tender for detection stack interoperability.

Resistance is expected from smaller member states and municipal utilities, who cite cost and capacity. To counter this, we are pairing the mandate with a €4bn Transition Shield — drawn from unused InvestEU allocations — to subsidise 70% of compliance costs for operators in states below the EU median in digital readiness. We are also launching a cross-border mutual aid protocol, so that Luxembourg can draw on Czech cyber responders and Portugal on Finnish bio-detection labs. This is not just about surviving an attack — it is about proving that the Union can act as a single system under pressure. Because if we cannot absorb harm when it comes, no amount of sovereign compute will save us.
