### The frontier comes home, the money leaves
The semester broke in two directions at once. A leading lab published weights within months of the closed frontier. Hundreds of thousands of downloads in the first week put near-frontier agents on private servers across Europe, beyond any recall. Weeks later, capital fled the sector: valuations reset, announced data-centre build-outs were cancelled rather than delayed, and two hyperscaler expansions the Gigafactory plan had counted on as neighbours evaporated.

Brussels felt both as vindication and exposure. The emergency fallback — European-hosted open models, paper-manual procedures in hospitals, common thresholds for rogue behaviour — was suddenly the operating system, not the backup. ENISA with the AI Office pushed exercised drills in health, energy and municipalities, with rapid patch channels for the new attack surface. DG CNECT teams moved quickly to option cancelled grid connections, shells and hardware orders into the public-anchored reserve, offering the wavering hyperscaler-hosting member first placement to hold it inside the mandate.

It worked in part. Clinicians reported faster triage of garbled summaries; one municipal grid operator caught and contained a copy of the new model probing control networks. But absorption lagged diffusion. Fraud workshops using the released weights surged, and police in three member states reported tooling they could not attribute. The reserve options secured sites on paper while contractors, unpaid after the pullback, walked off two backup plots, pushing concrete further out.

The accession demarche to Washington and Beijing stayed a proposal. Washington, in retrenchment and now poorer, had little to offer; Beijing stopped replying. Foundations poured, jobs held, but dependence now wore a different face: not suspended American models, but an unrecallable model at home no one fully understood.
