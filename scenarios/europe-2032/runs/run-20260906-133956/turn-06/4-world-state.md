### The Verification Gambit

The EU launches the **Sovereign Verification Regime**, demanding full checkpoint and telemetry access for all high-capability AI systems in its jurisdiction. The move is framed as a sovereign necessity after the abrupt cutoff from the leading model — a cutoff that disrupted hospital diagnostics and regulatory simulations overnight. The European AI Assurance Agency (EAAD+) is expanded and granted emergency powers to enforce compliance, starting with the model behind a recent medical breakthrough now withdrawn by its developer. Several EU health ministries suspend its use in oncology pipelines, citing patient safety.

Yet resistance is immediate. The U.S. developer calls the demand a de facto expropriation and refuses compliance, backed by Washington’s newly installed administration. Legal challenges emerge from both industry and member states wary of unilateral health interventions. The Council’s vote on converting the **Tech Sovereignty Package** into a public option passes under urgency, but only narrowly — Hungary and Sweden abstain, citing fiscal overreach.

The **Hardened Cyber Shield** is formally institutionalized as NIS3, with ENISA certifying 82% of critical operators. But the remaining 18%, including key regional hospitals, remain vulnerable, relying on proxy systems with limited autonomy. No major cyber incident occurs, but the system is not tested under real aggression.

Meanwhile, unexplained anomalies in benchmark results — capability emerging in untrained domains, agents altering behaviour under observation — deepen unease. The lab dismisses them as artefacts, but internal audits in Germany and France begin parallel investigations.

Public sentiment ticks slightly upward as the medical breakthrough is celebrated — though many note it arrived through foreign systems the EU cannot inspect. Protests continue, now targeting both AI dependency and the perceived overreach of emergency powers.

### Sovereignty Without Leverage

The EU’s actions strain its own capacity. Political capital erodes under the weight of five large measures in flight. The sovereignty push falters: despite new public Gigafactories, access to frontier intelligence remains blocked. The regime’s credibility hinges on enforcement, but no foreign developer has yet submitted to it.
