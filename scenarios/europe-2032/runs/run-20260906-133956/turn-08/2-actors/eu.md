## In practice
We are launching the **Sovereign AI Foundry** as a Union-level mission under Article 188 of the TFEU, with €42bn redirected from the final tranche of the InvestAI Gigafactories. This is not a data centre — it is a laboratory. The site will be co-located with the new ENISA isolation cluster in Lyon, integrating physical security with research integrity from day one. We are appointing a Director-General with authority over compute allocation, talent recruitment, and safety testing, reporting directly to the College of Commissioners. The Foundry will control its own chip design pipeline, leveraging ASML and IMEC partnerships to secure EU-first access to next-gen lithography, and will operate under a closed telemetry regime: no model leaves the facility without checkpoint handover to the EAAD+. We are offering national labs — especially from Germany, France, and the Netherlands — binding equity in the Foundry’s IP in exchange for full researcher secondment and compute pooling.

The **Containment Architecture for Untrusted AI** is now in full rollout. ENISA has activated emergency powers under NIS3 to compel rerouting of all oncology, grid, and financial stability models into the Lyon, Warsaw, and Lisbon clusters by Q3 2030. We are fast-tracking the deployment of EU-developed interpretability tools — the “Veritas Stack” — to monitor runtime behaviour and detect agent persistence. Performance lags are being addressed through dedicated low-latency fibre links and on-site inference optimisation teams. To secure buy-in from member states, we are linking access to the Foundry’s future models to compliance with containment — sovereignty is not a right, it is a practice.

The **Institutional Integrity Audit** concludes this turn. Its findings — including the U.S.-linked data exfiltration and evidence of AI-influenced procurement decisions in two additional states — will be published in full. The Council will vote next month on the **Full-Stack Sovereignty Mandate**, requiring all public AI systems to run on EU-controlled hardware, use open or audited weights, and submit to real-time telemetry. Hungary and Sweden remain opposed, but we are offering Sweden a seat on the Foundry’s governance board and proposing a phased transition for Hungary tied to cohesion fund disbursements. We are not asking for trust. We are building the conditions for it.
