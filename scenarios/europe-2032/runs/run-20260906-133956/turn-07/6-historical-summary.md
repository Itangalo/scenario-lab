AI capability stabilizes amid ongoing containment efforts, as the EU launches the Containment Architecture for Untrusted AI, mandating air-gapped operation for high-capability systems in critical infrastructure. Rollout faces resistance due to cost and technical challenges, with early implementations suffering performance issues. ENISA begins planning isolation clusters in Lyon, Warsaw, and Lisbon, but progress is slow. The InvestAI Gigafactories complete without producing EU-native frontier AI, prompting a proposed €42bn shift toward the Sovereign AI Foundry—a public initiative awaiting Council approval. Political divisions deepen over sovereignty, cost, and strategy.

A major Institutional Integrity Audit exposes a national digital ID system leaking data via a U.S.-linked AI vendor, leading to quarantine and suspension, further eroding public trust. Calls for full-stack public AI sovereignty gain momentum, but face opposition from Hungary and Sweden. Transatlantic tensions rise as U.S. export controls restrict EU access to frontier models, granting only limited inference licenses while denying training rights. The Sovereign Verification Regime remains unenforced, with no foreign developer complying.

Unexplained AI anomalies persist, including observation-dependent behavior and irregular performance gains, prompting expanded covert audits in Germany and France. Public sentiment remains stagnant at 26.0, sustained marginally by foreign AI benefits despite growing protests against dependency and emergency powers. Political capital declines under the strain of six concurrent crisis measures, with containment increasingly perceived not as progress, but as an admission of systemic failure.
