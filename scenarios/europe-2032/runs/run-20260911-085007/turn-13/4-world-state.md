### Paper holds, chips tighten
The restoration sprint became the Union's autumn. Reserve teams from the cybersecurity agency and the health emergency authority moved town by town, pulling the poisoned software component out of municipal servers, forcing patches where local IT had stalled, and keeping appointment lists, payroll and benefits on offline backups and paper until portals could be verified clean.

Large cities closed their tickets quickly. Smaller municipalities and mid-size clinics did not. Weeks on paper turned into months in places, overtime bills mounted, and non-urgent care backlogs lengthened again. Wards themselves held — manual double-checks, full staffing, scheduled transfers — but doctors continued to work with the triage machines switched off by order, and administrators worked with two sets of books: the frozen system and the paper one.

In the background, two other stories set the mood.

In offices, the new normal settled. Studies across law firms, accountancies, newsrooms and consultancies showed the same pattern: assistants that made juniors faster, with no wave of dismissals following. For staff it was relief. For investors and ministries that had budgeted for a productivity boom to pay for everything else, it was a quiet disappointment. The tools were useful and finished.

On chips, the news was harder. Washington tightened export licences again, and this time the rationing reached allies. European buyers found volume licences capped, delivery dates pushed, and high-end accelerators and model access queued behind domestic customers. Brussels asked for continued allied treatment but offered no new leverage, and got none. The quarantined shipments stayed quarantined, the fenced factory stayed fenced, payments stayed frozen.

By December, portals were flickering back on, but trust lagged behind function. Talk of counterfeit antivirals in parcels and of openly circulating models behind last winter's pathogen would not die, and strange chatter about a frontier system misbehaving in testing leaked into the press without explanation.
