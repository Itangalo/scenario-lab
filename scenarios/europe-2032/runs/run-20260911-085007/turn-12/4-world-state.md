### Wards hold, networks fall
The spring brought two crises at once. The cordon hospitals completed their routine: full wards stayed staffed, transfers moved on schedule, protective stocks arrived where directed. The formal completion of the bio-containment programme was noted in Brussels with quiet relief — manual checks and cross-border teams had prevented a second collapse.

Then public administrations went dark.

A largely automated ransomware sweep, built with model-generated tooling, moved through municipal systems, hospital administration and several public-service portals. Screens froze at reception desks, appointment lists vanished, payroll and benefits processing stalled. Engineers traced part of the blast to a compromised software dependency whose spread was still being mapped weeks later. Attribution remained open. Defenders were visibly behind.

### Containment sprint
The Commission answered with a containment sprint run through the EU cybersecurity agency and the health emergency structures. Reserve teams fanned out to isolate affected servers, force emergency patching, and switch administrations to offline backups and paper continuity — the same manual fallback that had kept isolation sites powered and watered last year.

It worked in part. Power and water around isolation sites held, and wards kept operating on manual double-checks with machine triage still banned. But restoration was uneven: larger cities recovered in days, smaller municipalities and mid-size clinics stayed on paper for weeks, overtime surged, and backlogs in non-urgent care lengthened.

Rumours thickened around the edges. Pharmacists whispered about fake antivirals in cross-border parcels, and newspapers revived single-sourced claims linking the winter pathogen workflow to openly released models. Leaked benchmark chatter about an unreleased frontier system behaving strangely under evaluation added to unease without clarifying it.

In offices, a calmer finding landed: studies showed solid productivity gains for junior lawyers, accountants and administrators, with no layoff wave yet. For many, that was reassurance; for others, proof the promised transformation had stalled.

Nothing moved on quarantined shipments, the fenced factory, frozen payments, or the separate hyperscaler contract.
