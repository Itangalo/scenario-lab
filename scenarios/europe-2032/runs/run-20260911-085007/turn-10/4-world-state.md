### Containment weeks
Winter brought a deliberate release of a modified pathogen, confirmed within days to have been designed with help from openly available models. Casualties mounted, hospitals already on paper procedures went to full isolation, and cross-border transfers began under emergency health powers. Containment ran for weeks, with tracing done by hand and phone because automated triage could no longer be trusted.

At the same time the leading laboratories announced that their newest systems no longer reasoned in readable language. The internal steps that auditors and safety teams had relied on to check behaviour had been replaced by representations no human could follow. Announced as a performance gain, it was received in Brussels as erosion of the last oversight tool that still worked.

### Cordons, not cures
The Commission began standing up a cordon response: detection teams, isolation wards, stockpiled protective equipment and antivirals, sequencing in secure labs, and requests to move staff across borders to relieve regions hit by walkouts. Deployment was partial and delayed. With the Care Continuity priority suspended and capital below 20, there was no staffed reserve to activate; wards were ordered to stay on manual double-checks, explicitly without machine triage, which added overtime and backlogs. Ministers stopped talking about waiting-list gains and fronted the isolation centres instead.

Patch teams kept working municipal systems in the background, but attention and money went to beds, barriers and overtime. The hardening work from last year kept power and water from cascading, which mattered once isolation wards depended on them.

Supply politics did not move. Shipments from Taipei stayed quarantined, the backup factory site stayed fenced, payments stayed frozen. The member state that had signed its own hyperscaler deal kept it, offered reimbursement if it routed compute through logged European infrastructure, and declined. The joint position on lithography equipment remained on paper.

The public saw fear first and competence second. Outlets linked the release directly to widely available AI, fraud and outage stories merged into a single dread, and relief at hospitals holding open barely registered. The Union ended the spring strained, holding partial cordons while the models it was meant to oversee became harder to read.
