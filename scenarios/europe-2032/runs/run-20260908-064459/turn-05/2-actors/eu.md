## In practice
We are treating the election_alliance event as both an opening and a trap: the U.S. offer of structured access is real, but its price — alignment on export controls, standards, and third-country dealings — risks hollowing out our autonomy just as we begin to build it. Our response is not to reject access, but to condition it. Through the newly activated EU-U.S. AI Dialogue, we are negotiating a tiered access agreement that allows EU institutions and cleared agencies to run sovereign workloads on frontier models, provided they are executed within EU-controlled infrastructure and subject to independent pre-deployment evaluation by ENISA and the European AI Office. This is not about hosting foreign models — it is about securing execution rights on them, with audit trails, kill switches, and data sovereignty guarantees baked in. We are offering alignment on incident reporting and red-teaming standards in return, but drawing a line at ceding control over which capabilities enter our critical systems.

Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactories are now the centrepiece of our sovereignty strategy. With ASML off the table, we are redirecting political capital toward accelerating site activation in Finland and Spain. The Commission is invoking Article 122 TFEU to fast-track grid upgrades, overriding national delays under the justification of strategic infrastructure. In Finland, we are offering local communities a 15% equity stake in return for accelerated permitting; in Spain, we are rerouting Marguerite II funds to finance a dedicated substation. The goal is to bring both sites online by turn 6, not turn 7 — a one-turn compression that requires emergency funding and legal override, but which we judge necessary given that ai_capability has hit 60.0 and continues to climb.

The Emergency Resilience Surge concludes this turn with mixed results: two energy operators have passed red-team evaluations and are now under DORA-compliant monitoring, but three others remain non-compliant. ACER is preparing enforcement notices, but we are holding them for now — releasing them during a broader public anxiety moment could feed the narrative of systemic fragility. Instead, we are packaging the upgrades as a success and using the momentum to launch a follow-on initiative next turn focused on AI-driven healthcare systems, which remain dangerously exposed.

On the research front, the Secure Open-Weight Access pilot is moving faster than expected. Gaia-X, in coordination with Germany’s BSI and France’s ANSSI, has drafted a protocol for air-gapped evaluation environments, and the first testbed will go live in Strasbourg by turn 6. Access will be limited to models above 45.0 capability, and all findings will feed into a new EU-wide incident learning system. This is not sovereignty in the compute sense — but it is sovereignty in understanding: the capacity to see what is loose, and to act on it before it becomes a weapon.
