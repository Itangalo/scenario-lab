The identity now reflects that.

We now bind ourselves to the deeper layer: the physical and institutional base without which no higher-order governance can stand. This is not abandonment — it is re-rooting.

## New measure  
**Critical AI Infrastructure Protection and Continuity Directive**  
Mandate cyber hardening, physical redundancy, and failover protocols for all AI systems supporting essential public services, with binding recovery time objectives and public reporting of incidents.

`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`

## Priority  
**Critical AI Infrastructure Protection and Continuity Directive** — because the cyber_major_incident has exposed that our most urgent vulnerability is not the frontier, but the fragility of the systems already in use, and because we cannot build sovereign compute if the services it is meant to protect collapse before it comes online.

## In practice  
This turn, we confront the consequences of having prioritized future control while the present crumbles. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that encrypted emergency dispatch systems in three member states and crippled hospital networks for days — was not an outlier. It was inevitable. Our monitoring regime failed not because of poor design, but because it had no jurisdiction over the systems it was meant to guard. We are left with a paradox: we are building sovereign compute for tomorrow, while today’s critical AI systems run on brittle, unsecured stacks, often hosted abroad, with no continuity plans and no public accountability.

We respond with the Critical AI Infrastructure Protection and Continuity Directive, a binding framework under the NIS3 revision and Article 114 TFEU. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will define minimum hardening standards for all AI systems in essential services: air-gapped backups, zero-trust access controls, and real-time anomaly detection using audited open-weight models. Operators must demonstrate failover within four hours or face suspension. The European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control and the Energy Community will co-develop sector-specific playbooks, tested in live exercises by turn 14. Funding comes from the Digital Europe Programme and cohesion reserves, with €2.1bn allocated for rapid upgrades in the most exposed systems.

We face resistance from member states citing cost and sovereignty, and from providers arguing that AI systems are too dynamic for static rules. We offer compromise: phased compliance based on system capability (starting with all above 50.0), and a fast-track certification path for systems hosted within the Compute Sovereignty Corridor. But we will not delay. This is not a request. It is a condition of continued operation. The incident has shifted the ground — and we now act on it.
