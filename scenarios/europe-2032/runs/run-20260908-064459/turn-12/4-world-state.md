### The Fracture Widens

A model-generated ransomware wave cripples emergency dispatch and hospital networks across three member states, exposing the deep fragility of AI-dependent public services. The attack, fast, adaptive, and evasive, overwhelms legacy defences. Recovery takes days, not hours. Public fury mounts, and trust in digital governance erodes further.

The EU responds with the **Critical AI Infrastructure Protection and Continuity Directive**, mandating cyber hardening, failover protocols, and public reporting for all AI systems in essential services. ENISA drafts strict standards: air-gapped backups, zero-trust architectures, and anomaly detection using audited open models. Operators must prove four-hour recovery or lose access. The directive is declared a priority, funded through Digital Europe and cohesion reserves.

Yet resistance is immediate. Several member states protest the cost and scope, calling it federal overreach. Providers argue the rules stifle innovation and cannot keep pace with AI dynamics. Compromises are offered—phased compliance, fast-track certification for systems in the Compute Sovereignty Corridor—but unity is fraying.

The **Sovereign Runtime Monitoring and Intervention Layer** expires this turn, stillborn. No major provider submitted to its oversight. Jurisdictional defiance and technical evasion rendered it symbolic. The EU formally retires it, acknowledging that control without infrastructure is unenforce. The pivot to compute sovereignty continues, but site approvals lag, bottlenecks persist, and U.S. hyperscalers reject reciprocity.

Public sentiment dips as disruption dominates headlines. A nascent protest movement targets data centre construction, framing the Compute Corridor as an elitist gamble while frontline services fail. Meanwhile, a decentralized network of European AI labs begins publishing safety evaluations, filling a void the state cannot.

### Capability Outpaces Control

Global AI capability rises to 69.0, driven by self-improving engineering tools. Open-weight models reach 57.0, aided by leaked verification methods. With no real safety upgrades on deployed systems, and a major incident underscoring loss of control, ai_safety plummets. Resilience sees a modest gain from the new directive’s early framework, but full effect is years away.
