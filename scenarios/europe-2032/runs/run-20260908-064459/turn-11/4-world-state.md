### A Shift in Foundations

The EU pivots decisively toward compute sovereignty, launching the Accelerated EU Compute Sovereignty Corridor across six member states. Invoking enhanced cooperation and strategic infrastructure designation, the Union fast-tracks permitting, offers state-backed financing, and mandates audit access in exchange for grid priority and tax incentives. The move responds to the failure of the Sovereign Runtime Monitoring Layer, which collapsed where U.S. cloud providers refused jurisdiction and a defecting member state blocked enforcement.

Despite strong legal framing under Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act’s Crisis Clause, rollout faces immediate friction. Two member states threaten legal challenges over cohesion fund penalties, and U.S. hyperscalers reject the reciprocity terms, calling them extraterritorial. Only limited co-location agreements emerge, and domestic packaging bottlenecks delay initial deployment. Still, site selection begins, and the European Investment Bank approves preliminary loans, laying groundwork for future capacity.

### Middle Powers Explore Coordination

Amid stalled transatlantic talks, the ongoing coordination among EU, Japanese, South Korean, and Canadian semiconductor producers continues to deepen. While no formal pact is concluded this turn, technical working groups align on shared concerns about compute access and export licensing transparency. Joint statements emphasize the importance of collective dialogue, but binding commitments are deferred pending domestic consultations. The forum remains an emergent process, not a new measure, preserving the EU’s focus on the Compute Sovereignty Corridor as its sole priority.
