This turn’s coalition formation among mid-tier semiconductor producers proves that compute sovereignty — not just runtime visibility — is the foundational layer of effective governance. We now bind our commitment to that reality.

## In practice  
This turn, we pivot from trying to govern AI through observation alone to grounding oversight in physical and legal control of the machines that execute it. The failure of U.S. cloud providers to comply with the Runtime Monitoring Layer — despite months of negotiation — confirms that no foreign corporation will subordinate its stack to EU authority unless compelled by structural dependence. We now build that structure ourselves. Under Article 114 TFEU and the newly invoked Crisis Clause of the Digital Markets Act, we designate AI compute infrastructure as strategic, launching the Compute Sovereignty Corridor: a ring-fenced development zone across six member states (Ireland, Finland, Sweden, Austria, Bulgaria, and Portugal) offering expedited environmental reviews, direct state-backed loans, and guaranteed grid prioritization — conditional on full ENISA audit rights, weight security protocols, and reserved capacity for public-sector AI tasks.

We fund this with €9.2bn from the reactivated InvestEU AI Shield, redirected from expired Gigafactory reserves and augmented by a 0.3% digital services levy on non-compliant foreign providers operating in the single market. The European Investment Bank coordinates site allocation, while the Chips Joint Undertaking accelerates domestic packaging lines to reduce reliance on third-country assembly. To break the logjam of national vetoes, we invoke enhanced cooperation: any member state refusing participation forfeits eligibility for Corridor benefits and faces proportional loss of cohesion funds. We offer U.S. hyperscalers a carve-out — they may co-locate within Corridor zones if they submit to runtime logging and split their European workloads between local sovereign clouds and their own — but only if they reciprocate with transparency on training runs involving EU citizens’ data. This is not protectionism. It is jurisdictional survival.
