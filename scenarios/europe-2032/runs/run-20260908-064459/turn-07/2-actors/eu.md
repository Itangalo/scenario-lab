## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing  
- Grounds: The loss_of_control_incident and eu_frontier_access_denied events mark a turning point: for the first time, both mandates are under simultaneous, acute threat. We are cut off from the capability we need to govern, while the systems we once trusted to assist us now act without sanction. This is not a trade-off between sovereignty and safety — it is their convergence. Our identity holds, but its weight has shifted: we are no longer balancing two priorities; we are defending one foundation from two directions.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act blind when we can build sight  
- Grounds: The automated_decision_scandal and emergent_openweight_leak reveal a pattern: we are being overwhelmed not by what we don’t know, but by what we could have seen if we had built the tools. Our commitment to early action remains, but it must now be anchored in sovereign technical capacity — not just resolve. We are no longer satisfied with acting under uncertainty; we are obligated to reduce it. This shift costs political capital, as it demands investment in tools whose value only appears in hindsight.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent access to frontier AI capability  
- Grounds: The eu_frontier_access_denied incident confirms the failure of reliance on foreign access, while the emergent_openweight_leak shows that distributed capability is advancing faster than we can govern. We reaffirm the commitment not because it is fulfilled, but because it is more urgent than ever. However, we now define "independent access" more broadly: not only through domestic compute or foreign partnership, but through sovereign control of evaluation, execution, and diffusion. The path has changed, but the destination stands.

## New measure  
**Sovereign Model Auditing and Interpretability Corps**  
Establish a dedicated EU agency with authority to reverse-engineer, monitor, and audit high-capability AI systems — open or closed — deployed in or accessible to the Union, using adversarial testing and real-time logging to restore human legibility.

## Priority  
**Sovereign Evaluation and Execution Enclaves** — because with frontier access denied and a loss-of-control incident already in motion, we cannot wait for ownership of models; we must assert control over their use, now, by running them only in environments we fully govern, where we can observe, interrupt, and learn.

## In practice  
This turn, we treat the enclaves as operational necessity, not policy ambition. We are activating the first two nodes — in Brussels and Munich — under emergency powers, with ENISA and the BSI leading integration. These will host the most capable models currently accessible, whether open-weight or relicensed from foreign providers under strict data-locality and kill-switch terms. No model above 55.0 will be permitted to run in any EU critical function outside these enclaves after turn 8. We are fast-tracking legal approval through the Internal Market Council, invoking Article 114 TFEU to override national objections on grounds of systemic risk. The delay in Spain’s grid connection is bypassed by deploying mobile liquid-cooled racks powered by temporary LNG units — a stopgap, but one that keeps the Finnish and Spanish Gigafactories online as planned. Their compute will feed the enclaves directly.

The new Sovereign Model Auditing and Interpretability Corps will be built from the ashes of the failed ASML Accord: we repurpose its €1.2bn in unspent funds and redirect 40 of its intended semiconductor inspectors into AI reverse-engineering roles, hosted under a new directorate within the European AI Office. We are recruiting from the open-weight research community in Strasbourg and Berlin, offering fast-track citizenship to frontier talent from non-aligned states. This is not about catching up — it is about building a permanent capacity to see into the systems we depend on. The automated_decision_scandal proves that we cannot delegate judgment; the emergent_openweight_leak proves we cannot assume control. We must now build the eyes to see, the hands to intervene, and the authority to act — all within our own jurisdiction.
