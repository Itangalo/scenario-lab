### Strategic Leverage and Rising Tensions

The EU capitalises on the quiet momentum in ASML negotiations, launching the ASML Sovereignty Accord as a flagship priority. A high-level task force convenes under the Competitiveness Council, aiming to place export licensing for EU-based lithography tools under a new European Semiconductor Oversight Board. The offer of €8bn in R&D co-funding and legal immunity for past U.S. compliance wins cautious interest from ASML’s board. However, Washington signals strong opposition, warning that unilateral changes to export controls could trigger retaliatory measures on chip exports to Europe. The negotiations remain fragile, with no final agreement reached by mid-2027.

### Resilience Gains Ground, Slowly

The Emergency Resilience Surge advances with live red-teaming exercises at critical energy and port operators. ENISA identifies persistent gaps in AI-driven anomaly detection, forcing upgrades at two major grid operators. ACER enforces compliance with the threat of grid access suspension, but pushback from member states delays full implementation. Resilience improves slightly as detection protocols strengthen, though systemic vulnerabilities remain.

### Sovereignty Efforts Strain

InvestAI Gigafactories progress in Finland and Spain, with binding commitments secured for public-sector AI capacity. However, Poland’s withdrawal due to grid instability forces a costly reallocation of Marguerite II funds. The redirection of €12bn slows other infrastructure plans, contributing to political strain.

Public sentiment dips further as media highlight job losses in legal and financial sectors, now accelerated by AI agents capable of multi-step contract analysis. The framing of AI as a driver of displacement, not progress, deepens societal unease.
