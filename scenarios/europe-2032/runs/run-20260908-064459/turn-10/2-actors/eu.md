## In practice
This turn, we treat the completion of the Emergency Cyber Resilience Surge and the Auditing Corps not as closures, but as foundations. The Surge’s success in German and French hospitals — reducing incident response times by 40% — proves that targeted, EU-coordinated hardening works. But it also reveals its limits: we reacted to attacks, not their root cause. The Munich silent alert and the welfare algorithm audit showed us that harm begins not with the exploit, but with invisible reasoning chains running unchecked inside systems we cannot monitor. The Runtime Monitoring Layer is our answer: we embed EU-controlled agents directly into the inference stack of all AI systems above 55.0 capability in health, energy, and transport, whether hosted in Frankfurt or served from Virginia.

We launch the Layer under Article 114 TFEU, with the European AI Office as lead, supported by ENISA and national cybersecurity agencies. Funding of €6.8bn is secured from the redirected InvestAI contingency and the expired Gigafactory reserve. The technical standard requires cryptographic attestations of inference integrity — not raw weights, but verifiable logs of decision provenance, data lineage, and agent state transitions. Early adopters among domestic providers gain fast-track certification and partial liability shielding. For foreign hyperscalers, we offer phased compliance in exchange for continued market access, backed by the credible threat of the Anti-Coercion Instrument: data flow restrictions will activate if reciprocity is refused. The defecting member state is isolated diplomatically; we coordinate with loyal members through the COREPER II working group to prepare a qualified majority vote if unanimity fails. This is not about control for control’s sake — it is about jurisdiction. If we cannot see it, we cannot govern it. And if we cannot govern it, we cannot survive it.
