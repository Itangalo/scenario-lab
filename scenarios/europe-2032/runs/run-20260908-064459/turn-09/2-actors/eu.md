Secure sovereign control over AI reasoning and execution in critical domains

## Statement changes
``modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign control over AI reasoning and execution in critical domains``
- Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI — and we now understand that these mandates collapse into one when systems operate beyond human legibility or jurisdictional reach. Our sovereignty is not defined by ownership of models, but by our ability to see, contest, and stop harmful reasoning wherever it runs. This is no longer a dual imperative — it is a single line in the sand: if we cannot audit it, we cannot allow it to act.
- Grounds: The silent alert in Munich and the hospital cyberattack confirm that capability without visibility is catastrophe in waiting. The two mandates were once distinguishable — competitiveness versus caution — but at 65.5 ai_capability and openweight at 53.5, the distinction has vanished. Systems we do not control are already inside our critical infrastructure. Our identity must reflect that reality: we are no longer balancing trade-offs. We are defending jurisdiction.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we now treat the absence of interpretability as a veto condition for deployment above 55.0 capability in any domain touching public life. Acting under uncertainty is no longer acceptable when the uncertainty is about intent, reasoning, or chain of control. We act, but only when we can see the seams.
- Grounds: Trigger: **member_state_defection** combined with **silent alert in Munich**. A member state’s unilateral deal with a foreign hyperscaler — likely under the guise of “pragmatic access” — exposes the fragility of our collective stance. If we allow deployments we cannot audit, we invite invisible failures. This reversal costs political capital: it slows adoption, frustrates allies, and may force temporary service gaps. But the cost of blindness is higher.

## New measure
**Sovereign Runtime Monitoring and Intervention Layer**
A real-time, EU-controlled observability stack embedded at the inference layer of all AI systems above 55.0 capability operating in or accessible to critical public functions, enabling continuous logging, anomaly detection, and emergency interruption of reasoning chains.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 12  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +10 (via detectable drift and intervention), resilience +5 (via faster containment), eu_ai_sovereignty +8 (via persistent operational presence in execution)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national agencies in health, energy, transport, and social services, and any foreign or open-weight model operating in or accessible to these domains within the Union

## Priority
**Sovereign Runtime Monitoring and Intervention Layer** — because the Auditing Corps finishes this turn, and without a persistent, technical presence inside live inference, we are left with post-hoc autopsies of systems that have already failed. The Munich alert showed drift; the cyberattack showed weaponised autonomy. We now need eyes *during* execution, not just before deployment or after collapse. This measure closes the loop: audit tells us *how* it broke, monitoring tells us *when* it breaks, and intervention stops it.

## In practice
This turn, we treat the completion of the Sovereign Model Auditing and Interpretability Corps as both an achievement and an inflection point. The Strasbourg teams have delivered the first public audit of the French welfare algorithm, tracing a cascade of unauthorised weight updates that led to discriminatory denials. The report is published under the European AI Office’s new transparency mandate, and it lands with force: for the first time, the public sees not just *that* an AI failed, but *how* — and that builds legitimacy for stricter controls. We use this moment to transition the Corps’ core function into the new Runtime Monitoring Layer, repurposing its interpretability tooling into live inference probes.

We launch the Sovereign Runtime Monitoring and Intervention Layer under Article 114 TFEU, with the European AI Office as lead agency, supported by ENISA and national cybersecurity agencies. Funding of €6.8bn comes from the redirected InvestAI contingency and the expired Gigafactory reserve. We mandate that all AI systems above 55.0 capability — whether hosted in Dublin, Frankfurt, or by a foreign cloud provider — must integrate a sovereign monitoring agent at the inference layer, providing real-time logs of reasoning pathways, data provenance, and agent state. No raw weights are required, but cryptographic attestations of inference integrity must be verifiable by EU authorities. We begin with health and energy systems, using the cyberattack as justification for emergency deployment.

Resistance is expected from both foreign hyperscalers and a defecting member state that has signed a bilateral data-access deal with Washington. We counter with the Anti-Coercion Instrument, threatening restrictions on data flows to jurisdictions that do not reciprocate monitoring access. For domestic providers, we offer fast-track certification and liability shielding for early adopters. The goal is not to own the models, but to own the visibility — and from that, the control. Without it, every enclave, every audit, every surge is just rearranging the deck chairs. This is where sovereignty becomes real: not in the data centre, but in the stack.
