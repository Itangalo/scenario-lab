### The Weight of Enforcement

The EU enforces the Critical AI Infrastructure Protection and Continuity Directive with binding delegated acts, setting hard deadlines for resilience planning and mandating zero-trust architectures. ENISA publishes rigorous standards, including hourly air-gapped backups and anomaly detection via audited open models. Yet compliance fractures along national and corporate lines. Germany and Austria challenge the recovery timelines as unworkable, while U.S. cloud providers threaten to withdraw from EU markets, citing innovation costs. A phased approach is negotiated—higher-capability systems must comply by mid-2033, others by end-2033—but credibility erodes.

The retired Sovereign Runtime Monitoring and Intervention Layer is formally decommissioned after failing to gain provider cooperation, symbolizing the EU’s limits in oversight without infrastructure. Meanwhile, the Compute Sovereignty Corridor progresses slowly, hampered by permitting delays and public protests against new data centres, now seen as elitist while emergency systems remain vulnerable.
