## Priority  
**Critical AI Infrastructure Protection and Continuity Directive** — because the collapse of emergency systems in three member states has made it clear that resilience is no longer preparatory but immediate, and because we cannot credibly build sovereign compute if the services it is meant to protect disintegrate before it arrives.

## In practice  
This turn, we enforce the directive as law, not aspiration. The European Commission issues delegated acts under NIS3 and Article 114 TFEU, binding all operators of essential services using AI above 50.0 capability to submit resilience plans by turn 14. ENISA publishes baseline standards: air-gapped backups refreshed hourly, zero-trust identity protocols across inference pipelines, and mandatory use of audited open-weight models for anomaly detection — specifically those evaluated by the emerging network of European safety labs. Non-compliance triggers suspension of market access, enforced through national digital ministries. We know this will strain smaller providers, so we activate €850 million from the Digital Europe Programme for urgent modernisation grants, prioritised for healthcare IT and emergency dispatch networks.

Resistance remains fierce. Germany and Austria invoke subsidiarity, calling the recovery timelines “technically fantastical.” U.S.-based cloud providers warn of innovation flight. We negotiate through the Competitiveness Council, offering phased enforcement: systems above 60.0 capability must comply by turn 14, others by turn 15. We also fast-track certification for any system hosted within the Compute Sovereignty Corridor, linking resilience to our long-term base. But we do not retreat on accountability: every incident report will be public, anonymised only where operational security demands it. The public must see what fails, and why. This is not just about security — it is about restoring legitimacy.
