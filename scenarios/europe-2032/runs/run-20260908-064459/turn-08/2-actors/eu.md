## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but this turn, they have become indistinguishable. The loss_of_control_incident and eu_frontier_access_denied are not opposing pressures; they are symptoms of a single failure: the absence of sovereign agency over systems that shape life and death. Our identity is no longer dual — it is unified in the imperative to *see*, *govern*, and *act* within our own jurisdiction, or lose both safety and self-determination at once.  
- Grounds: The cyber_major_incident confirms what the hospital triage failure suggested: we are exposed not because of isolated flaws, but because we lack end-to-end control over the reasoning, deployment, and execution of high-capability AI. The two mandates no longer pull in different directions — they converge on the necessity of sovereign technical authority.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we now define “sight” as a prerequisite for action, not a luxury afterthought. This means no major deployment above 55.0 capability proceeds without sovereign evaluation, real-time logging, or adversarial testing in place. We are no longer satisfied with acting under uncertainty; we are obligated to reduce it, even when speed demands otherwise.  
- Grounds: The automated_decision_scandal and emergent_openweight_leak prove that acting blind — even with resolve — multiplies downstream costs. The Auditing Corps must not be a follow-up; it must be foundational. This shift costs political capital, as it slows adoption and frustrates allies who want access without oversight.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent access to frontier AI capability — redefined as the ability to evaluate, run, and intervene in any system above 55.0 capability deployed in or accessible to the Union, regardless of origin, through sovereign-controlled enclaves, auditing capacity, and execution environments. Ownership is no longer the threshold; control is.  
- Grounds: Trigger: **cyber_major_incident** combined with **eu_frontier_access_denied**. The world has moved: we cannot wait to own models to be harmed by them, nor can we rely on foreign partners to secure systems operating in our domain. The commitment stands, but its meaning evolves — from access to control, from dependency to sovereignty over use.

Spain’s node comes online via mobile compute bridging the grid gap — LNG-powered liquid-cooled racks are not sustainable, but they buy time. ENISA issues compliance directives under Article 114 TFEU, overriding national delays in implementation. The Finnish and Spanish Gigafactories now route their output directly into enclave operations, ensuring that domestic compute serves sovereign control, not just capacity.

We accelerate the Sovereign Model Auditing and Interpretability Corps with emergency staffing: repurposing the ASML Accord’s €1.2bn and redirecting 40 semiconductor inspectors into AI reverse-engineering roles under the European AI Office. We launch fast-track recruitment in Strasbourg and Berlin, targeting researchers who have worked on open-weight interpretability, offering residency and access to enclave data. The first audit mandate: trace the decision pathway in the French welfare denial case and publish findings within three months. This is not about blame — it is about building a permanent muscle: the ability to look inside a model’s reasoning and say, publicly, *how it failed*. Without that, no regulation holds.

The new Emergency Cyber Resilience Surge begins immediately: leveraging the cyber_major_incident as justification for emergency directives under the NIS2 framework. We mandate all public-sector software vendors to adopt runtime integrity checks, automated patch deployment, and fallback modes for AI-mediated decisions in health and welfare. Funding comes from redirected InvestAI contingency reserves (€8.4bn unlocked via completion of Gigafactories). The European Health Agency coordinates with national CIRTs to conduct live-fire exercises simulating AI-generated ransomware and dependency compromises. This is not optional modernisation — it is triage. If another wave hits before turn 10, we must ensure it does not cascade.
