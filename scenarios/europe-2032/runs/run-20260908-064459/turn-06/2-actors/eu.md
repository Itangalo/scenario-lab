## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent access to frontier AI capability  
- Grounds: The two-year commitment period closes this turn, and while we have advanced domestic compute through InvestAI Gigafactories, the collapse of the ASML Sovereignty Accord and persistent dependence on foreign frontier models mean we have not achieved sovereign access. The openweight_frontier_release event — closing the gap between open and closed frontiers — suggests a new path: leveraging distributed capability as a stopgap while building long-term independence. We uphold the intent of the commitment but acknowledge it remains unfulfilled; the cost of this turn’s reckoning is political capital erosion, reflected in our continued low standing.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late  
- Grounds: The capability_plateau_evidence event introduces a new ambiguity: is progress slowing, or are we being misled by commoditisation masking hidden leaps? At the same time, verification_widens accelerates capability in new domains, and labour_displacement confirms real economic harm. We act on the signal, not the noise: the risk of underestimating progress outweighs the risk of overreacting. Our commitment to early action holds, but its burden grows as the world becomes harder to read.

- retire `ASML Sovereignty Accord`  
- Grounds: The measure finishes this turn, but the member_state_defection has rendered it politically inert. It leaves no operational framework, no enforcement mechanism, and no cohesion to rebuild from. We do not reclassify or replace it — its failure is structural, not temporal. It ends not as policy, but as lesson.

## New measure  
**Sovereign Evaluation and Execution Enclaves**  
Establish EU-controlled, air-gapped environments where vetted frontier models — whether foreign or open — can be independently evaluated and run for critical public missions, with full data sovereignty and kill switches.

**InvestAI Gigafactories** — because with the ASML Accord dead and open-weight models still lagging at 48.0, our only path to sovereign capability is domestic compute at scale, and delays in Finland and Spain threaten to break the timeline that keeps us in the race; we are now compressing the final phase by one turn to bring sites online by turn 6, not 7.

## In practice  
We are treating the end of the two-year commitment as a reckoning, not a reset. The Gigafactories remain our lifeline: in Finland, we have secured local buy-in by offering a 15% equity stake in the facility to host communities and are invoking Article 122 TFEU to override grid connection delays. In Spain, Marguerite II funds are being redirected to build a dedicated substation, bypassing national bottlenecks. Our aim is to bring both sites online by the end of this turn — a one-turn acceleration that demands emergency funding and legal override, but which we judge essential given that ai_capability has crossed 60.0 and openweight models are now within striking distance.

The Tech Sovereignty Package completes this turn with partial success: €82bn in private capital has been committed to AI infrastructure, far below target but concentrated in sovereign-aligned projects. We are not renewing it as-is, but folding its permitting reforms into the new Sovereign Evaluation and Execution Enclaves initiative, which will require the same fast-track treatment for secure facilities. That measure — starting this turn — will establish air-gapped, ENISA-supervised environments where frontier models can be evaluated and deployed under EU control, regardless of origin. This is not about owning the model, but owning the execution: data stays in EU jurisdiction, reasoning is logged, and kill switches are tested quarterly.

Meanwhile, the Secure Open-Weight Access pilot is live in Strasbourg, with BSI and ANSSI running the first audits of models at 48.0 capability. Findings are feeding into a new EU incident learning system, but we are already seeing limits: the models can execute multi-hour tasks under supervision, and with verification_widens, they are improving faster than our tools can interpret them. We are expanding the pilot to include adversarial testing, but only within the new enclaves — no model above 45.0 will be allowed to run in unsecured environments after turn 7. The Joint Access proposal with the U.S. concluded without agreement — Washington demanded alignment on export controls we could not accept — but we are keeping the channel open for a narrower deal on evaluation standards next turn.
