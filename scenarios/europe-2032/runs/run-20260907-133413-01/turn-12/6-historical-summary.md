The EU attempted a strategic pivot in 2031 by redirecting €4.2bn from the stalled EU AI Accountability Tribunal to fund *Empower Local AI Accountability Networks*, establishing the Local Accountability Enabling Facility to support regional audit collectives in Lyon, Barcelona, and Gdansk with grants, legal immunity, and access to redress mechanisms. However, implementation faltered due to Council resistance—Poland and Hungary condemned the initiative as “legal anarchy,” while Germany and Spain demanded control over certification, triggering legal disputes over Article 114 TFEU authority and Cohesion Fund conditionality. The Tribunal remained partially funded and active, blocking full repurposing of resources under constitutional limits.

No safety advances occurred—ai_safety stagnated at 7.0—as external audits were not integrated into formal assurance. A near-miss at a French grid operator underscored critical vulnerabilities, reducing EU resilience to 34.0, exacerbated by Poland’s full opt-out from the Sovereign Cyber Shield.
