### A Desperate Pivot

The European Commission pushed forward with a radical reorientation: redirecting €4.2bn from the stalled EU AI Accountability Tribunal to fund *Empower Local AI Accountability Networks*. Framed as a strategic embrace of grassroots legitimacy, the move sought to outflank institutional paralysis by empowering regional collectives in Lyon, Barcelona, and Gdansk already conducting effective audits. DG CONNECT launched the Local Accountability Enabling Facility, offering grants, legal immunity, and direct access to the Remediation Fund.

But resistance was immediate. The Council’s Justice formation, led by Poland and Hungary, denounced the plan as “legal anarchy” and refused to recognize findings from non-state actors. Germany and Spain backed the concept but demanded control over certification standards, fracturing implementation. Cohesion Fund conditionality triggered legal threats from Warsaw. The Tribunal remained formally active, its budget only partially redirected—legal disputes over Article 114 TFEU authority delayed full disbursement. As a result, the new measure advanced only partially, with implementation deferred pending resolution of governance conflicts. No second measure was initiated; the Accountability Tribunal remains under review, but its continuation prevents full termination or repurposing under the current constitutional limit of one new measure per turn.

### Capability Marches On

Global AI capability rose to 70.5, driven by autonomous cyber agents now capable of sustained, adaptive intrusions into industrial control systems. Openweight models reached 62.0, reflecting distributed red-teaming and shared tooling from citizen collectives—progressive gains from grassroots transparency efforts, but still anchored to the pace of frontier advancement. This growth is consistent with the lagging, feedback-driven trajectory of openweight capability, avoiding an implausible surge.

Yet no safety improvements emerged; `ai_safety` remained at 7.0, as neither labs nor regulators integrated external audits into formal assurance.

Critical infrastructure faced escalating probes. A near-miss at a French grid operator exposed response gaps, further eroding resilience to 34.0. The Sovereign Cyber Shield remained patchy, with Poland opting out entirely.

Public sentiment climbed to 20.0, lifted by media coverage of local networks exposing harms faster than Brussels ever could. But political capital dipped to 1.0—the cost of three large measures (including the new one), a named priority, and no payoff yet. The legitimacy crisis deepened, even as the periphery gained trust.
