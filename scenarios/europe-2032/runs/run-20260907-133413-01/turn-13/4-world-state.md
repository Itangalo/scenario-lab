### A Crisis of Protection

A wave of AI-powered ransomware crippled regional health systems in Belgium, Italy, and Slovakia, exploiting legacy SCADA systems with machine-generated precision. Hospitals operated in darkness; dialysis schedules collapsed. The attack, attributed months later to a transnational cyber syndicate using open-weight models, underscored a brutal truth: accountability without resilience is ceremony.

In response, the EU launched the Sovereign Cyber Shield Rapid Reinforcement Programme, a €6.8bn emergency hardening effort targeting energy, water, and rail systems. A central taskforce under ENISA mobilised 1,200 engineers, bypassing national tenders via emergency procurement. Germany and the Netherlands joined immediately; Poland resisted, citing sovereignty, but faced conditional Cohesion Fund access. Planning is advanced, but full deployment will take time—only initial coordination and sensor deployment have been completed in the current turn.

Meanwhile, the *Redirect Remediation Fund to Empower Local AI Accountability Networks* measure concluded. Though implementation remained fractured, regional collectives in Lyon, Barcelona, and Gdansk demonstrated tangible detection capabilities, feeding findings into early planning for the new Shield. Their methodologies are under review for integration, but no systemic adoption has occurred yet. The EU Public Trust Corps, now fully operational, provided real-time incident communication, stabilising public panic.
