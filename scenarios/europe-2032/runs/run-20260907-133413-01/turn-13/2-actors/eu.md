## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign Cyber Shield Rapid Reinforcement Programme  
Because a major cyber incident has landed, and with resilience at 34.0 and openweight capability at 62.0, the Union can no longer afford to treat preparedness as a shared responsibility in slow motion — we must act as a sovereign defender now, or lose the capacity to respond at all.

## In practice
We are acting under fire. The cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across regional health administrations in Belgium, Italy, and Slovakia, enabled by AI-generated exploits targeting legacy SCADA systems — has exposed the fatal gap between our accountability ambitions and our operational readiness. The Local Accountability Networks we empowered were the first to detect anomalies, but they could not stop the cascade. Hospitals were dark for 36 hours in Bratislava; dialysis schedules were disrupted. This was not a near-miss. This was harm.

We are therefore launching the Sovereign Cyber Shield Rapid Reinforcement Programme under Article 114 TFEU and the NIS2 Directive’s emergency provisions. The European Commission, through ENISA and eu-LISA, is mobilising a central taskforce of 1,200 engineers — drawn from national CERTs, the newly stood-up Public Trust Corps, and pre-vetted private contractors — to deploy standardised intrusion detection and failover systems across 12,000 critical nodes in energy, water, and rail within 18 months. We are invoking the EU’s emergency procurement clause to bypass national tenders, and we are allocating €6.8bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and repurposed Cohesion Fund reserves to cover 80% of implementation costs for member states that comply by Q2 2033.

Germany and the Netherlands have already signed on. Poland remains resistant, citing sovereignty concerns, but we are offering full cost coverage and joint command — and making future Cohesion Fund tranches conditional on participation. We are also fast-tracking integration of the emergent_local_transparency_breakthrough methodology into the Shield’s monitoring layer, ensuring that detection tools developed by citizen collectives are now part of the official defence stack. This is not centralisation for its own sake — it is survival. We are rebuilding trust not through declarations, but through demonstrable protection. The Accountability Tribunal continues, but its work now feeds into the Shield’s threat library; we are aligning our redress and resilience tracks, or we will have neither.
