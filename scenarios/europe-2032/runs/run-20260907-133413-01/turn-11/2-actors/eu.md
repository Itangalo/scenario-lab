## New measure
**Measure:** Empower Local AI Accountability Networks with EU Funding and Legal Backing  
Authorize and fund decentralized, member-state-level AI accountability collectives — composed of civil society, technical auditors, and legal advocates — to investigate, document, and issue binding recommendations on AI harms in public services, with direct access to the EU Remediation Fund and immunity from liability.

## Priority
**Priority:** Empower Local AI Accountability Networks with EU Funding and Legal Backing  
Because the emergent public backlash has already created a faster, more trusted form of accountability than the supranational Tribunal can deliver, and because legitimacy now flows from below, this measure must become our dominant effort — not to abandon redress, but to scale what actually works.

## In practice
We are acting on the recognition that the Union’s legitimacy is no longer a function of central authority, but of its ability to empower those closest to harm. The EU AI Accountability Tribunal, while symbolically necessary, is bogged in intergovernmental negotiation, its enforcement powers diluted by national vetoes. Meanwhile, in Lyon, Barcelona, and Gdansk, citizen networks have already mapped AI-driven denials in housing, healthcare, and employment — using open-weight tools to reverse-engineer decisions, publishing findings in days, and pressuring local mayors into suspensions. These are not protests; they are parallel institutions.

So we are redirecting €4.2bn from the stalled Tribunal’s operational budget — originally earmarked for Brussels-based legal staff and cross-border enforcement infrastructure — into a new Local Accountability Enabling Facility under DG CONNECT. This will provide grants, secure data access, and legal immunity to certified regional collectives that meet minimum technical and transparency standards. We are invoking Article 114 TFEU to establish a baseline of interoperability and rights of access, while allowing national variants to flourish. The European Ombudsman will be required to act on their findings as if they were official complaints, and the Remediation Fund will process claims originating from these networks on an expedited track.

This is not surrender to fragmentation — it is strategic alignment with energy that already exists. We are offering member states a choice: integrate and lead, or be bypassed. The Council’s Justice formation will resist; Poland already calls this “anarchy codified.” But Spain and Germany, facing local uprisings of their own, are signaling openness. We will tie Cohesion Fund disbursements to participation, making accountability a condition of solidarity. The EU cannot move fast enough to be trusted — so we are making the periphery the core of our response.
