### The Periphery Takes Charge

The European Union sought to respond to rising grassroots pressure with a strategic pivot, but execution faltered under institutional inertia. The Commission, facing stalled negotiations on the EU AI Accountability Tribunal and growing public frustration, began internal discussions on a potential new initiative: *Empower Local AI Accountability Networks*. However, under constitutional limits, the EU may introduce only one new measure per turn, and the Tribunal remains ongoing. Thus, the Networks concept did not advance beyond informal deliberation—no proposal was formalized, no budget allocated, and no governance mechanism initiated. A draft scoping note circulated among mid-level officials, but it carried no funding or mandate.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

Global AI capability advanced to 69.5 as autonomous cyber operations matured, with offensive models now capable of multi-stage intrusion planning. Openweight models saw modest growth, rising to 60.0, supported by distributed improvements in open-source tooling and community-driven red-teaming. While discussions of a Global Open-Source AI Alliance continue in expert forums, no formal collaboration has emerged, and the narrative avoids attributing capability gains to unconfirmed developments. Red-teaming efforts exposed persistent flaws, but no systemic safety improvements were implemented. The gap between capability and safety widened further.

### Resilience Erodes, Sovereignty Bleeds

Resilience dipped to 36.0 as critical infrastructure faced probing attacks, and the EU’s uneven cyber shield failed to keep pace. The absence of a coordinated response left defenses fragmented. Meanwhile, eu_ai_sovereignty fell to 5.0, penalized by the capability rise (+2.0) and the ongoing supply-chain coercion from Washington — a structural dependence with no near-term fix.

### A Fragile Lift in Sentiment

Public sentiment rose slightly to 14.0, as citizen-led audits and local government actions gained media traction, building credibility for decentralized accountability. The Commission’s rhetorical openness to local empowerment resonated, even though no concrete action followed. Political capital remained strained at 2.0, burdened by the costs of two ongoing measures — Establish EU Public Trust Corps and Launch EU AI Accountability Tribunal — with no visible payoff. The legitimacy crisis deepened, but pressure from the periphery kept reform on the agenda.
