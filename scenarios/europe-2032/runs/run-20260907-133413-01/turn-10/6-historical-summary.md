The EU entered 2031 in crisis after a damning ombudsman report exposed systemic harm from a public AI, denying benefits to over 12,000 citizens and triggering protests in Marseille. Poland demanded a moratorium on public-sector AI, deepening the legitimacy crisis. In response, the Commission launched the EU AI Accountability Tribunal and Remediation Fund to investigate harms and issue binding redress, but Council approval stalled due to resistance from Poland and Hungary, who secured national opt-outs and enforcement vetoes, undermining central authority.

A breakthrough in AI capability saw an AI autonomously design and neutralize cryptographic exploits, pushing global ai_capability to 68.5. No equivalent advance in safety followed; EU red-teaming exposed flaws without remediation, driving ai_safety down to 7.0. Meanwhile, U.S. pressure on ASML restricted Europe’s access to critical semiconductor equipment, exposing the lack of technological sovereignty and dropping eu_ai_sovereignty to 7.0.

Open-weight models reached 59.0, enabling targeted intrusions into industrial systems, while the Sovereign Cyber Shield remained unevenly deployed, with Poland and Spain opting out. Public trust eroded further, with political capital falling below 5.0 and sentiment dropping to 12.0 as institutional responses were seen as slow and bureaucratic. No completed policy measure reversed the downward trajectory, leaving the EU fragmented, vulnerable, and increasingly illegitimate.
