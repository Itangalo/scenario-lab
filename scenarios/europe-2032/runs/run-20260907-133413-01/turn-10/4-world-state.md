### The Weight of Justice

The EU entered early 2031 reeling. A landmark ombudsman report confirmed systemic harm in a public social insurance algorithm, denying benefits to over 12,000 citizens. Protests erupted in Marseille, and Poland demanded a moratorium on public-sector AI. Trust had collapsed into outrage.

In response, the Commission launched the EU AI Accountability Tribunal and Remediation Fund as its new priority. Framed as a restoration of justice, it aimed to investigate AI harms and issue binding redress. However, securing Council approval proved difficult. A bloc of six member states, led by Poland and Hungary, insisted on sweeping national opt-outs, delaying final adoption. Compromises on regional tribunals and enforcement vetoes weakened central authority, casting doubt on the Tribunal’s effectiveness.

### Capability Leaps, Safety Lags

A sudden capability jump sent shockwaves through the research community. A leading lab demonstrated an AI that autonomously designed and tested novel cryptographic exploits, then neutralised them — a feat previously requiring coordinated human teams. Cybersecurity experts warned that offensive capabilities had advanced faster than defences. The jump, while narrow, marked a turning point in autonomous cyber operations.

This surge pushed `ai_capability` to 68.5. Yet no corresponding safety measure landed. On the contrary, the EU’s own red-teaming labs, meant to bolster assurance, had exposed vulnerabilities without fixing them. `ai_safety` plummeted to 7.0 as confidence in control eroded.

### Fractured Sovereignty, Failing Resilience

Meanwhile, Washington pressured ASML to restrict exports of older lithography equipment, cutting off servicing for non-sanctioned customers. The move, leveraging U.S. supply-chain control, struck at Europe’s semiconductor access. With no sovereign alternative, `eu_ai_sovereignty` fell to 7.0, penalised by both the capability rise and the supply-chain coercion.

Resilience also declined. As open-weight models reached 59.0, mid-tier industrial systems reported targeted intrusions. The Sovereign Cyber Shield, though formally complete, remained unevenly deployed. Poland and Spain still opted out of full integration, leaving critical nodes exposed.

### A Capital Starved of Trust

The EU’s political capital, already at 5.0, eroded further. The new Tribunal, while symbolically bold, strained an overburdened system. Public sentiment dropped to 12.0 as protests framed the response as bureaucratic overreach without immediate redress. The cost of three ongoing measures and the priority penalty drained capacity, while no completed measure delivered a rebound.
