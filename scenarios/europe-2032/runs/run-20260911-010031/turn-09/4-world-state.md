### A win, a reset, and a leak
For the first time in years, Brussels had something to point at. Hospital waiting lists shortened in three regions where triage assistants and paperwork automation had been deployed on the certified backup stack, school pilots showed measurable gains, and local mayors credited a European choice rather than an American product. The Commission moved to bottle it, offering co-funding only to deployments with audit logs, appeal in days, and offline backup.

The backdrop swallowed much of it. Capital fled the AI sector in the autumn. Valuations reset, announced data-centre expansions were cancelled rather than delayed, and several hosting and supply arrangements European planners had counted on evaporated. Permit reservations for the gigafactory sites were kept alive cheaply, but no new capacity was promised.

In the same weeks a near-frontier open release landed. Hundreds of thousands of downloads in the first days put multi-hour offensive and bio-relevant capability on private hardware permanently. Phased gating of synthesis models was bypassed through mirrored copies already circulating outside any audit.

HERA held the bio shield work to schedule — sequencing surge continued, therapy procurement limped on import-dependent — and made new dividend money conditional on streaming data to the joint cell. The member state running its own hosting deal took the condition as coercion and still refused full telemetry, while keeping priority doses.

Hospitals degraded rather than stopped, towns leaned on the backups installed in spring, and voters saw both rescue and dependence. Trust recovered a notch, permission to act did not.
