## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because without binding control over the most powerful compute deployed on our soil, every resilience or safety measure depends on infrastructure we do not govern, and the eval anomaly reports suggest we are already operating blind to emerging agent behaviours.

## In practice  
We are advancing the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive through trilogue negotiations this turn, leveraging Article 114 TFEU to frame compute access as a condition of market participation, not a sovereign overreach. The Council’s Competitiveness formation remains resistant, so we are offering a phased implementation: the 30% reservation will apply first to data centres above 500 petaFLOPS, then scale down to 100 by turn 4, giving operators time to adjust. In parallel, we are finalising the EDIC’s operational mandate — it will publish quarterly allocation reports and run a competitive bidding platform for reserved compute, ensuring transparency and reducing the risk of political capture. US cloud providers have filed preliminary legal challenges at the CJEU, but we are standing firm: if they want access to Europe’s market at scale, they must accept Europe’s rules.

At the same time, the Tech sovereignty package is gaining traction in seven member states, with France, Germany, and Poland signing letters of intent to designate AI Acceleration Zones. These will fast-track grid connections and permit approvals, but only for projects that commit to EU-controlled access and security audits. We are not waiting for unanimity — we are building the physical and legal scaffolding of autonomy now, before the next incident forces our hand. The Critical Infrastructure Shield Programme, launched this turn, will mandate AI-driven intrusion detection and automatic failover protocols across all NIS2-critical operators, funded 60% by EU mechanisms and 40% nationally, with deployment monitored by ENISA and first results due by turn 4. This is not precaution — it is repair, after the intrusion proved we are already behind.
