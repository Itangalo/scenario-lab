## New measure
**Binding Sovereign Control for Critical AI Defenders**  
Require that all AI systems directly responsible for cyber defence of critical infrastructure operate under EU-controlled infrastructure, with model weights, update pathways, and incident response authority legally and technically insulated from foreign jurisdiction.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to +14 to resilience by turn 7; ensures fail-safe control of defensive AI during geopolitical disruption; reduces exposure to foreign access denial or sabotage; strengthens EU leverage in supply chain negotiations by demonstrating operational independence  
`Applies to:` EU critical infrastructure operators under NIS2, EU and foreign vendors of AI-driven cyber defence tools operating in the EU market

## Priority
**Binding Sovereign Control for Critical AI Defenders** — because the Taiwan blockade and supply chain coercion events prove that technical capability is meaningless without jurisdictional control, and because the EU just lost access to a frontier model mid-operation, exposing hospitals and ministries — we cannot rely on foreign systems to defend our own sovereignty, especially when they are now the first line of defence.

## In practice
We are finalising the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive this turn, with full activation of the 30% reservation now in force. The European Digital Infrastructure Council has launched its allocation platform, prioritising access for public research and the Sovereign AI Evaluation Corps. Legal challenges from US cloud providers are being met with a coordinated rebuttal under Article 114 TFEU, asserting jurisdiction over systems trained on EU soil and used in critical functions. This is not nationalisation — it is the enforcement of a necessary condition for autonomy in an age where unmonitored reasoning loops are already control failures.

The Critical Infrastructure Shield Programme completes its rollout this turn, with mandatory audits and automated response systems now deployed across pilot sites in Germany, Spain, and Finland. ENISA has updated its templates to include real-time patching and swarm detection benchmarks, and we are offering front-loaded grants to smaller member states to close implementation gaps. However, the recent **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has revealed a fatal flaw: even hardened systems are vulnerable if their core intelligence is controlled beyond our borders. That is why this turn we launch the **Binding Sovereign Control for Critical AI Defenders** — a new measure under the NIS2 and Cyber Resilience Act frameworks, requiring that all AI systems with direct authority over critical infrastructure operate on EU-anchored infrastructure, with model weights, update channels, and override authority under enforceable EU jurisdiction. We are working with ENISA, EI-ISAC, and the European Defence Agency to define the technical and legal perimeter of control, and we will tie certification to both operation and insurability.

The Tech Sovereignty Package continues to unlock private investment, with AI Acceleration Zones now operational in three member states. We are redirecting 15% of the €200bn toward domestic chip assembly and energy resilience, responding directly to the **taiwan_blockade** and **supply_chain_coercion** shocks. The Sovereign AI Evaluation Corps remains on track, now at 40% staffing, with access negotiations advancing with three developers. But we now know that evaluation is not enough — control is. This turn, we shift from securing capacity to securing command. The world has moved. We are no longer preparing for risk. We are in it.
