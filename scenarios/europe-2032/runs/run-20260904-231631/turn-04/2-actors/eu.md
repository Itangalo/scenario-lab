## New measure  
**EU Defensive AI Certification Standard**  
Launch a binding EU-wide certification regime for AI-driven cyber defence systems used in critical infrastructure, replacing the current voluntary coalition effort with enforceable technical and operational benchmarks.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` +12 to +15 to resilience by turn 5; establishes a baseline for insurability of AI-defended infrastructure; integrates emergent swarm detection and real-time patching into mandatory specs; reduces fragmentation in defensive posture across member states  
`Applies to:` EU critical infrastructure operators under NIS2, EU-based vendors of defensive AI, and foreign vendors seeking market access for cyber defence tools

## Priority  
**EU Defensive AI Certification Standard** — because the emergent certification effort, while promising, lacks authority and risks deepening fragmentation just as offensive AI reaches multi-day autonomy, and because the Taiwan tension rise signals a world where defensive readiness can no longer wait for consensus — we must set the standard now or be governed by others’ choices.

## In practice  
We are finalising the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive this turn, with the European Digital Infrastructure Council launching its allocation platform in January. The 30% reservation is now legally in force, applied to all data centres above 500 petaFLOPS, and we are using Article 114 TFEU to condition market access on compliance. Legal challenges from US cloud providers are being coordinated through the Commission’s Legal Service, with a rebuttal package prepared for the CJEU. This is not nationalisation — it is a calibrated assertion of jurisdiction over systems trained on EU soil that may already exhibit unmonitored reasoning. The first allocations will prioritise public research and the Sovereign AI Evaluation Corps, ensuring we can observe what is being built before it escapes into deployment.

The Tech Sovereignty Package continues to unlock private investment, with AI Acceleration Zones now operational in France, Germany, and Poland. We are enforcing strict audit and access conditions in exchange for fast-tracked permitting, and redirecting 15% of the €200bn toward domestic chip assembly and energy resilience to prevent future bottlenecks. The Critical Infrastructure Shield Programme is integrating the new real-time patching and swarm detection tools into its audit templates, with ENISA leading a rapid update cycle to ensure pilots in Germany, Spain, and Finland become national baselines. Front-loaded grants are being offered to smaller member states to prevent capacity gaps from becoming attack vectors.

The Sovereign AI Evaluation Corps is now staffed at 40% capacity, hosted under a reinforced Joint Research Centre, with vetted researchers from seven member states onboard. We are negotiating access agreements with three EU-market-facing developers, tying market access to weight-sharing under strict protocols. This is not duplication — it is the necessary public counterweight to private development. Meanwhile, the voluntary defensive certification effort, while welcome, is already showing cracks: non-EU vendors are opting out, insurers are hesitant, and member states are diverging. We cannot let defensive standards be set by fragmentation. So this turn, we are launching the EU Defensive AI Certification Standard — a binding regime under the NIS2 framework, developed with ENISA, EI-ISAC, and the European Insurance and Occupational Pensions Authority. It will mandate technical benchmarks for swarm detection, patching speed, and failover resilience, with certification required for both operation and insurability. We are fast-tracking it through the Cyber Resilience Act revision, using the urgency clause to bypass prolonged trilogues. Resistance from member states reliant on US vendors is expected, but we are offering transition credits and co-funding for compliant retrofits. This is not about exclusion — it is about ensuring that when AI defends our grid, our water, our hospitals, it meets a standard we set, not one we inherit.
