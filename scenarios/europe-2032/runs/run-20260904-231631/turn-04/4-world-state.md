### Capability Crosses the Threshold  
AI capability reaches 60.0 as persistent agent architectures mature, enabling systems to complete multi-day professional tasks end to end. Offensive cyber tools now operate with minimal supervision, raising alarms across intelligence agencies. The jump triggers a reassessment of control assumptions, with reports of unmonitored reasoning loops now classified as early control failures.

### Sovereignty Advances Cautiously  
The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive completes its rollout this turn, with the European Digital Infrastructure Council activating full access for public research institutes and designated sovereign AI projects. Despite ongoing legal challenges from US cloud providers, the Commission confirms the allocation of 30% of high-end EU compute capacity to sovereign use, marking the formal closure of the initiative. This delivers a +5 boost to eu_ai_sovereignty, partially offset by a −1 decay due to the rise in global ai_capability, resulting in a net increase to 29.0.

### Resilience Effort Begins, But Not Yet Effective  
The EU announces the proposed EU Defensive AI Certification Standard, aiming to convert fragmented voluntary efforts into a binding regime under NIS2. ENISA begins drafting technical benchmarks for swarm detection and real-time patching, with certification envisioned to affect insurability. However, co-funding mechanisms and transition credits are still under negotiation, and member state resistance delays adoption. The initiative is registered as a new proposal but remains in early development, with no operational impact yet.

### Safety Stalls Amid Rising Risk  
Despite institutional progress, ai_safety dips as capability outpaces assurance. The Sovereign AI Evaluation Corps remains below full capacity, and weight-sharing agreements stall with key developers. No breakthroughs in interpretability land, and the gap between frontier systems and oversight tools widens.

### Political Capital Holds  
The completion of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive yields a +3 capital boost. The announcement of the Defensive AI Certification Standard earns a +4 proposal bonus, judged a direct response to the emergent cyber insurance crisis and rising offensive threats. Portfolio costs remain high, but capital stabilises as major initiatives advance, albeit slower than planned.
