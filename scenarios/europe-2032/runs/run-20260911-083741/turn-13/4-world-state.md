### Patching at speed
The repair crews got one real win. New defensive tooling arriving from outside — patching that moves as fast as flaws are found, monitors that spot swarms rather than single intrusions — was pushed straight into town-hall systems by embedded teams and new hardening guides. In several municipalities dispatch and appointments came off paper for the first time since 2030, if only for weeks at a time.

It did not end the intrusions. Frontier capability crept forward again, and open models absorbed almost all of it. Attack kits adapted within months, and assurance on shipped systems slipped further behind. Engineers privately said failures were more legible, just more frequent.

### Waiting lists and warships
Where the care corps held, clinics could finally point to numbers: shorter queues, faster decisions on hosted models, mayors willing to say so on camera. Brussels claimed the improvement as a European choice in health meetings, and for the first time in two years some coverage credited the Union rather than just local crews.

The mood lift was thin. Commercial robots under foreign control models kept spreading through warehouses and ports, and country-tier rationing of chips and leading models stayed tight. Sites for future plants stayed warm, grid work inched on, no building vote was risked.

Then distant exercises in the Taiwan Strait pushed shipping insurance up and forced contingency memos in port authorities. Nothing stopped, but Rotterdam and Hamburg began asking what a disruption would mean for spares and leased robot fleets. A dockworker paper in one hub carried a single-source note about a brief fleet freeze elsewhere — unverified, quickly denied — that circulated among engineers without reaching the news.

Emergency footing held: sentinels tracking the lingering pathogen, hospitals on European-hosted fallbacks, overtime now normal. Stabilisation without recovery.
