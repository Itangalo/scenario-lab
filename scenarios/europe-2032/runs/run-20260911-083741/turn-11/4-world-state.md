### Patching while cut off
July to December 2031 was defined by two outside shocks arriving together. Leading American and Chinese labs demonstrated a sharp step forward in machine-written code and automated intrusion work. Within weeks, municipal IT staff saw break-in tools that rewrote themselves around routine blocks. At almost the same moment Washington and Beijing announced a limited pact on securing model weights and restricting certain biological design tools, with thin verification.

Brussels was not at the table. The Commission sought observer and technical access, arguing that European hospitals and grid operators needed joint patches and shared defensive research. It received briefings afterwards, not consultation beforehand.

### Holding wards open
Inside the Union, the strategy was continuity, not new building. Under health-emergency mandates, containment of the winter pathogen release dragged on through autumn, led by health emergency teams with sequencing and wastewater monitoring. Cyber repair teams stayed embedded in communes, keeping dispatch and appointments on offline backups and paper workarounds. Hospitals and ministries remained on European-hosted open models and older licensed systems, with reprogrammed digital funds paying for inference on European soil.

The Fallback Care and Bio-Response Corps, the only funded line, kept wards staffed through overtime, but did not expand. Gigafactory sites were kept warm, grid works crept forward, and votes on retaliation or new construction were again frozen. Mayors praised the crews while blaming the Union for the original dependence.

Public mood did not recover. Queues, ward closures and the sight of foreign systems switched off at a distance, now compounded by news of a great-power deal made over European heads, read as further humiliation. Trust remained exhausted, and political room for new action narrowed to zero.
