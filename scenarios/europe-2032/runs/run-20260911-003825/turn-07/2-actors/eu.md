## New measure
**EU-US Government-to-Government AI Access and Restraint Accord**
Negotiates state-to-state assured access, joint pre-deployment evaluation and bio-uplift restraint lines directly with Washington now that frontier labs are under US state control.
Why this and why now: nationalisation kills our market-leverage model under the AI Act and export-control tightening threatens allied rationing, while bio-uplift findings and eval anomalies mean unverified systems can no longer be imported on trust — only a government counterparty deal preserves access and gives us a restraint channel, which is core to our two-year commitment to trusted sovereign capacity for autonomous action.

## Priority
EU-US Government-to-Government AI Access and Restraint Accord — because with labs nationalised and allied access at risk of rationing, nothing in the Gigafactory or containment lines functions without a political access and verification basis first.

## In practice
We task EEAS and DG TRADE with a Council mandate under the Common Commercial Policy and CFSP to open a state-level channel to the new US AI authority, seeking published volume-licence access for EU grid-prioritised sites and vetted researcher model access in exchange for aligned lithography controls and weight-security commitments. We park AI Act conformity enforcement against US frontier providers pending the talks, to avoid a fight we cannot win against a state actor.

We run M7 through DG GROW, ENISA and national market-surveillance authorities as the domestic leg: fleet registry, cloud-independent stop audits and port/hospital exercises continue, now framed as verification of the US assurances. With political capital at 15 and sentiment at 16 we offer mayors and unions only what is already funded — continuity exercises and manual-fallback staffing — and present the Accord as preventing a cutoff rather than promising new capability.
