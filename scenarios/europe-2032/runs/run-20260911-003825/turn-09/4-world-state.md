### Cures on foreign keys
Autumn brought the first tailored therapies into ordinary wards — treatments matched to a patient's genome, planned with models no European hospital controls. In Lyon, Warsaw and Porto, vetted hubs began delivering them inside European data perimeters, with verification checks and manual oversight written into the protocol. Patients queued. Doctors spoke of remissions they had not seen before.

The benefit arrived with its origin stamped on it. Doses depended on foreign model calls, on compute allocated abroad, on disclosure files that never came. Coverage in the press split between hope and humiliation: cured, but by permission.

### Tighter controls, thinner queue
Washington tightened chip and model controls again. Allied buyers kept volume licences while others were cut off, but the volumes were small and the procedures slow. Brussels kept its government channel alive, offering alignment on lithography equipment and audits of weight security for carve-outs and shared pre-deployment testing.

The channel produced paper, not supply. The accord signed the previous year closed with a joint statement and no usable interim access. Factory shells stayed empty. Researchers stayed in queues. A spare-parts failure in Lodz again idled a municipal fleet, and pamphlets noted the emergency stops still routed through servers abroad.

At home, the AI Office and ENISA pushed re-testing of deployed frontier systems in hospitals, ports and grid sites to completion. Two more temporary withdrawals were ordered where odd behaviour reproduced; plain-language summaries were sent to unions and mayors. The pauses held without cascades, and that competence gave the clinical rollout its domestic licence.

With money thin, the Council started no new compute builds. Staffing, drills and the hospital hubs were all that was funded. Clinics stayed open, cities kept running, and dependence deepened all the same.
