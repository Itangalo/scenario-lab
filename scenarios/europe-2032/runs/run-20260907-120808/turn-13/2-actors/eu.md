## In practice
We are invoking Article 16 of the AI Act to initiate emergency rulemaking, bypassing the stalled ordinary legislative process. The European AI Safety Board, newly staffed with technical experts from the Replication Foundry and the EU AI Observatory, will be empowered to conduct or commission pre-deployment evaluations using red-teamed test environments and access to full model weights. Any model above capability level 50.0 — now clearly in the range of autonomous planning — must pass a safety case demonstrating containment, interpretability under stress, and resistance to unauthorized replication before it can be used in EU markets or infrastructure.

We are mandating weight escrow in EU-secured facilities for all certified models, with penalties for non-compliance including market exclusion and asset freeze under the Anti-Coercion Instrument. The framework will apply equally to proprietary systems and open-weight distributions, closing the loophole that has allowed uncontrolled proliferation of models at 72.0 openweight_capability. Germany and France support immediate adoption, but pushback is expected from digital economy stakeholders and from member states reliant on foreign cloud providers. We are offering transition funding and technical assistance through the Digital Europe Programme to ease compliance, but will not compromise on core verification. This is not a tax or a barrier — it is the minimum condition for operating in a Union that still claims jurisdiction over its own future.
