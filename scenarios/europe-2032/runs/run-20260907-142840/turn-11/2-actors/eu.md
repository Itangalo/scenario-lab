## In practice
This turn, we formally close the Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration, marking Lyon, Barcelona, and Helsinki as fully interoperable under ENISA’s oversight, with audit protocols for AI use in emergency response now embedded in their joint operating procedures. Certification planning for Bologna, Ljubljana, and Tallinn advances under the NIS2 framework, using reweighted allocations from the European Structural and Investment Funds to cover initial integration costs—no new budget lines, no Council vote, just operational repurposing within existing mandates. The model is clear: resilience through redundancy, authority through function, sovereignty through continuity. We do not wait for unanimity; we consolidate what already works.

At the same time, the Commission advances the Article 5 DMA review in parallel, treating it not as a substitute for sovereign compute but as a fallback lever: if we cannot build our own, we must at least see what others have. DG COMP, supported by ENISA and ACER, drafts an audit protocol requiring dominant AI providers to disclose EU energy consumption and verifiable spare compute capacity—data that could, in a crisis, allow for partial mobilisation of private infrastructure. Legal services are assessing whether the definition of “digital fairness” can credibly encompass strategic availability, and early indications suggest member states may tolerate this stretch, provided it stays within market enforcement rather than becoming de facto nationalisation. No one pretends this is ideal. But in a Union where political capital is 8.0 and fragmentation is accelerating, leverage is what you can exercise, not what you wish you had.
