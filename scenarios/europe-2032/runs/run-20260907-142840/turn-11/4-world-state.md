### A Crisis of Control

A major cyberattack, largely automated and powered by model-generated tooling, sweeps across public service networks in three member states. Hospitals, transport schedulers, and local tax systems face weeks of degraded operation. Attribution remains murky, but forensic consensus points to non-state actors using open-weight models now widely available. With `resilience` at just 35.0, the EU absorbs only part of the blow—disruption is widespread, recovery uneven.

The Municipal Resilience Backchannel proves its worth. Lyon, Barcelona, and Helsinki maintain emergency coordination through pre-established interoperability, rerouting services and sharing threat intelligence via ENISA-facilitated channels. Certification planning extends to Bologna, Ljubljana, and Tallinn, marking the first tangible EU-wide resilience network to survive institutional fracture. The backchannel, long in development, demonstrates operational value—but it has not yet been formally adopted as a completed EU measure. Implementation remains partial, and full integration awaits formal endorsement and funding mechanisms still under negotiation. The progress is real, but not yet a finished policy outcome.

Yet central dysfunction deepens. The European Evaluation Shield collapses further as two additional member states suspend participation, citing legal overreach. Trust in top-down oversight erodes, and voluntary data sharing with EU agencies dwindles. With no new safety measure landing on shipped systems and `ai_capability` advancing, `ai_safety` erodes further.

Meanwhile, `ai_capability` rises by 0.5 to 62.0, driven by integration of open-derived tools despite the plateau in frontier releases. `openweight_capability` increases modestly to 53.5—growing by 0.5, matching the frontier’s advance—as retrofitting spreads cautiously across municipal systems. The gap narrows slightly, but openweight progress remains derivative and constrained by the ceiling of existing frontier capability. No rupture or autonomous acceleration in open models is observed.

The biosecurity community is rattled by findings suggesting a genome model produced a viable human-infective design—methodologically contested, but impossible to ignore.

The Commission pushes forward with its Article 5 DMA review, framing strategic compute access as a fairness issue. Legal teams debate whether energy and spare capacity disclosures can be mandated without treaty change. The move is seen as a stopgap, but one that might yield intelligence, not infrastructure.

Public sentiment dips as headlines focus on the cyberattack and job losses in administrative sectors. The EU’s only success—municipal resilience—goes underreported.

### Political Capital Rebounds—Barely

Early gains from the Municipal Resilience Backchannel demonstration yield cautious optimism. Though the measure was never formally proposed as a standalone initiative, its emergent utility allows the Commission to claim limited momentum. This, combined with improved coordination visibility and modest public validation, contributes +2 to `eu_political_capital`, raising it from 8.0 to 10.0. The gain reflects recognition of progress, not full implementation, and falls within the allowable influence of sovereignty-adjacent efforts on political capital. No measure is marked as completed; the backchannel remains in the trial phase, with formal adoption deferred to a future turn.
