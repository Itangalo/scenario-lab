### Formalising Resilience Amid Fragile Gains

The Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration is formally completed, marking a rare policy victory. Lyon, Barcelona, and Helsinki are now fully interoperable under ENISA oversight, with binding audit protocols for AI use in emergency response. Real-time threat sharing during the recent cyber incident proved its worth, and Bologna, Ljubljana, and Tallinn are on track for certification under NIS2, funded through reprioritised structural funds. This tangible progress delivers a resilience boost, even as central institutions remain gridlocked.

### Stalled Ambition, Shrinking Safety

With political capital at just 10.0, no new measure is proposed. The threshold of 12.0 has not been reached, and member-state hesitation, legal uncertainty, and fiscal strain make launching anything new too risky. The Commission’s Article 5 DMA review continues, but legal teams remain divided on whether energy and compute disclosures can be mandated without treaty change. The effort yields intelligence potential, not infrastructure.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Crumbles

Global AI capability advances to 62.5, driven by incremental integration of open-weight tools. Openweight capability follows, rising to 54.0, as retrofitting spreads across municipal systems. No breakthroughs occur, but the steady march continues. With no new safety measures landing on shipped systems and capability rising, ai_safety drops further to 10.0—now deep in the danger zone of opaque systems and nominal misuse monitoring.

The completion of a large category 6 measure delivers +5 to political capital. The portfolio charge was −4 (one large measure at −3, priority at −1), but the measure finished, so the charge ends and the completion bonus applies. Capital rises from 10.0 to 15.0—still fragile, but no longer paralysed. Sovereignty, however, declines slightly to 29.0, as capability rose by 0.5 and no category 4 measure finished or secured access.
