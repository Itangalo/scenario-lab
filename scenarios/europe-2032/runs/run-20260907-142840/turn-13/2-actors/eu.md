## Priority
**Emergency AI Incident Response Protocol Activation and Expansion** — because a major cyber incident has just landed, defenders are visibly behind, and attribution remains months away; we must act now to contain damage, restore confidence, and prevent this from becoming the new normal.

## In practice
This turn, we invoke Article 4(3) of the NIS2 Directive and activate the Union’s standing authority to coordinate urgent cross-border responses to significant cyber disruptions. Using the momentum of the Municipal Resilience Backchannel’s success, we expand its operational framework into a full emergency protocol: ENISA is mandated to convene national CERTs, energy grid operators, transport authorities, and health data systems into a temporary Crisis Coordination Cell, hosted out of the EU Cybersecurity Centre in Bucharest. For the first time, real-time threat telemetry from private-sector AI service providers—including those operating open-weight models—will be required to flow into this cell under limited liability protection, enabling faster pattern detection and countermeasure deployment.

We fund this expansion through an emergency reprogramming of 470 million EUR from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, approved via comitology with minimal Council debate due to the declared urgency. The legal basis rests on NIS2’s “substantial impact” threshold, which the recent ransomware sweep across regional hospitals and transit hubs clearly meets. Resistance from some member states—particularly those reliant on third-party cloud providers—is softened by offering technical support and partial cost coverage for system hardening. We also pre-position public messaging templates and designate national spokespersons to ensure consistent communication, aiming not only to stop the attack’s spread but to halt the erosion of public sentiment that follows chaos more than code. This is not peacetime preparation—it is crisis response, and it must land as such.
