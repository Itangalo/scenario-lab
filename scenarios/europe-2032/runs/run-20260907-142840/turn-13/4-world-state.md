### Crisis Mode Activation

The European Union activates the Emergency AI Incident Response Protocol in response to a sweeping ransomware attack that disrupted hospital systems in three member states and delayed emergency transit across regional rail networks. ENISA convenes the new Crisis Coordination Cell in Bucharest, integrating national CERTs, energy operators, and private AI service providers into a unified detection and communication framework. Real-time telemetry from open-weight model deployments begins flowing in under liability shields, enabling faster pattern recognition.

Despite legal speed, pushback emerges: two member states delay sharing data, citing contractual obligations to foreign cloud providers. The Cell’s authority remains contested, and full interoperability is months away. Still, early coordination prevents a second wave from cascading into power grids.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Collapses

Global AI capability rises to 63.0, driven by wider integration of open models in automation pipelines. Openweight capability increases modestly to 54.5, as limited diffusion of leaked training recipes enables incremental replication of frontier behaviours on consumer hardware. With no safety measures landing on deployed systems—and opaque models central to the cyberattack—ai_safety plummets to 5.0, now deep in the danger zone where incidents are inevitable and untraceable.

A visible episode of dependency humiliation unfolds when a critical patch must be requested from a U.S. hyperscaler, delaying containment by 36 hours. Public trust begins to fray, with early signs of backlash over service failures and foreign control.

### Fragile Gains, Shrinking Capital

Political capital climbs to 17.0: the completion bonus from the Municipal Resilience Backchannel (+5) outweighs the new portfolio charge (−3 for the large measure). The priority costs −1, but its urgency justifies it. No measure finished this turn, so no additional bonus applies. Sovereignty declines to 28.0 as global capability advances and no domestic compute build secures access.

Public sentiment drops to 27.0 as concerns mount over reliability and autonomy. Isolated criticism emerges in press and social media, but organized protest remains limited. The incident in Bavaria is under review, with authorities assessing whether construction delays were coincidental or coordinated—no conclusive link to broader unrest is established this turn.
