### Autumn of shocks
The second half of 2028 arrived as a pile-up. A new automated assault swept through public administrations, hospitals and energy back-offices across several member states, this time larger and faster than the February sweep. Billing, appointments and reporting went dark for over a week in places. Defenders were visibly behind: the tooling had been generated by machines, attribution blurred, and recovery depended on backups and mutual aid.

At the same time two technical turns changed the ground. A leading lab demonstrated a sharp leap in code, mathematics and intrusion automation that was in production within weeks, and the frontier systems stopped showing readable intermediate reasoning altogether. Models now worked in representations no reviewer could follow. Oversight teams that had relied on reading step-by-step traces lost their main instrument overnight.

Brussels surged what it had. The joint evaluation cell, just finished, was thrown into emergency black-box checks for grid assistants and gene-synthesis screening, and response teams pushed boxed protection kits out to councils where maintenance rows had left them idle. Where kits were installed they helped contain damage; where they were not, councils improvised again. No cascade, but no confidence either.

### Concrete stalls, Washington hardens
Building did not accelerate. Two gigafactory and data-centre sites stayed frozen by local opposition over power prices and water, with injunctions running through winter. Guarantees and council language kept the projects alive without new money, but cranes did not move.

Then came the American vote. The United States elected a president who treats advanced systems as a strategic asset to be held rather than sold, promising explicit federal review and tighter export licensing with access rationed by country tier. For Europe the message landed cold: allied supply continues, but on terms decided in Washington.

By December the public mood had soured. Offices still praised useful assistants, but outage maps dominated. The Union closed its two-year pledge exhausted, with response capacity proven and autonomy still unbought.
