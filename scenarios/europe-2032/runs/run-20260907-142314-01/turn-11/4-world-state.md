### A Major Cyber Incident Tests Fragile Defences

A coordinated, AI-driven cyber intrusion sweeps across southern European public services, disrupting emergency dispatch systems, water treatment controls, and local tax platforms. The attack exploits chained vulnerabilities in legacy software dependencies, with model-generated payloads adapting in real time to bypass static defences. Attribution remains unclear months later, but forensic analysis points to non-state actors leveraging open-weight models fine-tuned on operational data. The incident confirms growing fears: offensive AI capabilities are now widely accessible, and defences are lagging.

### EU Responds with Quiet Planning, Not Rapid Scaling

With political capital critically low, the EU avoids new initiatives. Behind the scenes, ENISA begins drafting criteria for a potential "Trusted Resilience Node" designation, targeting Lyon, Barcelona, and Helsinki as pilot candidates. However, no certifications are issued—auditing protocols, legal waivers, and funding pathways remain under inter-agency review. Bologna, Ljubljana, and Tallinn are identified for future inclusion, but no fast-tracking occurs this turn. The idea gains traction in technical circles, but implementation awaits formal approval and capacity build-up in a future turn.

Meanwhile, the expired Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation leaves no physical infrastructure—but the Commission explores a quiet pivot. Internal memos circulate proposing a Digital Markets Act review to require dominant AI providers to disclose EU energy use and spare capacity. The idea is under legal scrutiny; no formal launch occurs this turn. If approved, such a review could create a covert audit trail and a potential lever for future crisis mobilisation, but for now it remains a contingency concept.

### Trust Erodes Further

Public sentiment dips as job losses in law, accountancy, and software administration mount, with graduates finding few entry-level roles. Media frames AI as both invisible and inescapable. The cyber incident amplifies anxiety, and a growing narrative of dependency—on foreign models, on unaccountable platforms—takes hold. Two member states’ rejection of the European Evaluation Shield’s authority further weakens central legitimacy.

Joint threat response efforts begin with early coordination toward a real-time cyber intelligence pool. While technical discussions advance, operational integration remains incomplete, yielding only a modest resilience signal. With ai_safety falling due to the widening capability-safety gap and no new safeguards landing, the foundation remains brittle.
