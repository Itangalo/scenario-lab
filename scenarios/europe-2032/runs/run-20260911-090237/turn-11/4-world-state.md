### The night the systems stopped
The attack came as a rolling dependency failure that turned into extortion. A widely used management component pushed a tainted update; within hours municipal administrations, regional clinics and logistics firms found files encrypted and backups unreachable. The tooling was clearly machine-made, the blast radius unclear for weeks. Attribution never arrived.

Defenders were behind, then caught up faster than expected. Response teams triaged clean images and pushed behaviour-based patching that had only just left the lab. To get coverage quickly, teams published stripped-down open-weight versions of the detection and patching models so municipal IT staff and clinic contractors could run them locally on isolated networks while central systems were down. The live outage became a harsh test-bed: incident data flowed back each night and the open tooling was retrained and re-released in rapid cycles. Certified hospitals restored in days. Two uncertified utility districts refused the push outright after last spring's breakages, running on paper procedures while operators denounced mandated updates on local radio. Recovery was uneven but visible — screens back on in town halls became the image of the autumn.

### A leap and a lens
In the middle of the outage, a leading lab demonstrated a sharp jump in general competence. Old timelines for multi-day autonomous work collapsed overnight. The same month, an interpretability group published a control result that actually worked on shipped systems — predictions of failure modes before they were seen, with a certificate rather than a promise. Labs adopted it because it reduced their own incidents.

Brussels fused the two: recovery money was tied to deploying the certified fix. Health and interior ministers gave the response agency a joint mandate, paired sampling funds with analysis staff to clear sequencing backlogs, and forced containment drills on the utilities that had rolled back. Concrete for backup compute kept pouring on old budgets. A graduate retraining plan was activated by reallocation, aimed at administrations where office productivity tools had frozen entry hiring without cutting jobs.

The response earned grudging credit but no surge of trust — coordination held, yet delivery was patchy. Squat carriers continued limited trials on fenced factory floors and mine-clearance pilots remained under close supervision, with wider use still blocked by unresolved safety certification and liability questions. The holdout capital took priority recovery aid while keeping its separate supply deal, leaving cohesion still cracked. Public mood soured: relief at restoration mixed with anger that it had happened at all.
