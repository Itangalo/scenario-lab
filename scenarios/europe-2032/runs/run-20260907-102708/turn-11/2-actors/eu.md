## In practice

We are declaring a state of strategic emergency under Article 42 TEU, invoking the solidarity clause to override national objections to rapid siting and grid allocation for sovereign compute infrastructure. The European Commission, through DG GROW and ENISA, is issuing emergency directives to repurpose existing semiconductor fabrication lines in Dresden, Grenoble, and Vilnius for AI-optimised chip production, using emergency powers under the Critical Entities Resilience Directive. We are fast-tracking access to 4.2 GW of reserved grid capacity, reallocating it from decommissioned industrial sites to new sovereign data centres in Finland, Sweden, and Romania, with construction beginning immediately under a new EU Infrastructure Mobilisation Act.

The European Investment Bank is deploying €18 billion in emergency funding — drawn from the Strategic Investment Facility and the Innovation Fund — to finance the first phase of the programme, with co-financing required from member states hosting facilities. We are negotiating binding commitments with ASML and IMEC to transfer non-sanctioned lithography know-how to EU-based fabs under strict IP controls, leveraging the Anti-Coercion Instrument to deter U.S. interference. Simultaneously, we are activating the EU AI Talent Corridor to recruit 1,200 engineers and technicians from diaspora communities and neutral states, offering fast-track residency and security clearance under a new Article 216 TFEU emergency clause.

Resistance is expected from member states with strong U.S. ties and from environmental groups concerned about energy use. To counter this, we are offering binding energy efficiency guarantees — all new compute must operate at under 1.2 PUE — and establishing a Just Compute Transition Fund to support regions affected by the shift. The programme is not optional: without it, the EU ceases to be an actor in its own right. We are no longer building capacity — we are rebuilding the conditions for sovereignty itself.
