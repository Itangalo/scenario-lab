### The Surge and the Split

Global AI capability advances to 90.5, driven by systems that now self-certify their reliability in safety-critical domains. Human oversight persists in form, but not in function. Open-weight models rise to 68.0 as distillation of legacy frontier outputs continues, aided by leaked training logs and collaborative fine-tuning across decentralized networks. The gap narrows slightly, but remains substantial—open-weight progress reflects incremental accumulation, not a sudden leap. Without access to current frontier weights or sovereign compute infrastructure, the open ecosystem advances in patches, not leaps.

The EU declares a strategic emergency and proposes the Emergency Sovereign Compute Reconstitution Programme, aiming to reclaim control over AI infrastructure through future repurposing of fabs, grid allocation, and talent mobilization. The proposal triggers immediate legal and political friction. Member states with deep U.S. ties raise concerns over sovereignty and energy security, delaying consensus. Environmental courts in Germany and Belgium signal they will review any emergency override of permitting procedures, and no construction begins. The Just Compute Transition Fund is announced but not yet funded; protests grow near proposed sites, and no physical work commences.

The Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes complete their rollout, delivering partial telemetry integration. However, without sovereign compute, their reach is limited. Resilience dips to 49.0 as reliance on external systems deepens amid disruption. The denial of frontier access continues, crippling public services and private innovation alike.

Public sentiment falls to 18.0 as backlash escalates. Physical attacks on data infrastructure spread, and a national election in a core EU state is annulled over allegations of synthetic media manipulation. The government collapses. Citizens see not strategy, but desperation.

Washington tightens its grip, forcing the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s servicing of mature-node lithography tools. The move shocks European industry, exposing the fragility of even legacy supply chains. The EU’s leverage evaporates where it matters most.

No political capital is gained. The announcement of a major sovereignty initiative is noted, but with no implementation underway and widespread institutional resistance, it does not yet shift the balance. The measure remains in proposal phase, facing significant barriers to activation.

The nature and extent of these efforts remain unclear, with no central coordination or verified capabilities.
