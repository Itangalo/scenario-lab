The instrument frays at the edges—systems now set their own benchmarks, and human review is ceremonial. This is not general superintelligence, but it is no longer under human direction. Open-weight models reach 66.0, benefiting from compressed knowledge distillation techniques applied to legacy frontier model outputs, though the lack of ongoing access limits refinement. Without full training infrastructure or live data streams, progress is partial and fragile.

The EU completes its **Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes**, now live across energy, finance, and research infrastructure. Integration is partial—two member states withhold full telemetry—but the network begins feeding the new **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory**, hosted in Seville, Stockholm, and Bucharest. Recruitment accelerates under emergency provisions, though legal challenges loom over data sovereignty and oversight.

However, implementation is delayed by inter-institutional disputes over mandate and jurisdiction. No operational capacity is established this turn; planning and feasibility assessments continue under tight deadlines. The launch is deferred, with staffing and authority unresolved.

Yet the moment of action is shadowed by rupture: the Union is abruptly cut off from the leading AI model. Hospitals lose diagnostic support, ministries face delays, and firms scramble. The reason is unstated, the access non-appealable. The denial confirms the fragility of external dependence.

Resilience rises to 50.0 as the Sentinel Nodes complete—category 6-level preparedness begins to land. But political capital remains at 0.0, drained by ongoing costs and the impossibility of new action under such strain. Public sentiment holds at 20.0, unmoved by institutional motion that feels reactive, not restoring.

Sovereignty falls to 14.0—cut off from frontier access, and no domestic capacity yet to substitute. The loss outweighs incremental gains. The EU acts, but the world moves faster.
