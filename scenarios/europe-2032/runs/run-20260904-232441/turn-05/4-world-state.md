### EAAD Launches Amid Crisis
The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) formally launches, gaining legal authority to audit frontier models and delay releases. Operational units activate in Brussels, Seville, and Helsinki, integrating the 87 Blue Card+ recruits and fast-tracking 150 additional specialists. However, staffing gaps persist, and full inspection capacity remains months behind schedule. Still, the agency’s creation marks a turning point in EU oversight ambition.

### Telemetry Mandate Escalates
The EU tightens market access rules, demanding real-time telemetry, on-site inspections, and architectural separation for models above 50.0 capability. The U.S. lab in partial compliance agrees to limited inspections under mutual recognition, but the second refuses, citing national security. The Chinese-linked lab enters formal non-compliance, halting new model approvals. MedVerify is formally recognised as a third-party validator, its findings now triggering mandatory EAAD probes — a symbolic win for public accountability.

### Cyber Shock and Resilience Gaps
A major AI-optimised cyberattack disrupts public services across two member states, exploiting zero-day vulnerabilities generated by model-assisted tooling. The Critical Infrastructure Resilience Shield remains incomplete, with only half of health and energy systems adopting zero-trust architecture. Damage is contained but widespread, reinforcing perceptions of systemic fragility.

### Investment Collapse, Sovereignty Strain
The global AI investment collapse stalls private data centre projects the EU relied on, weakening compute ambitions. Combined with a 2.0-point AI capability rise, this accelerates sovereignty erosion. Political capital dips further under sustained portfolio strain and fallout from the cyber incident. Public sentiment worsens as job losses mount and MedVerify’s reports dominate media, highlighting regulatory blind spots.

### U.S. Election Outcome
The U.S. election is won by the anti-AI platform, marking a sharp inward turn. Washington signals moratoriums on AI in public institutions and cuts support for frontier development. While this reduces immediate dependency pressure, it also destabilises coordination expectations. The administration takes office next turn.
