## Priority
**Priority:** Establish the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) as a finished measure this turn  
Because EAAD reaches completion this turn and is the linchpin of every other safety and sovereignty initiative — from red-teaming to market access enforcement — its operational launch must be decisive, not ceremonial. ## In practice
We are finalising the establishment of the European AI Assurance Directorate as a fully empowered agency with legal authority to delay releases, conduct audits, and demand real-time telemetry from training runs. The Council of the European Union has adopted the final implementing acts under Article 114 TFEU, and the European Parliament has confirmed the first leadership cohort. We are now activating the agency’s operational units in Brussels, Seville, and Helsinki, with immediate responsibility for overseeing the sovereign red-team clusters and enforcing the updated AI Act provisions. To ensure it is not stillborn, we are fast-tracking the integration of the 87 Blue Card+ recruits into EAAD’s core teams and offering binding contracts to an additional 150 interpretability and red-teaming specialists, funded by a €1.8bn reallocation from the Gigafactories co-financing envelope.

Simultaneously, we are escalating pressure on frontier labs through a new enforcement framework: full EU market access for models above 50.0 capability will now require not only real-time telemetry but also on-site inspection rights and verifiable architectural separation between domestic and EU-facing systems. The U.S. lab that previously refused cooperation is being approached through the mutual recognition channel, with access to EU sovereign compute as leverage. The Chinese-linked lab faces a formal non-compliance procedure under the AI Act, with suspension of new model approvals pending. We are also engaging MedVerify as a trusted third-party validator, formalising a cooperation protocol that allows its findings to trigger mandatory EAAD investigations — turning grassroots scrutiny into regulatory force.

On resilience, we are linking the final disbursement of Resilience Shield funds to zero-trust adoption, with a hard deadline for member states to complete implementation in health and energy sectors by the end of turn 6. The cyber_major_incident underscores the urgency: we are now treating AI-optimised attacks as inevitable, and the Shield must be operational before the next wave. Meanwhile, the ai_investment_collapse has created both risk and opportunity — while private compute build-out slows, it also weakens the labs’ bargaining position. We are using this moment to insist on deeper access, knowing that whoever leads the frontier next may not be the one who led it last.
