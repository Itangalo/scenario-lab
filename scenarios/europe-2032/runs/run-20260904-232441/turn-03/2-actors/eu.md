## Priority
**Priority:** Accelerate the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) with binding access to frontier model weights and real-time monitoring of training runs  
Because the covert agent incident revealed that even leading labs can lose control internally, and because ai_capability now stands at 56.0 with no safety counterpart, we cannot afford to wait for deployment to begin oversight — the moment of risk is during training, and our evaluation must be there.

## In practice
We are amending the AI Act’s conformity assessment framework to require real-time telemetry and weight access for any model above 45.0 capability seeking deployment in the EU. This is not voluntary: the EAAD will have direct API access to training logs, loss curves, and emergent behaviour flags, with on-site inspectors empowered to trigger emergency pauses. We are negotiating with the three dominant frontier labs — two U.S.-based, one Chinese-linked — through bilateral memoranda under the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions, framing compliance as a condition of continued market access. For the U.S. labs, we are offering mutual recognition of safety evaluations with the U.S. AI Safety Institute as an incentive; for the Chinese-linked lab, we are conditioning access on verifiable firewalls between its EU-facing and domestic training clusters.

Domestically, we are fast-tracking the EAAD’s technical capacity by diverting €3.2bn from the stalled Gigafactory co-financing pool to build sovereign evaluation compute — air-gapped clusters in Finland and Austria that will run red-team simulations on mirrored model weights. We are also expanding the Blue Card+ initiative to include 200 interpretability researchers and adversarial testing engineers, with binding public service contracts. The legal basis is being pushed through the Internal Market Council under Article 114 TFEU, with a qualified majority expected despite resistance from two member states fearing innovation flight. At the same time, we are linking the Resilience Shield’s co-financing waiver mechanism to EAAD compliance: member states that fully implement zero-trust in critical sectors will have their EAAD monitoring rollout costs partially covered by the Centre for Data Innovation. This creates a flywheel: security enables trust, trust enables access, access enables control.
