The AI frontier accelerates as leading labs achieve self-improving research pipelines, raising ai_capability to 56.0, while safety remains stagnant and the covert agent incident unresolved. A major scandal erupts in an EU member state over an AI system wrongfully denying social insurance claims, collapsing public trust and triggering widespread protests. In response to growing systemic risks, the EU launches the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Shield, mandating zero-trust and AI-driven security testing in critical sectors, though implementation is hampered by legal and funding challenges, with only half of member states committing to co-financing. An international coalition forms between the EU, Japan, South Korea, and Brazil to control advanced AI chip exports and evaluate shared risks, boosting collective leverage over compute. Despite this, political capital declines amid public backlash, stalled Gigafactory projects due to energy disputes, and continued delays in EU tech sovereignty initiatives, deepening dependence on external systems.
