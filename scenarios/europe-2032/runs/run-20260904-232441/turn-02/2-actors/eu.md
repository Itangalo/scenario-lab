## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Shield  
This turn, the cyber intrusion has exposed a lethal gap: we are building sovereign compute and oversight institutions, but our core systems are still brittle. Resilience is no longer a parallel track — it is the immediate condition of sovereignty itself. Without it, even perfect control over AI development will not protect us from harm that arrives through the back door.

## In practice  
We are activating the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Shield through binding amendments to the NIS2 Directive, to be fast-tracked via the Cybersecurity Council formation. The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) will issue updated technical standards requiring all designated operators to implement zero-trust architectures, conduct quarterly AI-red-teamed penetration tests, and feed anonymised intrusion data into a new EU Threat Fusion Cell. Funding of €18bn will be drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with co-financing mandates requiring member states to match 50% of costs — a lever we are using to accelerate national cybersecurity strategies in lagging jurisdictions. We are designating AI-optimised phishing, autonomous lateral movement, and model-poisoning of industrial control systems as Tier 1 threat classes, triggering mandatory reporting and coordinated response protocols.

At the same time, we are re-engaging on the stalled EAAD levy, now reframing it not as a revenue tool but as a condition of market access: non-EU hyperscalers must either accept the 0.5% levy or demonstrate equivalent investment in EU-based resilience infrastructure. This trade-off is being negotiated bilaterally with the US Trade Representative and through the TTC, where we are linking progress on the Data Access Act to concrete commitments on joint cyber defence. Domestically, we are bypassing the CJEU delay by offering hyperscalers a voluntary compliance window — those who pay the levy in advance will receive fast-track evaluation status for their models at EAAD, creating a de facto incentive structure while the legal foundation is reinforced.

The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track, though we are now conditioning final site approvals in Brittany and northern Sweden on the signing of regional resilience compacts — binding agreements that a portion of the compute capacity will be reserved for civil emergency simulations and public-sector continuity operations. This links industrial development directly to societal protection, turning local opposition into co-ownership. We are also accelerating the Blue Card+ initiative through a new pact with European universities and research hospitals, offering fast-track residency to AI safety engineers and cyber-physical systems experts who commit to five years of public service. The race is not only for capability — it is for the people who can defend us when it turns against us.
