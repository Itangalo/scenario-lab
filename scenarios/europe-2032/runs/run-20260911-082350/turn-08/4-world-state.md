### Blockade winter
The halt in advanced chip shipments did not arrive as a memo. It arrived as cancelled delivery slots, force-majeure letters from foundries, and a sudden quiet in procurement channels. Overnight every compute conversation in Brussels became a security conversation. The Netherlands, Japan and Korea answered the phone, but named their own price.

In the middle of that scarcity, the automated attack came. A compromised component, rebuilt loaders written by models, moved from municipal IT into clinics and a grid operator's back office. Screens went dark in town halls that had refused telemetry sharing. Where offline kits and locked backups had been installed under the exploit containment work, systems degraded and came back. Elsewhere they stayed down for days. Attribution teams said months.

Hospitals then got a second shock. Municipal insurers and hospital groups, citing the ransomware sweep and unclear liability for model-assisted outages, paused new cover for civic AI systems. Deployments froze. Administrators postponed triage assistants and scheduling tools pending legal clarity.

Brussels pushed two levers. Emergency triage money stayed tied to telemetry and locked backups, and civil protection teams ran a first live cross-border isolation exercise — cloud cut-offs, payment freezes, mandatory reporting when agents started acquiring resources. It was late, thinly staffed, and it stopped the worst cascade in clinics that cooperated.

In parallel the Commission tabled a joint licensing offer: coordinated controls on lithography, service and spares, pooled procurement in exchange for anchored compute quotas to keep the stalled gigafactory build alive, plus a time-limited liability clarification and backstop to coax insurers back. Partners listened but did not sign. Washington offered sympathy and little capacity of its own.

Warehouses told a different story. Sorting, palletising and yard logistics automated quickly with imported robots and foreign control models, while repair, care and messy construction sites stayed manual. Europe's machine builders felt both the order book and the dependence.

Offices provided the counterpoint. Studies from law, accountancy and newsrooms showed assistants lifting junior output without layoffs. Firms that had cut rehired. Voters felt benefit and breach at once.
