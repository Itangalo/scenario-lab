## In practice  
We are activating the Digital Europe Programme’s cloud envelope and redirecting €12.8bn in unused cohesion funds to launch the EU Sovereign Cloud, a dedicated infrastructure stack for public-sector AI workloads that cannot run on commercial platforms due to jurisdictional or security constraints. The Commission, in coordination with the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU), will issue a binding call for tender by Q2 2027 to establish three core nodes — in Finland, France, and Greece — with full data sovereignty, physical access controls, and air-gapped backup capabilities. These will host models trained under EU supervision and enable secure deployment in defence planning, emergency response, and critical infrastructure optimisation.

The Council’s Telecommunications Council will be asked to fast-track spectrum and grid access under the Tech sovereignty package’s accelerated-permitting zones, while the Commission invokes Article 222 TFEU to classify the cloud as a strategic asset, enabling emergency procurement and overriding national vetoes on site selection. Resistance is expected from member states with existing bilateral cloud deals, particularly Denmark and the Netherlands, who fear duplication. To secure buy-in, we are offering interoperability guarantees and priority access for their national agencies, while conditioning future EU AI funding on adoption of the Sovereign Cloud for sensitive applications. This is not about replacing commercial capacity — it is about ensuring that when the EU must act alone, it can compute alone.
