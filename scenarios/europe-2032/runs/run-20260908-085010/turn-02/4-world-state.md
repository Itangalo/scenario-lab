### A Strategic Leap Amid Mounting Pressure

The EU launches the **EU Sovereign Cloud for Public Sector AI**, a large category 4 measure aimed at securing sovereign compute for sensitive government and research applications. Despite invoking Article 222 TFEU and redirecting €12.8bn in cohesion funds, pushback from Denmark and the Netherlands delays site finalisation. Both nations contest the override of national vetoes, calling it a breach of digital sovereignty. Interoperability guarantees and funding conditionality secure reluctant agreement, but full deployment is now unlikely before turn 5.

The **Emergency Resilience Surge** concludes, having reached 60% coverage across critical infrastructure. While detection systems are now operational in key energy and port networks, uneven integration limits systemic resilience. Still, the partial success yields a modest boost.

### Capability’s Unrelenting Climb

Global AI capability advances by +2.5 to 57.0, driven by compressed release cycles and agent refinement. Openweight capability rises to 43.0, as tighter controls are partially offset by underground redistribution and the **cyber_defence_breakthrough**, which is rapidly replicated in open tooling.

The defensive leap — automated patching and swarm detection — improves operational security across Europe’s networks. Though not a systemic fix, it marks the first visible shift in the offence-defence balance in years, marginally reinforcing confidence in AI-managed systems.

### Gains and Erosion

The concluded resilience measure adds +4 to **resilience**, reaching 45.0. **ai_safety** rises to 39.0, supported by interpretability integration and the defensive breakthrough. However, **eu_ai_sovereignty** declines to 20.0: despite the new cloud initiative, no category 4 measure finishes this turn, and rising capability costs −1. The **emergent_global_ai_accord** strengthens diplomatic positioning but yields no immediate sovereignty gain.

Political capital falls to 39.0. Three large measures in flight cost −9, the priority −1, with no completion bonus. The new Sovereign Cloud earns +3 for addressing dependency risks amid rising capability, but this is offset by ongoing strain. Public sentiment holds at 42.0 — no backlash, but no visible benefit yet.
