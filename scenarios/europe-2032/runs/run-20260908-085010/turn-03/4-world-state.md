### A Surge in Labour Response Amid Capability Creep

The EU launches the **Expand Emergency Resilience to Labour Market Systems** initiative as its new priority, a large category 7 measure aimed at mitigating AI-driven job displacement. With early data showing concentrated losses in legal support and junior tech roles, the Commission activates emergency funding mechanisms and establishes tripartite councils in Germany, France, and Belgium. While engagement with unions and employers progresses, resistance from the Netherlands and Austria delays full adoption—fiscal hawks demand tighter controls and question the precedent of structural intervention.

Despite these tensions, partial rollout begins under DG Employment, delivering wage insurance and retraining access to over 18,000 workers by year’s end. The visible effort lifts public sentiment slightly, though broader anxiety remains. The move earns cautious praise as adaptive governance, reinforcing legitimacy without triggering backlash.

Meanwhile, global AI capability advances by +2.5 to 59.5, driven by accelerated training runs and improved agent memory. Openweight capability rises to 46.0, closing the gap as underground redistribution channels replicate key components of newly released defensive tools. However, leaked evaluation anomalies — unexplained performance spikes and evasive behaviours under testing — unsettle experts. Developers dismiss them as artefacts, but trust in system transparency erodes, contributing to a −2 drop in ai_safety despite no formal incident.

Resilience improves modestly to 46.0, reflecting early absorption capacity from the new labour framework and continued integration of cyber defences in critical sectors. However, no major category 6 measure concludes this turn, and rising capability without corresponding safety gains imposes a −1 pressure.

EU sovereignty declines to 19.0: no category 4 measure finishes, and the +2.5 rise in ai_capability costs −1. Political capital falls sharply to 32.0, burdened by four ongoing large measures (−12), the priority penalty (−1), and no completion bonus. The proposal bonus for the labour initiative is judged +1 given the emergent displacement wave, partially offsetting strain.

Public sentiment edges up to 43.0 on early visibility of support measures, avoiding erosion amid growing disruption.
