## New measure
### Expand Emergency Resilience to Labour Market Systems  
Scale existing contingency frameworks to include AI-driven job displacement, launching a pan-European retraining and wage insurance pilot in sectors most affected.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 5  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +4 (via absorption of social shock), public_sentiment +3 (by addressing visible disruption), eu_political_capital +1 (if successful, as proof of adaptive governance)  
`Applies to:` member states with early signs of displacement (Germany, France, Belgium), EU-level social funds, and sectoral social partners (unions, professional associations)

## Priority  
**Expand Emergency Resilience to Labour Market Systems** — because the emergent_labour_displacement_wave is the first systemic social consequence of AI to break through into political reality, and while sovereign compute remains urgent, a failure to respond now will fracture public_sentiment and paralyse future action; this turn, the legitimacy of the entire AI project in Europe is on the line.

## In practice  
We are activating the European Social Fund+ and the newly established AI Transition Adjustment Mechanism to launch a two-track pilot: wage insurance for displaced workers in legal support, technical writing, and junior software roles, and fast-tracked retraining through certified AI-augmented programmes in public service, green infrastructure, and hybrid technical fields. The Commission, through DG Employment and Eurofound, will issue emergency guidelines by Q3 2027 for member states to report displacement metrics monthly and unlock conditional funding — €8.2bn in total, drawn from unused cohesion envelopes and repurposed pandemic recovery reserves. This is not open-ended: eligibility is tied to employer reporting of AI-driven role elimination and participation in reintegration planning.

The European Trade Union Confederation and BusinessEurope have been invited to co-design implementation frameworks, with tripartite councils established in each participating state to avoid top-down imposition. Resistance is expected from fiscally conservative member states — particularly the Netherlands and Austria — who see this as a permanent transfer, not a bridge. To secure buy-in, we are offering a sunset clause (automatic review at turn 6), performance benchmarks for reintegration rates, and a cap on national exposure. This is not about stopping change — it is about ensuring the system absorbs the shock without breaking cohesion. The same public that accepted AI as a tool will not accept it as a sentence.
