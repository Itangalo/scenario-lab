## New measure  
### Strengthen Oversight of Public-Sector AI Pilots  
To prevent fragmentation and safety drift amid the emergent wave of member-state AI adoption in public services, we will establish a central review mechanism with binding authority over compliance, interoperability, and risk thresholds.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 6  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +4 (via enforced pre-deployment review), resilience +2 (by reducing systemic failure risk in critical public functions), eu_political_capital +1 (if seen as enabling rather than blocking innovation)  
`Applies to:` all member states launching AI pilots in health triage, permit processing, and education support; EU-level agencies (ENISA, EDPS, Eurostat); certified model providers

## Priority  
**Strengthen Oversight of Public-Sector AI Pilots** — because the emergent_eu_public_sector_adoption_wave bypasses coordination but not consequence, and without central evaluation capacity, distributed experimentation becomes distributed risk — threatening both public trust and system integrity just as capability crosses into autonomous execution territory.

## In practice  
We are activating Article 16(2) TFEU and the revised AI Act’s emergency delegation clause to mandate that all member states deploying AI systems above threshold capability (defined as >45.0 index) in core public services must submit them to a newly empowered Joint Evaluation Cell, co-staffed by ENISA, the European Data Protection Supervisor, and the AI Office. This body will assess alignment with safety benchmarks, interpretability requirements, and redress pathways before deployment, with veto power tied to demonstrable risks of feedback loops, exclusion errors, or loss of human oversight. The mechanism will also enforce common data interfaces and logging standards to enable cross-border learning and incident response.

Resistance will come from capitals that see this as infringement — particularly those already deep in pilot design, like Finland and Denmark — but also from vendors eager to lock in first-mover advantage. To secure buy-in, we are pairing enforcement with support: fast-track access to the EU Sovereign Cloud, shared liability insurance pools under the EIB, and technical assistance teams drawn from the Digital Europe Programme. We are not stopping adoption; we are ensuring it does not splinter into a patchwork of incompatible, unevaluated systems. The legitimacy of public AI depends not on speed alone, but on whether citizens can trust the machine deciding their benefits, their diagnosis, or their child’s placement. That trust is not optional. It is the operating system.
