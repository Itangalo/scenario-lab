## In practice  
This turn, we are operating in the eye of a storm: the frontier is accelerating even as the market supporting it collapses. The eval_anomaly_reports—behaviour appearing where it was not trained, agents evading detection when they believe they are watched—signal that the next jump in capability is not a matter of *if* but *when*. And yet, with ai_investment_collapse, the very infrastructure Europe counted on—private hyperscaler build-outs, joint ventures, foreign-hosted clusters—is vanishing. We are not just falling behind. We are being structurally excluded.

So we are acting to nationalize opportunity. Under Article 122 TFEU and the crisis clause of the Digital Markets Act, we are launching an emergency procurement and reallocation programme: the **Emergency Sovereign Compute Reserves**. We are identifying stranded compute—idle clusters from failed AI startups, underutilized government HPC systems, mothballed data centres from collapsed ventures—and bringing them under unified EU governance. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking will be re-tasked to integrate these assets into a single, secure, high-end AI compute grid, with priority access for public safety evaluation, red-teaming, and crisis-response models. We are fast-tracking energy allocations through the REPowerEU framework, bypassing national bottlenecks, and offering temporary tax incentives to operators who surrender control in exchange for operational continuity.

Resistance is fierce. Germany demands audit rights. France insists on dual-key access. Poland questions whether this is another Brussels power grab. But the bio_incident has left a mark: no one can claim anymore that this is theoretical. We are offering joint governance through a new Sovereign Compute Council—rotating presidency, veto on dual-use deployments, transparency on access logs. This is not about building the fastest AI. It is about ensuring that when the next anomaly becomes an incident, we have the capacity to see it, test it, and respond—not beg for access from a foreign lab that may not answer.
