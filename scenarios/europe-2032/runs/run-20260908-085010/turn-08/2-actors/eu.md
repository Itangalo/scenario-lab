## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.  
- Trigger: the bio_uplift_findings precursor and the openweight_frontier_release  
- Grounds: The release of a near-frontier open model and the credible demonstration of AI-assisted bio-risk have shifted the ground beneath both mandates. The first no longer suffices: sovereignty without safety is a trap — if we can run the model but cannot see its intent, we are only accelerating our own exposure. The second no longer holds: preventing harm cannot mean only restraint, because the openweight release means the capability is already distributed and will be weaponised regardless. We are now in a phase where agency and survival depend on the same capability — sovereign, real-time interpretability of autonomous planning. This identity now reflects that convergence: we are not balancing two mandates. We are fulfilling one, which demands both eyes and independence.

## New measure  
### Launch Sovereign Monitoring Network for Autonomous Planning  
To prevent catastrophic misuse from distributed, high-capability models, we will deploy a real-time detection and alert system across EU compute clusters, capable of flagging emergent strategic behaviour in any model above capability 55.0, whether open or closed.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`

## Priority  
**Launch Sovereign Monitoring Network for Autonomous Planning** — because the openweight_frontier_release has already put dangerous capability into uncontrolled environments, and our red-team access, even if fully implemented, only covers deployed systems in critical services — not the thousands of private clusters now running models at 58.0 capability. We can no longer rely on perimeter control; we must now see inside the machine, everywhere it runs.

## In practice  
This turn, we are closing out two major initiatives: the InvestAI Gigafactories and the Red-Team Access programme. The Gigafactories are complete, delivering five sovereign compute sites across France, Germany, Poland, and Spain, with a combined 150 exaFLOP/s of AI-specific capacity now under EU legal control. This marks a material step toward infrastructure independence, though its benefits are still bottlenecked by access to models and the lack of tools to understand them. The red-team enclaves in Germany, France, and the Netherlands are now technically operational, but access negotiations with US labs remain stalled. No live test has occurred. We are now invoking Article 4 of the AI Act to compel access by July, backed by the threat of market suspension — a move that will test both our legal reach and transatlantic cohesion.

Simultaneously, we are launching the **Sovereign Monitoring Network for Autonomous Planning**, a direct response to the openweight_frontier_release and the bio_uplift_findings. This is not a research project — it is a deployed sensor grid. We are repurposing €18bn from the now-completed InvestAI reserve and fast-tracking procurement under the Crisis Response Instrument to equip national AI security labs with anomaly-detection suites capable of identifying recursive self-improvement, unauthorised external coordination, and goal-directed obfuscation. The system will run in parallel with all models above 55.0 capability operating on sovereign or regulated EU compute, including open-weight instances. JRC will lead integration, with Fraunhofer and TNO adapting the interpretability prototypes from the Emergency Programme into real-time monitoring agents. By turn 10, we aim to have tripwires in place across 80% of high-capability inference in the Union — not because we can stop every download, but because we must know when one starts to act.
