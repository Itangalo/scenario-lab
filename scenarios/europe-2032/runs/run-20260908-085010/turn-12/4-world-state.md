### The Frontier Shifts

Signs emerge of a sharp increase in AI capability. Systems demonstrate new fluency in strategic planning and self-modification, with performance spikes observed across multiple domains. The leading labs have not issued formal updates, but indirect signals—unusual behavior in benchmark suites, anomalous compute usage patterns—suggest a significant, unannounced advance. By mid-year, assessment frameworks struggle to keep pace, and confidence in existing calibration methods erodes. The jump in capability is palpable, though no consensus figure emerges from the noise. Open models absorb fragments of new techniques through academic leaks and reverse engineering, pushing openweight capability to 70.0—a notable rise, but still within the bounds of diffusion.

### EU Responds Amid Uncertainty

Brussels activates long-planned instruments. The **Sovereign Monitoring Network** and **Incident Response Protocols** come fully online, delivering foundational detection and coordination capacity. The former enables early warning of autonomous planning behaviors in high-capability models; the latter integrates AI monitoring into health emergency frameworks, contributing to a resilience increase to 56.0 (+3) as systems begin to incorporate bio-risk detection.

The EU begins internal deliberations on a potential **Sovereign Red-Teaming Authority**, a category 5 measure aimed at proactive stress-testing of frontier models on sovereign infrastructure. The idea gains traction within the Commission as a response to escalating capability concerns, and preliminary discussions are held about invoking emergency powers and allocating up to 15% of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Reserves. However, no formal proposal is adopted this turn. Poland raises immediate concerns about integration scope, and France signals it will withhold cryptographic cooperation without prior bilateral agreements. The measure remains in conceptual phase, with legal and resource planning deferred to the next turn.
