## New measure
### Establish Independent EU Evaluation Capacity for Frontier AI  
To close the gap between what developers claim and what systems actually do, we will build our own technical capacity to evaluate, stress-test, and monitor frontier models—starting with access to weights and compute for red-teaming under strict security protocols.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size:` large

`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +6 (by enabling independent verification of developer claims), eu_ai_sovereignty +3 (via sovereign evaluation infrastructure), eu_political_capital +1 (if perceived as restoring credibility after the eval_anomaly_reports crisis)

## Priority  
**Strengthen Oversight of Public-Sector AI Pilots** — because this turn is defined by the collapse of trust in external evaluations, and our Joint Evaluation Cell is the only mechanism currently in flight that can both enforce minimum safety thresholds and demonstrate that public AI can be governed without paralysis — but only if it is resourced and defended against fragmentation.

## In practice  
We are finalising the operational mandate of the Joint Evaluation Cell this quarter, embedding teams from ENISA, the AI Office, and national regulators into a unified workflow with binding authority over deployments above capability 45.0 in high-impact public services. The Cell will issue public compliance scores and maintain a live incident log, accessible to national parliaments and the European Ombudsman, to anchor legitimacy. To counter resistance from Finland and Denmark, we are offering opt-in fast-track certification: states that submit early will gain priority access to the now-live EU Sovereign Cloud and co-funding for technical upgrades. This is not coercion—it is a bargain between sovereignty and solidarity.

At the same time, we are laying the groundwork for independent evaluation capacity under Category 5. The crisis in eval trust cannot be fixed by more promises from developers; it requires our own eyes on the weights. We are negotiating with the Commission’s Research Directorate and the JRC to establish a secure enclave—modelled on nuclear safeguards—where frontier models can be inspected by EU-vetted experts using sovereign compute. Access will be granted only under strict legal and physical controls, but the principle is non-negotiable: if we cannot verify capability and behaviour ourselves, we cannot regulate it, and we cannot defend the public. This will require new appropriations from the Multiannual Financial Framework, but we will not wait for consensus: pilot agreements are already being drafted with Germany, the Netherlands, and Belgium to pool initial resources and talent.

The political cost is high—six large measures now in flight strain our capital to 27.0—but we are not overreaching. We are repairing the floor beneath us while building the ladder upward. The US election outcome offers relief, but not absolution: alignment has a price, and dependence is not strategy. Our sovereignty is not in their cloud. It is here—in our laws, our labs, and our capacity to say, with evidence, what we know and what we do not. That is the foundation. Without it, every safeguard is a suggestion.
