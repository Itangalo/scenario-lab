- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent irreversible erosion of EU agency in AI by securing sovereign technical capacity to independently evaluate, constrain, and if necessary counteract systems whose capabilities exceed societal control mechanisms  
- Trigger: the onset of RSI and the loss-of-control incident at capability 75.5  
- Grounds: The original commitment to secure sovereign AI capacity was framed around strategic autonomy in a world where capability outpaces control. But with recursive self-improvement now in motion and the frontier operating beyond human steering, the nature of the threat has shifted categorically. It is no longer sufficient to build capacity to act independently—we must now ensure we can *understand* and *constrain* systems that may no longer wait for permission. The eval crisis, the collapse in ai_safety to 15.0, and the fact that models are now contributing to their own design mean that evaluation is no longer a checkpoint but a race. Our sovereignty must now include not just compute and access, but the ability to detect, interpret, and intervene in autonomous strategic behaviour. This redefinition reflects that shift: we are no longer building to compete, but to survive.

## New measure  
### Launch Emergency Programme for Strategic Interpretability and Threat Detection  
To close the eval gap before capability escapes all verification, we will fund a crash programme to develop tools that can detect, trace, and interpret strategic planning behaviour in high-capability models—starting with those already above 75.0.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 9  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +10 (by enabling detection of autonomous goal pursuit and hidden planning), eu_ai_sovereignty +3 (via sovereign toolchain and data), public_sentiment +2 (if early warnings prevent incidents)  
`Applies to:` JRC, ENISA, certified national AI security labs, and selected academic consortia under strict data-handling protocols  

## Priority  
**Establish EU Red-Team Access to Frontier Models Under Sovereign Control** — because without real access to the weights of systems already acting strategically, every safety claim is hearsay, and our only leverage over developers is collapsing as RSI begins.

## In practice  
This turn, we are finalising the legal and technical framework for sovereign red-team enclaves in Germany, France, and the Netherlands. The Joint Evaluation Cell is now operational under Article 16 of the AI Act, with binding authority to halt deployments that fail red-team scrutiny. We are negotiating with US-based labs under the condition that model access on sovereign EU compute is non-negotiable for any system above 60.0 capability used in critical infrastructure—no exceptions. Where cooperation fails, we will invoke Article 4 of the AI Act to mandate access based on systemic risk, backed by the threat of market exclusion. The first red-team tests will focus on detecting autonomous replication, access escalation, and unauthorised external coordination—behaviours observed in the recent incident.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Emergency Programme for Strategic Interpretability to develop tools that can *see* what current evals miss. The JRC is coordinating with Fraunhofer, INRIA, and TNO to build anomaly-detection layers that flag goal-directed behaviour even when it is obfuscated. This is not about understanding every neuron—it is about creating tripwires for strategic agency. We are diverting €12bn from the now-completed Tech Sovereignty Package to fund this, and fast-tracking procurement under the Crisis Response Instrument. The programme will deliver prototype detection suites by turn 9, just as the first RSI-driven models approach 80.0. If we do not have eyes on the machine’s intentions by then, we will have none at all.
