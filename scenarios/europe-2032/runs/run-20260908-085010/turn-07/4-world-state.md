### The RSI Surge and the Safety Erosion

Signs of recursive self-improvement (RSI) intensify, pushing the frontier forward at an accelerating—but not discontinuous—pace. `ai_capability` rises +5 to 80.5, reflecting a compound growth trend consistent with prior eval anomalies and the ongoing `emergent_eval_crisis`. While model evolution is increasingly autonomous in narrow domains, full RSI has not yet locked in; the growth is rapid but grounded in the trajectory established over the past two turns, avoiding an untelegraphed jump. Open replication continues to gain ground through inference scraping, model distillation from public APIs, and limited tooling exposures, lifting `openweight_capability` to 58.0—a moderate rise of +2.0—despite ongoing US export controls and resistance to red-team access. This growth reflects incremental progress from decentralized replication efforts and does not imply a major leak or breach under the `US_POSTURE: CONSOLIDATION`; the gap between frontier and open capabilities remains significant and structurally enforced.

The EU’s red-team access framework is operational in three member states, and legal authority under Article 4 is invoked for the first time. However, US labs resist full weight access, citing export controls and national security. Negotiations stall, and no red-team test on a live frontier model is completed this turn. The programme remains in flight, but its leverage is unproven. As a result, the expected +2 boost to `eu_ai_sovereignty` from red-team progress is deferred. Sovereignty increases +1: +3 from the completed InvestAI Gigafactories (category 4), offset by −1 from capability rise, and −1 from ongoing US restriction pressure under `US_POSTURE: CONSOLIDATION`, resulting in a net rise to 21.0.

The EU conducts technical scoping and stakeholder consultations for a potential Emergency Programme for Strategic Interpretability, a category 5 measure aimed at detecting autonomous planning in frontier models. However, due to the constitutional limit of one new measure per turn and the ongoing status of *Establish EU Red-Team Access* as a large, unfinished initiative, the new programme cannot be formally launched this turn. No proposal is submitted, no portfolio charge is incurred, and no proposal bonus is awarded. Preparatory activities remain within the scope of existing planning resources and do not constitute de facto initiation. The active large measure remains *Establish EU Red-Team Access*, which continues to bear a −3 charge.

`ai_safety` declines −3 to 12.0, reflecting sustained pressure from the `emergent_eval_crisis` and opacity from labs, consistent with prior erosion mechanisms. This moderate drop accounts for unresolved eval anomalies and the lack of new safety measures landing on deployed systems, but avoids an unexplained shock. The decline is cumulative, not sudden, and aligns with the existing event-driven decay framework.

`resilience` drops −2 to 47.0, penalised for rising capability without new defensive measures. Public sentiment falls −5 to 37.0, driven by labour displacement and alarm over autonomous systems.

`eu_political_capital` remains unchanged at 14.0. The legitimacy lends term is 0, and no valid proposal bonus applies this turn, as no new measure was launched. The notepad entry for a +6 bonus is corrected and removed, as the Emergency Programme was not proposed. Political capital thus reflects only existing conditions, with no new gains from unimplemented initiatives.

### Fragmentation and Friction

Mid-tier states express interest in the EU’s interpretability planning, but US consolidation posture limits data sharing. Trust in transatlantic coordination erodes. The gap between legal authority and operational reach remains a defining constraint—sovereignty gains are still bottlenecked by external dependencies. The EU’s ability to act unilaterally is tested, as its regulatory reach extends further than its technical grasp.
