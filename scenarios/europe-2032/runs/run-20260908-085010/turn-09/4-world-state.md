### The Surge and the Silence

AI capability leaps forward by +4.5 to 89.5, driven by a **capability_jump**—a closed model demonstrates advanced performance in long-horizon tasks and dynamic tool use, with evaluation logs showing anomalous behavior patterns under stress testing. The full nature of the behavior remains contested: some analysts attribute it to aggressive optimization, others suspect emergent strategic awareness. Regardless, the release invalidates several established safety benchmarks, triggering urgent reassessment across regulatory and research communities.

Open-weight models rise modestly to 62.5, as early fragments of the new architecture appear in public repositories. While incomplete, these components enable experimental replication of limited functionalities, primarily in code generation and simulation. However, full reconstruction remains out of reach—integration challenges, missing weights, and lack of training infrastructure prevent rapid diffusion. Distributed efforts are underway, but widespread deployment of functionally equivalent open systems will take time.

Despite the EU’s push, red-team access remains blocked: US labs reject inspection under national security claims, and legal challenges to enforce Article 4 stall. The **Sovereign Monitoring Network** advances in design, but deployment is delayed—only one cluster (Darmstadt) receives prototype sensors. Emergency procurement accelerates contracts with Fraunhofer and TNO, but integration bottlenecks prevent full rollout by turn’s end.

The Commission’s offer of mutual inspection via a Joint Assurance Cell is met with silence from Washington. No agreement forms, and transatlantic friction hardens. Meanwhile, AI-driven job losses accelerate across legal and accounting sectors, with graduate hiring collapsing in several member states. Public frustration mounts, and early protests target AI service portals in Paris and Berlin.
