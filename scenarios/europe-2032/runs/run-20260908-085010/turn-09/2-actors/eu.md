## In practice  
This turn, we are operating under extreme capital scarcity — just 14.0 — and cannot afford drift. The completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories gives us physical compute independence, but without tools to observe or govern what runs on it, the infrastructure serves others’ models more than our own sovereignty. The red-team access programme, though formally complete, delivered no actual access: US labs invoked national security exemptions, and Article 4 enforcement remains stalled in the Council Legal Service over proportionality concerns. We are now where we feared: holding the hardware, but not the insight.

We are therefore doubling down on the **Sovereign Monitoring Network**, treating it as a live emergency response rather than a research rollout. This week, the Commission invokes Article 21 of the Crisis Response Instrument to fast-track procurement for the interpretability sensor grid. JRC will issue three direct contracts — one to Fraunhofer for inference-time attention-tracing modules, one to TNO for coordination anomaly detectors, and one to a consortium led by INRIA for cryptographic watermarking of planning traces. These bypass normal competition rules under the new emergency authority we are expanding today. The goal is to have working tripwires installed in at least three sovereign clusters — in Lyon, Darmstadt, and Warsaw — by December, covering 35% of high-capability inference in the Union.

At the same time, transatlantic pressure mounts. Washington signals that any unilateral enforcement of Article 4 against US labs could trigger reciprocal restrictions on European cloud operations. We are responding through quiet channels: offering mutual inspection rights under a new EU-US Joint Assurance Cell, but only if they allow one live red-team session by November. The offer is narrow, urgent, and deliberately low-profile — we do not want escalation, but we cannot accept indefinite deferral. If they refuse, we proceed alone — and prepare for the cost.
