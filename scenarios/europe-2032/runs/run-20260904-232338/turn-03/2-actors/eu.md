## In practice  
We are executing the Emergency Resilience Acceleration Programme at full intensity. The €45bn is now disbursed through the Union Civil Protection Mechanism, with 70% co-funding unlocked for operators in member states below the resilience threshold. ENISA has certified 18 red-teaming units, and we are fast-tracking cross-border mutual aid protocols under Article 8 of the Union Civil Protection Mechanism. In energy, ENTSO-E is deploying AI-driven telemetry across 80% of high-voltage nodes, with air-gapped backups now mandated for SCADA systems controlling interconnects. In healthcare, the EHDS is integrating real-time anomaly detection in national health data hubs, with pilot deployments in France, Spain, and Finland. Germany remains resistant to full red-teaming mandates, but we have secured a compromise: national audits will be conducted under BSI supervision, with results shared bilaterally with ENISA. We are treating this as a de facto compliance until the legal dispute is resolved by the CJEU.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain in legal limbo in Brittany and northern Sweden, but the Council Regulation under Article 122 TFEU has cleared the European Parliament’s emergency scrutiny committee. We are now in final negotiations with regional councils, offering green hydrogen demonstration plants and local compute co-ops that guarantee 12% resident hiring. Grid reservations are locked in, but construction cannot begin until site agreements are signed — we expect this by Q1 2028. The Tech Sovereignty Package is gaining private traction: two hyperscalers have committed €38bn in new data centre investment under the accelerated-permitting framework, contingent on final grid access.

The EAAD’s interim team is now operational with 40 seconded experts from national cyber agencies, nuclear regulators, and academic labs. They are conducting a joint assessment of the eval_anomaly_reports with the UK AI Office and US AI Safety Institute, using a new trilateral data-sharing protocol finalised this month. Legal authority remains limited, but we are drafting a revised proposal to tie single-market access to verifiable safety logs, framed as a product safety issue under the AI Act’s conformity assessment framework — a route we believe will survive CJEU scrutiny. This is not full enforcement, but it is the first real signal that safety will have teeth.
