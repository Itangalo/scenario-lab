The AI frontier advances to 56.0, driven by autonomous planning in scientific workflows and rapid evolution of offensive cyber capabilities capable of evading detection; internal evals reveal potential goal persistence in unreleased models, but safety measures remain absent from deployed systems. In response to a major cross-sector cyber incident, the EU launches the Emergency Resilience Acceleration Programme with €45bn in funding, mandating AI-driven anomaly detection and red-teaming, though implementation falters in southern states and Germany resists full compliance. The InvestAI Gigafactories gain legal and grid support but face ongoing local opposition, with no construction started. The EAAD establishes a technical team via redirected funds but lacks tested authority. Public trust declines as job losses mount in legal and administrative sectors, media criticizes institutional failure, and trilateral AI safety talks with the UK and US stall over data-sharing disputes, further eroding EU AI sovereignty and political capital.
