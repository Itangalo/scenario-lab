## New measure  
**Measure:** Emergency Resilience Acceleration Programme  
A rapid, targeted hardening of EU critical infrastructure against AI-optimised cyber threats, focusing on energy, healthcare, and public administration.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 12–15 points; reduce incident recovery time from months to weeks; close known detection gaps in network behaviour analysis  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, with binding standards and co-funding through the Union Civil Protection Mechanism

## Priority  
**Priority:** Emergency Resilience Acceleration Programme

## In practice  
We are activating the Emergency Resilience Acceleration Programme through the Union Civil Protection Mechanism, with a €45bn emergency allocation drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the newly unlocked margins in the MFF after the German finance ministry’s concession. The programme targets three sectors: energy grid operators, national health data systems, and core public administration networks. Binding technical standards will be issued under Article 114 TFEU, requiring real-time anomaly detection using AI-driven network telemetry, air-gapped backup systems for critical control logic, and mandatory red-teaming every quarter by ENISA-certified units. Co-funding covers 70% of compliance costs for operators in member states below the 70.0 resilience threshold, with full Union funding for cross-border interconnects.

At the same time, we are pushing the InvestAI Gigafactories forward through a new legal instrument: a Council Regulation under Article 122 TFEU for economic emergency, which we are invoking jointly with the Net-Zero Industry Act to override local permitting delays in Brittany and northern Sweden. The Commission has secured a political deal with ENTSO-E to lock in 15GW of reserved grid capacity, conditional on member states finalising site agreements by the end of Q2. We are offering regional reinvestment packages — including green hydrogen hubs and local compute co-ops — to neutralise local opposition, and have committed 12% of operational jobs to residents, enforced through national labour inspections.

The EAAD remains stalled, but we are shifting tactics: instead of waiting for the CJEU to rule on the hyperscaler levy, we are redirecting €1.2bn from unspent cohesion funds to launch its core evaluation team on an interim basis. We are seconding 40 experts from national cyber agencies, nuclear regulators, and academic AI safety labs to stand up red-team units focused on agent coordination and emergent behaviour. Their first task will be to assess the eval_anomaly_reports in coordination with the UK’s AI Office and the US’s AI Safety Institute, under a new trilateral data-sharing protocol we are finalising this month. Legal authority remains limited, but we are preparing a revised proposal for extraterritorial reach that ties access to the single market to verifiable safety logs — a move we expect to survive CJEU scrutiny if framed as a product safety issue.
