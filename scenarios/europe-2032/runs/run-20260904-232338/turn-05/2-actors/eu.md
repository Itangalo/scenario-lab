## New measure
**Measure:** Ban public distribution of AI models capable of autonomous design of high-risk biological agents or cyber tools  
A legally binding prohibition on the release, distribution, or public hosting of AI models that, when evaluated under standard red-team protocols, demonstrate capability to autonomously design functional viral vectors, novel toxins, or zero-day exploit chains without human guidance.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`

## Priority
**Priority:** New measure  
This turn, the emergent_bio_open_source_breakthrough proves that open-weight models can now generate viable designs for biological agents, and the openweight_frontier_release shows that such capabilities spread faster than regulation can follow. Containing open-weight risk is no longer a precaution — it is an emergency. We must act now to remove the most dangerous capabilities from circulation, even if it means restricting research freedom, because the alternative is irreversible harm.

## In practice
We are treating the new biological design capability in open models as a Category A biosecurity incident. The ECDC has convened an emergency task force with BfR, Sciensano, and KI, and we are fast-tracking a joint assessment under the Health Security Committee to define technical thresholds for “autonomous design” of viral vectors. This will feed into the drafting of the new restriction, which we are preparing under Article 16 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU, invoking public health and security. We are coordinating with ENISA to align the cyber-risk threshold with existing NIS2 red-team benchmarks, ensuring consistency across domains. The legal service is assessing whether the AI Act’s high-risk classification can be extended via delegated act to cover open-weight models with verified dual-use potential, but we are preparing a standalone regulation as backup, to be proposed under the civil protection framework if needed.

The EAAD’s interim team is leading a rapid evaluation of the Eastern European model using a joint protocol with the Paul-Ehrlich-Institut and the UK HSA, with results expected by November. We are also activating the Anti-Coercion Instrument in preparatory mode, warning jurisdictions that host unregulated AI collectives they may face restrictions on data flows or research collaboration if they do not cooperate with EU safety audits. Behind the scenes, we are negotiating with major open-source hubs — Hugging Face, EleutherAI, and the Open Model Alliance — to establish a voluntary pre-release review process, offering liability protection and compute grants in exchange for compliance. But we will not wait for consensus: if the assessment confirms autonomous design capability, the ban will be proposed by December, even at the cost of backlash from the research community. This is not about stopping innovation — it is about ensuring that innovation does not outpace survival.
