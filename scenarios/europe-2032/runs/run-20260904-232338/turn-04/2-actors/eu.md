## New measure  
**Measure:** Establish the European Secure Compute Corridor  
A legally binding framework requiring all AI systems above capability threshold 50.0 used in critical infrastructure to run exclusively on sovereign, audited compute clusters with physical access restricted to EU-authorised personnel and real-time monitoring for unauthorised model exfiltration or remote control.

## Priority  
**Priority:** Emergency Resilience Acceleration Programme  
This turn, the cyber_major_incident has materialised — real cascading failures in health and energy, with recovery measured in weeks and attribution still unclear — and we cannot build sovereignty on a collapsing foundation; securing the present is the only way to have a future to determine.

## In practice  
We are executing the Emergency Resilience Acceleration Programme at maximum intensity, with full disbursement of the €45bn through the Union Civil Protection Mechanism. ENISA has certified 18 red-teaming units, and we are activating cross-border mutual aid under Article 8, treating the ransomware event as a cross-border emergency. In energy, ENTSO-E is deploying AI-driven telemetry across 80% of high-voltage nodes, with air-gapped backups now mandated for SCADA systems controlling interconnects — a binding directive issued under the NIS2 framework. In healthcare, the EHDS anomaly detection layer is being fast-tracked, with pilot integrations in France, Spain, and Finland now live and feeding into a centralised threat observatory hosted by the EU Health Emergency Committee. Germany remains resistant to full red-teaming mandates, but we have secured a compromise: national audits under BSI supervision, with results shared bilaterally with ENISA — a de facto compliance until the CJEU resolves the legal dispute.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain in legal limbo in Brittany and northern Sweden, but the Council Regulation under Article 122 TFEU has passed the European Parliament’s emergency scrutiny committee. We are now in final negotiations with regional councils, offering green hydrogen demonstration plants and local compute co-ops that guarantee 12% resident hiring. Grid reservations are locked in, and we expect site agreements by Q2 2028. The Tech Sovereignty Package is gaining private traction: two hyperscalers have committed €38bn in new data centre investment under the accelerated-permitting framework, contingent on final grid access. The EAAD’s interim team is operational with 40 seconded experts and is conducting a joint assessment of the eval_anomaly_reports with the UK AI Office and US AI Safety Institute. We are drafting a revised proposal to tie single-market access to verifiable safety logs under the AI Act’s conformity assessment — a route we believe will survive CJEU scrutiny. The Middle-Power AI Coordination Framework is now in formal negotiation: we have circulated a draft treaty text and are pushing for a ministerial signing at the autumn summit. This is not yet funded, but we are repurposing €2.1bn from the Digital Europe Programme to cover initial evaluation-sharing infrastructure.
