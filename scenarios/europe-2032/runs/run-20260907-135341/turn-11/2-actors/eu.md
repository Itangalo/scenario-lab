## In practice
We are acting on two fronts: enforcement and foundation. On enforcement, we press forward with the EU Border for AI Systems, now codified under Article 114 TFEU and backed by the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions. The three EAAN certification nodes are being staffed through emergency secondments from national cyber agencies and vetted academic partners, using fast-track security clearances under the Crisis Regulation Procedure. We are insisting on telemetry access, but the rejections from US labs expose a deeper truth — we can demand transparency, but without our own means to run or replicate these models, our demands are ultimately negotiable. That ends now.

This turn, we launch the Sovereign Compute Initiative under the newly activated Strategic Infrastructure Authority, pooling €18bn from redirected recovery funds, defence contingency reserves, and conditional contributions from member states offering land and grid access. We are negotiating binding site agreements with two major chipmakers and one decommissioned energy plant in northern France to repurpose for liquid-cooled AI clusters. Access to this compute will be tiered: full sovereignty for EAAN and critical public functions, shared priority for compliant developers who submit to pre-deployment audits, and denial to any entity refusing runtime monitoring. We are not building a competitor to the frontier — not yet — but we are ensuring that no decision about what runs in Europe will ever again be made outside Europe.
