### Holding the Line
July to December 2031 was the half-year Europe learned to live inside someone else's war without joining it. The February exchange over fabs, cables and data centres did not resume, but it did not repair either. Transatlantic cloud regions kept stuttering, satellite navigation stayed degraded over the continent, and reserve engineers became a permanent fixture in grid control rooms, hospitals and payment switches.

Brussels held its single message: non-belligerency, no targeting use of European territory, and care that runs on what Europe can actually run. Dispersed registries, clean clinical images kept on Union soil, and pooled reagents held together emergency wards through the autumn. Nurses again credited the staffing guarantees written into the bio-detection and lifeline operations. The lights stayed on, but slowly, and everyone knew it.

### Jobs That Do Not Come Back
What broke the mood was not the war but the hiring figures. Law firms, accountancies, software houses, customer operations and municipal administrations simply stopped replacing entry-level posts. Graduate hiring froze entirely. The numbers were argued over on television; the empty desks were not.

The Transition Shield that finished in this period softened the landing where it reached — paid placements tied to triage, sequencing and restoration crews, top-ups for displaced clerical staff. Where it did not reach, sorting machines and triage assistants spread, and unions called the support late and too small. Voters did not blame Brussels for the strikes or the stuttering cloud, but the question from June hardened: why do the cure and the computation both live elsewhere.

Paris and Warsaw accelerator halls stayed empty, now symbols in nightly news packages contrasting frontier-designed therapies curing untreatable conditions abroad with European wards keeping care going on backup paths. Faint rumours of insurers refusing war-risk cover for hardware and reagent shipments stranded in bonded warehouses added to a sense of being cut off, unconfirmed but widely repeated.

By December, Europe was intact, un-targeted, and exhausted — care continuing, services degrading rather than stopping, and a generation without offers.
