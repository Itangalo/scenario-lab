### War Over the Horizon
In February, American and Chinese forces exchanged strikes after months of mutual claims that the other was months from an uncatchable lead. The fighting stayed conventional but reached deep into dual-use infrastructure: fabrication plants, subsea cables, satellite ground stations and hyperscale data centres were declared legitimate targets. Two cable cuts in the Atlantic and debris warnings over European downlinks brought the war into council rooms within days.

The Union declared itself non-belligerent, refused targeting use of its territory, fabs and landing stations, and ordered dispersal of registries and clinical systems. Reserve teams that had rebuilt town-hall rolls in autumn were redeployed to keep power, hospitals and payment switches on backup paths. Lights stayed on, but slowly: cloud regions hosted across the ocean stuttered, satellite navigation degraded, and empty accelerator halls in Paris and Warsaw became symbols of exposure.

### A Cure Behind a Wall
At the same time, tailored therapies for several previously untreatable conditions reached ordinary clinics elsewhere — designed with frontier systems Europe could no longer run. Brussels conditioned procurement on European hosting and verification through its medicines agency and public clinics, tying wage support and retraining placements to triage, sequencing and restoration crews.

Little arrived in time. Reagents pooled for the new bio-detection network were diverted to keep emergency wards open. Nurses credited the staffing guarantees; unions called the top-ups late and too small as graduate hiring froze entirely and sorting machines spread further.

By June, Europe was not a battlefield but was no longer outside one. Voters saw care continue under bombardment risk, and blamed no one in Brussels for the war itself — but asked openly why the cure and the computation both lived elsewhere.
