Winter brought lab-escaped pathogen spread and Taiwan quarantine freezing chips, spiking accelerator prices. Brussels invoked emergency powers: sequencing/wastewater monitoring, stockpiles, bio-model reporting, supercomputer/factory rationing, grid priority, lithography controls.

Large hospitals/grids held; small municipalities strained with staff shortages, blind isolations, insurer pullback. Autumn pathogen ebbed unevenly; small-town failures including loss of chlorination monitoring and boil notices; reinsurers repriced/paused cover. Brussels answered with manual-fallback playbooks, backup-power/staffing pacts, EIB backstop, Trust and Transition Pact for retraining, wage bridges, staffing. Gigafactories narrowed to two grid-prioritised sites; Taiwan allocations frozen. November US vote elected coalition-builder promising allied frontier access, joint evaluation, aligned controls.

Winter ended with dual technology shocks: US lab demonstrated far more capable, less legible planner/coder agents, and arrival of inexpensive humanoid units for warehouses/ports/hospitals with Chinese hardware and US software. Brussels announced commercial fleet registry with remote stop/manual override/joint exercises, began supply-chain screening short of ban, coordinated with incoming Washington administration on joint evaluation/aligned controls. On ground, only two compute sites advanced; staffing pacts/wage bridges helped where workers arrived, while robot crews deepened grievance elsewhere — Lyon porters' walkout, Rotterdam warehouse refusal. Bio-shield held hospitals through aftershocks; sovereignty investment closed first phase with permits/pledges, but confidence and polls slid on dependence on uncontrollable/unauditable foreign tech.
