The EU's crisis deepened in 2031 as its emergency Interim Sovereign Compute Initiative (ISCI) failed to achieve meaningful integration, hampered by zero political capital and member state non-cooperation. Only four states contributed hardware, while Poland and Spain rejected the effort as symbolic, and a bilateral AI-for-rare-earths deal between a member state and a U.S. hyperscaler signaled de facto secession from EU coordination. Though the Public Trust Corps operationalized partial GPU federation and ENISA certified open-weight models (58.0+), deployment remained fragmented and disconnected from critical systems. The €18.7bn Crisis Reserve release was stalled by legal disputes over data and energy. 

Transparency increased with the AI Incident Dashboard revealing 78% dependency on a single foreign provider, amplifying public distrust. Job displacement in professional sectors fueled unrest, while informal innovation surged—Rhine-region networks expanded interoperable open models across municipalities and hospitals, bypassing EU institutions entirely. Despite marginal resilience gains from localized biosecurity and node integration, systemic fragility persisted as sovereignty eroded further, political capital collapsed, and institutional legitimacy waned.
