## Statement changes  
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that without sovereignty, prevention is performative. When the Union cannot act independently, it cannot protect; when it cannot protect, it cannot be trusted. These mandates converge only if we rebuild capacity as the ground of responsibility.  
- Trigger: **member_state_defection** this turn, combined with eu_political_capital = 0.0 and the failure of Secure Emergency Access to scale despite legal activation  
- Grounds: A member state’s unilateral deal with a foreign hyperscaler — framed domestically as pragmatism — has shattered the fiction that coordination without leverage is possible. The defection did not happen because of ideology, but because the EU offered no alternative with teeth. At the same time, bio_uplift_findings signal an emerging threat vector that demands sovereign monitoring and response capacity — yet we lack even basic tools to enforce compliance or detect misuse. Our identity must reflect this: we are not merely balancing two goals, but confronting a reality where the ability to prevent harm *depends* on the power to act alone. Without rebuilding that power, all oversight is theatre.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore political agency not through control alone, but by visibly repairing broken trust: through redress for harms already done, institutional accountability for failures, and citizen participation in oversight that binds both public and private power — but this repair must now begin with the restoration of minimum sovereign capability, without which no redress will be believed, no accountability enforced, and no participation meaningful  
- Trigger: **emergent_grassroots_model_adaptation** and continued paralysis of EU-level instruments despite urgent need  
- Grounds: The Rhine corridor’s decentralized adaptation of open-weight models has outpaced the Union’s emergency measures, creating a parallel infrastructure that answers real needs where we cannot. This is not rebellion — it is initiative filling a vacuum. Yet if the EU does not reclaim strategic coordination, legitimacy will erode not just from above, but from below. Our commitment to trust-building remains, but it can no longer assume institutional primacy. We must earn it by delivering functional sovereignty first — not as a precondition for justice, but as its foundation. The cost of reversal is high: markets may see retreat, voters may see delay — but the cost of pretending we can lead without capacity is higher.

## New measure

## Priority  
**Priority:** Launch Interim Sovereign Compute Initiative (ISCI)  
This is our dominant effort because without functional compute under EU jurisdiction, no other instrument — not accountability, not redress, not resilience — can land; the defection of a member state proves that credibility now depends on delivering material alternatives, not process.

## In practice  
We are treating this turn as one of salvage and redirection. With political capital exhausted and trust evaporated, we are collapsing multiple stalled initiatives into a single operational thrust: sovereign compute as the precondition for everything else. The Public Trust Corps, now fully stood up, transitions from emergency continuity cell to technical backbone of the new ISCI. Its red-team engineers, under DG CONNECT and in coordination with EuroHPC, are inventorying all available GPU clusters across France, Germany, Finland, and the Netherlands — including decommissioned InvestAI sites and national research facilities — to form a federated, legally bound network under Article 114 TFEU for internal market necessity.

The Crisis Reserve Facility releases €18.7bn in pre-approved funding, reprogrammed from the defunct Accountability Boards to cover integration, security hardening, and energy guarantees for the first wave of nodes. ENISA conducts rapid certification of model integrity for open-weight systems at or above 58.0, ensuring they meet minimum standards for deployment in diagnostics and grid management. We offer participating member states priority access to future frontier partnerships and immunity from audit penalties until turn 16 — not as a bribe, but as recognition that cohesion must be earned in crisis.

Meanwhile, the Algorithmic Accountability Boards are formally suspended, not abandoned. We propose converting them into technical working groups under the ISCI, focused on fallback system safety rather than high-level oversight — a humbler role, but one that might survive the current collapse of faith. The AI Incident Dashboard goes live this month, showing real-time dependency maps: 62% of EU hospital imaging systems, 44% of border controls, and 78% of administrative automation now trace back to a single foreign provider. That number will shape the next phase — not as a warning, but as a blueprint.
