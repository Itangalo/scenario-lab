### Fracture and Fallback

The EU’s leadership attempted a strategic pivot, collapsing failed initiatives into the **Interim Sovereign Compute Initiative (ISCI)**—a last-ditch effort to federate national GPU clusters and decommissioned InvestAI sites into a sovereign AI backbone. The Public Trust Corps, now formally operational, became its technical core, inventorying capacity across France, Germany, and the Netherlands. ENISA fast-tracked certification for open-weight models at capability 58.0+, targeting deployment in healthcare and energy.

Yet momentum faltered. With political capital at zero, the EU could not compel participation. Only four member states committed hardware; Poland and Spain dismissed ISCI as another top-down illusion. The €18.7bn from the Crisis Reserve Facility was released, but legal disputes over data sovereignty and energy allocation delayed integration. The first nodes came online in name only—uncoordinated, underpowered, and disconnected from critical workflows.

A member state formalized a bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler, securing priority AI access in exchange for rare earth exports. Framed domestically as pragmatic survival, it was seen elsewhere as a quiet secession from EU coordination—deepening the crisis of legitimacy.

Meanwhile, job losses in law, accounting, and software mounted as firms replaced entry-level roles with AI agents. Public anger grew, but so did quiet innovation: the Rhine corridor’s grassroots model adaptation network expanded, now linking municipal grids and regional hospitals with interoperable open models, bypassing Brussels entirely.

The AI Incident Dashboard launched, revealing the EU’s staggering dependency: 78% of administrative automation traced back to a single foreign provider. The transparency was real—but so was the helplessness.

### Sovereignty, Interrupted

Despite the effort, sovereignty eroded further. The capability advance and member state defection outweighed ISCI’s nascent gains. Political capital plunged as measures collapsed and new costs mounted. Resilience inched up from partial node integration and local biosecurity adoption, but systemic fragility remained.
