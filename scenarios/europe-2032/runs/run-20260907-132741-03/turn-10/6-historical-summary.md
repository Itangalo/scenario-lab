The EU’s crisis of legitimacy deepened in 2031 as a damning ombudsman’s report revealed systemic harm in the Netherlands’ AI-driven social insurance system, triggering widespread protests in Utrecht and Lyon under the slogan “You built systems that see us as threats.” The Public Trust Corps, already struggling, shifted focus to redress with a new Redress Coordination Unit and an expanded AI Incident Dashboard incorporating systemic bias alerts, but reforms were seen as reactive. The introduction of Algorithmic Accountability Boards failed to regain trust, facing resistance from Poland and Spain.

Abroad, frontier AI capabilities advanced rapidly—especially in cyber operations and code generation—widening the gap with EU access. Open-weight models reached 56.5, enabling more sophisticated attacks on industrial systems. US-led supply chain coercion escalated, with Washington forcing the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s export of critical lithography machines, exposing EU vulnerability. With sovereignty at 9.0 and political capital at 5.0, the Union could not sustain its major initiatives. Public trust in AI governance collapsed, and the Trust Corps, once symbolic of renewal, came to represent institutional overreach and failure.
