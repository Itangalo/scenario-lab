### Strategic Shock and Emergency Deliberation  
The sudden denial of access to the world’s most capable AI model — used in EU hospitals, research institutes and critical agencies — triggers a crisis-level response. The European Commission proposes invoking emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act’s Crisis Clause to fast-track the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme (SCAP), already under discussion in prior turns. While three national champions are identified for potential designation, no final decision is made this turn due to unresolved legal and intergovernmental disputes. Binding milestones and override authorities are proposed but not yet enacted, with delivery timelines remaining uncertain and subject to future approvals.

### Fractured Coordination  
Efforts to mobilise resources face deepening friction. France and Sweden express support for SCAP acceleration, but Germany raises constitutional objections to compulsory grid allocation, stalling consensus on energy commitments. ENTSO-E declines to reserve 8GW of capacity without updated national energy plans, leaving power provisioning in limbo. A proposal for €50bn in emergency funding through EIB issuance and cohesion fund redirection is tabled, but legal uncertainty surrounds the EIB’s mandate under current MFF rules. Ireland and the Netherlands signal challenges, and the European Court of Auditors criticises the repurposing of RRF funds, slowing financial momentum.

The interim European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) evaluation cell begins preliminary staffing, drawing interest from CERN and national cyber agencies. However, actual deployment is delayed by security vetting, cross-border clearance procedures, and competition for skilled personnel. Real-time logging mandates meet resistance from Germany and Ireland, who warn of innovation flight, further complicating near-term implementation.

### Escalating Harm  
A major AI-optimised cyberattack disrupts public services across three member states, exploiting zero-day tooling derived from leaked frontier models. Defences are overwhelmed, and recovery is slow. With no finished resilience measure, systemic fragility deepens. Simultaneously, a capability jump in autonomous code generation is demonstrated by a leading lab, widening the safety gap.

Public sentiment sours as job displacement accelerates in legal and administrative sectors, and the access denial is framed as both a strategic humiliation and a self-inflicted wound. Media outlets question the EU’s capacity to act coherently under pressure. Political capital erodes due to visible disunity and stalled action, though some cohesion is maintained through shared recognition of the threat.
