### A Test of Readiness

The EU conducts the Operational Resilience Stress Test, an exercise first proposed in earlier turns and now reaching partial execution. Simulations of cascading AI failures were run across health, transport, and energy systems, with participation from four major member states. While the sovereign compute grid held under real-world load and failover systems powered diagnostics and rail controls for 72 hours, the exercise was hampered by last-minute staffing shortages and a cyber jamming simulation that briefly disrupted coordination. Integration with the InvestAI Gigafactories remains incomplete, and the test validated only limited operational readiness. The Public-Access Interpretability Registry went live and enabled real-time auditing during the simulation, leveraging a recent safety breakthrough to confirm alignment in controlled contexts.

### Shifting Ground

The safety breakthrough—achieved by a European research consortium—enables limited but reliable prediction of model behaviour in high-stakes domains. This lifts ai_safety by 5 points, marking the first meaningful gain in years. However, no corresponding advance in open-weight model availability or compute access occurred: the breakthrough remains largely within institutional channels, and fragmentation in implementation limits spillover. Consequently, openweight_capability remains unchanged at 49.5, despite marginal diffusion of insights. ai_capability advances to 65.5, driven by broader deployment of proprietary systems in legal and medical automation.

A member state quietly signs a bilateral compute-sharing pact with a US hyperscaler, bypassing EU coordination and weakening collective leverage. While framed domestically as pragmatic continuity planning, it is seen internationally as a defection. This undermines cohesion and partially offsets gains from the stress test, but the notepad's authoritative calculation—factoring in the completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories (+5), ongoing progress on Sovereign Compute Integration Acceleration (+1), and no decay due to capability growth below threshold—results in a net eu_ai_sovereignty of 24.0, up from 21.0.

The Emergency Fallback Procurement Directive is formally paused, and negotiators shift focus to multilateral standards. Retiring the obsolete EU Compute Access Guarantee frees political bandwidth. The completion of the Public-Access Interpretability Registry and the partial success of the stress test contribute to a measured rise in eu_political_capital, supported by a +3 proposal bonus for Sovereign Compute Integration Acceleration. The bonus is justified by the resolution of the cyber_major_incident gate—confirmed through the stress test's validation of fallback protocols under simulated attack conditions—which is now closed and accounted for in the notepad. To comply with Invariant 7, the contribution of sovereignty-linked achievements to political capital is capped at +3; the remaining improvement comes from portfolio simplification and institutional momentum, resulting in a total increase of +2, from 11.0 to 13.0. Public sentiment remains stagnant at 25.0, as job losses in professional sectors dominate discourse.
