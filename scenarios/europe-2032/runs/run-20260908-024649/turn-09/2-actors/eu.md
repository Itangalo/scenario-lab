## Priority
``Operational Resilience Stress Test`` is the priority this turn because the cyber_major_incident and ongoing integration delays have revealed that sovereignty without operational resilience is ceremonial — and because the safety_breakthrough in interpretability offers a rare opportunity to test control under crisis conditions with greater confidence in what we are running.

## In practice
This turn, we close the gap between infrastructure and readiness. The InvestAI Gigafactories are now fully integrated — including the Polish site, where emergency power allocation and fast-tracked staffing under the Digital Europe Programme have brought the facility online. We are not waiting for perfect conditions. The Sovereign Compute Integration Acceleration measure has delivered: all four sites are now under unified EuroHPC JU command, running standardized runtime environments and linked to the EU Compute Grid. But integration is not enough. We must know whether these systems can survive a real crisis.

So we launch the Operational Resilience Stress Test: a six-week exercise beginning in September, simulating a cascading AI failure across health diagnostics, rail signalling, and energy grid management. Each affected system will attempt failover to sovereign compute, under conditions of simulated cyber jamming, power fluctuation, and limited operator availability. The test is not a drill on paper — it will run live workloads, with fallback systems temporarily taking over real services in controlled segments. The Public-Access Interpretability Registry, now complete, will be used to audit the reasoning of AI systems during the transition, leveraging the safety_breakthrough to verify alignment and control in real time.

At the same time, we are disengaging from the Emergency Fallback Procurement Directive. The US and Asian hyperscalers have made clear they will not accept liability terms that allow the EU to act autonomously in a crisis. Their position reflects a strategic choice: they will not bind their infrastructure to our continuity. We are not walking away empty-handed. We are extracting the diagnostic framework developed during negotiations — a common incident classification system — and feeding it into the emergent_multilateral_ai_initiative, where it can serve as a foundation for democratic alignment. But we will not burn political capital on a deal that cannot close. From now on, our continuity depends only on systems we can switch on, staff, and trust — not on promises from those who see us as a tiered partner. This turn, we test what we own. And we prepare to act on what we find.
