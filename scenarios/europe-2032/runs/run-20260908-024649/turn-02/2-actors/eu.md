## In practice  
We are advancing Cyber Shield Certification through delegated acts under Article 114 TFEU, allowing swift adoption without reopening the NIS2 Directive. ENISA is being empowered as the central audit coordinator, with €2bn disbursed this turn from the RRF top-up to launch pilot scoring in energy and transport sectors. Member states are being offered a 70/30 cost split and fast-track access to Just Transition Fund top-ups if they meet early milestones. Resistance from smaller states is being managed through bilateral side letters that preserve national oversight while accepting common baselines — a fragile balance, but one that holds for now.

InvestAI Gigafactories is entering its second phase: three sites have been shortlisted based on grid readiness and political buy-in, with environmental fast-tracking now under way via the new Strategic Infrastructure Regulation. We are pressing member states to implement the Recommendation on Electricity Prioritisation, but early signals show hesitation — particularly in regions facing power shortages. To counter this, we are linking compute allocation to regional innovation pacts: each site will reserve 15% of initial capacity for public-sector AI applications in health and climate, and fund 500 new vocational training slots per site, starting in Q3.

The Tech sovereignty package is nearing COREPER II consensus on accelerated permitting, but the unresolved dual-use export question looms. Hyperscalers are holding back firm commitments, demanding clarity on what models can be shared internationally. We are resisting premature rulemaking, betting that the visible progress on sovereign infrastructure will strengthen our hand by turn 3. For now, we are offering tax incentives tied to domestic hiring and open-access research partnerships — a compromise that keeps the door open, but not wide.
