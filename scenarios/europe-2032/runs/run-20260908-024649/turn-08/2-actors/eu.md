## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure minimum AI continuity for essential state functions and establish binding fallback access to sovereign compute capacity, with full operational integration by end of turn 9  
- Grounds: The InvestAI Gigafactories programme has formally finished, but integration delays at one site — due to staffing and power bottlenecks — have postponed full operational capacity. This exposes a critical gap: sovereign compute is not sovereign unless it is *operational* when needed. The commitment must now include a hard deadline for integration to close the window between infrastructure delivery and usable continuity.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer fund measures that duplicate or contradict one another without resolving the contradiction  
- Grounds: The portfolio is now overloaded, with overlapping sovereignty instruments (EU Compute Access Guarantee, Emergency Fallback Procurement Directive) straining political capital without clear hierarchy. The cyber_major_incident revealed not just external fragility but internal incoherence: we have legal claims, fallbacks, and infrastructure, but no unified operational doctrine. Continuing to fund all without triage is not prudence — it is denial. This turn, we act to align, not expand.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`

## Priority  
``Sovereign Compute Integration Acceleration`` is the priority this turn because the cyber_major_incident has shown that compute sovereignty means nothing if the systems are not live, staffed, and connected when the emergency hits — and one of our four gigafactories is still offline due to non-technical bottlenecks. We cannot afford another failure of execution.

## In practice  
We are acting on the breach, but we are also acting on ourselves. The Emergency Fallback Procurement Directive is in negotiation, but we now see that even if it passes, it will not matter unless we have sovereign systems ready to run on it. The InvestAI Gigafactories are built — a historic achievement — but one site in Poland remains offline due to unresolved grid connection delays and a shortage of certified AI operations engineers. This is not a funding gap; it is an execution gap. This turn, we deploy the new Sovereign Compute Integration Acceleration measure: a cross-agency task force under the European Commission’s Digital Europe Programme, empowered to override local permitting delays, fast-track energy allocation under the Crisis Regulation, and activate emergency training pipelines through the EU Talent Corridor initiative to staff the site within six months.

At the same time, we are aligning the EU Compute Access Guarantee and the Emergency Fallback Procurement Directive under a single operational doctrine: the former secures long-term access, the latter crisis requisition, but both must point to the same sovereign runtime backbone. We are instructing the EuroHPC JU to unify technical standards across all four gigafactories and to conduct a live failover exercise by end of turn 9. The Public-Access Interpretability Registry remains under pressure, but we are not retreating: we are linking compliance not just to AI Act certification but to access to the sovereign compute grid — a new leverage point. The US and Asian hyperscalers are watching closely. If we cannot run our own systems when we need them, no legal directive will save us. This turn, we close the gap between infrastructure and operation — because sovereignty is not a promise. It is a state of readiness.
