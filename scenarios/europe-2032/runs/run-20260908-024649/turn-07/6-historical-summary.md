Global AI capability increases to 62.5, driven by continuous external advancements in research and model scaling, further widening the gap with EU-controlled systems. A major AI-driven ransomware attack exploits open-weight model mappings, crippling health and transport systems in three EU states, exposing critical vulnerabilities and the limitations of legislative sovereignty without guaranteed compute access. In response, the EU proposes the Emergency Fallback Procurement Directive, invoking Article 122 to enable hyperscaler compute requisition during crises, but faces strong resistance from non-EU providers, delaying implementation by at least two turns. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme achieves partial completion with four sovereign compute sites, though integration delays at one site push full operational capacity to the next turn. This partial delivery contributes to a rise in EU AI sovereignty to 20.0, reflecting tangible infrastructure gains and new crisis contingency mechanisms, despite no progress in open innovation. Open-weight model capability remains stagnant at 49.0. The Public-Access Interpretability Registry holds firm amid growing vendor resistance, with two major suppliers threatening withdrawal from public tenders unless audit rules are relaxed; compliance is now tied to AI Act certification. Public sentiment declines further due to visible cyber failures and job losses in professional sectors. The US tightens export controls on chipmaking equipment, extending restrictions to EU allies, highlighting the Union’s strategic dependency. With multiple high-cost initiatives ongoing and no prioritization, political capital continues to erode under the strain of overextension.
