## New measure
**Sovereign Compute Integration Acceleration (Phase II)**  
Expands and hardens the EU’s sovereign compute grid by integrating regional fallback nodes, automating failover protocols, and pre-positioning verified AI stacks for critical functions.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases eu_ai_sovereignty by +8 through expanded sovereign infrastructure footprint and automated crisis readiness; supports resilience +3 by reducing failover time under stress.  
`Applies to:` EuroHPC JU, national digital agencies in all member states, critical infrastructure operators in health, energy, and transport.

## Priority
``Sovereign Compute Integration Acceleration (Phase II)`` is the priority this turn because the cyber_major_incident and the failure of external procurement have proven that continuity cannot depend on foreign goodwill — only on systems we own, control, and can activate without permission.

## In practice
This turn, we act on the lesson of the Operational Resilience Stress Test and the cyber_major_incident: sovereignty without speed is ceremony. The test showed that our sovereign systems *can* hold — but only if they are activated in time, staffed, and trusted. The Public-Access Interpretability Registry confirmed that we can audit alignment in real time. But the failure of the Emergency Fallback Procurement Directive proves that no external actor will bind their infrastructure to our survival. We are not tier-one partners; we are contingency cases.

So we double down on what we control. We launch Sovereign Compute Integration Acceleration (Phase II) under the Digital Europe Programme and NextGenerationEU, with €4.2 billion in committed funding. This expands the EU Compute Grid to include twelve regional fallback nodes — hardened facilities in less exposed geographies, each capable of running essential AI services for multiple member states. We automate failover using the interpretability protocols validated in the stress test, so that transition happens in minutes, not hours. We pre-load verified AI stacks for diagnostics, grid management, and emergency logistics — systems already audited, already trusted, already tested.

The resistance is real. Some member states argue that duplication risks waste; others fear centralisation. But the cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that crippled hospital scheduling in three countries — has shifted the balance. We are not building redundancy; we are building survival. We will fund this through the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with conditionality tied to national digital readiness scores. The Commission, in coordination with the ENISA crisis cell and EuroHPC JU, will oversee implementation. This is not a drill. We are preparing for a world where the frontier moves without us, and where only what we own will keep us standing.
