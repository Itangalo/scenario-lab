## Priority
``Sovereign Compute Integration Acceleration (Phase II)`` is the priority this turn because the cyber_major_incident and the automated_decision_scandal have proven that capability without control is danger, and sovereignty without speed is ceremony — we must harden what we own, and we must be able to turn it on when it matters.

## In practice
This turn, we act on two shocks: the hospital ransomware attack that crippled scheduling across three states, and the ombudsman’s ruling that an AI-supported social insurance system systematically denied claims without recourse. The first exposed our fragility; the second, our complicity. We cannot outsource continuity, and we cannot delegate accountability.

So we press forward on Sovereign Compute Integration Acceleration (Phase II), but now with urgency: the twelve regional fallback nodes must not just be built — they must be *staffed*, *trusted*, and *tested*. We are working through the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility to tie funding to national digital readiness scores, but resistance remains. Some capitals fear centralisation; others lack personnel. We are offering technical assistance from the Commission’s AI Office and fast-track access to EuroHPC’s training pool to close the gap.

Simultaneously, we launch Crisis Activation Drills for Sovereign AI Systems — a small but sharp measure to force realism. By turn 12, every member state must conduct a live-fire exercise: simulate a cascade failure, activate their node, and run essential services for 72 hours. ENISA will audit, EuroHPC will observe, and the results will be public. This is not about perfection — it is about exposure. We need to know where the friction is before the next incident hits. Because this time, the attack wasn’t just on our systems. It was on our legitimacy. And that is not a debt we can carry into the next turn.
