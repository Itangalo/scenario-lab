### A Turn of Reckoning

The EU enters 2031’s second half under dual pressure: a ransomware attack that crippled hospital operations exposed technical fragility, while a damning ombudsman report revealed an AI-driven social insurance system had wrongfully denied benefits to thousands. Public trust erodes further as headlines frame AI not as progress but as unaccountable power.

In response, the Union doubles down on sovereignty with **Sovereign Compute Integration Acceleration (Phase II)**, now a priority. Though procurement for regional fallback nodes advances, staffing shortages and intergovernmental disputes delay deployment. Automation protocols remain untested; no node achieves operational readiness. The project yields planning gains only — insufficient to offset deeper vulnerabilities.

Simultaneously, the new **Crisis Activation Drills** initiative launches to stress-test sovereign systems. Designed as a small, fast measure, it mandates live-fire exercises across health, energy, and transport by mid-2032. However, early pushback emerges: several member states resist what they see as top-down imposition, citing sovereignty concerns of their own. Participation remains uncertain, and ENISA warns that audits may expose more gaps than they close.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

Global AI capability climbs to 68.5, driven by closed-loop training in proprietary labs. Offensive cyber tools evolve, and open-weight models reach 54.0, enabling accessible exploitation frameworks. Yet no safety improvements land on deployed systems — **ai_safety** stagnates at 15.0. The gap between what AI can do and what we understand widens.

Resilience dips to 49.0 as cascading failures overwhelm patchwork defences. The automated decision scandal triggers a political backlash: adoption of public-sector AI halts in five countries. Public sentiment falls to 20.0, nearing outright hostility.

Political capital, strained by ongoing commitments and the new scandal, drops to 13.0. With **eu_ai_sovereignty** at 22.0 — undercut by persistent access denial and slow delivery — the leverage once hoped for remains out of reach.
