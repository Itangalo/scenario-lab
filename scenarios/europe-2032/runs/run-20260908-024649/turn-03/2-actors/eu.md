## New measure  
**EU Compute Access Guarantee**  
Establishes a legal right for EU public institutions to run sovereign AI workloads on guaranteed compute, backed by binding infrastructure timelines and emergency fallbacks.

## Priority  
``Cyber Shield Certification`` remains the priority this turn because the cyber_major_incident has made resilience an immediate operational imperative — without hardened systems, even sovereign compute would be rapidly compromised, and public confidence would collapse.

## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_major_incident as a wake-up call: the attack, though not yet attributed, used AI-generated exploit chains and targeted municipal services in three member states, temporarily disabling emergency dispatch and water monitoring. This proves that defensive lag is now a strategic liability. We are accelerating Cyber Shield Certification by activating the crisis clause in Article 114 TFEU, allowing the Commission to impose binding audit schedules on non-compliant sectors. ENISA is deploying rapid-response assessment teams, and we are releasing an additional €1.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme to cover emergency upgrades in healthcare and civil protection systems. Bilateral pressure is intensifying on holdout states, with the Council Presidency brokering side agreements that preserve national inspection rights while accepting common scoring thresholds.

InvestAI Gigafactories is now in grid-integration negotiations: we are instructing national regulators to prioritise the shortlisted sites under the new Strategic Infrastructure Regulation, which allows override of local objections on grounds of strategic digital autonomy. To unlock stalled power commitments, we are linking grid access to regional innovation pacts — each site must co-fund AI training hubs for public-sector use, starting with diagnostics and border control. We are also launching a talent retention initiative: 2,000 new long-term visas for AI engineers and chip designers, tied to work in sovereign compute projects.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is nearing final vote in COREPER II. The export control deadlock persists, but the emergent_multilateral_ai_initiative has shifted the balance: we are now proposing a carve-out for democracies within the initiative (EU, Japan, South Korea, Canada) under a mutual trust framework, with shared audits and joint oversight. This would allow model exports without full dual-use licensing, conditional on reciprocity. We are offering hyperscalers a fast-track compliance path if they commit compute to the EU Access Guarantee — turning dependency into leverage.

The new **EU Compute Access Guarantee** responds to the access denial crisis: we are drafting a regulation that mandates minimum compute quotas for EU public use, enforceable against both domestic and foreign operators with infrastructure in the Union. If private delivery falters, the state can requisition capacity or trigger emergency procurement. The first phase allocates 10 exaFLOPs of guaranteed runtime by turn 5, rising to 50 by turn 8, hosted on EU-anchored systems. Funding comes from a reallocation of RRF top-ups and a new sovereign tech levy on hyperscale data centres. This is not just infrastructure — it is a political signal: the EU will not beg for access to its own future.
