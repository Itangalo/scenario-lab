### A Winter of Access and Anxiety

Global AI capability creeps upward to 58.0, driven by continued automation in chip design and logistics, even as private investment collapses across the frontier. The leading labs scale back training runs, but incremental refinement sustains progress. With no safety breakthrough and opaque pipelines under scrutiny, `ai_safety` drops further to 22.0.

The sudden cutoff from a leading AI model paralyzes hospital diagnostic systems and border control platforms across multiple member states. No explanation is given, and fallbacks fail. Public anger mounts, framed not as a foreign provocation but as institutional failure. Protests erupt outside data centres, and political candidates on both flanks adopt anti-AI platforms.

Cyber Shield Certification completes its rollout, mandating audits for critical infrastructure. While compliance is patchy, early hardening prevents a second wave of attacks. The programme finishes successfully, contributing to resilience.

In response to the access crisis, the EU proposes the **Emergency Compute Assurance Programme**, aiming to secure minimal runtime for healthcare, emergency services, and border systems. It leverages existing contracts and emergency powers, but implementation is delayed by legal disputes and resistance from member states reluctant to requisition commercial capacity. The proposal is prioritized, but full deployment will take time. No operational response mechanisms are activated this turn.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** remains under review: the €200bn private investment target is under pressure, and the Commission acknowledges it may need revision. However, no formal recalibration is enacted this turn. Similarly, **InvestAI Gigafactories** faces scrutiny over power and funding, but no formal pause is issued; new site approvals proceed at a reduced pace pending review.

Public sentiment sours further, dropping to 32.0, as dependency and disruption dominate headlines. Political capital erodes, but less severely than proposed, now at 26.0, reflecting the burden of ongoing initiatives without the addition of a fully implemented new measure. The portfolio charge remains high, but within bounds: three large measures active, no new priority yet effective.

### Triage Over Vision

The EU’s pivot from sovereign build-out to minimum viable continuity reflects strategic triage. But the costs of overreach, external shocks, and eroding trust compound — sovereignty drops to 17.0, reflecting the unmitigated impact of the frontier access denial event. The modest gain in overall AI capability slightly offsets decay, but no operational mitigation occurs this turn to justify a rebound. Political capital tightens, but not as sharply, as the new programme remains in proposal phase rather than full execution. The window for coherent action narrows.
