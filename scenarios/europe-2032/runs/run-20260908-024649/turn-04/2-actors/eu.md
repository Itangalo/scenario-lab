## New measure  
**Emergency Compute Assurance Programme**  
Guarantees minimal runtime for essential public services — healthcare, emergency response, and border control — using existing EU-anchored infrastructure, fallback procurement, and failover protocols.  

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases resilience by +15, stabilises public_sentiment by preventing service collapse, and indirectly supports eu_ai_sovereignty by demonstrating functional independence even without full-scale compute build-out.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member states, and critical service operators dependent on AI systems.

## Priority  
``Emergency Compute Assurance Programme`` is now the priority this turn because the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has revealed that even basic continuity of government functions is now at risk — and without assured access to minimal compute, no amount of sovereign infrastructure planning can compensate in real time. This is not a shift from sovereignty, but a reordering: we must first ensure that the state does not go offline.

## In practice  
We are treating the denial of frontier access not as a diplomatic incident but as a system failure: hospitals lost diagnostic support overnight, border systems slowed, and ministries were locked out of planning models. This is the moment where dependence becomes surrender. The **Emergency Compute Assurance Programme** is not another industrial vision — it is triage. We are activating the Civil Protection Mechanism to map all AI-dependent public functions, identifying those whose failure would cascade into societal harm. For each, we are establishing a minimal runtime threshold — 1 exaFLOP per million population for health diagnostics, 0.5 for emergency dispatch — to be met through a combination of existing public cloud contracts, emergency requisition powers under the Digital Markets Act, and fallback procurement from non-frontier but stable providers.

At the same time, we are renegotiating the **Tech Sovereignty Package** to reflect the investment collapse: the original €200bn target is no longer credible, so we are shifting focus to securing 20% of existing EU-anchored compute for public emergency use, enforceable by audit. This is not about building faster than others — it is about ensuring we do not vanish from the board. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** project remains in flight, but we are suspending new site approvals pending updated power and investment assessments. Our message is clear: we are not stopping the build — but we must first ensure the lights stay on while we build them.
