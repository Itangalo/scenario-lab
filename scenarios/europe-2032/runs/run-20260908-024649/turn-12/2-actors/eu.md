## Statement changes  
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure minimum AI continuity for essential state functions and ensure sovereign systems are operationally resilient under crisis conditions → **Reaffirm and operationalise sovereign AI systems under real-world duress, with measurable readiness by turn 13: every critical node must have staff, protocol, and public legitimacy to activate within one hour of crisis declaration**  
- Grounds: The **backlash_physical** event and **election_annulled** development mark a categorical shift: AI is no longer a technical or economic question, but a constitutional one. The failure of Crisis Activation Drills to secure broad participation — despite their design — reveals that operational resilience requires not just hardware and code, but political buy-in and public trust. The commitment must now specify *measurable* readiness, not just intent, and bind legitimacy to activation speed. Without this, sovereignty is symbolic.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will now also institutionalise post-mortems for failed or stalled measures, ensuring that lessons are captured, costs are accounted for, and political capital is not spent twice on the same mistake → **We act before the picture is clear, accepting error as the cost of timeliness, and now mandate binding post-mortems: any measure consuming 2+ political capital per turn must undergo independent review at failure or completion, with findings directly shaping successor instruments**  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event exposed that our dependency was not just technical but institutional — we assumed access would persist because it had, and no mechanism existed to force re-evaluation. The automated decision scandal and stalled procurement show that inertia is structural. Binding reviews are no longer optional; they are the price of acting under uncertainty without repeating it.

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing → **We exist to preserve the Union’s capacity to act in a world transformed by AI, and to prevent that transformation from eroding democratic legitimacy — and we now recognise that without the second, the first is hollow**  
- Trigger: The **election_annulled** and **backlash_physical** events  
- Grounds: For the first time, AI has directly disrupted electoral legitimacy and triggered physical resistance to infrastructure. These are not external risks but internal ruptures. Our identity must reflect that political sovereignty — the people’s belief in their own agency — is now the core substrate of both mandates. Capability without consent is not sovereignty; it is occupation.

## Priority  
``Sovereign Compute Integration Acceleration (Phase II)`` is the priority this turn because the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has proven that external dependency is not a risk — it is a present reality, and without sovereign compute that can activate *immediately*, the Union cannot respond to cascading failure or political crisis.

## In practice  
This turn, we are acting on the collapse of trust and access simultaneously. The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has left hospitals, tax authorities, and transport agencies without core AI services overnight — not due to failure of EU systems, but because we never had control. Sovereign Compute Integration Acceleration (Phase II) remains our backbone, but we are now tying disbursement from the Digital Europe Programme and Recovery and Resilience Facility to monthly readiness dashboards: nodes must report staffing levels, protocol testing frequency, and public communication plans. The Commission’s AI Office is deploying rapid assistance teams to three lagging states, drawing from EuroHPC’s reserve pool.

The Crisis Activation Drills have concluded, but with mixed results: six member states completed full 72-hour simulations, five ran partial tests, and two refused. ENISA’s audit will be published next month, exposing gaps in coordination and public justification. In response, we are launching the Binding Readiness Certification — not another drill, but a legal standard. By turn 13, each node must be certified by ENISA and its national audit institution as staffed, tested, and transparent. Certification will be tied to continued access to EU AI funding and emergency mutual aid rights. This is not about punishment — it is about making sovereignty real, not rhetorical. Because if we cannot activate in crisis, we do not own what we built.
