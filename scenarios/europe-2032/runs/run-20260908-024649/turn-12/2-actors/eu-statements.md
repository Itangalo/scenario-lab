# Statements: The European Union (turn 12)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing → **We exist to preserve the Union’s capacity to act in a world transformed by AI, and to prevent that transformation from eroding democratic legitimacy — and we now recognise that without the second, the first is hollow**

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Secure minimum AI continuity for essential state functions and ensure sovereign systems are operationally resilient under crisis conditions → **Reaffirm and operationalise sovereign AI systems under real-world duress, with measurable readiness by turn 13: every critical node must have staff, protocol, and public legitimacy to activate within one hour of crisis declaration**
  - Grounds: The **backlash_physical** event and **election_annulled** development mark a categorical shift: AI is no longer a technical or economic question, but a constitutional one. The failure of Crisis Activation Drills to secure broad participation — despite their design — reveals that operational resilience requires not just hardware and code, but political buy-in and public trust. The commitment must now specify *measurable* readiness, not just intent, and bind legitimacy to activation speed. Without this, sovereignty is symbolic.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will now also institutionalise post-mortems for failed or stalled measures, ensuring that lessons are captured, costs are accounted for, and political capital is not spent twice on the same mistake → **We act before the picture is clear, accepting error as the cost of timeliness, and now mandate binding post-mortems: any measure consuming 2+ political capital per turn must undergo independent review at failure or completion, with findings directly shaping successor instruments**
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event exposed that our dependency was not just technical but institutional — we assumed access would persist because it had, and no mechanism existed to force re-evaluation. The automated decision scandal and stalled procurement show that inertia is structural. Binding reviews are no longer optional; they are the price of acting under uncertainty without repeating it.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_mandates` — applied
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing → **We exist to preserve the Union’s capacity to act in a world transformed by AI, and to prevent that transformation from eroding democratic legitimacy — and we now recognise that without the second, the first is hollow**
  - Trigger: The **election_annulled** and **backlash_physical** events
  - Grounds: For the first time, AI has directly disrupted electoral legitimacy and triggered physical resistance to infrastructure. These are not external risks but internal ruptures. Our identity must reflect that political sovereignty — the people’s belief in their own agency — is now the core substrate of both mandates. Capability without consent is not sovereignty; it is occupation.
  - Evidence quoted: **election_annulled:** An election in an established democracy is postponed, rerun or annulled with explicit reference to manipulation of the information environment. Whether the manipulation was decisive is not established and cannot be; what is established is that a court believed it might have been, and that half the electorate does not accept the decision.
**backlash_physical:** Protest against AI infrastructure moves from petitions and hearings to direct action: occupations at data centre sites, sabotage of grid connections, and in the harder cases injury. Policing it costs the Union more than the damage does.
  - Reason: These events directly challenge democratic legitimacy and public consent, which the revised identity now centers as essential to the EU's capacity to act.
