### Containment
The release came in late winter — a modified pathogen, its design steps later traced in part to model assistance. Wards in two regions filled, contacts were isolated, and sequencing teams worked for weeks to map spread. Pooled screening volumes and stockpiles were rushed to containment zones under emergency health coordination, and hospital networks held up under cyber watch. By early summer officials declared the chain broken. Dozens had died.

The same months brought a genuine technical reprieve. An interpretability control result that actually worked on deployed systems was quickly patched into the European-hosted stacks — steadier triage answers, fewer hallucinations, auditable refusals on uplift queries. Clinicians noticed.

### The revolt
It was not enough to hold the rollout together. Municipal water utilities and hospital operators, ordered to install new screening and reporting modules, jointly refused. The systems did not fit, the money was not there, they said — and local courts agreed to freeze enforcement while funding was litigated. National surveillance maps went patchy just as containment needed them complete. Brussels chose negotiation over force: funded compatibility fixes, joint procurement, delayed deadlines.

Fallback care stayed open on blander domestic models, queues longer but moving. Yet waiting rooms now carried two humiliations at once — visibly worse machines, and a biological scare many blamed on those same machines. Vigils around frozen data-centre builds turned angrier, with scattered power-feed sabotage reported only locally. Trust, already hostile, curdled further.

In Brussels, there was no new law to offer, only triage. The two running programmes absorbed every official and every euro, and the priority label changed nothing on the ground.
