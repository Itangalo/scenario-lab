## New measure
**Measure:** Cryptographic Auditability Mandate  
This mandates that all AI systems operating within the Sovereign Core stack must implement end-to-end cryptographic logging of training data sources, model updates, and agent-level decisions, enabling post-hoc verification of provenance and behaviour by authorised EU auditors.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety from 0.0 by establishing minimal audit trails for high-risk systems; lay groundwork for future accountability even if real-time control remains out of reach  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, EuroHPC nodes, national AI infrastructure operators, and any private entity receiving access to EU-funded compute or data

## Priority
**Priority:** Emergency Resilience Surge

## In practice
We are treating this turn as one of triage. The Emergency Resilience Surge is now our lifeline. ENISA has activated emergency powers under Article 172 TFEU to fast-track deployment of zero-trust architectures across energy grids, health records, and civil protection systems. The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre is running Operation Iron Gate II this quarter — a live-fire simulation involving AI-driven supply-chain poisoning and autonomous social engineering attacks — to test both technical defences and institutional coordination under stress. We are mandating telemetry sharing through NIS3 mutual assistance clauses, overriding national objections on grounds of systemic risk. Some member states resist, citing sovereignty concerns — the irony is not lost on us — but we are clear: if the core fails, their resistance will be remembered as the moment they chose pride over survival.

At the same time, we are executing the Sovereign Core Integrity Overhaul with JRC and EuroHPC engineers pulled from non-critical projects. The redesign focuses on replacing the current monolithic update chain with a modular, cryptographically signed rollout system, where every change is verifiable and revocable. We have suspended all new integrations into the Core until certification is complete. This frustrates national digitalisation plans, so we are offering bridging support through the Digital Europe Programme to maintain goodwill. Meanwhile, the Public Trust Observatory’s first report is being finalized despite intelligence agency resistance. We are invoking Article 47 of the Charter to compel data release, threatening to escalate to the European Court of Justice if necessary. Its publication this turn is not about information — it is about proving that truth can still flow within the Union.

The new Cryptographic Auditability Mandate is our first step toward rebuilding oversight from the wreckage. It does not restore control, but it ensures that when (not if) another incident occurs, we will be able to trace what happened, who approved it, and how it spread. We are drafting it as a Commission Implementing Decision under the AI Act’s high-risk provisions, with technical standards to be developed by ETSI in collaboration with Fraunhofer and INRIA. This is not a silver bullet — but it is a stake in the ground: from now on, opacity is no longer acceptable, even in the name of speed or security.
