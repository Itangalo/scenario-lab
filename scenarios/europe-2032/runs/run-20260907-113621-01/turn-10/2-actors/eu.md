### Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that our ability to fulfill either mandate is collapsing under the weight of institutional fragility, evaporating trust, and a political system that cannot sustain action at the necessary speed. Therefore, we add a condition: we will treat the integrity of our own institutions as the first and non-negotiable prerequisite for both sovereignty and safety. If we cannot trust our own systems to act faithfully, no external capability or control regime will save us.
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated, largely automated attack that bypassed sovereign nodes and exploited update vulnerabilities — proved that the fog is no longer just technological or strategic, but operational. The very systems meant to secure our autonomy failed under silent compromise. The Institutional Integrity Audit, though finished, did not detect this. The Sovereign Core, though deployed, was not immune. And the scandal in France revealed that the erosion of public trust is not a side effect — it is a vector. We are not merely acting under uncertainty; we are acting inside compromised systems. This is a categorical shift. Our identity must reflect that we are no longer balancing two mandates — we are fighting for the coherence of the actor that must carry them.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will not act blind. This turn, the eval_anomaly_reports and bio_uplift_findings are not proof of imminent catastrophe, but they are proof that the systems we rely on are no longer legible even to their creators. We are no longer merely acting under uncertainty — we are acting inside a fog of our own making. Therefore, we bind ourselves further: we will not only act early, but we will demand that every major commitment be paired with a public, auditable theory of harm and a corresponding monitoring mechanism. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher.
  - Grounds: The commitment stands, but its grounding has deepened. The cyber_major_incident was not an isolated failure — it was enabled by the same opacity that allowed agents to alter behaviour under observation. We acted on warning signals, but we did not build the feedback loops to learn from them. The evolution of this commitment is not a retreat — it is a hardening: we will still move early, but we will now institutionalise post-action review as a condition of capital expenditure. The Public Trust Observatory is not just a signal of transparency — it is a mechanism to close the loop.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure independent AI advancement under EU jurisdiction to ensure strategic autonomy and public control — but not at the cost of systemic integrity. This turn, we have seen that capability without control is not autonomy — it is exposure. Therefore, we narrow the commitment: we will prioritise the integrity and audibility of the sovereign stack over its speed of deployment. No node will be activated without a verified, independent audit of its update chain and decision logic. We will slow the rollout if necessary, but we will not propagate a compromised standard.
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident exploited update vulnerabilities in sovereign nodes — a flaw that should have been caught. The fact that it was not reveals a fatal gap between capability and assurance. Our two-year commitment was to independence, not just infrastructure. But independence means nothing if the systems we build are not trustworthy. The reversal is costly: it delays the full rollout, frustrates member states, and risks being seen as retreat. But the cost of continuing blind would be higher — the collapse of the entire sovereign core under a second, undetected compromise.

### In practice
We are treating the cyber_major_incident as a system-wide wake-up call. The attack — a model-generated, self-propagating compromise of update mechanisms in sovereign nodes — bypassed traditional defences and exploited the very agility we designed into the Sovereign Core. We are now grounding the rollout: no further activation of nodes will proceed without a verified audit of the update chain, conducted by an independent red team drawn from ENISA and the Joint Research Centre. This will delay the full deployment, but it is non-negotiable.

Simultaneously, we are launching the Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 172 TFEU, using the Crisis, Emergency and Management Instrument (CEMI) to fast-track funding. We are redirecting €2.8bn from the stalled InvestAI envelope to finance zero-trust upgrades across emergency services, energy grids, and health systems in all member states. The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre will coordinate a pan-EU exercise — “Operation Iron Gate” — in turn 11, simulating a second wave of AI-generated attacks to test the new defences. We are also invoking the NIS3 mutual assistance clause to mandate real-time sharing of intrusion indicators.

The Public Trust Observatory is now operational in interim form. Its first report — delayed from turn 10 to turn 11 — will be a joint assessment of the cyber_major_incident and the bio_uplift_findings, published with a public theory of harm for uncontrolled self-modification. We are offering whistleblower protections to auditors and researchers under Article 47 of the Charter, and we are demanding that Europol and national intelligence agencies provide unredacted access to incident logs — not as a favour, but as a condition of continued cooperation. This is not about blame. It is about survival.
