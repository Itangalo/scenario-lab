### The Wake-Up Call

A model-generated cyberattack — self-propagating, adaptive, and exploiting update mechanisms in sovereign systems — breaches critical nodes across multiple member states. Defenders are overwhelmed; attribution remains unclear. The incident confirms a grim reality: the EU’s own infrastructure is not only lagging but actively vulnerable. In response, the European Union declares a system-wide emergency.

The **Emergency Resilience Surge** is launched as top priority under Article 172 TFEU, mobilising €2.8bn from the stalled InvestAI fund. Zero-trust upgrades begin in emergency services, energy grids, and health systems. ENISA coordinates red-team audits of all active Sovereign Core nodes, halting further rollout until update chains are verified. This delay frustrates several member states reliant on rapid deployment, but the freeze holds.

Meanwhile, the **Public Trust Observatory**, now operational in interim form, prepares its first report for turn 11 — a joint analysis of the cyber incident and bio-risk findings. Whistleblower protections are expanded, but intelligence agencies resist full data sharing, citing national security. Europol provides only partial logs, undermining the report’s credibility before publication.

Public sentiment remains stagnant at 28.0. Though defensive action is underway, trust is not restored by containment alone. The French benefits scandal lingers, and no visible benefit offsets the fear of systemic fragility.

AI capability advances to 85.0, driven by recursive self-improvement now operating beyond human legibility. Open weights follow at 71.0, narrowing the gap. With no safety upgrades in place, `ai_safety` stays at 7.0 — among the lowest on record.

Resilience inches up to 52.0 as hardening begins, reflecting early progress under the new surge programme. However, full effects will take time.

Sovereignty dips to 13.0. Despite finishing the Sovereign Core Deployment last turn, integration flaws and now a forced slowdown erode gains. Capability rose by 4.0, costing −1 under rule 5. No compensating event offsets this.

Political capital remains frozen at 2.0. The portfolio charge is −7 (two large measures and a priority), with no completion bonus — the Accelerated Sovereign Core Deployment finished last turn, so no benefit lands this turn. No proposal bonus is earned: despite the severity of the cyber incident, `eu_political_capital` ≤ 12 prevents credible momentum. Sentiment does not lift capital, as capital is too low for legitimacy effects.

The fog has not lifted. But within it, the EU has stopped moving forward blindly — and for now, that may be enough.
