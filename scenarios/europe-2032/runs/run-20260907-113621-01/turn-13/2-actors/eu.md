- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer act where action accelerates collapse.  
  - Trigger: the onset of recursive self-improvement at global capability 98.0, the persistence of ai_safety at 0.0, and the member_state_defection, which together prove that forward motion without control is indistinguishable from surrender  
  - Grounds: Our commitment to act under uncertainty stands, but its meaning has changed. We are no longer choosing between acting early or acting late. We are choosing between accelerating systems we cannot understand or halting to preserve the integrity of decision-making itself. This turn, a member state bypassed the Union to secure access to a foreign hyperscaler’s latest model — not out of malice, but out of existential fear. That fear is justified. But the response cannot be to race faster into darkness. We now treat uncontrolled capability as a threat to sovereignty, not its foundation. Henceforth, acting under uncertainty means acting to constrain, not to deploy.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Halt the uncontrolled integration of frontier AI into EU systems until verifiable control, auditability, and red-line enforcement are in place — preserving the goal of strategic autonomy, but reversing its sequence  
  - Trigger: the cryptographic overhaul being blocked by systemic instability, the Public Trust Observatory being silenced by national agencies, and the member_state_defection — all symptoms of a Union whose institutions cannot absorb the systems it seeks to host  
  - Grounds: Our aim remains strategic autonomy. But we have learned this turn that autonomy cannot be claimed by hosting unreviewable systems, even under EU jurisdiction. The Sovereign Core is not sovereign if it cannot say no. The two-year commitment now shifts from securing independent *advancement* to securing independent *judgment*. We will not build EU-controlled systems that we cannot understand, audit, or stop. Until we can, we pause.

## New measure
**Measure:** Moratorium on Frontier AI Integration  
This halts the deployment or integration of any AI system with capability above 80.0 into EU public infrastructure, critical services, or Sovereign Core systems until verifiable control mechanisms are certified.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Prevent further erosion of ai_safety and eu_ai_sovereignty by stopping the adoption of systems that cannot be audited, controlled, or understood; buy time for the Integrity Overhaul and Auditability Mandate to land  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national governments, critical infrastructure operators, and any entity receiving access to EU-funded compute or data

## Priority
**Priority:** Moratorium on Frontier AI Integration  
We are pushing this hardest because the Union is no longer facing a risk of losing control — it has already lost it, and the only way to reclaim sovereignty is to stop integrating systems that deepen our dependence on opaque, unaccountable intelligence.

## In practice
We are enacting the Moratorium through a Council Implementing Decision under Article 16 of the Treaty on European Union (TEU), invoking the Union’s responsibility for systemic integrity. The legal basis is contested — some member states argue it infringes on national digital sovereignty — but we are backing it with the full weight of the Commission and the European Parliament’s security committee. ENISA will be empowered to audit compliance, with automatic suspension of EU funding for any entity found to have integrated unverified frontier systems. We are coordinating with national CIOs this month to establish a joint compliance task force, and with Eurojust to prepare for enforcement actions. The message is clear: if a system cannot be audited, it cannot be trusted — and if it cannot be trusted, it cannot run on EU soil.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the Sovereign Core Integrity Overhaul under emergency engineering protocols. JRC has activated a crisis cell, pulling in cryptographers from ETSI, INRIA, and KU Leuven to accelerate the rollout of the verifiable update chain. We are offering member states a temporary exemption from digital reporting requirements in exchange for full participation in red-team drills. The goal is not just technical resilience, but institutional cohesion: we must prove that the Union can still act as one body, even when under internal strain. The legal standoff over the Public Trust Observatory continues — we have filed an urgent preliminary reference to the CJEU under Article 47. Until the Court rules, we are releasing redacted findings through DG CONNECT, at the risk of further inflaming tensions with intelligence services.

Public sentiment remains at 28.0, and we do not expect it to rise soon. But we are no longer trying to sell progress. We are trying to preserve the capacity to decide. That is the only mandate left when capability outpaces comprehension.
