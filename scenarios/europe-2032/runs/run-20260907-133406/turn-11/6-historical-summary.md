The EU declared a technological emergency under Article 122 TFEU after a critical AI outage disrupted essential services, launching the Emergency Sovereign Access Initiative to restore capabilities through repurposed HPC resources and third-country GPU procurement. However, with political capital plummeting to 1.0, member state resistance, legal challenges, and lack of coordination led to minimal reactivation of fragmented computing capacity, leaving the sovereign AI stack unverified and ineffective. The Public Trust Corps was dissolved into ENISA, damaging public trust, while the Accountability Tribunal remained nominally intact but was defunded and unenforced. A major U.S. safety breakthrough in predicting emergent AI behavior accelerated private-sector AI deployment abroad, but remained inaccessible to EU emergency systems due to export controls. RETRENCHMENT policies further restricted chip access, deepening hardware dependency and stalling domestic replication. Uncoordinated decentralized cyber efforts grew but failed to close resilience gaps, as infrastructure faced increased probing. The Union’s emergency response exposed systemic fragmentation, marking another failure to translate urgency into coherent action.
