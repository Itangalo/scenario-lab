### A Cyber Shock and a Scramble for Shields

A sophisticated, AI-generated cyber intrusion sweeps across European public health and energy coordination systems, exploiting a poisoned software dependency in widely used grid management tools. The attack unfolds faster than human teams can respond, locking critical data and disrupting scheduling across multiple Tier 1 operators. ENISA confirms the tooling was model-assisted, marking a new threshold in offensive autonomy. Public services stagger, and confidence plummets further.

In response, the EU launches the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative as a triage measure, reprofiling €45bn from expired and stalled funds. ENISA begins drafting deployment protocols for AI-hardened defence stacks featuring swarm detection and zero-trust code verification. However, rollout is delayed by legal disputes over data sovereignty and operational control—Poland and Spain resist mandatory integration, citing national security prerogatives. Only partial pre-deployment testing occurs this turn.

The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme officially concludes, but physical implementation lags. Germany and Austria file a joint referral to the ECJ over the Commission’s use of Article 194 TFEU to override ENTSO-E, casting doubt on the legality of ring-fenced power. Microgrid projects in Sweden and Brittany proceed, but at reduced scale.

The Tech Sovereignty Package and EAAD certification framework finish on schedule, delivering modest institutional gains. Yet EAAD’s authority remains constrained without real-time logging, which Italy and Hungary continue to block. Public messaging reframes resilience as survival, but displaced professionals see few concrete pathways—transition schemes are announced but not yet funded.

AI capability advances to 62.0, driven by autonomous engineering agents. Open weights reach 50.0, aided by leaked training recipes. With no safety improvements landing on deployed systems, and capability widening, assurance erodes.

### US_POSTURE: RETRENCHMENT

The incoming U.S. administration takes office, immediately pausing federal AI contracts and tightening export controls on advanced chips. Global development slows perceptibly, though non-Western labs gain ground.
