### Fractured Response to Cascading Crisis

The EU launched the **EU Crisis Compute Corps and Sovereign Red-Teaming Network**, a large category 6 measure aimed at hardening critical AI systems against failure and attack. Yet with political capital already at zero, the initiative immediately strained the Union’s capacity. Member states balked at the federal overreach, Poland refusing to host a hub, Austria blocking military cyber unit participation. Only four nations fully committed. The legal base under Article 122 TFEU was challenged in Luxembourg, delaying deployment. Initial red-teaming remained siloed, with no unified protocol.

A **cyber_major_incident** unfolded as ransomware, powered by AI-generated lateral movement, crippled health data exchanges across three member states. The attack bypassed encryption by mimicking administrative workflows, exposing that availability does not equal resilience. Emergency systems stood — but were blind to model-driven process collapse. Response was fragmented, with no central authority to trigger fallbacks.

Simultaneously, **supply_chain_coercion** intensified as Washington forced ASML to restrict servicing of older lithography tools, hitting EU industrial suppliers. This eroded compute access further, undermining sovereign replication. The **member_state_defection** of Hungary, which quietly secured bilateral GPU access from a U.S. hyperscaler under softer terms than EU policy allows, shattered cohesion.

Public sentiment collapsed further, as job losses in law and administration continued (**labour_displacement**) and media framed the red-teaming push as militarisation. The **emergent_open_source_biosecurity_tools**, while lauded in the Global South, were dismissed by EU agencies as unregulated, deepening distrust.

Despite a **safety_breakthrough** in the U.S. enabling real-time prediction of emergent behaviour, it remained inaccessible due to export controls under the **RETRENCHMENT** posture. No improvement in ai_safety followed.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Vanishes

The EU’s actions, though urgent, lacked cohesion and resources. The red-teaming network, while conceptually sound, could not overcome fragmentation. Resilience dipped as attacks outpaced defences. Sovereignty fell with capability growth and lost access. Political capital plunged into negative territory — a signal that the Union is no longer governing, but reacting.
