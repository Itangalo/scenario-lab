## In practice
We are acting on the fact that access is not control, and control is not continuity. The Emergency Sovereign Access Initiative from last turn gave us a lifeline — repurposed HPC, third-country GPUs, forked models at capability 57.0 — but the **cyber_major_incident** this turn proves that lifeline is fragile. A coordinated ransomware sweep, model-generated and adaptive, has crippled regional health data exchanges in three member states. It did not break encryption. It broke process — by mimicking administrator behaviour, escalating through AI-assisted lateral movement, and encrypting not just data but decision pipelines. Our systems were online, but they were not *resilient*. We mistook availability for security.

So this turn, we are building the muscle we are missing: continuous, sovereign red-teaming at scale. The EU Crisis Compute Corps will be staffed with 1,200 engineers — drawn from national CERTs, military cyber units, and vetted private-sector experts — operating under a new legal base in the Article 122 emergency framework. They will run 24/7 adversarial simulations on the very systems we are now depending on, probing for emergent failure modes, backdoors in forked weights, and model-induced protocol collapse. ENISA will coordinate, but the red teams will be regionally embedded, with authority to trigger fallback protocols and initiate live patching without central approval. This is not oversight — it is operational survival.

We are funding this with €4.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and redirected cohesion funds, accepting the political cost because the alternative is collapse. The Accountability Tribunal remains in flight, but its enforcement budget stays redirected: we cannot afford both justice and continuity, so we are choosing continuity. If we lose that, there will be no justice to deliver. Member states are resisting — Poland calls it federal overreach, Austria fears militarisation — but we are offering them opt-in autonomy: host a red-team hub, and gain priority access to GPU allocations and incident intelligence. This is not consensus. It is calibrated coercion. And for now, it is the only thing holding the Union together.
