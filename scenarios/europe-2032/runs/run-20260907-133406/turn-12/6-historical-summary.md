The EU launched the EU Crisis Compute Corps and Sovereign Red-Teaming Network to harden critical AI systems, but the initiative collapsed under zero political capital, member state resistance, and legal challenges, with only four nations fully participating. A major AI-powered ransomware attack crippled health data systems across three countries, exposing systemic fragility and the inability to respond cohesively. Supply chain coercion worsened as U.S. pressure on ASML restricted access to vital semiconductor tools, while Hungary’s bilateral GPU deal with a U.S. hyperscaler marked a significant member state defection. Public trust eroded further amid rising labour displacement and backlash against perceived militarisation of AI governance. Despite a U.S. safety breakthrough in predicting emergent AI behaviour, export controls under the RETRENCHMENT policy kept it out of reach. Open-source biosecurity tools gained global recognition but were ignored by EU institutions. The Union’s fragmented response deepened the crisis, eroding sovereignty, resilience, and political legitimacy, with political capital falling into negative territory—signifying a complete breakdown in effective governance.
