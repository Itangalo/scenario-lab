### A Crisis of Justice

The EU launched the AI Accountability Tribunal in response to a damning ombudsman report revealing systemic denials of social benefits due to flawed algorithmic risk scoring. Designed as a supranational redress mechanism with investigative power and a €10bn remediation fund, it aimed to restore legitimacy by anchoring trust in justice rather than mere transparency. However, deep divisions among member states over sovereignty and enforcement delayed final approval. A coalition led by Poland and Hungary demanded sweeping opt-outs, forcing concessions on cross-border authority and regional control — undermining the body’s coherence before its first case.

Meanwhile, a capability jump in narrow domains—particularly automated code generation and cyber exploitation—was demonstrated by a private lab. While general competence rose only modestly, attackers now deploy self-modifying intrusion tools that evade legacy detection. Open-weight models, benefiting from leaked training data and distributed fine-tuning, advanced further, enabling mid-tier actors to execute targeted industrial breaches.

Resilience eroded as critical infrastructure operators reported increased probing, and the partial rollout of the Sovereign Cyber Shield left gaps. The Public Trust Corps’ beta dashboard went live but suffered credibility issues after misreporting an outage in the Baltic grid.

Worse, Washington compelled the Netherlands to restrict ASML’s servicing of older lithography machines, hitting EU semiconductor fabs reliant on legacy nodes. This supply-chain coercion exposed the Union’s strategic vulnerability, accelerating hardware delays for domestic AI projects.

Public sentiment dipped further after media coverage of the social insurance scandal intensified, framing AI as an unaccountable force within public administration. Labour protests in Marseille rejected “technical fixes” while demanding accountability.

Political capital collapsed under the weight of overlapping crises. Though the new Tribunal was prioritized, fractious negotiations, combined with ongoing commitments, drained already minimal capacity.

### No Ground Gained

Ambitious action faltered in the face of institutional fragmentation, external pressure, and eroding public confidence. The EU acted—but not decisively, not cohesively, and not fast enough to shift momentum.
