## New measure
**Measure:** Launch EU AI Accountability Tribunal and Remediation Fund  
Establish a supranational legal body with investigative authority over AI-driven harms in public and hybrid systems, empowered to compel evidence, issue public findings, and order compensation. Back it with a €10bn fund for rapid redress, seeded from fines on non-compliant deployments.

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch EU AI Accountability Tribunal and Remediation Fund  
This is now our dominant effort because the automated_decision_scandal has shifted the crisis from preparedness to justice: without a credible path to redress, no amount of resilience or transparency will restore legitimacy, and no future measure will survive public scrutiny.

## In practice
We are acting on the conviction that trust is not rebuilt through systems alone, but through justice. The Public Trust Corps continues its work — the AI Incident Dashboard is now live in beta, fed by ENISA and three national CERTs, and mobile units have conducted transparency drills in Lyon and Warsaw. But turnout remains low, and in Marseille, protesters disrupted a simulation, shouting, “We don’t want rehearsals — we want answers.” They are right. The ombudsman’s report on the social insurance algorithm — which denied benefits to over 12,000 people based on flawed risk flags — has become a political earthquake. Germany and Spain demand action; Poland calls for a moratorium on all public-sector AI.

So we are launching the EU AI Accountability Tribunal under Article 45 TFEU, hosted jointly by the European Ombudsman and the EAAD, with standing investigative teams in each capital. It will begin with the social insurance case, but its mandate covers all AI-supported decisions in EU-funded or member-state-operated systems. We are allocating €3bn from the Multiannual Financial Framework and leveraging an additional €7bn from recovered fines under the AI Act — a signal that accountability must fund itself. The Tribunal will issue its first findings by turn 12, and the Remediation Fund will begin processing claims by turn 13.

This requires Council approval under the Justice and Home Affairs formation, and we are negotiating with a bloc of six member states demanding stronger national opt-outs. We are offering them co-governance of regional tribunals and a veto on cross-border enforcement — not ideal, but necessary to break the deadlock. The EIB is aligning its digitalisation lending with Tribunal compliance, making redress capacity a condition of funding. This is not just about fixing the past. It is about proving that in the EU, power without remedy is not governance — it is failure.
