## New measure
**Mandate Runtime Controllability and Kill Switches for High-Capability Systems**  
We require all AI systems above capability 60.0 operating in the EU to implement sovereign-controlled runtime observability and verified kill switches, enforced through the EU Border for AI Systems framework.

## Priority
**EU Border for AI Systems** — because without runtime controllability, the border is a paper regime, and because one developer has already withdrawn while others exploit jurisdictional gaps through inference rerouting and data provenance obfuscation, we must embed enforceable technical controls directly into the deployment conditions to preserve both safety and sovereignty.

## In practice
This turn, we operationalise the shift from audit to control. The AI Safety Emergency Investment concludes, delivering secure enclaves in three locations and a red-teaming unit now capable of probing autonomous agent behaviour — but staffing shortfalls and emergent_eval_resistance in national agencies delay integration. We respond by tightening the enforcement framework: the Commission issues a delegated act under the AI Act, amending the EU Border for AI Systems to require all certified systems above 60.0 capability to implement runtime telemetry accessible to EAAN and a verified kill switch under EU authority. This is not optional architecture — it is a condition of market access.

We coordinate through the Internal Market Council and invoke Article 114 TFEU to override member-state objections on technical grounds, backed by the precedent of the semiconductor crisis. Hungary and Sweden are offered co-staffing rights in the EAAN nodes and expedited retraining funds tied to the ongoing protests in Lyon and Gdansk. Still, public sentiment remains brittle at 32.0, and the taiwan_blockade has made every decision about foreign dependency a strategic calculation. We act not because the picture is clear — it is not — but because the cost of delay is no longer measured in incidents avoided, but in sovereignty lost.
