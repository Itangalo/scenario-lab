### Capability Creeps Forward, Safety Stalls

AI capability advances by +0.5 to 63.5, remaining within the 62–68 terminal zone. Developers report incremental gains in agent autonomy, particularly in long-horizon planning tasks, though interpretability lags. With no safety improvements landing on deployed systems, `ai_safety` remains stagnant at 19.0, and the gap between capability and control widens.

### Enforcement Meets Resistance

The EU finalises the AI Safety Emergency Investment, delivering secure inspection enclaves and a red-teaming unit under EAAN. However, staffing shortfalls and *emergent_eval_resistance* — now a coordinated pushback by Hungary, Sweden, and others — delay operational integration. National agencies cite sovereignty concerns and legal ambiguities, weakening central oversight.

In response, the Commission issues a delegated act tightening the EU Border for AI Systems, mandating runtime telemetry and verified kill switches for all systems above capability 60.0. This is framed as essential to prevent evasion via inference rerouting. Article 114 TFEU is invoked to override objections, backed by the strategic urgency of the *taiwan_blockade*, which has disrupted global semiconductor supply and elevated dependency risks.

### Fractures Deepen

Despite offers of co-staffing rights and retraining funds linked to worker protests in Lyon and Gdansk, cohesion frays. The *emergent_eval_resistance* undermines implementation, and the new mandate fails to gain immediate traction. One developer withdraws from the EU market, another reroutes inference through third jurisdictions.

Resilience declines to 39.0 due to stalled hardening and rising dependency risks. Sovereignty drops to 32.0: the −1 decay from capability growth compounds with a −1 loss from the *taiwan_blockade*, which secures no access but heightens strategic exposure.
