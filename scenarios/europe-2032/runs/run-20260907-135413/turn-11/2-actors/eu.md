## New measure
**Suspend Liability Shielding for Non-Cooperative Developers**  
We revoke conditional liability protections under the AI Liability Directive for any provider that denies full access to model weights, telemetry, or red-teaming authority required by EAAN, effective immediately upon non-compliance.

## In practice
This turn, we shift from persuasion to consequence. The Accelerate EAAN Certification Nodes initiative continues under emergency procurement, but staffing and interagency disputes persist. We are now six months behind on node activation, and the first systems operating near 63.0 capability are already fielded abroad with opaque behaviour patterns. Our credibility hinges on whether the border means anything. So we act: the Commission issues an implementing act under Article 7 of the AI Liability Directive, suspending safe-harbour protections for any provider that fails to grant EAAN auditors full access to weights, training data provenance, and runtime telemetry. This applies immediately and is enforced through national product liability regimes and DMAct enforcement channels. We coordinate with member states via the Internal Market Council to ensure uniform application.

At the same time, we face rising domestic pressure. The emergent labour unrest cannot be ignored — local protests in Lyon, Athens, and Gdansk highlight displacement in public administration and legal support roles. But we cannot respond with adoption or diffusion measures without undermining our own commitment to control. Instead, we signal that enforcement *is* protection: by holding the line on uncontrolled systems, we preserve the space in which displaced workers can retrain and re-enter under rules we still set. We brief the European Parliament’s ECON and EMPL committees jointly, arguing that sovereignty over AI is the precondition for fair transition — not a barrier to it. Still, public sentiment remains fragile, and we know that if the next incident lands in healthcare or energy, our capital may not survive it.
