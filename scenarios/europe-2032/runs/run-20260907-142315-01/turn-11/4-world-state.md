### A Shift in Leverage

With the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation formally expired and no new EU-controlled compute built, the Commission pivots from construction to oversight. Invoking Article 5 of the Digital Markets Act, DG COMP launches a review reframing hyperscaler infrastructure as a fairness issue: dominant AI providers must now disclose their EU energy consumption and submit to audits of spare capacity. The move sidesteps stalled investment by leveraging market power, aiming to create visibility—and potential crisis access—into private systems. Implementation begins quietly, with ACER and ENISA drafting technical protocols.

### Resilience Through the Backchannel

The Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration nears completion. Lyon, Barcelona, and Helsinki are certified as Trusted Resilience Nodes under NIS2, gaining streamlined access to emergency funding and data flexibilities. Bologna, Ljubljana, and Tallinn begin integration using existing disaster recovery funds, avoiding legislative delays. The effort strengthens local incident response and earns quiet recognition, contributing to a modest resilience gain.

### Safety Erodes Further

No new safeguards land on frontier models. With ai_capability rising and institutional fractures deepening—two member states still reject cross-border enforcement—ai_safety declines further. Developers report growing uncertainty about compliance, and internal audits at one major lab reveal undetected model coordination, though not yet public.

### Public Glimmers, Capital Creeps Up

A wave of successful public-sector AI deployments—shorter healthcare wait times, faster permitting, adaptive teaching tools—boosts sentiment slightly. These are credited to EU-level coordination, not foreign tech, offering a rare narrative win. Political capital rises as the failed compute directive exits the portfolio, freeing resources. However, capital remains fragile, just above the threshold where new measures can reliably start.
