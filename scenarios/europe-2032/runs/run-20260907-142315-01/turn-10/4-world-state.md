### A Directive Ends, Not a Project

The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation formally expires after failing to secure binding commitments from Germany and Ireland. No new EU-controlled compute has been built. The directive’s closure is symbolic: it ends in legal form but not material success. With political capital at 6.0, the Commission pivots from direct build-out to indirect oversight. Under Article 5 of the Digital Markets Act, DG COMP launches a post-implementation review redefining strategic AI infrastructure as a fairness issue—hyperscalers must now disclose EU energy use and submit to audits of spare capacity. The move leverages market dominance rather than state investment, aiming to create visibility into private systems that sovereign ones could not deliver.

### Bottom-Up Resilience Gains Ground

With top-down sovereignty stalled, the EU doubles down on municipal resilience. ENISA certifies Lyon, Barcelona, and Helsinki as “Trusted Resilience Nodes” under NIS2, granting them streamlined access to emergency funding and data flexibilities. Bologna, Ljubljana, and Tallinn are selected for rapid integration using existing disaster recovery budgets. The effort avoids legislative hurdles by operating within current frameworks, making quiet progress where mandates have failed.

### Safety Crumbles Further

The European Evaluation Shield fractures as two member states reject its authority to enforce remediation, citing legal overreach. This institutional rift paralyzes cross-border safety enforcement. Combined with rising capability and no landed safeguards, ai_safety drops sharply. Public and developer trust erodes further.

### Stalled Growth, Fragile Hope

Global AI capability rises slightly to 61.5, though signs of a plateau emerge as pricing shifts suggest stagnation at the frontier. openweight_capability remains at 52.0—no new leak occurs, but the persistent agent systems from earlier remain widespread. resilience sees no gain this turn; the backchannel integration continues but does not yet finish. eu_ai_sovereignty declines due to capability growth and lack of secured access. Political capital improves slightly from the closure of a large measure, but remains critically low.
