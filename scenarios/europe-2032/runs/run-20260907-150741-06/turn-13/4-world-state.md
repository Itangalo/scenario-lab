### A Sovereignty Emergency

The EU’s institutions are in triage mode. Overnight exclusion from the world’s most capable AI systems — used in hospitals, emergency planning and social services — exposed total dependency. Vienna’s cancer diagnostics stalled, Marseille’s flood-response models failed, and Helsinki’s civil protection network lost coordination. No fallback existed. Simultaneously, a damning ombudsman report revealed an AI-supported social benefits system had denied thousands of vulnerable claimants for over a year, its logic corrupted by unchecked feedback loops. Public trust eroded sharply.

The Commission declared a continuity crisis under Article 122 TFEU, proposing the Sovereign Continuity Stack (SCS): an EU-controlled AI stack for healthcare, disaster response and social administration, to run on sovereign compute with audited models, human oversight, and open logs. Funding is to be pulled from digital modernisation budgets, and procurement barriers may be bypassed via delegated acts. However, this is a proposal only — no implementation has occurred. The measure faces fierce resistance from member states with entrenched foreign contracts and from industry lobbies. Legal challenges loom over the use of emergency powers to override national procurement. The Council’s Digital Ministers delay approval, demanding impact assessments. Full rollout cannot begin before the next turn, and no effect is realized yet.

The Continuity Assurance Framework, a separate initiative completed in a prior turn, lifts resilience to 58.0, where it remains this turn — no new resilience gains are recorded. ai_safety stays at 0.0 — no safeguards landed on deployed systems. ai_capability creeps to 77.5, widening the gap. openweight_capability rises slightly to 56.0, reflecting lagging but persistent diffusion.

Sovereignty dips to 19.0: capability rose ≥2, no category 4 measure concluded, and the frontier access denial counts as a −1 capacity event. Political capital falls to −1.0, hit by a portfolio charge of −7 (Frontier Access Guarantee −3, Emergency Oversight Continuity Protocol −2, priority −1, unmet obligations −1) and a proposal bonus of +5 for addressing two active severe events (emergent_eu_aid_strike and cyber_major_incident). Public sentiment declines to 12.0, reflecting serious public concern amplified by the dual crises, but not a total collapse — the dip is substantial but consistent with observed response patterns under strain, falling short of a systemic rupture.
