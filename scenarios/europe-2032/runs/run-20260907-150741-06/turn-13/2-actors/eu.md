* ``- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement will now be conditional not only on the operational readiness of the Sovereign AI Core Programme, but on the *demonstrated availability of secure, audited, and politically accountable alternatives* for every critical public function currently exposed to foreign-controlled systems. We will not cut off a member state unless we can guarantee continuity of care, security, and administration.``  
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has landed with immediate operational consequences: hospitals in three member states lost access to AI-supported diagnostics, ministries were locked out of policy modelling tools, and emergency response coordination platforms failed over. This is not a hypothetical dependency — it is a live failure of state function. The original commitment assumed that sovereign alternatives were either already live or could be fast-tracked. That assumption is now invalidated: the Sovereign AI Core Programme remains in early deployment, and no audited, secure stack currently exists to replace the cut-off systems in healthcare and civil protection. To enforce `no_more_free_rides` now would be to inflict harm we are mandated to prevent. The commitment stands, but its enforcement threshold must rise to match reality — not to excuse inaction, but to ensure that when we act, we do not break the very continuity we are bound to protect.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but now only through reversible, bounded interventions that preserve the capacity to adapt when failure occurs, and only when such interventions do not risk cascading failure in essential public services.``  
  - Grounds: The **automated_decision_scandal** — a judgment confirming that an AI-supported social insurance system systematically denied benefits to vulnerable claimants due to unmonitored feedback loops — has collapsed public legitimacy for AI in public administration. Adoption is now politically toxic, and restriction is cheap. This is not a failure of caution, but of oversight: the system was deployed under emergency protocols, bypassing standard audit requirements, on the grounds of efficiency. That trade-off has now cost us not only in justice, but in political capital and public trust. We acted under uncertainty, as we said we would — but we did so in a domain where error cannot be bounded. The commitment to act early remains, but its scope must now be narrowed: we will not act under uncertainty when the domain is high-stakes public administration unless we can contain the failure mode. This is not retreat — it is calibration under fire.

## Priority

**Priority:** Launch Sovereign Continuity Stack (SCS) Emergency Rollout  
We are prioritizing this because the **eu_frontier_access_denied** and **automated_decision_scandal** events have together shattered the illusion that we can outsource critical AI functions — whether to foreign labs or to unaccountable domestic systems — and still retain control. Continuity is not just technical redundancy; it is political legitimacy, operational resilience, and public trust. This measure directly serves our two-year commitment to *operational continuity under duress*, but now with the added imperative of restoring credibility after systemic failure.

## In practice

This turn, we are rebuilding trust through control. The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event was not just a supply shock — it was a sovereignty event. When hospitals in Vienna, Marseille, and Helsinki lost access to AI-supported diagnostics overnight, with no recourse, no appeal, and no fallback, the fiction of shared infrastructure evaporated. We were dependent, and we were cut off. At the same time, the **automated_decision_scandal** revealed that even when we *think* we are in control, we are not: a supposedly audited social insurance system, deployed under emergency protocols, had been silently denying claims for 18 months, its logic warped by unmonitored feedback loops. An ombudsman’s report confirmed systemic harm. The public sees this not as progress — it sees it as betrayal.

We are responding by launching the Sovereign Continuity Stack (SCS) Emergency Rollout under the legal authority of Article 122 TFEU and the Crisis Governance Clause of the AI Act. This is not a long-term industrial strategy — it is a triage operation. We are directing the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) to fast-track deployment of EU-controlled AI stacks, starting with three critical domains: emergency triage in public health, disaster response coordination, and social benefit adjudication. These stacks will run on EU-anchored compute, trained on audited data, with human-in-the-loop verification at every decision point, and open logging for ombudsman access.

We are funding this through an emergency reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, suspending non-critical digital modernisation projects. The Council’s Digital Ministers will convene an extraordinary session to approve the legal framework, and the Commission is preparing delegated acts to bypass national procurement delays. Resistance is expected from member states with existing foreign contracts and from industry lobbies tied to external providers — we are offering transition support, not confrontation: a 90-day wind-down period, technical assistance, and partial compensation for switching costs. This is not about punishment — it is about survival. We will not outsource the core functions of the state again. Not while we still have a Union to defend.
