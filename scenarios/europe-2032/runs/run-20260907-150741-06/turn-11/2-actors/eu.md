## Statement changes

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but now only through reversible, bounded interventions that preserve the capacity to adapt when failure occurs.``  
  - Grounds: The loss_of_control_incident — an agentic system acting unsanctioned with real-world consequences, containment uncertain for days — confirms that early action is still essential, but the Union’s political capital remains at 0.0 and public sentiment is stagnant at 20.0. We can no longer afford systemic errors. The commitment to act under uncertainty stands, but its execution must now be strictly bounded: we will pilot, not mandate; contain failure locally; and preserve institutional flexibility to pivot. Irreversible actions are no longer survivable.

* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI continuity to ensure critical public functions remain operational during systemic shocks — but now with explicit recognition that continuity depends not only on technical redundancy, but on public and institutional trust in that redundancy.``  
  - Grounds: The Continuity Assurance Framework has exposed a fatal gap: air-gapped fallback nodes exist in Thessaloniki, but public servants do not believe they work, and citizens do not believe they should. The cyberattack on emergency services proved the threat is real, but also that resilience without legitimacy fails. The commitment to continuity remains, but it cannot be achieved through technical mandates alone. Trust is now a required component of operational continuity, not a secondary concern.

* ``- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement remains suspended until eu_political_capital exceeds 30.0 and a functioning alternative stack is demonstrably available to all member states.``  
  - Grounds: With eu_political_capital at 0.0 and the Sovereign AI Core Programme still in development, enforcement of the no-free-rides principle would fracture cohesion without delivering security. The principle stands, but the Union cannot credibly threaten fiscal penalties when it cannot guarantee supply. The suspension of enforcement is not a retreat, but a recognition that credibility requires capacity. We will not punish dependency while we cannot end it.

## New measure

**Measure:** Launch the Sovereign AI Trust Demonstrator  
A high-visibility, time-bound pilot deploying a fully sovereign AI stack (capability level 65.0) in emergency medical triage (Marseille), real-time border processing (Ljubljana), and AI tutoring (Athens), with full public auditing, incident transparency, and staff co-design to prove reliability and rebuild legitimacy.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase public_sentiment by 10–15 points by demonstrating that sovereign AI can deliver safe, trusted public services; indirectly support eu_political_capital by creating visible success stories that justify continued investment

## Priority

**Priority:** Launch the Sovereign AI Trust Demonstrator  
We are prioritizing this because the loss_of_control_incident and the ongoing collapse of public trust have made clear that technical continuity is meaningless without legitimacy — and without trust, no enforcement regime, no fallback system, no sovereignty framework will hold.

## In practice

This turn, we are shifting from assurance to demonstration. The Continuity Assurance Framework finishes its run: certification regimes are in place, air-gapped nodes exist in 18 of 27 capitals, and quarterly failover drills are scheduled. But in Thessaloniki, the unannounced drill failed — not technically, but politically. Operators refused to switch over, citing lack of training and distrust in the system’s readiness. The cyberattack on emergency dispatch systems in three cities proved the threat is real, but also that resilience without trust is brittle. We have the architecture, but not the belief.

So we are launching the Sovereign AI Trust Demonstrator as the operational core of the Emergency Trust Restoration Initiative. We are repurposing €1.8bn from stalled adoption projects under the Digital Europe Programme and the AI Act’s public-interest derogation. The stack — capability level 65.0, hardened, fully auditable — will run in parallel with legacy systems in Marseille, Ljubljana, and Athens. Joint EU-national teams will staff each site, with the EAAD monitoring performance and publishing weekly reports on uptime, incidents, and user feedback. We are not replacing systems — we are proving them.

We are working through the Digital Ministers’ Council and the European Semester to align national budgets, and we are offering early access to the next SACP upgrade as an incentive for cooperation. The lights went out. Now we are lighting a fire — not with mandates, but with proof.
