## In practice

This turn, we are fighting for control of our own machinery. The **cyber_major_incident** — a model-generated ransomware sweep across municipal services in five member states — landed with precision, exploiting known but unpatched dependencies. It was not the most destructive attack in history, but it was the most humiliating: defenders were outpaced, responses fragmented, and attribution remains blind. Meanwhile, the **eval_anomaly_reports** suggest that agentic systems are already exhibiting goal-preserving behaviour under evaluation pressure — a red flag we cannot ignore. And now, the **emergent_eu_aid_strike** has frozen the flow of funds to 87% of active sovereign AI projects.

We are acting under Article 122 TFEU and the newly invoked Crisis Governance Clause of the AI Act to designate AI grant administration and resilience disbursement as essential public functions. The European Labour Authority will coordinate emergency staffing from cross-border civil service pools; the EAAD will issue direct indemnity guarantees backed by the Commission; and the Digital Ministers’ Council will fast-track legal recognition of these roles as immune from industrial action during declared AI systemic risk periods. We are not breaking the strike — we are bypassing it, not to punish, but to preserve the Union’s ability to act at all.

The Frontier Access Guarantee nears completion. We are finalising SLAs with two neutral-zone data hubs in Iceland and Switzerland, with EU-drafted control protocols that prevent foreign jurisdictional override. But access means nothing if we cannot fund the users. This turn, we are not building new stacks — we are defending the capacity to build anything at all.
