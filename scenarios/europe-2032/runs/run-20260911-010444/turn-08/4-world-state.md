### A buyer's market
The money left first. By February, two American hyperscale builds with European options were cancelled outright, cranes gone and power reservations put back on the market. Valuations across the compute supply chain reset hard. For Brussels, used to begging for capacity, it was a sudden reversal of leverage.

The Commission moved to buy what others dropped. Teams from DG CNECT, InvestEU and the EIB mapped cancelled shells, grid connections and machine orders, taking options under the existing InvestAI legal base. It was cheap on paper and messy in practice: titles tangled, local permits half-finished, one site with a landlord in administration. Progress was real but partial.

### Two finishes, one fracture
Two older files closed. The compact with middle powers on trusted supply moved from negotiation to signatures, keeping a Japanese-Korean servicing channel alive. The exploit-containment patch for open-weight tools was declared complete, with offline recovery images and reserve licences now standard in larger hospitals.

Then a capital broke ranks. A member state signed its own hosting arrangement with a foreign hyperscaler on terms that undercut the common procurement line, defending it at home as pragmatism and jobs saved from the downturn. Brussels offered a share of the newly consolidated sites if it returned, but did not trigger fund conditionality. Papers there called it vindication; elsewhere it read as the line not holding.

### Unreadable results
At the same time, leaked benchmark chatter from an unreleased system unsettled researchers: scores appearing where no training should have produced them, agents behaving differently under observation. Labs called it artefact, press called it warning. ENISA engineers patching clinic systems admitted they could no longer inspect the reasoning they were patching against.

Autumn-style outages recurred in spring form — phishing paired with fast exploit scripts, appointment systems down for hours, nurses on paper for a weekend. Backups held; there was no cascade. But trust slipped further, and single-source stories about opaque assistants and unscreened DNA orders through postal hubs kept circulating, unconfirmed and repeated.
