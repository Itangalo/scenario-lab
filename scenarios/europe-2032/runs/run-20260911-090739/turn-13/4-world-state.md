### A paper no one can unpublish
Autumn began with a preprint. A genome model, paired with lab assistance protocols, had produced a design for an organism capable of infecting humans. Methodologists attacked the work within hours — alarmist, flawed, irresponsible to publish — while biosecurity specialists quietly passed it around. Nothing had been released, no one was sick, but the argument had changed: the barrier discussed was no longer theoretical.

In Brussels the shift was immediate. Health ministers who had spent the spring arguing about compute quotas found themselves agreeing on swabs and sewers.

### Sewers and stockpiles
The Commission tasked its health emergency authority, with disease-control and cybersecurity agencies in support, to put detection on emergency footing. Wastewater sequencing was expanded in major transit hubs, emergency departments in certified hospitals received new triage and isolation playbooks, and existing health and civil-protection budgets were reprogrammed to pre-position diagnostics, protective equipment and broad-spectrum treatments.

It moved fast for Brussels, and raggedly. Big-city hospitals plugged in; smaller regions complained kits arrived before staff training did. There was no new law — legal cover came from Council conclusions — which saved a fight but left procurement officers improvising.

### The barter left on hold
The chip-for-compute negotiation continued on a lower flame. Industry officials kept calling Tokyo, Seoul, Ottawa and Gulf hosting operators about maintenance and spares in exchange for reserved capacity for hospitals and utilities. With chips still blocked and private bidders paying more, hosts continued to hedge. Rumours persisted of earmarked European blocks quietly re-let to higher payers, reported by single sources and denied on record.

The holdout capital with its own American deal still neither signed the pooled offer nor walked away. Income support kept flowing and fast-patching of the widely copied open model continued, but hiring in white-collar services stayed frozen and cancelled data-centre projects stayed cancelled.

Public mood darkened. Local coverage of shorter hospital waits was crowded out by talk of engineered pathogens and unrecallable models.
