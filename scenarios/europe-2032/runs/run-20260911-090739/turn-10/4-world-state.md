### Patch Tuesday, every day
The winter brought the first good defensive news in years. Research teams on both sides of the Atlantic showed tooling that patched common software flaws almost as fast as they were found, and that spotted coordinated agent swarms by behaviour rather than by signature. ENISA moved quickly to push the techniques through certified hospitals and larger utilities where containment drills already worked. There, spring exercises went noticeably better: suspect orders isolated in minutes.

In smaller municipalities the picture stayed mixed. Unpatched systems and thin staff meant the new detectors raised more alarms than anyone could chase, and two town utilities spent weeks distinguishing a real intrusion attempt from a misconfigured assistant.

### A recipe argument
At the same time a contested biosecurity paper rattled health officials. A genome model, with non-expert prompting, had produced what its authors called a viable design for a human-infecting organism. Critics called the methods alarmist and the publication reckless. Inside HERA and the ECDC the debate was narrower: detection stocks were checked, sampling protocols tightened, and samples and evaluation tasks were quietly shared with partners in Japan, Korea and Canada for joint black-box testing.

No illness followed, but headlines did.

### Paychecks for the missing cohort
The political fight stayed on jobs. With graduate hiring still frozen in law, accountancy, software and customer operations, labour and finance ministers agreed a capped, temporary guarantee: EU co-financed income bridging, six-month retraining vouchers and first-job credits in the four frozen sectors, with large deployers of assistants required to report automation levels to tie them to the fund.

Rollout was uneven. Denmark, France and Spain opened voucher desks by May; elsewhere budget fights and employer pushback slowed hiring credits. Graduate fairs remained thin, but the first cheques and training places eased the worst anger. The holdout capital with its separate supply arrangement still neither refused nor signed the offer of pooled hosting volume, leaving the middle-power compact running but incomplete.
