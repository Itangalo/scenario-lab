### The Compute Gambit

Frontier AI capability edges into the 60.0 range, marking the threshold where autonomous multi-day research projects become routine. Breakthroughs in self-supervised reasoning and recursive optimisation at leading labs enable closed systems to now complete full scientific cycles—from hypothesis to validation—in domains like materials science and synthetic biology. The open frontier lags, with openweight capability rising only to 47.5, reflecting partial diffusion of last year’s models under constrained compute and growing interest in adversarial robustness.

AI safety deteriorates to 20.0. Despite the activation of the Sovereign Evaluation Corps framework, no inspections have occurred, and anomalous agent behaviours in test environments go unaddressed. The gap between capability and control widens, feeding expert alarm.

The EU declares a turning point. The **Frontier Access Guarantee** and **Emergency Resilience Surge** formally conclude, delivering limited but tangible gains: sovereign fallback protocols are codified, and red-teaming simulations expose critical vulnerabilities in energy grid AI controllers. These completions free political capital and validate parts of the resilience agenda.

In response to physical protests at data centres and Hungary’s continued alignment with U.S. hyperscalers, the Commission launches the **Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive**, invoking emergency powers to override national permitting and mandate compute repurposing. Finland, France, and Poland fast-track grid access for new sovereign data centres, while Germany and Italy begin disclosing idle national capacity. However, Lisbon challenges the directive’s legality, and Budapest refuses to comply, citing sovereignty concerns.

A nascent **middle_power_coalition** with Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan strengthens export leverage on AI accelerators, enabling limited bargaining with U.S. firms. This coordination is credited with securing a provisional data-sharing pact on frontier model evaluations.

Yet public sentiment dips to 32.0 as protests intensify in rural France and northern Germany, where communities denounce AI infrastructure as a threat to energy stability and local control. The perception of democratic overreach shadows the Union’s assertive stance.

### Fractures and Footholds

Despite resistance, the EU consolidates enough national compute to marginally boost sovereignty. Resilience improves as red-teaming yields actionable defences. But the political cost is steep: five active measures strain thin capital, and emergency powers fuel backlash.

The world enters a new phase—autonomous AI is here, but control is not.
