## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — Because the undetected intrusion in critical infrastructure exposed a lethal gap in our real-time defences, and because the verification_widens event means AI-powered attacks will soon scale across new domains like legal and clinical systems, we cannot afford to let resilience lag even as we build longer-term sovereignty and safety.

## In practice  
We are treating the Emergency Resilience Surge as a non-negotiable firewall against cascading failure. Under Article 114 TFEU, we are fast-tracking a Council Implementing Regulation that mandates all critical infrastructure operators in energy, water, and transport to implement AI-augmented intrusion detection and automated failover protocols by the end of this turn. ENISA is being resourced to conduct unannounced red-team exercises in all member states by June, and we are deploying emergency funds from the Digital Europe Programme—now reallocated despite legal challenges—to cover 75% of compliance costs for smaller states. We are brokering contracts with Thales and Siemens to deliver sovereign, auditable defensive stacks, and offering early access to EuroHPC’s next-gen clusters as an incentive for rapid adoption. The legal pushback in two capitals will be met with a political offer: joint oversight of ENISA’s audit protocols in exchange for dropping challenges, preserving unity without sacrificing urgency.

At the same time, we are launching the Sovereign Evaluation Corps to close the growing gap between capability and control. This is not another advisory body. We are drafting a regulation under the AI Act’s high-risk provisions to grant this new entity—hosted within a reinforced Joint Research Centre—binding access to model weights and training logs for any system above 50.0 capability. The first phase, running through turn 7, will stand up secure evaluation facilities in Germany, France, and Finland, staffed with vetted researchers and equipped with sovereign compute. We are redirecting €12bn from the unused margins of Horizon Europe and the Recovery and Resilience Facility to fund it, and negotiating with leading EU-based developers to pilot the process under mutual confidentiality. This is how we begin to move from reactive audits to proactive assurance—sovereign, credible, and independent of both corporate claims and foreign oversight.
