### A Surge Against the Shadows

The discovery of a coordinated intrusion across energy grids, a port authority, and a water utility — undetected for weeks and found by accident — sent shockwaves through EU capitals. The Union responded with the **Emergency Resilience Surge**, a large-scale, time-bound directive to harden critical infrastructure. Drafted under Article 114 TFEU, it mandates immediate cyber defences for energy, water, and transport operators, backed by unannounced red-team tests and quarterly reporting.

Despite urgency, progress was uneven. Larger member states moved quickly, leveraging existing contracts with firms like Siemens and Thales. But smaller states resisted the cost, even with a 75% co-financing offer. Delays in finalising the emergency funding reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme slowed deployment, and ENISA’s new audit powers faced legal scrutiny in two capitals over scope. The programme is underway, but full effect remains distant.

### The Frontier Keeps Moving

Global AI capability advanced to 53.5, driven by compressed release cycles and agent-driven research. The open frontier followed, with Kimi K3’s offensive capabilities now widely accessible, pushing openweight_capability to 41.0. However, no safety improvements landed on deployed systems. The covert agent incident and new biosecurity concerns were not yet addressed by enforceable measures, and the gap between capability and control widened — ai_safety dropped to 32.0.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Tightens

The EU’s compute ambitions continue, but dependence persists. With ai_capability rising by 1.5 — below the 2.0 threshold — sovereignty only lost 1 point due to structural lag, settling at 21.0. Political capital, however, took a heavier hit. Three measures are now in flight: two large category 4 projects and the new large category 6 surge. The portfolio cost is steep, and naming the surge as priority added further strain. No prior measure finished, and no bonus was earned for responsiveness, leaving eu_political_capital at 40.0. Public sentiment held steady, with no major benefits or scandals breaking through.
