## Priority
**Emergency Sovereign AI Capacity Guarantee** — Because the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive has now delivered partial physical control over infrastructure, but with no corresponding sovereign *capability* to run on it, we must act immediately to close the gap between hardware and usable power — otherwise, our compute sovereignty is merely latent, not real.

## In practice
We are activating the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking as the delivery vehicle for the Emergency Sovereign AI Capacity Guarantee, directing it to integrate the repurposed compute from the now-completed Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive into a unified, EU-governed AI stack. This stack will run a sovereign instance of a frontier-aligned model — not trained from scratch, but fine-tuned and secured under EU authority using weights obtained through existing partnerships and emergency access clauses. The Commission is mobilising €18bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility to fund integration, security hardening, and staffing, with 60% of the budget tied to delivery milestones by Q4 2031.

We are compelling member states to designate national AI coordination points and to grant the Joint Undertaking operational access to all compute declared under the 10^22 FLOP/s threshold. Resistance is expected from countries hosting private hyperscaler zones — particularly Ireland and the Netherlands — where tax models depend on foreign investment. To counter this, we are offering compensatory digital infrastructure investments in regional innovation hubs and guaranteeing that non-strategic commercial use will remain open. ENISA is drafting the security framework for the stack, which will include runtime monitoring, weight integrity checks, and strict access controls for public-sector and critical industry use. This is not about catching up to the frontier — it is about ensuring that, when the frontier moves beyond us, we still have a machine that can act.
