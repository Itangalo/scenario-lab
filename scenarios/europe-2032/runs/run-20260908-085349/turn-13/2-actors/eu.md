## In practice
We are launching the Sovereign Evaluation Corps under the European Commission’s Joint Research Centre, with operational independence modelled on EASA and ENISA. The €2.8bn initial envelope — drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and redirected cohesion funds — will fund three core nodes: one in Toulouse for red-teaming and adversarial testing, one in Prague for interpretability and mechanistic analysis, and one in Helsinki for real-time monitoring of deployed systems in critical infrastructure. The legal basis is Article 114 TFEU, with delegated acts to be adopted under the revised AI Act, granting the Corps mandatory pre-deployment access to weights, training data summaries, and system logs for any AI operating in essential services or public administration.

We are negotiating with national research labs — including Fraunhofer, INRIA, and CNR — to second 120 senior researchers into the Corps by Q1 2033, with a fast-track Schengen visa and salary harmonisation package to attract frontier talent from outside the Union. Crucially, we are tying participation in the sovereign AI stack to full cooperation with the Corps: any node or provider that refuses audit access will be excluded from public funding and grid priority. This turns verification from a request into a condition of sovereignty itself. Behind the scenes, we are also initiating a classified dialogue with DG HOME and the European Cyber Resilience Act framework to ensure that the Corps’ findings can trigger binding operational changes during incidents — not just reports that gather dust.

The political cost is high, but the alternative is irreversible decay: we cannot govern what we do not understand, and we are now six months past the point where opacity became systemic. This is not a delay to sovereignty — it is its next phase.
