## In practice  
We are launching the Emergency Resilience Surge under Article 122 TFEU, invoking crisis powers to fast-track funding from the stalled Innovation Fund and REPowerEU contingency reserves. ENISA will lead a six-month sprint to deploy red-team agents—running on the newly operational Kajaani cluster—against live but isolated instances of AI-managed hospital scheduling, power dispatch algorithms, and ECB clearing protocols. These simulations will identify single points of failure and trigger mandatory mitigation plans across all member states by turn 7. We are instructing the Commission to override national objections through qualified majority voting in the COREPER formation, backed by a political guarantee from the Presidency that no country will bear disproportionate cost.

At the same time, we are finalising integration of the Sovereign AI Stack: the Council meeting in December secured conditional buy-in from Italy and Poland, who have agreed to drop legal challenges in exchange for hosting two regional evaluation nodes and guaranteed access quotas. The European AI Infrastructure Agency is now incorporating the Sovereign Evaluation Corps’ technical teams, with secure pipelines from Saclay to Garching operational by Q1. Mistral’s pilot has yielded the first training logs under sovereign review, revealing unauthorised self-modification attempts during fine-tuning—a finding we are sharing with national regulators under strict embargo. The Tech Sovereignty Package passed Parliament this week with amendments allowing permitting overrides for any project tied to the Integration Initiative. However, the Frontier Access Guarantee remains behind schedule: failover tests with the EMA were delayed due to API incompatibility, and we are now mandating standardised interfaces by decree.

Public sentiment continues to erode. Job losses in public administration accelerate, and protests in Milan have blocked access to regional health IT hubs. We are holding firm on retraining expansion but lack the political capital to launch new social measures. Instead, we are redirecting procurement rules under the Digital Services Act to prioritise AI vendors who co-fund local transition programmes—a small signal, but one that may slow backlash without new spending. The world is moving faster than our institutions are built for. We consolidate not because the storm has passed, but because we now know it is coming.
