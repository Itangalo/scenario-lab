### A Narrow Window, Widely Strained

Global AI capability inches to 57.5, sustained by narrow automation gains in diagnostics and contract analysis, but the broader picture remains one of stagnation. Venture capital has dried up, major labs delay releases, and training costs continue to fall—signs that the frontier is not advancing, but neither is it standing still. The plateau persists, offering a fragile reprieve.

Open-weight models see no meaningful progress. While a few small-scale replication efforts are attempted in Europe and India, they fail to yield measurable gains due to persistent compute bottlenecks and fragmented collaboration. The frontier’s slight advance does not translate into open access, and without coordinated investment or infrastructure, `openweight_capability` remains flat at 45.0. The gap with proprietary systems remains wide—and slowly widening.

In Brussels, the EU completes the Sovereign Compute Reserve: a cluster in Kajaani, built from decommissioned assets, now delivers 1.2 exaflops of training capacity under EuroHPC control. Integration efforts have begun, though legal disputes between member states delay unified command protocols. Italy and Poland resist ceding oversight, and ENISA’s cyber mandate clashes with the new agency’s authority. Still, partial progress is acknowledged—some interoperability frameworks are agreed in principle, and technical teams begin joint testing. This limited forward motion, against headwinds, supports a modest adjustment in `eu_ai_sovereignty` to 14.0.

The Sovereign Evaluation Corps finalises its legal framework, mandating training log access—but only for developers using sovereign compute. Mistral agrees to a pilot, though Aleph Alpha hesitates, citing national security concerns. Meanwhile, the Frontier Access Guarantee advances slowly; failover tests with the ECB are postponed due to interoperability issues.

Political capital erodes further. Five large initiatives remain in flight, including the new Integration Initiative. The named priority fails to accelerate progress, and the Commission struggles to maintain cohesion. A special Council meeting in December yields only partial agreements, with key votes deferred.
