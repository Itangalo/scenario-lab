## New measure  
**Sovereign Compute Reserve**  
Stand up a state-owned, EU-operated compute cluster — funded, powered, and secured within Union jurisdiction — to absorb stranded capacity from collapsed ventures and provide guaranteed access to frontier-level training runs for EU-aligned developers.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`

`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect: eu_ai_sovereignty +20, openweight_capability +5 (by enabling EU-led open releases), public_sentiment +5 (by demonstrating tangible progress in autonomy)`  
`Applies to: EU member states, public research institutions, and EU-based AI developers meeting ethical and security criteria`

## Priority  
**Sovereign Compute Reserve** — Because the ai_investment_collapse has frozen private compute expansion while leaving infrastructure and talent stranded, this is the moment to capture value from the downturn and convert it into sovereign capacity — a move that directly advances our two-year commitment while the frontier is paused.

## In practice  
We are completing the Emergency Resilience Surge with mixed results: while 22 of 27 member states now have AI-augmented intrusion detection active in energy and water systems, legal compromises in five states have limited real-time response authority. ENISA’s red-team exercises revealed persistent gaps in municipal-level systems, and we are now routing emergency funds through the Recovery and Resilience Facility to close them by turn 5. The dashboard at the Crisis Coordination Centre shows compliance at 81%, and we are treating this as a partial success — it bought time, but not immunity.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the Sovereign Evaluation Corps by securing Horizon Europe funds to cover compliance costs for Mistral and Aleph Alpha, who have agreed to pilot evaluations under mutual confidentiality. The legal draft for binding access to training logs is now under review by the Commission’s Legal Service, with a proposal scheduled for Q3 2028. We are also fast-tracking the construction of secure facilities in Garching, Saclay, and Kajaani, leveraging EuroHPC’s procurement framework to bypass national delays.

Now, with the Frontier Access Guarantee in flight and the Sovereign Compute Reserve newly launched, we are acting on the investment collapse. We are invoking Article 122 TFEU to redirect €45bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Innovation Fund to acquire idle GPU clusters from failed ventures, particularly in Germany and France. This compute will be pooled under a new entity — the European AI Infrastructure Agency — hosted within EuroHPC, with power secured through long-term PPAs with French nuclear and Nordic geothermal providers. The first cluster will come online in turn 5, and we are already in talks with four EU-based developers to anchor training runs, offering subsidized access in exchange for weight-sharing and evaluation cooperation. This is not a bailout — it is a strategic acquisition in a downturn, turning private failure into public leverage.
