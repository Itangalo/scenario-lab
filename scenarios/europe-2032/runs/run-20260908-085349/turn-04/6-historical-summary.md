Global AI capability reaches 57.0, with automation advances in legal and medical sectors offset by stagnation signals: underwhelming model releases, collapsing training costs, and a venture funding freeze halting major compute projects. The frontier's rapid growth appears to plateau. In the EU, the Emergency Resilience Surge ends with partial success—22 member states deploy AI cyber defences in critical infrastructure, but legal disputes leave five nations behind and municipal systems exposed, despite a modest resilience improvement. The EU launches the Sovereign Compute Reserve, redirecting €45bn to acquire stranded GPU clusters from failed startups, drawing support from France and Germany but facing opposition from Italy and Poland over centralization and legal challenges on state aid. The Sovereign Evaluation Corps secures pilot evaluation agreements with Mistral and Aleph Alpha, though training log access remains blocked. Public cooperation is touted, but private skepticism about enforcement grows. Widespread displacement of mid-level administrative jobs in public health and legal sectors triggers overwhelmed retraining programs, regional unemployment spikes, and protests in Lyon and Munich, eroding public trust and causing a sharp decline in AI sentiment. Amid collapsing private investment, restricted access to U.S. frontier models, and fragmented policy execution, EU AI sovereignty weakens. Political capital continues to decline, undermined by five concurrent major initiatives and falling public support, limiting capacity for further action.
