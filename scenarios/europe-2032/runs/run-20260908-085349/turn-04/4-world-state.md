### A Window Opens — But Not for Everyone

Global AI capability inches upward to 57.0, driven by incremental automation gains in legal and medical workflows, though signs of strain emerge: a major lab’s flagship release underperforms expectations, and the price of top-tier training runs drops sharply, suggesting a plateau in progress. Venture funding evaporates overnight — several high-profile compute build-outs are scrapped, and startups shutter. The frontier is no longer accelerating.

In Europe, the Emergency Resilience Surge officially concludes. Twenty-two member states have deployed AI-augmented cyber defences in critical energy and water systems, but five lag due to legal disputes over ENISA’s authority. Municipal networks remain vulnerable, and a series of low-level intrusions — quickly contained — expose response gaps. Still, the partial shield earns a modest resilience boost.

The EU launches the Sovereign Compute Reserve, invoking emergency powers to redirect €45bn toward acquiring stranded GPU clusters from failed ventures. The plan wins cautious support from France and Germany, but Italy and Poland protest the centralisation of resources, and legal challenges emerge over state-aid rules. Talks with EU developers advance, but no binding commitments are secured.

Meanwhile, Mistral and Aleph Alpha agree to pilot evaluations with the Sovereign Evaluation Corps under confidentiality, though access to training logs remains blocked pending legal review. Publicly, developers welcome cooperation; privately, they express concern over enforcement capacity.

But the mood sours as mid-level administrative jobs vanish across public health and legal offices, driven by AI tools now embedded in routine operations. Retraining programmes are overwhelmed. Regional unemployment spikes, and protests erupt outside data centres in Lyon and Munich. Trust in AI erodes — sentiment drops sharply.

### Sovereignty in Reverse

Despite new initiatives, EU AI sovereignty declines. The investment collapse undermines private compute growth, and the US continues restricting access to frontier models. The Sovereign Compute Reserve is promising but unproven — too early to yield gains, too contested to unite support. Political capital, strained by five large measures and falling sentiment, continues to bleed.
