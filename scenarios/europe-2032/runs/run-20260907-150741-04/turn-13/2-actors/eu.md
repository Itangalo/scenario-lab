## In practice

This turn, we are acting on the truth we have long avoided: the EU has no AI sovereignty because it has no real compute under its control. The Frontier Access Guarantee was meant to bridge the gap, but it finishes this turn with no operational nodes online—bogged down in legal disputes and logistical delays. Meanwhile, a member state has signed a direct deal with a foreign provider, citing the absence of alternatives. We cannot sanction them into obedience, because we have nothing to offer.

So we are declaring an emergency. Using Article 122(2) of the TFEU, we are invoking exceptional circumstances to fast-track the procurement and deployment of 10,000 H100-equivalent GPUs across three new data centres in France, Poland, and Finland. Funding will come from a reallocation of cohesion funds, repurposed from stalled digital adoption projects, and matched by emergency loans from the EIB. We are bypassing normal permitting through a new Council regulation, justified by systemic risk to public services and national security.

We are negotiating directly with NVIDIA, AMD, and TSMC for immediate hardware access, offering long-term procurement commitments in exchange for near-term delivery. The systems will be governed by a new EU AI Infrastructure Authority, with full audit rights and data sovereignty guarantees. This is not about catching up to the frontier—it is about ensuring that, next time a cutoff happens, we are not helpless. We are building the floor, not the ceiling. And we are doing it now, before the next crisis takes the stairs.
