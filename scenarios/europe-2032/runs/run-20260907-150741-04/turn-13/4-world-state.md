### The Emergency That Was Not Met

The European Union declares an emergency. Faced with a member state’s formal bypass of its AI sovereignty framework and the collapse of public-sector AI cooperation, Brussels invokes Article 122(2) to launch the Emergency Compute Mobilisation: 10,000 GPUs to be deployed by turn 15. But political capital stands at 0.0 — consumed by the lingering costs of stalled initiatives — and the new measure immediately deepens the deficit.

The narrative of urgency clashes with material reality. Cohesion funds are frozen in legal disputes over their repurposing; member states argue the reallocation violates structural spending rules. Fast-tracking permitting triggers lawsuits from environmental agencies in Finland and Poland. NVIDIA offers hardware only under US export licensing, now tightened under **export_control_escalation**, which excludes even allied buyers without volume commitments the EU cannot yet make. TSMC refuses direct sales, citing Washington’s tiered access policy. No chips are delivered.

Meanwhile, **eu_frontier_access_denied** lands with full force. Hospitals relying on foreign models for diagnostics face outages. Ministries using AI for legislative analysis revert to manual workflows. The Frontier Access Guarantee, meant to prevent this, finishes this turn — but no operational nodes exist. It is a paper shield.

A second member state begins quiet negotiations with a US hyperscaler, citing “critical infrastructure continuity.” The commitment *no more_free_rides* fractures further, unenforceable without alternatives.

Public sentiment dips to 18.0 as media frame the emergency mobilisation as desperation. The institutional revolt spreads: civil servants in Germany and Belgium decline to input data into any AI-assisted workflow, calling them “unaccountable black boxes.”

Resilience erodes as another cyber intrusion — likely enabled by open-weight model misuse — disrupts transport scheduling in two capitals. With no category 6 measure delivering tangible upgrades, the system absorbs less than before.

Sovereignty declines: despite intent, no capacity is secured, and **ai_capability** rises by 2.0, triggering the decay term. The US posture of **CONSOLIDATION** ensures dependence remains costly.

There is movement, but no momentum.
