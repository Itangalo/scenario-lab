### A Fragile Pivot

The EU formally concludes the Continuity Assurance Framework, marking it a paper victory with no operational teeth. Two member states refused certification, and a drill in Thessaloniki exposed fatal hesitation: no official would authorize the fallback system to activate, even in simulation. With political capital at zero, enforcement collapses. The framework’s architecture survives, but its authority does not.

In response, the EU launches the SACP Public Assurance Pilots in Marseille, Ljubljana, and Athens—rebranding the stalled Trust Restoration Initiative as a transparency campaign. The goal is clear: prove sovereign AI works by showing it in action. Emergency triage, border processing, and AI tutoring are selected for high visibility. Performance dashboards go live, and weekly reports are promised.

But momentum stalls. The emergent eu_aid_strike intensifies: public-sector AI teams across member states begin coordinated work-to-rule actions. They refuse integration prep, delay data sharing, and withhold validation—citing unsafe deployment conditions and lack of liability protection. In Marseille, ambulance dispatch algorithms remain offline. In Ljubljana, border logs go unverified. Only Athens sees partial rollout, with tutors operating under strict caps.

The emergent_global_ai_watchdog gains traction, publishing real-time risk scores and exposing undocumented agent coordination in a major lab. Its credibility grows as parliaments in Germany and Belgium begin citing its reports. Yet no formal cooperation emerges with EU institutions, which remain paralyzed.

Political capital remains frozen at 0.0. The portfolio charge continues: −3 for Frontier Access Guarantee, −2 for the new SACP Public Assurance Pilots, −1 for priority = −6 total. No proposal bonus applies—the measure is small and answers a slow-burning crisis, not a fresh shock. Public sentiment holds at 20.0; no visible benefit has yet landed.
