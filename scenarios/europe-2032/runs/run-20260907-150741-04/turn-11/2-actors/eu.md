## In practice

This turn, we are pivoting from architecture to proof. The Continuity Assurance Framework has officially concluded — a binding regime on paper, but in practice, stillborn. Two member states refused certification, and the Thessaloniki drill showed that even where systems exist, no one trusts them to activate. The framework’s logic was sound: air-gapped fallbacks, quarterly testing, central oversight. But it assumed compliance would follow from threat. It did not. With political capital at zero, we have no leverage to enforce. So we shift: from rule to demonstration, from penalty to persuasion.

We are launching the SACP Public Assurance Pilots in Marseille, Ljubljana, and Athens — the same sites as the stalled Trust Restoration Initiative, but now with a sharper focus: prove that sovereign AI can run safely, visibly, and reliably in real time. In Marseille, the SACP will manage emergency triage during peak ambulance load, with human-in-the-loop validation and live performance dashboards published by the EAAD. In Ljubljana, it will process Schengen border entries during holiday surges, with real-time audit logs shared with national oversight bodies. In Athens, AI tutors will operate under strict usage caps, with weekly reports on student engagement and error rates published by the Ministry of Education.

We are funding this through repurposed Digital Europe Programme allocations — €1.2bn redirected from stalled adoption projects — and working through the Digital Ministers’ Council to secure national buy-in. The offer is clear: early access to the next SACP upgrade (capability level 68.0) for full participation, and a seat on the EU AI Assurance Board for transparent feedback. No new penalties. No new mandates. Just proof. The lights went out. Now we are showing the circuit works.
