### Cut off
In February, administrators in hospitals, ministries and logistics firms found the leading American model refusing their logins. No explanation, no appeal channel, only a short notice about revised availability tiers. Teams that had built triage summaries, procurement drafting and clinic scheduling on top of it scrambled for workarounds.

Brussels chose not to escalate. The Commission kept fallback routines running, told health authorities to stay on European-hosted tools, and repeated that steady office assistants were the real destination, not a stepping stone. In private, officials admitted there was no substitute to offer.

The humiliation landed harder than the technical loss. Talk shows replayed ministers touring showcase clinics that had gone dark in the autumn and were now cut off in the spring. Crowds that had blamed Brussels for frozen screens now blamed it for building on borrowed ground.

### A seat in the pooled command
The one opening came from partners hit by the same automated extortion wave. They pooled live telemetry, samples and patch feeds into a joint cyber command and invited the Union in.

The Council accepted within weeks. A dozen analysts from the Union's cyber agency and the emergency response team were seconded, municipal telemetry and manual-isolation reporting were plugged into the shared feed, and showcase clinics were put first in line for warnings.

It did not restore systems overnight. Patches still lagged, attribution remained thin, and the freely circulating frontier-matching model meant new variants kept appearing. But night-shift operators began receiving alerts hours earlier, and two further sweep attempts were contained before reception desks froze.

### Ordinary offices
Meanwhile, studies of law firms, accountancies, newsrooms and consultancies showed clear gains: junior staff finishing drafts faster, no wave of layoffs following. The Commission seized on the figures as vindication for useful, finished tools.

The public did not. With trust at rock bottom, good news about productivity read as proof that gains would stall while dependence deepened. Dividend showcase events drew sparse, hostile audiences, and rumours persisted of utilities skipping drills to save money and of over-quota chips changing hands through intermediaries.

By June the Union was still standing, connected to a protection it could not build alone, and poorer in leverage than before.
