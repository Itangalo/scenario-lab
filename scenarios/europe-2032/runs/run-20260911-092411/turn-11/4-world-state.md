### The sweep and the leak
Autumn brought two blows at once. A largely automated ransomware sweep rolled across municipal services, clinics and logistics firms in half a dozen member states, exploiting a compromised software component. Screens froze at reception desks, prescriptions queued on paper, and in several cities the showcase health pilots Brussels had been touring went dark for days.

At the same time a new openly downloadable model matching the closed frontier spread to hundreds of thousands of machines in a week. Security services warned that the tooling behind the sweep had been generated with its help, and that no recall was possible.

Defenders were visibly behind. Attribution stalled, patches lagged, and evening news cut between locked hospital terminals and queues at reception desks.

### What held
The fallback network adopted the previous half-year paid out. Cross-border fuel and spares reimbursement kept flowing, breaker-log repairs were rushed to sites where outages and the digital sweep overlapped, and manual-isolation drills turned into real operations. Hospitals degraded — lights dimmed, non-urgent care delayed — but did not stop.

The Commission threw its one remaining instrument at keeping the pilots alive. Waiting-list dashboards stayed published under EU badges, guard details were extended to showcase clinics first, and representation offices ran a steady stream of visits crediting European hosting. Where the numbers held, mayors defended Brussels; where systems stayed down, crowds blamed it.

Whispers grew that some municipal utilities were quietly skipping drills to save guard costs, and that over-quota chips were quietly available through intermediaries at steep markups. Neither rumour could be confirmed, but both circulated in council corridors.

By December the common line had survived, brittle and exhausted. Restoration had proved the plan was more than paper, yet trust had slipped further where the freezes dragged on, and Brussels gained only a small measure of credit for keeping essential services running.
