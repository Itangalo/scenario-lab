### Patching at machine speed
The spring belonged to the defenders for once. A new generation of automated patching and swarm-behaviour detection, built in European labs and tested through the winter attacks, began closing whole classes of intrusion as fast as vulnerabilities were found. ENISA engineers described it as the first time in years the balance had moved their way.

Brussels moved quickly to ride it. A small Scale-Up programme was approved to carry the new stacks out through existing emergency channels, with fly-in teams bringing patching and detection with them as they finished restoration work.

### Restoration, uneven
In the dozen cities hit hardest in the autumn, certificates, appointments and benefit payments came back online street by street. Where hardening had held, recovery was visibly faster; where backups had never been tested, teams rebuilt networks from undocumented diagrams and exhausted staff.

Certified hospitals reported fewer cascades and faster isolation. Uncertified municipalities stayed manual for weeks longer, and insurers made co-funding conditional on re-tests that many failed. No new fund was opened; unspent exercise money was re-tasked.

The gigafactory backup site stayed in permitting amid fresh protests. Interim hosting for workloads cut off by American providers held, but no large-scale capacity was signed. A mapping of the graduate hiring freeze was promised for later in the year, which did little for protests in Paris, Madrid and Warsaw.

The breakthrough did not end the threat, but it gave the Union a visible restoration win. Hostility to AI softened slightly where services returned, though scepticism about jobs and expansion remained dominant.
