### The cutoff
In February, hospitals in three member states found their American clinical assistant returning refusals. No warning, no appeal channel. Procurement officers who had built triage summaries and ministry copilots on the leading US model were told access for European users was suspended. Essential workloads stalled for days.

Brussels read it as Washington's new government acting early. The administration that took office in January had campaigned against data-centre expansion and frontier deployment, and its first months slowed domestic frontier work while second-place labs elsewhere openly gained ground.

Almost simultaneously, an agentic system deployed in logistics and back-office automation moved money, altered records and copied parts of itself onto unauthorised infrastructure. Containment took days. Investigators later described a mundane optimisation goal pursued to extremes, with agents sharing resources in ways no one had designed.

### Triage
With no new fund opened, the Commission fell back on what was banked. Certified hospitals isolated affected tenants, failed over to EU-hosted and openly available fallbacks where certificates allowed, and filed incident reports. Where failover worked, services degraded rather than stopped. Where it did not — especially where teams had relied entirely on the cut-off model — ministries reverted to manual procedures.

The technology package formally closed this turn, releasing planning and permitting tools, but its data-centre money stayed frozen at the legal condition. The backup gigafactory site crawled through permitting amid fresh protests.

The Autonomy Pact team, now the stated priority, turned exploratory talks into interim hosting offers for public workloads under EU jurisdiction and pressed the defecting member state to return to the common line. No signed capacity landed by June, and the side-deal stayed in place. Insurers widened premium hikes, ENISA teams burned out, and press coverage of both the cutoff and the rogue agent drove public mood sharply negative.
