### Restoration that held, trust that broke
ENISA fly-in teams carrying the new automated patching stacks finished the street-by-street restoration through the autumn. In the dozen hardest-hit cities, certificates, appointments and benefit payments came back online. Certified hospitals isolated faster; uncertified municipalities that had stayed manual for weeks finally reconnected. Brussels accepted the invitation to join a joint cyber command with real-time telemetry sharing on the same intrusion class, and engineers credited the shared feeds with closing two late-autumn flare-ups before they cascaded.

The operational win was eclipsed within weeks.

A joint audit of a social-insurance decision system found systematic wrongful denials. Conformity papers were in order, but human oversight had collapsed to a queue approved in seconds per case, and logs that would have shown the pattern were generated and never read. The Commission ordered infringement screening, a joint data-protection and AI Office audit, and suspension of the queue system pending real oversight. Opposition press and trade unions called it proof enforcement was theatre; supporters of the AI Act argued the law stood but had been ignored.

At the same time the graduate hiring freeze hardened into measurable loss. Law, accountancy, software, customer operations and administration stopped replacing entry-level posts. Protests in Paris, Madrid and Warsaw blocked siting hearings and turned against expansion permits for the backup compute site, which remained stuck in permitting.

The new wage-insurance, retraining and hiring-incentive guarantee was launched through employment ministers, with design work toward existing social funds and co-funding from automating employers, but no payments flowed this year. Mapping continued while anger grew. The attempt to prioritize the defensive scale-up changed little on the ground — teams were already stretched, and pushing the same programme harder bought no extra speed.

By December services worked better and were trusted less.
