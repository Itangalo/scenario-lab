## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a fortress with no people left inside. However, we now see that neither control nor resilience can be imposed from above: political fragmentation at the member-state level is the decisive vulnerability, and cohesion is not a background condition — it is the central battleground. This turn, we go further: we see that the Union’s survival depends not on enforcing compliance, but on restoring legitimacy — because when citizens strike and states defect, it is not just rules that fail, but belief in the Union itself.
  - Grounds: The *emergent_labour_unrest* across legal, accounting, and public administration sectors — not centrally organised, yet converging on shared demands — reveals that the crisis is not only political but existential. The *member_state_defection* is mirrored by *public_sentiment* collapsing to 22.0, where hostility is no longer abstract but operational: strikes disrupt governance, and trust in EU-level institutions evaporates. The identity must now reflect that the Union’s authority is not derived from control, but from consent — and that without it, no directive, no air gap, no dual key will hold.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience lags, even without a direct mandate, because harm finds the weakest joint. This turn, we extend that principle: we will enforce cohesion even at the cost of cohesion, because a Union that cannot bind its parts cannot protect any of them — but we now recognise that enforcement without legitimacy is self-defeating, and that political capital is not just spent, it is earned.
  - Grounds: The *capability_plateau_evidence* suggests the frontier may be stalling — but the *openweight_frontier_release* proves that whatever capability exists is now permanently distributed. The *supply_chain_coercion* by Washington shows that leverage flows to those who control chokepoints, not those who appeal to law. And yet, our own enforcement measure — meant to bind members — has fractured trust further. The commitment evolves because we see that acting under uncertainty now means acting in the dark *and* under fire: we must move, but we must also rebuild the ground beneath us.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse — but we now acknowledge that control without cohesion is an illusion, and that sovereignty cannot be built on a foundation of coercion. Therefore, we shift the focus of this commitment: from securing control through enforcement, to securing the Union’s ability to act as one by rebuilding political capital from the ground up — starting with the legitimacy of its rules, the fairness of their application, and the protection of those displaced by the very systems we govern.
  - Trigger: The *emergent_labour_unrest* and the failure of the cohesion enforcement measure to gain traction despite its legal basis.
  - Grounds: The *Enforce Cohesion Through Conditional Access* measure, while legally defensible, has failed to mobilise support and instead deepened resistance. Poland and Hungary have filed a preliminary reference to the CJEU; France and Germany remain silent. The strikes have made clear that the social contract is breaking faster than policy can respond. We cannot secure AI if we lose the Union. The reversal costs us credibility — Washington sees hesitation, Brussels sees division — but the cost of continuing is the Union’s dissolution.

## New measure

**Measure:** Establish the European Adjustment and Reintegration Facility  
We will create a pan-European fund to support workers displaced by AI, with binding requirements for transparency and limits on autonomous decision-making in public services.

`Category: 7 (Labour and social protection)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by reducing social disruption from AI-driven job losses; improve public_sentiment by demonstrating tangible support for affected workers; modestly increase eu_political_capital by rebuilding legitimacy through action that aligns with strike demands

## Priority

**Priority:** Establish the European Adjustment and Reintegration Facility  
We are pushing this hardest because the *emergent_labour_unrest* has made clear that the Union’s greatest immediate threat is not foreign coercion or technical failure, but the collapse of domestic legitimacy — and without a credible response to displacement, no amount of sovereign compute or containment protocol will matter.

## In practice

This turn, we are shifting from enforcement to reintegration. The *capability_plateau_evidence* gives us a narrow window: the frontier is not accelerating, but the open release at 57.5 means that even if it stalls, the tools for misuse are already in circulation. The *supply_chain_coercion* by Washington confirms that our industrial sovereignty remains under external pressure — but the *emergent_labour_unrest* reveals a deeper crisis: the Union is losing its people.

We are therefore launching the European Adjustment and Reintegration Facility under Article 122 TFEU, funded at €45bn over two years, with 70% co-financing for national retraining and wage insurance programmes that meet EU standards for speed, coverage, and dignity. The facility ties access to cohesion funds not to AI containment compliance, but to verifiable action on displacement — a pivot from punishing defection to rewarding alignment with shared fate. We are also mandating public-sector employers to disclose AI deployment in hiring, evaluation, and service delivery, and to retain human final decision authority in all high-stakes domains.

The Commission is drafting the regulation with DG REFORM and DG EMPL, to be adopted via the Social Policy Committee and fast-tracked through the EP’s Employment Committee. France and Germany are cautiously supportive, seeing it as a way to defuse strikes; Poland and Hungary welcome the funds but resist conditionality. We are offering a compromise: full access to the Facility for two years without performance audits, in exchange for a binding commitment to establish national AI transparency portals by turn 10. The goal is not control, but consent — and not sovereignty as power, but sovereignty as survival.
