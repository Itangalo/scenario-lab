### A Surge of Capability, a Crack in Unity

A sudden leap in AI capability reshapes the strategic landscape. The frontier surges to 69.0 as a leading laboratory demonstrates autonomous agent chains capable of sustained, cross-domain operations — from code generation to physical system manipulation — without human oversight. This discontinuous advance renders prior deployment timelines obsolete overnight.

Simultaneously, a major interpretability breakthrough allows developers to predict certain emergent behaviours before deployment, significantly improving control — but access is limited to those with sovereign infrastructure. The EU, still in the early stages of its own stack, cannot integrate the advance at scale.

Worse, a member state bypasses EU containment efforts, securing bilateral access to a 68.0 model from a US hyperscaler. This move, defended domestically as economic necessity, fractures the Union’s coordinated stance and exposes the fragility of its governance model.

In response, the EU elevates the AI Containment Protocol as its top priority, invoking emergency powers to enforce air-gapped operation and dual-key access for high-capability systems. Preparations begin for the first sovereign clusters in Saint-Malo and Kiruna, and the European AI Assurance Directorate initiates setup for operational audits. However, staffing shortages and legal challenges mean no audits occur this turn, and physical infrastructure remains in early construction.

The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative launches, targeting patching delays and bio-detection gaps. Yet with six major measures still active, political and fiscal strain deepens. Public sentiment remains stagnant at 25.0, as visible disruptions outweigh perceived benefits.

The US administration takes office, marking a shift toward strategic rationing of frontier access — a posture of consolidation now formally in place.
