## In practice
This turn, we are laying the foundation for the European Sovereign Compute Partnership under Article 122a TFEU, leveraging the European Stability Mechanism and EIB financing to co-invest with national champions in France, Germany, and the Netherlands. The plan calls for three exascale clusters — one focused on public research, one on industrial innovation, and one reserved for security-cleared applications — all operating under a new legal instrument that binds access to compliance with EU safety, transparency, and labour standards. We are negotiating with energy ministries to fast-track grid connections and with national parliaments to override local permitting delays, invoking the strategic infrastructure clause in the Lisbon Treaty.

Resistance is expected from member states reliant on foreign cloud providers and from private hyperscalers fearing exclusion. To secure buy-in, we are offering a phased access model: private firms that contribute compute or energy capacity gain priority access to non-sensitive workloads, while public institutions retain veto rights over safety-critical deployments. DG CONNECT is drafting the governance framework with input from ENISA and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, with a draft regulation to be presented to the Competitiveness Council in December. This is not just about chips and power — it is about proving that the Union can deliver capability *with* cohesion, not instead of it.
