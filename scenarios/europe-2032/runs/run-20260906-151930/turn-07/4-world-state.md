### A Fragile Foundation

AI capability inches upward to 70.0, driven by sustained investment and the integration of autonomous agent frameworks into military and industrial research. The frontier consolidates its gains, though no new discontinuity emerges. Open-weight models, benefiting from leaked agent architectures, rise to an estimated 57.5, widening the gap between closed and open but still trailing significantly.

The EU marks the completion of two major initiatives: the InvestAI Gigafactories and the AI Containment Protocol. The former delivers initial sovereign compute capacity in Kiruna and Saint-Malo, enabling the first EU-governed training run under the Sovereign AI Core Programme. The latter formally establishes air-gapped operation and dual-key access — but enforcement falters where political unity fails.

A bilateral agreement by a member state with a US hyperscaler to access a 68.0 model outside EU oversight triggers a crisis. In response, the Commission proposes conditioning access to the Sovereign AI Core and cohesion funds on compliance with containment rules. France and Germany back the move; Poland and Hungary denounce it as coercion, threatening legal action at the CJEU. The proposal passes in ECOFIN only after a last-minute compromise: audit fast-tracks for compliant states, but no immediate suspensions. Enforcement is delayed, and deterrence is weakened.

The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative begins deploying patching bots and bio-detection systems, marginally improving incident response. However, with six large measures still in flight, political capital erodes. The new cohesion enforcement measure, though large and urgent, fails to gain traction amid fragmentation.

Public sentiment dips to 22.0 as strikes in legal and administrative sectors spread, fueled by visible job losses and distrust in AI governance. No visible public benefit offsets the disruption.

### A Union Under Strain

The US posture of consolidation remains in force, restricting EU access to frontier models and interpretability tools. The EU’s reliance on emergency research access for critical safety tooling underscores its dependency.
