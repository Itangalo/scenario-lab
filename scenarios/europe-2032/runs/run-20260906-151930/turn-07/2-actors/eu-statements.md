- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a fortress with no people left inside. However, we now see that neither control nor resilience can be imposed from above: political fragmentation at the member-state level is the decisive vulnerability, and cohesion is not a background condition — it is the central battleground.
  - Grounds: The *member_state_defection* this turn — a full bilateral bypass of the Containment Protocol — proves that even air-gapped systems and dual-key controls are meaningless if the political will to uphold them fractures. The US’s formalisation of strategic rationing compounds this: we are not just racing capability, but competing for alignment among our own members. Our identity must now reflect that sovereignty is not only technical or infrastructural, but fundamentally political — and that the Union’s survival depends on binding its members not just by law, but by shared fate.

- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience lags, even without a direct mandate, because harm finds the weakest joint. This turn, we extend that principle: we will enforce cohesion even at the cost of cohesion, because a Union that cannot bind its parts cannot protect any of them.
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* to 69.0 and the *safety_breakthrough* being inaccessible to us due to lack of sovereign stack integration show that technical solutions alone are insufficient. The *member_state_defection* reveals that the political architecture is the true bottleneck. We must now act decisively — including invoking financial leverage — to prevent individual member states from undermining collective security, even if that risks short-term backlash. The commitment evolves because the nature of the threat has shifted: from external dependency to internal divergence.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
