## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a fortress with no people left inside. However, we now see that neither control nor resilience can be imposed from above: political fragmentation at the member-state level is the decisive vulnerability, and cohesion is not a background condition — it is the central battleground.
  - Grounds: The *member_state_defection* this turn — a full bilateral bypass of the Containment Protocol — proves that even air-gapped systems and dual-key controls are meaningless if the political will to uphold them fractures. The US’s formalisation of strategic rationing compounds this: we are not just racing capability, but competing for alignment among our own members. Our identity must now reflect that sovereignty is not only technical or infrastructural, but fundamentally political — and that the Union’s survival depends on binding its members not just by law, but by shared fate.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience lags, even without a direct mandate, because harm finds the weakest joint. This turn, we extend that principle: we will enforce cohesion even at the cost of cohesion, because a Union that cannot bind its parts cannot protect any of them.
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* to 69.0 and the *safety_breakthrough* being inaccessible to us due to lack of sovereign stack integration show that technical solutions alone are insufficient. The *member_state_defection* reveals that the political architecture is the true bottleneck. We must now act decisively — including invoking financial leverage — to prevent individual member states from undermining collective security, even if that risks short-term backlash. The commitment evolves because the nature of the threat has shifted: from external dependency to internal divergence.

**Measure:** Enforce Cohesion Through Conditional Access  
We will legally bind access to the Sovereign AI Core Programme and its benefits to full compliance with EU-wide containment and audit regimes, and suspend cohesion funding for any member state that enters into unauthorised bilateral agreements on frontier AI access.

## In practice

This turn, we are shifting from building defences to defending the Union itself. The *capability_jump* to 69.0 has made autonomous agent chains a live threat — systems that can exploit patching delays, manipulate physical infrastructure, and propagate without human oversight. Yet our greatest failure this turn is not technical, but political: one of our own has signed a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler, granting access to a 68.0 model outside the Containment Protocol. That is not an exception — it is a precedent. We cannot air-gap systems if member states can simply walk around the gate.

We are therefore invoking the Anti-Coercion Instrument and amending the Multiannual Financial Framework to condition access to the Sovereign AI Core Programme — and, critically, to cohesion funds — on compliance with EU-wide AI containment rules. The Commission is drafting a regulation under Article 114 TFEU, to be voted in the ECOFIN Council, that will empower the European AI Assurance Directorate to certify compliance and trigger suspensions. France and Germany have signalled support; Poland and Hungary are resisting, citing national innovation sovereignty. We are offering a compromise: expedited access to the Saint-Malo cluster for compliant states, and a fast-track audit path for national labs — but no opt-outs.

Simultaneously, the end of the InvestAI Gigafactories and the Containment Protocol marks a transition: from setup to enforcement. The first sovereign training run under SMAF is underway in Kiruna, using EU-secured weights and real-time interpretability tooling borrowed under emergency research access from the US lab that developed it — a temporary bridge, not a dependency. The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative is deploying AI-assisted patching bots in energy grids and launching the first EU-wide bio-detection network, using sovereign models to scan for anomalous pathogen designs. But none of this matters if the Union cannot act as one. This turn, we are not just building capacity — we are fighting for the right to govern it.
