### The Fracture Deepens

A wave of model-generated ransomware cripples public health systems in three member states, while coordinated AI-driven grid instability events force emergency manual overrides across Central Europe. The attacks exploit feedback loops in balancing algorithms, revealing that cognitive infrastructure—once assumed safe—is now a frontline of systemic risk. Defenders are visibly outpaced, and attribution remains unclear months later.

In response, the EU formally concludes the **Loss-of-Control Containment Framework**, having established detection baselines but failing to prevent cascading failures. Building on its momentum, the Union launches the **Critical Infrastructure Cognitive Hardening Initiative**, a large category 6 measure under ENISA and the European Defence Agency. It mandates red-teaming, fallback protocols, and algorithmic diversity in energy, finance, and emergency systems. Germany and France commit, Poland expands its pilot, but Hungary refuses, citing cost and sovereignty—echoing its earlier defection.

Legal authority is drawn from Article 17 of the AI Act and the 2028 Crisis Protocol, with funding from the Connecting Europe Facility. A European Cognitive Resilience Cell is established in Brussels to coordinate cross-sector red-teaming. However, implementation delays emerge: national regulators dispute audit scope, and cohesion funds fail to fully offset resistance. No operational improvements are observed yet; the measure remains in early rollout, with first assessments expected in the next turn.

Public sentiment rises slightly on visible action, but the dual shocks erode trust. Verification widens into structural engineering, accelerating capability gains while safety assurance collapses further. With no major safety breakthrough and rising capability, control gaps widen.

### The Weight of Response

The EU acts decisively, but fragmentation and pace undermine cohesion. The new initiative is in motion but not yet yielding measurable resilience gains. Political capital declines under the burden of a large measure and a named priority. Sovereignty erodes further as capability advances outstrip domestic capacity.

Open-weight model development sees modest progress, supported by continued diffusion of verification tools and growing interest in sovereign alternatives. The **emergent_sovereign_tech_alliance** contributes to incremental gains, but no sudden leap occurs—development remains within the envelope of general AI capability. `openweight_capability` rises moderately, reflecting steady uptake of existing open models and tooling, not a disruptive release.
