## New measure
**Sovereign Control Stack Initiative**  
Develop and deploy an EU-owned, open-audit software stack for training, monitoring, and securing frontier AI systems—including agent coordination, red-teaming, and safety interlocks—ensuring that sovereign compute is not just physically located in the Union but operationally controllable without foreign dependencies.

## Priority
**Sovereign Control Stack Initiative** — because the Emergency Compute Talent Corridor remains stalled and the physical compute under InvestAI will be useless without a secure, auditable control layer, we must now build the software foundation that makes sovereignty real: not just chips and power, but the stack that ensures no agent runs, no model trains, and no decision executes without EU visibility and authority.

## In practice
We are acting on the recognition that compute without control is not sovereignty—it is theatre. The Gigafactories are rising, and the Talent Initiative is beginning to return researchers, but we still depend on closed-source frameworks from US hyperscalers to manage training runs, monitor agent drift, and enforce safety constraints. This is not sustainable. With AI capability now at 58.0 and persistent agents running unattended in frontier labs, the absence of a sovereign control stack means we cannot verify what our systems are doing, even if they are on EU soil. The US election alliance offers access, but at the price of alignment—and that access could vanish with the next administration. We need our own stack: open for audit, secure against tampering, and integrated with EU safety standards.

This turn, we launch the Sovereign Control Stack Initiative under the Digital Europe Programme and co-funded by the InvestAI Gigafactories reserve. The European Commission, through a new mandate to the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking and in partnership with Fraunhofer, INRIA, and TNO, will coordinate the development of a full-stack, open-core framework for AI training and agent management, including built-in evaluation hooks, red-team orchestration, and real-time interpretability modules. Adoption will be mandatory for all EU-funded AI projects above 40.0 capability, and we are offering compliance bonuses through the Tech Sovereignty Package to private operators who adopt it early. Germany and France have signalled technical support, but resistance remains from member states reliant on US cloud providers, who fear lock-in and interoperability loss. We are addressing this by guaranteeing NATO-compatible interfaces and offering transition funding through the Cohesion Fund. The initiative closes the loop: we are no longer just importing talent or building data centres—we are ensuring that every cycle run on EU soil answers to EU rules. This is what irreversible sovereignty looks like.
