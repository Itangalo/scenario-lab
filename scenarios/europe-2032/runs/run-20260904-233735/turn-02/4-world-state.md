### Accelerating Capability, Weakening Defences
AI capability advances to 55.0 in the first half of 2027, driven by compressed release cycles and AI-aided development. The open release of offensive tools by Kimi K3 continues to diffuse high-end cyber capabilities, and `openweight_capability` climbs to 43.0. With no safety measures landing on shipped systems and rising opacity in agent behaviour, `ai_safety` drops to 28.0, reflecting growing unease among developers and regulators alike.

### A Cyber Shock and a Fragile Response
A coordinated ransomware attack cripples public services in three member states, exploiting AI-generated zero-day vulnerabilities. Detection systems failed to flag the intrusion until critical systems were encrypted. The incident, while contained through emergency protocols, confirms systemic fragility. In response, the EU activates a nascent joint cyber response framework with NATO, the UK, and key Indo-Pacific partners. Real-time telemetry sharing enables faster containment, and a Joint Cyber Operations Cell is established under ENISA and NATO CCDCOE—locking in a resilience gain despite the breach.

### Sovereignty Under Pressure
The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive advances through legal scrutiny but faces fierce resistance. Germany and Ireland warn of investment freeze risks, and a major US hyperscaler signals withdrawal from new EU data centre projects. The Commission offers fast-track Gigafactory access and liability shields to ease concerns, but the directive remains in limbo. Meanwhile, a member state quietly finalises a bilateral cloud deal with Washington that undermines the EU’s unified stance, exposing cracks in cohesion.

The Tech Sovereignty Package progresses slowly: MOUs on AI Acceleration Zones are near-signature in Spain and Poland, but legal challenges loom over grid access and environmental permits. No new compute capacity comes online.

### Political Capital Erodes
The EU launches the Joint Cyber Shield Initiative as a large category 6 measure, responding directly to the cyber incident. However, with three large measures already in flight and the priority declared, political capital continues to drain. Public sentiment dips to 39.0 as job losses in logistics and customer service fuel anxiety, amplified by media coverage of the cyberattacks.
