## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that compute is power, and that power cannot be outsourced if sovereignty is to mean anything. This turn, the Commission advances the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive through COREPER II, using Article 114 TFEU to frame the 30% reservation as a necessary internal market condition for high-end data centres. The text is now under legal scrub with DG Competition and the AI Office to ensure it withstands challenge at the CJEU, particularly from hyperscalers citing existing bilateral hosting agreements. To break the deadlock in the Competitiveness Council, we are offering a tripartite package: fast-track access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund for compliant operators, a liability shield for actions taken under EU scheduling, and an independent, algorithmic auction model managed by EDIC to allocate reserved capacity—removing political discretion. Germany and Ireland remain hesitant, but the recent cyber intrusion has shifted some voices in ECOFIN toward conditional support, provided the directive does not freeze new investment.

Simultaneously, we are operationalising the Tech Sovereignty Package by finalising MOUs with seven member states on AI Acceleration Zones. These will offer capped permitting (six months), guaranteed grid access (within 18 months), and mandatory community benefit agreements—modelled on the Green Deal’s energy islands. The first two sites, in Spain and Poland, are close to signing, with French and Italian delegations in advanced talks. These zones are not just about speed—they are the physical foundation of EU-controlled capacity, designed to attract private capital under binding sovereignty terms.

The new Joint Cyber Shield Initiative responds directly to the cyber_major_incident event: a ransomware sweep across public services in three member states, enabled by AI-generated zero-days and evading legacy detection. We are leveraging the joint_threat_response window to lock in real-time telemetry sharing with NATO’s Cyber Security Centre and the UK’s NCSC, establishing a Joint Cyber Operations Cell under ENISA with standing authority to coordinate cross-border mitigation. This is not a substitute for sovereignty—it is a stopgap while we build it. Without it, we are exposed; with it, we gain time. The initiative will cost political capital, but less than waiting for the next attack to fail.
