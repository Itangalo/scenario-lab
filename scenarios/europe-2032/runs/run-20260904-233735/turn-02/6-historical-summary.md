A major AI-powered ransomware attack disrupts public services across three EU member states, exposing systemic vulnerabilities. In response, the EU activates a joint cyber response framework with NATO, the UK, and Indo-Pacific partners, establishing a Joint Cyber Operations Cell under ENISA and NATO CCDCOE, which improves resilience despite the breach. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive advances but faces strong opposition from Germany, Ireland, and industry, with a major US hyperscaler halting EU data centre expansion and a member state striking a bilateral cloud deal with the US, undermining EU unity. The Commission offers incentives to secure support, but the directive remains stalled. Progress on the Tech Sovereignty Package slows, with AI Acceleration Zone MOUs pending in Spain and Poland amid legal and infrastructure hurdles. No new compute capacity is deployed. Political capital declines further due to implementation fatigue and public sentiment drops to 39.0 amid job displacement concerns and cyberattack fallout.
