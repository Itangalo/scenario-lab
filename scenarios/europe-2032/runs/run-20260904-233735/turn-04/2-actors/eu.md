## In practice  
We are acting on the recognition that sovereignty requires not only ownership of compute and the return of intellectual leadership, but also the practical mastery of running it. The Joint Cyber Shield Initiative has now **finished**, validating our preparedness architecture and delivering a modest but real uplift in resilience. Its success proves that binding operational agreements across allies can work—but it also underscores that such defences are only as strong as the systems behind them, and ours remain undermanned. With the Sovereign Talent & Knowledge Repatriation Initiative underway, we are beginning to attract high-profile researchers, but we are not hiring fast enough the engineers who configure clusters, secure training runs, and integrate safety monitors into live systems. This is not a research gap—it is an operations deficit, and it is holding back the entire sovereignty agenda.

This turn, we launch the Emergency Compute Talent Corridor under Article 80 TFEU (internal market derogation for public interest) and co-funded by the InvestAI Gigafactories fund. The European Commission, in coordination with ENISA and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, will issue 100 binding talent permits with guaranteed housing near designated AI Acceleration Zones, fast-tracked recognition of credentials, and direct placement into sovereign compute enclaves. Member states must accept these placements or face infringement proceedings; we are offering compensation through the Cohesion Fund for local infrastructure strain. The programme targets engineers with proven experience in large-scale training runs, agent monitoring, and red-team integration—profiles currently concentrated in US hyperscalers and absent in EU public capacity. We are also renegotiating the Tech Sovereignty Package with Spain and Poland to tie grid prioritisation to talent corridor uptake, ensuring that compute does not come online without the people to run it.

Public sentiment remains low at 35.0, and the supply_chain_coercion event has exposed our strategic vulnerability: the Union’s one chokepoint in the global tech stack is being weaponised by Washington, not by us. This reinforces the urgency of our two-year commitment—sovereignty is not about matching scale, but about securing irreversibility. We are not building faster than the frontier; we are building something that cannot be switched off. That requires people, not just policy. This is why we act now, at cost, to import the missing layer of operational mastery. Without it, our compute is inert, our defences hollow, and our independence performative.
