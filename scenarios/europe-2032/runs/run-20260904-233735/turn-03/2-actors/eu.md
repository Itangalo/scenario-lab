## In practice  
We are acting on the recognition that compute without competence is not sovereignty. With the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive now **finished**, we have secured the legal right to 30% of frontier-class capacity on EU soil—but we lack the talent to use it for high-stakes public missions. The directive was a victory of legal architecture, but the real bottleneck has shifted: it is no longer permission, but personnel. This turn, we launch the Sovereign Talent & Knowledge Repatriation Initiative under the Horizon Europe framework, co-financed by the InvestAI Gigafactories fund, to offer binding five-year contracts, housing and lab start-up packages, and research independence to EU-origin AI scientists in US and Chinese labs. The programme targets 200 key individuals over three years, with an initial cohort of 50 to be secured by turn 5. We are working through national research councils and the European Research Council to identify candidates, and offering access to sovereign compute enclaves—firewalled, audited environments where they can work on public-interest problems without fear of IP leakage or surveillance.

Simultaneously, we are pressing forward on the Tech Sovereignty Package, finalising MOUs with Spain and Poland despite legal challenges over grid access. The Commission has brokered a deal with ENTSO-E to prioritise AI Acceleration Zones in the 2028 grid expansion plan, and we are offering environmental permitting fast-tracks under the revised Industrial Emissions Directive. In parallel, the Joint Cyber Shield Initiative is now operational: the Joint Cyber Operations Cell under ENISA has conducted its first live-fire exercise with NCSC and NATO CCDCOE, validating real-time telemetry sharing and pre-authorised response protocols. This is not a substitute for sovereign capability, but it is buying time—and credibility—while we build it.

Public sentiment remains fragile at 39.0, and the emergent labour displacement wave is being exploited by anti-AI movements. We are resisting calls for a moratorium, but we are preparing a targeted response under category 7 (Labour and social protection) for next turn, should displacement accelerate. For now, we are holding the line: sovereignty is not built by retreat, but by decisive investment in the foundations we lack.
