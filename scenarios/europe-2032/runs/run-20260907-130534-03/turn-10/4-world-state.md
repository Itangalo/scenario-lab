### A Turning Point, Not a Breakthrough

The European Union declares the launch of the EU AI Safety Assurance Infrastructure, a bold step to transform its newly operational Sovereign AI Core into an enforcement platform. Empowered by Article 114 and emergency overrides, the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) begins deploying public evaluators with real-time access to high-risk model internals. The first audits target foreign systems using EU-facing APIs, particularly those derived from open-weight models recently weaponised in cyberattacks.

### Global Shifts, Local Limits

The emergent supply chain pact among South Korea, the Netherlands, Finland, and Taiwan formalises into a functional bloc, aligning export controls and red-teaming protocols. This quiet consolidation of mid-tier powers shifts leverage away from unilateral dominance, offering the EU new alignment opportunities—but no immediate agreements materialise. Japan and South Korea express conditional interest in mirroring EU safety enforcement, pending transparent standards, but stop short of commitment.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Stalls

AI capability advances to 68.0, driven by closed-lab progress in recursive optimisation and planning depth. Yet no corresponding safety gains land on deployed systems. The EU’s new infrastructure remains in early rollout, yielding no measurable assurance improvement this turn. Interpretability gaps persist globally, and no lab reports meaningful breakthroughs. The resilience buffer holds—logging systems again prove vital—but no category 6 measure finishes to strengthen it.

### Political Capital Frozen

The Middle Power AI Coalition measure concludes, removing its cost. However, the new large safety initiative imposes a −3 political capital charge. With public sentiment stagnant at 14.0 and capital already strained, the EU limps forward without momentum. Germany and Austria continue to resist centralised intervention, accepting only narrowly scoped co-governance. The promised sovereignty dividend from the Core remains unrealised—access is secured, but control is contested.
