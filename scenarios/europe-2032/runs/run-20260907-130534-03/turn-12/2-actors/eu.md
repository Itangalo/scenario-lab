## Statement changes

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Maintain independent access to AI capabilities sufficient for strategic autonomy and crisis response  
  - Grounds: The openweight frontier release has collapsed the lead time between closed and open capabilities, making any reliance on foreign models or delayed domestic build untenable. The cyber_major_incident and the rise of regional civil defense pacts prove that access without operational control is not autonomy—it is exposure. Our commitment must now specify not just access, but sovereign enforcement capacity: the ability to act within our jurisdiction against threats enabled by that access.

## New measure
**Establish Sovereign Enforcement Nodes in Critical Infrastructure (category 6, costs 3 per turn, started turn 12, finishes on turn 15):** Deploy and operationalise autonomous defensive AI agents under EU control within healthcare, energy, and transport networks to detect, isolate, and counter adversarial AI activity in real time.  
`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `resilience` by 15–20 points; reduce time-to-intervention in AI-enabled cyber incidents from hours to seconds; demonstrate EU-level capacity where member states have begun to act alone.  
`Applies to:` EU critical infrastructure, starting with healthcare systems in member states affected by the recent cyber incident.

## Priority
**Establish Sovereign Enforcement Nodes in Critical Infrastructure**  
This is the first measure that turns detection into action at the level of infrastructure under attack—not because it replaces the Safety Assurance Infrastructure, but because monitoring without counteraction is now indefensible in the face of distributed, adaptive threats and collapsing public trust.

## In practice
We are no longer waiting for consensus to protect our citizens. The cyber_major_incident was not a failure of awareness—it was a failure of authority. The Sovereign AI Core detected the attack, but had no mandate to stop it. That ends now. Under Article 122 TFEU and the emergency override in the EAAD, we are deploying the first wave of enforcement nodes into the healthcare networks of Belgium, Italy, and Austria—states hardest hit by the ransomware sweep. These nodes run on EU-sovereign clusters in Brittany and northern Sweden, using hardened models developed by public research teams under contract to ENISA and the newly operational Public AI Lab in Helsinki.

Germany remains resistant to centralised intervention, but we have offered co-development rights and real-time audit access to its Federal Office for Information Security. Finland and Estonia are contributing offensive red-teaming capacity to stress-test the nodes before rollout to energy and transport grids. The nodes themselves are not autonomous in goal-setting—they are sovereign in deployment and response speed. They do not plan; they act. Their logic is transparent, their triggers published, and their first rule is: if you touch a hospital, we touch back. This is not escalation—it is deterrence by presence. The human futures movement demands global treaties, but we have citizens to protect today. We act.
