## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_test_shot proves that detection has failed, that intrusions can persist undetected across jurisdictions, and that resilience is now the only active defence we have.

## In practice  
We are acting on the intrusion findings by invoking Article 12 of the NIS2 Directive to mandate immediate, audited cyber hardening across electricity transmission, water, and port operators in all member states. The ENISA Rapid Response Mechanism will be activated to coordinate cross-border red-teaming and patch deployment by turn 2, with funding drawn from the Digital Europe Programme’s emergency reserve. Member states will be required to report compliance within six months, and non-compliant operators will face temporary disconnection from cross-border critical networks.  

At the same time, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories by negotiating site allocations in Finland, Spain, and Poland under the new Strategic Infrastructure Fast-Track regime. These will be legally anchored under EU ownership with veto-proof governance, and we are offering tax incentives to attract private partners — but only if compute access is guaranteed to public agencies during emergencies. Resistance from national governments over energy allocation is being met with binding grid-sharing commitments under the revised TEN-E regulation.  

The Tech sovereignty package is being reoriented to prioritise not just scale but control: we are drafting amendments to the AI Act’s general-purpose provisions to require that any foreign hyperscaler receiving EU investment must submit to EU jurisdiction over model deployment and incident response. This will be tied to the upcoming review of the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper obligations, leveraging our position in the semiconductor supply chain to strengthen enforcement. ASML’s export terms will be renegotiated to include reciprocal access to advanced packaging facilities, turning supply leverage into sovereign capacity.
