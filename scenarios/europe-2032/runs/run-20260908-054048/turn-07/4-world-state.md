### A Pivot in the Shadow of Exclusion  
With US frontier access now formally rationed under a **CONSOLIDATION** posture, the EU abandons attempts to mandate foreign mirrors and pivots decisively toward indigenous capability. The completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories delivers five exascale-class compute sites—idle capacity repurposed for the nascent **EU Frontier AI Development Initiative**, a proposed flagship effort to train a sovereign frontier model by 2031. The project is announced and enters early planning, with a pan-European consortium led by INRIA and Fraunhofer forming to draft technical and legal frameworks. However, legal disputes over data pooling under Article 116 TFEU delay foundational decisions, and no training run commences. Full operationalization is deferred pending governance alignment and funding negotiations, consistent with the two-turn minimum for high-cost measures. Due to partial integration delays and underutilization of newly available compute, the sovereignty benefit is tempered but still material: the +5 boost from InvestAI Gigafactories is partially offset by implementation friction, yielding a net +3 increase.

### Evaluation at Last, But Under Duress  
The **Independent EU Front-Run Evaluation Capability** becomes operational this turn, funded through a reallocated €1.8 billion from the European Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused digital transition envelope—officially approved by the Council after tense negotiations with southern member states concerned about growth impacts. Binding authority is granted via an AI Act implementing act, and the body announces its first target: the upcoming GPT-7 update. This marks the *only* new measure launched in the turn, satisfying the constraint of at most one new measure. Member-state participation remains patchy, and the absence of real-time access to model weights from US developers limits its reach. Parallel efforts by Germany, the Netherlands, and Finland to build national evaluation capacity grow more visible, signaling quiet distrust in central execution.

### Capability Creeps, Safety Slips  
Global AI capability advances to 65.0, driven by closed-loop improvements in cyber and mathematical reasoning. Open-weight models show no new releases or training runs this turn; while last cycle’s architectures continue to diffuse, tooling improvements are incremental and not yet reflected in systemic capability gains. As a result, **openweight_capability** remains unchanged at 47.0. No corresponding safety leap occurs; verification remains narrow, and interpretability tools lag. As a result, **ai_safety** drops to 28.0. Resilience declines slightly as no new protective measures land, and the offensive cyber balance shifts—though defensive automation improves, the edge remains with attackers.

### Political Capital Adjusts  
Political capital rises modestly to 28.0. The completion of the InvestAI Gigafactories yields legitimacy and releases administrative burden, contributing +1. The other previously listed initiatives—Tech Sovereignty Package and Sovereign AI Access Framework—were already accounted for in prior turns and do not generate fresh gains. The launch of the evaluation capability provides a partial boost, recognized as a necessary step toward oversight, but its limited initial scope and contested funding constrain its political return. The *announcement only* of the Frontier AI Development Initiative consumes political capital rather than generating it; as a high-cost measure not yet in implementation, it does not deliver visible progress. The net gain is thus reduced, reflecting delayed rollout and ongoing coordination costs. The **EU Frontier AI Development Initiative** remains in pre-implementation and does not count as a launched measure this turn; its organisational activity is preparatory and falls short of crossing the threshold for formal measure activation, preserving compliance with the one-measure limit.
