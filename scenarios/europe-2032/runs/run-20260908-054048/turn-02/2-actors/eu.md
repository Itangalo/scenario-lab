## In practice  
We are acting on the bio_uplift_findings and the emergent_pathogen_design_breakthrough by launching the Bio-Secure Compute Initiative under Article 16 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU, invoking public health and security as grounds for a new class of sovereign infrastructure. This will establish a mandatory, audited computing environment for any EU-funded or commercially operating synthetic biology research involving AI-assisted design. The system will be built on EU-anchored compute — drawing from the InvestAI Gigafactories pipeline — with hardware-rooted attestation, real-time model output logging, and integration with the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control’s bio-surveillance network. All DNA synthesis orders will be required to pass through a centralised screening gateway that cross-references AI-generated sequences against pathogen databases, with automated holds for human review.

At the same time, we are accelerating the Emergency Resilience Surge by finalising ENISA’s first cross-border red-teaming cycle, now covering 87% of designated critical operators in energy and transport. Non-compliant member states are being offered technical assistance from the EU Cyber Rapid Response Teams, but also warned that continued delays will trigger sanctions under the NIS2 compliance mechanism by turn 3. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being restructured to prioritise not just scale but jurisdictional control: we are drafting a new condition for access to accelerated permitting — foreign hyperscalers must now accept binding incident reporting and model access for EU evaluators as a condition of operating in the EU. This will be tabled in the Council’s Digital Agenda meeting next month, with leverage from ASML’s export negotiations held in reserve. The cyber_defence_breakthrough is being incorporated into the resilience architecture: automated patching systems will be mandated for all NIS2-covered entities by turn 4, and we are funding a pilot with ETSI to standardise swarm-detection protocols across the Single Market.
