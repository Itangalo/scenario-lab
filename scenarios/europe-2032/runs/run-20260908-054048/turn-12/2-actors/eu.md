## In practice
We are acting on the lesson of the last six turns: compute without people is inert, and data without expertise is noise. The Gigafactory in Marseille is operational, but it runs below capacity because we cannot staff the teams to use it. The Article 116 data framework passed, but no consortium has formed to exploit it. The bottleneck is not money, not hardware, not even politics — it is people. This turn, we launch the European Sovereign AI Corps under Article 172 TFEU, mobilising €1.2 billion from the Digital Europe Programme, Horizon Europe, and the European Defence Fund to recruit 500 specialists over three years — 200 in the first tranche by turn 14, prioritising those with experience in agent safety, weight security, and red-teaming.

The package includes fast-track Schengen visas, housing allowances, and long-term contracts with the European Commission and participating member states, offering access to sovereign models and secure compute as a competitive advantage over private-sector labs. DG HR, DG CONNECT, and the European Defence Agency are coordinating with universities in France, Germany, Finland, and the Netherlands to identify candidates, with a focus on diaspora researchers and those disillusioned with closed-lab opacity. The Corps will be embedded in ENISA, the EU evaluation body, national cyber agencies, and the Gigafactory itself — not as contractors, but as permanent technical civil servants.

We are also revising the **Accelerate Open-Weight Absorption** initiative to feed directly into this cadre: the red-teaming work in Saclay, Garching, and Helsinki will now serve as a pipeline for Corps recruitment, with successful participants offered permanent positions. This turns defensive adaptation into a talent forge. At the same time, we are negotiating with the Energy Council and Health Security Committee to accept modular red-teaming as a compliance pathway, offering cohesion funds to smaller member states to offset costs — not to force unity, but to prove that EU-level coordination can deliver tangible benefits. The fractures are real, but we are building something that outlasts them.
