## In practice
We are treating the open-weight release not as a setback but as a strategic opportunity: the same architecture that powered the ransomware attack is now in the wild, and we will not leave it solely in adversarial hands. This turn, we launch the **Accelerate Open-Weight Absorption** initiative under the NIS3 preparedness framework, directing DG CONNECT and the European Defence Agency to coordinate with certified AI labs in France, Germany, and Finland to validate and repurpose the model for defensive use — red-teaming critical systems, simulating cascading failures, and training human responders. We allocate €300 million from the Digital Europe Programme and the European Defence Fund, fast-tracked through the Council’s emergency clause, to cover compute, clearance, and coordination. The model will be run only on air-gapped, EU-sovereign hardware, with weight integrity verified by the newly operational evaluation body.

Meanwhile, the **Secure the Core** directive moves from planning to enforcement: ENISA issues binding technical standards this quarter requiring AI-driven anomaly detection and automated containment in all NIS2-designated operators by turn 10. Member states must report compliance starting turn 11, with peer review mechanisms activated through the Cooperation Group. Resistance remains in smaller member states, where SMEs fear cost and complexity — we are countering with expanded subsidies from cohesion funds and a simplified compliance toolkit co-developed with Estonia and the Netherlands. The **EU Frontier AI Development Initiative** continues on track: the Article 116 data pooling regulation clears the Council’s legal committee this month, and the first Horizon call for consortia launches in September. Talent remains a bottleneck — we are finalising a fast-track visa and housing package with seven member states to attract 200 core researchers by turn 10, leveraging our strengthened security clearance framework to offer access to sovereign models as a competitive advantage. We are still building in the dark — but we are no longer blind.
