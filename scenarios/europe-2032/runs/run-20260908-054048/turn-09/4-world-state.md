### The Aftermath of Fire and the Push for Readiness

The EU’s response to the recent AI-driven ransomware attacks crystallises into action, but not without friction. The **Accelerate Open-Weight Absorption** initiative launches amid urgency, aiming to repurpose the newly released offensive-capable model for defensive use. Fast-tracked funding flows to certified labs in France, Germany, and Finland, where red-teaming of critical infrastructure begins on air-gapped systems. Early validation suggests the model can simulate cascading failures with high fidelity, offering a new tool for resilience planning. However, integration bottlenecks emerge: secure compute remains underprovisioned, and clearance delays slow researcher onboarding.

Meanwhile, the **Secure the Core** directive gains momentum. ENISA issues binding standards requiring AI-driven anomaly detection in all NIS2 operators, marking a shift from voluntary to enforced resilience. Smaller member states resist, citing SME burden, but compliance subsidies and a streamlined toolkit from Estonia and the Netherlands ease adoption. Cross-border coordination improves, though full implementation remains two turns away.

The **Independent EU Front-Run Evaluation Capability** begins operations this turn, but is not yet fully functional. Initial staffing is confirmed and baseline protocols drafted, but technical validation and weight verification remain pending. Its first assessment is underway but incomplete; no final results are issued this turn. Parallel evaluations by Germany and Finland continue, reflecting ongoing fragmentation in evaluation authority.

The **EU Frontier AI Development Initiative** progresses slowly—data pooling clears legal hurdles, and Horizon funding launches—but talent shortages persist, and Gigafactory utilisation remains low.

The open-weight release contributes to a measured rise in global open capability, reaching 51.0 as adoption spreads through research networks. While diffusion is rapid, full adaptation requires tuning and infrastructure, limiting immediate gains. Offensive use increases, but structured safety insights begin to form from early defensive testing, marginally improving **ai_safety**. **Resilience** rises due to partial activation of new protocols and red-teaming progress. However, **eu_ai_sovereignty** dips slightly: despite ongoing efforts, the gap between rising **ai_capability** (+1.5) and domestic capacity persists, and no sovereignty-boosting measure concluded this turn.

Political capital declines by one point to 29.0, reflecting the real costs of fast-tracked programs and cross-border coordination without offsetting gains or external funding streams. Public sentiment remains stagnant, caught between alarm over the attacks and cautious hope in the response.
