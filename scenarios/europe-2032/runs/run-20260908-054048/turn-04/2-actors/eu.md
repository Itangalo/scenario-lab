## New measure  
**Sovereign AI Access Framework**  
Establish a legal and technical framework to ensure EU institutions and essential public services retain access to capable AI systems even under foreign supply disruption or political coercion.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +15 (by securing sovereign access to frontier-adjacent capability through binding redundancy and local control), ai_safety +3 (via mandatory evaluation access and logging), eu_political_capital -3 (due to friction with foreign providers and internal coordination burden)  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national governments, and designated critical public services (health, energy, civil protection) across all member states

## Priority  
**Sovereign AI Access Framework** — because the widening gap between closed and open capabilities, now at 16 points, means that dependence on foreign AI providers is no longer a competitiveness issue but a sovereignty emergency, and the cyber shock has shown that access can fail when it is most needed.

## In practice  
We are advancing the Sovereign AI Access Framework by initiating emergency negotiations under Article 114 TFEU to establish binding redundancy obligations on foreign hyperscalers serving EU essential functions. Any provider whose systems are used in core public services must now either (a) deploy a sovereign mirror — a physically and legally ring-fenced instance of their model stack on EU-controlled infrastructure with real-time evaluation access — or (b) face exclusion from public procurement. This will be enforced through a new designation: “AI-Critical Supplier,” overseen by the European Commission with technical validation from ENISA and the newly expanded AI Office. We are fast-tracking the deployment of this mirror infrastructure using capacity from the InvestAI Gigafactories and Bio-Secure Compute nodes in Heidelberg and Paris, repurposing 15% of their compute for sovereign public redundancy by turn 5.

At the same time, we are pressing forward on all existing measures: the Tech Sovereignty Package is now tied directly to this framework — accelerated permitting zones will only be granted if the applicant commits to sovereign mirror deployment — and we are leveraging ASML’s export leverage to extract model access guarantees from U.S. and Asian providers. The Bio-Secure Compute Initiative is entering its integration phase, with the EBI and ECDC finalising joint protocols for DNA synthesis screening and model output logging; pilot runs will begin in Uppsala by the end of this turn. Secure the Core continues implementation, with ENISA conducting its first AI-driven red-teaming exercises against municipal transport operators in Belgium and Italy, revealing critical gaps in supply-chain trust models that are now being patched through automated dependency verification. Despite strained political capital, we are not pausing or canceling any in-flight measure — the moment demands persistence, not retreat.
