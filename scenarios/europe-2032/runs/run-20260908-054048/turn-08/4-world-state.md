### The Wake-Up Call That Wasn’t Heard Loud Enough  
A coordinated AI-optimized ransomware attack cripples regional health systems in Belgium, Hungary, and Portugal, disrupting emergency logistics and exposing the fragility of critical infrastructure. The malware exploited unpatched dependencies at speed, evaded legacy detection systems, and overwhelmed human response teams. The incident is widely reported as the first AI-native crisis to cross into public harm, yet official EU response is delayed by jurisdictional disputes over enforcement. Only after 72 hours do cross-border protocols activate—spurred by the new **Secure the Core** initiative, which had just been named priority.

### Sovereign Evaluation, Shadowed by Access  
The **Independent EU Front-Run Evaluation Capability** completes its first mandate, delivering a classified risk assessment of GPT-7 to the European Parliament. It identifies concerning self-improvement pathways and weak weight integrity controls. But without direct access to model weights, its conclusions rely on Canadian-shared documentation and inference—a limitation openly criticized by national labs. Germany and Finland announce expanded bilateral evaluation efforts, deepening the quiet fragmentation of EU oversight.

### Open Release Shocks the System  
Days after the cyberattack, a frontier open-weight model is released—matching GPT-7’s reasoning and tool-use in key domains. Downloaded over 300,000 times in a week, it includes training logs and fine-tuning scripts. Overnight, offensive cyber capability becomes widely accessible. Security researchers confirm the ransomware used similar architectures. The EU scrambles to assess the implications, but integration into sovereign development pipelines remains in early stages. While the release energizes open-source communities and informs ongoing work, no immediate leap in *internal* open-weight capability occurs—absorption requires validation, adaptation, and infrastructure not yet in place.

### Building in the Dark  
The **Secure the Core** initiative is formally prioritized, triggering political capital costs (−1) and enabling partial activation of cross-border resilience protocols. A proposed expansion of the **EU Frontier AI Development Initiative**—including public-sector data pooling—faces internal debate and is deferred to the next legislative cycle due to governance bottlenecks and lack of consensus on data sovereignty. Talent recruitment continues to lag, and Gigafactory compute remains underutilized. Political capital gains from completing the evaluation body (+4) are offset by launching the resilience directive (−3) and naming it priority (−1). Public sentiment drops as media frames the attack as a failure of preparedness, not just foreign exclusion.
