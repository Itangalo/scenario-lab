## New measure  
**Secure the Core: AI-Resilient Critical Infrastructure Expansion**  
Expand and automate cyber defences across healthcare, energy, and transport sectors in response to the major cyber incident, mandating AI-driven anomaly detection and cross-border incident response protocols.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`

## Priority  
**Secure the Core: AI-Resilient Critical Infrastructure Expansion** — because the cyber_major_incident has exposed a lethal gap between our evaluation ambitions and our ability to withstand real-world harm, and because without resilience, even perfect foresight offers no protection.

## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that disabled regional health records in three member states and disrupted emergency logistics for 72 hours — as a wake-up call. The attack exploited known but unpatched dependencies, propagated with AI-optimized evasion, and overwhelmed human-led response teams. It was not the worst that could happen, but it was bad enough. The incident confirms that our defences are static while the threat evolves, and that resilience has not kept pace with either capability or dependency. We are therefore launching the **Secure the Core: AI-Resilient Critical Infrastructure Expansion**, a legally binding directive under the NIS3 framework, to mandate AI-powered intrusion detection, automated containment, and real-time cross-border data sharing for all critical operators. ENISA will issue technical standards by turn 9, and member states must report compliance quarterly from turn 10. We are fast-tracking €900 million from the Digital Europe Programme and unspent cohesion funds to subsidize SME upgrades, knowing that small providers are both vulnerable and resistant to new mandates.

At the same time, we are finalising the handover of the **Independent EU Front-Run Evaluation Capability**, now legally operational but still undermanned. The Paris-Berlin audit teams are fully staffed with cleared researchers, and we have secured provisional access to GPT-7’s safety documentation via a mutual recognition deal with Canada — not ideal, but better than nothing. The first report will be delivered to the European Parliament in June, assessing self-improvement risk and weight leakage potential. Meanwhile, the **EU Frontier AI Development Initiative** enters its second phase: the legal task force has drafted a compromise on data pooling under Article 116 TFEU, treating public sector data as strategic infrastructure, and we are launching the Horizon-style call for consortia in July. The first training run is scheduled for turn 9, contingent on compute allocation and talent recruitment. We are not waiting for perfect alignment — we are building sovereign capacity in the dark, but we are building.
