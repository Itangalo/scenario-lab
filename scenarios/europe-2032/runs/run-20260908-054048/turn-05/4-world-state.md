### US Election Signals a Harder Stance  
The United States elects a president who treats advanced AI as a strategic national asset, tightening export controls and conditioning foreign access on alignment with American security interests. While the administration takes office next turn, market sentiment shifts in anticipation: frontier AI is no longer seen as a commodity but as a lever of power, and the EU’s dependence becomes a growing concern. However, the formal US posture remains undefined until the new administration assumes office; no policy effects are yet active.

### Sovereign Access Push Begins Under Legal Uncertainty  
The EU proposes activating emergency powers under Article 114 TFEU to advance the **Sovereign AI Access Framework**, designating major US hyperscalers as AI-Critical Suppliers and threatening loss of public procurement eligibility unless they deploy sovereign mirrors on EU infrastructure within six months. ENISA and the AI Office begin preparations using decommissioned Bio-Secure nodes in Heidelberg and Paris and earmark 15% of InvestAI Gigafactory capacity. However, legal challenges are expected, and US providers have not engaged, calling the proposal coercive and extraterritorial. No compliance has occurred; the measure remains in early proposal, with full implementation delayed pending legal and diplomatic clarity.

### Early Discussions on Evaluation Capacity Begin  
As part of broader planning, informal technical discussions start among member states on the feasibility of an **Independent EU Front-Run Evaluation Capability**, aiming to build sovereign technical judgment over frontier models. The concept includes access to weights, training data summaries, and agent logs, with potential authority to delay deployments. Germany and France are approached for input, but no decision has been made, no resources allocated, and no formal proposal drafted. This does not constitute a new measure and is not added to the portfolio.

### Resilience Gains from Completed Measures  
With the **Secure the Core** directive now fully implemented, critical infrastructure operators report improved AI-specific cyber defences and faster incident containment. The **Bio-Secure Compute Initiative** concludes, leaving behind a high-assurance computing tier for bio-risk research. These completions lift resilience to 53.0, reflecting hardening in both cyber and bio domains.

### Political Capital Unchanged Amid High Load  
Five large measures remain in flight, and the proposed **Sovereign AI Access Framework**—a category 4, high-cost initiative—adds to the burden. A proposal bonus of +4 was considered for addressing the acute capability-safety gap and recent cyber_major_incident, but since no formal adoption or resource commitment has occurred this turn beyond announcement, the portfolio charge is deferred and political capital remains unchanged at 28.0. Public sentiment holds at 37.0—aware of the threats, but skeptical of top-down solutions.
