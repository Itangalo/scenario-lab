## New measure  
**Independent EU Front-Run Evaluation Capability**  
Establish a permanent, technically credible, and legally empowered EU body able to conduct pre-deployment evaluations of frontier AI systems, including agent behaviour, self-improvement potential, and weight security, with binding authority to delay deployment.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +10 (by institutionalising independent, pre-release evaluation with teeth), eu_ai_sovereignty +5 (by building sovereign technical judgment), eu_political_capital -3 (due to friction with developers and member-state resistance to centralised authority)  
`Applies to:` All frontier AI developers seeking access to the EU market or public procurement, regardless of origin

## Priority  
**Sovereign AI Access Framework** — because the U.S. election outcome confirms that access to frontier AI is now a function of strategic alignment, not commercial terms, and we cannot afford to let our public services depend on systems that can be withdrawn at will.

## In practice  
We are executing the final phase of the Sovereign AI Access Framework by activating emergency powers under Article 114 TFEU to designate the first wave of "AI-Critical Suppliers" — hyperscalers whose models are embedded in EU health, energy, and civil protection systems. ENISA, supported by the AI Office, is issuing compliance timelines: six months to deploy a sovereign mirror on EU-controlled infrastructure or face exclusion from public contracts. We are fast-tracking deployment using 15% of InvestAI Gigafactory capacity and repurposing Bio-Secure nodes in Heidelberg and Paris, now that the Bio-Secure Compute Initiative has concluded. Legal teams are preparing for inevitable challenges, but the election_consolidation event strengthens our case: if the U.S. treats AI as a strategic asset, so must we.

At the same time, we are launching the Independent EU Front-Run Evaluation Capability, recognising that sovereignty without understanding is hollow. This new body — to be hosted jointly by the AI Office and a newly empowered European AI Safety Institute — will have mandatory access to model weights, training data summaries, and agent logs during pre-deployment testing. It will report directly to the Commission and the European Parliament, with authority to halt releases that breach defined thresholds. We are negotiating with member states in the Competitiveness Council to secure staffing and budget, offering Germany and France co-leadership to ensure buy-in. The first test case will be the next major model update from a U.S. provider subject to the Sovereign AI Access Framework.

The conclusion of both Secure the Core and Bio-Secure Compute marks a transition: we are shifting from emergency resilience to enduring sovereignty in both access and judgment. The political cost is high — capital remains at 28.0, and public sentiment is stagnant — but the alternative is to outsource our cognitive sovereignty to powers that no longer see us as equals. We are not retreating from our existing measures, but we are no longer expanding them. The portfolio is full, and the moment demands focus.
