## In practice
We are acting on the breaking point of credibility. The Mandatory Pre-Deployment Biosecurity Audit Regime was designed as a standards-based lever of sovereignty, but without teeth, it is a signal with no force. Meta’s refusal, echoed in quiet by others, has exposed that the frontier does not fear exclusion from our market — yet. This turn, we change that. Under Article 45 of the Digital Markets Act and the emergency powers of the Cyber Resilience Act, we are instructing BEREC, ENISA, and the national digital regulators to begin technical implementation of access suspension for developers who do not submit to audit. This means requiring EU-based cloud providers to block training and inference API calls to non-compliant models, instructing national CERTs to coordinate DNS sinkholing during declared risk periods, and mandating that payment processors refuse transactions routed to un-audited AI services. The legal basis is contested — Germany and the Netherlands have raised proportionality concerns — but we are moving under the doctrine of anticipatory self-defence in high-consequence domains. We are offering a six-week grace period for compliance, with a fast-track audit lane for those who enter before turn’s end.

At the same time, we are accelerating the European AI Emergency Response Corps’ operational mandate. With the Resilience Surge Protocol now complete, we are transferring its cyber and bioresponse teams into the Corps’ standing structure, embedding them in a new Joint AI Crisis Cell co-located between ENISA and ECDC. Recruitment has reached 320 of 500 roles, with France, Poland, and Finland fast-tracking security clearances. The Council’s legal working group has approved intervention protocols under Article 122a TFEU for bio-incidents, and we are now negotiating with Sweden and Germany to extend this to cascading cyber failures in energy and transport. The Corps will not wait for unanimity: if a member state blocks activation, we will activate bilateral mutual aid channels in parallel, using the Nordic-Baltic AI Security Pact as a template. This is no longer about preparedness. It is about creating a credible, armed hand.
