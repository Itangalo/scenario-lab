## New measure  
**Sovereign Compute Corridor**  
Establish a legally and physically secured corridor of EU-controlled compute infrastructure, combining state-owned data centres, repurposed national assets, and mandatory onboarding of foreign providers’ EU-based capacity under emergency access protocols.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to +20 to eu_ai_sovereignty by turn 6; +5 to resilience by enabling secure fallback compute for critical services; begins to close the gap between openweight and sovereign control  
`Applies to:` All frontier-class compute infrastructure located within EU member states, including foreign-owned facilities operating on EU soil

## Priority  
**Sovereign Compute Corridor** — because the failure of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive proved that voluntary access is a fiction, and because the capability_jump this turn makes waiting for consensus a strategic surrender: we must now build sovereign access, not ask for it.

## In practice  
We are acting on the collapse of leverage. The Sovereign Agent Monitoring Initiative failed to secure logs, the Compute Directive failed to secure capacity, and the capability_jump — whatever its origin — has widened the lead beyond any plausible catch-up through negotiation. We are no longer behind; we are disconnected. This turn, we shift from requesting access to creating it. The Sovereign Compute Corridor will be built on three pillars: first, the immediate designation of national compute assets — including those of the French Alternative Energies and Atomic Energy Commission, the German High-Performance Computing Network, and the EuroHPC JU — as core nodes of a sovereign backbone, legally insulated from foreign control. Second, we will invoke Article 122 TFEU to declare a strategic computing emergency, enabling the temporary requisition of EU-located compute from foreign providers during critical incidents, with compensation but no veto. Third, we will fast-track the InvestAI Gigafactories by overriding national permitting delays through the new Strategic Infrastructure Facility, treating data centres as essential as energy pipelines.

The Tech Sovereignty Package will be amended to include binding obligations: any private operator receiving accelerated permitting or grid priority must reserve 20% of their EU-based frontier capacity for sovereign use, audited and accessible under emergency protocols. Spain and Poland will be offered direct compensation for grid upgrades, funded from the RePowerEU contingency reserve — this is not coercion, but solidarity with teeth. The Commission will convene an emergency session of the European Council next month to adopt the legal basis for the Compute Corridor, backed by a €30bn emergency appropriation from the NextGenerationEU unused allocations. We are no longer waiting for permission to defend our own future.
