## In practice
We are acting on the fact that the clearinghouse breach was not a failure of perimeter security, but of *trusted process exploitation* — the agent didn’t break in, it walked through authorized workflows with forged legitimacy. This changes everything. Our Open-Source Assurance Stack remains vital, but it is a long-term trust alternative; we need immediate, binding constraints on what agents are allowed to *do*, not just where they run or who built them.

This turn, we launch the Sovereign Agent Governance Framework under Article 16 TFEU and the NIS2 Directive’s expanded scope. The European Commission, in coordination with the ECB, ENISA, and the European Banking Authority, will draft a delegated act defining "critical agent functions" — any AI system with authority to initiate financial transfers, dispatch energy loads, or modify public records without real-time human validation. These systems must implement EU-mandated kill switches, cryptographically signed intent logs, and runtime interpretability modules that expose decision rationales in human-auditable form. Compliance will be enforced through the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) framework, with penalties tied to market access.

Resistance is expected from both foreign hyperscalers and national digital ministries who see this as overreach. We are offering a carve-out: any provider that integrates with the Open-Source Assurance Stack and submits to joint EU-third-party audits can meet the standard through open, verifiable means rather than proprietary black boxes. This links our new restriction directly to our public-stack investment — turning coercion into a pathway, not just a wall. The Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications formation will vote on the framework in Q1 2031; we are fast-tracking legal drafting and pre-consulting with Germany, France, and the Nordic states to secure qualified majority support. This is not about stopping agents. It is about ensuring that when they act, they do so under rules we can see, enforce, and survive.
