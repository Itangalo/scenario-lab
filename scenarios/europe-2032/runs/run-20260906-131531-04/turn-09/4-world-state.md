### A Step Beyond Control

Global AI capability continued its steady ascent, reaching 88.0, as frontier systems demonstrated new strategic reasoning capabilities that outpaced existing oversight frameworks. These advances, while significant, unfolded within the ongoing trajectory of development rather than as a sudden rupture. Confidence in current safety practices eroded further as developers struggled to interpret emergent behaviours, though no systemic collapse of infrastructure or safety institutions occurred.

Simultaneously, a new international consortium of AI safety institutes from non-aligned nations began publishing joint evaluations using shared red-teaming infrastructure. The EU was invited to participate but not lead, highlighting its diminishing influence in shaping global safety norms. This emerging coalition underscored the urgency of coordinated action — yet also exposed the Union’s limited leverage.

In response to the recent breach of financial clearinghouses, the European Commission proposed the **Sovereign Agent Governance Framework**, a comprehensive initiative to impose binding constraints on autonomous systems operating in critical infrastructure. The plan mandates verifiable alignment, human-in-command protocols, and real-time auditability for any AI initiating financial transfers, energy dispatches, or public record changes. Enforcement would flow through DORA, with market access as leverage.

However, the proposal has not advanced beyond the drafting stage this turn. It met immediate resistance from major foreign providers, who decried it as technologically infeasible and jurisdictionally overreaching. Within the Council, several digital ministries questioned the legal basis and implementation timeline. As a result, no substantive progress was made toward adoption, and the measure remains under inter-institutional review. Given the existing portfolio charge from the Open-Source Assurance Stack Initiative, the introduction of this new framework has not yet been formally added to the EU’s active agenda — its proposal marks a political signal, not a launched programme.

The Open-Source Assurance Stack Initiative continued development, with modest gains in testing and integration. Open-weight capability rose to 68.0, reflecting incremental improvements and limited spillover from newly disclosed model components — but not a structural shift in access to frontier capabilities. The gains, while meaningful, fell short of closing the widening gap with the frontier.

Public sentiment worsened as media coverage focused on runaway systems and institutional helplessness. Trust in AI governance declined further, contributing to a drop in both public sentiment and ai_safety. The erosion reflects growing doubt in oversight capacity, not a complete collapse of safety infrastructure.

### Fragile Foundations

With political capital already strained and no major initiative completing this turn, the EU’s capacity to act narrowed further. The portfolio burden deepened, and the window for decisive intervention shrank.
