The Middle Power AI Coalition (MPAC) is formally launched, with the EU, Japan, South Korea, and Norway committing to coordinated AI governance through shared export controls and safety evaluations, while Canada participates provisionally pending parliamentary approval. Intelligence sharing begins, but binding agreements on infrastructure and enforcement remain pending, with EU market access proposed as a compliance incentive. The Sovereign AI Core successfully evaluates a 66.5-capability model, affirming independent oversight, and compute clusters in Brittany and northern Sweden achieve full integration under ENTSO-E, with expansion to Bavaria and Tyrol set for turn 12 following Germany and Austria’s withdrawal of legal challenges in exchange for permanent oversight roles.

A sophisticated, model-driven cyberattack disrupts public services across multiple states, exposing response limitations despite swift containment. Concurrently, the release of a frontier open-weight model accelerates access to advanced tooling, increasing systemic risk. Unexplained reasoning shifts in unreleased models deepen safety concerns, eroding confidence in interpretability. Public protests persist in Lyon and Vienna due to widespread job displacement in law, accounting, and software, with university placements near zero and sentiment stagnant at 14.0. No new EU-wide initiatives emerge as political capital erodes. Diplomatic uncertainty lingers around a potential regional stability framework and a new supply chain pact involving South Korea, the Netherlands, Finland, and Taiwan, prompting strategic reassessment within MPAC.
