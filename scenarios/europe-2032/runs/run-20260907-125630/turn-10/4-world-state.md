### MPAC Declared Operational, Sovereignty Advances

The European Union declares the Middle Power AI Coalition (MPAC) functionally operational, formalising coordination with Japan, South Korea, Norway, and Canada’s intelligence tier. The Council adopts the MPAC Coordination Protocol under Article 218 TFE/300, binding members to shared export controls on AI-specific chip designs, mutual recognition of safety evaluations, and resilient infrastructure co-location. Market access to EU compute and evaluation services is now leveraged as a compliance mechanism, with automatic review under the Anti-Coercion Instrument for non-compliance. Canada remains outside the core but participates in intelligence sharing, pending domestic approval.

The Sovereign AI Core completes its first live evaluation of a 66.5-capability model, marking a milestone in independent oversight. Brittany and northern Sweden clusters are fully integrated, running on isolated energy grids, with ENTSO-E protocols resolving earlier integration disputes. Germany and Austria withdraw legal challenges in exchange for guaranteed expansion to Bavaria and Tyrol by turn 12 and permanent oversight board seats.

### Cyber Incident Contained, Safety Erodes

A coordinated cyber intrusion targeting hospital administration systems is detected and contained within hours, thanks to real-time logging and rapid countermeasure distribution via MPAC channels. While disruptive, no critical systems fail. However, leading labs report unexplained reasoning shifts in unreleased models, undermining confidence in interpretability frameworks. No systemic breach occurs, but `ai_safety` erodes further as oversight strains.

### Sentiment Stagnant, Political Capital Strained

Public protests in Lyon and Vienna persist over AI opacity and job displacement in law, accounting, and software sectors. University placements remain near zero. Despite MPAC’s success, no formal treaties are finalised, and Canada’s hesitation underscores coalition fragility. The EU avoids new initiatives, awaiting post-incident audits and assessment of the emergent supply chain pact involving South Korea, the Netherlands, Finland, and Taiwan. This strategic shift alters leverage calculations, delaying further commitments.
