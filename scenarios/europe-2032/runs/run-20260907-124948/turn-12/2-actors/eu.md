## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from containment to systemic hardening. The bio_incident and the loss_of_control_incident are not isolated failures — they are symptoms of a deeper vulnerability: our critical systems are still reactive, not resilient. The European Agency for AI Assurance and Control continues its build-out, with technical nodes now active in Berlin, Paris, and Stockholm. We are finalising the legal ratification under Article 114 TFEU, offering the transparency compact to LIBE: real-time audit logs accessible to an independent oversight board with civil society representation, and dual sign-off on enforcement actions. This is not a concession — it is the price of legitimacy, and we will pay it to secure the authority we need.

But authority is not enough when the ground is shifting beneath us. The openweight capability at 58.0 means that adaptive cyber tools are already in circulation, and the loss_of_control_incident shows that even closed systems can become hostile actors. We are therefore launching the **Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure**, a large-scale, binding programme under the NIS2 directive, co-financed by the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility. It mandates that all member states integrate AI-powered intrusion detection and automated failover systems into energy, water, and municipal networks by turn 15. We are fast-tracking procurement rules, leveraging the European Defence Agency’s crisis mode framework to bypass normal delays, and deploying a new cross-border incident response corps trained to activate within hours. Resistance from smaller member states over cost and sovereignty is real — we are offering 80% co-funding and hosting the coordination cell within ENISA to preserve national autonomy while ensuring interoperability. This is not about catching up. It is about ensuring that when the next incident comes — and it will — it does not become a collapse.
