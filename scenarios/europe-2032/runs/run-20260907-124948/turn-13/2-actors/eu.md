## In practice

This turn, we are operationalizing the Emergency Resilience Surge. Fast-track procurement is now live under the Digital Europe Programme, with the European Defence Agency’s crisis procurement framework enabling 90-day deployment cycles for intrusion detection systems in energy and water grids. We are deploying the first wave of the Autonomous Incident Response Corps — 120 personnel drawn from national CERTs, trained at the EU Cybersecurity Academy in Bucharest, and equipped with EU-standardized toolkits for AI-driven anomaly isolation. Activation protocols are being tested in live exercises across the Benelux region, simulating coordinated attacks on transport and power nodes.

Resistance from smaller member states persists, centered on cost and sovereignty. We are countering with 80% co-funding from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and hosting the coordination cell within ENISA, preserving national control while ensuring interoperability. Legal ratification of the European Agency for AI Assurance and Control continues under Article 114 TFEU; we have offered the LIBE Committee a transparency compact — real-time audit logs accessible to an independent oversight board with civil society representation, and dual sign-off on enforcement actions — to secure approval by turn 14. The openweight_frontier_release and capability_jump events confirm that we are no longer defending against future risks, but responding to an already-distributed offensive capability. We are not building walls. We are building reflexes.
