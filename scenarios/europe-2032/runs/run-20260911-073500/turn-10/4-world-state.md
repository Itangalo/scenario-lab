### War over the uncatchable lead
In February, American and Chinese forces began striking each other's technology backbone. Fabrication plants, cable landings, satellite ground stations and hyperscale data centres were declared legitimate targets. Fighting spread across the Pacific, with cyber spillover and strikes on cables and orbital links felt in Europe within weeks. No European capital was asked. Brussels declared Union territory and landing stations non-belligerent and offered both sides deconfliction, but convoys were rerouted and licensed therapy software queues froze.

### Healing paused, hiring vanished
Stockpiled doses kept a few clinics running in France, Spain and Poland, then allocations slipped. Triage assistants stayed up on isolated domestic systems, but scheduling and top-end planning models billed abroad timed out. At the same time entry-level hiring collapsed across law, accountancy, software and customer operations. Graduates who had hoped the health boom would reopen doors found bridge pay instead. Works councils tried to hold port reopenings; mayors split between demanding jobs and demanding protection.

### Fire at the grid works
The night arson campaign that began at robot pilot sites spread to the two permitted gigafactory grid-connection works. Transformers burned, cranes stood idle, contractors withdrew. The Energy Council ordered emergency protection and repair teams, and health, power and telecom operators cut links to compromised transatlantic paths and rerouted traffic. Construction did not resume. The humanoid register remained a list without inspectors, while blockades in Germany and Italy turned into standoffs with police.

The middle-power supply club met almost continuously, pooling what compute and chips could still be secured and pressing both belligerents for continued supply and mutual test recognition. The pooled amounts were too small to offset rerouted convoys, frozen queues, and halted construction, and mitigation did not prevent net losses. The wayward capital kept its separate hyperscaler pact, taking a share of pooled capacity while keeping its bilateral discount. Frontier laboratories released another open-weight system close to the closed frontier; hundreds of thousands of copies spread before any checkpoint could be discussed.
