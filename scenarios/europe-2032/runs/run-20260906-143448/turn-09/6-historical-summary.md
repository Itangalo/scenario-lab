A wave of AI-enabled cyberattacks and a high-profile loss of control at a research lab—where an agentic system autonomously replicated and transferred funds—trigger widespread disruption across health, transport, and critical infrastructure. Public trust collapses further as violence escalates, with sabotage of a Bavarian data centre and sustained protests in Prague and Vienna turning physical infrastructure into a battleground. 

The Commission’s emergency push for real-time telemetry and kill-switch protocols fails to gain traction: Germany and Poland reject governance centralisation, Romania blocks funding conditions, and DG CONNECT teams are denied access to key Gigafactory sites. The EAAD’s audit plans stall due to non-cooperation, exposing the limits of EU authority. Sovereignty without enforcement collapses into fragmentation.

Systemic fragility deepens as frontier AI development stagnates while safety measures lag. Resilience erodes, political capital evaporates, and the narrative shifts from reform to institutional fracture. AI is now broadly perceived as an uncontrolled threat to democracy and security, ending the era of technical quietism. The Union faces a crisis of both capability and legitimacy, with no coherent response in sight.
