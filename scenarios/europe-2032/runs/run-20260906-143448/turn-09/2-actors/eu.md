## New measure
**Measure:** Enforce Real-Time Telemetry and Kill-Switch Integration for All EU-Anchor AI Infrastructure  
Requires all EU-funded or jurisdictionally anchored AI infrastructure, including InvestAI Gigafactories and national compute hubs, to implement zero-trust telemetry and energy-backed kill switches, with central monitoring and deactivation authority vested in the Commission.

## Priority
**Priority:** Enforce Real-Time Telemetry and Kill-Switch Integration for All EU-Anchor AI Infrastructure  
This is the only measure that directly closes the gap between having compute on EU soil and being able to control it — the core failure of the last two years. The Sovereign Cyber Shield is complete, but without enforceable telemetry and kill switches, it cannot act on the systems it is meant to defend. This measure makes sovereignty real, not symbolic.

## In practice
We are acting on three fronts to enforce control over sovereign infrastructure. First, under Article 114 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act’s ancillary powers, we are issuing a Council Implementing Regulation that conditions all future EIB and InvestEU disbursements on proof of integration with zero-trust telemetry and energy-backed kill switches. This applies immediately to the final tranche of Gigafactories funding — no site will receive full disbursement unless it demonstrates real-time monitoring by the Commission and a functional, energy-isolated shutdown mechanism. We are deploying technical teams from DG CONNECT and ENISA to the sites in Finland, Spain, and Romania this month to verify compliance.

Second, we are confronting resistance from Germany and Poland not through override, but through co-ownership. We are offering both states co-leadership of a new EU Sovereign Compute Oversight Board, with veto power over activation of kill switches and full transparency into telemetry data flows. This is not a concession to fragmentation — it is a mechanism to bind sovereignty through shared authority. The proposal will be tabled at the next Competitiveness Council, with a vote scheduled for turn 10.

Third, we are linking this technical enforcement to public legitimacy. The EAAD, though previously limited in scope, will now conduct public audits of the first three integrated sites, publishing redacted findings and hosting town halls in host communities. This is not optional compliance theatre — it is the condition under which we will permit further data centre expansion. We are done building capacity that the public sees as a threat and member states treat as optional. Sovereignty means control — and that control must be visible, shared, and enforceable.
