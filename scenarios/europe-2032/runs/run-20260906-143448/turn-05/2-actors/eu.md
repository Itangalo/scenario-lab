## New measure
**Measure:** Activate Emergency Resilience Corridors  
Establish legally binding, pre-exercised response protocols for AI-driven cyber and critical infrastructure failures, starting with financial systems, power grids, and healthcare, using the cyber defence breakthrough to harden defences and the verification widening to automate oversight.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 7  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 15–20 points by turn 7, reduce the impact of AI-enabled attacks by institutionalising rapid response, and restore public confidence through visible preparedness  
Applies to: Member states, ENISA, EIB, national CERTs, critical infrastructure operators, and the EAAD

## Priority
**Priority:** Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty  
This remains the decisive lever: without sovereign energy, there is no sovereign compute, and without sovereign compute, all other measures—resilience, audit, control—are contingent on the goodwill of others. The collapse of private investment and the failure of SCAP have made it clear that only state-backed, physically grounded infrastructure can anchor our autonomy, and this measure locks in the 5GW of low-carbon power needed to run it by turn 6.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the inflection from emergency reaction to durable sovereignty. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme is now operational under Article 122 TFEU, with the Commission co-proposing a Council Regulation that legally ring-fences 5GW of low-carbon power for sovereign AI systems. We are directing the EIB to reprofile €30bn from the failed SCAP envelope into long-term, output-linked loans for nuclear microreactors in Brittany and geothermal integration in northern Sweden—sites tied to the InvestAI Gigafactories. ENTSO-E is being instructed to treat these allocations as binding under the network development plan, with microgrid isolation and fuel stockpiling where needed. Germany and Austria will be offered a joint oversight mechanism on energy market impacts, but the directive will proceed: strategic autonomy cannot be vetoed by single-member state objections when our hospitals and banks have just been left blind.

The EAAD has now launched as a fully independent agency, with its first mandate being pre-deployment certification for all models above 50.0 capability using the new interpretability tools. We have integrated the verification widening into its protocol: contract review and clinical coding systems must now demonstrate automatic correctness checks before deployment in public services. Real-time logging for critical infrastructure use is being pushed through a revised AI Act amendment in COREPER II, with a firm line: no audit access, no access to EU-wide services. Italy and Hungary are being offered technical assistance from ENISA and a seat on the EAAD advisory board, but the red line holds.

Public sentiment remains at 26.0, and the labour displacement crisis is deepening. We are preparing a communication campaign around the Emergency Resilience Corridors, framing them as the first visible step toward protecting citizens from AI-driven shocks. The cyber defence breakthrough gives us a rare moment of advantage: we are directing ENISA to operationalise automated patching and swarm detection across all Tier 1 critical operators by turn 6, funded through the EIB’s resilience window. This is not just defence—it is demonstration. If we cannot show that the Union can protect its people, no amount of compute will restore our legitimacy.
