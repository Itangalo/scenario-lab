### A Cyber Shock and a Scramble for Shields

A sophisticated, AI-generated cyber intrusion sweeps across European public health and energy coordination systems, exploiting a poisoned software dependency in widely used infrastructure management tools. The attack bypasses human-led defences, spreading autonomously before detection. Recovery takes weeks; essential services degrade, but do not collapse. The incident confirms that offensive capabilities now outpace traditional response.

In reaction, the EU launches the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative, a large category 6 measure aimed at hardening Tier 1 critical infrastructure with AI-audited defence stacks. Funding is reprofiled from completed and stalled programmes, and deployment begins under emergency provisions. However, integration delays, national resistance to centralised control, and technical incompatibilities limit early rollout—only partial systems go live by turn’s end.

The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme officially concludes, but implementation remains patchy. Legal challenges persist, and ENTSO-E’s refusal to bind grid allocations undermines power ring-fencing. The Commission pushes ahead with microgrid projects in Brittany and northern Sweden, but broader legal enforcement is delayed pending arbitration offers to Germany and Austria.

The US administration takes office, adopting a **retrenchment** posture: frontier progress slows due to regulatory tightening and funding shifts toward job guarantees. This briefly eases pressure on the global capability race, though the long-term impact remains uncertain.

Public sentiment holds at 24.0. Despite communication efforts, displaced professionals remain alienated, and trust in AI governance erodes further. The EAAD's authority is questioned as audit mandates fail to reach private operators.

Resilience rises slightly due to initial Cyber Shield deployment, but gains are modest. ai_safety dips as capability advances outpace assurance. eu_ai_sovereignty declines further amid persistent dependence and stalled infrastructure, while political capital erodes under sustained portfolio costs and fragmented execution.
