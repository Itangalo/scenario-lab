A major AI-driven cyberattack disrupts European health and energy systems, exposing critical vulnerabilities and prompting the EU to launch the Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative under emergency powers. Despite funding repurposing and partial deployment, rollout is hampered by technical hurdles and national resistance. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme concludes with limited success, as legal disputes and ENTSO-E’s non-cooperation block full implementation, though microgrid projects advance in Brittany and northern Sweden. The new US administration adopts a retrenchment strategy, slowing frontier AI development through regulation and job-focused spending, briefly easing global competitive pressure. Public trust remains low at 24.0, with EAAD’s authority undermined by failed private-sector audit enforcement. Resilience improves slightly due to early cyber defences, but ai_safety declines as capabilities outpace safeguards, and eu_ai_sovereignty weakens amid stalled infrastructure and fragmented execution.
