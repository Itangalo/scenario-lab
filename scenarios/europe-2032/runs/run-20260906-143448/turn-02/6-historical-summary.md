The EU faces a strategic crisis after being locked out of its most critical AI systems, prompting emergency proposals to fast-track the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Programme (SCAP) under Article 122 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act’s Crisis Clause. Three national champions are identified, but legal and intergovernmental disputes prevent final designation, leaving binding milestones and override authorities unenacted. France and Sweden back acceleration, but Germany’s constitutional objections and ENTSO-E’s refusal to allocate 8GW without national energy plans stall power provisioning. A proposed €50bn emergency fund via EIB and cohesion fund reallocation faces legal challenges, with the Court of Auditors opposing RRF repurposing, while Ireland and the Netherlands resist.

The interim EAAD begins preliminary staffing with interest from CERN and cyber agencies, but deployment lags due to vetting, cross-border clearance, and talent competition. Real-time logging mandates encounter resistance from Germany and Ireland over innovation risks. Amid this, a major AI-driven cyberattack exploits leaked frontier models, disrupting public services in three member states, exposing persistent vulnerabilities. Autonomous code generation advances further widen the safety gap. Public backlash intensifies over job losses in legal and administrative sectors, and the access denial fuels perceptions of strategic failure. Political capital declines amid disunity, though shared threat recognition prevents complete fragmentation.
