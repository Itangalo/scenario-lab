### A Measured Rise in Open Capability, Mounting Trust Deficits

The open-weight frontier advances as a new model is released months after its closed counterpart, gaining rapid adoption across research networks and private enterprises. This progress lifts `openweight_capability`, though not at the pace of closed systems—distribution bottlenecks and integration delays prevent full parity. While the model spreads widely, it lacks the embedded safeguards or infrastructure to push capability close to the closed frontier, keeping `openweight_capability` below 85% of `ai_capability`. The rate of gain remains tethered to the broader compute and deployment ecosystem, and without a surge in open-aligned investment or compute access, growth is constrained. A rise of +1.5, aligned with but slightly lagging `ai_capability`, reflects continued diffusion under existing structural limits.

Public anger intensifies following the automated decision scandal in Hungary, with protests spreading to Vienna and Prague. Data centre construction sites face blockades, and a grid connection in southern Germany suffers sabotage—marking a shift from legal resistance to physical confrontation. Public sentiment declines further, now below 15, as trust in both national and EU governance erodes.

The Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative reaches its formal completion date, but implementation remains patchy. Germany and Poland resist full integration, citing constitutional concerns over centralised control. Peer-assistance teams are deployed, yet interoperability flaws and delayed telemetry prevent cohesive defence. No major incident occurs, but confidence in the system’s effectiveness is low.

In response to the crisis, the Commission proposes a new mandate requiring real-time telemetry and kill-switch protocols on all sovereign AI infrastructure, including Gigafactories sites. However, no enforcement mechanism exists, and Romania immediately signals non-compliance pending legal review. The Council defers action, demanding impact assessments and clarity on jurisdiction. The proposal remains in early debate and has not yet secured any binding commitments or operational changes. Without evidence of cross-border alignment or leverage over resisting states, the initiative does not yet counterbalance the failure to trigger `eu_access_secured`.

Efforts to expand the EAAD’s audit powers remain stalled. Though framed as accountability, the initiative is seen as punitive and poorly timed. With political capital strained and no measure achieving priority status, momentum stalls. The portfolio charge remains high: Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative (−3), proposed telemetry mandate (−2), and lingering costs from earlier initiatives keep pressure on capacity.

The Gigafactories programme concludes without full integration into EU-wide access frameworks. Despite technical completion, `eu_access_secured` has not been triggered—operational control remains fragmented, and sovereignty gains unrealised. Systemic fragility persists, with rising capabilities outpacing safety and resilience investments.

### Reactive Posture, Diminished Leverage

The Union struggles to assert authority amid institutional inertia and public backlash. Its proposals aim to restore control—but without capacity, consensus, or enforcement tools, they remain aspirational. Regulatory reach extends only as far as compliance allows, and for now, that limit is clear. No measure has yet demonstrated traction at the scale needed to close the sovereignty gap, and rewards for incomplete or unadopted actions are withheld. The narrative of response cannot substitute for binding outcomes.
