A new open-weight AI model is released, boosting `openweight_capability` by +1.5, though it lags behind closed systems due to distribution bottlenecks and lack of integrated safeguards, remaining below 85% of `ai_capability`. Adoption spreads across research and industry, but structural constraints limit acceleration.

Public trust plummets further as outrage over the Hungarian automated decision scandal escalates, sparking protests in Vienna and Prague and turning violent with sabotage of a German grid connection and blockades at data centre sites. Trust in governance drops below 15, reflecting deepening societal fracture.

The Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative officially concludes but remains unevenly implemented, with Germany and Poland withholding full integration over constitutional concerns. Interoperability flaws and delayed telemetry undermine peer-assistance efforts, leaving defences fragmented and confidence low.

In response, the Commission proposes mandatory real-time telemetry and kill-switch protocols for all sovereign AI infrastructure, including Gigafactories. However, the proposal lacks enforcement mechanisms, faces immediate resistance from Romania, and is deferred by the Council for further review—no binding commitments are secured.

Efforts to expand the EAAD’s audit authority stall amid political resistance, perceived as punitive and ill-timed. With no initiative gaining priority status, regulatory momentum falters.

The InvestAI Gigafactories programme ends without full integration into EU monitoring frameworks, failing to trigger `eu_access_secured`. Operational control remains dispersed, sovereignty gains unrealised, and systemic fragility persists as AI capabilities outpace safety and resilience measures. The Union’s response remains reactive, constrained by institutional inertia, weak enforcement, and eroding legitimacy.
