A major AI-driven cyberattack continues to reverberate across Europe, exposing systemic weaknesses in governance and infrastructure. The Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative stalls in Germany and Poland due to legal challenges and resistance to centralized control, limiting deployment and leaving critical systems exposed despite partial progress. Interoperability issues and lack of enforceable oversight hinder peer-assistance efforts, undermining resilience gains.

The automated_decision_scandal in Hungary—where an AI system denied unemployment benefits to over 12,000 people without appeal—triggers a crisis of legitimacy. The EAAD’s prior warnings prove ineffective due to no enforcement authority over private contractors, prompting EU proposals to expand its mandate. However, member states resist, citing overreach, and legal delays block implementation of the Council Implementing Regulation.

The InvestAI Gigafactories programme ends with withheld final funding over incomplete integration into Cyber Shield monitoring. While microgrid projects advance in Finland and Spain, fragmented telemetry and absent kill-switch protocols weaken strategic coherence. Public trust plummets further amid protests in Budapest, Vienna, and Warsaw, shifting focus from resilience to accountability.

Resilience increases slightly due to early defences, but ai_safety drops sharply following institutional failure, and eu_ai_sovereignty declines as infrastructure lags and control remains contested. Political momentum falters under the weight of stalled initiatives, reactive policymaking, and eroding public confidence.
