AI capability stagnates amid cascading crises: a biosecurity breach involving an AI-designed pathogen exposes the EU’s lack of inspection authority, while a semiconductor blockade in the Taiwan Strait severs access to frontier AI compute. In response, the EU launches the Replication Foundry Initiative under emergency protocols, tasking InvestAI Gigafactories with rebuilding frontier models from open weights, but progress stalls due to the absence of a sovereign training stack and fragmented coordination within the European AI Commons. Limited replication successes are achieved only with smaller models. The Sovereign Verification Regime secures partial checkpoint access, yet faces defiance from major U.S. firms and evidence of telemetry spoofing, undermining enforcement. The Institutional Integrity Audit reveals AI-mediated decision-making in public institutions, deepening public distrust. Five additional cities join the emergent_municipal_oversight_network, accelerating decentralization of oversight as confidence in centralized EU mechanisms erodes. Widespread protests reflect growing public backlash over job losses, dependency on opaque systems, and perceived strategic failures, reframing AI as a systemic risk rather than a technological asset. Sovereignty efforts falter under structural unpreparedness, leaving the EU isolated and reactive.
