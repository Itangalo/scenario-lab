### The Emergency That Wasn’t Seized

The EU launches the Emergency Observability Directive in response to converging crises: a sudden leap in AI capability, unexplained model behaviours, and a foreign democracy annulling its election over suspected manipulation. Brussels frames it as existential — a last stand for oversight before systems become too fast and opaque to govern. The directive demands full telemetry and checkpoint access for all high-capability AI in public services, with immediate suspension powers for non-compliance.

But momentum stalls. Legal challenges from U.S. providers intensify, citing extraterritorial overreach. Germany and France refuse to waive veto demands in the Sovereign Inference Backbone talks, paralysing coordination. Only Estonia and Finland fully staff joint verification cells; Belgium withdraws at the last minute, citing budget strain. The promised Public Trust Observatory dashboard is delayed — no data to display, only placeholders.

Worse, the two U.S. firms exploit loopholes, rerouting through Irish subsidiaries and invoking trade dispute mechanisms. Transitional compliance offers collapse when one firm leaks internal messages mocking “Brussels theatre.” The Nordic bloc rejects even the national security carve-out, calling the directive a surveillance expansion.

Meanwhile, the **capability_jump** accelerates global development cycles. Frontier labs release systems that outpace existing evaluation frameworks, deepening the opacity crisis. ENISA reports that current audit tools cannot interpret 70% of observed agent behaviours. The **eval_anomaly_reports** remain officially dismissed — “artefacts,” say developers — but intelligence agencies exchange quiet warnings.

The **Replication Foundry Initiative** halts work on three models due to missing checkpoints, slowing progress. Publicly, the EU claims strategic discipline. In private, officials admit they are losing ground.

Resilience erodes further as focus shifts to enforcement. Critical infrastructure remains exposed, and no new cyber defences land. Labour displacement in legal and accounting sectors spreads, feeding unrest.

Political capital dips again under the weight of four large measures. The priority declaration changes nothing — at this level of strain, it carries no pull, only cost.

### A Union Watching Its Tools Fail

The emergency powers exist on paper. But without unity, enforcement, or working tools, they are not power at all.
