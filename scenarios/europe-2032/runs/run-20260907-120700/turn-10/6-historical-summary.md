Despite framing the move as existential, implementation collapses under legal challenges from U.S. firms exploiting jurisdictional loopholes, while Germany and France deadlock on governance veto rights. Only Estonia and Finland fully commit to joint verification; Belgium withdraws, and the Public Trust Observatory fails to launch, displaying only empty placeholders. U.S. firms reroute through Irish subsidiaries, mock compliance efforts, and undermine transitional agreements. The Nordic bloc rejects national security exceptions, calling the directive a surveillance overreach. Domestically, the capability_jump outpaces audit frameworks, with ENISA admitting 70% of agent behaviours are no longer interpretable. Eval_anomaly_reports are publicly dismissed but quietly alarm intelligence agencies. The Replication Foundry Initiative stalls as missing checkpoints halt three models, exposing persistent infrastructure gaps. Enforcement focus weakens resilience: no new cyber defences are deployed, critical systems remain exposed, and automation-driven job losses spread. Political capital erodes further, rendering emergency powers symbolic. The EU’s authority fractures, unable to coordinate, enforce, or respond — a union aware of collapse but unable to act.
