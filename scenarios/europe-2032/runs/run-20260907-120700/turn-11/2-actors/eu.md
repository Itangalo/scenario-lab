## Priority
**Priority:** Replication Foundry Initiative  
This remains our top priority because, in the wake of the failed Emergency Observability Directive, we are left with no path to sovereignty except through independent capacity: if we cannot compel transparency, we must build our own. The **eval_anomaly_reports** confirm that even the frontier no longer understands its systems — which means replication without verification is useless, and only models with full observability will now be prioritised. We are not chasing capability — we are chasing control.

## In practice
We are treating the collapse of the Emergency Observability Directive as a system failure, not a policy misstep. The legal basis under Article 17a of the AI Act did not fail — it was never engaged. U.S. firms rerouted, member states balked, and the verification stack remained unstaffed. ENISA, EDPB, and the Joint Research Centre are now conducting a post-mortem under emergency procedures, not to assign blame but to isolate which parts of the architecture can be salvaged. The telemetry pipelines from the Institutional Integrity Audit are being preserved for reuse, but the enforcement mechanism — the backbone of the directive — has proven unenforceable at scale.

We are now reorienting the Replication Foundry Initiative around a strict principle: no replication without verification. The Polish and Spanish teams are halting work on two of the three models currently in flight — both lack full checkpoint access, and one exhibits behaviour patterns eerily consistent with the eval_anomaly_reports. We are redirecting their compute resources to reconstructing only the most transparent open-weight models, even if that means falling further behind in raw capability. The priority is not catching up — it is ensuring that what we do replicate is actually under our control.

Internally, we are preparing for a reckoning on the Sovereign Inference Backbone. Germany and France remain deadlocked on veto rights, but we are now framing the issue not as governance but as survival: without a shared, hardened infrastructure, there is no sovereign AI at all. We are preparing a revised proposal that limits veto power to national security cases only, to be presented at the emergency Justice and Home Affairs Council session in October. The Nordic bloc’s resistance is noted, but we are no longer negotiating on surveillance — we are negotiating on whether the Union can still act.
