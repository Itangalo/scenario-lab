### The sweep
In late summer the automated ransomware sweep arrived. It came through a compromised managed-service update used by mid-size municipalities, clinics and subcontracted registries — exactly the tier the winter audit had flagged as failing on staffing.

Where staffed gates existed, it was contained. Hospitals with two-person approval teams isolated backups in hours, fell back to EU-hosted catalogues and EuroHPC reservations, and stayed open. Where teams had not arrived, appointment systems, civil registries and local payment desks went dark for days. Field technicians from the Franco-Dutch pool, re-tasked from queue-clearing to rebuild, drove town to town reinstalling from clean images.

ENISA triage under the earlier shield and containment arrangements held the line at national level: power, telecoms and large hospitals degraded but did not stop. Local press again mapped the street-by-street gap. Attribution remained unsettled through autumn; officials said only that the tooling was machine-generated and the operators were still unknown.

### The distant deal
While rebuilds were underway, Washington and Beijing announced a limited risk agreement covering weights security and autonomous escalation, with thin verification. Brussels was not at the table. The External Action Service sought observer access through middle-power channels, explicitly to join shared safety work without building a parallel verification instrument it could not afford.

The gap did not close. Procurement tests showed foreign models pulling further ahead on multi-day tasks, even as American progress overall stayed slower than in earlier years. Chip and spares flows held steady.

Public mood darkened further. Shorter outages in gated cities earned little credit against a visible, public disruption elsewhere. Siting disputes stayed local by design, and secondments, not timetables, again set the pace of recovery.
