Late summer 2032 tested the human-gate law with an automated ransomware sweep via a compromised managed-service update hitting the understaffed mid-size municipalities, clinics and subcontracted registries flagged in the winter audit.

Where two-person approval teams existed, hospitals and registries isolated backups, fell back to EU-hosted catalogues and EuroHPC reservations, and stayed open; where not, appointment systems and payment desks went dark for days. Franco-Dutch pool technicians shifted to rebuilds, and ENISA triage held power, telecoms and large hospitals from stopping. Attribution stayed unsettled — machine-generated tooling, unknown operators.

Meanwhile Washington and Beijing struck a limited weights-security and autonomous-escalation risk deal with thin verification; Brussels, not at the table, sought observer access via middle powers rather than build unaffordable verification. The commercial gap widened on multi-day tasks despite slower U.S. progress, while chip/spares flows held.

Public mood darkened further as gated-city resilience earned little credit against visible local disruption, with secondments still setting recovery pace.
