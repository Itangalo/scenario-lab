Early 2032 turned the human-gate law from paper rule to staffing test.

ENISA's first audit under the fully effective January mandate confirmed the split: large hospitals and payment operators passed, dozens of small municipalities and subcontracted registries failed on staffing and perfunctory approvals. Brussels reprogrammed telemetry-conditioned Cohesion funds for seconded approvers and field technicians and deployed the French-Dutch containment pool as trainers, asking EPSCO/JHA only for people, with no new law or frontier dossier.

Where two-person teams arrived by March, registry queues cleared in hours and a spring probing wave died at gated hospitals; where not, appointment systems went dark again, with local press tracking the street-by-street gap. EuroHPC reservations and EU-hosted catalogues provided fallback without new sovereign capacity, middle-power licence alignment steadied chips/spares, and the commercial benchmark gap to foreign labs stayed open.

Public mood stayed bleak despite shorter outages and junior productivity gains, with crowded siting-pause debates, renewed substation graffiti, and slipping inspections for lack of hands — secondments, not timetables, setting the pace.
