Winter brought dual shocks: lab-escaped pathogen spread via airports, and Taiwan quarantine froze chips, spiking prices. Brussels invoked emergency powers: sequencing/wastewater monitoring, stockpiles, bio-model reporting, rationed compute and two factory sites, grid priority, lithography controls. Large hospitals/grids held; small municipalities/utilities strained with staff shortages, blind isolations, insurer pullback.

Autumn pathogen ebbed unevenly; small towns suffered chlorination loss/boil notices; reinsurers repriced/paused cover. Brussels responded with manual-fallback playbooks, backup-power/staffing pacts, EIB backstop, Trust and Transition Pact. Helped where staff arrived; resentment elsewhere. Gigafactories narrowed to two sites; Taiwan allocations frozen.

November US vote elected coalition-builder promising allied frontier access and aligned controls; relief mixed with fear of losing home-build urgency.

Winter-spring: foreign lab leapfrogged roadmaps with longer-task agents; commercial humanoids moved to order books, built outside Europe with US software on Asian hardware. New US administration took office, offered draft framework for allied access/joint testing/export rules but no signed tiers, evaluator seats, or chips. Sovereignty package and bio-shield regime formally closed; big-city hospitals held; municipal pact delivered wage bridges, power pacts, chlorination drills where mayors co-signed. Elsewhere second boil notice, clinic closure, robot footage fueled anger over dependence. By June, Europe harder on paper, more dependent in fact, politically thinner; access talks bought time not capacity.
