### Cut off
The notice arrived on a Friday afternoon with no reason attached. Clinics, ministries and contractors that had built triage, translation and planning workflows on the leading American model found queries refused. In Washington it was described as a technical tiering step; in European hospitals it meant diverted appointments and a weekend of paper forms.

Brussels tabled it as a breach of the draft access framework and sent negotiators back to demand interim health and administration tiers and evaluator seats, paired with export-control alignment. They returned with process, not capacity.

At the same time one capital signed its own hosting and supply arrangement, defended at home as pragmatism. The Commission called it a breach of the common line; the capital called it insurance.

### A faster frontier
Behind the access fight, the frontier shifted. A new release cadence — longer-running agents, faster iteration, less human hand in training itself — made last year's evaluations stale. Safety teams admitted they could no longer characterise reasoning traces reliably, and coverage slipped back from recent gains.

Open models crept closer, good enough to carry part of the fallback load, though compute constraints and fragmented deployment kept their advance slower than the frontier jump this turn. DG CNECT, HERA and ENISA pushed hospitals and administrations onto European-hosted models and revived manual drills for chlorination, grid and clinics. Where mayors co-signed, waiting lists eased and boil notices ended. Where they did not, blockades around grid-priority building sites hardened, with water, power prices and jobs fused into one protest.

### Jobs and cures
Autumn brought measured layoffs in law, accountancy, software support and customer operations. Graduate hiring froze. At the same moment tailored therapies reached ordinary wards — real remissions — but delivered on models Europe could not run itself. Gratitude curdled into resentment.

A joint cyber telemetry and biosurveillance pact offered some cover: shared attribution and sample-sharing that blunted two intrusion waves. The two concrete gigafactory shells kept rising, permits and state-aid cover intact, with first build-out milestones now recorded as sovereign capacity in construction, though suppliers still quoted years for equipment and full operation remained distant.

By December Europe was kept running by fallback, not by access regained, with sovereign build-out one step further along but not yet operational.
