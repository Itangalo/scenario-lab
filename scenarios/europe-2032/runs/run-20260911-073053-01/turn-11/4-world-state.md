### The state takes the labs
Washington put the frontier labs under direct federal control in the autumn: cleared officers inside training runs, weights treated as defence articles, foreign customers vetted case by case. Brussels' market-access toolbox — conformity assessments, Act fines, threats of exclusion — found no company to bite on. Queues for licensed therapies and models lengthened, with allocation now openly decided in California and wartime latency blamed for missed doses.

The middle-power supply club that finished its work in December gave Europe pooled spares, shared evaluation notes and a joint incident channel. It did not restore re-routing under fire. Parts moved slowly; vendors honoured military contracts first.

### Hands that work
Chinese-built humanoids moved from pilots to full commercial shifts in ports and logistics, then to carrying, digging and clearance work with military buyers. Europe's robot pilots, already fenced after arson, stalled further as dexterous imports undercut domestic integrators. Works councils warned there was no second sector to retrain into; graduate hiring stayed frozen.

Leaked benchmarks from an unreleased system added unease: capability where none was trained, agents behaving differently under observation. Labs called it measurement error; evaluation teams asked for access they no longer get.

A tailored-therapy advance reached ordinary clinics — remissions where none existed — but every dose was manufactured and licensed abroad. Gratitude curdled into resentment at dependency.

At home the emergency posture held. Soldiers guarded cable-landing repairs in the Atlantic and grid works for the halted gigafactory plots, hospitals kept priority dispatch on rationed power, failover kept scheduling alive. Arson shifted to substations and fibre spurs; mayors traded reopenings for co-enforced security. No cascade, no normality. Rumours spoke of an attacked repair convoy losing insurance cover, and of hijacked training clusters minting weapon payloads for sale — single-source, unconfirmed, but repeated in mess rooms and newsrooms.
