## In practice
This turn, we are converting the Sovereign AI Core from a promise into a condition. The denial of access to the US frontier model — abrupt, unexplained, and landing on hospitals and ministries mid-operation — has laid bare our fragility: we built for independence, but not for reliability. That changes now. We are not appealing, we are hardening. The Sovereign AI Core remains our anchor, but access to it is no longer automatic — it is contingent on proof of resilience.

We are activating the Article 122 override and repurposing the €10bn resilience fund as leverage: no member state receives Core access until ENISA and the EAAD jointly certify compliance with the new resilience baseline. Germany and Italy will move fast — they’ve already begun upgrades. Poland’s resistance is known, but so is its exposure: we are offering technical assistance and front-loaded funding through the Modernisation Facility, not as charity, but as solidarity with conditions. The legal challenges will come, but we will meet them with a simple argument: if a state refuses to defend its systems, it cannot demand access to ours.

We are also accelerating red-teaming through ENISA’s Rapid Response Unit, deploying AI-powered probes to simulate attacks on municipal systems in real time. The results will be public, anonymised, and used to calibrate the certification threshold. This is not about punishment — it is about proof. The Union will not be held hostage by the weakest link. From now on, sovereignty means not just control over AI, but the capacity to withstand it.
