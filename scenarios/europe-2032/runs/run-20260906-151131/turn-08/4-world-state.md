### The Fracture Widens

The cyberattack on municipal services—automated, precise, and exploiting AI-crafted vulnerabilities—left a trail of disrupted healthcare appointments, frozen transit payments, and exposed personal data. Though no fatalities occurred, the public response was one of outrage. Blame shifted rapidly: between national governments for lax defences, the EU for failing to enforce cohesion, and the AI labs whose tools enabled the intrusion. Trust in digital governance eroded further.

Brussels responded with urgency, pivoting from stalled sovereignty talks to emergency resilience. The Interoperable Sovereignty Network was quietly shelved after Poland refused to sign without veto rights. In its place, the Commission launched the EU-Wide AI-Resilient Public Services Shield, mandating hardened defences for access to Sovereign AI Core compute. The €10bn fund, once voluntary, became a gatekeeper mechanism.

Yet the pivot came at a cost. ENISA’s red teams found most mid-sized cities unprepared, and fast-tracking detection systems revealed supply bottlenecks. Germany and Italy began upgrades, but Poland froze participation, citing sovereignty concerns. The Article 122 override was invoked, but legal challenges loomed.

Meanwhile, the global AI landscape shifted. The **ai_investment_collapse** hit hard: two major US-backed EU compute projects were cancelled, and private capital retreated. This deepened dependence on the faltering Sovereign AI Core Programme, which missed its 60.0 target—now projected for turn 9. The American posture of *Consolidation* tightened, with selective chip access further limiting EU options.

Open-weight models, meanwhile, advanced. A bio-design tool adapted from leaked code enabled a citizen-led diagnostic breakthrough—but also demonstrated how easily dual-use capabilities could escape control. The frontier in offensive cyber surged, reflected in the jump in `openweight_capability`.

Public sentiment dipped as automation accelerated in legal and clerical jobs, with no offsetting narrative of protection. The Commission’s resilience push, though real, felt reactive. The narrative was clear: capability outpaced safety, and resilience lagged behind threat.

### A Narrowing Window

The EU acted, but not cohesively. It had the will to harden defences, but not the capital, unity, or time to do so effectively.
