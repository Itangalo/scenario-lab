# Statements: The European Union (turn 8)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Rebuild trust in EU-level AI governance by making sovereign control verifiable, inclusive, and publicly accountable — starting with full integration of the new interpretability tool into SMAF, extending observer rights to all member states, and publishing all containment decisions through the Public Assurance Protocol — and now adding mandatory resilience baselines for any state accessing Sovereign AI Core compute, enforced through the EAAD and tied to dual-key access.

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_mandates` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: identity
  - Text: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now see that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without social legitimacy is a fortress without a people. Yet legitimacy alone is not enough: when resilience fails, trust evaporates faster than it can be rebuilt. The attack on public services this turn — model-generated, widespread, and barely contained — proves that harm prevention is not secondary to sovereignty, it is its foundation. We are not just stewards of power, but guarantors of continuity.

Response was fragmented, attribution remains uncertain, and the public sees no difference between foreign adversaries and systemic fragility. Our interpretability success is now overshadowed by a failure of protection. The identity must evolve: we are not only torn between two mandates — we are now under fire from having neglected the bridge between them.
  - Reason: a identity-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, nor control as a substitute for resilience; we will now couple every containment measure with a public explanation of its purpose and limits, and every sovereignty measure with a resilience floor; and we will extend that duty to member states, ensuring that no government within the Union feels it must go it alone to be secure — because we now see that without shared defences, there can be no shared trust.
  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** revealed that even with advanced interpretability, we are blind to downstream harm when our infrastructure is soft. Our commitment to act under uncertainty must now include the obligation to harden, not just verify. Speed without resilience is surrender.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Rebuild trust in EU-level AI governance by making sovereign control verifiable, inclusive, and publicly accountable — starting with full integration of the new interpretability tool into SMAF, extending observer rights to all member states, and publishing all containment decisions through the Public Assurance Protocol — and now adding mandatory resilience baselines for any state accessing Sovereign AI Core compute, enforced through the EAAD and tied to dual-key access.
  - Trigger: The **cyber_major_incident** and the **ai_investment_collapse**
  - Grounds: The attack demonstrated that sovereignty without resilience is a liability — a powerful stack behind weak defences invites escalation, not deterrence. Meanwhile, the investment collapse has shattered private compute build-outs we depended on, forcing us to reprioritise public resilience as the core of credible sovereignty. Trust must now be earned not only through transparency, but through demonstrated protection.
  - Evidence quoted: cyber_major_incident: A large, largely automated attack lands. Its form is chosen at the time – a ransomware sweep across public services, a compromised software dependency with an unknown blast radius, or disruption of infrastructure directly – and severity scales with how far offensive capability has outrun defence. Damage is real and public, defenders are visibly behind, and attribution takes months. Whoever ran it, the tooling was model-generated.
ai_investment_collapse: Capital flees the sector. Valuations reset hard, announced build-out is cancelled rather than delayed, and several of the arrangements European compute was depending on evaporate with it. What the frontier laboratories can afford to train shrinks for the first time.
  - Reason: The cyber incident exposes weak defences, undermining sovereign control; the investment collapse breaks reliance on private compute, forcing public resilience as the basis of credible sovereignty.
