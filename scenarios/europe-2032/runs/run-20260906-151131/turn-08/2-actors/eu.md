## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now see that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without social legitimacy is a fortress without a people. Yet legitimacy alone is not enough: when resilience fails, trust evaporates faster than it can be rebuilt. The attack on public services this turn — model-generated, widespread, and barely contained — proves that harm prevention is not secondary to sovereignty, it is its foundation. We are not just stewards of power, but guarantors of continuity.

  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** has landed with severity. Ransomware swept municipal services across three member states, exploiting AI-designed zero-days in legacy software stacks. Response was fragmented, attribution remains uncertain, and the public sees no difference between foreign adversaries and systemic fragility. Our interpretability success is now overshadowed by a failure of protection. The identity must evolve: we are not only torn between two mandates — we are now under fire from having neglected the bridge between them.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, nor control as a substitute for resilience; we will now couple every containment measure with a public explanation of its purpose and limits, and every sovereignty measure with a resilience floor; and we will extend that duty to member states, ensuring that no government within the Union feels it must go it alone to be secure — because we now see that without shared defences, there can be no shared trust.

  - Grounds: The **cyber_major_incident** revealed that even with advanced interpretability, we are blind to downstream harm when our infrastructure is soft. Our commitment to act under uncertainty must now include the obligation to harden, not just verify. Speed without resilience is surrender.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Rebuild trust in EU-level AI governance by making sovereign control verifiable, inclusive, and publicly accountable — starting with full integration of the new interpretability tool into SMAF, extending observer rights to all member states, and publishing all containment decisions through the Public Assurance Protocol — and now adding mandatory resilience baselines for any state accessing Sovereign AI Core compute, enforced through the EAAD and tied to dual-key access.

  - Trigger: The **cyber_major_incident** and the **ai_investment_collapse**  
  - Grounds: The attack demonstrated that sovereignty without resilience is a liability — a powerful stack behind weak defences invites escalation, not deterrence. Meanwhile, the investment collapse has shattered private compute build-outs we depended on, forcing us to reprioritise public resilience as the core of credible sovereignty. Trust must now be earned not only through transparency, but through demonstrated protection.

## In practice

This turn, we are shifting from covenant back to concrete. The Interoperable Sovereignty Network was the right idea at the wrong time — and the **cyber_major_incident** has shown we cannot bind member states through trust alone when their hospitals, transit systems, and tax portals are crumbling under automated assault. We are now acting on the lesson: sovereignty must be defensible, or it is not sovereignty.

We are repurposing the €10bn resilience fund — previously underutilized — into an emergency compliance mechanism. No member state will gain access to Sovereign AI Core compute unless it has implemented AI-resistant digital service architecture by turn 10. We are using the Modernisation Fund and Article 122 override not as incentives, but as levers: compliance unlocks access, non-compliance means exclusion from the Union’s most advanced stack.

ENISA is deploying rapid red teams to assess the weakest links, starting with municipalities in Germany, Italy, and Poland. The EAAD is expanding its mandate to include not just model audits, but incident readiness reviews. We are fast-tracking AI-powered intrusion detection systems through the CISA-ENISA joint procurement channel, and mandating air-gapped fallbacks for essential services — not perfect, but enough to break the cascade.

We are not waiting for consensus. We are building the floor beneath sovereignty — and we will raise the bar, not lower it, when the next attack comes.
