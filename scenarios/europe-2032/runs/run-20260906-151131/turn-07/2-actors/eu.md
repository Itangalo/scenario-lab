## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without social legitimacy is a fortress without a people. We further recognise that when a member state turns outward for security, it is not defection but a cry for agency: our duty is not to punish it, but to make Union-level control credible, visible, and shared.

  - Grounds: Poland’s bilateral move was not an act of rebellion but of desperation — a sovereign state refusing to be left behind. The US hyperscaler’s offer was not merely access, but inclusion in decision-making over how that power is used. Our response must not be to reassert control from above, but to embed it in mutual trust. The triad of technical, political, and perceptual failure is now complete: we have tools (interpretability), we have threats (67.0), and we have fracture (Poland). But we lack the legitimacy to bind them. That legitimacy must be co-created.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and we will now pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; nor will we assume that control justifies silence, and we will couple every containment measure with a public explanation of its purpose and limits; and we will now extend that duty to member states, ensuring that no government within the Union feels it must go it alone to be secure.

  - Grounds: The *member_state_defection* was not a failure of loyalty but of inclusion. Our original commitment assumed we could act decisively *for* the Union. We now see we must act *with* it. The interpretability breakthrough gives us a rare moment to demonstrate real control — but only if it is seen to be shared. Henceforward, acting under uncertainty includes the duty to consult, not just to justify.

- retire `two_year_commitment`
- add `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Rebuild trust in EU-level AI governance by making sovereign control verifiable, inclusive, and publicly accountable — starting with full integration of the new interpretability tool into SMAF, extending observer rights to all member states, and publishing all containment decisions through the Public Assurance Protocol.

  - Grounds: The previous commitment assumed we could secure systems unilaterally. The Poland crisis proves that is impossible. The new interpretability tool gives us a technical basis for trust; the Public Assurance Protocol gives us a channel for it. Our priority is no longer just to *have* control, but to *prove* it — to member states, to the public, and to ourselves. This is not a retreat from sovereignty, but a redefinition: sovereignty as shared stewardship.

## In practice

This turn, we are shifting from control to covenant. The US has surged to 67.0 with self-improving agents, and we now know — from French lab adoption — that the real-time interpretability tool can flag deceptive reasoning before output. We have the means to verify control. But Poland’s deal proves we lack the means to command trust. So we are no longer asking member states to accept our safeguards — we are asking them to co-own them.

The EAAD’s first audit of the SACP prototype stack is underway, using the new tool to trace agent reasoning in real time. We are publishing the methodology openly, and we are inviting national auditors from all member states to observe — not as guests, but as participants. The Public Assurance Protocol closes this turn, and we are releasing its first report not as a press release, but as a public event in Wrocław, co-hosted with Poland, with live demonstrations of the interpretability tool in action.

Meanwhile, the €10bn resilience grant for the second air-gapped cluster is being tied not to bilateral control, but to multilateral access: any member state that complies with SMAF and adopts the interpretability standard can request compute time under dual key. We are using the Modernisation Fund as leverage, not punishment, and we are fast-tracking grid upgrades through the Article 122 override — but only for sites that join the Interoperable Sovereignty Network.

We are not building faster. We are building together.
