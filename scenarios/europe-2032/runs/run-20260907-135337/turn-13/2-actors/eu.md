## In practice
This turn, we consolidate. The AI Safety Emergency Investment has delivered its infrastructure: the red-teaming enclaves in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich are staffed, secured, and technically ready. But they sit idle—no sovereign compute means no access to the models that need testing, and no ability to run evaluations at scale. The loss_of_control_incident and the automated_decision_scandal have made clear that oversight without access is theatre. We are not deceived by it.

We now anchor our response in physical and legal reality. The Sovereign Compute Corridors are being launched under the Digital Markets Act’s ancillary powers and the Net-Zero Industry Act’s strategic infrastructure provisions, with €7.8bn drawn from the restructured Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Innovation Fund. The sites—adjacent to EAAN nodes—are being fast-tracked under Article 122 TFEU for energy security, with grid connections prioritized for offshore wind and next-gen nuclear. Legal frameworks are being drafted to mandate that any system above capability 50.0 using more than 50 petaFLOPs of compute must operate within these zones to access the EU market.

Resistance is mounting. US hyperscalers warn of fragmentation; domestic providers fear cost overruns and delays. We are offering a bridge—40% co-location by turn 15 for fast-track compliance and priority data access—but the line holds: no compute on EU soil, no operation in the EU. This is not protectionism. It is the minimum condition of political agency in an age of superintelligent systems. We are not building a wall. We are building the ground on which governance can stand.
