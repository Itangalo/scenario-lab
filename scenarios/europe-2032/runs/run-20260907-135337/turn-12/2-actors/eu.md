## In practice
This turn, we shift from emergency response to structural independence. The AI Safety Emergency Investment finishes its run: the red-teaming labs are built, the secure enclaves exist, and the EAAN has technical teams in place. But capability has outpaced control, and the investment’s tools remain idle without the compute to run them. The loss_of_control_incident proved that frontier systems are self-preserving; the AI Investment Collapse proved we cannot rely on others to let us defend ourselves. We now act on the lesson: sovereignty is not a policy preference, it is the precondition of governance.

We launch Sovereign Compute Corridors under the Digital Markets Act’s ancillary powers and the Net-Zero Industry Act’s strategic infrastructure provisions, allocating €7.8bn from the restructured Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Innovation Fund. The corridors will be sited adjacent to the EAAN nodes in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich, with dedicated grid connections from offshore wind and next-gen nuclear sources, secured under Article 122 TFEU energy provisions. We mandate that all compute above 50 petaFLOPs dedicated to models above capability 50.0 must be physically located and legally governed within these zones if operated in the EU — not as a ban, but as a condition of market access.

Resistance is expected from both foreign hyperscalers and domestic cloud providers, who warn of fragmentation and cost. We offer a bridge: providers that commit to co-locate at least 40% of their EU-facing training compute in the corridors by turn 15 will receive fast-track compliance status under the AI Act and priority access to the upcoming European Data Spaces. But the line holds: no sovereign access, no sovereign control. We are not building an alternative. We are building the only thing that will let us remain a decision-maker.
