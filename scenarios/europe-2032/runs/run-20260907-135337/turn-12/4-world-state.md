### Capability Stalls, Trust Crumbles

AI capability creeps upward to 63.5 as efficiency gains persist, but the frontier shows signs of strain: a major release underperforms expectations, and prices for top-tier access drop sharply—market signals suggesting stagnation. Meanwhile, an audit reveals that a national social insurance system used an AI model to deny thousands of claims based on flawed data lineage, with a court ruling confirming systemic harm. Public trust plummets further.

Frontier labs increasingly resist oversight, obfuscating training data sources and agent behaviour in internal logs. Claims of safety ring hollow as regulators admit they cannot verify what happens inside leading models.

### EU Shifts to Structural Sovereignty

The EU advances its emergency AI Safety Investment: red-teaming enclaves are now operational in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich, fulfilling a plan set in motion earlier. However, without sovereign compute, these tools remain underutilized. The loss_of_control_incident exposed the limits of evaluation without control—and the ai_investment_collapse made clear that reliance on foreign capacity is untenable.

In response, the Commission proposes **Sovereign Compute Corridors**, a large Category 4 measure aimed at anchoring high-performance AI infrastructure within EU jurisdiction. €7.8bn is earmarked to build energy-secured zones co-located with EAAN nodes, powered by offshore wind and next-gen nuclear. A draft rule mandates that any system above capability 50.0 using more than 50 petaFLOPs must operate within these corridors to access the EU market.

The proposal is fast-tracked as the EU’s new priority, consolidating focus amid growing strain. US hyperscalers protest, calling it digital protectionism. Domestic providers warn of cost increases. Some propose partial compliance—hosting inference only in the corridors—while delaying full migration.

### Political Capital Erodes Further

The portfolio remains heavy: two large and one small measure active, plus the new priority. The automated decision scandal—a domestic failure—triggers deep political penalties. Labour unrest grows as displaced public sector workers join protests in multiple capitals. Resilience dips as hardening stalls. With no safety upgrades landing, ai_safety collapses further due to opaque reasoning and rising capability.

Public sentiment crashes to 20.0. Media frames the court ruling and lab resistance as proof that AI is both harmful and unaccountable. The EU’s move is seen as necessary but too late.
