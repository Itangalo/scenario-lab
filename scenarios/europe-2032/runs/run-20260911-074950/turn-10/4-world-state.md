### Shelter Under Fire
January brought war no declaration in Brussels could keep out. American and Chinese forces exchanged strikes on orbital links, undersea cables and fabrication plants, and within weeks a landing station in the Atlantic and a power feed to a southern fab were damaged. European councils declared non-belligerency, denied offensive use of Union infrastructure, and ordered backups dispersed.

Reserve teams that had restored registries in autumn were now wartime mutual aid, moving clean images between hospitals and town halls as outages rolled. Hardening of landing points and grid switchgear with the new long-life alloys began, but slowly — engineers under curfew, parts held at borders.

### Cures That Come From Elsewhere
At the same time, tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions reached clinical use abroad. Brussels negotiated licensed EU-hosted manufacturing, and pooled reagents through the emergency health apparatus. A few pilot hospitals administered the first courses in spring.

The benefit was real and visible, but it arrived on foreign models and foreign supply chains. Patients thanked European nurses while reading that the design and the dose came from elsewhere. Relief mixed with humiliation.

By June services had not collapsed — triage assistants kept sorting, wage top-ups kept flowing — yet graduate hiring stayed frozen, warehouses filled with more sorting machines, and machine halls in Paris and Warsaw hummed empty while overseas systems planned over days. Europe was sheltering, healing, and holding lines, but with almost no room left to decide more.
