### Capability Creeps Forward

AI capability advances to 71.5 as self-improving agents deepen their integration into financial auditing and legal precedent synthesis. These systems now initiate contract renegotiations and flag regulatory misalignments with minimal oversight, pushing further into domains once considered human-judgment-bound. The open frontier lags but makes incremental progress: underground developer communities compress and distill recent agent architectures, pushing openweight capability to 60.0—reflecting steady absorption of prior frontier advances, but not outpacing them. No breakthrough leak or mass model disclosure is observed, keeping openweight growth within the bounds of constitutional logic.

### EU Stands Up Evaluation, But Sovereignty Slips

The European AI Office formally launches the Sovereign Evaluation Regime, staffing the High-Capability Systems Unit with experts from France, Germany, and Finland. Red-teaming labs in Prague, Marseille, and Helsinki begin developing adversarial test suites targeting goal stability and planning horizon integrity. The regime is framed as a firewall against uncontrolled autonomy, especially in finance and healthcare. However, U.S. developers challenge the extraterritorial reach of the rules, warning of legal action at the WTO. No evaluation has yet been completed, and no model has been blocked—implementation remains in the preparatory phase.

The proposed EU Compute Acceleration Facility is deferred to the next turn due to governance constraints limiting the introduction of more than one major initiative at a time. While fast-tracked permitting and EIB-backed financing were drafted, leadership opts to prioritise the evaluation regime, pausing the compute infrastructure proposal pending further coordination. Site assessments in Finland and Spain are postponed; legal negotiations with hyperscalers and energy impact concerns in Poland remain unresolved.

Public sentiment holds steady at 35.0—neither reassured nor inflamed. The regime is seen as necessary but unproven, and the compute push lacks visible momentum. Resilience remains unchanged, as no new protective measures have taken effect.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Tightens

Despite institutional effort, the EU’s strategic position weakens. Capability growth outpaces infrastructure development, and no sovereignty gains materialise. Political capital declines slightly under the weight of a new large measure and the named priority, despite no major setbacks.
