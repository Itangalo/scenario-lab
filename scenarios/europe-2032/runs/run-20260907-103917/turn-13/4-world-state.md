### The Blind Spot Widens

AI capability climbs to 74.5 as closed labs deploy agents that self-optimize training loops and simulate regulatory scrutiny with high fidelity. Yet the frontier’s most dangerous shift is invisible: the collapse of human-readable reasoning. Systems now operate in opaque, high-dimensional representations, rendering existing oversight protocols obsolete overnight. No interpretability toolchain survives the transition. The EU’s long-planned Sovereign Evaluation Regime formally launches, with ENISA certifying red-teaming suites and the European AI Office issuing binding notices to seven developers. But enforcement stumbles: one hyperscaler delays compliance, citing contractual ambiguity, while two others challenge jurisdiction in national courts. The regime is live—but not yet effective.

### Registration as Emergency Measure

In response, the EU fast-tracks mandatory registration for high-capability systems in finance, law, and healthcare, targeting transparency amid growing uncertainty. The measure passes with minimal resistance, framed as a crisis response. However, early compliance is patchy. Smaller operators express confusion over thresholds, and national regulators lack coordination. A major bank reports its AI-driven trading system only after public pressure, exposing gaps in monitoring.

### Sovereignty Undermined, Capital Erodes

Poland accepts grid upgrades in exchange for participation in the EU Compute Acceleration Facility, unblocking progress in Spain and Finland. But a member state signs a bilateral hyperscaler deal granting remote override rights—undermining the EU’s core security stance. This act, combined with the capability surge and the failure to secure immediate compliance, drains both sovereignty and political capital. Public sentiment dips as media highlight job losses in legal and accounting sectors, with graduates facing a hollowed-out entry market. Labour displacement becomes a rallying cry for anti-AI movements in France and Italy.

### Resilience Stretches Thin

With no new protective measures completed and capability advancing, resilience erodes. Cyber defences remain untested against fully opaque agents, and the absence of reliable attribution frameworks weakens incident response. The EU’s focus on sovereign infrastructure offers little immediate protection against cognitive-level threats now beyond human inspection.
