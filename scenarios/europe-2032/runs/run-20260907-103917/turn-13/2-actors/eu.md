## In practice
This turn, we operationalise the final phase of the Sovereign Evaluation Regime, delivering ENISA’s accredited red-teaming protocols and formally launching the first cycle of adversarial testing. With the regime now finished, we transition from development to enforcement: the European AI Office activates its High-Capability Systems Unit, integrating experts from ANSSI, BSI, and NCA, and issues binding registration notices under Article 12a of the AI Act to seven developers operating agent systems in financial settlement and clinical decision-support above 65.0. These are not voluntary submissions—they are legally mandated, and non-compliance triggers immediate suspension procedures. We frame this not as overreach but as the minimum condition for market access: if you deploy at this level, you are in scope.

Simultaneously, the EU Compute Acceleration Facility advances in Finland and Spain, where EIB-backed projects clear preliminary environmental reviews. Legal teams finalise model clauses prohibiting remote override, and we condition further funding on binding commitments from private operators to cede root access to EU-controlled entities. In Poland, we break the deadlock by offering RRF-backed grid modernisation in exchange for participation, turning energy concerns into co-investment. We also initiate technical talks with ASML and Infineon to co-locate sparse-AI clusters, aiming to close the loop between sovereign compute and control-aware development.

In response to opaque_reasoning, we cannot wait for full evaluation—because we can no longer read what the models are doing, we must at least know where they are. That is why we launch the mandatory registration framework this turn, converting a previously withheld proposal into action. Using the European AI Office as the central node, we require operators in finance, law, and healthcare to disclose system capabilities, deployment scope, and human oversight arrangements by turn 14. This is not a substitute for evaluation—it is its prerequisite. Member states’ regulators are tasked with enforcement, and we offer technical support and partial cost coverage to ensure compliance. The measure is narrow by design: it targets only the most sensitive sectors, avoids burdening smaller deployments, and builds political feasibility by showing tangible action in the wake of visible risk. Without this, we are blind at the worst possible moment.
