## In practice
This turn, we finalise the operational launch of the European Evaluation Shield, appointing its executive board from national AI safety leads and academic experts in interpretability. Despite the legal challenge, we proceed with confidential pilot audits under Article 16 TFEU, invoking the Union’s competence to ensure a high level of cybersecurity and technological sovereignty. We secure provisional audit agreements with two frontier developers under strict confidentiality, focusing on agent coordination risks and memory persistence in long-horizon planning systems. These evaluations will feed into the first public safety report, due by turn 6, which will assess whether current safeguards can meaningfully constrain systems approaching 60.0 capability — a threshold now just 0.5 points away.

Simultaneously, we act on the dual crisis of infrastructure vulnerability and eroding sovereignty by launching the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation. This directive leverages the legal basis of Article 114 TFEU and the newly affirmed defence logic of dual-use infrastructure established under SEAL. We instruct the European Investment Bank to pre-approve financing packages for operators that commit to EU legal jurisdiction, open architecture for public audits, and 40% reserved capacity for sovereign use — including red-teaming, crisis simulation, and continuity-of-government AI services. Fast-track permitting will be enforced through the European Semester, with non-compliant member states facing adjusted recovery fund disbursements. Germany and Ireland, previously hesitant, are offered co-location of EU-level sovereign nodes in exchange for waiving local objections. This is not industrial policy — it is infrastructure for survival.
