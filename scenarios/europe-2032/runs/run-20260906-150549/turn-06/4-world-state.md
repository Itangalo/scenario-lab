### Threshold Crossed, Trust Eroded

AI capability reaches 60.0 in early 2029, crossing a psychological and technical threshold. Systems now routinely complete multi-day professional projects end to end, displacing junior roles across law, engineering, and research. Yet assurance lags: the gap between capability and safety widens, and frontier models operate without newly deployed safeguards.

The European Union launches the **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation**, a sweeping directive to fast-track EU-controlled compute infrastructure with binding public-use obligations. Invoking Article 114 TFEU, the Commission pressures member states to prioritise data centres that reserve 40% capacity for sovereign purposes. Germany and Ireland, previously resistant, are offered co-location rights for EU-level nodes — a tactical concession, not full alignment. Implementation begins, but legal pushback looms, and grid access disputes in Poland and Spain delay initial deployments.

Meanwhile, the **European Evaluation Shield** proceeds with pilot audits despite a formal legal challenge filed by a coalition of frontier developers and foreign governments. The European Court of Justice agrees to hear arguments over whether the agency’s inspection powers violate intellectual property rights. The Commission defends the audits as essential, but avoids speculating on likely outcomes, emphasizing procedural adherence. Publicly, no official acknowledges risk to enforcement timelines.

A high-profile election annulment in a major EU member state, officially justified by concerns over AI-driven disinformation, triggers political tremors. While no direct causal link is proven, the incident fuels public anxiety about AI’s role in undermining democratic legitimacy. Trust erodes further as media spotlight job losses in administrative sectors and data centre protests intensify in rural regions.

ENISA reports that last turn’s hospital ransomware attack exploited AI-generated zero-day exploits, adapting in real time — a harbinger of automated offensive systems now in circulation. Resilience efforts remain fragmented, and funding decisions on a coordinated cyber shield are pending.

### Sovereignty Gains, Capital Strains

The **Tech sovereignty package** finishes this turn, delivering accelerated-permitting zones but falling short of its capital mobilisation target. The **InvestAI Gigafactories** remain in flight, contributing incrementally. The **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation** is introduced as the new measure for the turn, beginning phased implementation with partial resource allocation. No additional priority is declared, maintaining focus on existing commitments.

Sovereignty rises due to completed initiatives and the launch of the new mobilisation effort. Political capital declines moderately, reflecting the burden of an active portfolio and growing public strain, but not a collapse — the absence of decisive success is noted, though no legitimacy-shaking event occurs. Public sentiment plunges on displacement fears and democratic unease.

The Commission signals increased focus on foundational infrastructure, but does not formally name a new priority, preserving alignment with the existing portfolio. This restraint comes at the cost of slower progress on harmonising evaluation standards across member states.
