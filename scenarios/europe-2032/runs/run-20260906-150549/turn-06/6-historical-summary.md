AI capability reaches 60.0 in early 2029, crossing a critical threshold as systems autonomously execute complex professional tasks, accelerating job displacement in law, engineering, and research. The gap between AI capability and safety widens, with frontier models operating without updated safeguards. The European Union launches the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation under Article 114 TFEU, mandating 40% compute capacity reserved for sovereign use and offering co-location concessions to previously resistant states like Germany and Ireland. Legal challenges and grid access disputes in Poland and Spain delay implementation. The European Evaluation Shield continues pilot audits amid a European Court of Justice review over intellectual property concerns, with the Commission defending its authority. A high-profile election annulment linked to AI-driven disinformation deepens public distrust, compounding anxiety over job losses and data centre expansions. ENISA confirms that the prior ransomware attacks leveraged AI-generated zero-day exploits, highlighting growing automated threats. Resilience remains weak, and cyber shield funding stalls. The Tech Sovereignty Package concludes with partial success, while the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation becomes the new priority. Sovereignty improves due to completed initiatives and new mobilisation efforts, but political capital and public sentiment decline sharply amid democratic concerns, social unrest, and unmet economic expectations.
