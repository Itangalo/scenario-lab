**European Evaluation Shield**  
A new public agency with legal authority to conduct independent, pre-deployment evaluations of any AI system above capability threshold 50.0, with power to require interpretability audits, agent monitoring, and weight inspections.

**Secure European Access to Advanced Lithography (SEAL)** — because without sovereign control over the servicing of the tools that keep EU chip fabs running, even legally reserved compute and new gigafactories are hostage to foreign goodwill, and the current US campaign_security_hawk posture proves that goodwill cannot be assumed.

## In practice  
This turn, we advance the implementation of the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive by tasking the European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) with drafting the allocation framework for the 30% reserved compute, based on a utility-style auction model prioritising public research, critical infrastructure resilience, and sovereign AI development. We activate the liability shield and fast-track access to InvestAI Gigafactories funding for cloud providers that commit to EU-controlled allocation, but uptake remains low: Germany and Ireland continue to hedge, citing concerns over retaliation. We respond by accelerating bilateral assurances and offering joint risk-sharing on export-controlled components through the European Defence Fund.

Simultaneously, we push SEAL into operational design. The €12bn co-funding envelope is ring-fenced from the Chips Act’s unused reserves and the European Defence Fund, and we convene ASML’s EU-based engineers, STMicroelectronics, and Infineon to map out a parallel maintenance network anchored in Dresden, Crolles, and Catania. Legal drafting proceeds under Article 114 for internal market integrity and Article 42 for defence capability, with the new Joint Undertaking under the European Chips Institute set to take charge by turn 5. The goal is clear: no EU-operated lithography tool should ever go uncalibrated because a foreign government blocks a technician’s visa or software update.

Amid rising concern from the eval_anomaly_reports event and the growing campaign_backlash against unchecked AI, we launch the European Evaluation Shield as a necessary counterweight: a public institution with real teeth, modelled on the European Medicines Agency, to evaluate high-capability systems before deployment. We begin staffing from national agencies and academic labs, and initiate negotiations with two frontier developers for pilot audits under mutual confidentiality. This is not about slowing progress — it is about ensuring that when systems begin to coordinate in ways we cannot explain, we are not left asking permission to understand them.
