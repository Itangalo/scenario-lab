### A Turn of Shifting Ground

The EU launches **Sovereign Evaluation Enforcement**, a large category 1 measure aiming to transform the European Evaluation Shield into a fully empowered regulator with authority over high-capability AI systems. The Commission drafts a regulation under Article 114 TFEU, backed by redacted audit findings showing unmonitored agent memory in frontier models. Early outreach to Parliament and national agencies begins, but legal and political resistance is immediate.

Despite the urgency, the automated decision scandal dominates public discourse. A court rules that an AI-assisted welfare denial system violated due process across three member states, triggering widespread condemnation. Trust in algorithmic governance plummets, and public sentiment drops sharply. While the new measure aims at safety, its association with AI governance in general deepens public suspicion rather than allaying it.

Meanwhile, signs of a capability plateau emerge: a leading lab’s flagship model fails to meet internal benchmarks, and prices for top-tier inference drop unexpectedly. Yet `ai_capability` still rises by 0.5, reflecting slow but persistent progress. No corresponding safety gains land—assurance remains shallow, and the gap between capability and control widens. The cyber defence breakthrough improves resilience prospects, but no structural upgrades are completed this turn.

The US posture of **ALLIANCE** continues, enabling quiet coordination on evaluation standards, though no joint action materializes. Internal cohesion holds, but the political cost of the new initiative bites quickly.

### Setbacks and Signals

Germany insists on expanded veto rights over audits touching dual-use systems, threatening to delay parliamentary approval. Legal experts warn the proposed regulation may exceed EU competences, especially regarding non-EU developers. The measure is formally in flight—but its path is narrow, its support conditional, and its success far from certain.

ENISA detects continued probing of industrial systems, now linked to fine-tuned open models trained on leaked technical data. The threat evolves quietly, beneath the threshold of incident.
