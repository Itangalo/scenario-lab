## Priority

## In practice  
This turn, we initiate the legislative and institutional process to transform the European Evaluation Shield from a contested pilot into a fully empowered agency. The Commission drafts a regulation under Article 114 TFEU to grant the Shield direct enforcement authority over AI systems above capability threshold 52.0, requiring pre-deployment audits, agent monitoring, and weight inspections for any model deployed in or serving the EU market. The proposal includes a legal mandate to compel disclosure of training data summaries, architecture decisions, and agent memory architectures, with penalties for non-compliance including market exclusion and compute access revocation.

We engage the European Parliament’s ITRE and LIBE committees early, sharing redacted findings from the pilot audit to demonstrate necessity. Simultaneously, we coordinate with national cybersecurity agencies and ENISA to establish a joint inspection protocol, ensuring technical credibility. Germany and France signal conditional support, provided veto rights over defence-adjacent workloads are preserved; we offer a compromise: a tripartite review board including national representatives, but with final authority resting in the Shield’s executive directorate. The legal basis will be contested — we expect challenges — but the automated decision scandal has shifted public sentiment toward oversight, and the capability plateau gives us breathing room to build not just faster, but better.
