## In practice  
This turn, we advance the legislative drafting of the Sovereign Evaluation Enforcement regulation under Article 114 TFEU, targeting adoption by the European Parliament and Council within two turns. The Commission’s Legal Service is conducting a thorough competence assessment to pre-empt challenges, particularly regarding extraterritorial application and dual-use systems. We are engaging national regulators through the Digital Policy Cooperation Forum and coordinating with ENISA to align technical protocols for agent monitoring and weight inspection.  

Germany’s demand for expanded veto rights over audits on defence-adjacent systems has been met with a proposal for a tripartite review board — composed of the European Evaluation Shield, national security representatives, and independent technical experts — though final authority would rest with the Shield’s executive directorate. Simultaneously, we are preparing for legal challenges from frontier labs by compiling redacted evidence of unmonitored agent memory persistence and sharing it selectively with friendly members of the LIBE Committee to build parliamentary backing.  

Despite the drop in public sentiment, we are reframing the initiative not as expansion of AI governance, but as restoration of democratic control: a regulator empowered not to accelerate deployment, but to halt systems that operate beyond human understanding. The message is narrow, urgent, and defensive — sovereignty not as technological triumph, but as minimal condition for survival.
