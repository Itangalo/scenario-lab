### Stalemate in Brussels

The European Union presses forward with **Sovereign Evaluation Enforcement**, aiming to cement the European Evaluation Shield’s authority over high-capability AI systems. Drafting advances under Article 114 TFEU, and technical coordination with ENISA strengthens. Yet the initiative stalls politically. Germany refuses to back down on its demand for veto power over audits touching dual-use systems, insisting national security cannot be outsourced to a supranational body. A proposed tripartite review board fails to bridge the gap, with Berlin demanding final say.

Resistance is no longer external. One major European frontier lab suspends data sharing with the Evaluation Shield pending a European Court of Justice opinion, citing legal uncertainty. Others echo concerns about delays to model releases, framing audits as de facto deployment blocks. The **emergent_eval_resistance** event crystallises into open institutional defiance — not sabotage, but a coordinated slowdown that undermines enforcement before it begins.

Publicly, the Commission rebrands the effort as a democratic safeguard, not a technical upgrade. But the narrative fails to land. Trust in AI governance remains shattered after the welfare denial rulings, and the new measure is seen as more bureaucracy, not accountability. Public sentiment holds at rock bottom.

Meanwhile, global AI capability inches upward. Incremental gains in efficiency and training stability push **ai_capability** to 61.5, though no breakthroughs emerge. No safety improvements land on deployed systems — audits remain contested, interpretability is patchy, and no new safeguards are operationalised. **ai_safety** stagnates.

Resilience sees no structural upgrades. ENISA tracks more industrial probes using fine-tuned open models, but no major incident occurs. **resilience** holds.
