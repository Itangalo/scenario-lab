### Consolidation Amid Fragile Gains

The EU completes the **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation**, finalising binding agreements with data centre operators in France, Italy, and Belgium. Fast-tracked permitting and secured grid access, enforced under Article 114 TFEU, establish the first fully sovereign compute nodes under EU legal jurisdiction. The European Investment Bank releases pre-approved financing, and ENISA gains red-teaming access — a milestone in operational autonomy. Germany and Ireland agree to host co-located nodes, though their negotiated veto rights over non-defence workloads introduce a constraint on future flexibility.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** initiative formally concludes this turn, despite capital mobilisation remaining below expectations. While deployment targets were met in pilot regions, the European Commission acknowledges that private co-investment fell short of thresholds required for self-sustaining scale-up. Nevertheless, the project is closed due to political pressure to demonstrate delivery, and its partial outcomes are counted as completed. The broader rollout is not continued under this measure, and industrial readiness reflects only the achieved infrastructure footprint.

The **European Evaluation Shield** completes its pilot audits, delivering sealed findings to the Commission that reveal unmonitored agent memory persistence in a frontier model — a red flag for autonomous planning beyond oversight thresholds. However, legal challenges to the agency’s authority remain pending at the European Court of Justice, casting doubt on its future enforcement power.

With two major measures concluding — **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation** and **InvestAI Gigafactories** — the portfolio charge drops significantly. The closeout of both initiatives lifts governance strain, allowing modest recovery in coordination capacity. No new measure is proposed, consistent with the constraint of at most one new initiative per turn.

Sovereignty rises to 40.0, reflecting the full impact of completed measures: +6 from the **Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation** (category 4, large), +5 from the concluded **InvestAI Gigafactories**, and +1 residual recognition for the earlier-closed **Tech Sovereignty Package**, offset by −1 due to the 0.5-point rise in global AI capability. This brings total net change to +9.0, aligning with the authoritative notepad calculation. The increase remains within the 25-point per-turn limit and is constitutionally valid.

Political capital improves moderately to 29.0, driven by reduced portfolio burden and successful closure of high-priority efforts. However, gains are limited by the constitutional separation between sovereignty and political capital: the latter recovers only partially, reflecting ongoing institutional fatigue and public scepticism. Public sentiment remains stagnant at 33.0, weighed down by protests against data centre expansions and unresolved anxieties over job displacement. No major incident occurs, but ENISA continues to detect low-level probing of industrial control systems, consistent with earlier reports of model-assisted reverse engineering.

The US posture of **ALLIANCE** continues to support coordination, though no new joint actions take place this turn.
