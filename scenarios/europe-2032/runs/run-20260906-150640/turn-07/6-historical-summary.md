AI capability shows signs of plateauing in closed labs, yet offensive AI tools proliferate, exemplified by a widespread ransomware campaign using fine-tuned 60.0-class models that disrupts municipal services across EU member states. In response, the EU deploys swarm-detection systems for real-time cyber defense and launches the Openweight Containment Initiative, mandating watermarking, tracking, and remote deactivation for open-weight models above 45.0—though enforcement faces resistance from academia and cloud providers. Major foundational programmes conclude: the Tech Sovereignty Package secures €200bn in private investment, SMAF finalizes auditability standards, and EAAD and CIRS become permanent institutions. The Public Assurance Protocol is completed but fails to restore public trust. Poland delays commitment to the Wrocław sovereign cluster despite a €10bn resilience grant. Public sentiment deteriorates further, driven by job losses in public IT and growing backlash against open-model releases, undermining containment efforts and exposing widening gaps between policy ambition, implementation, and societal resilience.
