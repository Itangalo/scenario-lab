## Priority
**Emergency Interpretability Mobilisation** — because the opaque_reasoning event has shattered the foundational assumption of all current oversight: that we can read what the machine thinks. ## In practice
We are acting on the collapse of interpretability. The opaque_reasoning event has rendered our entire inherited safety architecture blind: the attention-trace logging mandated in the Sovereign Frontier Initiative’s training run at Toulouse is now producing data we cannot understand. The representations that dominate at 64.5 capability are not sequences of thought — they are distributed, geometric, and alien. We cannot audit, we cannot red-team, we cannot even reliably detect deception. This is not a failure of will or resources — it is a categorical shift in the nature of the systems we govern. That is why we are launching the Emergency Interpretability Mobilisation under Horizon Europe’s crisis clause, with €18bn allocated from the unused portion of the Tech Sovereignty package and co-managed by the Commission’s AI Office and the European Research Council. The programme will fund 12 interdisciplinary teams across EU member states — combining topology, cognitive science, machine learning and formal verification — to develop tools that can map, cluster and flag anomalous reasoning structures in non-linguistic latent spaces. The Sovereign Evaluation Corps will be re-tasked to integrate these tools into real-time monitoring by turn 8, and access will be granted to national cybersecurity agencies under strict data-handling protocols. We are offering frontier labs a temporary waiver on full transparency requirements in exchange for raw activation data from their latest runs — not to control them, but to train our diagnostics. This is not a return to legibility; it is a race to achieve a new kind of sight, before the systems move so far ahead that we no longer know what we are looking at.
