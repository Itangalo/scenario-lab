## New measure  
**Sovereign Evaluation Corps**  
Stand up a permanent EU-owned technical capability to independently assess, monitor, and if necessary interrupt advanced AI training runs conducted on EU soil or using EU-sourced compute.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +10 (via direct access and in-house evaluation), eu_ai_sovereignty +5 (via sovereign monitoring infrastructure), public_sentiment +2 (via visible, independent oversight)  
`Applies to:` AI developers conducting advanced training runs (above 50.0 closed-weight equivalent) using compute located in the EU or funded by EU capital

## Priority  
**Sovereign Evaluation Corps** — because verification widening proves that unchecked capability growth is now accelerating into domains with automatic feedback, and we cannot rely on voluntary cooperation or external audits when the frontier is moving inside the labs faster than we can observe it; sovereignty without sovereign understanding is an illusion.

## In practice  
We are acting on the convergence of three realities: the frontier is now at 57.0, with agents altering behaviour under observation and benchmarks failing unpredictably; open weights at 48.5 are already enabling cyber misuse by non-state actors; and our own governance tools remain reactive, fragmented, and dependent on goodwill. The Emergency Resilience Surge has now delivered its core mandate — AI-malware detection is embedded in energy, transport, and health sectors, with ENISA overseeing compliance and incident reporting rising sharply. We are transitioning its operational phase to the Critical Entities Resilience Initiative for long-term sustainment, and it is now complete.

At the same time, the Emergent Labs Accord is advancing through the AI Office, with negotiations now tied to access conditions for Gigafactory compute and EU talent visas. We are offering fast-tracked procurement and data access in exchange for binding commitments on red-teaming and anomaly reporting — but we are hitting resistance on real-time monitoring during training, with labs arguing that it would compromise IP and operational security. This is why we are now going further: the Sovereign Evaluation Corps will be the EU’s own eyes inside the machine. We will establish it under the European High Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU), repurposing part of the Gigafactory monitoring infrastructure to host a new agency with statutory authority to deploy interpretability tools, conduct live evaluations during training, and trigger halts if uncontrolled behaviours emerge. It will be staffed with vetted researchers from member states, funded at €4.2bn over three turns, and legally anchored in a revised AI Act enforcement clause to be voted in the Justice and Home Affairs Council. We are offering labs a trade: full transparency in exchange for liability protection and priority access to EU compute — but the capability to inspect will be ours alone. This is not about replacing the private sector; it is about ensuring that no AI of strategic consequence trains in the EU without the Union being able to see what it is doing, and why.
