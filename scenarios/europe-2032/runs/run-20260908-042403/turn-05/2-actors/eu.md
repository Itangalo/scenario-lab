## In practice
We are acting on the narrowing window for credible sovereignty. The Sovereign Frontier Initiative has now launched its first training run at the Toulouse Gigafactory node, using 15% of Phase 1 compute allocation under Commission Directive 2028/AI-1, activated via Article 114 TFEU with a new annex on public-purpose AI. The dataset is drawn from the European Open Science Cloud, filtered for clinical and regulatory domains where autonomous capability is already disruptive, and trained under strict interpretability constraints requiring full attention-trace logging. The EU Talent Retention Facility has secured the return of 28 senior researchers from North American labs, with relocation packages funded through Horizon Europe’s emergency clause. We are now confronting resistance from two major labs that refuse to allow Sovereign Evaluation Corps inspectors real-time access to their training clusters, citing proprietary risk. This is why we are launching the Frontier Access Compact: we will offer them tiered access to the sovereign model’s weights and training infrastructure — including joint fine-tuning rights for commercial derivatives — in exchange for binding adherence to our evaluation protocols and a moratorium on open-weight releases above 55.0. The offer will be tabled at the Special Council on AI Sovereignty in November, with participation tied to eligibility for future Gigafactory compute allocations. This is not coercion — it is the construction of a European AI ecosystem with a core we control, and a perimeter we can defend.
