## New measure
**Emergency Audit Access Framework**  
Establish binding legal authority for EU-certified evaluators to conduct deep technical audits of all AI systems deployed in critical infrastructure, using tools from the Emergency Interpretability Mobilisation to extract non-linguistic representations of model reasoning.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 11`  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +6 (by enabling pre-deployment audits of high-risk systems), eu_ai_sovereignty +2 (by asserting jurisdiction over system cognition), resilience +1 (via early detection of emergent risks in operational models)  
`Applies to:` All AI systems used in energy, health, transport, and democratic infrastructure across EU member states, including those developed or hosted outside the Union

## In practice
We are finalising the Emergency Cyber Shield under Article 114 TFEU and NIS2 emergency derogations, with ENISA’s Crisis Attribution Cell now operational in Budapest. Real-time telemetry is flowing from pilot nodes in Germany, Poland, and Spain, and we have pre-positioned forensic teams to respond within 90 minutes of a confirmed intrusion. But the shield is only as strong as the trust behind it: the election annulment has shattered confidence, and southern and eastern member states are withholding full data access, demanding guarantees on compute equity. To hold the coalition, we are pairing the Shield’s rollout with immediate compute allocations from the Gigafactory reserve — 10% of capacity ring-fenced for public-sector AI in resisting states, conditional on full integration by Q1 2031.

At the same time, we are launching the Emergency Audit Access Framework to close the cognitive gap. The tools from the Interpretability Mobilisation — though experimental — can now map latent goal structures in agent behaviour. We are drafting a Council Regulation under Article 16 TFEU to mandate access, using the newly formed European AI Safety Board as the certifying authority. Resistance is expected from both foreign providers and domestic ministries accustomed to opacity. To break the logjam, we are offering a phased rollout: voluntary submission with safe harbour for six months, then mandatory audits with public redacted summaries. The legal basis is fragile, but the bio uplift findings have shifted the Overton window — even sceptical member states now admit we cannot regulate what we cannot see. This is not about stopping the frontier. It is about ensuring that when it hits, we know what hit us.
