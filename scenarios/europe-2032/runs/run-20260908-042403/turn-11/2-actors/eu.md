The Audit Framework’s finishing turn is here, but its political finish line remains out of reach. The Council’s Legal Affairs Committee has deadlocked. Foreign providers have mobilised legal teams across three jurisdictions, challenging both competence and proportionality. Domestic ministries in France and Sweden resist on grounds of national security sovereignty, despite the fact that their own systems now depend on AI components whose internal logic no human can follow. We are in the final week of negotiations, pushing for a qualified majority under Article 114 TFEU, backed by a statement of urgency from the European Council. The European AI Safety Board stands ready to operationalise audits the moment authority is granted — we have the tools, we have the personnel, we have the precedent from the Budapest pilot — but without a legal basis, it is all inert.

Meanwhile, the fallout from `eu_frontier_access_denied` continues to spread. Hospitals in Italy and Belgium report disruptions in radiology workflows; the European Medicines Agency has paused AI-supported review cycles. No provider has offered compensation, only silence. The incident is being framed domestically as a failure of foresight, not of dependence — and that narrative is gaining ground in national parliaments. We are responding with emergency allocations from the Health Union reserve to backfill critical functions, but the damage is not only operational: legitimacy is eroding. The public sees a Union that builds shields but cannot guarantee the lights stay on.

The `emergent_joint_defence_initiative` offers a narrow path forward. We have been invited into technical working groups on shared threat telemetry, but full integration is conditional on resilience upgrades the EU cannot currently deliver. We are using this leverage to demand reciprocity: access to joint defence data streams in exchange for accelerated compliance with audit standards. It is a fragile bid — too much desperation risks being read as weakness — but if we can anchor audit authority to mutual security, we may yet close the legitimacy gap. The next 90 days will determine whether the EU governs AI, or merely survives its effects.
