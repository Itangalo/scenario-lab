## Priority  
**Sovereign Frontier Initiative** — because the emergent_lab_defection proves that relying on private labs to anchor EU sovereignty is failing, and with the frontier at 59.5 and rising, we cannot wait for persuasion or alignment; we must now build our own path to capability or accept permanent dependency.

## In practice  
We are acting on the irreversible erosion of trust and cohesion. The Emergent Labs Accord has now concluded — it delivered red-teaming protocols and a shared incident registry, but failed to secure real-time access to training runs or binding commitments on open-weight releases. With one leading lab now threatening to relocate, and others citing legal uncertainty, we can no longer treat cooperation as the default. The Sovereign Evaluation Corps is progressing under EuroHPC JU, but its authority remains contingent on lab compliance, and we are already seeing resistance to inspection protocols. This is why we are now escalating to full-stack sovereignty: we will launch the Sovereign Frontier Initiative under the same legal base as the Gigafactories — Article 114 TFEU for internal market harmonisation — but with a new annex granting the Commission direct control over a training run reserved for public purpose. We will allocate 15% of Gigafactory Phase 1 compute to this effort, draw on the European Open Science Cloud for data, and activate the EU Talent Retention Facility to bring back researchers working abroad. The model will be trained under full interpretability constraints, with all weights secured and evaluated by the Sovereign Evaluation Corps before any deployment. We are offering labs a new bargain: full participation in this initiative — with IP co-ownership and first access to outputs — in exchange for dropping resistance to inspection. But if they refuse, we will proceed without them. This is not nationalisation; it is the Union claiming its right to exist as a technological actor in its own right. The first training run begins in turn 5, and we will report progress to the European Council in December.
