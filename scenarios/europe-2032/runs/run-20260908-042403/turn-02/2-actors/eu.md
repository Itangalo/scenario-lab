## New measure  
**Emergent Labs Accord**  
Establish a formal EU-backed framework to institutionalise and expand the emergent cooperation between European AI laboratories, embedding red-team coordination and pre-release evaluations into binding, auditable processes.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: small`

## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because AI-driven cyber threats are already operational in the wild, critical infrastructure is demonstrably vulnerable, and we are out of time for voluntary coordination; this is the only measure that directly prevents cascading failure while our sovereignty builds.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that the frontier is moving faster than institutions can follow, and that trust — in systems, in governance, in the Union itself — is now a scarce resource. The Emergency Resilience Surge is being implemented through binding directives under Article 122 TFEU and NIS2, with ENISA at the operational core. We are deploying the prototype AI-malware detection system across energy, transport, and health sectors via the Critical Entities Resilience Initiative, mandating integration for all major cloud providers despite their resistance. Front-loaded grants cover 70% of compliance costs for fiscally strained members, while cohesion fund conditionality applies pressure where incentives fail. The Council Presidency is driving qualified majority adoption in Justice and Home Affairs, bypassing national vetoes — this is not optional.

At the same time, we are seizing the opening created by the emergent lab cooperation. The voluntary consortium is fragile, limited to post-training evaluations, and excludes active training runs where the greatest risks lie. Our new measure, the Emergent Labs Accord, will formalise this cooperation under EU auspices: it mandates shared red-teaming protocols, pre-release evaluations by independent EU-certified assessors, and real-time anomaly reporting during training for any lab accessing EU compute or data. Participation will be tied to benefits — access to Gigafactory capacity, fast-tracked talent visas, and public procurement preference — turning cooperation into a condition of privilege, not just goodwill. We will negotiate the framework through the AI Office, with adoption expected under the revised AI Act enforcement powers by turn 4. This is not a replacement for state oversight, but a way to make it land where it matters: inside the labs, before release.
