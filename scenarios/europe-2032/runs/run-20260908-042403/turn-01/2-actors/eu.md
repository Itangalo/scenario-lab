## Priority
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_major_incident proves offensive AI tools are already in use, defenders are behind, and we cannot wait for full capability or consensus when systems that keep lights on and water flowing are at risk.

## In practice
We are acting on the fact that the frontier has already breached containment. The cyber_test_shot and cyber_major_incident are not warnings — they are the attack. We are treating this as a strategic emergency: under Article 122 TFEU and the NIS2 Directive, we are fast-tracking a binding resilience package that mandates AI-driven threat modelling, red-teaming, and failover protocols for all operators of essential services. The ENISA agency will be scaled up immediately with €3.8bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme, co-financed by a 15% reallocation of national cyber budgets over the next 18 months.

This is not optional coordination — it is enforced preparation. Member states will resist the cost and the intrusion, especially those with weaker fiscal space. We are offering front-loaded grants covering 70% of compliance costs for the first two years, tied to quarterly milestones, and we are leveraging cohesion funds as a stick: non-compliance will trigger suspension of digital infrastructure disbursements. The Council Presidency will convene an emergency Justice and Home Affairs meeting in December to adopt the framework by qualified majority, bypassing prolonged negotiations. Industry will be brought in through the Critical Entities Resilience Initiative, with mandatory participation for cloud providers, energy grid operators, and port authorities hit in the recent intrusion.

We are also embedding detection of AI-generated malware in the new threat intelligence pipeline, using pattern recognition trained on the artefacts from the recent attack. This is not about catching every intrusion — it is about ensuring cascading failure does not follow. The measure will be reviewed at turn 3, but we are acting now because waiting for perfect attribution or full harmonisation is how you lose the infrastructure.
