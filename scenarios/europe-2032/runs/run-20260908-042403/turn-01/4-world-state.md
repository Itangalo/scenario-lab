The AI frontier advances relentlessly, with capability rising to 54.5 as leading labs compress training cycles and scale coordination between agent systems. ### Crisis Response Meets Resistance

The EU launches the Emergency Resilience Surge as a binding directive under Article 122 TFEU, aiming to harden critical infrastructure against AI-driven cyber threats. Despite front-loaded grants and cohesion fund leverage, pushback emerges immediately. Two fiscally strained member states threaten legal challenges, arguing the fast-track bypasses national sovereignty. Coordination is uneven: while energy and port operators begin red-teaming, cloud providers resist mandatory integration into the new threat pipeline.

ENISA’s expansion is approved, but staffing and procurement delays slow deployment. The detection system for AI-generated malware goes live in prototype form, offering partial coverage. Though the measure is in flight, its full effects are deferred.

### Trust Erodes Further

A major automated decision scandal erupts when an AI-supported welfare eligibility system in a large member state is found to have wrongfully denied thousands of claims, with a national ombudsman confirming systemic bias. The incident, rooted in domestic deployment, triggers widespread public anger. Trust in AI erodes, dragging public sentiment down to 37.0. The backlash complicates the EU’s broader AI agenda, framing all initiatives as risky by default.
