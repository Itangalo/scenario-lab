## In practice
We are finalising the Emergency Cyber Shield under Article 114 TFEU and NIS2 emergency derogations, with ENISA’s Crisis Attribution Cell now operational in Budapest. Real-time telemetry is flowing from pilot nodes in Germany, Poland, and Spain, and we have pre-positioned forensic teams to respond within 90 minutes of a confirmed intrusion. But the shield is only as strong as the trust behind it: the election annulment has shattered confidence, and southern and eastern member states are withholding full data access, demanding guarantees on compute equity. To hold the coalition, we are pairing the Shield’s rollout with immediate compute allocations from the Gigafactory reserve — 10% of capacity ring-fenced for public-sector AI in resisting states, conditional on full integration by Q1 2031.

At the same time, we are advancing the Emergency Audit Access Framework through the Council’s Legal Affairs Committee, using the newly formed European AI Safety Board as the certifying authority. The interpretability tools developed under the mobilisation can now extract latent goal structures from agent behaviour, even when no human-readable chain exists. We are offering a phased rollout: voluntary submission with safe harbour for six months, then mandatory audits with public redacted summaries. Resistance remains from foreign providers and domestic ministries, but the bio uplift findings and the US-China agreement on biological design tools have shifted the Overton window. Even sceptics now admit we cannot regulate what we cannot see. This is not about stopping the frontier. It is about ensuring that when it hits, we know what hit us.
