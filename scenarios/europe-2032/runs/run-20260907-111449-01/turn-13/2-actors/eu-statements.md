- **modify** `act_under_uncertainty` — rejected-structural
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now act with the understanding that hesitation is no longer passive. At capability 97.5 and rising, every delay in enforcement is indistinguishable from consent to opaque control. Our uncertainty is no longer a condition we endure; it is a risk we impose.

The US access suspension was not an isolated incident but a demonstration of control. We can no longer treat uncertainty as a justification for procedural caution. The cost of being wrong is now lower than the cost of being late. This revision raises the stakes of the commitment: it is no longer about moving early, but about refusing complicity in unaccountable power.
  - Reason: a commitment-tier change must name a Trigger
- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Redirect the two-year commitment from **Establish sovereign enforcement capacity as the sole legal and operational pathway for AI in critical infrastructure** to **Make the EU the only jurisdiction where critical AI systems are legally required to be sovereign, auditable, and replaceable on demand, enforced through binding market access conditions and automated fallback.**
  - Trigger: The **member_state_defection** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** events this turn have proven that sovereignty cannot be assumed even within the Union’s own legal space. National bilateral deals and foreign access denials are not external shocks — they are structural features of the current order.
  - Grounds: The original commitment assumed enforcement could be built *within* the EU framework. But when a member state can legally undermine supply-chain unity and a foreign power can suspend access without consequence, enforcement is not a technical gap — it is a jurisdictional deficit. The new formulation shifts from internal compliance to *external conditioning*: we will make market access to the EU contingent on full auditability and fallback integration, and we will automate replacement so that no single decision — human or machine — can block it. This is no longer about governing our own systems; it is about making the EU a zone of enforceable AI sovereignty by design, not hope.
  - Evidence quoted: a member state signs a bilateral AI infrastructure deal with a US hyperscaler, granting preferential access in exchange for data sovereignty concessions, fracturing the Union’s negotiating front.
  - Reason: The member state defection directly undermines internal enforcement unity, which this statement seeks to reframe as external market conditioning.
