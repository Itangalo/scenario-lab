This is no longer about governing our own systems; it is about making the EU a zone of enforceable AI sovereignty by design, not hope.

## New measure  
**Condition Market Access on Fallback Integration**  
Require all foreign AI providers operating in the EU to integrate their systems with the Open Science Resilience Initiative’s fallback models, enabling immediate, automated replacement upon access denial, non-compliance, or failure to meet audit standards.  

- Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)

## Priority  
**Condition Market Access on Fallback Integration** — because the directive alone cannot stop a foreign provider from withholding access or a member state from bypassing rules; only by making market access conditional on *technical surrender* — the ability to be replaced — can we enforce sovereignty in practice, not just in law.

## In practice  
This turn, we are finalising the draft of the **Mandate Auditable AI in Critical Infrastructure by Directive** under Article 114 TFEU, working through the European Commission’s Legal Service and the JURI and ITRE committees in the European Parliament to ensure it is framed as a market harmonisation measure. ENISA is being tasked with certifying auditable models, and we are embedding fallback triggers directly into procurement contracts for energy, health, and transport sectors. The directive will enter trilogue negotiations in the next turn, with the Commission offering fast-track access to the EU’s emergency procurement framework to secure member state buy-in. We are also preparing a communication campaign to reframe resistance not as sovereignty protection but as dependency entrenchment — a narrative we will push through national audit offices and public media.

Simultaneously, we are launching the **Condition Market Access on Fallback Integration** measure by drafting an implementing regulation under the Digital Markets Act (DMA) and the proposed AI Liability Directive. This will require all foreign AI providers with significant market presence in the EU to demonstrate, via third-party audit, that their systems can be automatically replaced by certified open models from the Open Science Resilience Initiative upon EU declaration of access failure or non-auditable behaviour. The technical integration will be verified by a new unit within ENISA, and non-compliance will trigger immediate suspension of market access under Article 22 DMA. We are already in closed consultations with Germany, the Netherlands, and Finland to build a blocking minority in the Council against any attempt to dilute the fallback requirement, and we are offering US hyperscalers a compliance pathway that includes joint testing and phased integration — not as a concession, but as a controlled surrender. This is not negotiation. It is terms of entry.
