## In practice  
This turn, we are shifting from institutional creation to legal imposition. The failure of EASA in Parliament has shown that co-leadership bargains and emergency procedures are not enough — we must act within the existing architecture of Union law. We are now drafting a Directive under Article 114 TFEU (internal market harmonisation), to be adopted via the ordinary legislative procedure, that will prohibit the use of non-auditable AI in energy, health, transport, emergency response, and financial systems. The directive will define "auditable" as meeting EU-recognised standards for model transparency, traceability of decision logic, and weight security — standards already met by the Open Science Resilience Initiative.

We are working with the European Parliament’s JURI and ITRE committees to frame the directive as a market integrity measure, not a security override, to avoid triggering national sovereignty objections. ENISA will be tasked with certification, and fallback deployment will be automated through pre-integrated APIs from the Open Science Resilience Initiative. To counter resistance from member states citing procurement autonomy, we are offering fast-track access to the Union’s emergency procurement framework and guaranteeing that sovereign models will be cost-competitive. The directive will also include a clause that triggers automatic suspension of foreign AI contracts upon any future access denial — turning dependency into a self-correcting legal failure mode.

We are not building a new agency. We are weaponising the acquis.
