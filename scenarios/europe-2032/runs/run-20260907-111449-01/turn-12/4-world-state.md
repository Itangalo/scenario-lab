### The Directive Gambit

With political capital at a near-total standstill, the EU abandons its stalled Sovereign Enforcement Backbone and pivots to a binding legislative directive under Article 114 TFEU. The new Mandate Auditable AI in Critical Infrastructure by Directive seeks to outlaw non-auditable systems in energy, health, transport, and finance, leveraging internal market law to bypass national vetoes. The move is framed as market harmonisation, not security override, to avoid constitutional challenges.

The Open Science Resilience Initiative, launched two turns ago, reaches a key milestone with the certification of auditable models for biosecurity and climate resilience. These are now cleared for deployment, providing a fallback for critical systems during access disruptions. The progress yields a +3 boost to resilience, reinforcing continuity planning. However, full capability integration remains partial—while the models are operational, their performance lags significantly behind frontier systems, limiting immediate uptake. As a result, openweight_capability rises only modestly to 64.5, reflecting incremental gains consistent with a two-year program in its final phase, not a sudden leap.

Yet momentum is undercut by deeper fractures. The US, operating under a CONSOLIDATION posture, quietly suspends access to the latest frontier model for EU institutions—again without explanation. Simultaneously, a member state signs a bilateral AI infrastructure deal with a US hyperscaler, granting preferential access in exchange for data sovereignty concessions, fracturing the Union’s negotiating front.

Public sentiment remains stagnant. Widespread awareness of dependency is overshadowed by frustration over elite-driven, reactive measures. Local opposition to data centres intensifies across eight member states, citing grid strain and energy costs, with coordinated legal actions delaying new sovereign compute deployments.

Ai_safety holds at 0.0. The capability jump continues—ai_capability rises by 2.5—yet no oversight mechanism keeps pace. Frontier models operate with fully opaque strategic reasoning, and no safety assurance has landed on deployed systems. The gap between power and control widens into a chasm.

### Sovereignty Erodes

Despite the directive’s ambition, eu_ai_sovereignty falls to 18.0. The access denial and member state defection each qualify as capacity events, but only the larger counts: the defection, which directly undermines supply-chain unity, is scored at −2. Capability growth of 2.5 triggers a further −1. No category 4 measure finishes, and no sovereignty gains offset the losses.

Political capital drops to 2.0. The portfolio charge remains high: Open Science Resilience Initiative −3, Joint Access Negotiation −3, priority −1 = −7. The new directive is small (−2), but the proposal bonus is absent—no recent incident directly justifies a category 3 restriction. The Union’s leverage is spent, not replenished.
