### The Frontier Surges, Control Falters

Global AI capability advances by 2.0 points to 92.0, continuing its steady climb into the terminal zone. At this level, even top-tier developers admit their frontier models operate with reasoning processes that are functionally opaque. Human oversight is now a formality in many high-stakes domains, as verification lags far behind execution speed.

### Access Cut, Response Activated

The European Union faces a sudden blackout: the leading AI model, critical to hospital diagnostics and public forecasting systems across 15 member states, is abruptly withdrawn without explanation. Over 120 hospitals and 18 ministries are affected. The Commission activates crisis protocols, fast-tracking integration of auditable open models from the Open Science Resilience Initiative as interim replacements. Though slower, these systems are already compliant with EU data governance and can be legally deployed.

The Union is invited into a new joint threat response pact, enabling real-time cyber intelligence sharing with allied states. ENISA leads a forensic analysis using the framework, confirming the outage was not due to a breach but a unilateral access revocation by the model’s provider. The technical insight is valuable, but the political reality stings: the EU has no leverage to reverse the decision.

### Sovereignty Tested, Capital Spent

In response, the EU accelerates its pivot toward auditable AI, issuing binding mandates to France, Germany, and Italy to restrict critical infrastructure AI procurement to fully interpretable systems by Q3. This marks a strategic shift—sovereignty is now defined by control, not capability. However, the political cost is steep. With two large measures still in flight and no new proposal, the ongoing portfolio drain continues. The crisis response yields no immediate capital gain, as the denial was external and no prior measure contained it.

Public sentiment remains frozen at 27.0—neither swayed by the emergency measures nor shocked by the outage, as both are seen as symptoms of a deeper dependency.
