### The Leap and the Void

A sudden leap in global AI capability—driven by an opaque but transformative advance—pushes `ai_capability` to 95.0. Developers report systems now routinely execute strategic reasoning beyond full human traceability. Oversight is no longer symbolic; it is structurally absent. The jump widens the chasm between power and control, and no safety gains accompany the surge.

### Sovereignty in Crisis

The EU responds to its second major access disruption in six months by proposing the Sovereign Enforcement Backbone for Auditable AI: a new agency with authority to mandate, verify, and replace AI in critical infrastructure. The European Council convenes under emergency provisions, and the Commission fast-tracks legislation for the European AI Sovereignty Authority (EASA). Co-leadership offers to Germany and France secure initial backing, and funding is repurposed from stalled digital infrastructure.

Yet momentum stalls in the European Parliament. Legal challenges emerge over Article 114 TFEU’s applicability to national security-adjacent systems, and smaller member states denounce the plan as a Franco-German power grab. The proposal does not pass this turn. No legal basis is secured, no agency is established.

### Political Paralysis

With `eu_political_capital` at 3.0, the Union lacks the room to force consensus. The new measure fails to start, joining a growing list of stalled initiatives. The portfolio charge remains unchanged. Public sentiment, already low, shows no reaction—many see the effort as too late, too elite, and too fragile.

Meanwhile, openweight capability creeps to 62.0, fed by older spillovers now widely redistributed. Resilience holds at 50.0, sustained by patchwork use of open models, but no systemic upgrade lands. Ai_safety remains at 0.0—no deployed system benefits from the safety breakthrough this turn, which is absorbed only by frontier labs.
