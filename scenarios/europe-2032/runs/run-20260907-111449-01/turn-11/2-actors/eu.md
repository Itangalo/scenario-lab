## In practice  
This turn, we are treating the crisis not as an anomaly but as a pattern. The Commission has convened an emergency session of the European Council under Article 122a TFEU to justify the creation of a new enforcement agency — the European AI Sovereignty Authority (EASA) — which will be empowered to mandate the use of auditable AI in energy, health, transport, and emergency response systems. This is not a research body or a coordination forum; it is a regulator with teeth. It will have the authority to audit all AI systems in critical functions, certify compliance with EU interpretability standards, and — crucially — to trigger the automatic deployment of fallback models from the Open Science Resilience Initiative if access to any system is revoked or compromised.

We are negotiating with the European Parliament’s ITRE and LIBE committees to fast-track the legal basis under Article 114 TFEU (internal market harmonisation), despite resistance from member states concerned about national procurement autonomy. To secure buy-in, we are offering Germany and France co-leadership of EASA’s technical board and guaranteed hosting of its evaluation clusters. Funding will come from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, reprioritising 1.2 billion EUR from stalled broadband projects. The agency will integrate with ENISA for cybersecurity validation and with the European Medicines Agency for health AI compliance, creating a unified enforcement chain. This is not just about building capacity — it is about making sovereignty operational, enforceable, and irreversible.
