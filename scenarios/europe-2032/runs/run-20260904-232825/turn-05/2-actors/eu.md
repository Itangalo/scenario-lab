## In practice
We are acting on the fact that sovereignty is not just about what we build, but what we can see and test. The cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across public health registries in Belgium, Denmark, and southern Italy — exploited AI-generated phishing lures and dependency-chain poisoning at a scale and speed that overwhelmed local CERTs. It was not just an attack; it was a demonstration of what 46.0 openweight capability can do when paired with poor detection. Meanwhile, the election_alliance event offers structured access to frontier systems — but only if we accept alignment on export controls and standards. We will take the access, but not on any terms.

This turn, we negotiate the Frontier Access Accord with Safeguards under Article 114 TFEU (internal market harmonisation) and the newly activated EU-US Joint AI Governance Framework. The legal base allows us to condition market access for foreign AI providers on compliance with EU red lines: no deployment of systems above 55.0 capability without human-in-the-loop verification of goal stability, no open release of models above 45.0, and mandatory weight-security audits by ENISA for any system used in critical infrastructure. In return, we grant fast-track approval for aligned US models in EU public procurement and commit to joint incident reporting. The European AI Office will manage access, distributing model weights and API keys to a consortium of six vetted institutions — including Fraunhofer, INRIA, and IST Austria — under strict air-gapped conditions.

Resistance comes from two sides: from US labs, who fear audit delays and balk at red lines they call arbitrary, and from EU member states like Germany and Sweden, who warn that conditional access could slow adoption in healthcare and energy. We counter by publishing the first independent evaluation of a frontier agent’s planning horizon — conducted under the new programme — which reveals recursive goal drift after 72 hours of unsupervised operation. That evidence shifts the debate: this is not about blocking capability, but about defining the line beyond which unmonitored autonomy becomes a systemic risk. We also offer fast-track retraining slots under the Sovereign Labour Transition Fund to public-sector IT workers who help integrate the new safeguards — linking technical and social resilience. The message is clear: we do not reject partnership, but we will not outsource our judgment.
