## New measure  
**Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Programme**  
A targeted, time-bound investment to retrofit cyber detection, response protocols, and autonomous failover systems across energy, water, and transport networks using sovereign AI tools.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 5`  
`Targeted effect:` +10 to +15 resilience by turn 5; begins to close the gap exposed by the recent intrusion; reduces systemic fragility that undermines both public trust and sovereign action  
`Applies to:` EU member states’ critical infrastructure operators, with mandatory compliance for entities in sectors designated under the revised NIS2 Directive

## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because without binding control over compute deployed on our soil, all other measures depend on permission from others, and the Kimi K3 release proves that openweight capability is already weaponising at scale beyond governance reach.

## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that compute is power, and that power cannot be outsourced if sovereignty is to mean anything. This turn, the Commission advances the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive through trilogue negotiations, using Article 114 TFEU to frame the 30% capacity reservation as a necessary internal market condition. The text will be amended to include a sunset clause tied to the EU achieving 60.0 in eu_ai_sovereignty, easing concerns about permanence, while the liability shield for compliant operators is expanded to cover regulatory transition costs. We are engaging the Competitiveness Council directly, offering fast-track access to the InvestAI Gigafactories fund for any hyperscaler that pre-certifies under the directive — a bridge between compliance and investment. The EDIC will publish its draft allocation protocol this quarter, showing that the reserved capacity will be auctioned transparently, not allocated politically.

At the same time, we are responding to the automated decision scandal by pausing all new AI adoption in public services pending a review by the AI Office and the European Data Protection Board. This is not a retreat from diffusion, but a recalibration: public trust is a non-renewable resource when lost, and the scandal confirms that adoption without control is self-defeating. The new Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Programme will instead focus sovereign AI tools on defending systems, not administering them — using EU-controlled models to monitor for anomalous behaviour, simulate attack vectors, and automate containment. Implementation will run through the Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) and the newly empowered Critical Infrastructure Resilience Board, with co-funding required from member states to ensure ownership. The programme will draw compute from the sovereign pool once the Directive passes, closing the loop between capacity and resilience.

We are not slowing our sovereignty push. But we are no longer pretending that compute alone is enough. The world is showing us that capability without control, and infrastructure without defence, leaves us exposed on both fronts of our mandate. We are building autonomy — but we are also learning how to survive it.
