## New measure  
**Sovereign Labour Transition Fund**  
A binding, EU-level mechanism to guarantee income support, retraining, and job placement for workers displaced by AI-driven automation in public and private sectors, with co-financing from automating firms and sovereign AI dividends.

## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that the crisis has shifted terrain: it is no longer only in the networks, but in the streets. The emergent_labour_disruption_wave — a rolling wave of strikes across logistics hubs in Hamburg, rail operations in Lyon, and public service centres in Athens — is not a reaction to speculation. It is a response to real job losses, accelerated by AI agents now running scheduling, diagnostics, and customer routing without human oversight. Workers are not rejecting technology; they are demanding that it does not erase their place in the economy. The pause on public-sector AI adoption bought breathing room, but it did not address the underlying contract. That contract is now being renegotiated — in real time, in the streets, and we are late to the table.

This turn, we break from the assumption that industrial capacity and crisis response alone will carry legitimacy. We launch the Sovereign Labour Transition Fund to pair our technological sovereignty with social sovereignty. The Fund will require any firm benefiting from EU-subsidised compute, access to sovereign models, or participation in the Tech sovereignty package to contribute 7% of automation-related productivity gains — measured in reduced headcount over 12 months — into a pooled mechanism managed by the European Labour Authority in coordination with national agencies. This pool will finance wage insurance for displaced workers (capped at 75% of prior income for up to 18 months), fast-tracked retraining in AI-augmented roles, and mobility support. Crucially, activation will be automatic when sectoral displacement exceeds thresholds verified by Eurostat and the ETUC. The legal base will be Article 122 TFEU (economic and social cohesion), with co-decision in ECOFIN and EMPL Council formations. Resistance is expected from competitiveness hawks in northern member states, who will see this as a tax on efficiency — we will counter by tying disbursement to national re-employment rates and offering matching grants for regional upskilling clusters. The Taiwan blockade’s semiconductor pressure makes this harder, not easier: we cannot afford to lose time to strikes when every wafer counts. But we cannot build autonomy on broken trust. This Fund is not a concession. It is the operating system for a sovereign transition.
