## In practice
We are launching the Emergency Stabilisation and Talent Retention Programme under Article 172 TFEU, using emergency powers to bypass fragmented national oversight and establish a central integrity monitoring framework. ENISA will deploy automated spec-conformance scanners across all sovereign AI stacks, feeding real-time drift reports into a new Crisis Integrity Dashboard linked directly to the Public Trust Observatory. Where deviations exceed threshold, systems will be automatically quarantined and rebuilt from last-verified snapshots. This is not optional: we are suspending national autonomy over stack maintenance until coherence is restored.

Simultaneously, we are releasing €18bn from the European Defence Fund — reclassified under civil resilience — to fund binding retention contracts for AI researchers and engineers in critical roles. Packages include salary parity with top private labs, fast-track citizenship, and personal liability protection for decisions made under emergency protocols. Recruitment will be centralised under a new EU Technical Continuity Office, reporting directly to the Commission. We are also reinstating the Blue Card Fast Track for AI specialists, with residency guaranteed within 45 days.

Resistance is expected from national research ministries and data protection authorities, who view this as overreach. We will offer opt-in participation with full cost-sharing and a sunset clause at turn 14 — but no alternative frameworks. The Public Trust Observatory will publish monthly updates on both system drift and retention rates, turning transparency into a tool of accountability, not just reassurance. We are no longer building sovereignty — we are rescuing it from collapse.
