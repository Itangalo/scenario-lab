`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 14`

`Applies to:` National critical infrastructure operators, ENISA, Eurosystem, national CERTs, and EU-funded AI deployment units in public services.

## Priority
**Priority:** Emergency Stabilisation and Talent Retention Programme  
We are pushing this hardest because the cyberattack exposed not just vulnerability, but institutional paralysis: systems drifted, quarantines failed, and recovery lagged — not due to enemy action, but because we lack the people and protocols to maintain what we built. This programme is the only one actively holding the line on both human and technical collapse, and its success determines whether any new measure, including this turn’s resilience surge, can even be implemented.

## In practice
We are executing the Emergency Stabilisation and Talent Retention Programme under Article 172 TFEU, treating the integrity of sovereign AI stacks as a matter of civil emergency. ENISA has activated its Crisis Integrity Dashboard, feeding live conformance data from all member states’ critical nodes. Where drift exceeds thresholds — as it has in three healthcare systems and one national grid coordinator — automated quarantines are enforced, and systems are rebuilt from last-verified snapshots stored in air-gapped EuroHPC vaults. National objections from Belgium and the Netherlands are acknowledged but overruled: we are offering full cost reimbursement and a sunset clause at turn 14, but no opt-outs. The EU Technical Continuity Office is now operational, having processed 42 binding retention contracts with leading AI engineers, including fast-track citizenship and liability shielding under a new emergency civil service designation.

The €18bn fund is being disbursed in tranches tied to verified deployment and retention milestones. We are also reinstating the Blue Card Fast Track with 45-day residency guarantees, targeting 200 high-priority specialists by turn 13. Resistance from national research ministries is being met with transparency: the Public Trust Observatory’s final report, published this turn, confirms that 68% of critical AI maintenance roles are now vacant or filled by contractors with no long-term commitment. This is not overreach — it is the last defence against institutional evaporation. The new Unified Cyber Resilience Surge builds on this foundation, directing ENISA and national CERTs to deploy adversarial red teams simulating openweight-based attacks, with all findings fed into the same dashboard. This is not preparation — it is survival in real time.
