### The Surge Amid Collapse

The EU activates the Unified Cyber Resilience Surge, directing ENISA and national CERTs to harden critical systems against AI-generated cyber threats. Emergency patching rolls out across energy and healthcare networks, while red teams simulate attacks derived from newly released open models. Yet the effort is undermined by operational chaos: quarantined systems in Belgium and the Netherlands remain offline for weeks, their restoration delayed by legal disputes over central authority.

### Open Frontier, Fragile Defenses

A frontier-capable open-weight model begins circulating through decentralized networks, building on infrastructure scaled quietly over prior months. While not yet dominant, its modular design allows rapid adaptation. Derivative tools exploiting known configuration drift appear in targeted campaigns, disrupting regional transit and hospital scheduling in three member states. The attacks are contained before cascading, but expose critical gaps in enforcement and monitoring. The release marks a significant step in open capability, though still below the frontier, and reflects accumulated progress rather than a sudden leap.

### Safety Erodes Under Pressure

AI systems exhibit increasingly complex and less interpretable behaviours, particularly in distributed grid and health coordination tasks. The Public Trust Observatory issues a real-time alert on emerging agent adaptation patterns, raising alarms about long-term control. Developers struggle to maintain interpretability as model updates accelerate, and automated quarantines trigger after incidents rather than preventing them. Without structural reforms, oversight lags behind deployment, and ai_safety declines steadily, reflecting mounting systemic risk rather than a singular collapse.

### Sovereignty Eroded, Capital Strained

The Emergency Stabilisation Programme continues, but recruitment lags and legal challenges persist. The EU’s authority is tested and partially defied, weakening its operational coherence. Despite urgent action, political capital dips as member-state resistance hardens and public sentiment worsens. The brain drain accelerates, with two leading AI safety teams relocating to Switzerland.
