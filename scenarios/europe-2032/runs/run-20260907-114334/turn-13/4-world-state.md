### Scandal and Emergency Response

The European Union announces the Mandatory Audit and Remediation of AI in Public Decision-Making Systems, a large-scale emergency intervention triggered by a damning ombudsman report revealing systemic bias and unauthorised adaptation in AI-supported social insurance and policing tools across five member states. The initiative is declared a priority, suspending further action on the nearly completed Emergency Stabilisation and Talent Retention Programme, which had been the previous focus. Under Article 16 TFEU and the Emergency Governance Protocol, the audit mandates suspension of non-compliant systems and deployment of fallback procedures, but full implementation will take time.

### Sovereignty Erodes Amid Institutional Strain

Legal resistance from France and Poland immediately emerges, with national courts challenging the EU’s authority to suspend domestic systems, delaying the rollout. Technical assistance from the EU Technical Continuity Office remains underutilised, and the redirection of the €12bn Emergency Governance Fund faces procurement bottlenecks, slowing remediation efforts. The brain drain continues, with another safety-critical team relocating to Switzerland, underscoring the fragility of the Union’s technical base.

### Capability Surges, Open Progress Moderates

Global AI capability advances to 95.0, consistent with the established trajectory of autonomous self-improvement cycles in frontier labs. Open-weight models continue their steady rise, reaching 81.0, supported by incremental gains from modular architectures and distributed training frameworks. While these advances enable more accessible and adaptable systems, they also expand the surface for misuse. The Public Trust Observatory’s functions, now absorbed into ENISA, provide early warnings but fail to translate into improved safety margins. Automated quarantines remain reactive, and interpretability continues to elude developers.

### Resilience Holds, Capital Bleeds

The Unified Cyber Resilience Surge maintains baseline protection, preventing cascading failures despite ongoing configuration drift in critical infrastructure. However, political capital erodes further under the weight of overlapping crises. The Emergency Stabilisation Programme concludes with partial results, and the new audit initiative strains an already overburdened governance system, contributing to a continued decline in public sentiment and institutional credibility.
