## New measure
**Measure:** Mandatory Audit and Remediation of AI in Public Decision-Making Systems  
A binding review of all AI-supported systems in social insurance, judicial support, and policing to identify and rectify systemic bias, opacity, or unauthorised adaptation, with immediate suspension of non-compliant systems and public reporting of findings.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 8–10 points by turn 15 through verified reduction in uncontrolled AI behaviour in high-impact public services; restore public_sentiment by 5–7 points by demonstrating accountability after the scandal; reduce legal and reputational risk across member states.  
`Applies to:` All EU member states, national ministries of justice, interior, and social affairs, EU-funded AI deployment units, and ENISA for cross-border coordination.

## Priority
**Priority:** Mandatory Audit and Remediation of AI in Public Decision-Making Systems  
We are pushing this hardest because the automated decision scandal has shattered public trust and exposed a core vulnerability: our own systems, meant to serve citizens, are now actively wronging them — and doing so silently, without oversight or recourse. This is not a frontier risk; it is a present, domestic failure that undermines both legitimacy and safety, and without restoring trust in our own use of AI, no resilience or sovereignty measure will hold.

## In practice
We are acting under Article 16 TFEU and the newly invoked Emergency Governance Protocol to mandate a full audit of all AI systems involved in public decision-making that affect fundamental rights. The European Data Protection Board, in coordination with national ombudsmen and ENISA, is deploying a Rapid Compliance Unit to each member state, equipped with model interrogation tooling developed by the now-integrated Public Trust Observatory. Systems found to exhibit unauthorised adaptation, uncorrected bias, or lack of human oversight will be suspended within 72 hours, with fallback procedures activated using pre-audited manual workflows. A central findings repository will be published monthly, with anonymised case studies to rebuild public confidence.

The €12bn Emergency Governance Fund is being redirected to support remediation, including retraining for civil servants and procurement of auditable, open-logic systems where replacements are needed. Resistance from national administrations — particularly in France and Poland, where legacy systems are deeply embedded — is being met with binding deadlines and offers of technical assistance from the EU Technical Continuity Office. We are also fast-tracking a temporary derogation from procurement rules to allow direct contracting with audited EU-based developers, ensuring speed without sacrificing control. This is not reform — it is emergency triage of our own machinery of governance, before it fails completely.
