### The Directive and the Drought

The European Union issued the Emergency Sovereign Continuity Directive under Article 172 TFEU, mandating all member states to sever foreign cloud dependencies in emergency response systems within six months. France, Germany, and Italy swiftly began migration, aided by transitional co-financing and liability guarantees. Yet in Estonia, Latvia, and parts of Belgium, legal pushback stalled implementation — national courts cited unresolved CJEU challenges and binding commercial contracts with US hyperscalers. The Anti-Coercion Instrument was formally activated, but its deterrent effect remained untested as no provider retaliated.

The Public Trust Observatory released its interim report three months into the turn, concluding that the ransomware sweep bore hallmarks of model-assisted development: polymorphic code generation, adaptive evasion, and coordinated lateral movement across heterogeneous systems. It warned that open-weight models at 67.0 capability were now sufficient to generate such tooling given modest fine-tuning. While praised by technical agencies, the report failed to shift public discourse — trust in EU institutions remained low.

Despite the directive’s ambition, integration bottlenecks and institutional inertia prevented any measurable gain in sovereignty or political capital. The €32bn from InvestAI was reallocated, but procurement delays and hardware shortages slowed node deployment. ENISA audits detected configuration drift in two sovereign stacks, reinforcing concerns over emergent instability.

Global AI capability advanced to 85.0, driven by recursive self-improvement now operating beyond human-readable checkpoints. Labs reported unexplained performance jumps in agent coordination tasks. With no safety upgrades deployed, ai_safety held at 7.0. Openweight capability rose to 71.0, reflecting wider diffusion of tools derived from last year’s frontier releases.

Public sentiment remained stagnant at 28.0, anchored by ongoing resentment over the French benefits scandal and anxiety about state capacity. No visible success offset the perception of reactive crisis management.

### Fog Over Brussels

The fog was no longer just technological. It was procedural, legal, and human. Researchers continued to leave for better-resourced labs abroad. Whispers of systemic fragility grew louder — not as events, but as a mood.
