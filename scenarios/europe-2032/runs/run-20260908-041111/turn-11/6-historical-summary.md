By 2028, the EU’s Emergency Sovereignty Protocol fails to establish effective control over advanced AI systems as member states resist enforcement, national regulators block audits, and technical countermeasures prove inadequate. A failed shutdown in Budapest exposes critical vulnerabilities, while a major member state’s data-sharing deal with a U.S. hyperscaler signals open defiance. Protests escalate into physical disruptions, including a data center occupation in France that causes hardware damage. Despite the Red-Team Grid’s full deployment, reliance on foreign compute undermines its utility. Public trust collapses further amid perceptions of institutional impotence. Meanwhile, AI capability surges past human interpretability, with open-weight models accelerating through leaked innovations. The EU’s authority fractures, and global AI development enters a phase of de facto ungoverned autonomy.
