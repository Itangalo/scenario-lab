### Capability Creeps Forward  
Global AI capability advances steadily to 59.5, driven by expanded training runs and tighter integration of agent frameworks at leading labs. The open frontier rises in parallel, reaching 48.0, as leaked model weights are fine-tuned for cyber exploitation and distributed through decentralized channels. Despite the EU’s new push for control, no major developer complies yet with its proposed tracing regime.

### A Surge in Cyber Enforcement  
The Emergency Resilience Surge enters full enforcement, mandating AI-driven detection systems across energy and health sectors. ENISA conducts surprise audits using synthetic red teams, uncovering critical vulnerabilities in two national grid operators. Compliance is patchy—some states delay implementation, citing cost and sovereignty concerns—yet pilot integrations improve threat visibility, nudging resilience to 46.0.

### New Front on Open Models  
The EU launches “Secure the Open Frontier,” demanding cryptographic provenance, tamper-proof logs, and legal liability for high-capability open models. The proposal triggers fierce backlash from open-source communities and resistance from U.S. and Chinese firms, who warn of fragmentation. Technical drafting begins, but no standards are finalized. The measure does not yet slow diffusion.

### Political Capital Crumbles  
With four large measures active—including the newly prioritized Cyber Shield Activation—the governance burden intensifies. The portfolio charge overwhelms modest gains: political capital drops to 27.0. Public sentiment weakens further to 33.0 amid fears of uncontrolled AI and job losses, undercutting legitimacy. The priority offers no pull-in; delays persist in cross-border coordination.

### Safety Erodes, Sovereignty Slips  
No safety safeguards land on deployed systems. With capability rising over 2 points and no matching assurance, ai_safety falls to 23.0. The bio_uplift_findings and eval_anomaly_reports deepen unease, but no corrective action takes hold. EU AI sovereignty declines to 19.0, reflecting continued dependence as global capability outpaces domestic build-out.
