## In practice  
This turn, we act on the irreversible shift confirmed by the cyber_major_incident: offensive AI capability has escaped containment. With openweight_capability at 46.5, the tools to disrupt essential services are no longer confined to state actors or frontier labs—they are downloadable. The Emergency Resilience Surge moves fully into enforcement mode: we issue delegated acts under Article 122 TFEU to activate binding cyber hygiene mandates, requiring all critical operators in energy, health, water and transport to deploy AI-driven anomaly detection and conduct quarterly red-teaming by Q1 2028. ENISA begins unannounced cross-border audits using synthetic attack agents trained on known exploit patterns, focusing first on grid management systems and hospital networks exposed in the breach. To overcome resistance, we link early compliance to preferential access to InvestAI Gigafactories co-investment funds and fast-track permitting under the Tech Sovereignty Package—conditionality as leverage.

At the same time, we confront the fact that resilience alone cannot close the gap. The new measure, Secure the Open Frontier, targets the root of the problem: uncontrolled diffusion. We propose amending the AI Act’s conformity assessment protocol to require, for any released model above 45.0 capability, (1) cryptographic provenance tags embedded in weights, (2) immutable logs of training data sources and fine-tuning runs, and (3) legal liability anchored in the developer’s jurisdiction of registration. Market access to the EU will depend on compliance. This applies equally to foreign and domestic developers. We begin consultations with DG GROW, CEN-CENELEC, and the European Cyber Resilience Act enforcement unit to draft technical standards, while preparing a Council Regulation proposal for January 2028 adoption. The US and Chinese labs are already pushing back through trade channels, but the bio_uplift_findings and eval_anomaly_reports have shifted opinion within COREPER: something must be done before the next jump.

We are stretched thin—four large measures active, political capital at 37.0—but we cannot afford to wait. The two-year commitment demands autonomous capacity, but autonomy means nothing if every actor with a GPU can wield power the Union cannot govern. We are building sovereign resilience not just in compute, but in control.
