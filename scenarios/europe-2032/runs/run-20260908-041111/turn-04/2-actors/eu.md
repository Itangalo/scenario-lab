## In practice

This turn, we act on the shock of the capability_jump — a leap of +6.5 in ai_capability to 59.5, driven by a new agent architecture that sustains goal-directed behaviour across weeks of simulated time. Intelligence leakage from multiple labs confirms that the model was trained using synthetic data pipelines that recursively improved its own reasoning — a self-bootstrapping loop now accessible in principle to any actor with sufficient compute. Worse, early variants of the core architecture have already appeared in underground forums, fine-tuned for phishing, vulnerability discovery, and disinformation. openweight_capability has jumped to 48.0 not through innovation, but through rapid redistribution. The frontier is no longer led — it is loose.

We cannot harden fast enough if the threat keeps accelerating outside our borders. The Emergency Resilience Surge and Cyber Shield Activation have now finished — they delivered binding standards and a cross-border red-teaming capability, and we are beginning to see improved detection in grid and health systems. But resilience is not enough when the attack surface grows faster than we can patch it. We are now in a race not just to defend, but to *stop* the spread of dangerous capabilities.

Thus, we invoke Article 7 of the AI Act to impose an emergency restriction: no model above 45.0 may be released into the public domain without a pre-release audit by a designated EU body, proof of weight containment, and a verified plan for misuse monitoring. We task CEN-CENELEC with drafting the technical standards by end of Q1, and we instruct the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre to begin building audit capacity using red-team agents trained to probe for autonomous replication risks. Market access to the EU will be suspended for non-compliant releases, and hosting platforms will be required to block distribution under the DSA. We coordinate with DG TRADE to prepare for retaliation, but we also reach out to like-minded democracies — Canada, Japan, South Korea — through the Global Partnership on AI, proposing mutual recognition of audit standards as a counterweight to U.S. and Chinese resistance.

We are down to 27.0 political capital — barely enough to sustain four large measures, let alone launch a fifth. So we let the Emergency Resilience Surge and Cyber Shield Activation conclude formally this turn. Their frameworks are now embedded in ENISA’s mandate and national cyber agencies; they will continue operationally without central oversight. We redirect saved capital to the new ban enforcement effort. The Tech Sovereignty Package and InvestAI Gigafactories remain active — we cannot abandon the long game — but we slow their permitting acceleration by deferring two regional fast-tracks pending audit of local grid resilience. This is not retreat. It is focus: we cannot be sovereign if we do not first survive.
