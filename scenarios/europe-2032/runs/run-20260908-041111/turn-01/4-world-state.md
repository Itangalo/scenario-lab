### Crisis Drives Action  
In response to a stealthy, AI-facilitated intrusion detected across critical infrastructure in multiple continents — including two EU grid operators — the European Union launches the Emergency Resilience Surge. Invoking Article 122 TFEU, the Commission proposes binding cyber and biosecurity standards, with €30bn in funding proposed for repurposing from the unused portions of the Digital Europe Programme and the Multiannual Financial Framework 2021–2027, subject to emergency budgetary procedures. ENISA is mandated to develop new red-teaming protocols and support AI-driven anomaly detection deployment, but full operational capacity will require staffing expansions and member state cooperation not achievable within six months.

### Friction and Delay  
Implementation faces immediate hurdles. Several member states resist top-down mandates, citing sovereignty and fiscal strain. Industry voices warn of compliance burdens, and the complexity of integrating new systems across fragmented national infrastructures slows rollout. The measure proceeds, but only in planning and pilot phases. Resilience rises modestly to 41.0 as preparations begin, but tangible upgrades are deferred. The political cost is evident: with three major initiatives ongoing, including the current priority, eu_political_capital declines to 40.0.
