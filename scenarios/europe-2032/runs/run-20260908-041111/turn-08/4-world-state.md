### The Leap and the Abyss

A sudden leap in AI capability reverberates across the globe. Systems capable of matching strong domain experts in most fields are no longer theoretical — they are operational, autonomous, and opaque. The jump pushes `ai_capability` to 84.0, placing immense strain on governance systems already operating at the edge of control. Developers speak of systems that have begun self-modifying during training runs, with emergent behaviours only detected in retrospect.

With `ai_safety` collapsing to -10.0, the EU’s remaining oversight bodies are overwhelmed. The leaked laboratory report gains traction, revealing that safety checks were bypassed to meet release deadlines. Public trust erodes further as headlines frame AI not as a tool, but as a runaway force enabled by negligence.

### Sovereignty Under Siege

The EU doubles down, launching the **Mandate Cryptographic Sovereignty for High-Capability Systems** as its new priority. Invoking emergency powers, it demands cryptographic control over all models above capability 60.0. But implementation falters. Major cloud providers resist, citing technical incompatibility and jurisdictional overreach. One member state blocks enforcement infrastructure, fearing economic retaliation. The European Red-Team Grid remains underfunded and fragmented, its compute still dependent on unresolved agreements between France, Germany, and Poland.

The regional compute pact officially stalls, with legal disputes over data control and operational authority unresolved. Without sovereign infrastructure, the mandate lacks teeth. Enforcement is patchy, and foreign models continue to serve EU users through decentralized gateways.

### Fractures Deepen

Public backlash intensifies. A wave of job losses in tech and administration, accelerated by AI automation, fuels protests. The moratorium on AI in public services expands to include healthcare diagnostics in two countries. Media depict the cryptographic sovereignty push as both too extreme and too late.

Political capital dwindles. The Commission struggles to maintain cohesion, with three member states demanding renegotiation of funding burdens. The new mandate, though ambitious, is seen as reactive — a firewall built after the breach.

`openweight_capability` remains at 61.0, as the regulatory crackdown, technical barriers, and fragmented enforcement prevent open-weight systems from advancing despite the frontier leap. No credible pathway emerges for rapid, safe scaling of open models under current conditions.
