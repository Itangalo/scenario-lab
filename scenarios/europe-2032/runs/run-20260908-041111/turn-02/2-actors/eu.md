`Emergency Resilience Surge` — because the cyber_major_incident proves that detection failed not once but systematically, and that openweight_capability at 44.0 means offensive AI tools are now in reach of non-state actors; we are no longer preparing for a future threat but responding to an active one, and this measure is the only coordinated framework we have to force compliance across fragmented jurisdictions.

## In practice
This turn, we act on the fact that the cyber_major_incident was not an outlier but a signal: AI-generated attack vectors have outpaced human-driven defence. The intrusion bypassed legacy detection systems, exploited zero-day logic in widely used grid management software, and persisted for weeks before discovery. We now treat AI-enabled systemic risk as a live operational reality. The Emergency Resilience Surge moves from drafting to enforcement: we invoke Article 122 TFEU to fast-track a Council Regulation mandating minimum cyber hygiene baselines — including AI-driven anomaly detection, air-gapped backups, and quarterly red-teaming — for all critical operators. Funding of €30bn is repurposed from unused MFF and Digital Europe Programme allocations, with 70% co-financing offered to member states that comply within 12 months.

Resistance remains high in several capitals, particularly from states citing fiscal strain and sovereignty concerns. We counter with a dual track: fast-track access to InvestAI Gigafactories funding for early adopters, and a mutual recognition mechanism that allows compliant states to share security certifications across borders, reducing redundancy. ENISA expands its mandate to conduct unannounced red-team exercises, using AI-generated attack simulations to stress-test systems in real time. We also activate a joint procurement framework for AI-driven detection tools, pooling demand across 15 member states to cut costs and ensure interoperability. Biosecurity components proceed more slowly: Horizon Europe repurposes €1.2bn for AI-powered genomic screening at EU DNA synthesis hubs, but rollout is limited to six pilot sites due to legal fragmentation.

Meanwhile, the gap between ai_capability and ai_safety widens, now at 54.5 to 29.0. We cannot yet close it, but we can narrow the window of uncontrolled damage. The new measure, Cyber Shield Activation, institutionalises red-teaming as a standing function — not a one-off test. It builds directly on the Emergency Resilience Surge, using the same legal base and funding stream, but escalates from planning to enforcement. This is not sovereignty through compute alone, but sovereignty through operational resilience: the capacity to endure, detect, and respond when the frontier is no longer contained.
