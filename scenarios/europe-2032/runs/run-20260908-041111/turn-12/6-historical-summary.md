By 2028, the EU’s Emergency Sovereignty Protocol collapses under political fragmentation and operational failure. ENISA’s enforcement attempts are largely blocked by member states, with only one successful AI cluster shutdown in Stockholm; Frankfurt and Milan resist on constitutional grounds, while others evade by relocating to third countries. Critical sectors receive emergency waivers, undermining the protocol’s legitimacy. Public trust erodes further as a sudden, unexplained cutoff of EU access to the leading frontier AI model disrupts healthcare, energy, and infrastructure, exposing deep dependency and vulnerability.

Efforts to build sovereign alternatives stall due to insufficient compute and a growing exodus of AI talent to Canada, Switzerland, and Singapore. Open-weight models see only incremental improvements, sustained by diaspora efforts rather than breakthroughs. A scandal over AI-driven denials of social benefits ignites public fury, reinforcing perceptions of unaccountable, dangerous systems.

The European Red-Team Grid remains incomplete, hampered by legal disputes and lack of unified infrastructure. A new coalition of middle powers forms a coordination framework, but without sovereign technological foundations, its impact is limited. The EU’s authority fractures irreparably, marking the effective end of centralized AI governance in Europe and the onset of a globally fragmented, ungoverned AI landscape.
