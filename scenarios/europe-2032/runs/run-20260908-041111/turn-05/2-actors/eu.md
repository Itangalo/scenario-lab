## In practice
This turn, we close the emergency ban on open-weight releases. The measure lands formally under Article 7 of the AI Act, enforced through notified bodies under the Digital Services Act, but its real test comes in implementation. We have already seen non-compliance from two U.S. developers who argue the audit regime exceeds EU jurisdiction; we respond by suspending their access to EU markets and initiating infringement proceedings, backed by the European Commission’s Legal Service. Simultaneously, we task CEN-CENELEC with finalizing technical standards for containment verification by Q1 2029, and we direct the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre to begin training red-team agents to probe for replication risks in submitted models.

Yet the cyber_major_incident — a ransomware sweep across regional health systems, powered by model-generated zero-days — exposes the limits of restriction alone. The attack exploited a dependency chain poisoned by an AI-fine-tuned compiler backdoor, now traced to a single compromised CI/CD pipeline. The breach was not stopped by audits or bans, but by a national red team in Estonia that flagged anomalous build activity — a capability we embedded through the now-finished Cyber Shield Activation. This reinforces our conviction: resilience is not optional, but we cannot scale it fast enough without compute sovereignty.

Meanwhile, the election_alliance shifts the strategic ground. The U.S. offers structured access to frontier models for vetted EU institutions, contingent on alignment in export controls and standards. We accept the offer for emergency response and critical infrastructure evaluation, but we condition full participation on the EU’s own audit framework being recognized as equivalent. This is not just about fairness — it is about preserving the authority of our own rules. We cannot let access become a substitute for capacity. That is why, even as we welcome the relief, we double down on InvestAI Gigafactories: we instruct DG ENERGY and EIB to fast-track grid connections for two of the four sites, using emergency infrastructure provisions, and we tie access to U.S. models to performance benchmarks in domestic build-out. The message is clear: partnership must not erode urgency. We survive the open frontier not by closing our eyes to it, but by building through it.
