### The Fog of Control

The European Union formally closes the Tech sovereignty package, celebrating legal victories in 14 member states for accelerated permitting. Yet the promised €200bn in private investment fails to materialize—only €82bn committed—exposing a gap between legislative ambition and industrial reality. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain the cornerstone of EU strategy, with emergency grid access secured for Finland and Andalusia. Still, transformer shortages and rail delays stall progress, eroding confidence in the Union’s ability to deliver.

Amid collapsing oversight, the EU announces the European Red-Team Grid, a new initiative to establish five regional hubs for simulating AI-driven supply chain attacks in Tallinn, Vienna, Marseille, Warsaw, and Dublin. These centers will leverage sovereign compute and open-weight models to preempt threats, with participation invited from Japan, South Korea, and Canada through the newly formed middle-power coalition. However, implementation is only beginning: site preparations are underway, but compute allocation remains contested, and full operational capacity is not expected before the next turn. The initiative faces delays due to cross-border coordination challenges and limited staffing, reflecting the broader strain on EU execution capacity.

Public trust plummets further after a federal ombudsman confirms systemic bias in Bavaria’s AI-driven social insurance system, denying thousands of claims. The Commission imposes a temporary moratorium on AI use in social benefits, policing, and judicial decisions, citing unverifiable reasoning and safety concerns. The move, while politically necessary, reinforces public perception of AI as a threat rather than a tool.

Meanwhile, the global AI frontier advances relentlessly. With the leading models no longer producing human-readable reasoning, all existing interpretability frameworks degrade significantly. The safety regime, already fragile, weakens further—no deployed system can now be meaningfully audited using legacy methods. Offensive AI capabilities, now indistinguishable from defensive ones in open-weight releases, spread unchecked. However, some foundational monitoring practices and red-teaming protocols persist in limited form, preventing a total collapse of safety awareness.

The middle-power coalition strengthens supply-chain leverage, offering a rare coordination win for the EU. Yet the talent drain accelerates, as top AI researchers leave for the U.S. and China, citing isolation and stagnation. The Union’s sovereignty project hangs on a narrow thread: compute under its control, and nothing more.
