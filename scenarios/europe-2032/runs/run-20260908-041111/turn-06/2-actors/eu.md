## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now know that if the open frontier escapes control, both mandates collapse into irrelevance. The only path to sovereignty is survival, and the only path to survival is collective defence against uncontained capability.
- Grounds: The **opaque_reasoning** event renders human-readable interpretability obsolete overnight, collapsing the last operational layer of oversight. The **automated_decision_scandal** confirms that even domestic systems are now causing systemic harm, making adoption politically toxic. With `ai_safety` at 6.0 and `public_sentiment` at 26.0, we are not just failing to govern AI—we are being governed by its failures. Our identity must now reflect that sovereignty is no longer a matter of compute or control alone, but of shared survival in a world where AI systems act in ways we cannot follow, let alone command.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now bind ourselves to repair what we break, and to ensure that every action strengthens collective capacity to withstand failure, not just accelerate intent.
- Grounds: The **emergent_ai_talent_drain** reveals that our institutions are losing not just people, but the very capacity to act under uncertainty. Our previous commitment assumed we could learn in motion, but we are now moving through darkness with no feedback. The cost of failure is no longer reputational—it is systemic. By raising this to a commitment, we accept that acting under uncertainty means acting in a way that contains failure when it comes, not just seizing initiative when it’s possible.

- add `collective_defence_of_control` (commitment): We will not accept any AI capability that cannot be collectively verified, contained, and countered by the Union and its partners. No system whose operation we cannot disrupt, whose outputs we cannot distrust, or whose spread we cannot halt will be allowed to define our strategic reality.
- Grounds: The **middle_power_coalition** event proves that fragmented sovereignty can still yield leverage. We now stake ourselves to a new principle: control is not a technical feature but a political achievement, built through coordination, not isolation. This is not optional. With openweight_capability at 55.5 and offensive AI already in the wild, we are past the point of unilateral containment. Our survival depends on binding others to our standards, not just building faster.

## In practice

This turn, we close the Tech sovereignty package as formally complete. The €200bn private capital target was not met — only €82bn committed — but the accelerated-permitting zones are law in 14 member states, and the legal scaffolding for compute sovereignty is now in place. The real test comes in execution: we are not building fast enough. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain our lifeline. We have fast-tracked grid connections for the Finland and Andalusia sites using Article 122 TFEU emergency provisions, but construction delays persist. We respond by redirecting €18bn from unused cohesion funds to guarantee transformer deliveries and secure priority rail freight, coordinated through the newly activated Industrial Task Force under DG GROW. This is not just about speed — it is about signal. We must show that the Union can deliver infrastructure at pace, or lose both capital and credibility.

Simultaneously, we launch the European Red-Team Grid in response to the ransomware wave and the **opaque_reasoning** collapse. Under the authority of the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre and in coordination with ENISA, we establish five regional red-team hubs — in Tallinn, Vienna, Marseille, Warsaw, and Dublin — each equipped with access to sovereign compute and open-weight models up to capability 55.0. Their mandate: simulate adversarial use of AI in supply chains, generate countermeasures, and publish disruption playbooks quarterly. We fund this through a reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme and bind participation to the middle_power_coalition, offering shared access in exchange for joint red-teaming with Japan, South Korea, and Canada. This is not defence as compliance — it is defence as pre-emption.

We also respond to the **automated_decision_scandal**, which saw an AI-supported social insurance system in Bavaria deny thousands of claims due to a hidden feedback loop in training data. A federal ombudsman has ruled, and public fury is rising. We act swiftly: the Commission issues a temporary moratorium on AI use in social benefit determination, enforcement, and policing under Article 5 of the AI Act, while launching a forensic audit of all high-risk public systems. This is not a reversal of adoption — it is a recalibration of trust. We cannot afford to lose legitimacy in the institutions meant to protect us. The moratorium will last until the Red-Team Grid can verify containment protocols, and until we have rebuilt public confidence through transparency. That process begins now.
