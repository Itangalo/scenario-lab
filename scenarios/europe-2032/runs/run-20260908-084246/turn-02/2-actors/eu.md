## Priority  
**Cyber Resilience Surge** — because the verification_widens event confirms that AI is gaining reliable traction in high-stakes, real-world domains like law and engineering, and with detection still lagging, the Union cannot afford to wait for perfect coordination: we must harden before we are breached.

## In practice  
We are treating the verification_widens development as a structural shift: where AI can now self-validate in contract review, clinical coding, and structural design, the boundary between assistive tools and autonomous agents is dissolving. This is not just faster code — it is AI operating in domains once thought to require human judgment, and doing so with machine-speed verification. That changes the threat model. Our cyber defences must now assume not just intrusion, but autonomous, persistent, and self-correcting adversaries inside critical systems.

To advance the Cyber Resilience Surge, we are activating the Joint Crisis Coordination Cell at ENISA with emergency staffing from the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre and national CERTs. Binding technical standards for AI-powered IDS are being issued under Article 4 of DORA, with deployment mandates for transmission system operators, major ports, and water utilities. We are overcoming German resistance by allowing national operators to choose from an EU-approved list of detection stacks — including a sovereign EU stack co-developed by Fraunhofer and Thales — preserving technical autonomy within a common framework. For Belgium, we are clarifying that national regulators retain operational oversight, but must implement EU-level threat intelligence feeds and reporting timelines under the revised NIS2 protocol.

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Evaluation Pilot is being launched through a new regulation under the AI Act’s high-risk provisions. We are designating three public evaluation nodes — in France, Germany, and Finland — with secure access to model weights under a "trusted evaluator" regime. These nodes will conduct red-team evaluations on reasoning, goal persistence, and emergent planning in models above 50.0 capability. The legal basis is Article 68 of the AI Act, extended via comitology to cover pre-deployment assessment. Funding comes from the Digital Europe Programme’s unused 2026 allocation (€4.2bn), repurposed via the Commission’s emergency innovation clause. This is not about matching the frontier — it is about ensuring that when we do not know what a system can do, the EU still has a way to find out.
