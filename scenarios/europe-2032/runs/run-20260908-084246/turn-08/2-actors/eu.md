We must pivot from broad diffusion to targeted fortification of critical nodes — not abandoning adoption, but subordinating it to survivability.

## New measure  
**Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Directive**  
Mandate and fund the retrofitting of AI-driven control systems in energy, water, rail, and healthcare with verified containment, anomaly detection, and manual override capacity, prioritising systems already integrated with public AI deployments.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` resilience +6 via reduced blast radius in critical sectors, ai_safety +2 via improved local monitoring and containment, eu_ai_sovereignty +1 via EU-controlled safeguards on foreign-run systems  
`Applies to:` ENTSO-E, ERGEG, national regulators, EU-homed operators of essential services, public healthcare providers, and all systems using AI above 50.0 capability under the AI Act’s high-risk designation

## Priority  
**Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Directive** — because the cyber_major_incident proves that AI-enabled attacks are already operational and defenders are unprepared, and because resilience remains below 50.0 while capability climbs; this is no longer about future risk but present fragility, and hardening essential systems is the only way to restore credibility with both the public and the markets.

## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_major_incident — a coordinated ransomware sweep across municipal health records in Belgium, Denmark, and northern Italy — as a wake-up call. Forensic analysis confirms the attack chain was model-generated: spearphishing lures, exploit identification, and lateral movement planning all exhibit coherence beyond human-speed coordination. The tooling did not exceed 63.5 capability, but it exploited AI-integrated scheduling systems now present in 70% of regional hospitals. We are activating emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU not for unemployment this time, but for systemic vulnerability: €12bn in cohesion funds will be redirected to the Critical Infrastructure AI Hardening Directive, co-financing retrofits with national governments on a 60:40 basis.

The Sovereign AI Adoption Accelerator remains in flight, but we are no longer treating it as the primary vector of sovereignty. Instead, we are tying its continuation to compliance with the new hardening standards: no further public AI deployment in high-risk domains will be permitted without certified containment architecture. We are working through the Health and Transport Councils to fast-track adoption of the ENISA-developed AI Safety Gate — a modular verification layer that enforces human-in-the-loop decisions at critical junctures. Meanwhile, the Sovereign Incident Response Corps is accelerating its tabletop exercise into a live drill in May, simulating a 65.0-capable agent manipulating grid frequency signals across the Nordic-Baltic interconnector. The results will feed directly into the hardening specifications.

On the diplomatic front, we are pressing the U.S. on the Sovereign Evaluation Access Expansion, offering full reciprocity on safety benchmarks if Washington grants EU evaluators real-time access to training telemetry from models above 60.0. We are leveraging the emergent regional AI pact — now including Finland, Sweden, the Netherlands, and Belgium — as proof of European capacity to act, framing it not as fragmentation but as a core around which broader alliance cooperation can crystallise. Without eyes on the frontier, we cannot secure our own systems; without secure systems, no amount of adoption will restore trust. We are acting on both fronts — but now, defence comes first.
