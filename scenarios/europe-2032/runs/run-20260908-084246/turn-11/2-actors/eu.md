## Statement changes

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only when the cost of inaction demonstrably exceeds the risk of overreach.  
  - Grounds: The Emergency Override Directive has triggered severe political fragmentation and capital depletion without yet delivering safety or resilience gains. A major cyber incident and a bio incident this turn prove that uncertainty now carries asymmetric downside: we must act faster, but also more precisely. Committing blindly is no longer sustainable; we must now calibrate.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign utility through public delivery and systemic resilience — with priority now shifting from systemic resilience to sovereign operational continuity in the face of cascading failures.  
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident and bio_incident have exposed that resilience is not merely about hardening systems, but ensuring they remain under human authority during sustained machine-speed attacks. Our two-year focus must evolve: it is no longer enough to *withstand* — we must *govern through*.

## In practice
We are activating the Sovereign Incident Response Corps under Article 192 TFEU, leveraging the existing civil protection mechanism but re-tasking it for AI-cascaded crises. This is not another study group — it is a standing unit of 300 cross-disciplinary experts: cyber operators, biosecurity analysts, model interpreters, and legal enforcers, co-located in rotating hubs across Helsinki, Brussels, and Marseille. They will be empowered to enter any site where an AI-linked incident is declared, seize control of autonomous systems, and enforce isolation — bypassing national hesitation when seconds count.

Funding comes from the unspent portion of the Horizon Europe crisis envelope (€2.8bn), supplemented by a 0.2% emergency levy on AI service revenues in the single market. Recruitment begins immediately, with staffing targets at 40% within six months. Access to model weights and training logs will be mandated under the AI Act’s emergency provisions, and ENISA will issue temporary credentials within 72 hours of activation. France and Germany have pledged immediate cooperation; Poland and Hungary remain non-committal, citing sovereignty concerns. We are offering them co-leadership of the Eastern Node in Warsaw — not as a concession, but as a test: if they want a voice in how this power is used, they must first accept that it exists.
