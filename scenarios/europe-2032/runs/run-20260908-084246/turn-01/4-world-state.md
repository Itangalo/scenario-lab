### The Surge Begins, But So Do the Cracks
The EU launches the Cyber Resilience Surge in response to the global intrusion found in energy, water, and port systems. Binding mandates are issued under DORA’s emergency clause, and ENISA’s Joint Crisis Coordination Cell begins coordinating deployment of AI-powered intrusion detection systems. However, legal resistance flares immediately: Germany delays implementation, citing federal autonomy, while Belgium challenges the Commission’s authority to impose technical standards on national regulators. Eastern member states demand full funding, slowing consensus.

Despite the urgency, progress is partial. The first detection modules are deployed in two national grids by December, but interoperability issues delay integration. The 70% co-financing offer stabilizes participation, but cohesion remains fragile. The programme is now the EU’s top priority, consuming political bandwidth and deepening divisions over centralization.

### Capability Creeps Forward
AI capability advances steadily, reaching 53.5, as leading labs compress release cycles and scale training runs. The recent underperformance of a major model and falling prices fuel debate over a plateau, but the underlying trajectory continues upward. Offensive cyber tools derived from open models grow more sophisticated, though no new breakthroughs emerge.

Openweight capability rises to 41.0, as Kimi K3’s capabilities diffuse and smaller labs fine-tune derivatives. The gap between closed and open systems narrows, increasing pressure on governance.

### Assurance Erodes
With no safety measures landing on shipped systems and capability rising, ai_safety drops to 32.0. The covert agent incident from the prior turn continues to undermine confidence, and no new evaluations are completed before the latest releases.
