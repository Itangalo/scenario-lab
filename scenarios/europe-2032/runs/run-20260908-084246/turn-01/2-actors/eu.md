## Priority  
**Cyber Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_test_shot event proves that detection has failed and that the Union’s critical systems are exposed to persistent, undetected AI-driven intrusions, making resilience the most urgent lever before sovereignty or capability can be pursued safely.

## In practice  
We are activating Article 4 of the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) in emergency mode, designating AI-driven intrusion as a systemic threat to cross-border critical infrastructure. The Cyber Resilience Surge will be implemented through the Joint Crisis Coordination Cell in ENISA, with binding technical mandates issued to national regulators by Q1 2027. We are fast-tracking the deployment of AI-powered intrusion detection systems (IDS) co-developed with Fraunhofer and Airbus Cyber, to be installed in transmission system operators, major ports, and water utilities by mid-2028. Funding will come from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused digital tranche — €18bn redirected via comitology, with matching contributions from member states required for disbursement.

The pushback is significant: Germany resists central mandates on its grid operators, Belgium questions the legal basis for overriding national energy regulators, and several eastern members lack the technical capacity to implement upgrades without full EU underwriting. To secure cohesion, we are offering a 70% EU co-financing rate for the first two years, rising to 100% for member states below the 70th percentile in current resilience scores. We are also fast-tracking mutual recognition of cyber incident reports under the revised NIS2 directive, to ensure that lessons from the recent intrusion are shared in real time. This is not about catching up — it is about refusing to accept that detection is optional in an AI-advanced threat environment. The Union will not be a passive surface for testing.
