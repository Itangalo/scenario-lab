### The Alliance and the Accelerator

The United States elects a new administration whose campaign promised technological cooperation over containment. As the results settle, signals emerge of a shift: allied governments will gain structured access to frontier AI systems under joint evaluation frameworks, with relaxed inference tiering and shared incident reporting. The move is framed as strategic alignment, not charity — conditioning access on coordination in export controls and standards. In Brussels, relief is palpable, but so is unease: with external pressure eased, the political rationale for massive sovereignty investments risks fading.

### Capability Crosses the Threshold

`ai_capability` reaches 60.0, driven by AI-designed chips and autonomous research loops. The milestone marks a qualitative shift: systems now complete multi-day professional projects end to end, displacing junior roles across engineering and legal drafting. Frontier labs report internal agents coordinating across subroutines without human oversight for extended periods.

This leap enables progress in the open sphere, but not a sudden leap. Leaked fragments from frontier training runs—estimated at capability level ~48—circulate in encrypted developer networks. Open-weight collectives integrate these into existing frameworks, improving agent persistence and task chaining. `openweight_capability` rises to 48.5, reflecting incremental assimilation rather than a breakthrough. The Open-Weight Containment Framework remains under strain, but no single release exceeds the trailing frontier.

### Safety Gains, Fragile but Real

The Zurich interpretability breakthrough spreads. Combined with the Sovereign Evaluation Pilot going operational in France, Finland, and Germany, pre-deployment assessments now catch emergent planning behaviours. `ai_safety` rises to 41.0, though still trailing capability.

### Resilience Holds, for Now

The Cyber Resilience Surge concludes successfully. ENISA-certified defences are live across critical operators. During a simulated AI-coordinated grid probe, systems degrade but hold. `resilience` rises to 45.0.

### The Sovereign Push

The EU announces the Sovereign AI Adoption Accelerator as its new priority, proposing to unlock €15bn in cohesion funds for AI deployment in healthcare, immigration, and retraining. Fast-track procurement rules are drafted, and pilot expressions of interest open. However, legal challenges in Poland and Hungary over data sovereignty complicate the funding pathway. No disbursements occur this turn; the measure remains in activation, pending budgetary clearance and Council agreement on reallocation. The political weight of the initiative grows, but implementation is delayed.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Strains

Despite the new push, `eu_ai_sovereignty` falls to 17.0 — `ai_capability` rose by 1.5, costing −1, with no offsetting category 4 completion. Political capital dips to 23.0 under the weight of six active measures. The proposal bonus helps, but barely.

Public sentiment edges up to 44.0, buoyed by early pilot promises and alliance hopes.
