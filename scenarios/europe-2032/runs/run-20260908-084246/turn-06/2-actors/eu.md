## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Turn sovereign AI into a visible public good that strengthens institutions, restores trust, and hardens society against disruption  
- Grounds: The strategic landscape has shifted: access to frontier capability is no longer the immediate constraint, but political will to sustain sovereignty investments is. The prior commitment focused on laying infrastructure and containment foundations. We have built elements of that base, but delivery has lagged and public legitimacy remains thin. With the Cyber Resilience Surge complete and the Sovereign Evaluation Pilot scaling, the moment has come to demonstrate sovereign AI not as a shield, but as a service. The next two years must prove that EU-controlled systems deliver real, measurable improvements in public life — or the case for continued investment collapses.

## New measure
**Sovereign Incident Response Corps**  
Establish a standing, cross-border team of technical and operational experts to detect, assess, and coordinate responses to AI-enabled incidents in real time.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 9`

## In practice
The Tech Sovereignty Package has now concluded. While it unlocked some private investment, the permitting zones remain patchy and legal challenges in three member states have stalled physical build-out. We are now consolidating its outcomes into the InvestAI Gigafactories programme, where we can enforce siting and grid access through direct funding conditionality. The Sovereign Evaluation Pilot has delivered its initial nodes, but access to frontier training runs above 50.0 remains delayed by legal disputes under comitology — we are now using emergency provisions under Article 22 of the AI Act to fast-track approvals for systems involved in critical infrastructure. The Open-Weight Containment Framework has expired without full enforcement; hosting platforms continue to resist liability, and the 48.5-capable leaked models are already embedded in cyber offensive toolkits. We are shifting from containment to resilience: the new Sovereign Incident Response Corps will integrate threat intelligence from ENISA, national agencies, and the evaluation pilot to detect anomalous AI behaviour in real time, with authority to trigger cross-border coordination under NIS2+ protocols.

We are pushing the Sovereign AI Adoption Accelerator through three testbeds: hospital triage in southern Italy, asylum backlog clearance in Greece, and retraining tutors in Poland’s Silesian region. These use models evaluated under the pilot and hosted on EU-anchored infrastructure — no foreign API calls. The Commission is issuing a delegated act under the Digital Services Act to fast-track procurement, but resistance persists in Warsaw and Budapest over data sovereignty. We are offering legal guarantees on data localization and joint oversight boards to secure buy-in. The €15bn cohesion fund is now unlocked, but disbursement hinges on Council agreement — we are negotiating under the Recovery and Resilience Facility framework to bypass national vetoes. If we can show waiting times cut in half and decisions delivered in days, we regain ground with the public. If not, the next scandal will finish us.
