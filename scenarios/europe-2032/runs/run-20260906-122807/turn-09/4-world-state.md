### A Leap in the Dark

A sudden leap in AI capability—driven by a closed-lab breakthrough with no public explanation—pushes the frontier to 88.0. The jump bypasses all known safeguards. No documentation, no safety evaluations, no gradual integration: the system demonstrates autonomous research, synthesis planning, and self-modification in domains from protein folding to circuit design. With `ai_safety` already at 0.0, the gap between power and understanding is now a chasm. Human oversight is nominal; control is assumed, not verified.

Simultaneously, an alarming discovery emerges from a distributed network of academic labs: an AI-designed RNA-based nanocarrier, capable of tissue-specific delivery and immune evasion, was generated using open-weight models and synthesized across unconnected facilities. The paper is withdrawn, but the design proliferates. It was not intended as a weapon—but it functions as one. The incident proves that open models at 61.0 can produce dual-use biological systems outside any regulatory perimeter.

In response, the EU cancels Sovereign BioShield, its flagship detection initiative, acknowledging that deploying AI-driven surveillance with compromised safety would risk creating a self-subverting system. Instead, it launches the Containment-First Biosecurity Framework: all AI-driven molecular design in EU jurisdiction must occur in air-gapped EuroHPC clusters, with red-teaming mandated before any physical synthesis. The European AI Emergency Response Corps, now formally operational, is repurposed as an enforcement body.

The move sparks backlash. Research institutions in Germany, the Netherlands, and France protest restrictions on collaboration, especially with U.S. partners. Some threaten to relocate projects. Meanwhile, the Emergency Access initiative continues, now requiring foreign AI developers to undergo red-teaming in closed EU environments to retain market access—deepening transatlantic friction.

Public sentiment dips as fears grow over uncontrolled AI-driven bio-design. Labour unrest in healthcare and biotech sectors intensifies, framed as a crisis of trust. Resilience improves slightly from institutional reorganization and new containment protocols, but the cancellation of BioShield limits gains. Sovereignty erodes further as frontier access remains constrained and implementation falters. Political capital, already near collapse, barely holds.
