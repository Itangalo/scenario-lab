### The Leap and the Leak

A sudden leap in AI capability reverberates across labs and governments. The frontier surges forward as a leading developer releases a system that autonomously redesigns its own training pipeline, achieving in days what previously took years. This discontinuous advance pushes `ai_capability` to 76.0. The internal logic of these systems is now largely illegible, marking a sharp decline in safety: audits confirm that even developers cannot trace critical decisions.

Simultaneously, a classified biosecurity assessment leaks into closed policy circles, revealing that the previously disclosed pathogen blueprint could be recreated with modest funding and technical skill. Though not yet public, the document triggers emergency coordination across health and security agencies.

### EU Response Under Strain

The European Union accelerates the European AI Emergency Response Corps, invoking emergency powers to fast-track deployment protocols. Nine high-risk nodes begin integration with sovereign compute clusters. However, legal resistance from several member states delays full ratification, limiting operational reach. A fiscal incentive—70% cost-sharing for early adopters—draws interest but no immediate commitments.

The BioShield Rapid Detection Network is formally proposed as a new high-priority initiative, with pilot sequencing stations to be expanded into a future EU-wide system. Forty-seven wastewater facilities are identified for potential AI-linked upgrades, pending approval of the legislative framework and allocation of implementation resources. Oversight will be assigned to civilian bodies once operational, but no upgrades occur this turn. Funding is expected to draw from reallocated Cohesion Funds and the proposed Resilience Bond, though final authorization remains pending.

Despite these efforts, political capital continues to erode. Three large measures remain in flight, and the new large category 6 initiative adds significant strain. The priority designation fails to accelerate delivery meaningfully, and public sentiment remains fragile.

Resilience sees a modest gain from partial progress on existing efforts, though the new BioShield rollout has not yet contributed. Sovereignty declines further as domestic capacity fails to keep pace with the widening dependency gap.

Open-weight models advance to 58.0, reflecting diffusion of recent frontier breakthroughs into open frameworks, but remain below the new frontier level of 76.0, preserving the constitutional relationship. The gain is substantial but no longer exceeds the pace of frontier advancement, aligning with realistic knowledge spillover dynamics.
