## New measure  
**Emergency Access to Life-Saving AI Models**  
Establish a sovereign legal and technical pathway for the EU to access and deploy frontier AI models essential for public health, even when denied by foreign providers.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to eu_ai_sovereignty by securing legal authority and technical means to access or replicate life-saving models; +5 to public_sentiment by demonstrating that the Union can guarantee access to transformative medicine; begins to close the gap between EU dependence and public need  
`Applies to:` European Medicines Agency, EuroHPC centres, national health ministries, EU diplomatic corps, and foreign AI laboratories operating in or supplying the EU market

## Priority  
**Emergency Access to Life-Saving AI Models** — because the medical_breakthrough delivered by inaccessible models proves that resilience is not just about surviving harm, but about securing access to benefit, and because without this, every resilience measure we build rests on foreign permission.

## In practice  
We are acting on a new fault line: capability without access is not sovereignty, and resilience without medicine is not protection. The medical breakthrough—personalised cancer therapies derived from models trained in California—arrived with fanfare, but not for us. Hospitals in Berlin, Marseille, and Lisbon were denied access within hours of attempting deployment. No explanation, no appeal. This is not an outage. It is a demonstration of structural vulnerability. We cannot harden wastewater plants against bio-threats while our hospitals are left defenceless against algorithmic exclusion.

So we are launching the Emergency Access to Life-Saving AI Models initiative under Article 168 TFEU and the newly invoked Crisis Response Protocol. This gives the European Medicines Agency the legal authority to mandate disclosure of model weights or full replication rights for any AI system delivering essential public health benefit, if access is denied or unstable. We are pairing this with a technical track at EuroHPC: a sovereign sandbox environment where replicated models can be validated and deployed under civilian oversight. The first target is the oncology suite currently locked behind a U.S. firewall. We are offering developers a binding agreement: cooperate with EMA validation and retain commercial rights, or face compulsory licensing and reputational cost. This is not nationalisation—it is contingency for when private control becomes public risk.

At the same time, we are completing the Resilience Surge Protocol and the InvestAI Gigafactories this turn. The Gigafactories will now pivot from general compute to sovereign health-AI hosting, with two sites redesignated for medical model deployment. The Resilience Surge’s final deliverables—cyber-hardened health networks in 14 member states—are being certified by ENISA this month. And we are pressing the European AI Emergency Response Corps into pre-operational status: teams in Lyon, Milan, and Warsaw are now on 72-hour standby, linked to the BioShield detection nodes and empowered to isolate infected systems in critical infrastructure. Legal resistance remains, but the denial of medical AI has shifted the debate: sovereignty is no longer abstract. It is the difference between life and algorithmic rationing.
