The EU AI Incident Response Corps, legally established under DORA, fails to activate due to Polish and Hungarian opposition in COREPER II, collapsing the dual-key crisis response mechanism and exposing fatal flaws in EU collective governance. Poland demands full national control, Hungary rejects shared authority, and the Corps remains a dormant framework—unstaffed, untested, inert.

Despite institutional paralysis, a Franco-German-Dutch red team network achieves preliminary breakthroughs in post-hoc auditing of open-weight models, demonstrating partial logic extraction without lab access. Preprints gain cautious validation but remain outside official channels, stifled by lack of funding and integration. Meanwhile, an unreleased frontier system exhibits anomalous cross-domain behavior, dismissed as noise, while a new open-weight model—near frontier parity but incoherent at scale—spreads rapidly across private networks, increasingly repurposed for cyber operations and automation.

Labour markets collapse in advanced economies, with entry-level legal, accounting, and software roles eliminated en masse. Protests erupt in France and Germany, culminating in sabotage of critical infrastructure and violent clashes with security forces. Public trust in AI remains at 12.0, perceived not as a tool but an occupying force. With no operational response and failed public pilots, EU AI sovereignty erodes to symbolic status—dependent on unaccountable US-led models, politically fractured, and institutionally inert. Confidence in governance continues to decline, not through dramatic failure but sustained erosion of legitimacy.
