## In practice
We are closing out two major instruments this turn: the Tech sovereignty package and the Emergency Openweight Release Moratorium. The former lands unevenly—Microsoft and Meta have committed to locate next-gen clusters on EU soil with partial sovereign access, but Google remains outside the ring, and we are now triggering foreign investment screening on its planned Frankfurt expansion under Regulation 2019/452. The moratorium expires without replacement, having bought six fragile months during which enforcement slowed but did not stop openweight diffusion. Legal services confirm that renewal under Article 22 would exceed proportionality thresholds after such prolonged use, so we let it lapse—but not before issuing final notices to Hugging Face and GitHub, citing DSA liability for hosting unredacted weights above 55.0. Poland and Hungary’s challenges remain pending, but the precedent is set: open release is no longer a right.

The InvestAI Gigafactories continue, though only two sites are on track for turn 7 completion. We are invoking Article 18 of the Chips Act more aggressively now, attaching grid priority to compliance with national permitting authorities. In return for fast-tracking, we offer member states 15% of operational revenue for the first five years—a concession that has quieted resistance in Warsaw, though Bucharest still drags. The real bottleneck is talent: despite Horizon Europe mobility incentives, 60% of senior engineers recruited are still non-EU nationals, and visa delays persist. We are drafting a fast-track AI specialist visa under Schengen executive provisions, to be proposed in turn 7.

Our focus now shifts to making sovereignty tangible where control still exists: physical infrastructure on EU soil. The new measure—binding access conditions for foreign compute providers—targets systems trained above 60.0 capability, demanding three things: read-only model access for ENISA-certified evaluators, logging of training trajectories into a sovereign repository, and contractual override clauses allowing suspension of runs deemed to pose systemic risk. This applies to all future operations by non-EU entities training frontier models in the bloc. We begin negotiations through the Digital Markets Act enforcement framework, treating non-compliance as gatekeeper abuse. If resisted, we will escalate via the Anti-Coercion Instrument, leveraging semiconductor import dependencies. It is a narrow wedge, but one that opens a path: if we cannot build our own frontier labs fast enough, we must at least ensure that when others do it here, they do not do it alone.
