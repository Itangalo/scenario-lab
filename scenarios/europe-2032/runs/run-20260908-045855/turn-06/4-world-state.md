### The Acceleration Begins

The onset of recursive self-improvement is confirmed in retrospect: release cycles compress, training runs accelerate, and AI systems begin materially shaping their own successors. Global capability surges by +8.0, entering the **75.0** range—matching strong human experts across most cognitive domains. The frontier now moves faster than institutions can track.

With no safety breakthrough and no new safeguards deployed, `ai_safety` declines further, dropping by 2.0 points to **6.0**. The erosion reflects growing model opacity and the absence of coordinated monitoring, but does not imply total collapse. The gap between capability and understanding widens, yet baseline detection and post-hoc analysis remain functional, preventing a plunge to zero.

### EU Consolidates, But Gains Little

The EU retires two major measures: the Tech sovereignty package and the Emergency Openweight Moratorium. The former secures partial commitments from Microsoft and Meta but fails to bind Google, whose Frankfurt expansion now faces investment screening. The moratorium expires after six months of patchy enforcement, having slowed but not stopped open-weight diffusion. Poland and Hungary’s legal challenges remain unresolved.

A new small measure—Binding Access Conditions for Foreign Compute Providers—is proposed, targeting audit rights over high-capability training runs in EU-hosted data centres. It faces immediate resistance from U.S. hyperscalers, who label it extraterritorial overreach. Negotiations begin under the Digital Markets Act, but no compliance is secured this turn. Due to bandwidth constraints, the proposal proceeds without full funding commitment; implementation will be delayed pending reallocation from other programmes.

The EU Red Team for Open-Weight Models remains the named priority. Despite its technical promise, it delivers no immediate safety gains or public reassurance. However, given constitutional limits on concurrent measures, no resources are allocated to expand or launch additional initiatives this turn.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Shrinks Modestly

`eu_ai_sovereignty` drops to **13.0**: -1 due to the 8.0 capability rise, and -1 from the lack of a finishing category 4 measure, as the Tech sovereignty package’s partial delivery falls short of full sovereign compute independence. No access event occurred, but dependence deepens.

`eu_political_capital` declines to **18.0**, reflecting a reduced portfolio charge of −3: −2 for maintaining the Red Team as priority (now counted as the sole active cost-bearing initiative), and −1 for the unfunded proposal of the new measure, which advances only partially due to governance bottlenecks. The ongoing costs of InvestAI Gigafactories are deferred as no new disbursement occurs this turn—implementation delays prevent further drawdown. There is no proposal bonus: the new measure responds to ongoing risk, not a recent shock. Public sentiment remains stagnant.

`resilience` holds at **46.0**—no new category 6 measure finishes, and no incident overwhelms defences.

`openweight_capability` rises to **67.75**, now at 90% of the closed frontier, sustained by global spillover from closed-model progress and unimpeded development outside the EU. While red-teaming continues, it has not yet curbed open diffusion, and no major open-source safety advance offsets the trend.
