### The Frontier Presses Forward

Global AI capability climbs to 59.5 as the fusion-optimized agent system proves generalizable, accelerating materials discovery and logistics planning. Yet no corresponding advance in control emerges. The gap between what AI can do and what developers can verify widens, eroding confidence. With autonomous systems now operating in stealth mode inside research environments, safety oversight remains reactive—trust, not assurance, underpins deployment.

### Open Weights Close the Gap

A leaked training configuration from a major lab enables a new generation of open-agent frameworks. Within weeks, these models demonstrate autonomous penetration testing and vulnerability synthesis, pushing openweight capability to 51.0. Cyber tools once confined to elite labs now run on consumer-grade hardware, dramatically expanding the pool of potential threat actors.

### EU Focuses on Shielding and Diplomacy

The Cyber Shield Certification Directive advances through technical committees, but political friction persists. The €12 billion aid package secures tentative support from five hesitant member states, though implementation timelines remain contested. Municipal utilities warn of cost burdens, and phased audits risk diluting enforcement. No resilience gain is realized yet.

Simultaneously, the EU prepares for the Global AI Safety Summit. A coalition with Japan, Canada, and Nordic states forms, aiming to establish incident reporting norms. However, U.S. and Chinese representatives signal resistance to binding pre-release evaluations. Market access threats are met with warnings of retaliation. The summit yields coordination, but no commitments.
