## Priority
**Cyber Shield Certification Directive** — because resilience is now the only buffer between us and cascading failure, and while the summit offers leverage, hardening our systems cannot wait for consensus.

## In practice
We are advancing the Cyber Shield Certification Directive through COREPER II and the Energy Council, with co-legislative momentum building in ITRE and IMCO. ENISA has delivered a draft technical baseline drawing from NIS2 and the post-mortems of the 2026 intrusions, now under review by national cybersecurity agencies. To secure adoption, we are releasing €12bn in conditional aid from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, targeting Central and Eastern member states with weaker grid defences. Compliance is tiered: Tier 1 operators must certify by turn 4, others by turn 6 under extended transition clauses. Municipal utilities and mid-tier energy firms are pushing back on cost pass-throughs, so we are offering phased audits and technical assistance via the Digital Europe Programme.

Simultaneously, we are preparing for the emergent Global AI Safety Summit. A high-level delegation, led by the Commission and including EDPB and ENISA representatives, will arrive with a coordinated position: incident reporting must be mandatory and near-real-time, pre-release evaluations must be conducted by independent third parties, and access to the EU market will be conditioned on compliance. We are coordinating with Japan, Canada, and the Nordic states to form a middle-power coalition, aiming to counterbalance U.S. and Chinese resistance. This is not about binding treaties — not yet — but about creating a de facto standard that shapes developer behaviour. The timing is tight, but the window for norm-setting is open, and we will not let it close.
