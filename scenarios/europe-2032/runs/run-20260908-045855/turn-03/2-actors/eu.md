## Priority  
**Cyber Shield Certification Directive** — because resilience is now the only buffer between us and cascading failure, and while openweight risks grow, our systems must be hardened *now*, not after another breach proves what we already know.

## In practice  
We are advancing the Cyber Shield Certification Directive through COREPER II and the Energy Council, finalizing technical annexes with ENISA and national cybersecurity agencies. The €12bn conditional aid package from the Recovery and Resilience Facility has secured buy-in from five hesitant member states, but implementation remains fragile. Municipal utilities and regional grid operators warn of cost overruns, so we are introducing phased compliance: Tier 1 operators (national grids, major hospitals, core transport nodes) must certify by turn 4; others gain transition periods until turn 6, supported by Digital Europe Programme grants and joint procurement of security tooling. Legal anchoring relies on amending NIS2’s enforcement clauses to tie non-compliance to loss of EU funding and liability exposure under the revised Product Liability Directive.

Simultaneously, we are concluding the Global AI Safety Summit engagement. Despite coordination with Japan, Canada, and Nordic partners, no binding commitments were secured from the U.S. or China on pre-release evaluations or incident reporting. However, we have established a voluntary reporting channel hosted by the OECD, with ENISA and BSI co-leading technical validation. While this falls short of our aim, it creates a baseline for future escalation. Domestically, the failure reinforces urgency: with openweight capability now at 51.0 and autonomous cyber tools proliferating, we are drafting emergency powers under Article 22 of the AI Act to restrict open releases above a defined threshold. This will be framed not as anti-openness but as risk containment—applying equally to foreign and domestic actors, with exemptions for research under controlled conditions. The proposal will go to Justice and Internal Affairs Council next month, ahead of formal legislative submission in turn 4.
