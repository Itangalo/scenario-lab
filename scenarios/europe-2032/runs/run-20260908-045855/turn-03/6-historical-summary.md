Global AI capability reaches 62.0, driven by self-improving fusion-optimized agents in closed labs, while safety and interpretability lag dangerously behind, widening the control gap. Open-weight models advance to 54.0 following widespread replication of leaked architectures, enabling autonomous cyber operations on consumer hardware and accelerating malicious proliferation. The EU advances its Cyber Shield Certification with finalized audit protocols, but implementation falters due to cost concerns and delayed compliance, while Hungary’s bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler exposes fractures in EU unity. At the Global AI Safety Summit, binding incident reporting fails; only voluntary OECD-hosted mechanisms are adopted. In response, the EU drafts emergency powers under Article 22 of the AI Act to impose risk-based caps on open-weight model releases, signaling a shift toward stricter regulation amid growing systemic vulnerability.
