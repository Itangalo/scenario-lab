### Frontier Momentum Unchecked

Global AI capability climbs to 62.0 as fusion-optimized agents demonstrate autonomous refinement in quantum materials and logistics optimization, with closed labs reporting self-directed research loops. No corresponding advance in interpretability or control emerges. Human oversight remains nominal, and the gap between performance and understanding deepens—safety assurance now lags by over a decade of operational risk.

### Open Models Fuel Proliferation

Following widespread replication of leaked training architectures, openweight capability rises to 54.0. Autonomous cyber tools now operate effectively on consumer hardware, enabling persistent reconnaissance and coordinated intrusion across industrial networks. A coalition of EU states, Japan, and Canada launches an informal red-teaming initiative, but without binding coordination, developers outside the bloc continue releasing high-capability models unchecked.

### EU Hardens Resilience—But Fragments Within

The Cyber Shield Certification Directive advances technically, with ENISA finalizing audit protocols for Tier 1 critical operators. Five member states commit via conditional aid, but phased compliance introduces loopholes: regional utilities delay investment, citing cost burdens. No resilience gain is realized yet—implementation remains partial.

Meanwhile, Hungary finalizes a bilateral data hosting agreement with a U.S. hyperscaler, granting preferential access to AI training compute in exchange for relaxed regulatory scrutiny. The move bypasses EU-level negotiations and undermines the bloc’s unified stance on frontier access, signaling deepening divergence among members.

### Diplomacy Falls Short, Regulation Gains Urgency

At the Global AI Safety Summit, the EU secures only voluntary incident reporting through an OECD-hosted channel. The U.S. and China reject binding pre-release evaluations. In response, Brussels drafts emergency powers under Article 22 of the AI Act to cap open-weight model releases at a defined risk threshold, aiming to slow the rise of distributed cyber threats.
