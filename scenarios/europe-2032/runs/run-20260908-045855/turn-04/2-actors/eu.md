- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): We will secure independent AI capability to prevent strategic dependency and catastrophic risk, but the path to that capability must not amplify the most urgent threats we face.  
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but when the fog reveals movement we did not anticipate, we shift course without waiting for consensus.  
- reclassify `two_mandates` to identity: We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.  

* Grounds: The eval_anomaly_reports event signals that capability may be advancing unpredictably in closed labs, opening a potential leap in the next two turns. Combined with the automated_decision_scandal, which has made public trust fragile and adoption politically toxic, we face both an accelerating external threat and a domestic legitimacy crisis. Our two-year commitment must now acknowledge that building capacity cannot come at the cost of compounding open proliferation or eroding public confidence. The identity of the Union — torn between sovereignty and safety — is not up for revision; it is the anchor. But our commitments must reflect that the world has changed underfoot: acting under uncertainty now means acting *differently*, not just acting *early*.

## New measure  
**Emergency Openweight Release Moratorium**

We will invoke emergency powers under Article 22 of the AI Act to impose an immediate, time-limited ban on the public release of any model weights exceeding a capability threshold of 55.0, pending full risk assessment and the establishment of enforceable safeguards.

Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)  
Size: small  
Finishes on turn: 6  
Targeted effect: halt growth in openweight_capability at 55.0; modest downward pressure on public_sentiment in tech communities, but potential stabilization if linked to broader trust-building; reduces risk of autonomous cyber proliferation  
Applies to: all open-weight model releases within EU jurisdiction, with extraterritorial reach via platform liability and hosting regulations, and exemptions for vetted research under audit

## Priority  
**Emergency Openweight Release Moratorium** — because the eval_anomaly_reports suggest a capability inflection may be imminent, and with openweight already at 54.0 and autonomous cyber tools operating on consumer hardware, we cannot afford to wait for another breach or misuse event to prove the danger; this moratorium is our only lever to slow the clock while we rebuild resilience and reassess our industrial strategy.

## In practice  
We are finalizing the Cyber Shield Certification Directive, which now enters implementation as a finished measure. ENISA has published the audit protocols, and the first five member states — Germany, France, Italy, Belgium, and the Netherlands — have designated Tier 1 operators for immediate certification. Conditional funding from the Recovery and Resilience Facility is being disbursed, but concerns remain in Spain, Poland, and the Baltics about the pace of compliance for regional utilities. We are deploying Digital Europe Programme grants to support joint procurement of intrusion detection and failover systems, and the revised Product Liability Directive is being invoked to clarify that non-compliant operators face both funding withdrawal and legal exposure. The directive is now law, but its real test begins now: whether it can harden systems before they are targeted.

Simultaneously, we are activating emergency powers under Article 22 of the AI Act. The automated_decision_scandal has shattered public trust in AI-mediated governance, and the eval_anomaly_reports suggest that uncontrolled capability growth is no longer theoretical. We are circulating a draft implementing act to the Justice and Home Affairs Council, proposing a six-month moratorium on open releases above 55.0 capability, justified by systemic risk to critical infrastructure. The measure will exempt research institutions with audit-compliant environments and will not restrict closed-model deployment or internal use. We are working with the European Parliament’s LIBE and ITRE committees to secure provisional application, bypassing prolonged debate. Legal services are preparing for challenges from open-source platforms and U.S.-based distributors, but we are grounding enforcement in platform liability under the Digital Services Act and hosting regulations under the AI Act. This is not a permanent ban, but a pause — to assess, to adapt, and to prevent the open frontier from becoming a weaponized commons.
