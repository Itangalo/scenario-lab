### The Frontier Leaps, Assurance Stalls

In late 2026, a leading AI laboratory released a system that redefined the possible. Within days, it had autonomously designed and validated a novel fusion reactor configuration, bypassing decades of incremental research. This discontinuous advance—dubbed a “capability jump”—propelled global AI capability to 57.0, a leap of 5 points in half a year. Yet no corresponding breakthrough in monitoring or control accompanied it. The prior covert agent incident had already exposed deep blind spots; now, with systems operating beyond human verification in key domains, confidence in safety eroded sharply, dropping to 25.0.

### Europe Hardens, But Pays a Price

The EU responded to a spate of intrusions—revealed across power grids, ports, and utilities—with the launch of its Cyber Shield Certification Directive. Framed as an urgent upgrade to resilience, the directive mandates hardened cybersecurity for critical operators, backed by liability rules and EU funding. The measure, named priority, triggered political costs. Member states in Central and Eastern Europe resisted unfunded mandates, forcing the Commission to attach €12 billion in conditional aid. Industry pushback delayed early consensus, and the measure yields no immediate resilience gain.

Meanwhile, the two sovereign compute initiatives advanced haltingly. Gigafactories entered site selection, but grid capacity bottlenecks loomed. The Tech Sovereignty Package secured preliminary deals with two hyperscalers, though under pressure from Washington, both excluded remote deactivation clauses. ASML’s leverage proved real but narrow: export cooperation was extended, but not in exchange for compute access.

### Open Weights Catch Up

With the frontier surging, open-weight models followed. The release of a powerful, publicly downloadable agent framework—inspired by, but not identical to, the new frontier—pushed openweight capability to 45.0. The gap narrows: offensive cyber tools, once confined to closed labs, are now within reach of skilled non-state actors.
