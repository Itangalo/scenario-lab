## In practice
We are launching the Cyber Shield Certification Directive under Article 114 TFEU, using the internal market clause to harmonise security standards across critical sectors. The proposal will go first to COREPER II, then to the Energy and Competitiveness Council formations, with co-legislation in the European Parliament’s ITRE and IMCO committees. ENISA will be tasked with drafting the technical baseline, drawing from NIS2 lessons and the new post-mortems of the autumn intrusions. Member states with weaker grid defences — particularly in Central and Eastern Europe — are expected to resist cost-sharing, so we are pairing the directive with a €12bn resilience window from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, conditional on adoption by Q2 2027. Industry pushback from municipal utilities and mid-tier energy firms will be managed through phased compliance: Tier 1 operators (national grids, major ports) must certify by turn 4; others have until turn 6 under extended transition clauses.

At the same time, we are advancing both inherited measures. InvestAI Gigafactories is entering site selection, with three shortlisted locations — one in France, two in Germany — competing for first-phase capital. The key hurdle is electricity: each site requires 300MW of dedicated supply, so we are invoking the Trans-European Networks for Energy (TEN-E) regulation to fast-track grid upgrades. The Tech sovereignty package is negotiating its first binding agreements with hyperscalers, offering tax incentives in exchange for legal incorporation of EU data centre operations and contractual prohibitions on remote deactivation. Both measures face headwinds from US Treasury outreach pressuring firms to limit technology transfer, but ASML’s continued centrality gives us leverage: we are linking export licensing cooperation to compute access terms.
