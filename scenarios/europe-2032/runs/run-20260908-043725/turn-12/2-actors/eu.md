## Statement changes  
* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Ensure sovereign AI systems are integrated, tested, and trusted in real-time crisis response by the end of 2033, with binding interoperability among at least 15 member states``  
  - Grounds: The current level of fragmentation — with Italy, Spain, and Poland withholding full support — risks rendering the Cyber Shield a collection of isolated capabilities rather than a unified defence. The doctrine initiative has revealed that trust is not merely technical but political: without a clear threshold for participation, the core group may solidify but the Union as a whole will not follow. To meet the original commitment’s intent, interoperability must now be measured not just by design but by adoption. The modification raises the ambition but also grounds it — the target is no longer cohesion in principle, but in binding practice across a critical mass.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will now anchor such commitments in shared doctrine, not unilateral action, to preserve cohesion``  
  - Grounds: The failure to secure full buy-in for the Joint Operational Doctrine — despite completion of the Cyber Shield — demonstrates that unilateral assertions of sovereignty fracture rather than strengthen collective resilience. The Nordic-Benelux core shows what is possible, but also the limits of coalitions of the willing. Going forward, acting under uncertainty must include mechanisms that bind others, not just protect ourselves. This recalibration does not retreat from decisiveness; it redirects it toward institution-building as the higher form of sovereignty.
