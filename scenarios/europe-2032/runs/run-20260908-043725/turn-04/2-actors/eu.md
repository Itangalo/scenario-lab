## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that the cyber_major_incident was not just an attack — it was a message. The tooling was model-generated, the sweep automated, and attribution remains months out. Our defences were too slow, too siloed, and too manual. That ends now. We are formalizing the emergent_regional_cyber_collective — the joint Nordic and Benelux cell — as the operational nucleus of a new EU-wide rapid response system. Under Article 18 of the Treaty on European Union, we are activating the Integrated Political Crisis Response framework to bind this cell into ENISA’s authority, granting it real-time access to intrusion data across energy, healthcare, and municipal networks, and pre-authorized protocols for autonomous countermeasures when human response time is insufficient.

This is not a directive, not a study, not another pilot. It is command architecture: shared AI monitoring agents trained to detect anomalous model use in network traffic, deployed across participating states with mandatory data pooling. We are releasing €120 million from the Digital Europe Programme to harden integration by turn 6, and we are offering full liability shielding to member states that adopt the protocol early. Germany and the Benelux states are on board; Poland and Hungary remain hesitant, citing sovereignty concerns — so we are offering them co-lead roles in the monitoring design phase, and a veto on any countermeasure that crosses into domestic law enforcement. We are also linking this to the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield: when it finishes next turn, its protocols will feed directly into this new cell. This is how we close the gap — not by waiting for perfect systems, but by stitching the best of what we have into something that can act.
