### A Surge in Offensive Capability, a Step Toward Control

Global AI capability advances to **65.5**, driven by a **capability_jump** in automated cyber operations and algorithmic engineering. The leap is narrow but consequential: attackers now generate and adapt exploits faster than patch cycles can respond. In parallel, a major ransomware campaign—powered by AI-generated payloads—disrupts hospital systems in three member states, exposing systemic fragility. Attribution remains unclear, but the tooling bears hallmarks of frontier model outputs.

In response, the EU launches **Accelerate Compute Sovereignty**, a large category 4 measure, fast-tracking grid access for InvestAI Gigafactories and establishing legal jurisdiction over high-capability models. France and Finland accept the terms; Poland consents only after securing a €300 million grid resilience buffer. Hungary refuses to implement key provisions, triggering legal disputes under the Digital Markets Act. Negotiations with hyperscalers yield partial alignment—some agree to store model weights locally, others resist, citing operational sovereignty.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** complete this turn, boosting compute capacity. However, the broader sovereignty push is uneven: while legal anchoring strengthens long-term control, immediate access remains constrained. **eu_ai_sovereignty** rises to **18.0**, reflecting partial gains.

The **Joint Cyber Shield** absorbs some of the ransomware impact, limiting cascading failures. Combined with crisis coordination, this **joint_threat_response** lifts **resilience** to **54.5**.

**ai_safety** dips to **36.0**, as the capability jump outpaces safeguards, and no new safety measures land on shipped systems.

Political capital falls to **21.0**: the new large measure costs −3, the priority −1, and **Tech sovereignty package** (still in flight) −3. No proposal bonus is earned—the measure responds to recent shocks, but legal resistance and partial buy-in limit perceived urgency. **public_sentiment** declines to **28.0**, as the cyberattack fuels anxiety and Hungary’s defiance frames the policy as divisive.
