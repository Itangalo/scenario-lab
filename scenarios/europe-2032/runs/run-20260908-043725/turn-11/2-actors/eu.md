## In practice  
This turn, we complete the foundational work of Accelerate Compute Sovereignty. The Council Regulation on Model Weight Anchoring is now in force, and ENISA has designated the first three sovereign anchoring facilities — in Finland, France, and the Netherlands — with audit logs accessible to the Commission. The Sovereign AI Corps reaches 65% staffing, enabling live simulations using these weights in the ongoing Cyber Shield drills. Poland’s grid buffer is fully drawn, and despite Hungary’s CJEU challenge, we are treating anchoring as a de facto standard: no sovereign model access without compliance.  

With infrastructure secured, we now force the harder question: will these systems actually work together when seconds count? The emergent Nordic-Benelux cyber collective — coordinating red-teaming and pre-authorised countermeasures — is the best signal we have that operational cohesion is within reach. We are convening ENISA and COREPER next week to launch the Joint Operational Doctrine initiative, starting with these states as a pilot. The framework will codify three things: escalation thresholds for AI agent autonomy (Level 2 response requires dual national and ENISA approval), data jurisdiction rules (data generated in drills remains under joint control), and mutual aid protocols (a breach in one triggers automated alert and resource pooling in all). We are offering full Digital Europe Programme funding and fast-track access to InvestAI’s sovereign models to any member state that signs on by turn 13. Italy and Spain remain hesitant, but we are offering them co-chairmanship of the doctrine’s legal working group — not as a concession, but as a test of their willingness to shape the rules rather than block them. This is not about consensus. It is about creating a core of capable, committed states that can act as the nucleus of a broader shield. If we do not lock this in now, the next incident will fracture us.
