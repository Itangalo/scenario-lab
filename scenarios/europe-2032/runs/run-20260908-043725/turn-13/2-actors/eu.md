## In practice  
We are acting on three fronts. First, the completion of the Joint Operational Doctrine marks a milestone — but one that exposed its limits. The doctrine is now formally adopted by the Nordic-Benelux core, yet Italy and Spain still withhold full escalation authority, and Poland has openly rejected it in favour of national red-teaming. We cannot allow the Union to splinter into competing security epistemologies. That is why we are launching a new consortium under ENISA’s authority, not as a replacement, but as the operational arm of the doctrine itself — one that offers Poland a seat at the table with co-design rights, not just compliance.  

Second, the Sovereign AI Talent Initiative is moving from proposal to implementation, but resistance is stiff. Germany and France support fast-track mobility, but Austria and Belgium fear wage erosion and brain drain from public research. We are offering a carve-out: priority access to EU compute for institutions that participate in the Sovereign AI Corps, tied to binding safety audit roles. This links talent to mission — not just recruitment, but purpose. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking will allocate 15% of its next procurement round to institutions that commit to the anchoring programme.  

Third, the cyber_major_incident has shifted the political gravity. A ransomware sweep disabled hospital scheduling systems in three member states, exploiting a model-generated zero-day in a widely used EHR dependency. No lives were lost, but trust was. We are using the incident to accelerate consensus: the new red-teaming consortium will simulate not just cyber, but bio and hybrid threats, leveraging the emergent cross-domain insight to design attacks that reflect real-world convergence. This is not preparation — it is deterrence by demonstration. The Council will vote on the legal basis under Article 114 TFEU, with the Commission fast-tracking a delegated act to bypass national vetoes on cross-border testing. We are offering Poland co-location of the consortium’s primary node in Warsaw — sovereignty not as withdrawal, but as leadership within the whole.
