## New measure  
**Emergency Bio-Design Governance Framework**  
Launch a rapid legislative and technical process to establish controls on AI-assisted biological design, starting with a moratorium on public deployment of models capable of generating functional pathogen blueprints, paired with a public research programme to close detection gaps.

## In practice  
We are treating the biosecurity warning not as a hypothetical but as a fuse burning toward an unacceptably high-risk future. The release of Kimi K3 variants into open ecosystems, combined with accessible wet-lab automation, means that the threshold for designing dangerous biological agents is no longer in the hands of state bioweapons programmes alone. We cannot afford to learn this lesson through an attack. That is why we are activating Article 16 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU to fast-track a temporary moratorium on the deployment of any model or toolchain that can generate functional pathogen designs — defined by a forthcoming technical specification from the European Commission’s Joint Research Centre. This will be enforced through cloud provider compliance checks and model registration requirements, with penalties under the AI Act’s high-risk framework.

At the same time, we are directing €450 million from Horizon Europe into a new Bio-Secure AI Initiative, co-led by the Paul-Ehrlich-Institut and the Francis Crick Institute, to develop detection tools that can flag AI-generated genetic sequences in screening pipelines. This dual track — restriction now, capability soon — mirrors our approach to cyber, but with greater urgency: the window to prevent harm is closing faster than our institutions move. We are offering model developers a safe harbour: full immunity from liability and access to EU supercomputing for those who voluntarily submit their systems to pre-deployment review. The goal is not to stifle innovation, but to ensure it does not become a delivery mechanism for catastrophe. We are acting under uncertainty, yes — but this is precisely when the commitment to act matters most.
