### The Weight of Response

The EU’s activation of the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield in response to the cyber_major_incident was operationally decisive but politically costly. The rapid integration of model fingerprinting and cross-border data sharing allowed the Joint Attribution Cell to isolate the compromised software dependency within 72 hours, preventing cascading failures in healthcare and transport. ENISA-led red-teaming confirmed the exploit’s AI origin, marking the first time an offensive model was retroactively profiled in real time. This bolstered resilience, as systems degraded rather than collapsed.

However, the mandatory data-sharing mandate triggered fierce resistance. Hungary and the Czech Republic challenged its legality under the NIS2 Directive, arguing that sovereignty over national infrastructure includes control of threat intelligence. The European Court of Justice fast-tracked a preliminary reference, injecting uncertainty into the Shield’s enforcement. While the technical response worked, the political cohesion required to sustain it frayed.

Meanwhile, the Commission’s push to insulate semiconductor strategy from the Taiwan blockade yielded only partial progress. The ASML-led DUV upgrade pathway was validated, but delays pushed the first 2nm-equivalent output to late 2029. No new H100-class chips arrived, and cloud providers began rerouting EU-facing AI workloads through U.S. and Singaporean nodes, deepening dependency. The Tech Sovereignty Package’s energy compromise with Fortum held, but only after slashing initial data centre capacity projections by 30% to preserve winter heating access — a concession that slowed rollout in Sweden and Spain.

Public sentiment dipped further as media coverage focused on job losses in mid-tier IT security firms displaced by automated defences. The narrative of AI as protector clashed with its role as disruptor.

### Capability and Safety Drift

Global AI capability advanced to 56.0, driven by rapid iteration in offensive cyber tools. With no matching safety upgrade in shipped models, ai_safety fell by 3. The open release of Kimi K3 variants pushed openweight_capability to 44.0, widening the safety gap. The EU’s measures, while urgent, were too narrow to offset systemic trends.
