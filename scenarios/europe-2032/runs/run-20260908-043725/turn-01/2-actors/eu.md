## In practice
We are activating Article 13a of the NIS2 Directive to mandate immediate deployment of AI-powered intrusion detection systems at Tier 1 operators—electricity transmission system operators in Germany, France, and Poland; the Port of Rotterdam Authority; and two regional water utilities with cross-border supply chains. ENISA will lead red-teaming exercises every quarter starting Q1 2027, funded from the Digital Europe Programme’s newly reprioritised €1.8bn envelope. The resistance from national regulators, particularly in countries treating grid security as sovereign, is being met with binding mutual aid clauses: if one state fails its audit, others can temporarily assume monitoring authority.

At the same time, we are fast-tracking environmental waivers under the Tech sovereignty package for three InvestAI shortlisted sites—Lithuania, Andalusia, and Northern Sweden—by invoking the new crisis clause in the Trans-European Networks for Energy regulation. This bypasses standard permitting timelines but requires side agreements with local governments on grid-stability guarantees. The Commission is negotiating directly with EDF, Fortum, and Ignitis to co-invest in microgrid buffers that isolate AI facilities from civilian load, addressing public resentment over energy allocation.

The Taiwan blockade has crystallised what was abstract: without control over the full stack—from lithography to electricity to deployed models—we are not making decisions, we are receiving them. That is why, even as we harden defences, we are re-baselining the InvestAI Gigafactories timeline: we now assume no additional non-Chinese H100 equivalents will be available globally before 2030. Our build-out must run on whatever domestic silicon we can secure through ASML leverage and alternative architectures. This shifts the entire rhythm of our industrial planning from procurement to invention.
