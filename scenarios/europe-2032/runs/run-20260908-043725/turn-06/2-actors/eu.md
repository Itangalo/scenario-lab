## In practice
We are acting on the fact that we can no longer afford to be observers in a world shaped by autonomous agents we cannot run, test, or counter. The Joint Cyber Shield Integration is necessary, but it is reactive — it detects and responds. We now need to *anticipate*, and that requires running systems at the edge of what is possible, under our own authority, on our own soil. We are launching the EU Sovereign AI Corps: a new public unit, hosted jointly by DG CONNECT and the Commission’s Crisis Room, with secure access to the most capable models available to the Union — drawn first from allied labs under emergency data-sharing agreements, then from EU-developed systems as they come online. Its mission is threefold: red-team all critical infrastructure using AI-generated attack vectors, simulate bio-design risks in collaboration with the WHO working group, and maintain a live threat library that feeds into ENISA and Europol.

This is not a research programme — it is an operational capability. We are allocating €4.2 billion over three turns, drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU recovery funds, and fast-tracking recruitment of 500 AI engineers and red-team specialists through a new European Talent Shield visa. The first deployment will use Kimi K3-class models on sovereign compute clusters in France and Finland, with strict logging, no external connectivity, and a standing audit mandate from the European Data Protection Supervisor. Germany and the Nordic states are supportive; Poland and Hungary remain cautious, so we are offering them observer status with full transparency and a co-chair role in the ethics board. This is how we begin to close the capability gap — not by waiting for permission, but by building the capacity to act.
