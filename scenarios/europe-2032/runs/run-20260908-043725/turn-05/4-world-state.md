### A Leap in Capability, a Leap in Control

A sudden advance in AI-driven cyber operations reshapes the threat landscape: autonomous agents now design and deploy multi-vector attacks with minimal human oversight, marking a qualitative shift. General AI capability rises to **62.5**, and the operational impact is widely observed. Simultaneously, a major interpretability breakthrough allows developers to predict and certify model behaviour before deployment, significantly improving control. This **safety breakthrough** lands on shipped systems, boosting **ai_safety** to **38.0**.

The WHO’s Global Health Security Summit fast-tracks an international working group on AI and bio-risk, citing early drafts of a potential EU framework as a model. Though voluntary, the move signals growing global alignment on pre-deployment audits, reinforcing the EU’s normative influence.

At home, the EU advances its preparedness agenda. The **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield** is proposed, mandating real-time intrusion detection across energy, water, and ports, with implementation scheduled over the next two turns. This is the EU’s sole new measure this turn. Work has begun on drafting the **Emergency Bio-Design Governance Framework**, including a proposed moratorium on pathogen-capable models and plans for detection research funding, but it remains in the conceptual phase and has not been formally proposed as a measure. Systemic readiness improves modestly, lifting **resilience** to **48.5**.

The Union announces the **EU Sovereign AI Corps**, a new operational unit under the Commission to run high-capability models on sovereign infrastructure for red-teaming and threat simulation. Backed by €4.2 billion, the initiative is named the priority. Germany and the Nordic states endorse the plan; Poland and Hungary accept observer roles but withhold full operational integration, citing data sovereignty concerns. However, due to its scale and cross-institutional demands, full deployment is deferred—organizational design and legal frameworks will take at least one full turn before initial operations can begin. Recruitment will proceed gradually, constrained by existing training pipelines and security clearance bottlenecks, with meaningful staffing levels not expected until turn 7. No immediate operational capacity is created.

Despite technical progress, **eu_ai_sovereignty** remains flat at **16.0**—rising global capability outpaces domestic build-out, and no access event offsets the −1 decay. **openweight_capability** sees a modest rise to **48.5**, reflecting limited diffusion of new safety techniques to open-weight systems, but not a full leap in parallel with frontier models. **public_sentiment** holds at **31.0**, unchanged by recent announcements.

The US 2028 election concludes with a decisive **anti-AI backlash**, electing a retrenchment-aligned administration. The incoming government signals an intention to impose sweeping domestic restrictions upon taking office, though the operational impact on American frontier development will only become clear once the new posture is implemented. The EU’s strategic window may widen—but so does uncertainty in transatlantic coordination.
