## New measure
**Measure:** Sovereign Compute Access Guarantee  
We are launching a binding legal instrument to guarantee EU institutions and critical national operators priority access to any advanced AI compute physically located within the Union, regardless of ownership.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 7  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–18 points by anchoring control over existing and incoming compute; deter foreign hyperscalers from treating EU infrastructure as a mere export node; lay groundwork for future sovereign training runs  
Applies to: All data centres above 100 petaFLOPS of AI-specific compute located in EU member states, including foreign-owned facilities; EU institutions, national governments, designated research labs, and critical infrastructure operators

## Priority
**Priority:** Sovereign Compute Access Guarantee  
This is the first measure that directly severs the link between physical presence and foreign control — turning compute on EU soil into a lever of sovereignty rather than a hostage to external decisions, especially after election_consolidation makes clear that even allied access is now rationed by Washington.

## In practice
We are drafting the Sovereign Compute Access Guarantee under Article 114 TFEU, treating compute as essential infrastructure akin to energy or telecoms. The legal basis will be internal market harmonisation for critical digital services, with a national security override codified in secondary legislation. Our lead negotiators are in Brussels preparing the text for COREPER II, backed by the Commission’s Digital Directorate and the Council’s Legal Service. We are offering foreign hyperscalers a trade: guaranteed market access and streamlined permitting under the Tech Sovereignty Package in exchange for legally binding commitments to reserve 40% of their EU-based compute capacity for sovereign workloads — and to allow real-time monitoring of usage by the EAAD.

Resistance is expected from both US cloud providers and fiscally conservative member states. To break the deadlock, we are pairing the proposal with a revised fiscal envelope: €30bn in loan guarantees from the EIB, conditioned on domestic reinvestment, and a temporary suspension of state-aid limits for compute projects that accept sovereign access terms. The first targets are three existing hyperscale facilities in Ireland, Finland, and Germany where expansion permits are pending — we will make approval contingent on signing. Simultaneously, we are instructing ENISA and the EAAD to begin mapping all high-capacity data centres in the Union, creating the inventory needed to enforce the rule.

On resilience, we are executing the final phase of the Critical Incident Resilience Activation: the AI Threat Fusion Cell has issued its first set of detection signatures, now deployed across 87% of DORA-covered entities. Two cross-border exercises are scheduled for November, and the €8.2bn emergency fund is 64% disbursed, with strict conditions tied to open-source audit trails. Behind this, we are preparing a post-incident revision of the AI Act to classify all AI in essential services as high-risk — but that will wait until political capital stabilises. For now, we hold the line, harden the core, and claim what is on our soil.
