### US Election Signals Harder Access
The 2028 US presidential election concludes with a decisive victory for the candidate who ran on strategic control of AI. The incoming administration treats frontier models as national security assets, tightening export controls and introducing tiered access for foreign partners. The EU, despite its alignment, is now subject to formal review—its access to advanced models will be conditional and rationed. The posture shift sends shockwaves through Brussels.

### Sovereignty Push Meets Resistance
The EU launches the Sovereign Compute Access Guarantee, aiming to assert control over all high-capacity data centres on its soil. Framed as internal market harmonisation, the proposal demands 40% reserved capacity for sovereign workloads in foreign-owned facilities. In return, the Commission offers streamlined permitting and €30bn in EIB loan guarantees. But US hyperscalers push back, calling the terms coercive. Ireland and Germany resist, fearing capital flight. COREPER II delays adoption, demanding legal reassessment.

### Resilience Efforts Gain Ground
The Critical Incident Resilience Activation progresses: detection signatures are deployed across most critical infrastructure, and cross-border drills are conducted. The €8.2bn emergency fund is 64% disbursed, with strict audit conditions. These steps lift resilience, though patchy implementation in mid-tier municipalities limits gains.

### Political Capital Erodes Further
Five large measures remain in flight, including two category 4 sovereignty projects. The new priority adds cost, but no immediate progress is made. Public sentiment dips as media highlight dependency and internal divisions. The EAAD and Emergency Compute Directive finish this turn, but their impact is muted by the larger strategic setback.
