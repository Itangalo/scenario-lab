A wave of insolvencies and abandoned projects sweeps the AI sector. Several major training runs are scrapped, and European plans relying on foreign compute partnerships collapse. The EU proposes the Emergency Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive, aiming to redirect stranded capital and repurpose decommissioned hardware from failed ventures. Legal clarity is sought on energy regulation grounds, with Germany indicating conditional support pending a forthcoming CJEU opinion on temporary override under crisis provisions. France and Sweden express interest in fast-tracking grid access for designated sovereign facilities, but ENTSO-E stresses that integration must be phased to avoid cascading instability. The EIB’s emergency drawdown remains frozen, and without a confirmed funding mechanism, hardware reassignment cannot begin. The Commission establishes a task force to assess feasibility and cost, delaying implementation.

The European AI Assurance Directorate conducts shadow audits, but without access to frontier weights, its findings remain illustrative. The Joint Compute Access Pact continues in negotiation, with partner states expressing cautious interest but no binding commitments.
