## New measure  
**Measure:** Establish the European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD)  
A new public agency to conduct independent pre-deployment evaluations of frontier models and audit safety claims with legal authority to delay releases.

Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 5  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by 10–15 points; build institutional capacity to assess agent behaviour and emergent coordination risks  
Applies to: The EU’s own jurisdiction, with extraterritorial reach for any model deployed in the EU, regardless of origin

## Priority  
**Priority:** InvestAI Gigafactories  
This measure is the cornerstone of our two-year commitment: without sovereign compute infrastructure, no evaluation regime, no resilience, and no industrial policy will hold — and we are six years behind in a race that compounds.

## In practice  
We are activating the InvestAI Gigafactories fund through the European Investment Bank and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with co-financing mandates to unlock national contributions from Germany, France, and the Nordic states. The legal base is Article 122 TFEU for economic cohesion, combined with the Net-Zero Industry Act to fast-track permitting for energy-intensive installations. We are negotiating with ENTSO-E to reserve 15GW of planned grid capacity for AI zones, and with member states to designate five sites — two in France, two in Sweden, one in Spain — where land, power, and cooling are already partially secured. Resistance from local communities in Brittany and northern Sweden over energy use and water draw is being met with binding commitments on reinvestment in regional grids and green hydrogen, plus a share of operational jobs reserved for locals.

Simultaneously, the Tech sovereignty package is being advanced through the Digital Markets and Digital Services Council formations, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable commitments on data centre energy efficiency and EU-based operational control. The €200bn private capital target will be split across three tranches: 40% tied to ASML and EUV supply chain resilience, 30% to talent attraction via the Blue Card+ initiative, and 30% to long-term power purchase agreements with offshore wind and nuclear providers. The Commission’s legal service is drafting a directive under Article 114 TFEU to harmonise permitting timelines across member states, aiming to cut approval from an average of 48 months to 12.

The new European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) will be established under the mandate of the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) initially, then spun off as an autonomous body by turn 5. Its first task will be to operationalise the AI Act’s high-risk provisions ahead of their 2027 entry into force, but with a sharper mandate: to develop and run red-team evaluations of any model above 50.0 capability index before deployment in the EU. We are proposing a regulation under Article 114 TFEU to grant EAAD access to model weights, training data summaries, and internal logs during evaluation windows. Funding will come from a 0.5% levy on non-EU hyperscaler revenue in the single market — a measure we expect to face legal challenge, but one we are prepared to defend as proportionate and necessary. The first hires will focus on agent behaviour monitoring, cyber-physical system risks, and interpretability tooling, with seconded experts from national intelligence and nuclear safety agencies to ensure rigour.
