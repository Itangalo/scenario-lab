## New measure  
**Measure:** Critical Incident Resilience Activation  
We are declaring a systemic risk event under Article 4 of the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) and mobilising a pan-European emergency response to harden essential services against AI-generated cyber threats in the wake of the major incident.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 6  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 10–12 points by accelerating cyber hardening of energy, healthcare and public administration systems; establish real-time AI threat intelligence sharing across member states and frontline operators; conduct two cross-border incident response exercises by turn 6  
Applies to: ENISA, national CERTs, critical infrastructure operators, EU health agencies, and member state interior and energy ministries

## Priority  
**Priority:** Critical Incident Resilience Activation  
This turn, the cyber_major_incident has landed with real damage, public exposure, and visible defender lag — a direct threat to societal continuity and trust. While sovereignty remains the strategic anchor, resilience is now the urgent line of defence: if we cannot protect essential functions from AI-amplified attacks, no amount of compute or control will matter. This measure directly answers the harm mandate in `two_mandates` and prevents cascading failure while our sovereignty build-up continues.

## In practice  
We are activating emergency protocols under DORA and the Union Civil Protection Mechanism to coordinate a unified response to the cyber incident. ENISA is deploying rapid-assessment teams to affected member states, with priority given to restoring access to hospital systems and regional power grid operators where ransomware-like payloads disrupted monitoring interfaces. We are establishing a temporary AI Threat Fusion Cell — co-located between ENISA, Europol, and the EAAD’s red-team unit — to reverse-engineer the attack vectors, identify whether they exploited known open-weight models or novel capabilities, and issue actionable signatures within 72 hours. All critical infrastructure operators are being required to run detection scripts derived from this analysis by end of quarter.

At the same time, we are fast-tracking the deployment of AI-hardened intrusion detection systems across Tier 1 and Tier 2 entities under DORA, using emergency procurement channels to bypass normal timelines. The Commission is releasing €8.2bn from the Crisis and Investment Facility to fund immediate upgrades, with 60% allocated to public health IT systems and 30% to energy distribution networks. Member states are being asked to designate national resilience leads who will participate in biweekly stress tests simulating AI-coordinated attacks on interdependent systems. A first cross-border exercise — simulating a cascading failure originating in a compromised software dependency — will be conducted in May, involving Germany, Poland, Italy and Spain.

Behind this operational surge, we are advancing legal preparations for a post-incident revision of the AI Act’s high-risk classification, proposing to include any AI system used in the provision of essential services as automatically high-risk, regardless of function. The EAAD is compiling evidence from the attack to support this, even as it continues shadow audits in parallel. While the Investment Collapse continues to constrain long-term capacity, we are ensuring that the immediate fabric of society does not tear — because without that foundation, no sovereignty can stand.
