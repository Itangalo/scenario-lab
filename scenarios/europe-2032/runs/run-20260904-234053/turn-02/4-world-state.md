### Frontier Momentum Continues
AI capability advances to 55.0 as leading labs extend autonomous research agents to full-week operation, integrating self-critique and recursive refinement loops. No safety breakthroughs accompany the leap, and assurance remains anchored at legacy levels. The gap between capability and safety widens, raising quiet alarm among technical observers.

### Middle Powers Align
A coalition of middle powers — including the Netherlands, Japan, South Korea, and Canada — formalises a joint framework to coordinate export controls and compute access. The pact leverages collective chokepoints in lithography, chip packaging, and energy infrastructure to deny advanced AI training capacity to actors violating shared safety norms. The agreement stops short of mutual defence, but establishes a joint evaluation cell and real-time intelligence sharing, marking a shift from rhetoric to operational coordination.

### EU Pushes Sovereign Compute
Construction progresses on the InvestAI Gigafactories, with France and Sweden breaking ground under fast-track permitting. Grid integration remains a bottleneck, and local opposition in Brittany delays foundation work by three months. The Just Transition Fund mitigates some friction, but concerns linger over long-term energy draw. The Tech Sovereignty Package sees €84bn in private capital formally committed, below the 50% target, as investors await clarity on U.S. export policy.

### Assurance Efforts Stall
The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) hires core staff and begins drafting evaluation protocols. However, legal challenges loom as hyperscalers contest the extraterritorial levy and access to model weights. No frontier lab has yet submitted to audit, and the first red-team exercise is postponed due to lack of cooperation. Internal assessments warn the agency may become a symbolic body without enforcement teeth.

### Political Capital Holds
Despite mounting pressures, the EU’s political capital rises slightly, buoyed by the coalition’s formation and visible progress on compute infrastructure. The new Joint Compute Access Pact is seen as a strategic win, reinforcing the Union’s leverage without direct confrontation.
