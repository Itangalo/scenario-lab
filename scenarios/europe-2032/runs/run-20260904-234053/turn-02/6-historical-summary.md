AI capability reaches 55.0, driven by extended autonomous operation and self-improving research agents, yet safety assurance remains stagnant, widening the risk gap. A coalition of middle powers—Netherlands, Japan, South Korea, and Canada—establishes the Joint Compute Access Pact, operationalizing export controls and intelligence sharing to restrict unsafe AI development, marking a shift toward coordinated technological governance. Within the EU, InvestAI Gigafactories advance in France and Sweden under fast-track permits, though grid constraints and local opposition in Brittany cause delays; €84bn in private investment is secured, falling short of targets amid uncertainty over U.S. policy. The European AI Assurance Directorate (EAAD) begins staffing and protocol design but faces legal challenges and non-cooperation from frontier labs, raising concerns it will lack enforcement power. Despite these hurdles, EU political capital improves slightly due to diplomatic momentum from the new tech pact and visible infrastructure progress.
