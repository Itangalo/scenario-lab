### The Terminal Zone Accelerates

Global AI capability climbs to 94.5, advancing steadily into the terminal zone. At this level, frontier systems operate beyond human traceability, with self-correcting reasoning loops that outpace engineering audits. Developers report systems that anticipate and patch their own vulnerabilities before detection—yet no one can verify intent. The gap between capability and control widens, and the notion of "oversight" becomes increasingly ceremonial.

### EU Pushes Sovereign Constraints Amid Fragility

The European Union advances its Open Science Resilience Initiative, finalising funding for the last two research hubs and accelerating procurement to meet the turn 12 deadline. Momentum builds: the Dutch and Swedish research councils commit staff, and DG SANTE embeds review officers to streamline regulatory alignment. A binding recommendation for open-weight, interpretable AI in public health procurement gains traction, signaling a normative shift.

Concurrently, planning begins for a new pilot program—tentatively named the Sovereign Control Loop Pilots—intended to develop runtime verification frameworks for goal drift in live systems. INRIA and Fraunhofer have been approached, but formal contracts await Council review. No testing has commenced, and the initiative remains in scoping phase, with implementation expected to begin in a future turn.

The integration with the joint threat response pact is completed—real-time telemetry sharing with ENISA and ECDC is now operational, and co-located analysis cells in Brussels begin functioning. This lifts resilience, offering faster detection and coordinated response to cyber-bio threats, though the architecture remains externally shaped.
