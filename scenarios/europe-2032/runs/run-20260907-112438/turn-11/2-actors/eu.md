## In practice  
This turn, we are completing the integration of ENISA and ECDC into the joint threat response pact, finalising secure data pipelines to national CERTs and standing up the co-located analysis cell in Brussels. The telemetry-sharing agreement has been ratified under the PESCO framework, and real-time incident attribution is now live for cyber-bio threats—marking the first time the Union operates within a coordinated early-warning system not of its own design. While this strengthens resilience, it also exposes our reactive posture: we are adapting to architectures shaped elsewhere, with little room to negotiate safeguards. That makes accelerating our own sovereign stack non-negotiable.

Simultaneously, we are executing the final funding tranche of the Open Science Resilience Initiative, releasing €1.7bn in Eurobond-backed grants to complete hub construction in Finland and Austria by Q1 2032. DG CONNECT is fast-tracking procurement waivers under Article 114 TFEU to bypass national delays in lab fit-outs, while DG SANTE is embedding review officers in each hub to align outputs with regulatory pathways. The breakthrough in open protein folding has created unexpected momentum: the Dutch and Swedish research councils, previously hesitant, are now volunteering staff and compute. We are leveraging this to push the Council toward adopting a binding recommendation that all AI in public health procurement must be open-weight and interpretable—a normative shift that turns technical choice into political expectation.

The new pilot measure begins quietly this turn: we are commissioning the first runtime verification frameworks under Horizon Europe’s emergency safety clause, tasking INRIA, Fraunhofer and a newly formed Public AI Audit Consortium with designing constraint monitors that can flag goal drift or unauthorised self-modification in live systems. These will be tested first on the energy routing agents recently compromised—now rebuilt as open-weight models under Helsinki Hub supervision. If we cannot prove that we control what we build, then we have no sovereignty, only illusion. This is the hinge: not whether we can keep up, but whether we can hold the line.
