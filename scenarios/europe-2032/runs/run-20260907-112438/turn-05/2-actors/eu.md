## In practice
We are acting on the reality that resilience is no longer a policy option but a survival function. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep across regional health systems in three member states — exposed how thin our response layer is: detection came too late, coordination failed, and recovery took weeks. At the same time, the bio_uplift_findings have shifted the risk from hypothetical to imminent. We can no longer rely on national agencies alone — we need binding, operational authority at the EU level.

This turn, we activate the Resilience Surge Protocol in full. Under Article 122 TFEU, we are releasing €9bn in emergency funding for cyber hardening of energy, water, and election infrastructure, with mandatory AI-driven red-teaming every quarter. ENISA’s Rapid Response Corps is being doubled and granted cross-border deployment rights during declared incidents — a legal shift being pushed through the Council under the Integrated Political Crisis Response framework. The BioShield Initiative is launching with pilot nodes in Amsterdam, Lyon, and Milan, co-located with EuroHPC centres, using AI to monitor wastewater and research supply chains for synthetic biology anomalies. And the European Social Shock Facility is being pre-funded with €6bn from redirected InvestEU capital, tied to national reforms in retraining access for displaced logistics and clerical workers.

The Sovereign Agent Monitoring Initiative has now finished — we have the legal right to logs, but not the means to act on them in time. That is why we are launching the European AI Emergency Response Corps: a standing unit of 500 technical operators, embedded in ENISA and ECDC, with pre-authorised access to intervene in compromised systems, isolate runaway agents, and restore control. This will be funded through the new Resilience Bond facility, and its mandate will be ratified by the Council in December. We are not building faster — we are building deeper.
