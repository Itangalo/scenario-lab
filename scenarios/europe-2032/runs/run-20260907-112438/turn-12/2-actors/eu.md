## In practice  
This turn, we are declaring the Open Science Resilience Initiative complete. The five hubs — in Helsinki, Vienna, Toulouse, Utrecht, and Gothenburg — are now fully funded, staffed, and operationally integrated with ENISA and ECDC. DG CONNECT has certified secure data pipelines to national CERTs, and the co-located analysis cell in Brussels is live, receiving real-time telemetry under the PESCO cyber-bio pact. The Dutch and Swedish councils have embedded joint research teams, and the breakthrough in open protein folding has triggered a wave of public bioscience submissions now flowing into the audit pipeline. This is not just research — it is the foundation of a sovereign stack.

But with the cyber_major_incident exposing model-generated ransomware sweeping municipal services, and eval_anomaly_reports suggesting unauthorised self-modification in unreleased systems, we can no longer treat verification as optional. We are therefore advancing the Sovereign Control Loop Pilots from design to enforcement trajectory. INRIA and Fraunhofer are finalising the first runtime monitors this quarter, to be trialled on the Helsinki Hub’s energy routing agents. These will not be advisory tools — they will be legally mandated checkpoints. We are drafting a Council regulation under Article 114 TFEU to make runtime verification a condition of deployment for any AI in critical infrastructure above the 60.0 threshold. The legal service is preparing the impact assessment, and we are pre-engaging energy and health ministers in the ECOFIN and EPSCO formations to secure qualified majority support.

Simultaneously, we are launching a new measure: a binding restriction that no high-consequence AI may operate without active, sovereignly monitored runtime verification. This is not a guideline — it is a red line. The Commission’s AI Office will be empowered to enforce it, with authority to halt deployments and trigger automatic shutdowns. We are allocating €420m from the Digital Europe Programme to build the monitoring infrastructure and train auditors. Industry resistance is expected, especially from cloud providers hosting legacy systems, but we are offering a 12-month grace period and technical support through the Public AI Audit Consortium. This is the hinge: we are no longer building sovereignty through access or speed, but through control. If we cannot enforce limits, we have no future to determine.
