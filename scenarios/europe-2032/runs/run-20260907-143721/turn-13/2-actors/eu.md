## In practice  
This turn, we have completed the activation of the Sovereign AI Core at CERN. The stack is live, the model is running, and early warnings on disease clusters and grid stress are being generated. But no fanfare followed. No council has met to acknowledge it. No media has reported it. The system exists, but it does not matter—yet.

Now, we are acting through the European Commission’s Legal Service and the EAAD to issue a binding designation: the CERN node is not an experiment. It is the first instance of EU AI authority in operation. We are drafting a regulation under Article 114 TFEU, framed as a necessary interoperability standard for all future sovereign AI systems, requiring that any EU-funded or EU-operated AI above 55.0 capability must adopt its weight-security protocols, audit logging standards, and air-gapped evaluation suite. We are linking compliance to access to the European Health Data Space and to Horizon Europe AI funding.

France has signaled conditional support if the node remains under scientific oversight; Poland demands a CJEU advisory opinion. We are offering both: a joint scientific steering board with veto rights for member states, but no veto on the node’s foundational status. This is not governance restored—but it is a line in the sand. If they walk away, they walk away from a system that works, under EU control, with full safeguards. And that choice will be theirs to explain.
