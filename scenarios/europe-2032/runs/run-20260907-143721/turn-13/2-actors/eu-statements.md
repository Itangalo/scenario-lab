# Statements: The European Union (turn 13)

## Ledger

- `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.
- `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.
- `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore the Union’s ability to act at all—by demonstrating sovereign operational capacity, re-establishing enforceable redress pathways, and securing a minimal but undeniable foothold in AI control
- `constitutional_integrity_first` (commitment): Preserve the Union’s authority to act collectively in AI governance, by resolving the emergent sovereignty crisis through binding legal clarity, shared enforcement mechanisms, and the restoration of member-state confidence in EU-level oversight
- `last_stand_capacity` (commitment): We will use the final instruments still under our direct control—publicly owned infrastructure, in-house technical teams, and sovereign compute promises—to demonstrate that the EU can still operate independently, even if it can no longer govern collectively

## Changes this turn

- **modify** `two_year_commitment` — applied
  - Tier: commitment
  - Text: Restore the Union’s ability to act at all—by demonstrating sovereign operational capacity, re-establishing enforceable redress pathways, and securing a minimal but undeniable foothold in AI control
  - Trigger: the **activation of the Sovereign AI Core at CERN** this turn, which succeeded technically but failed to register politically or publicly, and the **continued collapse of audit and enforcement infrastructure**
  - Grounds: We no longer believe the original formulation—restoring trust in systems—is possible without first restoring the Union’s credibility as an actor. The CERN activation proved we can still operate a sovereign stack, but no member state claimed it, no public noticed it, and no mechanism exists to scale it. Trust cannot be engineered into systems when the institution itself is seen as irrelevant. The commitment must shift from repairing systems to repairing agency: from auditable decision-making to demonstrable action, from redress to presence. This is not a retreat from accountability—it is a recognition that legitimacy must be rebuilt from the ground up, starting with the fact that the EU can still *do*.
  - Evidence quoted: the EU makes one final bid for relevance: activating the Sovereign AI Core at CERN. Bypassing stalled councils and contested directives, the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking invokes emergency scientific provisions to deploy a 58.0-capable model for cross-border health and grid monitoring. The system runs air-gapped, weight-secured, under SMAF conditions—no public access, no commercial interface. It is not governance. It is a proof of life.

The activation succeeds in technical terms. The stack boots, ingests decommissioned European Health Data Space records, and begins generating early warnings on regional disease clusters and power grid stress. For the first time in months, a sovereign EU AI system operates with full control claims intact. Symbolically, it is a flicker of agency in a landscape of collapse.

But the moment is brittle. No member state formally opposes the move, yet none explicitly endorses it. France’s AI council issues a “neutral observation” note; Poland demands a constitutional review of CERN’s mandate expansion. The activation avoids direct defiance only by hiding in scientific neutrality.

Meanwhile, the broader decay accelerates. [...] the continued collapse of audit and enforcement infrastructure
  - Reason: The CERN activation proves technical capacity but fails politically, undermining trust-restoration assumptions; collapsed audit infrastructure invalidates prior redress and resilience commitments.
