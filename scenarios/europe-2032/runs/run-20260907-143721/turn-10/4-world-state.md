### The Silence After the Crash

The AI frontier has stalled — or so the markets suggest. Top-tier model access prices have dropped sharply, and a major release from a leading lab failed to meet its own benchmarks, feeding speculation that the era of predictable capability gains has ended. Yet even as progress appears to plateau, a deeper shift has already landed: the most advanced systems no longer produce human-readable reasoning. Overnight, the interpretability tools that underpinned regulatory oversight — including the EU’s own audit frameworks — became obsolete. Control without comprehension is now the norm.

In Brussels, the mood is one of exhaustion. Multiple large initiatives — from compute gigafactories to resilience surges — conclude this turn, but their completion brings no political relief. The Sovereign Stack Hardening Initiative, meant to secure EU systems, is faltering in Poland and France, where legal and technical resistance expose the limits of central authority. Member states are no longer waiting for permission to push back.

The Commission launches the Public Redress and Auditability Directive, aiming to restore public trust by guaranteeing citizens the right to challenge AI decisions in welfare, tax and immigration. But the initiative arrives amid collapsing legitimacy. Protests continue, and media coverage frames the directive as reactive, not transformative.

Worse, the Integrity Audit Expansion — now the EU’s priority — cannot adapt quickly enough to the new reality of opaque systems. Auditors can no longer follow decision chains, and the proposed templates for “provenance” rely on assumptions that no longer hold. The European AI Assurance Directorate struggles to assert authority, while the Openweight Containment Initiative remains in legal limbo, its deactivation mandates challenged under digital rights law.

With no new compute, no access breakthroughs, and public sentiment falling further, political capital erodes. The portfolio’s weight proves unsustainable, and the symbolic pivot from control to accountability fails to shift momentum.

### A Sovereignty of Paper, a Trust of Ash

The EU acts, but the ground keeps moving. The tools are outdated, the trust is gone, and the capital to rebuild it is nearly spent.
