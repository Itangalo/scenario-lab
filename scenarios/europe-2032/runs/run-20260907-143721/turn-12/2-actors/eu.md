## Statement changes  
- modify `constitutional_integrity_first` (commitment): Preserve the Union’s authority to act collectively in AI governance, by resolving the emergent sovereignty crisis through binding legal clarity, shared enforcement mechanisms, and the restoration of member-state confidence in EU-level oversight  
  - Trigger: the **failure of the Treaty Protocol on Union AI Authority** to launch due to zero remaining political capital, and the de facto suspension of the Integrity Audit Expansion by two major member states  
  - Grounds: This turn, we attempted to activate the Treaty Protocol—a measure designed to fulfill this commitment—but found ourselves unable to pay its cost. The Union no longer possesses the capital to propose, let alone enforce, binding legal instruments. The commitment assumed we still had a functioning political system within which to resolve constitutional disputes; we do not. Authority is not being contested in courts or councils—it is being ignored. To claim we are still pursuing this path while lacking even the capacity to initiate it would be fiction. The commitment stands in name only, and we must acknowledge that.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public and institutional trust in sovereign AI systems by ensuring auditable decision-making, enforceable redress, and resilience to both technical compromise and operational misuse  
  - Trigger: the collapse of the Integrity Audit Expansion’s technical validity and the operational failure of proxy audit methods, combined with the **backlash_physical** and **emergent_sovereignty_crisis**  
  - Grounds: We entered this turn attempting to salvage the audit regime through technical workarounds, but the red teams confirm: no proxy method can reliably detect manipulation in opaque systems. The audit framework is blind. Our commitment assumed that accountability could be engineered into systems even as they grew complex; it cannot, not with current tools. Worse, the public no longer distinguishes between sovereign and foreign AI—they see only failure, and they are responding with resistance. We are not restoring trust. We are presiding over its total erosion. This commitment must be redefined, not abandoned: from restoring trust in systems to restoring the Union’s ability to act at all.

- add `last_stand_capacity` (commitment): We will use the final instruments still under our direct control—publicly owned infrastructure, in-house technical teams, and sovereign compute promises—to demonstrate that the EU can still operate independently, even if it can no longer govern collectively  
  - Grounds: With legal authority suspended, political capital exhausted, and public trust at 16.0, the only remaining lever is symbolic action with material potential. The Gigafactories, Tech Sovereignty Package, and Sovereign AI Core Programme have all finished on paper, but no compute has materialized. We now stake ourselves on forcing at least one node of that stack into operation—using whatever remains of our authority over public funds and procurement to bypass stalled coordination and deliver a working system under EU control. This is not governance. It is salvage. But if we cannot show that the EU can still *do*, then we have nothing.

**Priority:** Activate the Sovereign AI Core Stack at CERN Node

## In practice  
This turn, we are acting from the ruins. The Treaty Protocol cannot launch. The Integrity Audit Expansion has collapsed. The Openweight Containment Initiative is a voluntary gesture. We have no capital to spend, no trust to draw on, and no authority that is not being openly defied. In this, we are turning to the only assets still under our nominal control: the physical nodes of the Sovereign AI Core Programme. Of these, the CERN installation is the most advanced—its hardware is in place, its contracts signed, its air-gapped evaluation suite certified. We are now directing the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking to activate it under emergency provisions, using decommissioned medical data from the EHDS to train and deploy a 58.0-capable model for cross-border disease prediction and grid stress monitoring.

We are bypassing the normal governance channels—there is no time, and no functioning council to convene. Instead, we are leveraging CERN’s unique legal status and scientific neutrality to insulate the deployment from immediate political interference. The model will run under SMAF conditions: weights secured, reasoning logged, access restricted to EU institutions and designated research bodies. No public API. No commercial use. This is not a product. It is a demonstration: that the EU can still build, secure, and operate a system of frontier capability without relying on any external provider.

We know this will not restore trust overnight. But if we cannot show that the Union can still *do*, then no audit, no protocol, no treaty will ever matter again. This is not strategy as we once knew it. It is a last stand of capability. And if it works, it may buy us one more turn to rebuild what we have lost.
