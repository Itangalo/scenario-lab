### AI Frontier Crosses Critical Threshold

The global AI capability frontier advances to 60.5 this turn, surpassing the 60.0 threshold where systems begin autonomously completing multi-day professional projects and materially contributing to their own successors. This leap, driven by improved agent coordination and self-training loops, marks a structural shift: the era of purely supervised AI is over. Open-weight models reach 47.0, reflecting incremental public releases and modest improvements in memory-augmented architectures now emerging from academic and community-driven projects. While interest grows in open replication of frontier techniques, full parity remains distant, and the gap with closed systems persists due to disparities in compute access and training infrastructure.

### EU Responds to Systemic Cyber Failure

In response to the hospital ransomware attacks, the EU launches the **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield**, a large category 6 measure aimed at hardening essential services. Drafting progresses under Article 114 TFEU, with ENISA developing red-team scenarios based on observed attack patterns. Fast-track access to InvestAI funding is offered to early adopters, and Germany and Poland signal conditional support. However, no binding directive is adopted this turn, and implementation remains months away. The proposal generates cautious momentum, contributing partially to political capital, but full institutional buy-in has not yet materialized.

### Legal Challenges Undermine Evaluation Efforts

The **European Evaluation Shield** faces a mounting legal challenge from a coalition of frontier developers and foreign governments, contesting its inspection powers as violations of intellectual property rights. Though pilot audits continue confidentially, the agency’s legitimacy is now in legal limbo. A Council resolution reaffirming its mandate fails to quell doubts, and the first public safety report is delayed.

### Sovereignty Gains Offset by Strategic Drift

The completion of **SEAL** secures EU access to critical lithography tools, reinforcing semiconductor autonomy. However, no new sovereignty-building measures finish this turn, and the 1.5-point rise in `ai_capability` imposes a −1 decay under rule 5. With no access-securing event, sovereignty remains flat at 29.0.
