## In practice
This turn, we conclude the EU AI Incident Response Corps’ operational launch cycle. The Czech live-fire exercise confirms that red-team agents can simulate AI-coordinated ransomware attacks with high fidelity, but also exposes gaps in cross-border command protocols — particularly in medical data access and emergency override authority. We finalise integration with the Civil Protection Mechanism and designate ENISA as the permanent coordination node, but defer full activation until member states ratify mutual aid clauses by Q1 2031. The Corps’ framework now stands, but its teeth depend on enforcement follow-through.

Simultaneously, we operationalise the Labour Transition Solidarity Fund, releasing €15bn in initial disbursements through the European Social Fund Plus. Germany, Spain, and Finland activate pilot pacts linking displaced legal researchers to public-sector AI auditing roles, while Finnish universities pilot AI-curriculum transitions into climate resilience planning. Resistance in Poland and Hungary over co-investment rules slows broader rollout, so we offer temporary flexibility on matching ratios for nations below 50.0 resilience, conditioning future access on progress toward digital sovereignty benchmarks. The fund is not a brake on AI, but a stabiliser — ensuring that transformation does not fracture the Union’s social contract.
