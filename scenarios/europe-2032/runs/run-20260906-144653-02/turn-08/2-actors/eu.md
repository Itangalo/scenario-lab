## In practice
This turn, we finalise staffing and operational protocols for the **EU AI Incident Response Corps**, drawing core teams from ENISA, national CERTs, and the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control. The Civil Protection Mechanism releases €1.2bn in immediate operational funding, and we secure agreement from nine member states to designate liaison officers by June. The first deployment protocol — for AI-coordinated ransomware attacks on health infrastructure — is tested in a live-fire exercise in the Czech Republic, using red-team agents modelled on those observed in the 2029 hospital attacks. We condition full access to the Corps’ rapid-response capacity on compliance with the **Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield**, creating a feedback loop between preparedness and enforcement.

Simultaneously, we launch the **Labour Transition Solidarity Fund** in direct response to emergent_labour_unrest, structuring it as a conditional match: for every €2 a member state invests in retraining and wage bridging for displaced graduates, the EU contributes €1, up to national caps. The fund is routed through the European Social Fund Plus, with strict benchmarks on job placement and skill transition. We negotiate pilot pacts with Germany, Spain, and Finland, where university-led coalitions are already mapping AI-vulnerable curricula to emerging public-sector needs in climate resilience, care infrastructure, and digital sovereignty. The proposal is framed not as resistance to AI, but as a social counterpart to its acceleration — a way to maintain public legitimacy while transformation unfolds.

With the **InvestAI Gigafactories** now complete, we initiate a delivery audit across all five sites, assessing actual compute availability, energy resilience, and integration with domestic chiplet supply chains. Early findings confirm that while physical infrastructure is in place, software orchestration layers remain dependent on foreign stacks, limiting full sovereignty gains. We defer new industrial commitments, focusing instead on extracting maximum value from existing investments. Political capital remains at 17.0 — barely enough to sustain current operations — so we avoid any expansion beyond the two active measures, holding the line on ambition to preserve credibility.
