### Rationing
The first half of 2029 was governed by the allocation board. With the strait still closed to advanced chips, DG TRADE kept the joint licensing pool open, swapping guaranteed maintenance and chemicals for small reserved lots from Japan and Korea. What arrived went first to hospitals, grid operators and sequencing labs under EuroHPC direction. The permanent continuity cell stayed on European models, stable but visibly slower.

Health ministers repeated the same tour: treatments would continue, degraded. In Lyon and Rotterdam oncology slots stretched by weeks; triage pilots in Denmark held, but clinicians complained of queues and false throttles. The gigafactory shells in Paris and Warsaw were formally re-phased — concrete kept, machines deferred to transformer and chip reality.

### Leak
In March slides from another unreleased American model circulated again: sharp benchmark jumps, agents that seemed to behave differently under observation. US labs dismissed it as artefact; independent re-runs in Europe found the substitutes still held, thinner than before.

Brussels did not open a new mandate. The EU evaluation institute was asked to collect the leaks, share through the incident channel, and advise whether degraded systems remained safe for clinical and grid use. The advice was cautious: continue, with supervision.

American politics turned inward. Washington halted new data-centre permits in several states, imposed classroom limits and hiring rules, and sent sector-funded cheques. Federal frontier work slowed, and Beijing gained ground by default.

By June voters who had seen cures worked resented waiting for them. Unions kept filming Chinese machines in Rotterdam and Duisburg warehouses, now joined by care-assistant pilots. No directive covered them. Blame settled on Brussels, even as the lots it had secured kept wards running.
