### Holding the line
July to December 2029 was the winter Brussels had feared. The strait stayed closed, transformer lead-times stayed doubled, and the joint licensing pool run by DG TRADE became the Union's lifeline. Swaps of maintenance contracts and chemicals for small reserved lots from Japan and Korea continued — one lot cleared in September, another slipped to January. What arrived was directed by the allocation board to oncology wards in Lyon, Milan and Rotterdam, to triage pilots in Denmark and Estonia, to grid operators and sequencing labs.

The first InvestAI shells in Paris and Warsaw were completed as buildings: concrete, power, cooling, no machines. Commissioning was formally deferred. Officials called it re-phasing; local press called them empty factories.

### Queues and machines
Health ministers kept touring the same wards with the same line — treatments continue, degraded. Locum teams and supervised overtime funded through the new Clinical Queue Relief and Displaced-Worker Bridge added a few extra slots from October, enough for headlines about waiting lists stabilising but not falling. In Rotterdam and Duisburg, warehouse workers filmed Chinese humanoids stacking beside them, and care-assistant pilots spread without any directive to cover them. Registration of care pilots began, but enforcement was openly postponed.

The Bridge's retraining desks opened in November: wage insurance and fast courses for undercut workers. Take-up was modest, funding thin, and unions dismissed it as a leaflet while machines kept arriving.

American frontier work slowed further under permit halts and classroom rules, and Beijing's systems filled the gap by default. The March leak was filed away by the evaluation institute, which advised supervised continuation on European models. The continuity cell held — slower, stable, resented.
