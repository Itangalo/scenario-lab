## In practice
We are acting on a single, urgent truth: sovereignty without access is fiction. The European AI Emergency Response Corps has now formally completed its development phase — it is staffed, trained, and technically integrated — but its operational teeth remain untested, and its access to sovereign compute is still contingent. That changes this turn. We are invoking Article 122a TFEU and the Resilience Bond framework to issue the Emergency Sovereign Compute Activation Directive, which mandates that 40% of all EuroHPC and national sovereign compute clusters — including LUMI, Leonardo, and upcoming InvestAI nodes — reserve capacity exclusively for AI safety evaluation, crisis simulation, and emergency response operations. This allocation will be legally binding, enforced through the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper obligations and the AI Act’s high-risk system provisions, and prioritised over all non-essential commercial contracts, including those with foreign hyperscalers.

The directive is not a call for new infrastructure — that would take years — but a reallocation of what we already fund and claim to control. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking will issue compliance guidelines within 60 days, and national regulators will audit usage logs quarterly. We are fast-tracking access for the AI Office, ENISA red teams, and the newly operational Emergency Response Corps, ensuring that when an unexplained agent behaviour emerges, we have the compute to replicate, probe, and contain it — not just observe it. Resistance is expected from commercial cloud providers and some member states benefiting from foreign investment, so we are offering compensation through expanded access to open-weight safety tooling and preferential placement in future InvestAI expansions. This is not austerity — it is triage. We are securing the minimum viable foundation for sovereign agency: the ability to see, test, and act on AI threats without asking permission.
