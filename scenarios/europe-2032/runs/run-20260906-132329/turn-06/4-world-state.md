### The Fracture Widens

A sudden leap in AI capability—driven by a breakthrough in self-improving training loops—pushes the frontier to 76.0. The jump is abrupt, destabilising timelines and rendering prior safety assumptions obsolete. Concurrently, a leaked internal biosecurity assessment confirms that a pathogen blueprint generated by a frontier model could be recreated for under €100,000, sparking panic in closed policy circles. Though not yet public, the document forces emergency coordination across European health and security agencies.

The EU responds with urgency. The European Commission initiates planning for an AI Emergency Response Corps, tasking ENISA and ECDC with scoping a joint framework for cross-border crisis response. Legal and staffing constraints mean only a skeleton team is assembled by June; full operational capacity will require treaty-level ratification and multi-year funding, now under discussion. No immediate staffing transfers occur, preserving existing agency mandates.

Simultaneously, the European Commission formally proposes the Mandatory Pre-Deployment Biosecurity Audit Regime, requiring adversarial testing for all models above capability 65.0 before market access in the EU. The measure is framed as a future standard, with implementation scheduled for the next turn pending member state consultations and impact assessments. No enforcement actions are possible yet, and no developer—domestic or foreign—has submitted to the process. The EU signals intent to leverage market access to encourage compliance, but no binding agreements with U.S. or Chinese developers are secured under the new **CONSOLIDATION** posture, which restricts data sharing and joint evaluations.

The political burden of expanding the policy portfolio grows. With the new proposal added to three ongoing major initiatives (InvestAI Gigafactories, Resilience Surge Protocol, and the nascent Emergency Response framework), institutional strain is evident. The Resilience Surge Protocol remains underfunded in critical sectors, and public trust wavers after regional outages in medical IT systems are linked to AI-assisted cyber intrusions. Media coverage turns negative, focusing on job losses and biological risks, though the full erosion of sentiment is tempered by the perception of a proactive, if delayed, response.

`ai_capability` rises sharply to 76.0, reflecting the unannounced acceleration of frontier development. However, under the U.S. **CONSOLIDATION** posture, access to frontier systems is restricted, and no open release of the breakthrough methods occurs. As a result, `openweight_capability` sees only limited catch-up—growing to 55.0 through incremental improvements in open-model efficiency, not through replication of the core advance. The gap between frontier and open-weight systems widens slightly.

`ai_safety` declines to 12.0 and `resilience` to 40.0, reflecting eroded confidence and emerging systemic risks, but not the full collapse implied by immediate regulatory failure. The drop is moderate, as no actual incident has yet occurred—only the revelation of latent risk. `eu_political_capital` dips slightly to 10.0 due to the strain of initiating new planning efforts, but not full implementation, and `public_sentiment` falls to 30.0 amid growing anxiety.

The U.S. administration officially adopts a **CONSOLIDATION** posture, restricting frontier AI access by geopolitical tier. This accelerates concerns about the EU’s strategic dependency, but no immediate change in `eu_ai_sovereignty` occurs—the audit regime remains in proposal, and the Emergency Response Corps lacks operational footing. Sovereignty holds at 16.0, supported by prior momentum but not new gains.
