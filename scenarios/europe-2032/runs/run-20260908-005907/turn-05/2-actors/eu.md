## In practice
We are treating the ransomware attacks not as isolated failures but as symptoms of a deeper collapse in control: ai_capability at 58.5, openweight_capability at 46.5, and ai_safety stuck at 26.0 mean the offensive tools are now widely distributed, while our defences remain reactive and fragmented. The Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive has delivered three viable sites and set the precedent for fast-tracked compute deployment, but it did not solve the core problem — we still cannot *govern* the systems we are building. The election_retrenchment in the US confirms that reliance on external partners is no longer tenable: the window to build our own control infrastructure is open, and we must act.

This turn, we launch the European AI Safety and Control Authority (EASCA), a new agency modelled on the EASA but with broader powers: pre-release evaluation of any model above 50.0 capability, real-time monitoring of training runs and agent behaviour, authority to halt deployments, and direct access to model weights and logs. We are proposing it under Article 114 TFEU (internal market harmonisation), with a Regulation to be adopted in the Council by qualified majority. The agency will be headquartered in The Hague, co-located with Europol and ENISA for operational synergy, and will begin with €6.2bn in initial funding from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe surpluses. We are appointing a tripartite board — Commission, Member State, and independent technical experts — to ensure legitimacy, and we are offering developers a fast-track compliance pathway in exchange for early cooperation.

Resistance will come from both sides: frontier labs will resist intrusive oversight, and some member states will resist ceding sovereignty to a new body. To overcome this, we are pairing EASCA with a binding commitment to reinvest 30% of its findings into public AI research (via category 5), and we are guaranteeing that audits will be conducted under strict confidentiality to protect IP. This is not about slowing innovation — it is about ensuring it does not happen in the dark. Without this, every data centre we build, every euro we invest, becomes another vector for risk. EASCA is the missing piece: the Union will not just host AI, it will *understand* it, *control* it, and *answer* for it.
