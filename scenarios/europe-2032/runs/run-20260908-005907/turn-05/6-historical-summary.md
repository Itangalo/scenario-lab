Global AI capability reaches 60.0 as closed-lab AI agents achieve autonomy in multi-day professional tasks and begin self-improvement, widening the control gap amid stagnant safety progress. In response to escalating cyber threats and governance challenges, the EU proposes the European AI Safety and Control Authority (EASCA), a supranational regulator with intervention powers over high-capability AI, sparking debate over innovation, sovereignty, and legal basis. The Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive concludes with only three operational data centres, falling short of strategic goals due to delays, legal challenges, and a major hyperscaler's withdrawal, underscoring ongoing structural dependencies. Cyber resilience declines further as attacks persist and insurers retreat from critical sectors, while accelerating labour displacement fuels public distrust. The US elects an anti-AI administration, signaling strict domestic controls and ending hopes for transatlantic alignment, leaving the EU to navigate sovereignty and security alone amid growing uncertainty.
