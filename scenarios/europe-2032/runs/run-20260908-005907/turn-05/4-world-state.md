### A Turning Point in Capability

Global AI capability reaches 60.0 as closed laboratories deploy agents capable of completing multi-day professional projects end to end. These systems now materially contribute to their own improvement, marking a qualitative shift. Despite this, no corresponding safety improvements land on deployed systems—interpretability remains shallow, oversight fragmented, and the control gap widens.

The EU responds to the recent cyberattacks and stagnant safety by launching the European AI Safety and Control Authority (EASCA), a supranational body with authority to evaluate, monitor, and intervene in high-capability AI development. The proposal, framed as essential to the Union’s renewed two-year commitment to full-stack sovereignty, triggers fierce debate. Frontier developers warn of stifled innovation; several member states resist ceding regulatory authority. Legal challenges emerge over the use of Article 114 TFEU for such sweeping powers.

### Sovereignty Under Strain

The Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive officially concludes, delivering three operational data centres. However, delays, legal disputes, and one hyperscaler’s exit limit its impact. With ai_capability rising by 1.5, eu_ai_sovereignty declines further due to persistent structural dependence.

Resilience erodes slightly as repeated cyberattacks expose systemic fragility, and the emergent cyber insurance crisis deepens—insurers withdraw coverage from critical public services, citing unmanageable risk. Labour displacement accelerates, feeding public unease.

### A Shifting Transatlantic Landscape

The US 2028 election results are confirmed: a decisive victory for the anti-AI platform. The incoming administration signals sweeping domestic restrictions, including data centre moratoriums and sector-wide hiring bans. While this reduces immediate competitive pressure, it also severs expectations of transatlantic coordination. The EU can no longer rely on American technological leadership—or stability.

Public sentiment dips as fears of uncontrolled AI and job losses dominate discourse. The EASCA proposal, though framed as protective, is met with scepticism.
