### The Core Under Pressure

The EU launches “Secure the Core,” a high-stakes push to deploy sovereign AI in healthcare, emergency response, and judicial systems by mid-2031. Repurposing 40% of InvestAI Gigafactory capacity, the plan aims to break dependency on foreign models. However, integration bottlenecks persist, and member state resistance flares—particularly in nations with deep contractual ties to U.S. providers. Legal challenges delay the reallocation of compute, and early deployment timelines slip.

The newly operational EU AI Safety and Control Authority (EASCA) certifies its first open-weight model for limited judicial use, marking a symbolic win. Still, audits reveal opaque reasoning patterns, and the authority lacks enforcement teeth outside pilot zones. Meanwhile, a major AI-driven cyberattack—using model-generated exploits—cripples emergency dispatch systems in three countries. Response is slow, underscoring that resilience remains brittle despite ongoing hardening efforts.

Public trust wanes further after a court rules that an AI welfare system systematically disadvantaged rural claimants, amplifying backlash against public-sector AI. Regional rollouts stall, and protests target data centre expansions. The Commission insists the crisis demands urgency, but critics argue the Union is building sovereign systems on flawed foundations.

In Washington, the RETRENCHMENT posture holds. U.S. labs slow frontier progress due to internal regulatory gridlock, briefly easing pressure on the capability front. Yet this does not translate into access: export controls remain tight, and no new cooperation emerges. The EU’s leverage is strained—its market is large, but its capacity to act is fraying.

### Sovereignty, Step by Step

Despite setbacks, the completion of EASCA delivers a modest safety boost, and the resilience initiative progresses. Sovereignty inches upward as fallback planning gains traction, though the core stack remains aspirational. Political capital erodes—burdened by three large measures and a priority—while public sentiment dips on fresh scandal and disruption.
