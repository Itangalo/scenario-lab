## New measure

**Secure the Core: Sovereign AI for Critical Functions**  
We launch a binding programme to deploy EU-controlled, auditable AI systems in healthcare, emergency response, and judicial support within 18 months, using repurposed InvestAI capacity and open-weight breakthroughs.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 11  
`Targeted effect:` eu_ai_sovereignty +12 (by establishing sovereign fallback and primary systems in critical domains), ai_safety +5 (via enforceable transparency and control), resilience +3 (through assured continuity), eu_political_capital -4 (due to speed, cost, and resistance from member states with entrenched foreign dependencies)  
`Applies to:` EU member states, European Commission, ENISA, EuroHPC JU, InvestAI Gigafactories, national health and justice ministries

## Priority

**Secure the Core: Sovereign AI for Critical Functions** — because the *cyber_major_incident* and the *eu_frontier_access_denied* shock together prove that resilience and safety are now inseparable from sovereignty: we cannot defend what we do not control, and we cannot trust what we cannot inspect. This is no longer about market position or long-term ambition — it is about ensuring that when the next cutoff comes, or the next attack lands, our hospitals still function, our courts still operate, and our emergency lines still answer. Only a sovereign stack, built fast and grounded in EU law and infrastructure, can guarantee that.

## In practice

We are acting on a dual failure: the world’s most capable AI has been weaponised not through code, but through access, and our own defences are blind to both. The cyberattack on public services — enabled by model-generated exploits — and the sudden withdrawal of frontier AI from critical systems reveal the same truth: we are operating on borrowed capability and borrowed time. The Emergency Resilience Mobilisation Initiative is helping, but it hardens the shell while the core remains foreign-controlled. That is not resilience. That is managed dependence.

So this turn, we move from audit to action. The “Reclaim Access & Audit the Black Box” initiative laid the legal and political groundwork — but audits alone do not run hospitals. Now, we build. Under Article 16 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act enforcement framework, we direct the European Commission, in coordination with ENISA and EuroHPC, to operationalise a sovereign AI stack for critical functions by Q3 2031. We repurpose 40% of the completed InvestAI Gigafactories’ capacity to host and train EU-controlled models, prioritising open-weight systems that meet audibility and fallback standards. We task the newly established AI Safety and Control Authority — now operational — with certifying these models before deployment. Funding of €14.3bn is drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with disbursement tied to national compliance and integration timelines.

Resistance is expected: some member states have deep contractual ties to foreign providers and fear disruption. To them, we offer transition support and shared liability during the switchover. To the public, we offer transparency: every system will be publicly registered, its decision logic inspectable, its fallback mode tested annually. This is not a retreat from global cooperation — it is the price of being able to cooperate as an equal. We will not outsource survival. The core of our public realm must be under our own authority, built on our own soil, and answerable to our own citizens. The age of dependency ends now.
