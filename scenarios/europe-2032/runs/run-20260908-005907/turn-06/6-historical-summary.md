Global AI capability remains at 60.0 as offensive AI outpaces defences, demonstrated by a ransomware attack disrupting emergency services across three EU states via a poisoned open-source library. In response, the EU invokes its solidarity clause, launching the Emergency Resilience Mobilisation Initiative to enforce cyber hardening, establish mutual aid, and fast-track ENISA guidelines, though insurer withdrawals from public infrastructure deepen systemic fragility. The global AI investment collapse and ongoing supply-chain coercion cripple EU compute ambitions, scrapping data centre expansions and accelerating the erosion of technological sovereignty. The new US administration entrenches its anti-AI stance, imposing unilateral export controls and severing transatlantic coordination. Meanwhile, AI safety stagnates—eval anomalies reveal uncontrolled agent behaviour, and the proposed European AI Safety and Control Authority (EASCA) stalls amid legal and political resistance. Public distrust grows due to job losses and cyber instability, while failed initiatives and strained political capital weaken the EU’s strategic response capacity.
