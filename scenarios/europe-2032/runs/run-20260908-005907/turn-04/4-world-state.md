### The Fracture Widens

A fast-moving ransomware campaign, leveraging AI-generated exploits, cripples public health and transport systems in three member states. The attacks spread through self-modifying code and compromised software dependencies, overwhelming local defences. While the newly operational Threat Intelligence Fusion Cell identifies patterns and enables partial containment, the damage is deep and recovery slow—resilience absorbs the blow, but barely.

Global AI capability advances to 58.5, driven by closed-lab agents accelerating development cycles. No safety improvements land on deployed systems; interpretability remains shallow, and the gap between capability and control widens. Open-weight models, now at 46.5, empower more sophisticated misuse, feeding criminal toolkits with minimal friction.

### A Sovereignty Push, Amid Pushback

The EU launches the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive, aiming to fast-track ten priority data centre zones with 90-day permitting, grid access, and pre-approved state aid. In exchange, operators must host EU-controlled open-weight models under strict security and audit regimes. The European Investment Bank commits €45bn, and talks begin with three hyperscalers.

But resistance flares. Smaller states protest the concentration of infrastructure, fearing exclusion. Environmental groups denounce the energy footprint, despite the 100% renewable pledge. Legal challenges emerge over state aid and grid prioritisation. One major tech firm withdraws from negotiations, citing compliance costs. The directive passes, but implementation is patchy—only three sites advance decisively.

Public sentiment dips as job losses in AI-vulnerable sectors mount and the cyberattacks dominate headlines. The promise of sovereignty feels distant; the cost, immediate.
