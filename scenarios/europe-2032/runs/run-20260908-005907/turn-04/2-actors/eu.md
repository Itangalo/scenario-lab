## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_major_incident — a fast-moving, polymorphic ransomware sweep across public health and transport services in three member states — not as a one-off but as proof that openweight_capability at 44.5 is now weaponised at scale and beyond containment. The Offensive AI Monitoring and Containment Initiative has delivered its core capability on time: the Threat Intelligence Fusion Cell is live, ingesting telemetry from 80% of NIS2 entities, and red-teaming AI has already flagged two novel attack vectors now being patched. But detection is not deterrence. We are still reacting, and we are still dependent.

So this turn, we pivot to build sovereign capacity that cannot be switched off. The Sovereign Compute Acceleration Directive will designate up to ten priority zones across the Union — existing or planned data centre clusters in Finland, Ireland, and Romania — where permitting for AI-scale compute will be capped at 90 days, grid connection fast-tracked, and state aid pre-approved under revised IPCEI rules. In return, operators must host EU-controlled open-weight models under a new security regime: air-gapped training clusters, mandatory weight encryption at rest, and real-time audit access for ENISA-certified inspectors. We are offering the European Investment Bank a €45bn fast-disbursing tranche to co-fund the first wave, and we are negotiating with three major hyperscalers to anchor their next-gen clusters under this regime — not as foreign providers, but as joint stewards of EU strategic capacity.

We are using Article 118 TFEU (energy infrastructure) and Article 107(3)(b) (state aid for important projects of common European interest) as legal bases. Resistance is expected from smaller member states fearing exclusion and from environmental groups concerned about energy use, so we are pairing this with a binding commitment to 100% renewable sourcing by 2030 and a solidarity mechanism to distribute compute access to all member states via the Digital Europe Programme. This is not just about chips and power — it is about decision space. If the frontier keeps moving and we remain tenants, not owners, of the systems that shape our economy and security, then eu_ai_sovereignty will continue to erode. We are acting now to ensure that by 2030, the EU can run, inspect, and control systems of at least 60.0 capability — on its own soil, under its own law, and without asking permission.
