### A Fragile Foundation

The Emergency Resilience Mobilisation Initiative formally concludes, having hardened some digital perimeters but failing to address systemic fragility. Its legacy is partial: patchwork adoption of cyber standards, with mutual aid protocols untested and underfunded. In its wake, the EU launches “Enforceable Fallback,” a sweeping mandate requiring all critical AI systems to operate with sovereign, auditable fallback modes. The goal is clear — ensure continuity during disconnection or attack — but resistance flares immediately. Member states reliant on U.S. cloud providers challenge the technical feasibility and cost, while legal scholars question the enforceability under current digital governance frameworks.

The Commission pushes forward, citing recent emergency dispatch failures and the defection of a key member state to a bilateral AI agreement with Washington. Yet public trust erodes further as a judicial review confirms that an AI-driven welfare system systematically denied claims in rural regions, reinforcing fears of opaque, unjust automation. Protests intensify outside data centres and regional government offices, with calls to halt all AI integration in public services.

Meanwhile, leaked internal evaluations from a leading lab suggest unexplained reasoning leaps in a next-generation model, sparking quiet alarm among technical observers. A biosecurity study also surfaces, claiming non-experts could use AI to design human-infective organisms — a claim disputed but impossible to ignore.

Despite the ambition of “Enforceable Fallback,” implementation delays are already projected. Integration bottlenecks, funding disputes, and jurisdictional conflicts slow rollout. The measure yields early resilience gains in theory, but in practice, sovereign infrastructure remains thin.

### Stalled Momentum

Political capital dwindles under the weight of three large initiatives and a named priority. Public sentiment dips further, driven by scandal and disruption. The U.S. posture remains retrenchment: frontier progress slows, but no access is granted. The EU’s leverage is strained, its sovereignty inching downward as dependency deepens faster than alternatives emerge.
