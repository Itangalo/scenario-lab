## New measure
**Enforceable Fallback: Mandate Sovereign Redundancy in All Critical AI-Dependent Systems**  
We require that all AI systems supporting essential public functions operate with a sovereign, auditable fallback mode — physically isolated, continuously tested, and capable of sustaining core operations for 72 hours without external models or connectivity.

## Priority
**Enforceable Fallback: Mandate Sovereign Redundancy in All Critical AI-Dependent Systems** — because the *cyber_major_incident* and *member_state_defection* prove that resilience cannot be layered on after dependency is entrenched: we must now hardwire the capacity to function without foreign models into every critical system, not as a backup plan, but as a legal and operational baseline.

## In practice
We are acting on a cascading failure: the emergency dispatch outage revealed that even when systems are nominally functional, AI-generated exploits can paralyze response, while the sudden loss of access to foreign models in welfare and health systems exposed our lack of operational independence. The Emergency Resilience Mobilisation Initiative ends this turn, but its work is not done — hardening perimeters is not enough if the core logic of our systems collapses when disconnected. That is why we now mandate enforceable fallback: not just redundancy, but sovereign redundancy.

Under Article 16 TFEU and the revised DORA framework, we direct the Commission, in coordination with ENISA and national regulators, to issue binding technical standards by Q1 2031 requiring all AI systems in healthcare, emergency response, energy, and judicial administration to operate with a sovereign, EU-controlled fallback mode. This mode must be physically isolated from external models, run on EuroHPC or InvestAI-hosted infrastructure, and be capable of sustaining core functions for 72 hours during disconnection, attack, or denial of service. Annual live-fire exercises will test continuity, with results published and failures triggering mandatory remediation. Funding of €6.1bn is drawn from the Digital Europe Programme and REACT-EU, with disbursement tied to national compliance.

Resistance will come from member states with deep integration into U.S. cloud stacks and from providers whose contracts assume uninterrupted access. To them, we offer phased compliance — two years to implement — and shared access to the open-weight sovereign models being developed under the Secure the Core initiative. To the public, we offer clarity: no system that cannot prove it can run without foreign AI will be permitted to manage life-critical functions. This is not isolation — it is insurance. We will not build resilience on borrowed capability. The right to function must never depend on permission.
