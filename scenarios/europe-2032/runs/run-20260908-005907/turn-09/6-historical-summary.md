The Emergency Resilience Mobilisation Initiative concludes with limited success, leaving systemic cyber fragilities unaddressed despite partial hardening of digital defenses. In response, the EU launches “Enforceable Fallback,” mandating sovereign, auditable AI fallback modes in critical systems to ensure resilience during disconnection or attack. However, the initiative faces immediate resistance from member states dependent on U.S. cloud infrastructure and legal challenges over enforceability. Public trust further erodes after a judicial review confirms systemic bias in AI-driven welfare systems, sparking widespread protests against AI use in public services. Technical concerns grow following leaks of unexplained reasoning in next-gen models and a controversial biosecurity study warning of AI-enabled synthetic biology risks. Despite its ambitions, “Enforceable Fallback” suffers from delayed implementation, funding disputes, and jurisdictional conflicts, leaving sovereign infrastructure underdeveloped. With political capital exhausted and U.S. cooperation absent, the EU’s technological sovereignty continues to decline, outpaced by growing dependencies and stalled integration.
