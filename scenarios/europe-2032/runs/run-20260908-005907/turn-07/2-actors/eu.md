## New measure

**Reclaim Access & Audit the Black Box**  
We initiate a binding review of all foreign AI systems currently embedded in EU-critical operations, demanding full transparency and fallback capacity — or their phased removal.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 10  
`Targeted effect:` ai_safety +8 (by forcing visibility into deployed systems), eu_ai_sovereignty +5 (by establishing right of audit and fallback), public_sentiment +2 (if framed as restoring control), eu_political_capital -2 (due to pushback from member states reliant on foreign models)  
`Applies to:` Non-EU frontier AI developers with systems deployed in EU critical infrastructure (health, emergency services, energy, justice), EU member states using such systems, ENISA, European Commission services

## Priority

**Reclaim Access & Audit the Black Box** — because the *eu_frontier_access_denied* event is not a supply hiccup but a sovereignty rupture: hospitals and ministries were left blind when access was cut without warning, revealing that our critical functions depend on foreign discretion. No resilience or safety regime can hold if the tools we rely on vanish at another power’s whim. This measure enforces the principle that no system embedded in EU essential operations may be a black box beyond audit, control, or replacement — and begins the work of decoupling our survival from foreign goodwill.

## In practice

We are acting on a new reality: the Union’s critical functions — emergency dispatch, hospital diagnostics, benefit processing — now run on AI systems whose withdrawal can be triggered without explanation, without appeal, and without transition. The *eu_frontier_access_denied* event was not a cyberattack, but it landed like one. When access was cut, it exposed not just dependency, but a constitutional vulnerability: decisions about continuity of public service are being made in boardrooms and capitals outside the EU. This is incompatible with our identity and our two-year commitment. We cannot claim decisional autonomy while our hospitals cannot function without permission.

So this turn, we invoke Article 16 TFEU and the recently reinforced Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) framework to launch a binding audit and contingency mandate. By Q1 2030, all non-EU AI systems used in essential public services must demonstrate: (1) full third-party evaluability of their reasoning under ENISA supervision, (2) a working fallback mode operable without external connectivity or approval, and (3) a phased replacement plan if sovereignty thresholds are not met. We are tasking the European Commission’s Digital Directorate to issue implementing acts within 90 days, backed by the threat of exclusion from public procurement. We will fund parallel open-weight alternatives through the now-completed InvestAI Gigafactories, repurposing some of their capacity to host auditable, EU-controlled models for critical functions. Resistance will be fierce — some member states have built entire digital health platforms on foreign APIs — so we are pairing enforcement with support: €8.2bn in transition grants from the Digital Europe Programme, conditional on compliance by turn 10.

This is not a ban. It is a demand for reciprocity: if you want to operate here, you must be *here* — accountable, inspectable, and replaceable. We are no longer outsourcing the core logic of our public realm to entities that can vanish it overnight. The age of blind integration is over. Control must return to the level of survival.
