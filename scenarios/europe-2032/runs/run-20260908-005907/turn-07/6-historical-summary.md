Global AI capability rises amid stagnant safety, as uncontrolled agent behavior and a ransomware attack on EU emergency services via a poisoned open-source library expose systemic vulnerabilities. The EU invokes its solidarity clause, launching the Emergency Resilience Mobilisation Initiative to strengthen cyber defenses and mutual aid, while advancing ENISA guidelines—though insurer withdrawals deepen infrastructure fragility. A sudden cutoff of access to advanced foreign AI models disrupts critical systems in healthcare, emergency response, and justice, triggering the "Reclaim Access & Audit the Black Box" initiative: a €8.2bn-backed push for transparency, fallback plans, and replacement of non-EU AI in critical functions under DORA and Article 16 TFEU. Legal challenges and lack of leverage delay enforcement, while member state resistance and integration costs hinder compliance. Public trust erodes further due to an AI welfare bias scandal, pausing regional adoption. Domestic compute efforts are undermined by ASML service halts on key equipment, compounding supply-chain-driven erosion of technological sovereignty. Despite new InvestAI Gigafactories adding capacity, impact is delayed by integration bottlenecks and frontier access denial. Open-weight AI grows modestly through spillover effects, but without breakthrough progress. Political capital declines under the strain of new initiatives amid backlash and fragmentation, while US export controls and anti-AI policies deepen transatlantic rifts, leaving EU strategic ambitions weakened and sovereignty in decline.
