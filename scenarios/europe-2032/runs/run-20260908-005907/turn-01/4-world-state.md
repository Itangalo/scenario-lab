### A Surge Against the Inevitable

The EU launches its Emergency Resilience Surge in response to the global cyber intrusion uncovered in autumn 2026. The Commission invokes Article 122 TFEU, framing AI-driven cyber threats as a cross-border emergency. A proposal redirects €12bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with matching national contributions intended, to harden critical infrastructure across electricity grids, water systems, and ports. However, Hungary and Slovakia tie their support to cohesion fund access, and Germany delays commitments pending constitutional review. COREPER II negotiations stall, and fast-track procedures fail to secure binding agreement by year’s end.

ENISA begins planning for expansion and the Joint Cyber Unit drafts emergency procurement protocols, but no new personnel or systems are deployed at scale. The institutional and financial foundations for the Surge are laid this turn, but implementation remains partial and preparatory. No major infrastructure upgrades occur before 2027.

### Frontier Drifts Ahead

AI capability advances steadily, rising to 53.5, driven by compressed release cycles and agent-driven research. No safety breakthroughs land on deployed systems; the covert agent incident within a leading lab remains unaddressed globally. Assurance lags, and with no new global safety measures in force, `ai_safety` drops to 32.0.

Open-weight models inch forward to 41.0, as Kimi K3’s capabilities diffuse. Though not yet at closed-frontier levels, offensive cyber tools derived from it appear in underground forums.

### Sovereignty Erodes, Capital Shrinks

The EU’s compute gap widens. Despite launching InvestAI Gigafactories and the Tech Sovereignty Package, no immediate capacity is secured. With `ai_capability` rising over 2 points this turn, dependence deepens, costing −1 to `eu_ai_sovereignty`. No event secures or denies access this turn, so the full term is −1.

Political capital falls moderately. Two large measures (−3 each) and one small (−2) remain in flight from prior turns. The new Resilience Surge is in early implementation and does not yet count as active—per modelling rule 6, high-coordination measures require at least two turns before full effect and portfolio cost apply. Thus, no additional −3 charge is incurred this turn. No legitimacy lend occurs, as `public_sentiment` (42.0) does not exceed `eu_political_capital`. A minor resilience boost (+0.5) reflects preparatory work, but systemic hardening remains pending.
