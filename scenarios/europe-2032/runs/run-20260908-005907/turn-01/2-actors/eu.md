## New measure
Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure  
A six-month programme to harden electricity, water and port operators against AI-enabled cyber intrusion, using emergency procurement and binding security standards.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`

## Priority  
Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure — because the cyber_test_shot proves that detection has failed, and we cannot wait for assurance to catch up when the next intrusion may cascade.

## In practice  
We are activating Article 122 TFEU to justify emergency intervention in energy and transport infrastructure, treating AI-enabled cyber risk as a cross-border threat to essential services. The ENISA budget is being redirected and expanded with €12bn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, matched by national co-financing, to deploy AI-driven intrusion detection systems and zero-trust architectures across the most exposed grid operators, water utilities and port authorities. We are mandating participation through the NIS2 Directive enforcement framework, with the Commission empowered to impose corrective measures on member states that delay.

The Council Presidency is convening an extraordinary meeting of COREPER II and the Energy, Transport and Internal Market Councils to fast-track adoption under urgency procedures. Resistance from member states citing cost and sovereignty is being met with conditional access to cohesion funds and guarantees of technical support from the newly expanded Joint Cyber Unit. Meanwhile, the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre is being repurposed to coordinate rapid deployment, using pre-qualified vendors under emergency procurement rules suspended from normal tender timelines.

This is not a permanent shift in posture — it is a surge, bounded in time and scope, but necessary to close the gap between the pace of AI-enabled threats and the glacial rhythm of EU infrastructure policy. We are buying time for InvestAI and the Tech Sovereignty Package to land, while acknowledging that even the best compute on EU soil is useless if the systems it runs on can be disabled from within.
