A major AI-enabled cyberattack disrupts public services in three EU member states, exploiting poisoned software and polymorphic ransomware. The incident triggers emergency action: the Commission invokes Article 122 TFEU to override national delays, mandating zero-trust architecture and AI monitoring for 120 critical operators, and unilaterally disburses €6bn. ENISA accelerates vendor deployment via fast-track procurement. The Cyber Shield Certification is launched, setting strict standards for AI intrusion detection systems under a revised Cyber Resilience Act. However, legal challenges emerge as Germany appeals the funding to the CJEU and Hungary threatens budgetary retaliation, citing federal overreach. Despite resistance, operational implementation begins. Public-sector AI successes in healthcare and administration bolster political support for EU intervention. Globally, AI capability rises to 55.0 with no safety gains; open-weight models reach 43.0, increasing misuse risks, while the EU’s compute dependence deepens, further eroding its AI sovereignty.
