## Priority

## In practice  
We are treating the cyber_major_incident as the wake-up call it is. The Emergency Resilience Surge is no longer preparatory — it is operational. We are invoking the emergency powers under Article 122 TFEU to override national delays, conditioning cohesion fund disbursements on compliance with binding security upgrades by Q3. ENISA, backed by the Joint Cyber Unit, is issuing mandatory deployment orders to the 120 most exposed operators in energy, water and ports, requiring zero-trust architecture and AI-powered monitoring within six months. Pre-qualified vendors from the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre’s emergency pool are being fast-tracked under derogated procurement rules, with €6bn of the €12bn now released unilaterally by the Commission where national co-financing is withheld.

At the same time, we are launching the Cyber Shield Certification to close a critical gap: not all AI defences are equal, and we cannot afford to deploy systems that fail under pressure. ENISA, in coordination with national agencies, will certify only those detection models that pass red-team evaluations against known AI-generated attack patterns, including polymorphic ransomware and dependency poisoning. Certified stacks will be pre-deployed in cloud environments operated by EU-secured providers, enabling immediate roll-out. This is not optional — from turn 4 onward, only certified systems will qualify for EU incident response support or liability protection under the revised Cyber Resilience Act.

We are paying a steep political price: Germany challenges our legal basis, Hungary threatens to block the next MFF review, and public sentiment remains ambivalent. But the adoption_success — visible improvements in hospital wait times and permit processing due to trusted public AI — gives us cover. We are using that goodwill to argue that resilience enables adoption, not restricts it. We are not slowing AI — we are making it survivable.
