### The Threat Evolves

A wave of coordinated, AI-assisted cyber intrusions sweeps across mid-tier firms and municipalities in five member states. Though individually contained, the attacks share hallmarks of automated tooling derived from open models—polymorphic payloads, self-propagating exploit chains, and adaptive evasion. ENISA confirms a loose but growing coordination among criminal groups, exploiting the widening gap between offensive accessibility and defensive unity. The Emergency Resilience Surge concludes its six-month mandate, having hardened 120 critical operators, but its static defences prove insufficient against this new, fluid threat.

### A New Shield, Just in Time

The EU launches the EU Offensive AI Monitoring and Containment Initiative, a large-scale resilience measure aimed at building real-time detection of AI-generated threats. Leveraging red-teaming AI and mandatory telemetry from NIS2 entities, the plan establishes a Threat Intelligence Fusion Cell under ENISA. Funding is redirected from the Surge’s contingency envelope, sidestepping immediate fiscal pressure. Germany agrees to drop its CJEU challenge in exchange for oversight influence; Hungary suspends retaliation after a side deal on rural digital funds. Implementation begins, though full operational capability is not expected before 2028.

### Frontier Pressures Mount

Global AI capability advances to 56.5, driven by agent-led development in closed labs. No safety improvements land on deployed systems, and interpretability remains shallow—ai_safety drops to 28.0. Open-weight models, now at 44.5, enable more sophisticated misuse, feeding the criminal federation’s toolkit. The EU’s compute dependence deepens as no new sovereign capacity comes online, and rising capability further erodes eu_ai_sovereignty.

Public sentiment holds steady at 44.0—neither panic nor optimism dominates. Labour displacement signals grow louder in policy circles, but no crisis erupts.
