A wave of AI-assisted cyberattacks targets mid-tier firms and municipalities across five EU member states, revealing the limitations of the recently completed Emergency Resilience Surge. The attacks, powered by open-weight AI models, demonstrate adaptive, self-propagating capabilities and indicate growing coordination among criminal groups. In response, the EU launches the Offensive AI Monitoring and Containment Initiative, establishing a Threat Intelligence Fusion Cell under ENISA to enable real-time detection of AI-generated threats using red-teaming AI and mandatory telemetry from NIS2 entities. Funding is reallocated from existing reserves, avoiding immediate budget conflicts. Political tensions ease as Germany withdraws its CJEU challenge in exchange for oversight rights, and Hungary halts retaliation following a side agreement on digital infrastructure funding. Implementation begins, with full capability expected by 2028. Globally, AI capability rises to 56.5, driven by closed-lab advancements, while safety lags (ai_safety: 28.0) and open-weight models (44.5) amplify misuse risks. The EU’s compute dependence worsens, further undermining its ai_sovereignty. Public sentiment remains stable at 44.0, though concerns over labour displacement grow.
