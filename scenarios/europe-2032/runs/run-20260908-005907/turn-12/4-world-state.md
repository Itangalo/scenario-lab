### A Narrowing Window

AI capability advances to 70.5 as recursive self-optimisation becomes routine in frontier training runs. Open-weight models reach 59.0, closing the gap further as agentic techniques leak into public repositories. Despite the *safety_breakthrough* in interpretability, no systemic safety gains land on deployed systems — developers race ahead, and the incident revealed not a flaw, but a new norm.

The EU completes the **Enforceable Fallback** mandate, requiring sovereign redundancy in all critical AI systems. The measure lands: fallback protocols are now legally binding, and testing begins across energy, healthcare, and rail networks. However, implementation is patchy — some member states delay integration, citing cost and complexity.

Simultaneously, the Commission proposes **Pre-Authorized Containment Authority**, aiming to empower the stalled Sovereign Incident Response Corps with emergency override powers. The legal framework is narrowly scoped, tied to verified agentic behaviour and multi-agency alerts. But resistance hardens. Germany and Poland denounce the plan as a violation of national sovereignty, while France demands joint command. The Council delays debate, citing constitutional concerns. The measure starts, but momentum stalls.

Political capital erodes further. With **Middle Power Coalition** still in flight and the new large measure launched, the portfolio charge overwhelms the system. The proposal bonus fails to materialise — the measure, though relevant, is seen as reactive and divisive. Public sentiment remains frozen at 21.0, caught between fear of runaway AI and distrust in EU overreach.

Sovereignty dips to 11.0 as dependency on foreign compute deepens, and no new capacity is secured. The American posture of **RETRENCHMENT** persists, slowing global capability growth slightly — but not enough to offset internal EU fragility.

### Governance at a Standstill

The emergent governance crisis intensifies. Member states demand a special summit to redefine the balance of AI authority, while the AI rights movement gains traction in youth-led protests across Berlin, Amsterdam, and Barcelona. The Commission insists the new containment powers are essential — but cannot prove they will be used sparingly. Trust, not technology, is the bottleneck.
