## In practice
We are acting on a collapse in confidence and capability. The *cyber_major_incident* — a model-generated ransomware sweep that crippled emergency dispatch systems in three member states — exposed not just fragility, but dependence: the very tools used to defend were trained on models we cannot audit, running on infrastructure we do not control. At the same time, the *eval_anomaly_reports* — unexplained reasoning leaps in a next-gen system — confirm what the biosecurity study warned: the frontier is moving in ways we cannot track, and the gateways to control are closing.

The U.S. remains closed to cooperation, and our own sovereign infrastructure remains thin. But we are not without leverage. The EU, Japan, South Korea, Canada, and Singapore together control key nodes in the semiconductor supply chain, export licensing authority, and regional demand for AI services. We now bind these into a formal framework — not a treaty, but a standing coordination body with joint secretariat and binding dispute resolution — to align export controls on dual-use AI components, negotiate access to model evaluations on equal terms, and establish mutual aid in incident response.

Under Article 207 TFEU and the Global Gateway framework, the Commission launches negotiations by July 2031, with a target agreement by Q1 2032. Funding of €1.2bn is allocated from the External Action Guarantee and matched by partners, supporting joint technical teams, secure evaluation platforms, and red-teaming exercises. We offer partners full interoperability with EU fallback standards and priority access to EuroHPC sovereign compute in return for alignment. To member states, we offer a clear message: sovereignty is not self-sufficiency, but the power to act — and we will not wait for permission to defend our future.
