## Statement changes

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we now act only where sovereign observability can be established, and we treat any action without it as provisional, reversible, and politically indefensible. This turn, we extend that principle: we will no longer accept dependency on systems we cannot replicate, audit, or replace within our own jurisdiction.  
  - Grounds: The **capability_jump** this turn confirms a leap in global AI capability to 81.0 — a level at which systems are no longer tools but initiators, capable of sustained autonomous operation across strategic domains. The **eval_anomaly_reports** reveal untrained capabilities emerging in model behaviour under observation, suggesting internal dynamics we cannot yet interpret. These are not isolated incidents. They are symptoms of a control deficit that grows with capability. Our commitment to act under uncertainty holds, but its condition has changed: we must now assume that any system we do not fully observe is already compromised. Therefore, we act only where we can see, verify, and replicate.

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that without sovereign technical capacity, neither mandate can be fulfilled. Control is not policy, law, or negotiation. It is access, compute, and the ability to run, audit, and counter systems independently. When that is absent, all action is reactive, all regulation is theatre, and all resilience is borrowed.  
  - Grounds: The **election_annulled** event this turn — the first in an EU member state citing AI-driven information manipulation as grounds for invalidation — proves that the threat is no longer prospective. It is structural. The public no longer believes elections are secure, and member states are fracturing over how to respond. Our legitimacy depends on restoring agency, not just responding to harm. That agency is not found in declarations or audits. It is found in the ability to run competing models, detect manipulation independently, and provide trusted alternatives. Sovereignty is no longer a means. It is the mandate.

## New measure

**Measure:** Emergency Replication Mandate  
A binding directive to accelerate the Replication Foundry Initiative into full operational status by integrating all available public compute, enforcing unified training stacks, and mandating open-weight distillation of every verified checkpoint.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 14  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 8–12 points by achieving sovereign replication of 70+ capability models; raise openweight_capability by 5–7 points to narrow the gap with the closed frontier  
Applies to: EU member states, national research institutions, and public cloud providers required to contribute compute and personnel

## Priority

**Priority:** Emergency Replication Mandate  
This is now our top priority because the **capability_jump** to 81.0 and the **eval_anomaly_reports** confirm that the closed frontier is no longer just ahead — it is diverging in kind, not degree. The **Sovereign Verification Regime** has ended, but its data is useless without the capacity to act on it. We cannot audit what we cannot run. We cannot counter what we cannot replicate. This mandate turns verification into sovereignty: it forces integration of the Replication Foundry across national silos, mandates shared training infrastructure, and makes open-weight distillation a legal obligation, not an experiment.

## In practice

We are declaring an emergency in sovereign capability. The **Emergency Replication Mandate** is being drafted under Article 114 TFEU for internal market necessity, with co-legislation fast-tracked through the Internal Market Council and European Parliament’s IMCO committee. The EAAD+ is issuing binding directives to all national research clouds — including Germany’s Gauss Centre and France’s GENCI — to allocate 40% of their frontier-class compute to the Replication Foundry by turn 11. We are invoking the Digital Markets Act’s interoperability provisions to compel data format standardisation and break down training stack fragmentation. A new taskforce, ReplicateEU, will be stood up under DG CONNECT with €12bn in emergency funding from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, repurposed from stalled digitalisation projects.

The **Sovereign Inference Backbone** is being restructured in light of the **election_annulled** crisis. We are no longer treating it as a technical project but as a democratic safeguard. The ten designated sites will now include dedicated clusters for election integrity, media provenance, and public information verification, running counter-models to detect manipulation in real time. We are offering Germany and France co-stewardship of the access protocol in exchange for dropping veto demands, while guaranteeing the Nordic states full opt-out rights for national security systems — but only if they contribute equivalent capacity to the civilian backbone. Construction will proceed under emergency permitting rules, with the Commission assuming liability for environmental assessments under Article 12 of the Crisis Regulation.

We are acting on the **capability_jump** and **eval_anomaly_reports** by treating all unreplicated systems as uncontrolled. The Institutional Integrity Audit’s findings are being escalated to the European Council, and we are preparing a Communication on AI and Democratic Integrity for the June European Council summit. The Public Trust Observatory will release a real-time dashboard tracking replication progress, spoofing incidents, and model provenance — not as reassurance, but as proof of effort. There is no trust left to restore. Only ground to retake.
