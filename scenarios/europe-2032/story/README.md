# Story branch log

The narrative built on this scenario does not follow one run. It follows a path chosen through a tree of runs, and this directory is the record of where the tree forks, what was chosen, and which runs belong to which choice.

Two things come out of the simulations, and they need different material:

1. **The path** – the sequence of turns the writeup actually follows.
2. **What could have happened** – the roads not taken, which is what the other runs at each fork are for.

Nothing here is generated by the simulator. It is the editorial layer on top of it, and it has to be maintained by hand as the tree grows.

## Convention

Each fork gets a directory `turn-NN/` holding the pinned actor responses, one file per road, named `road-<letter>-<slug>.md`. Runs exploring a road are named `run-<letter><NN>` under `runs/`, so `run-a03` is the third run down road A. The mapping is restated in the table below, because run directories are gitignored and will not survive a clone.

A pinned response is fed to the Game Master as the actor's turn for that turn, in place of a generated one. Everything before the fork is identical across roads; everything after it is the dice and the model.

## Fork 1 – turn 1, the second half of 2026

The world at this point is the scenario's fixed opening: the test shot against critical infrastructure on three continents, discovered by accident, with nothing actuated. It is the same in every run and in every trajectory arm.

All three roads below are real actor responses drawn by `sample-actor` from an identical turn-1 prompt (50 draws, prompt hash `0256ccd76ffd1c29`). They were selected, not written. They differ on the axis the scenario is built to separate: which lever the Union reaches for, how long it takes to bite, and whether it can be undone.

| road | measure | category | cost | lead | applies to | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | EU Sovereign Compute Initiative | 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity) | high | 3 turns | own jurisdiction | `plateau-turn-01-v3/sample-01` |
| **B** | Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure | 6 (Preparedness and resilience) | medium | 1 turn | particular member states | `plateau-turn-01-v3/sample-09` |
| **C** | Secure Access for EU Evaluators | 1 (Evaluation and oversight) | medium | 2 turns | frontier developers operating in the EU | `acceleration-turn-01/sample-08` |

**A – build the base.** Legally anchored EU-controlled compute, €12bn redirected out of the stalled InvestAI Gigafactory allocation, ASML's chokepoint played as a counterweight, a Sovereign AI Infrastructure Act to govern it. Reads the test shot as evidence of structural dependence. The slowest lever, the only one that compounds, and the one bet that cannot be un-bet.

**B – absorb the blow.** Hardening of energy, water and port operators through the Union Civil Protection Mechanism and money that already exists, plus a binding but limited-duration directive on minimum standards. Reads the test shot as evidence of exposure. The Union's strongest and fastest lever; it insures without empowering.

**C – reach into the developers.** Emergency rulemaking under the AI Act to compel auditable access to weights for EU-appointed evaluators, hosted on sovereign infrastructure, with market suspension as the penalty. Reads the test shot as evidence that self-reporting is finished. The classic Brussels-effect play, and by construction the weakest of the three levers: it reaches the frontier only through market access.

### Provenance

A and B are verbatim, from the current configuration – freely chosen standing commitment, corrected category tags – so they reproduce from the scenario as committed. C predates those two fixes and carries two edits, both recorded here because they change what the Game Master receives:

- the ledger line ``add `standing_commitment` (commitment): …`` was added under Statement changes, carrying C's own commitment wording unchanged. Without it the commitment never enters the ledger and is lost after turn 1.
- the category tag was corrected from `4` to `1 (Evaluation and oversight)`. Mandated pre-release evaluation is category 1's own anchor; the sample was drawn before the prompt carried the category names.

Both A and B write the raw event id `cyber_test_shot` in their prose, as C does once. Left as drawn.

### Fork 1 results – plateau arm, turns 2–5 complete

Thirty runs, 120 simulated turns, $0.68. One run (`run-c05`) died mid-turn on a malformed OpenRouter response and was resumed; nothing else failed.

Medians at turn 5, the end of 2028:

| road | capability | open weights | safety | resilience | sovereignty | capital | sentiment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A build | 57.5 | 49.2 | 34.0 | 38.0 | 24.0 | 34.5 | 33.5 |
| B absorb | 56.0 | 49.0 | 34.0 | 35.0 | 24.5 | 34.0 | 22.0 |
| C reach | 59.0 | 50.0 | 34.0 | 38.0 | 24.0 | **42.0** | 35.5 |

Read with care – ten runs per road is far too few to separate a road from its dice, and the spread inside each road is wider than the gaps between them (road B's resilience runs from 33 to 52). What is worth noticing anyway:

- **Nobody built sovereignty.** All three roads end within 3 points of the starting 22, road A included, despite road A spending high capital for three turns on exactly that. Compute is slow by design, and four turns is not enough for it to land. Whether that is the rules being right or being too harsh is the first thing to check when calibrating.
- **Road B bought resilience and paid in sentiment**, ending 11 points below the other two. Hardening is visible, unglamorous and reads to the public as an admission of exposure.
- **Road C ends with the most room to act** and is the only road that finishes above where it started on capital. Cheap, fast, legible – and it did nothing for safety either, which is the point the metric design keeps making.

### Defects this batch exposed – both since fixed

The two below were found in these 30 runs and are now repaired in the engine and the scenario. The runs themselves still carry the broken elections, so they remain usable as material for what-could-have-happened but not for anything turning on the American posture.


Both bear on turns 3–5, so treat the election and anything downstream of it as provisional until they are fixed.

1. **The 2028 election was not certain in practice.** It fired in 22 of 30 runs, though its condition said it always happens: `probability_samples: 3` averages the events step's three draws, and the model omitted the event from one or two of them, giving a mean of 0.33 or 0.67 that the dice could then fail.
2. **The resolution rule was not applied.** `campaign_backlash` fired in 12 runs, which by the rule gives Retrenchment, yet every run that recorded a posture recorded CONSOLIDATION – the no-current default. The metrics step, which writes the world state, cannot see the event record the rule depends on. Four runs recorded no posture line at all.

Both are gone. The three outcomes are now a mutually exclusive event group resolved in turn 5: Python guarantees exactly one fires, and the events step decides which by weighing the three against each other from the world as it stands. A third failure was avoided on the way – deriving the outcome mechanically from the last campaign current made the presidency hang on two narrow events in a four-turn window, and `campaign_atlanticist` fired 0 times in 30 runs, so Alliance was unreachable rather than rare. The campaign events stay in the pool as evidence the model reads; they decide nothing.

Checked on three branches from turn 4: weights came back graded and all three outcomes were live in every sample – consolidation 0.43–0.52, alliance 0.22–0.30, retrenchment 0.27–0.32 – rather than collapsing onto one. All three elected Retrenchment, which is the shared dice seed rather than a degenerate reading: runs at the same index carry the same seed by design.
