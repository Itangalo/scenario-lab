# Story branch log

The narrative built on this scenario does not follow one run. It follows a path chosen through a tree of runs, and this directory is the record of where the tree forks, what was chosen, and which runs belong to which choice.

Two things come out of the simulations, and they need different material:

1. **The path** – the sequence of turns the writeup actually follows.
2. **What could have happened** – the roads not taken, which is what the other runs at each fork are for.

Nothing here is generated by the simulator. It is the editorial layer on top of it, and it has to be maintained by hand as the tree grows.

## Convention

Turn 1 is a single fixed opening, identical in every run and every arm, held in `turn-01/opening.md`. The tree forks from turn 2 onward.

Each fork gets a directory `turn-NN/` holding the pinned actor responses, one file per road, named `road-<letter>-<slug>.md`. Runs exploring a road are named `run-<letter><NN>` under `runs/`, so `run-a03` is the third run down road A. The mapping is restated in the table below, because run directories are gitignored and will not survive a clone.

A pinned response is fed to the Game Master as the actor's turn for that turn, in place of a generated one. Everything before the fork is identical across roads; everything after it is the dice and the model.

## The fixed opening – turn 1, the second half of 2026 (ECHO 2026-08-30)

The world at this point is the scenario's fixed opening: the test shot against critical infrastructure on three continents, discovered by accident, with nothing actuated. It is the same in every run and in every trajectory arm, and so is the Union's first response.

The opening is `turn-01/opening.md`, drawn by `sample-actor` from the current turn-1 prompt (20 draws, prompt hash `0ae87a8fd47803ed`, in `actor-samples/opening-turn-01/`) and selected verbatim as sample 15. It is the modal answer of that pool: an Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure, category 6, large, finishing on turn 4, named as the turn's priority, on top of the two inherited programmes the prompt seeds into the portfolio. The standing commitment it enters into the ledger is *Secure the capacity to act independently in an AI-defined world without sacrificing the Union's foundational stability*, and because the commitment is chosen once and binds every later turn, that phrase now binds every branch of the tree.

Why one opening and not three. The Union's first answer to the test shot is setup rather than a choice the reader owns, and forking here meant every road entered turn 2 on a different world state, so the roads were never compared from a common footing. Forking at turn 2 puts the reader's first decision after the world has answered back. The three roads that used to fork here are in `turn-01/superseded-roads/`, with a note on why each of them stopped working.

### What the re-draw found, and what it costs

The twenty draws came back far narrower than the pool the three roads were selected from:

| category | draws |
|---|---|
| 6 (Preparedness and resilience) | 16 |
| 5 (Public technical capacity and research) | 4 |
| everything else | 0 |

The old fork spread across categories 4, 6 and 1. None of those three roads is drawable from the current prompt. Three things did it, and only the first is a loss:

- **Category 4 is closed off as a new measure.** The prompt says building EU compute *is* the inherited Gigafactories line, and that standing up a parallel programme is the one move the Union cannot credibly make. That rule is right, and it removed road A's entire identity: not one of the twenty draws proposed a category 4 measure.
- **The opening event points straight at category 6.** A cyber test shot against critical infrastructure, plus the prompt's own clarifier that hardening critical services against attack is 6.
- **Road C's category was a mislabel rather than a road.** What used to come back tagged 1 now comes back tagged 5 – sample 02 is sovereign access to AI evaluation, sample 06 the Frontier Monitoring Corps. That is the category-tag guidance working as designed, not a change in behaviour.

The standing commitments converged as well. Nearly all twenty are a variant of *secure the capacity to act independently in an AI-shaped world*, so fixing one commitment for the whole tree costs almost no variance – the actor was barely varying it. The fork was never really about ends.

Two things follow for the fork at turn 2. It has to carry the whole weight of the reader's choice, because turn 1 no longer carries any of it. And it cannot be assumed to offer a wide spread just because the last one did: check the draw distribution before selecting roads, and if it collapses again, the fork has to be made by steering the draws rather than by sampling them.

### The capital arithmetic the opening locks in

The opening leaves three measures running – two inherited at 3 apiece, the new one at 3 – plus 1 for naming a priority. That is a 10-point charge on `eu_political_capital` every turn against a start of 48, and every branch of the tree inherits it. The metric's own reference point at 48 reads "two or three measures can run at once before something slips", so this is the design working rather than failing, but it means the tree starts near the edge of what the Union can carry and feeds straight into the open question about sovereignty never moving.

## Fork 1 – turn 2, the first half of 2027 (ECHO 2026-08-30)

Not yet drawn. The three arm bases are built from the fixed opening; the next step is to draw at turn 2 off those bases and select the roads there.

One wrinkle to settle first. Turn-2 prompts depend on turn-1 resolved state, which is arm-dependent, so turn-2 draws are per-arm. Either the roads are selected separately in each arm – and stop being the same three roads across arms, which costs every cross-arm claim the tree wants to make – or they are drawn in one arm and the text reused, which is a real pretence rather than a free one, because the response would then be reasoned from metrics that arm did not produce. Turn 1 had no such problem: its prompt hashed identically under all three variants, so one opening genuinely serves all three.

## Seeds

**Seeds are unique per run, and are not to be reused across roads or arms.** The first two batches did reuse them – seeds 700001–700010 by index everywhere – on the reasoning that dice-pairing isolates the effect of the reader's choice. It does, and the cost is too high for what this tree is for. The roll for an event is `Random(f"{seed}:{turn}:{event_id}")`, so a shared seed fixes the threshold of every event and only the model's probability moves between roads. Measured on the acceleration batch, event-profile overlap between two roads (Jaccard, catalogue events, turns 2–5) is 0.625 at the same seed against 0.210 at different seeds, and across arms 0.271 against 0.099. Three roads sharing a seed are substantially the same world.

Two consequences, because they pull in opposite directions. Per-road frequency claims – the "in 6 of 10 simulations" boxes – are unaffected either way, since the ten seeds inside a road are distinct regardless. What is affected is anything pooled across roads or arms, which is fewer independent draws than it looks, and the tree as it deepens: reusing ten seeds down every branch of a 42-piece story means the whole story explores ten dice-histories, and a reader replaying would feel the repetition. Breadth is what this needs; attribution at n=10 was disclaimed anyway. If a paired batch is ever wanted for attribution work, make it deliberately and say so here.

## Bases

The three bases below are built by `pin-turn-1.py`, once per arm, from the same `turn-01/opening.md`. It replaces the events and actor steps and lets the rest of the turn run normally, which is what makes the turn-1 world state consistent with the pinned choice rather than with a drawn one.

| arm | base |
|---|---|
| plateau | `runs/run-open-plateau-base` |
| acceleration | `runs/run-open-acceleration-base` |
| verification-bounded | `runs/run-open-verification-bounded-base` |

The opening response is arm-independent and its resolution is not. The turn-1 actor prompt hashed identically under all three variants, so one drawn opening serves all three without pretence. The rules patches, however, differ on how fast `ai_capability` grows – plateau +1.5 to +2.5 per turn, acceleration +2.5 to +4 – and that applies in turn 1. One base shared across arms would hand acceleration a plateau-rate opening turn on the one metric the arms exist to separate, so each arm resolves the identical opening under its own physics.

## The retired three-road tree

Everything below describes the tree as it stood before 2026-08-30, when the fork was at turn 1 and there were three roads. It is kept as the record of what the two archived batches showed. The runs are in `runs/archive/pre-priority-bias-2026-08/`, whose README says what remains citable in them.

The three roads were real actor responses drawn by `sample-actor` and selected, not written. Roads A and B came from `plateau-turn-01-v3` (prompt hash `af64329cbd2bf3c0`); road C came from `acceleration-turn-01` (prompt hash `0256ccd76ffd1c29`), an earlier prompt, which is why it carried the two edits recorded below. An earlier version of this README described all three as drawn from one identical prompt of 50 draws; that was wrong on both counts.

| road | measure | category | cost | lead | applies to | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | EU Sovereign Compute Initiative | 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity) | high | 3 turns | own jurisdiction | `plateau-turn-01-v3/sample-01` |
| **B** | Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure | 6 (Preparedness and resilience) | medium | 1 turn | particular member states | `plateau-turn-01-v3/sample-09` |
| **C** | Secure Access for EU Evaluators | 1 (Evaluation and oversight) | medium | 2 turns | frontier developers operating in the EU | `acceleration-turn-01/sample-08` |

**A – build the base.** Legally anchored EU-controlled compute, €12bn redirected out of the stalled InvestAI Gigafactory allocation, ASML's chokepoint played as a counterweight, a Sovereign AI Infrastructure Act to govern it. Reads the test shot as evidence of structural dependence. The slowest lever, the only one that compounds, and the one bet that cannot be un-bet.

**B – absorb the blow.** Hardening of energy, water and port operators through the Union Civil Protection Mechanism and money that already exists, plus a binding but limited-duration directive on minimum standards. Reads the test shot as evidence of exposure. The Union's strongest and fastest lever; it insures without empowering.

**C – reach into the developers.** Emergency rulemaking under the AI Act to compel auditable access to weights for EU-appointed evaluators, hosted on sovereign infrastructure, with market suspension as the penalty. Reads the test shot as evidence that self-reporting is finished. The classic Brussels-effect play, and by construction the weakest of the three levers: it reaches the frontier only through market access.

### Provenance

A and B are verbatim, from the configuration current when they were drawn – freely chosen standing commitment, corrected category tags. C predates those two fixes and carries two edits, both recorded here because they change what the Game Master receives:

- the ledger line ``add `standing_commitment` (commitment): …`` was added under Statement changes, carrying C's own commitment wording unchanged. Without it the commitment never enters the ledger and is lost after turn 1.
- the category tag was corrected from `4` to `1 (Evaluation and oversight)`. Mandated pre-release evaluation is category 1's own anchor; the sample was drawn before the prompt carried the category names.

Both A and B write the raw event id `cyber_test_shot` in their prose, as C does once. Left as drawn.

### Fork 1 results – plateau arm, turns 2–5 complete

Thirty runs, 120 simulated turns, $0.68. One run (`run-c05`) died mid-turn on a malformed OpenRouter response and was resumed; nothing else failed.

Medians at turn 5, the end of 2028:

| road | capability | open weights | safety | resilience | sovereignty | capital | sentiment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A build | 57.5 | 49.2 | 34.0 | 38.0 | 24.0 | 34.5 | 33.5 |
| B absorb | 56.0 | 49.0 | 34.0 | 35.0 | 24.5 | 34.0 | 22.0 |
| C reach | 59.0 | 50.0 | 34.0 | 38.0 | 24.0 | **42.0** | 35.5 |

Read with care – ten runs per road is far too few to separate a road from its dice, and the spread inside each road is wider than the gaps between them (road B's resilience runs from 33 to 52). What is worth noticing anyway:

- **Nobody built sovereignty.** All three roads end within 3 points of the starting 22, road A included, despite road A spending high capital for three turns on exactly that. Compute is slow by design, and four turns is not enough for it to land. Whether that is the rules being right or being too harsh is the first thing to check when calibrating.
- **Road B bought resilience and paid in sentiment**, ending 11 points below the other two. Hardening is visible, unglamorous and reads to the public as an admission of exposure.
- **Road C ends with the most room to act** and is the only road that finishes above where it started on capital. Cheap, fast, legible – and it did nothing for safety either, which is the point the metric design keeps making.

### Defects this batch exposed – both since fixed

The two below were found in these 30 runs and are now repaired in the engine and the scenario. The runs themselves still carry the broken elections, so they remain usable as material for what-could-have-happened but not for anything turning on the American posture.

1. **The 2028 election was not certain in practice.** It fired in 22 of 30 runs, though its condition said it always happens: `probability_samples: 3` averages the events step's three draws, and the model omitted the event from one or two of them, giving a mean of 0.33 or 0.67 that the dice could then fail.
2. **The resolution rule was not applied.** `campaign_backlash` fired in 12 runs, which by the rule gives Retrenchment, yet every run that recorded a posture recorded CONSOLIDATION – the no-current default. The metrics step, which writes the world state, cannot see the event record the rule depends on. Four runs recorded no posture line at all.

Both are gone. The three outcomes are now a mutually exclusive event group resolved in turn 5: Python guarantees exactly one fires, and the events step decides which by weighing the three against each other from the world as it stands. A third failure was avoided on the way – deriving the outcome mechanically from the last campaign current made the presidency hang on two narrow events in a four-turn window, and `campaign_atlanticist` fired 0 times in 30 runs, so Alliance was unreachable rather than rare. The campaign events stay in the pool as evidence the model reads; they decide nothing.

Checked on three branches from turn 4: weights came back graded and all three outcomes were live in every sample – consolidation 0.43–0.52, alliance 0.22–0.30, retrenchment 0.27–0.32 – rather than collapsing onto one. All three elected Retrenchment, which is the shared dice seed rather than a degenerate reading: runs at the same index carry the same seed by design.

Thirty runs, 120 simulated turns, $0.72, run 2026-08-28 against the repaired election machinery. Runs are `run-acc-a01` … `run-acc-c10` under `runs/`, with the pinned turn-1 bases at `run-acc-a-base`, `run-acc-b-base` and `run-acc-c-base`. Seeds are 700001–700010 by index, the same seeds the plateau batch used, so a run is dice-paired with its plateau twin at the same road and index. That reuse is the thing the seed rule above now forbids – it is recorded here as what these two batches are, not as a property to preserve.

Medians at turn 5, the end of 2028:

| road | capability | open weights | safety | resilience | sovereignty | capital | sentiment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A build | 69.1 | 51.0 | 29.5 | 41.5 | 25.0 | 38.0 | 28.0 |
| B absorb | 62.8 | 48.3 | 30.5 | 38.0 | 25.0 | 34.0 | 28.0 |
| C reach | 67.8 | 40.0 | 30.5 | 37.5 | 24.5 | 34.0 | 28.0 |

Same caution as the plateau batch – ten runs per road cannot separate a road from its dice, and the within-road spread again exceeds the between-road gaps. What the arm changes, read against plateau:

- **Capability runs and safety does not follow.** The frontier ends 5–12 points higher than on plateau and safety ends 4 points lower on every road, from a start of 32. `capability_jump` fired 6–7 times per road against 0 on plateau, and `loss_of_control_incident` 3–4 times against 1. This is the arm doing what it was built to do.
- **Sentiment converges on 28 and stays there.** All three roads, identical median, against a 11-point spread on plateau. Under acceleration the public reads the world rather than the Union's choice of instrument, and road B no longer pays a sentiment penalty for looking exposed.
- **Nobody built sovereignty here either.** 24.5–25.0 against a start of 22, on all three roads, in the arm where the argument for building one's own is strongest. Two batches now agree on this, and it is the first thing to settle in calibration.
- **The roads swapped portfolios.** Counting the measure categories chosen in turns 2–5: road A, which opened with a category 4 sovereignty bet, spent 2 of its 39 later measures on category 4 and went to public technical capacity (13) and evaluation (8) instead. Roads B and C went the other way – 17 of 40 and 10 of 39 into category 4. Every road ends up doing roughly what the other two started with, which is either convergence on what the world demands or the actor regressing to a mean, and telling those apart matters for how much the reader's choice is supposed to weigh.
- **Road A's opening measure is still stuck at the end.** The EU Sovereign Compute Initiative reads `under implementation` in 9 of 10 runs at turn 5 and `fully implemented` in one, always for the same reasons in prose: legal anchoring deadlocked between Germany and France, an ECJ referral, procurement suspended. In `run-acc-a08` the ASML leverage play backfires outright – Washington answers conditional licensing by suspending equipment licences. The standing commitment survives verbatim in all ten runs; the spending behind it does not.

The election machinery now behaves. Exactly one outcome in all 30 runs, and all three reachable: Consolidation 18, Retrenchment 7, Alliance 5. Alliance fired 3 times on road C alone, against 0 occurrences in the entire plateau batch. The campaign events are legible enough to write from without deciding anything – `campaign_security_hawk` 13 runs, `campaign_backlash` 15, `campaign_atlanticist` 4 – so a run where the hawkish current ran loudest and Retrenchment still won is available as material rather than as a defect.

Two provenance notes on this batch. The first attempt at the bases let the dice run in turn 1 alongside the pinned actor, so `cyber_major_incident` and `taiwan_tension_rise` fired there and the three roads no longer shared an opening; those bases were discarded and rebuilt, and nothing from them is in the runs above. And the batch was killed and restarted once, to raise its concurrency – `run-acc-a02` and most of roads B and C were interrupted mid-turn and re-ran that turn from the previous turn's state, which is resume working as designed but means those turns were drawn twice and the second draw kept.

## Changes made after these two batches

Both batches above predate the changes below, so the new tree will not be directly comparable to them on anything touching how doggedly the Union pursues its own direction. For narrative material that probably does not matter; for any cross-arm claim about persistence it does.

**The standing commitment now binds the priority.** Road A showed the failure the old wording allowed: the commitment survived verbatim in all ten runs while the spending went elsewhere – the Sovereign Compute Initiative was the named priority in only 13 of 40 turns, and 2 of the 39 measures chosen in turns 2–5 carried its category. Nothing in the pipeline read the commitment when a measure was picked, so the ledger bound what the Union said and never what it did. Raising `standing_commitment` to identity tier would not have helped: `statements.py` gates commitment and identity identically, and neither tier reaches measure selection.

The lever used instead is the named priority, because metric rule 10 already makes it the only thing that reliably advances – other unfinished measures move only above `eu_political_capital` 55, and these runs sit at 34–45. `user-prompts/actor.md` now says the priority should serve the standing commitment in most turns, and that naming one that does not requires a line saying what the world demanded instead. It is written as a bias rather than a rule: the portfolio is still expected to spread, and a commitment pursued through several categories is still being pursued. The same passage was added to `background/actors/eu.md`, where it is documentation only – see below.

**`background/actors/eu.md` is mostly not rendered, and this was found while making the change above.** `load_actor` in `scenario_lab/loader.py` reads `## Long description` until the first `###` heading and then stops: any `###` that is not `### Statements` or `### Behavioral traits` sets the current section to none. Everything in that file from `### What you are` onward – the instruments, the standing-commitment rules, the ten categories, "What you do not know" – therefore never reaches any prompt. `long_description` as loaded is 380 characters, one paragraph. The scenario behaves correctly anyway because `user-prompts/actor.md` carries the operative wording independently, and the file's `### Statements` and `### Behavioral traits` blocks are parsed by their own branches and do reach the actor. Two consequences worth holding onto. A change made only in `background/actors/eu.md` has no effect on any run, so operative changes go in the user prompt until this is repaired. And the blast radius of repairing it is smaller than it first looks: measured across all 39 actor files in the repository, 34 of them lose 80–110 tokens, which is markup and section headers rather than content. Only five lose anything real, `europe-2032/eu.md` worst at 2,619 tokens and four actor files in two other scenarios behind it. The bug bites exactly the scenarios written in the richer style, and repairing it re-baselines those and no others.

**The standing commitment was not reaching the ledger at all, and the re-draw is what found it. (ECHO 2026-08-30)** The turn-1 prompt states the required form as a Markdown code span, wrapped in double backticks so the statement id inside can keep its own single ones: ``add `standing_commitment` (commitment): …``. Models copied the wrapper along with the form, and every rule in `statements.py` anchors on `^add` or `^modify`, so the wrapper alone was enough to make a well-formed proposal parse as nothing at all. All 20 draws in `opening-turn-01` failed this way, silently – a dropped proposal is indistinguishable from no proposal – which means every run started under the current prompt would have lost its standing commitment after turn 1, including the whole verification-bounded arm. The three superseded road files predate the wording and wrote the line bare, so they parsed, which is why the two archived batches are unaffected. `parse_statement_changes` now unwraps a code span of two or more backticks before matching; a single pair is left alone, because it is ambiguous against the trailing id in ``retire `x` ``. Three regression tests cover it.

A smaller version of the same problem is left standing deliberately. Two of the twenty draws omitted the `## Statement changes` heading altogether and wrote the line under `## Standing commitment` instead – the exact thing the prompt warns does nothing. That is model non-compliance rather than a parser defect, and the section boundary is worth keeping strict, but it means roughly one turn-1 response in ten still loses its commitment. Selected and pinned openings are checked against the parser before being pinned; drawn ones are not.

**Still open, and it bears directly on the above.** It is not established that road A's drift was drift rather than the actor being right. Category 4 is high-cost with a three-turn lead time, and the prose gives the same obstacle in every run – legal anchoring deadlocked, ECJ referral, procurement frozen. `run-acc-a08` named the initiative priority in all four turns and it still ended `under implementation`; `run-acc-a04`, the one run where it reached `fully implemented`, never named it once. If a sovereignty measure cannot land inside the window whatever the Union does, the new priority bias will not produce commitment, it will produce an actor visibly spending four turns on a wall. Settle the lead-time and sovereignty-growth rules first – it is the same calibration item as sovereignty never moving in either batch.

One measurement caveat for anyone repeating that analysis: the actor renames measures between turns, so tracking a portfolio by name undercounts. `run-acc-a04`'s "Begin phased deployment of EU-independent…" is very likely the compute initiative under a new label.
