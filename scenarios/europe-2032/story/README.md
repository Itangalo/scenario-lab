# Story branch log

This scenario is used to build an interactive story. As such, it doesn't follow a single run through the scenario, but a path through a tree of runs. On top of that, the scenario exists in three different variants, giving different mechanics to how AI advances.

This directory is the record of where the tree forks, what was chosen, and which runs belong to which choice.

Two things come out of the simulations, and they need different material:

1. **The path** – the sequence of turns the writeup actually follows.
2. **What could have happened** – the roads not taken, which is what the other runs at each fork are for.

Nothing here is generated by the simulator. It is the editorial layer on top of it, and it has to be maintained by hand as the tree grows.

## The tree structure

There are three underlying world mechanics for AI advancements, called *arms*. They are:

- Acceleration (A): Recursive self-improvement (RSI) and then full human-level intelligece/artificial general intelligence (AGI) is achieved within 5–10 years.
- RLVR-bound acceleration (R): AI becomes superhuman in areas where success can be verified by machines.
- Plateau (P): AI progress slows down and basically stalls within a few years.

The story progresses in half-year segments, with H2 2026 as the starting point and H2 2032 as the end. The reader of the story does not know which of these arms they are on, and every time the story starts over the arm is randomized.

Turn 1 (H2 2026) is a *shared* starting point for all three arms. It is called `turn-01`. It has a fictive key event, being a warning shot for massive cyber disruption.

At the end of turn 1, the gets to choose between two key AI strategies for the EU, which branches the story. On the current arm there are thus two branches, but on all arms taken together, there are sex branches. Note that both turn 1 and the available options are identical across the arms. The options that start the six branches are called `option-02-A1`, `option-02-A2`, `option-02-R1`, `option-02-R2`, `option-02-P1` and `option-02-P2`.

After the first choice, the story progresses four turns (two years). The turns are called `turn-02-A1`, `turn-03-A1`, `turn-04-A1` and `turn-05-A1` for the A1 branch, and so on. Then the reader is presented with another choice, again pointing the EU AI policy in one direction or the other. There are six sets of choices, one for each branch, creating twelve options (four per branch). The options are called `option-06-A11` and `option-06-A12` for the first branch, and so on.

The story continues based on the reader's choice, for the the next two years (four turns), with turns named `turn-06-A11`, `turn-07-A11`, `turn-08-A11` and `turn-09-A11` for the first branch. This is followeb by a final set of options (`option-10-A111` and `option-10-A112` for the first branch), and then the final four turns (`turn-10-A111`, `turn-11-A111`, `turn-12-A111` and `turn-12-A111` for the first branch).

The files representing the turns and options are stored under `tree`. The share opening is stored in `turn-01/opening.md`, while the final turn for the last branch is stored in the directory `turn-13-P222`.

Each turn and option has a directory `turn-NN-branchid/` or `option-NN-branchid/`, holding necessary stored information.

## How options and paths are created

**The options** are created by running (at least) ten instances of the upleadning turn, collecting only the actor output. From these, two outputs are selected. These should be representative of two larger groups of outputs. The actor answers are collected by the special call used for only collecting actor output, not running the full turn.

All outputs are stored, also those not used as options. The plan is to use the answers for describing how common the two options are among the choices made in the simulation.

**The turns following each option** are created by running (at least) ten simulations of the following turns. From these, one is selected randomly. (Always choosing a representative or median path would put all parts of the story in the middle of a bell curve, which is sub-optimal in several ways.)

All the simulated paths are stored. The plan is to used them for describing how often certain events occured in parallel simulations, or mention some events that could have occured but didn't.

## Seeds

Seeds are unique per run, and are not to be reused across roads or arms. Re-using seed would narrow down the explored space considerably, which is sub-optimal in several ways.
