## In practice
This turn, we are accelerating the operational integration of the Open Science Resilience Initiative by binding its outputs directly to crisis response and public procurement. The Commission, through DG SANTE and DG ENER, is drafting an Implementing Act under the Crisis Management Protocol to mandate that all AI systems used in pandemic early-warning networks and national energy grid coordination must, by Q1 2032, be either fully auditable or subject to real-time interpretability logging. This creates immediate demand for the initiative’s open protein folding and agent monitoring models. We are working with the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking to allocate 15% of pre-exascale compute time to auditable model training runs, prioritizing those that can demonstrate traceable reasoning in biological threat detection.

Simultaneously, we are responding to eu_frontier_access_denied by invoking the Anti-Coercion Instrument’s digital continuity clause, triggering a Council-level review of critical dependencies on foreign AI providers. This is not about retaliation—it is about resilience. The Joint Threat Response has offered us real-time cyber telemetry sharing, but only if we can prove sovereign detection capacity. To meet that threshold, we are fast-tracking the deployment of the first auditable intrusion-detection agent from the Initiative into ENISA’s cross-border monitoring network, with pilot integration in Germany, Spain, and Poland by June. The message to member states is clear: sovereignty is no longer theoretical. It is the difference between being locked out of your own systems and being able to rebuild them, in the open, under your own authority.
