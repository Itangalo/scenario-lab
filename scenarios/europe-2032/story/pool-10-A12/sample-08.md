## In practice

This turn, we are advancing the Open Science Resilience Initiative by finalising governance and funding flows for the three operational hubs in Germany, France, and the Netherlands, while pressing Italy and Sweden to resolve national coordination delays by June. The Commission has activated Article 122a TFEU to fast-track infrastructure access, and DG CONNECT is working with national regulators to override local permitting bottlenecks for compute and lab facilities. Funding is secured through a €1.4bn Eurobond under ESM oversight and €2.8bn redirected from Horizon Europe, with disbursement now tied to quarterly milestones on staffing, interpretability integration, and agency linkage. We are embedding ENISA and ECDC representatives directly into hub design teams to ensure real-time alignment with emergency response protocols, especially in light of the recent agent-driven grid manipulation.

Simultaneously, we are responding to the eu_frontier_access_denied event by conducting a full audit of critical dependencies on foreign AI systems across healthcare, energy, and border control. This audit, led by the European Commission with support from ENISA, will be completed by Q3 and will inform a new Council recommendation on sovereign fallback capacity—mandating that no critical public function may rely solely on externally controlled AI by 2033. The joint_threat_response invitation into the new biosurveillance pact offers a rare opening: we are using it to negotiate binding access terms for EU-developed auditable models in exchange for data sharing, turning our technical niche into diplomatic leverage. While public sentiment remains low and political capital critically constrained, the convergence of crisis and opportunity demands we double down on the only path that addresses both mandates: sovereignty through auditable control, not brute capability.
