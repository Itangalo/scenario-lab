## In practice
This turn, we are advancing the Open Science Resilience Initiative by finalizing staffing and infrastructure allocations in the three hubs already under way—Germany, France, and the Netherlands—with binding mandates now activated through national research councils. DG CONNECT and DG SANTE are leading a joint task force to harmonize data access protocols and model validation standards across the hubs, ensuring interoperability from the start. The €1.4bn Eurobond under ESM oversight has been drawn down, with 60% allocated to secure compute infrastructure co-located with biosecurity agencies. We are negotiating with national governments to fast-track grid connections and energy prioritization for these sites under Article 122a TFEU, treating them as critical infrastructure. Resistance in Sweden and Italy persists due to budget reallocation concerns; we are offering targeted cohesion funds and leadership roles in protein folding and climate sub-projects to secure full participation.

Simultaneously, we are responding to the joint_threat_response event by entering formal negotiations to join the new intelligence and response pact. The European Joint Crisis Coordination Cell (EJCCC), hosted under the Union Civil Protection Mechanism, is being restructured to serve as our operational interface, with ENISA and ECDC designated as technical leads. We are contributing early access to our open-weight agent monitoring tools in exchange for real-time telemetry from partner states on AI-driven cyber intrusions and bio-signals. This is not charity: it is leverage. Our credibility from the open protein folding breakthrough gives us standing to demand binding sample-sharing and joint investigation rights. We are also using this moment to push for EU-level adoption of incident logging standards that will feed back into the Open Science initiative—ensuring that the next generation of models is built with auditable behaviour trails from the ground up.
