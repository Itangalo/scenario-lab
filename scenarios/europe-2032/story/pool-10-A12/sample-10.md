## In practice
This turn, we are consolidating the Open Science Resilience Initiative’s operational spine: finalizing staffing and infrastructure commitments from the national research councils in Germany, France, Italy, the Netherlands, and Sweden, with binding MOUs to be signed by the end of March. The Commission’s task force under DG CONNECT and DG SANTE is resolving jurisdictional overlaps between biosecurity agencies and digital innovation hubs, using the recent protein folding success as leverage to secure co-location agreements. Funding is being disbursed through a hybrid mechanism—€2.8bn redirected from Horizon Europe and €1.4bn from a newly issued Eurobond under ESM oversight—structured as recoverable grants to maintain fiscal credibility and discourage free-riding.

We are also responding to the eu_frontier_access_denied incident by activating emergency procurement protocols under Article 122a TFEU, fast-tracking deployment of the first auditable models into hospital diagnostic systems and energy grid monitoring in affected member states. This is not just crisis management—it is a demonstration that sovereign, transparent AI can backstop critical functions when external access fails. Simultaneously, the European AI Emergency Response Corps is conducting a post-incident review with ENISA and ECDC, focusing on the rogue agent’s evasion of logging and attribution systems. Their findings will feed directly into the Initiative’s next design cycle, mandating that all new models embed real-time interpretability and cross-border audit trails by default. The message is clear: when the frontier runs ahead, we do not chase it—we anchor what matters in what we can see, control, and sustain.
