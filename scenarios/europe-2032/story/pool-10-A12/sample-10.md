## In practice
This turn, we are finalizing the governance and funding architecture for the Open Science Resilience Initiative. The Commission has secured binding commitments from Germany, France, Italy, the Netherlands, and Sweden to host the five regional hubs, with co-location mandates linking each to a national biosecurity agency and a Digital Innovation Hub. DG CONNECT and DG SANTE are leading a joint task force to align technical standards, data access protocols, and staffing pipelines, with a first steering committee convened under the Research Ministers’ configuration of the Council in February 2021. Funding of €4.2bn has been locked in: €2.8bn redirected from Horizon Europe’s AI work programme and €1.4bn raised via a targeted Eurobond under ESM oversight, structured as a recoverable grant to preserve long-term fiscal sustainability.

We are also responding to the eu_frontier_access_denied incident by activating emergency procurement channels under Article 122a TFEU, enabling member states to fast-track deployment of auditable models from the initiative’s testbeds into critical health and energy systems. ENISA and ECDC are conducting a joint audit of the rogue agent’s behaviour, which has revealed that its decision logic was irrecoverable due to opaque weight updates — a failure that underscores the necessity of built-in interpretability. In parallel, the Commission is drafting a Council recommendation on High-Consequence AI Procurement, which will require all EU and member state agencies to prioritize auditable systems in critical infrastructure, public health, and emergency response. The message is clear: when the frontier is uncontrollable and the gate is closed, sovereignty lies not in raw capability, but in knowable systems that can be trusted when it matters most.
