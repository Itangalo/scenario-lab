## In practice
This turn, we are finalizing the governance and funding architecture for the Open Science Resilience Initiative. The Commission has secured binding commitments from Germany, France, and Italy to host the first three operational hubs, co-located with national biosecurity agencies and integrated into the Digital Innovation Hub network. DG CONNECT and DG SANTE are leading a joint task force to finalize staffing, legal mandates, and data-sharing protocols by June, with Sweden and the Netherlands expected to confirm participation by Q3. Funding is being drawn from redirected Horizon Europe allocations (€2.8bn) and a €1.4bn Eurobond under ESM oversight, structured as a recoverable grant to preserve long-term fiscal sustainability.

Simultaneously, we are responding to the eu_frontier_access_denied event by activating emergency procurement clauses under the Single Market Emergency Instrument to ensure continuity of AI-dependent services in healthcare and public administration. Hospitals in five member states lost access to diagnostic support models overnight; we are fast-tracking deployment of the initiative’s open protein folding models as a stopgap, while launching a Council inquiry into the legal and strategic implications of foreign-controlled critical infrastructure. The election_annulled event further strengthens our case: we are drafting a Communication on Democratic Resilience in the Age of Uncontrolled AI, linking the erosion of public trust to the opacity of systems shaping information, infrastructure, and institutions. The message is clear—sovereignty is not about matching brute capability. It is about being the power that knows what its AI does, why, and under what constraints. That is the foundation we are building now.
