## In practice
This turn, we are embedding the Open Science Resilience Initiative in operational reality. The Commission, through DG CONNECT and DG SANTE, is finalizing binding implementation agreements with the three national hubs—Germany’s Max Planck Digital Society Node, France’s ANRSI-affiliated centre in Saclay, and the Italian CNR hub in Pisa—securing staff secondments, compute allocations, and integration protocols with national biosecurity and energy agencies. Legal frameworks are being aligned under Article 114 TFEU for internal market harmonization and Article 188 for public health emergencies, enabling rapid adoption of outputs. The delayed hubs in the Netherlands and Sweden remain under negotiation, with fiscal reservations in Utrecht and Stockholm tied to broader concerns over Horizon Europe reallocations.

We are also responding to the election_annulled and eu_frontier_access_denied shocks through crisis diplomacy and institutional hardening. On access denial, we have invoked the Anti-Coercion Instrument’s emergency clause, launching a legal and political assessment of countermeasures—ranging from data flow restrictions to compute export licensing—while simultaneously accelerating the openweight replication of critical model functionalities within the Initiative’s testbeds. The message is clear: sovereignty is not a backup plan. It is the only way to guarantee continuity in health, energy, and democratic infrastructure. On resilience, we are accepting the joint_threat_response invitation into the emerging biosurveillance and cyber command pact, but conditioning full participation on reciprocal access to EU-developed interpretability tools—turning technical openness into diplomatic leverage. This is not charity. It is a trade: transparency for trust, and trust for inclusion.
