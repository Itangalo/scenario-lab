## In practice
This turn, we are embedding the Open Science Resilience Initiative into the core of EU crisis response and technical sovereignty. The Commission has activated the initiative’s emergency protocol under Article 122a TFEU, directing the three operational hubs—Berlin, Paris, and Milan—to produce auditable agent frameworks capable of managing grid stability without opaque decision loops. These will be stress-tested this quarter by ENISA and ACER in a live simulation of the January incident, with results feeding into a Council-level review of AI in critical infrastructure. DG CONNECT is finalizing data-sharing agreements with national grid operators, overcoming resistance from private utilities through binding arbitration under the Clean Energy Package, while DG SANTE negotiates access to hospital energy management systems as testbeds—framing the intervention as patient safety, not AI policy.

We are also responding to eu_frontier_access_denied by re-routing urgent public health AI demand—particularly in diagnostics and drug interaction modelling—through the open protein folding stack now running in Utrecht and Stockholm. Though slower than the frontier, its outputs are legally defensible and politically controllable, a distinction now decisive in procurement. A working group under the Digital Ministers’ Council is drafting a Binding Technical Standard under Article 15 of the AI Act to mandate auditable reasoning traces in all AI used in essential services, starting with healthcare and energy. The standard will be ready by turn 11, timed to coincide with the first full outputs of the initiative. This is no longer about catching up. It is about defining a different race—one we can win without losing control.
