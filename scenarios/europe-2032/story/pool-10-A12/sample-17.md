## In practice
This turn, we are activating emergency powers under Article 122a TFEU and the Digital Markets Act’s systemic provider designation to fast-track the Emergency Sovereignty Acceleration Facility. The Commission is drafting a temporary derogation from state aid rules and public procurement directives to allow direct, non-competitive funding of auditable AI deployments in energy grid management, hospital diagnostics, and cross-border cyber defence—targeting operators who have already integrated models from the Open Science Resilience Initiative. Funding of €4.2bn will be drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s unused digital tranche and reallocated cohesion funds, with disbursement tied to binding commitments on interpretability, logging, and EU-based hosting.

We are confronting resistance from national regulators and private operators who argue that auditable models are slower and less capable than frontier alternatives. To overcome this, we are offering liability shielding for approved systems and mandating that any AI operating in critical infrastructure must either be EU-auditable or backed by a sovereign failover system built on open, verifiable weights. The Council Presidency is convening an extraordinary meeting of the Competitiveness and Internal Market formations to secure qualified majority support, bypassing deadlock in the Digital Agenda group. ENISA and ECDC are being tasked with issuing emergency technical standards by Q3 2031 to define what constitutes a sovereign failover, effectively setting the benchmark for what counts as controllable AI in the terminal zone. This is not a choice between innovation and safety—it is a race to define which kind of capability will anchor our future: the kind we can see, or the kind we cannot.
