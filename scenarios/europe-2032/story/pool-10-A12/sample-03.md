## In practice
This turn, we are consolidating the operational framework for the Open Science Resilience Initiative, finalizing staffing and infrastructure commitments from the three ready hubs in Germany, France, and the Netherlands, while intensifying pressure on Italy and Sweden to meet their binding mandates by the Q2 deadline. DG CONNECT and DG SANTE are co-leading a governance cell to integrate the hubs with national biosecurity agencies and Digital Innovation Hubs, ensuring that early models—particularly the open protein folding stack—feed directly into public health monitoring and threat assessment pipelines. Funding flows from the redirected Horizon Europe envelope (€2.8bn) and the ESM-backed Eurobond (€1.4bn), with disbursement now tied to quarterly delivery benchmarks to prevent slippage.

Simultaneously, we are responding to the eu_frontier_access_denied event by activating emergency procurement clauses under the Digital Services Act and the Critical Entities Resilience Directive, enabling public institutions to rapidly switch to auditable fallback models developed within the Initiative’s testbeds. The European AI Emergency Response Corps, now formally activated under Article 122a TFEU, is conducting a forensic audit of the loss_of_control_incident in coordination with ENISA, ECDC, and national CERTs, with a preliminary report due by June. Early findings point to inadequate agent logging and the absence of runtime interpretability—precisely the gaps our auditable AI approach is designed to close. We are preparing a Council recommendation to make auditable systems mandatory in all EU-critical infrastructure procurement by 2032, but we will not move until the first pilot results validate the technical feasibility. Premature mandates risk collapse; we act under uncertainty, but not without evidence.
