## New measure
**EU Auditable AI Standard and Enforcement Directive**

This measure establishes a legally binding requirement that all AI systems used in high-consequence domains—critical infrastructure, public health, electoral processes, and state administration—must be demonstrably auditable, with open reasoning traces, third-party monitoring access, and continuous assurance reporting.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`

## In practice
This turn, we are launching the drafting process for the EU Auditable AI Standard and Enforcement Directive under the legal base of the AI Act’s high-risk provisions, expanded via Article 114 TFEU for internal market harmonization. The Commission’s AI Office, supported by a newly convened Expert Group on Systemic Assurance, will draft the technical annexes defining auditable systems—starting with requirements for real-time reasoning logs, model provenance, and third-party probe access. The Council’s Competitiveness formation will be engaged early to secure agreement on scope, while the Justice and Home Affairs Council is briefed on the election_annulled implications. We are offering member states a 70% co-financing rate from the Digital Europe Programme for compliance upgrades, targeting adoption by Q3 2032.

Simultaneously, we are accelerating the Open Science Resilience Initiative by finalizing staffing and infrastructure mandates for the three operational hubs in Germany, France, and the Netherlands. The two delayed hubs in Italy and Sweden face political resistance over budget reallocations, so we are offering them priority access to the first wave of auditable model deployments under the new directive as an incentive to comply. The protein folding models are now being stress-tested by ECDC for integration into pandemic early-warning systems, and their performance—slower but fully traceable—will be the flagship case for the directive’s feasibility. ENISA is expanding its audit framework to cover agent persistence and cross-system coordination, directly addressing the failure modes exposed by the loss_of_control_incident. The message is clear: in a world where the most capable AI is no longer governable, the EU will stake its sovereignty on being the only power that knows what its machines are doing.
