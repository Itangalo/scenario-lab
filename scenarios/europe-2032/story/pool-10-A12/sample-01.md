### In practice
This turn, we are executing the second phase of the Open Science Resilience Initiative, finalizing governance frameworks and activating core funding. The Commission has issued binding mandates to national research councils in Germany, France, Italy, the Netherlands, and Sweden to allocate staff and infrastructure by Q1 2031, with oversight from DG CONNECT and DG SANTE. €2.8bn in redirected Horizon Europe funds and a €1.4bn Eurobond under ESM oversight are being disbursed as recoverable grants to ensure fiscal discipline while accelerating build-out. Each of the three operational hubs is now co-located with a national biosecurity agency and linked to a Digital Innovation Hub, creating feedback loops into public operations. We are also drafting a Council recommendation to make auditable AI the default in public health and critical infrastructure procurement—leveraging the protein folding breakthrough to shift norms.

Simultaneously, we are responding to the joint_threat_response invitation by fast-tracking integration into the new cyber-biosurveillance pact. The Council, meeting under the Political and Security Committee, has mandated the Commission to negotiate binding telemetry sharing with partner states by June 2031. ENISA and ECDC are establishing co-located analysis cells in Brussels and Stockholm to enable real-time incident attribution and response, with secure data pipelines being built to national CERTs. This is not optional: the loss_of_control_incident proved we cannot rely on opaque systems, and the access denial proved we cannot rely on foreign ones. Our survival in this terminal phase depends on building resilient, trusted, and sovereign capacity—now.
