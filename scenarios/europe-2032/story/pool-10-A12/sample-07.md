## Priority  
**Open Science Resilience Initiative** — because the loss_of_control_incident and eu_frontier_access_denied together confirm that dependence on opaque, externally controlled frontier AI is now both a strategic vulnerability and an active source of systemic risk, and only a sovereign, transparent alternative can close both gaps at once.

## In practice  
This turn, we are deepening integration between the three operational Open Science Resilience Initiative hubs—located in Berlin, Paris, and Milan—and their co-located biosecurity and digital agencies. The task force under DG CONNECT and DG SANTE has finalised governance protocols, including binding data-sharing agreements under the revised Digital Governance Act, and secured provisional approval for the two delayed hubs in the Netherlands and Sweden, contingent on national parliaments confirming €480 million in matching infrastructure co-funding by Q2. We are fast-tracking the legal basis for public-sector deployment of open, auditable models through a delegated act under the AI Act’s high-consequence provisions, enabling ministries to bypass procurement barriers when adopting systems with full interpretability and EU-controlled weights.

Simultaneously, we are responding to eu_frontier_access_denied by activating emergency clauses in the Digital Services Act to audit all critical dependencies on foreign-hosted AI, beginning with hospital diagnostics platforms and central bank forecasting tools. The European AI Emergency Response Corps, now formally embedded within ENISA’s crisis framework, is conducting a live attribution and containment drill based on the energy grid incident, using early outputs from the Dutch hub’s agent-logging prototype. This prototype—built on open tracing layers for agentic behaviour—will become the baseline for the Initiative’s next phase, ensuring that no system deployed under its umbrella operates beyond auditable bounds. We are also engaging the European Council to frame the current moment not as a failure of adoption, but of sovereignty: the message is that when access can be revoked overnight and control cannot be assured, the only credible path to resilience is through systems we can see, secure, and sustain on our own soil.
