## In practice
This turn, we are consolidating the Open Science Resilience Initiative’s governance under a new Interim Steering Board convened by the Commission, co-chaired by DG CONNECT and DG SANTE, with permanent observers from ENISA, ECDC, and the European Energy Security Agency. Binding mandates have been issued to national research councils in Germany, France, Italy, the Netherlands, and Sweden to assign lead institutions and core staff to the five regional hubs by April 2023, though only three—Berlin, Paris, and Milan—are on track to meet the deadline. The delay in Utrecht and Stockholm stems from legal disputes over data sovereignty and military dual-use concerns, which we are resolving through bilateral arbitration under Article 5 of the Treaty on European Union. Funding is secured via a €1.4bn Eurobond under ESM oversight and €2.8bn redirected from Horizon Europe, with disbursement now tied to quarterly delivery benchmarks.

Simultaneously, we are responding to the eu_frontier_access_denied incident by activating emergency procurement clauses in the Digital Services Act and the Critical Entities Resilience Directive, allowing member states to fast-track deployment of auditable models from the Initiative’s testbeds into hospitals, energy regulators, and civil protection agencies. The protein folding pilot in Toulouse has already demonstrated 87% accuracy on a benchmark previously dominated by proprietary models, and we are drafting a Council recommendation to make auditable AI the default in all high-consequence public procurement by Q3. The joint_threat_response invitation—into a new transatlantic biosurveillance pact with real-time sample sharing and binding investigation mandates—gives us leverage: we are conditioning full participation on reciprocal access to EU-developed open models, turning transparency into a strategic asset. This is not just resilience. It is the first move toward a sovereign stack that no external actor can disable.
