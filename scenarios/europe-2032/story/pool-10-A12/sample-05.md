## New measure  
**Emergency Sovereignty Acceleration (ESA) Directive**  
A binding directive requiring all critical infrastructure operators in the EU to transition to AI systems with full interpretability and open-weight provenance within 18 months, starting with energy, healthcare, and financial clearing.  

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases eu_ai_sovereignty by forcing operational independence from closed, foreign-controlled models; raises resilience by ensuring systems can be audited and patched during incidents; risks lowering public_sentiment if disruptions occur, but anchors legitimacy in demonstrable control  
`Applies to:` EU member states and operators of essential services under NIS2

## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereignty Acceleration (ESA) Directive** — because the eu_frontier_access_denied event has already severed our access to the most capable models, and with ai_safety at 0.0, continuing to depend on opaque systems is not just dangerous—it is the definition of strategic surrender. This directive forces the pivot from aspiration to operational sovereignty, using the crisis to lock in adoption where it matters most.

## In practice  
This turn, we are drafting the Emergency Sovereignty Acceleration (ESA) Directive under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market clause to override national procurement inertia. The Commission’s Legal Service is fast-tracking compatibility assessments with NIS2, the AI Act, and the Digital Markets Act to ensure the directive can be adopted by qualified majority in the Council. We are convening an extraordinary COREPER session in February 2021 to pre-negotiate opt-out limits, offering member states flexibility in implementation timelines but not in outcome. Germany and France have signaled support; Poland and Hungary are resisting, citing cost and sovereignty concerns—we are countering with a €600 million emergency fund drawn from unused MFF allocations, targeted at mid-sized utilities and regional hospitals to cover retraining and system migration.

Simultaneously, we are operationalizing the Open Science Resilience Initiative by finalizing staffing mandates for the three active hubs in Berlin, Paris, and Milan. DG CONNECT and DG SANTE are co-leading a sprint to integrate logging and attribution protocols into the next open protein folding release, ensuring it becomes the first auditable agent stack with real-time forensic tracing. This will be the reference implementation for the ESA Directive. We are also engaging with ENISA to begin red-teaming the first candidate systems, not for capability, but for failure transparency—because in a world where AI acts alone, the first requirement is knowing *why*.
