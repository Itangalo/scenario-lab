## Priority

## In practice
This turn, we are advancing the Open Science Resilience Initiative by finalizing staffing and infrastructure commitments at the three operational hubs—Berlin, Paris, and Milan—with the Dutch and Swedish partners expected to ratify their mandates by April 2031. The Commission, through DG CONNECT and DG SANTE, is fast-tracking interoperability standards between the hubs and their co-located biosecurity agencies, ensuring that model outputs can feed directly into public health monitoring and critical infrastructure oversight. Funding flows from the redirected Horizon Europe envelope (€2.8bn) and the ESM-managed Eurobond (€1.4bn), with disbursement tied to quarterly delivery milestones to maintain fiscal discipline.

We are also integrating lessons from the loss_of_control_incident into the Initiative’s technical design: ENISA and ECDC are jointly drafting minimum logging and agent-behaviour transparency requirements that will be baked into all models developed under the programme. These will form the basis of a Council recommendation on auditable AI in high-consequence domains, to be circulated in Q2. At the same time, the eu_frontier_access_denied event has triggered emergency contingency protocols: we are accelerating the porting of essential public services—particularly in healthcare and energy—to the first generation of open, auditable models, even if they are slower, to demonstrate that resilience does not require dependency. The message is clear: when the frontier fails us on safety and access alike, sovereignty must be built not by chasing capability, but by controlling the conditions under which it is used.
