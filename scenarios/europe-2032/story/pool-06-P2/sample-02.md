## New measure
**Measure:** Establish the Open-Weight Monitoring and Response Unit (OWMRU)  
A dedicated public unit to continuously assess the capabilities and diffusion patterns of open-weight models, with authority to trigger containment measures, coordinate takedowns, and fund countermeasures when thresholds are crossed.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 5–8 points through early detection of misuse-ready open models; slow the uncontrolled spread of dangerous capabilities in the open ecosystem; strengthen enforcement of existing open-release thresholds under SMAF and national laws  
`Applies to:` EU member states, open-weight developers receiving public funding or hosting infrastructure in the EU, and any entity distributing models above 45.0 openweight_capability

## Priority
**Priority:** Enact the AI Containment Protocol  
We are pushing the Containment Protocol hardest this turn because the *capability_jump* has elevated multiple sovereign and near-sovereign systems into a range where autonomous operation poses unacceptable risks, and the *safety_breakthrough* only applies to closed, auditable systems — making air-gapped, multi-party control the only viable way to ensure that gains in safety are not immediately outpaced by gains in capability.

## In practice
This turn, we are acting on the conviction that control is no longer a supplement to capability — it is the condition of its survival. The *capability_jump* has pushed the frontier to 62.0, and while we do not yet know whether this leap was narrow or general, we cannot assume it was contained. The *safety_breakthrough* in interpretability is welcome, but it only benefits systems that are already under sovereign, auditable control — which most are not. That is why the AI Containment Protocol is now our priority: we are drafting the delegated acts under Article 16 of the AI Act to bind all systems above 55.0 to air-gapped operation with dual-key access, real-time anomaly detection, and mandatory human escalation for any autonomous goal pursuit. The EAAD, now formally operational, will lead compliance assessments, with support from ENISA and national cybersecurity agencies. We are fast-tracking integration with the SACP’s first cluster in Sweden, where physical air-gapping is being designed into the facility from day one.

At the same time, the *member_state_defection* — a major Central European state signing a bilateral compute access deal with a US hyperscaler under non-disclosure terms that bypass SMAF — has exposed a critical gap: our rules only bind those who choose to comply. To close it, we are launching the Open-Weight Monitoring and Response Unit (OWMRU) under the EAAD, with a lean mandate but real teeth: continuous scanning of model repositories, coordination with domain registrars and cloud providers to delay distribution of high-risk releases, and emergency funding to develop countermeasures when needed. This is not about stopping diffusion entirely — that ship has sailed — but about ensuring the EU can see threats coming and act before they land. The OWMRU will be staffed from existing digital vigilance pools and reinforced with rotating experts from national agencies, funded through the Digital Europe Programme’s emergency clause.

SMAF, EAAD, and CIRS all finish this turn, marking the end of a phase. Their institutional cores remain, but their emergency status does not. We are transitioning SMAF into a standing regulation, EAAD into a permanent directorate with expanded remit, and CIRS into ENISA’s new AI Threat Monitoring Division. The Tech Sovereignty Package is nearing its end too, but its permitting reforms are stalled in the Council over energy allocation disputes. We are offering a compromise: binding solidarity clauses in return for accelerated approvals, tied to the InvestAI Gigafactories’ progress. Public sentiment remains low, but we are not chasing approval — we are building legitimacy through demonstrable control. The message is clear: Europe will not be a bystander to its own future, nor will it gamble that the next leap in capability comes with a warning.
