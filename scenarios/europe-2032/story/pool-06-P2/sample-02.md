## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without societal resilience is a fortress with no people left to defend it.  
  - Grounds: The convergence of *capability_jump*, *member_state_defection*, and stagnant *resilience* at 38.0 reveals that even a perfectly secured sovereign AI stack is meaningless if critical infrastructure collapses under AI-amplified attack, or if public legitimacy evaporates. Our identity must now encompass not only control over systems but the capacity to absorb harm when it comes — not because we expect failure, but because only then can we act with credible authority.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also institutionalise the duty to adapt when execution lags strategy.  
  - Grounds: The *member_state_defection* this turn proves that our current portfolio — six major measures in flight — has exceeded our capacity to govern them coherently. We acted under uncertainty, but failed to build feedback loops that halt or redirect failing initiatives. Henceforward, acting under uncertainty includes mandatory mid-course reviews with teeth: if a measure does not deliver at least 60% of its intended progress by midpoint, it will be suspended unless a binding recovery plan is approved.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): From turn 6 onward, our two-year commitment is: **Ensure sovereign AI systems are both operationally secure and socially sustained — by hardening critical infrastructure against AI-enabled threats and anchoring public legitimacy through enforceable containment and transparent resilience.**  
  - Trigger: the *capability_jump* and *member_state_defection* this turn fundamentally alter the strategic landscape. The frontier has surged past 65.0, outpacing our sovereign stack; meanwhile, a core member state has bypassed SMAF to secure direct access to a US frontier model, fracturing our control regime. This proves that sovereignty cannot be built on control alone — it must also rest on credible protection of people and services, and on public trust that containment is real. We pivot from building sovereign capability *as an end* to treating it as a means: to protect, to stabilise, and to earn the mandate to continue.

## New measure

**Measure:** Launch the Critical Resilience Reinforcement Initiative (CRRI)  
A binding programme to complete the hardening of all critical infrastructure nodes against AI-powered cyber and physical attacks, with mandatory real-time intrusion detection, automated patch deployment, and cross-border failover protocols.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience from 38.0 to at least 60.0 by turn 8; reduce systemic patching delays; ensure that no single AI-amplified incident can cascade across more than one member state’s essential services  
`Applies to:` All member states, with binding targets for energy, water, rail, and healthcare control systems; enforced through the revised NIS2 directive and conditional cohesion funding

## Priority

**Priority:** Establish the Sovereign Model Assurance Framework (SMAF)  
We are pushing SMAF hardest this turn because the *member_state_defection* proves that without a legally enforceable, technically airtight regime for sovereign model control, even our most advanced domestic capabilities will be undermined by backdoor dependencies — and because SMAF is the only measure in flight that can still unify sovereignty, safety, and credibility before the next capability jump.

## In practice

We are acting on two fronts: closing the control gap and rebuilding the legitimacy gap. SMAF is now our centrepiece — not because it builds capability, but because it is the only thing left that can prevent its erosion. The Commission is issuing implementing acts under Article 114 TFEU to ensure SMAF applies retroactively to all SACP model development, including architecture-level audits before training begins. We are fast-tracking the EAAD’s first inspection cycle, leveraging its newly acquired legal authority to delay any sovereign model release that does not meet weight-security and air-gapping standards. France and Germany are finalising dual-key protocols for the first sovereign cluster in Lyon, with physical isolation to be verified by ENISA and the European Defence Agency jointly.

At the same time, we are confronting the consequences of CIRS’s incomplete rollout. The *capability_jump* has already enabled new forms of AI-driven zero-day exploitation, and with only 40% of systems upgraded, we are vulnerable. The new CRRI will use conditional access to the InvestAI grid and cohesion funds to compel full compliance by turn 8. We are invoking the Article 122 solidarity mechanism again, but this time with teeth: member states that fail to meet biannual resilience benchmarks will face automatic redistribution of their AI infrastructure subsidies to those who do. The Council will vote on this under the ECOFIN formation next month.

Public sentiment remains at 25.0, and the *member_state_defection* has been spun domestically as “pragmatic access” — a narrative we must break. We are launching a counter-campaign under the Digital Europe Programme, using regional town halls and AI-literate civil servants to demonstrate how CRRI and SMAF work together: not to build AI for its own sake, but to ensure that when AI strikes — as it will — our lights stay on, our hospitals run, and our people know who is accountable. This is no longer about catching up. It is about proving that the EU can still protect its people — and that, ultimately, is the only sovereignty that matters.
