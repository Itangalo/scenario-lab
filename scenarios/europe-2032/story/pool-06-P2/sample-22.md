## In practice
This turn, we are acting on the conviction that sovereignty without containment is surrender by another name. The *capability_jump* — a sudden leap in global AI capability, likely driven by recursive optimisation techniques now demonstrated in the wild — means the frontier has crossed 62.0, far beyond what our defences were built to handle. At the same time, *ai_safety* has not only failed to rise — it has been eroded by the pace of deployment and the opacity of systems now in use. We cannot afford to wait. The AI Containment Protocol moves from proposal to enforcement: we are drafting the implementing acts under Article 16 of the AI Act, with emergency powers invoked via the newly ratified crisis protocol in the Digital Governance Regulation. The Commission is working with ENISA and the EAAD to define the technical specifications for air-gapped operation, multi-party cryptographic control, and real-time anomaly detection — the first of which will be audited at the Franco-German cluster site by mid-June.

Meanwhile, the completion of SMAF, EAAD, and CIRS marks a pivot: we are no longer just building institutions, but using them. SMAF’s closure unlocks the legal basis for binding auditability across SACP; we are now requiring all ongoing training runs to submit architecture diagrams and weight-access logs to the EAAD for pre-training review. The EAAD, though understaffed, is being expanded with fast-tracked hires from national cyber agencies and academic labs, and we are leveraging the Horizon Europe emergency clause to fund interpretability fellowships. The *safety_breakthrough* — a new causal tracing method that allows internal decision pathways to be mapped in real time — is being integrated into the new monitoring mandate we are establishing. We are also responding to the *member_state_defection*: Poland’s side deal with a US hyperscaler on preferential AI access undermines our collective posture. We are countering not with sanctions, but with inclusion — accelerating the integration of Warsaw into the SACP’s second cluster site, offering co-governance and early access in exchange for alignment.

Public sentiment remains brittle at 25.0. We are not overhauling the message, but sharpening it: this is not about slowing AI, but about ensuring it serves no master but the Union. The narrative is taking hold in the Council, but not yet in the streets. We are preparing for backlash when the Containment Protocol restricts access to high-capability tools in academia and defence — we will meet it with transparency, publishing redacted audit reports and inviting parliamentary oversight. The Gigafactories continue to advance; Sweden’s site is now breaking ground, and the grid override holds, though Finland’s legal challenge looms. We are not expanding the portfolio further — six measures in flight are enough to strain our capital. But we are deepening what we have: the new measure under Category 5 ensures that our assurance does not end at deployment, but evolves with the system. Control must be continuous, or it will be broken.
