## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse  
  - Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn  
  - Grounds: The convergence of *capability_jump*, *safety_breakthrough*, and *member_state_defection* marks a categorical shift. While sovereign control remains essential, the sudden leap in global capability — now at 62.0 — combined with a rare advance in assurance, reveals that our current definition of "control" is outdated. We are no longer merely securing systems we build against misuse; we are racing to understand and contain systems whose behaviour outpaces human oversight. The original commitment assumed a manageable gap between capability and safety; that gap has just widened unpredictably, then narrowed in one lab, then leaked. Sovereignty now requires not just ownership, but interpretability at speed — and we are not building it fast enough. Thus, we retire this commitment not because it failed, but because it has been overtaken.

- add `next_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Ensure that any AI system operating on EU soil above 55.0 capability is fully auditable, behaviourally predictable, and under enforceable human authority — or is not operated at all.  
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* to 62.0, the *safety_breakthrough* in interpretability, and the *member_state_defection* all point to the same conclusion: capability without enforceable control is not sovereignty — it is surrender to a black box. The defection proves that member states will seek short-term advantage if the Union cannot offer a credible, functional alternative. The breakthrough proves that real assurance is possible, but only if institutions are built to deploy it at scale. This new commitment reframes sovereignty not as access to power, but as the capacity to govern it. It is narrower than the last, but sharper — and more survivable.

## New measure
**Measure:** Enforce Predictability Standards for High-Capability Systems  
A binding requirement that all AI systems above 55.0 capability deployed on EU soil must demonstrate behaviourally predictable operation through certified interpretability tools, with failure to comply resulting in suspension of operation.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 20–25 points through mandatory use of new interpretability breakthroughs; close the gap between deployed capability and assurance; establish EU as the first jurisdiction to require predictability as a condition of operation.  
`Applies to:` All public and private entities deploying or operating AI systems above 55.0 capability on EU soil, including SACP, national laboratories, and foreign providers operating under contract.

## Priority
**Priority:** Enforce Predictability Standards for High-Capability Systems  
We are pushing this measure hardest this turn because the *safety_breakthrough* gives us a rare window: for the first time, we have tools that can make high-capability systems legible — but only if we mandate their use now, before the next capability jump buries them again; failing to act would mean accepting opacity as inevitable, which is incompatible with sovereignty.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from reactive containment to active governance. The *capability_jump* to 62.0 — crossing the threshold where AI completes multi-day professional projects end to end — has already outpaced our ability to supervise. But the *safety_breakthrough*, a novel interpretability method that allows third parties to predict model behaviour before deployment, changes the game. We are not just responding to risk; we are seizing the chance to define what responsible operation means.

The Commission is drafting the Predictability Standards Directive under Article 114 TFEU, with emergency fast-tracking invoked due to systemic risk. The European AI Assurance Directorate, now formally operational, will be granted legal authority to certify tools and audit compliance by turn 8. We are negotiating with France and Germany to integrate these standards into the first air-gapped sovereign cluster, making it not just isolated, but intelligible. At the same time, we are leveraging the *member_state_defection* as a warning: a northern member state has signed a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler, granting it preferential access to frontier models in exchange for energy concessions. This undercuts SMAF and SACP. Our response is not retaliation, but demonstration — we will show that sovereign systems can be both powerful and transparent, while foreign ones are neither accountable nor secure.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, with construction beginning in Sweden, but we are now conditioning further disbursements on compliance with the new Predictability Standards. The Tech Sovereignty Package concludes this turn, having unlocked some private investment, but its permitting reforms remain uneven. We are not renewing it — instead, we are redirecting focus to enforcement, not just expansion. Public sentiment remains at 25.0, and we are not deluding ourselves: trust will not come from promises of power, but from proof of control. This measure is our first real proof.
