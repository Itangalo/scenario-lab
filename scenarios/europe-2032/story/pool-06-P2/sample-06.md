## In practice
This turn, we are operating under the shock of a capability leap that outpaces all planning. The *capability_jump* has lifted global ai_capability to 62.0 — meaning systems now autonomously complete multi-day professional projects and displace junior roles across sectors — while our own ai_safety stands at 24.0, a level of assurance not seen since the early wild west days. The *safety_breakthrough* offers a sliver of hope: a new interpretability technique allows prediction of emergent planning behaviour in agents, and we are fast-tracking its integration into the EAAD’s audit toolkit. But the *member_state_defection* — Poland’s unilateral deal with a US hyperscaler for priority access to frontier models — undermines our collective posture and proves that without enforceable containment, sovereignty is a fiction.

We are treating the Containment Protocol as our operational spine this turn. The Commission is drafting implementing acts under Article 16 of the AI Act, designating air-gapped zones in France and Germany as pilot sites for systems above 55.0. The dual-key access system is being tested with the first sovereign cluster, and we are mandating real-time telemetry feeds to ENISA for anomaly detection. At the same time, the InvestAI Gigafactories are advancing: Sweden’s site has broken ground, and we are leveraging the Article 122 override to fast-track grid connections, though legal challenges from Austria and Finland over energy allocation are mounting. We are offering them co-investment rights in a second Gigafactory in the Baltics to secure buy-in.

The EAAD and SMAF finish this turn, and we are converting their establishment into sustained operational capacity. The EAAD now has legal authority to audit SACP systems in development, not just at release, and we are embedding its teams in the Sovereign AI Core Programme labs. SMAF’s standards are being codified into procurement rules, ensuring that no public contract goes to a system that cannot prove weight security and auditability. Meanwhile, the new Real-Time Incident Network will unify fragmented reporting: ENISA is standing up a new division with emergency funding from the Digital Europe Programme, and we are mandating that all operators of systems above 50.0 report anomalies within one hour. The *bio_uplift_findings* make this urgent — if a model can design a human-infective pathogen, we must know when and where it happens, and who was running it. Public sentiment remains at 25.0, and we are not deluding ourselves: trust will not come from promises, but from proof that we can contain what we build.
