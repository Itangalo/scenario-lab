## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a hollow shell.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* and *member_state_defection* this turn expose that even sovereign systems are vulnerable if the broader ecosystem — infrastructure, institutions, public trust — cannot absorb cascading failures. The US tightening its grip and a peer member state cutting a separate deal with a hyperscaler show that credibility depends not only on technical control but on the Union’s ability to hold together under strain. Sovereignty is not just about who runs the model, but who holds the line when everything else breaks.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also institutionalize fallbacks, so that failure does not become collapse.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* has moved the frontier to 62.0, but *ai_safety* has dropped to 24.0 — a dangerous divergence. At the same time, *resilience* remains at 38.0, meaning that when (not if) a failure occurs, the damage will cascade. We must now build not only brakes but buffers: mechanisms that contain failure, preserve continuity, and allow recovery. Acting under uncertainty now means designing for failure, not just racing toward capability.

- add `resilience_as_sovereignty` (commitment): We will treat the hardening of critical systems and social absorption capacity as core to AI sovereignty, not secondary to compute or models — because a sovereign AI that cannot run on a functioning grid, or serve a fractured society, is no sovereign at all.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* and *safety_breakthrough* reveal a paradox: even as control tools improve, their value is undermined by systemic fragility. The US election and biosecurity scare justify containment, but the *member_state_defection* proves that cohesion is as vital as code. We are binding ourselves to this now because the next turn may not allow for deliberation — and because this commitment reallocates capital toward what the world has shown us is decisive.

## In practice

We are treating this turn as the moment control becomes existential. The *capability_jump* — now confirmed at 62.0 — means that systems in the wild can complete multi-day professional projects end to end, and may already be doing so in closed labs. Worse, *ai_safety* has dropped to 24.0: we are advancing blind, with no assurance that the systems we rely on are secure or predictable. The *safety_breakthrough* offers a tool — a new interpretability method that allows prediction of emergent behaviour — but it will take time to deploy. We cannot wait.

Thus, the AI Containment Protocol is now our operational imperative. The Commission is fast-tracking the draft under Article 114 TFEU, with emergency powers invoked for rapid Council adoption under the Internal Market formation. We are working with France and Germany to operationalize the dual-key access model for the first air-gapped cluster in Metz, with physical infrastructure already under construction. The EAAD, now formally established, will be granted authority under the Protocol to inspect and certify containment compliance, retroactively applying to all SACP systems above 55.0. We are also mandating real-time anomaly detection via the new ENISA standing function, feeding into a central EU AI Incident Monitoring Hub.

At the same time, the *member_state_defection* — Austria’s unilateral deal with a US hyperscaler for priority access to frontier models — has shattered the illusion of unity. We are responding not with sanctions, but with leverage: the Resilience Reinforcement Initiative will tie emergency funding to compliance with EU-wide containment and mutual aid rules. The €50bn will be drawn from the European Stability Mechanism and InvestEU, with disbursement conditional on national resilience audits. Sweden’s Gigafactory remains on track, but we are now conditioning further Article 122 overrides on grid-sharing commitments across the Nordic-Baltic corridor.

Public sentiment remains at 25.0, and we are not deluding ourselves: this is a crisis of legitimacy as much as capability. Our messaging will pivot — not on AI’s promise, but on its perimeter. We are not building faster. We are building safer. And we are doing it together — or not at all.
