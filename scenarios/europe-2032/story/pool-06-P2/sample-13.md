## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a hollow shell.
  - Grounds: The convergence of *capability_jump*, *member_state_defection*, and the stalled rollout of CIRS reveals that even a sovereign stack is meaningless if the society it serves cannot withstand cascading failures. The Union’s political agency depends not only on owning its AI, but on ensuring that its hospitals, grids, and institutions remain functional when attacked — whether by AI-generated pathogens, cyber intrusions, or political fracture.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; nor will we allow urgency to excuse fragility, and will now treat resilience as a core component of strategic credibility.
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* has widened the gap between what systems can do and what we can defend against, while *member_state_defection* proves that cohesion fails when burden-sharing is uneven. Acting under uncertainty now means building systems that degrade gracefully, not just ones that launch fast.

## Priority
**Priority:** Enact the AI Containment Protocol  
We are pushing the Containment Protocol hardest this turn because the *capability_jump* has rendered pre-existing safeguards obsolete — systems now exist that can autonomously exploit vulnerabilities faster than humans can patch them — and without enforceable operational isolation, even our sovereign stack risks becoming a vector of catastrophic failure.

## In practice
This turn, we are acting on the conviction that sovereignty is not just about who owns the AI, but whether it can be trusted not to collapse the house around it. The *capability_jump* has pushed ai_capability to 62.0 — systems now complete multi-day professional projects end to end — while ai_safety remains at 24.0, dangerously low. The US is tightening access, a member state has defected to secure bilateral terms with a hyperscaler, and public sentiment is stagnant at 25.0. We are outpaced, outmanoeuvred, and running out of capital — but not out of agency.

We are finalizing the AI Containment Protocol under Article 114 TFEU, with emergency adoption procedures in the Council’s Internal Market configuration. The Protocol will bind SACP participants, EU agencies, and any national entity operating a model above 55.0 to air-gapped operation, dual-key access, and real-time anomaly reporting to EAAD. France and Germany have agreed to host the first two sovereign clusters under this regime, but Spain and Poland are resisting the compliance burden — we are offering them priority access to InvestAI’s second gigafactory in exchange for alignment. Meanwhile, the EAAD, now formally established, is short 40% of its technical auditors; we are fast-tracking recruitment through Erasmus for AI Safety and offering triple salaries for red-team leads, funded from the Digital Europe Programme’s contingency line.

The Resilience Reinforcement Initiative launches this turn to close the gaps CIRS left behind. ENISA will issue binding patching SLAs for energy and water operators, with AI-driven compliance monitoring. HERA is standing up three regional bio-detection hubs using open-source pathogen classifiers, and we are mandating that all hospitals report anomalous infection clusters in real time. The Tech Sovereignty Package finishes this turn — it unlocked only €80bn in private investment, far below target — but we are not renewing it. Instead, we are redirecting its political capital toward enforcement of the new resilience standards, knowing that without them, every exaflop we build could serve only to accelerate our own disruption.
