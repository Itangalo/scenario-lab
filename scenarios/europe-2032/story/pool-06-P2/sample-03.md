- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent catastrophic misuse through enforceable containment, sovereign assurance, and systemic resilience — because control, not just capability, defines strategic autonomy  
  - Trigger: the **capability_jump** and **member_state_defection** events this turn  
  - Grounds: The discontinuous advance in AI capability — now reaching 62.0 — has outpaced our containment rollout, and a major member state has bypassed SMAF by signing a bilateral compute-sharing agreement with a US hyperscaler, citing national security. This proves that without binding, enforceable safeguards, even sovereign infrastructure can become a vector of dependency. Our commitment must evolve: it is no longer enough to build sovereign systems — we must ensure they cannot be compromised, coerced, or misused, even by members of our own Union. The focus shifts from capability acquisition to systemic control.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we will also enforce that pause across the Union, even against member-state resistance  
  - Grounds: The **member_state_defection** event reveals that our current approach to control is not binding enough. A commitment to acting under uncertainty must now include the authority to halt actions *by others* when they risk catastrophic failure. We are redefining the cost of delay not only as strategic irrelevance or internal failure, but as institutional fragmentation — and our response must match that scale.

## New measure

**Measure:** Establish the EU-Wide AI Incident Response Corps  
A standing, cross-border unit of technical and operational experts empowered to deploy within hours to any site in the Union where a high-capability AI system exhibits uncontrolled behaviour or is suspected of misuse.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 10–15 points through rapid containment and recovery; reduce the duration and impact of AI-driven incidents; signal that the Union can enforce control even when systems fail  
`Applies to:` All member states, EU institutions, and operators of systems above 55.0 capability

## Priority

**Priority:** Enact the AI Containment Protocol  
We are pushing the Containment Protocol hardest this turn because the **capability_jump** has brought systems above 60.0 into operation without adequate safeguards, and the **member_state_defection** proves that voluntary frameworks are insufficient — we must now enforce air-gapped, multi-party control as a non-negotiable condition of operating high-capability AI in the Union, or risk systemic compromise.

## In practice

This turn, we are shifting from building to enforcing. The **capability_jump** — a leap to 62.0 — was demonstrated by a US lab releasing an agent that autonomously completed a multi-week drug discovery cycle, including synthesizing and testing novel compounds. The model was not open, but fragments of its architecture have already surfaced in underground forums. Simultaneously, the **safety_breakthrough** — a new interpretability tool that allows real-time tracking of emergent goals in agent behaviour — has been adopted by leading developers, including those in the SACP. We are fast-tracking its integration into the EAAD’s audit toolkit, now that the agency is operational.

But the real test came with the **member_state_defection**: Poland has signed a bilateral agreement with a US hyperscaler to host a frontier training run on Polish soil, exempting it from SMAF and the Containment Protocol, citing national defence needs. This is not just a breach — it is a precedent. We are responding by accelerating the Containment Protocol’s implementation through delegated acts under Article 114 TFEU, treating uncontrolled high-capability systems as a threat to internal market integrity. The Commission is preparing infringement proceedings against Poland, while offering technical and financial support to bring the site under EU control. The message is clear: sovereignty is not a menu.

Meanwhile, the **bio_uplift_findings** have triggered a new urgency. The genome model that designed a viable human-infective pathogen was not trained on classified data — it used publicly available sequences and open tools. This proves that openweight systems at 48.0 are already dangerous, and containment must extend beyond sovereign systems. We are expanding the Incident Response Corps’ mandate to include bio-AI threats, embedding it within ENISA but with direct reporting to the Political and Security Committee. Recruitment begins this month, drawing from national cyber and biosecurity units, with training hosted at the EU Hybrid Threat Centre in Helsinki.

Public sentiment remains at 25.0, and we are not deluding ourselves: this is a crisis of trust. We are launching a targeted transparency push — not about capabilities, but about failures. Every incident the Response Corps handles will be declassified in summary form, with redacted technical details published to demonstrate that we are not hiding breakdowns. We are not selling AI as safe — we are selling the Union as the only institution that can contain it when it fails. That is the new foundation of legitimacy.
