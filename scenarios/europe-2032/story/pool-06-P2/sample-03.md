## In practice
This turn, we are racing to operationalise control where capability is already outpacing us. The *capability_jump* — a leap from 62.0 to at least 65.0, possibly higher — confirms that we are no longer building safeguards against future risk, but responding to present danger. The *safety_breakthrough* offers a narrow window: a new interpretability technique allows us to certify certain decision pathways in models before deployment, and we are fast-tracking its integration into EAAD’s evaluation protocols. But the *member_state_defection* — a northern member state signing a bilateral compute-sharing deal with a US hyperscaler, bypassing SMAF and Containment Protocol requirements — reveals that our legal framework is only as strong as its weakest enforcement point. We cannot afford to wait.

We are treating the Containment Protocol as the spine of our response. The Commission is drafting delegated acts under the AI Act’s emergency clause to apply the Protocol immediately to all SACP sites and any model above 55.0 capability, regardless of origin, if operated on EU soil. France and Germany are finalising dual-key access protocols for the first air-gapped cluster in Lorraine, with physical isolation and continuous monitoring to be operational by turn 7. ENISA is being tasked with red-teaming the Protocol’s weakest link: human-in-the-loop escalation. Simulations will run this quarter to test whether operators can realistically intervene when autonomous agents exceed behavioural thresholds.

At the same time, we are forcing EAAD into action before it is ready. Despite staffing shortfalls, we are activating its authority under Article 336 TFEU, deploying emergency teams composed of seconded national experts, cleared researchers from the EU’s quantum and space programmes, and vetted external auditors. These teams will begin on-site evaluations at the Swedish Gigafactory and the SACP testbed in Toulouse by mid-March, using the *safety_breakthrough* tools to probe for hidden optimisation pathways. We are also redirecting €12bn from the Tech Sovereignty Package’s permitting fund to accelerate grid resilience for sovereign clusters — a move that will anger developers in Ireland and Finland, but we will trade it for binding commitments on energy solidarity and audit access.

Public sentiment remains a liability at 25.0. We are not launching new campaigns this turn; instead, we are embedding containment messaging into every public statement on AI — framing air-gapping and dual-key control not as restrictions, but as the price of autonomy. The narrative must be clear: if we cannot contain it, we cannot trust it; and if we cannot trust it, we cannot govern.
