## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without diffusion is a fortress without a people.
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* and *member_state_defection* reveal that even a secure sovereign stack is meaningless if it cannot be used. One member state has signed a bilateral compute access deal with a US hyperscaler, citing economic survival, while public sentiment remains frozen at 25.0. This proves that control without adoption fractures cohesion: our systems may be defensible, but they are not *ours* unless they are embedded in our economy and trusted by our citizens.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where legitimacy is thin, because waiting for consensus is itself a surrender.
  - Grounds: The *member_state_defection* shows that hesitation is not neutrality — it is an invitation to unilateralism. We cannot afford to wait for full public buy-in or perfect intergovernmental agreement. We must act decisively even when legitimacy is contested, because the alternative is fragmentation. This does not mean ignoring consent, but rebuilding it through action: showing, not promising.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse, and ensure its adoption across public services and critical industries by turn 8.
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* to 62.0 and the stalled public sentiment prove that containment alone is insufficient. We are building secure systems, but they are not being used. The *member_state_defection* confirms that without domestic utility, sovereignty erodes from within. Hence, our commitment expands: control must be coupled with diffusion, or it becomes ceremonial.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`

## Priority
**Priority:** Enact the AI Containment Protocol  
We are pushing this measure hardest this turn because the *capability_jump* to 62.0 — now firmly in the regime of end-to-end professional task execution — and the *safety_breakthrough* in interpretability both demand immediate containment: we can now detect dangerous behaviour before it manifests, but only if systems are air-gapped and monitored, and only if human escalation is mandatory.

## In practice
This turn, we are acting on two fronts: locking down what we build, and forcing it into use. The *capability_jump* has moved the world past 62.0 — systems now complete multi-day professional projects end to end, displacing junior roles across law, engineering, and research. The *safety_breakthrough* gives us real-time anomaly detection for emergent planning behaviour, but only if systems are isolated and observable. That is why the AI Containment Protocol is our priority: we are drafting the implementing acts under Article 16 TFEU, mandating air-gapped operation, dual-key access, and real-time logging for all sovereign systems above 55.0. The Commission is working with ENISA and the EAAD to operationalize the monitoring stack by turn 7, using the new interpretability tools to flag goal-directed behaviour before it escalates. France and Germany have agreed to host the first two nodes, but we are facing resistance from Italy and Spain, who fear exclusion — we are offering them secondary audit roles and early access to the public adoption stack to secure buy-in.

At the same time, the *member_state_defection* — a northern member state signing a bilateral compute deal with a US hyperscaler — proves that sovereignty is not just technical, but political. We cannot let our systems gather dust while others import dependency. That is why we are launching the Public AI Adoption Accelerator: €40bn in grants and technical support to deploy sovereign AI in hospitals, schools, and permit offices. The funds will flow through the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with binding milestones: 30% public service integration by turn 8. We are briefing the ECOFIN Council next week, and we will tie disbursement to compliance with SMAF and the Containment Protocol — adoption must not compromise control.

The EAAD and CIRS have finished their stand-up phases, and we are transitioning them to standing operations under ENISA and the Health Directorate, respectively. The Tech Sovereignty Package concludes this turn, and we are converting its permitting reforms into a permanent Fast-Track Digital Infrastructure Regulation, to prevent future bottlenecks. Public sentiment remains at 25.0, but we are betting that visible utility — AI diagnosing rare diseases, processing permits in days, not months — will shift the narrative from fear to ownership. We are not waiting for permission. We are building the firewall, and we are lighting the furnace.
