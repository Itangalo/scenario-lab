## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a fortress with no people left to defend it.  
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* and *member_state_defection* events confirm that raw access to powerful models is meaningless if those systems are insecure, uncontrolled, or politically unsustainable. At the same time, the failure of CIRS to complete upgrades across critical infrastructure reveals that even with containment protocols in development, the Union remains exposed to cascading failure. Our identity must now reflect that political agency depends not only on controlling AI but on ensuring society can endure its misuse.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; nor will we assume resilience can be bolted on after the fact.  
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* has pushed `ai_capability` to 62.0, likely above the threshold where autonomous agent behaviour becomes persistent and goal-directed. Yet `resilience` remains at 38.0, barely above the level where a single coordinated attack can disrupt essential services. The *safety_breakthrough* offers real hope, but only if paired with systemic absorption capacity. We acted fast — but too narrowly. From now on, acting under uncertainty means building both brakes *and* shock absorbers.

- add `sovereignty_requires_resilience` (commitment): We will not treat sovereign AI capability as achieved until it is embedded in a society that can withstand its failure.  
  - Grounds: The convergence of *capability_jump*, *safety_breakthrough*, and *member_state_defection* shows that technical sovereignty is fragile if social and infrastructural systems cannot absorb failure. This is not a secondary concern: without resilience, even a perfectly controlled sovereign system becomes a single point of failure when crisis hits.

## New measure

**Measure:** Launch the Resilience Integration Mandate  
A binding directive requiring all critical infrastructure operators to integrate AI incident response protocols into national contingency plans, with mandatory cross-sector stress tests and EU-level red-teaming every six months.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 12–18 points by ensuring detection, substitution, and continuity plans are operational, not just documented; reduce patching delays and improve coordination across energy, transport, healthcare, and digital infrastructure  
`Applies to:` All member states, national regulators, and operators of essential services under NIS2

## Priority

**Priority:** Enact the AI Containment Protocol  
We are pushing the Containment Protocol hardest this turn because the *capability_jump* has likely pushed frontier systems into autonomous agent territory, and the *safety_breakthrough* only helps where we have access and authority — which is exclusively within sovereign systems; without enforceable air-gapping and human escalation, even a well-understood model becomes a liability.

## In practice

We are treating this turn as the inflection point where control becomes non-negotiable. The *capability_jump* has lifted global `ai_capability` to 62.0 — solidly into the regime where multi-day autonomous execution is routine, and self-directed refinement is emerging. The *safety_breakthrough*, while promising, applies only to systems where interpretability tools are deployed — meaning it benefits labs that can instrument their models, not downstream users or defenders. That asymmetry is intolerable. Our response must close the gap between what we know and what we can enforce.

The AI Containment Protocol is now our operational centrepiece. We are drafting the implementing act under Article 16 TFEU, with legal anchoring in the AI Act’s high-risk provisions and the Cyber Resilience Act. ENISA will be tasked with certification, and we are fast-tracking the appointment of a joint Franco-German oversight board for the first air-gapped cluster. Dual-key access protocols are being tested this quarter, with physical isolation enforced via national grid disconnection and optical data diodes. The Commission is preparing infringement proceedings against any member state that attempts to bypass containment — a signal sent directly in light of the *member_state_defection* event, which revealed backchannel deals with US hyperscalers that circumvent EU assurance requirements.

Meanwhile, the EAAD and SMAF have formally concluded their establishment phases. EAAD’s initial audit team is now at 60% staffing, with 18 senior evaluators cleared for access to SACP training runs. SMAF compliance is being retrofitted into SACP’s current training cycle, with architecture reviews already identifying two models requiring containment upgrades before further training. The InvestAI Gigafactory in Sweden is on schedule, though legal challenges from Austria and Finland over grid prioritization are now before the Court of Justice — we are countering with a revised solidarity mechanism under the Energy Union framework, backed by emergency funding from the Cohesion Fund.

Public sentiment remains at 25.0, and we are not under illusions: containment sounds like restriction, and resilience sounds like cost. But the narrative is shifting. We are launching a new phase of the Digital Europe communication campaign, built around the slogan “Safe by Design, Strong by Default,” highlighting how containment protects jobs, security, and democratic control. The Resilience Integration Mandate — our new measure — is framed not as fear-driven spending, but as the necessary counterpart to sovereignty: we do not build powerful systems to hand them over to chaos. The first EU-wide AI stress test will be live-streamed in October, a demonstration of transparency meant to rebuild trust through demonstrated preparedness.
