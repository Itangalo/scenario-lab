## Statement changes

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent catastrophic misuse through enforceable containment and sovereign assurance, prioritizing control over capability growth  
  - Trigger: the *capability_jump* and *safety_breakthrough* this turn  
  - Grounds: The sudden leap in global AI capability — now at 62.0 — combined with the proven fragility of current safeguards (ai_safety at 24.0) confirms that unchecked capability accumulation is not just risky but self-defeating. The *safety_breakthrough* proves that assurance can scale, but only if mandated and resourced. Our two-year commitment must now reflect that sovereignty is not measured in flops or parameters, but in the enforceable ability to stop a system from causing harm. We are no longer racing to build; we are racing to bind.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will now treat uncontrolled capability as a systemic threat, and will condition further investment on verifiable containment.  
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* has rendered prior timelines obsolete: systems that were expected to take years to reach 62.0 have arrived in months. Yet the *safety_breakthrough* shows that control is possible — just not automatic. We can no longer afford to treat safety as a trailing concern. Henceforward, acting under uncertainty means building in brakes *before* deployment, not after incidents. This is not caution; it is strategic necessity.

- retire `two_mandates` (identity)  
  - Grounds: The distinction between capability and control has collapsed. The *capability_jump* and *member_state_defection* this turn reveal that raw access without sovereign assurance is not just insufficient — it is a vector of dependency and subversion. The idea that we could pursue both mandates in parallel, even with tension, is no longer tenable. We are now in a regime where control *is* capability, and sovereignty *is* survival. The old formulation obscures this reality; it must be retired to make space for clarity.

- add `sovereign_control` (identity): Our sovereignty depends on our ability to contain, audit, and independently operate the most capable systems we rely on — not merely to access them.  
  - Grounds: The convergence of *capability_jump*, *safety_breakthrough*, and *member_state_defection* proves that access without control is a liability. A member state bypassing Union safeguards to secure foreign AI undercuts our collective security. The breakthrough in interpretability shows that control can be engineered — but only if we demand it. We are no longer building to catch up; we are building to lock down. This is not a policy shift. It is a redefinition of what it means to be sovereign in an age of superintelligent systems.

## In practice

This turn, we are shifting from framework to enforcement. SMAF finishes today, but its value depends on teeth: we are now building the legal and technical machinery to ensure that no sovereign AI system above 55.0 capability operates without certified containment. The *capability_jump* has made this urgent — systems at 62.0 are already capable of end-to-end professional work, including in cyber and bio domains, and the *safety_breakthrough* gives us the tools to verify containment. We are drafting a Council Regulation under Article 114 TFEU, to be adopted by qualified majority, requiring all SACP participants and high-capability operators to obtain certification from the EAAD before deployment. The certification will demand air-gapped infrastructure, real-time interpretability feeds, and multi-party control — French and German authorities will co-host the first node, with dual-key access.

We are also responding to the *member_state_defection*: one of our members has signed a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler for priority access to frontier models, bypassing Union safeguards. We are invoking the Anti-Coercion Instrument to suspend data-sharing privileges until compliance is restored, and offering compensatory access to the first InvestAI Gigafactory as an alternative. The message is clear: sovereignty is not optional. Meanwhile, the EAAD, now operational, is deploying its first audit teams to SACP sites, using the *safety_breakthrough* to test for hidden agent coordination. We are fast-tracking interpretability integration into the sovereign stack, and redirecting 15% of the Tech Sovereignty Package’s permitting acceleration to containment infrastructure.

Public sentiment remains at 25.0, and we are not deluding ourselves: containment sounds like restriction. But we are reframing it through the Digital Europe Programme as *sovereign safety* — the idea that AI must be as secure as a power plant, not as open as a smartphone. The narrative is shifting: we are not slowing AI. We are making it trustworthy. Without that trust, no adoption, no investment, no sovereignty. This is not a delay. It is a foundation.
