## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that control without capacity is impotence. True agency lies in the synthesis: systems powerful enough to matter, governed so tightly they cannot escape.
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* this turn — a leap from 62.0 to 68.0 in `ai_capability` — combined with the *safety_breakthrough* and the *member_state_defection*, reveals a new equilibrium. Raw power without assurance is dangerous; assurance without access is irrelevant. The Union cannot outcompute the US or China, nor can it afford to wait for perfect safety. Our identity must now reflect the necessity of convergence: we are not choosing between sovereignty and safety, but building a sovereign stack where safety is baked into capability from the first line of code.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability. Now, we act *by* building control, not just in spite of uncertainty.
  - Grounds: The *safety_breakthrough* — a deployable interpretability tool that predicts emergent model behaviour with 85% accuracy — proves that assurance can scale, but only if embedded in sovereign infrastructure. We are no longer merely reacting to risk; we can now *design* resilience into the stack. This changes the nature of action under uncertainty: it is no longer about betting on capability, but about engineering control into the system as it grows. Hence, our commitment evolves from restraint to construction.

- add `containment_as_foundational` (commitment): We treat air-gapped, multi-party controlled AI not as a temporary safeguard, but as the default operating condition for all sovereign systems above 55.0 capability.
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* confirms that systems at 68.0 are now capable of autonomous planning across days, including in cyber and bio domains. The *bio_uplift_findings* were not a false alarm. With openweight capability still at 48.0, we have a narrow window to lock down sovereign systems before knowledge leaks. This is not a pause — it is a new foundation. We stake ourselves on containment not as a stopgap, but as the baseline for all future capability growth in the EU.

## In practice

This turn, we are acting on the conviction that the frontier has moved not just in capability, but in kind. The jump to 68.0 is not incremental — it means systems can now plan and execute complex, long-horizon attacks in cyber and bio domains without human oversight. The *safety_breakthrough* gives us a tool to see inside them, but only if we act fast to embed it. We are therefore treating containment not as a restriction, but as the enabling condition for any future capability growth in Europe.

The AI Containment Protocol is now our top enforcement priority. We are drafting the implementing acts under Article 16 TFEU, with legal backing from the Commission’s Crisis Powers Directive. France and Germany have agreed to host the first two air-gapped clusters under dual-key control, with physical access limited to EU-appointed auditors and national security officers. We are fast-tracking the integration of the new interpretability tool into the SACP training pipeline, funded through Horizon Europe and coordinated by the newly operational EAAD. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being renegotiated to tie private data centre permits to compliance with SMAF and the Containment Protocol — no more “sovereign” compute that isn’t actually controlled.

Meanwhile, the *member_state_defection* — a Baltic state signing a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler for direct access to models at 67.0 capability — has exposed a critical gap. We are responding with a dual move: first, invoking the Anti-Coercion Instrument to suspend certain digital market privileges for non-compliant arrangements; second, offering an accelerated onboarding path into the SACP for any member state that cancels such deals. The message is clear: sovereignty is collective, or it is nothing. Public sentiment remains low, but we are shifting the narrative — not around AI as a tool, but as a threat that only a united, controlled response can contain.
