## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that without resilience, even controlled systems can collapse the societies they were meant to serve.

  - Grounds: The convergence of *capability_jump*, *safety_breakthrough*, and *member_state_defection* reveals that control is not enough: a sovereign system that cannot withstand cascading failure, or whose legitimacy fractures at the first sign of strain, is no more secure than one under foreign control. The Union must now treat resilience not as a background condition but as a core pillar of sovereignty — one that cannot be retrofitted after crisis.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control or resilience, and will pause where assurance or societal absorption cannot scale with capability.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* has pushed the frontier to 69.0, well into territory where uncontrolled systems can initiate cascading failures, while *safety_breakthrough* proves that assurance *can* keep pace — but only if given space to land. Meanwhile, *member_state_defection* shows that political cohesion frays when measures are seen as imposing asymmetric burdens. We must now build brakes that are not just technical, but social and institutional.

- add `resilience_as_sovereignty` (commitment): We will treat the hardening of critical systems and the absorption of disruption as equally strategic to AI sovereignty as compute or model control, and will reallocate capital accordingly.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* and the failure of CIRS to complete upgrades in time expose that even a perfectly controlled AI system is useless if the infrastructure it runs on — or the society it serves — collapses under AI-enabled attack. Resilience is not a supporting function; it is a condition of survival. This turn, we bind ourselves to it.

## Priority

**Priority:** Enact the AI Containment Protocol  
We are pushing the Containment Protocol hardest this turn because the *capability_jump* has moved the frontier to 69.0 — deep into territory where autonomous systems can initiate cascading failures — and the *safety_breakthrough* gives us a narrow window to lock in enforceable safeguards before labs scale again; without binding containment, even auditable models become vectors of harm.

## In practice

This turn, we are operating under fire. The *capability_jump* has vaulted the frontier to 69.0 — a leap that renders every deployment timeline obsolete and places autonomous, multi-day planning systems in the hands of private actors who are not bound by our rules. The *safety_breakthrough* — a real-time circuit-breaking technique for emergent goal pursuit — is a gift: it works on deployed systems, it is adoptable now, and the labs are eager to implement it. But it only works if we can mandate it. That is why we are fast-tracking the AI Containment Protocol through the Council’s Internal Market and Telecommunications configuration, using Article 114 TFEU as legal basis, with a qualified majority already secured through bilateral assurances to Poland and Spain on transition periods. The Protocol will require all sovereign systems above 55.0 to run air-gapped, under dual-key access, with the new EAAD — now operational — granted real-time monitoring authority and escalation power to halt operations.

At the same time, the *member_state_defection* — Hungary’s direct deal with a US hyperscaler for preferential model access — has shattered the pretense of unity. We are responding not with sanctions, but with leverage: the Commission is invoking the Anti-Coercion Instrument to condition access to InvestAI subsidies on adherence to SMAF and the Containment Protocol, making clear that sovereignty is not à la carte. The Tech Sovereignty Package finishes this turn, and we are converting its momentum into binding grid-access guarantees for Gigafactory sites, using the Article 122 override precedent set in Sweden to fast-track connections in Brittany, Sardinia, and Eastern Poland.

But the deepest shift is in doctrine: we now treat resilience as sovereignty. The Resilience Integration Directive, proposed this turn, will compel every critical operator to run AI-specific stress tests every quarter, report patching delays above 72 hours to ENISA, and integrate cross-sector response playbooks. We are funding this through the Digital Europe Programme’s emergency reserve, and we are backing it with a new mandate for the European Cyber Resilience Act to cover AI-driven supply chain attacks. The public may not see these systems, but they will feel the difference when the next wave hits — and this time, it will not cascade.
