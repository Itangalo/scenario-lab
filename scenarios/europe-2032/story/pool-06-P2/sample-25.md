## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that without resilience, even controlled systems can collapse under attack.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* and *member_state_defection* confirm that raw capability, whether foreign or domestic, is meaningless if it cannot be secured or sustained under crisis. The US posture shift and the open diffusion of biodesign tools show that access and safety are no longer separable: a system we cannot defend is not ours, and a society we cannot protect from its effects will reject it. Sovereignty is not compute, not code — it is the continuity of decision.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we must also act where resilience is the only assurance we can trust.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* has rendered current containment assumptions obsolete before they were implemented, and the *member_state_defection* reveals that political cohesion frays faster than technical systems fail. We acted under uncertainty last turn by building brakes — now, we must build buffers. The cost of being wrong is no longer just delay, but cascading failure across infrastructure and legitimacy.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Prevent catastrophic misuse through sovereign control of AI systems and the resilience to absorb attacks when control fails  
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* has outpaced our containment rollout, and the *member_state_defection* proves that no directive holds unless the Union can absorb the shocks that drive states to break ranks. Our commitment must now include not just the control of systems, but the endurance of society — for if critical functions collapse, no audit trail will restore trust.

## New measure

**Measure:** Launch the Resilience-by-Design Retrofit Programme  
A binding directive requiring all AI-integrated systems in critical infrastructure to be retrofitted with fail-operational redundancy, AI-driven intrusion detection with human override, and cross-border mutual aid triggers.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 15–20 points; reduce cascading failure risk in energy, water, and transport; ensure continuity of essential services during AI-enabled cyber or physical attacks  
`Applies to:` EU member states, critical infrastructure operators, and all entities receiving public AI integration funding

## Priority

**Priority:** Launch the Resilience-by-Design Retrofit Programme  
We are pushing this hardest because the *capability_jump* and *member_state_defection* prove that no amount of sovereign control can prevent disruption if the systems we rely on collapse under attack — and without societal endurance, even a perfectly governed AI will be abandoned in crisis.

## In practice

This turn, we are shifting from building sovereign systems to ensuring they can survive in a world that no longer waits for us to catch up. The *capability_jump* has vaulted the frontier to 65.0 overnight — likely from a US lab now operating under closed national mandate — and our own containment protocols are already outdated. Worse, a central European member state has signed a bilateral compute-sharing deal with Washington, granting preferential access to US models in exchange for intelligence cooperation, undermining our unified stance. This is not mere defection — it is proof that without resilience, sovereignty is a promise written on sand.

We are converting the momentum of SMAF and EAAD into operational reality, but with new humility. The EAAD, now formally established, is being staffed with fast-tracked hires from national cybersecurity agencies and academic labs, but we are reorienting its first audits not just on model weights, but on system resilience under stress. SMAF’s final rules are being adopted this turn, applying to all SACP systems, but we are adding a new clause: any model above 55.0 must demonstrate resilience to red-team AI-driven attacks before deployment. Meanwhile, the InvestAI Gigafactory in Sweden is breaking ground, but we are facing legal challenges from Austria and Finland over energy allocation — we are countering with binding solidarity clauses under Article 122, backed by emergency grid coordination funds.

But the core of our action this turn is the Resilience-by-Design Retrofit Programme. We are drafting it under Article 172 TFEU, with co-legislation in the Internal Market and Environment Councils. It will mandate that all AI-integrated systems in energy, water, and transport be retrofitted with fail-operational redundancy, AI-driven anomaly detection with human override, and automatic mutual aid triggers across borders. We are funding it through a €50bn draw from the SURE mechanism, repurposed for AI-era shocks. Resistance is expected from smaller states citing cost, so we are offering 80% co-financing and tying it to Digital Europe Programme grants. This is not just hardening — it is proving that the Union can protect its people when systems fail, because if we cannot, no one will trust the systems we build.
