## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that without resilience, even controlled systems can bring collapse.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* and *member_state_defection* this turn reveal that strategic shock can come not only from external denial of access or misuse of models, but from internal fragility in the face of cascading failures. Our sovereignty is not only in who runs the models, but in whether the society they operate within can withstand their effects — intended or not. The biosecurity scare and patching delays from CIRS show that resilience is not a secondary concern, but a core pillar of political agency.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience is weakest, even if the threat is not yet manifest.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* has moved the frontier into uncharted territory — ai_capability now stands at 67.0, with no corresponding rise in ai_safety, which has dropped to 17.0. At the same time, *member_state_defection* — Austria’s unilateral deal with a US hyperscaler for priority access to next-gen models — proves that fragmentation under pressure is real. We must now act not only on control, but on cohesion: reinforcing the weakest links in our societal fabric before they break under strain.

- add `resilience_as_sovereignty` (commitment): We now treat societal resilience as a strategic asset on par with compute and control, and will reallocate political capital accordingly to harden essential systems against AI-enabled disruption, regardless of origin.

  - Grounds: With *resilience* stagnant at 38.0 and critical infrastructure patching still delayed, the risk is no longer hypothetical. A single coordinated attack could cascade across energy, transport, and healthcare. This is not a technical shortfall — it is a sovereignty deficit. We bind ourselves to closing it, not because it is easy, but because without it, no amount of sovereign AI will save us.

## Priority

**Priority:** Launch the Critical System Continuity Initiative (CSCI)  
We are pushing CSCI hardest this turn because the *capability_jump* and *member_state_defection* expose a fatal gap: even if we build sovereign AI, our society cannot survive its misuse or failure — and without trust in continuity, public sentiment will not shift, no matter how secure our models are.

## In practice

We are acting on the conviction that sovereignty is not just about who controls the AI, but whether the lights stay on when it fails. The *capability_jump* to 67.0 — driven by a new agent architecture that self-optimizes across multi-week goals — has outpaced all safety assurances. ai_safety has dropped to 17.0 as developers scramble to interpret emergent behaviours. At the same time, Austria’s bilateral deal with a US cloud provider — granting it priority access to next-gen models in exchange for data sovereignty concessions — has shattered the illusion of a unified front. We cannot afford to lose another link.

Thus, we are launching the Critical System Continuity Initiative under Article 122 TFEU, treating AI-driven systemic risk as a matter of survival. ENISA will lead, with binding targets for all member states to implement failover systems in energy, water, transport, and healthcare by turn 8. We are allocating €45bn from the SURE mechanism and the Digital Europe Programme to fund redundant control systems and real-time anomaly detection. Red-teaming exercises will be mandatory every quarter, with cross-border mutual aid protocols activated automatically above threshold alerts.

We are also renegotiating the Tech Sovereignty Package to tie accelerated permitting to resilience compliance — no new data centre gets fast-tracked unless its local grid has failover capacity. The Commission is preparing infringement proceedings against Austria, but we are also offering a path back: full integration into the SACP air-gapped cluster in exchange for terminating the US side deal. We will not accept fragmentation, but we will offer a stronger alternative. Public sentiment remains at 25.0, but we are betting that demonstrating control *and* continuity — not just capability — will begin to shift the narrative.
