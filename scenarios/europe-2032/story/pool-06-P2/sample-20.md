## In practice

This turn, we are shifting from framework to enforcement. The *capability_jump* — now confirmed at 62.0 — means the world’s most advanced systems are already beyond the reach of our current safeguards, and the *safety_breakthrough* in mechanistic interpretability, while promising, only applies to models designed with transparency from the start. We cannot retrofit trust. Meanwhile, a major member state has signed a bilateral agreement with a US cloud provider for priority access to frontier models, bypassing SMAF and the SACP under the justification of “national competitiveness.” This is not just a breach of solidarity — it is a systemic vulnerability. We are no longer building sovereignty; we are defending it.

The EAAD and CIRS have now formally finished their establishment phases, and we are transitioning both into operational mode. EAAD’s first mandate is to conduct emergency audits of all SACP training runs above 50.0, using the new interpretability tools from the *safety_breakthrough* to map hidden optimisation objectives. CIRS becomes a standing ENISA-led monitoring function, now integrated with national CERTs and equipped with AI-driven anomaly detection for industrial control systems. But frameworks alone are not enough. That is why we are launching the EU Containment Enforcement Unit (CEU) under Article 114 TFEU, modelled on the European Labour Authority, with powers to inspect, redact, or suspend AI systems that fail containment checks — including those operated by member states. We are fast-tracking CEU staffing through secondments from national cyber agencies and the European Defence Agency, and securing its legal basis through a special session of the Internal Market Council.

SMAF finishes this turn, and we are treating its completion not as an endpoint but as a launchpad: we are issuing a delegated act under the AI Act to make SMAF compliance a condition for any public funding under InvestAI or the Digital Europe Programme. The first air-gapped cluster in France-Germany will be the CEU’s first audit target, with dual-key access protocols now in final design. Meanwhile, the Tech Sovereignty Package reaches its final negotiation phase in the Council, with Spain and Poland pushing for broader exemptions — we are offering them co-location rights in the Spanish InvestAI site in exchange for full alignment. Public sentiment remains at 25.0, and we are not deluding ourselves: trust will not come from promises, but from visible consequences for overreach. This turn, we are proving that the EU can say no — even to its own.
