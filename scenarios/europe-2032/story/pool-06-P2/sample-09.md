- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without societal resilience is a hollow shell.
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* and *member_state_defection* this turn expose a new truth: even if we build sovereign systems, they cannot protect the Union if its critical functions collapse under AI-enabled attack or if member states abandon collective discipline. Our identity must now encompass not just control over AI, but the capacity to endure its consequences — politically, socially, and physically.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; nor will we act without ensuring the Union can absorb the fallout if we are wrong.
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* has pushed ai_capability to 69.0, crossing the threshold where systems can autonomously execute multi-day strategic tasks — including cyber and bio-design — while ai_safety remains at 24.0. Meanwhile, *member_state_defection* proves that top-down control fails if local legitimacy erodes. Acting under uncertainty now requires not just brakes on capability, but shock absorbers in society and institutions.

## New measure

**Measure:** Launch the Critical System Resilience Reinforcement (CSRR)  
A rapid, EU-coordinated programme to harden and diversify essential services — energy, water, health, and transport — against AI-driven cascading failures, with mandatory failover protocols, real-time intrusion detection, and cross-border mutual aid triggers.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`

`Finishes on turn: 8`

## In practice

We are treating this turn as a breaking point. The *capability_jump* — a sudden leap in AI capability, likely from a self-improving training loop in a US lab — has pushed the frontier to 69.0. Systems can now autonomously plan and execute multi-day strategic tasks, including cyber intrusions and bio-design, with minimal human oversight. Yet ai_safety remains at 24.0: no meaningful assurance, opaque systems, poorly secured weights. The *safety_breakthrough* — a new interpretability tool that allows prediction of emergent behaviour — is promising, but it will take time to integrate. Worse, a major member state has just signed a bilateral deal with a US hyperscaler, granting it preferential access to frontier models in exchange for relaxed data governance — a clear *member_state_defection* that undermines our entire SMAF framework.

We are responding on three fronts. First, we are accelerating the AI Containment Protocol through COREPER II and the Justice and Home Affairs Council, invoking Article 83 TFEU for cross-border security. The directive will mandate air-gapped operation, dual-key access, and real-time anomaly detection for all systems above 55.0 on EU soil. France and Germany have agreed to pilot it at the new sovereign cluster in Saarbrücken, but we face resistance from smaller states fearing exclusion — we are offering them guaranteed audit access and shared training slots to secure buy-in.

Second, we are launching the Critical System Resilience Reinforcement (CSRR) to close the gap left by CIRS’s incomplete rollout. ENISA will lead, with binding targets for patching velocity, intrusion detection coverage, and failover testing. Funding will come from unspent RRF allocations and a new solidarity levy on critical infrastructure operators. We are already negotiating with the German and Polish grids to test failover protocols under AI-red team conditions next quarter.

Third, we are converting the EAAD from a start-up agency into an operational authority. With CIRS and SMAF now finished, we are reassigning their evaluation teams to the EAAD, consolidating our oversight capacity. We are also fast-tracking the safety_breakthrough for integration into sovereign systems under SACP, using the new Article 336 emergency research clause. Public sentiment remains at 25.0, but we are shifting messaging: this is no longer about catching up to the US, but about survival in a world where AI can break everything at once — and only collective, controlled systems can hold it together.
