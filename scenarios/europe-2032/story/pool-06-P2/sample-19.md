## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without societal anchoring is a fortress without a people.
  - Grounds: The convergence of *capability_jump*, *member_state_defection*, and stagnant public sentiment at 25.0 reveals that technical control alone cannot sustain political agency. The Union’s legitimacy depends not only on securing systems against external coercion or internal failure, but on ensuring those systems are seen as serving European interests — or risk being dismantled from within by democratic backlash.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control or legitimacy, and will pause where assurance or public trust cannot scale with capability.
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* has widened the gap between what systems can do and what we can govern, while *member_state_defection* proves that unilateral technical action erodes cohesion. We remain committed to decisive action, but now define the cost of delay not only as strategic irrelevance or catastrophic failure, but also as democratic disempowerment.

## Priority

**Priority:** Enact the AI Containment Protocol  
We are pushing this measure hardest this turn because the *capability_jump* has vaulted the frontier to 62.0 — well above the 55.0 threshold — and the *safety_breakthrough* only applies to systems we can inspect and constrain; without enforceable containment, even improved assurance tools cannot prevent catastrophic misuse in operational environments.

## In practice

This turn, we are acting on the fact that the world has moved faster than our defences. The *capability_jump* has lifted ai_capability to 62.0 — systems now complete multi-day professional projects end to end, displacing junior roles and contributing to their own successors. Yet ai_safety stands at 24.0, barely above the level of nominal monitoring. The *safety_breakthrough* offers real promise — interpretability tools that can predict behaviour before observation — but only if applied to systems we control and can inspect. That is why the AI Containment Protocol is now our operational priority: we are drafting the implementing act under Article 16 TFEU, mandating air-gapped operation, dual-key access, and real-time anomaly detection for all sovereign systems above 55.0. The Council’s Legal Service is reviewing compatibility with SACP’s research mission, but we are holding firm: no exemption for experimentation, no loophole for speed. France and Germany have agreed in principle to host the first cluster under these terms, but Austria has raised concerns about duplication and cost — we are offering them a seat on the oversight board and a pilot node for biosecurity monitoring to secure buy-in.

Meanwhile, SMAF and EAAD have formally finished this turn, marking a threshold: we now have both a legal framework and an agency with authority to enforce it. But authority means nothing without legitimacy. Public sentiment remains frozen at 25.0, and the *member_state_defection* — Poland’s unilateral deal with a US hyperscaler for priority AI access — proves that fear of exclusion is driving fragmentation. We cannot build a sovereign stack if member states see it as a bottleneck rather than a shield. That is why we are launching the European Social Licence Initiative: through the Digital Europe Programme and the upcoming Cohesion Policy review, we are directing €12bn toward co-designed AI pilots in public services, starting with cancer diagnostics in Lisbon, teacher support in Helsinki, and permit processing in Bologna. These are not showcases — they are commitments to deliver visible benefit, with citizen panels embedded in design and audit. We know this won’t move the needle overnight, but without it, no amount of air-gapping will protect us from democratic revolt. The Gigafactories and SACP continue on track, but their future depends not on exaflops, but on whether Europeans believe this project is theirs.
