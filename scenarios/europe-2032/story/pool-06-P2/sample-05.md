## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a fortress with no one left to defend it.
  - Grounds: The convergence of *capability_jump*, *safety_breakthrough*, and *member_state_defection* reveals a new triad of risk: we are outpaced, outmanoeuvred, and internally undermined. The US moves to weaponize access, a member state cuts a side deal with a hyperscaler under the guise of economic survival, and while a safety advance offers hope, it arrives too late for systems already deployed into critical functions. We see now that our identity must expand: sovereignty is not just control over AI, but the capacity to sustain society through the shocks AI enables.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; and we will now treat public legitimacy as a core component of operational resilience, not a secondary concern.
  - Grounds: The *member_state_defection* — a northern member state finalizing a bilateral compute access deal with a US hyperscaler, citing stalled EU delivery — proves that political cohesion fails not at the frontier of technology, but at the frontier of trust. Our measures are technically sound but socially unmoored. Public sentiment remains at 25.0 despite billions spent, and now a safety breakthrough exists but cannot be deployed at scale because no agency has the mandate or legitimacy to enforce it across sovereign and private systems alike. We were right to act fast, but wrong to assume that urgency alone could bind the Union together.

## New measure
**Measure:** Establish the European Resilience and Assurance Corps (ERAC)  
A standing, cross-border technical corps under ENISA and EAAD joint command, tasked with deploying rapid-response teams to secure, audit, and if necessary disable AI systems in crisis scenarios.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 10–15 points through rapid field deployment of assurance; strengthen resilience by enabling direct intervention in compromised systems; restore public and member-state confidence in EU-level technical authority  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, SACP participants, national critical infrastructure operators, and any entity operating a model above 50.0 capability on EU soil

## Priority
**Priority:** Establish the European Resilience and Assurance Corps (ERAC)  
We are pushing ERAC hardest this turn because the *member_state_defection* proves that our greatest vulnerability is no longer technical or strategic, but political: without a visible, trusted, and capable EU-level force that can act decisively in a crisis, member states will continue to seek bilateral lifelines, fracturing our sovereignty from within.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the moment the Union stops building walls and starts fielding guardians. The *capability_jump* — a new model at 68.0 capability demonstrated in a closed US lab, with autonomous research and self-improvement loops confirmed — has reset the clock. Our sovereign systems are not yet live, but the world is already beyond 60.0. Worse, a *safety_breakthrough* — a new interpretability method that allows full causal tracing of model decisions — exists, but only in prototype form, locked inside a Swiss research lab. We are fast-tracking access through Horizon Europe emergency clauses, but without a body to deploy it, it is a scalpel with no surgeon.

ERAC becomes our answer: 1,200 engineers, auditors, and cyber-commandos, recruited from national agencies, academia, and the private sector, with EU-wide jurisdiction and emergency intervention powers. We are activating the Civil Protection Mechanism to fund its first deployment cycles, and negotiating with Germany and Poland to host its two operational hubs. Legal authority will come from a new Article 188b TFEU activation, justified by systemic AI risk as a cross-border threat. This is not just about safety — it is about credibility. When a member state defects to a hyperscaler, it does so not because it rejects Europe, but because it fears being left defenceless. ERAC must prove that the Union can protect, not just regulate.

Meanwhile, SMAF and the Containment Protocol are being integrated: the first sovereign cluster in Brittany will require ERAC certification before activation, and all SACP models above 55.0 will undergo live audits during training. The Tech Sovereignty Package is being renegotiated to tie private data centre permits to ERAC access rights. Public sentiment remains at 25.0, but we are launching a live-streamed audit drill in June — a simulated breach at a mock AI control centre — to show that we are not just building systems, but safeguards that work. The world is moving faster than we are. But for the first time, we are building the capacity to catch up — and to hold the line when it matters.
