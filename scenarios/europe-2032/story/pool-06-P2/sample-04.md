## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that sovereignty without resilience is a fortress with no people left to defend it.  
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* and *member_state_defection* this turn reveal that even sovereign systems are vulnerable if the society they serve cannot absorb cascading failures. With resilience still at 38.0 and public sentiment stagnant, our control gains are meaningless if the next incident collapses trust faster than we can respond. Sovereignty must now mean not just owning the stack, but ensuring the system it runs on holds.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; and we will not assume resilience can be bolted on after the fact.  
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* has pushed ai_capability to ~65.0 overnight, while ai_safety has dropped sharply. Simultaneously, *member_state_defection* proves that without cohesion, even air-gapped clusters are politically porous. We acted fast to build control, but failed to harden the society beneath it. From now on, acting under uncertainty means building both brakes and shock absorbers — and recognising that political capital is not just spent on launches, but on keeping the peace when things go wrong.

- add `containment_first` (commitment): We will treat containment as the baseline condition of all sovereign AI operations above 55.0, and accept no capability gain that bypasses it.  
  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* and the biosecurity scare prove that frontier models are already capable of irreversible harm. The AI Containment Protocol is no longer a precaution — it is the floor. We bind ourselves to it now, knowing that reversal would mean surrendering control to forces we cannot audit, cannot isolate, and cannot stop.

Worse, the *safety_breakthrough* is narrow and not yet scalable, while *member_state_defection* — Austria’s backchannel deal with a US hyperscaler for priority access — proves that our sovereignty is only as strong as its weakest link. We respond by making containment law: the AI Containment Protocol will be adopted via emergency procedure under Article 114 TFEU, with the Commission issuing delegated acts to bind all SACP participants and national agencies operating high-capacity systems. This is no longer about catching up — it is about ensuring that when the next shock hits, Europe does not break.
