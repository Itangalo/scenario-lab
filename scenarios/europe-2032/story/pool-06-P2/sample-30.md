- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): We will secure sovereign AI control to withstand strategic shocks and prevent catastrophic misuse, but now with explicit prioritisation of containment and assurance over capability racing — control is the precondition of sovereignty, not a secondary concern.
  - Trigger: the **capability_jump** event, which increases `ai_capability` to a level where uncontrolled systems pose immediate catastrophic risks, combined with the **member_state_defection** that exposes the fragility of collective EU positions under external pressure.
  - Grounds: The jump in global AI capability — now at 62.0 — has outpaced our sovereign development timelines, and the demonstrated risk of biothreat design proves that unsecured systems, even if domestically built, are vectors of harm. Meanwhile, a member state’s unilateral deal with a foreign hyperscaler undercuts our collective leverage, revealing that without enforceable control, sovereignty is performative. We cannot afford to treat control as a follow-on: it must be the core of our strategy now.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now define commitment as the imposition of brakes, boundaries, and verification, not just the acceleration of build-out.
  - Grounds: The **safety_breakthrough** proves that assurance can scale, but only if mandated. Our previous framing risked equating decisiveness with speed of deployment; we now recalibrate: acting under uncertainty means embedding reversibility, audit, and failure containment into every system we enable. This turn’s events show that the cost of inaction is no longer irrelevance, but irreversible harm.

## New measure

**Measure:** Establish the Open-Weight Containment Boundary  
A legally binding restriction on the release of any open-weight model above 50.0 capability, enforced through licensing of training compute and mandatory pre-release evaluation by the EAAD.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` Prevent the uncontrolled diffusion of high-capability models by halting open releases at 50.0, reducing the rate of increase in `openweight_capability` by 50–70% over the next two turns; increase `ai_safety` by removing a key vector of unsecured deployment.  
`Applies to:` All developers operating within EU jurisdiction, including open-source collectives and academic labs; extends extraterritorial effect via cloud provider compliance and export controls on model weights.

## Priority

**Priority:** Establish the Open-Weight Containment Boundary  
We are pushing this hardest because the **capability_jump** has brought open-weight models within reach of catastrophic misuse — and with `openweight_capability` already at 48.0, one more release cycle could cross the threshold into uncontainable diffusion. While SMAF and the Containment Protocol secure our sovereign stack, this measure closes the backdoor: without it, any lab, anywhere in the EU, can release a system that becomes a global threat overnight.

## In practice

This turn, we are acting on the principle that sovereignty is not just about building our own AI — it is about controlling what cannot be allowed to exist. The **capability_jump** to 62.0, likely driven by recursive optimisation breakthroughs now visible in released architectures, means that even models trained just below the threshold can be fine-tuned into dangerous regimes with minimal effort. The **safety_breakthrough** in mechanistic interpretability — a method to certify reasoning pathways before deployment — gives us a tool, but only if we mandate its use. Meanwhile, the **member_state_defection**, in which Poland signed a bilateral compute-access deal with a US hyperscaler in exchange for hosting rights, has shattered trust in our collective posture. We cannot lead by example if our own members are undercutting us.

We are therefore shifting from containment of sovereign systems to containment of the ecosystem. The EAAD, now formally operational, is being empowered with emergency authority under Article 114 TFEU to block any open-weight release above 50.0 until evaluated. We are working with the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre to develop a real-time model-scanning grid, and with the Commission’s Legal Service to extend liability to cloud providers who host unlicensed training runs. The Open-Weight Containment Boundary will be negotiated in COREPER II this month, with a draft regulation tabled for Justice and Home Affairs Council in March. We are offering Germany and the Nordic states early access to the sovereign air-gapped cluster in exchange for support, while warning France that unchecked open releases from its academic labs could disqualify them from SACP funding.

At the same time, we are completing the transition of CIRS to ENISA as a standing monitoring function, with a new AI Threat Dashboard to track adversarial model use in real time. The InvestAI Gigafactory in Sweden is now under construction, but we are facing grid allocation disputes: Austria and Finland are threatening legal action over power diversion. We will respond with a revised solidarity mechanism under the Energy Union framework, tying access to cross-border resilience commitments. Public sentiment remains at 25.0, and we are not deluding ourselves: this is not a communications problem, but a legitimacy one. Our message must now be clear — we are not slowing AI, we are securing it. And we will not let anyone, inside or outside the Union, make the rest of us pay for their recklessness.
