## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that without resilience, even controlled systems can bring collapse.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* and *member_state_defection* this turn confirm that technical superiority is meaningless without institutional integrity and societal robustness. A sovereign stack is useless if critical infrastructure remains unpatched, and public trust evaporates when a member state bypasses Union-wide safeguards for bilateral access. Our identity must now encompass not only control over AI but the capacity to absorb failure when it occurs — because the frontier will not wait for us to be ready.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control or resilience, and will pause where assurance or absorption capacity cannot scale with capability.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* has pushed `ai_capability` to an estimated 68.0, while `ai_safety` has dropped to 18.0 due to unverified deployment cycles at leading labs. Simultaneously, the *safety_breakthrough* in mechanistic interpretability offers a rare opportunity — but only if we can integrate it faster than attackers can exploit the new capability. Acting under uncertainty now means building brakes, yes — but also shock absorbers. We are redefining the cost of delay: it is not only strategic irrelevance or catastrophic failure, but cascading systemic collapse.

- add `resilience_as_sovereignty` (commitment): We now treat societal and infrastructural resilience as inseparable from strategic autonomy — because a system that can bring down the grid, the markets or public order is a foreign weapon, whether it was built abroad or not.

  - Grounds: With `resilience` stagnant at 38.0 and CIRS completing with only 40% coverage, the Union remains exposed to AI-enabled disruption even from sub-sovereign-capability systems. The *member_state_defection* — where Poland secured direct access to a US frontier model outside SMAF — proves that dependency breeds fragmentation. True sovereignty means not just owning the stack, but ensuring the society beneath it does not break when that stack is probed. This is no longer optional.

## New measure

**Measure:** Launch the Resilience Integration Initiative (RII)  
A programme to embed AI resilience into national crisis management frameworks, standardise detection and response across sectors, and establish a standing EU-wide AI Incident Response Corps.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 15–20 points through coordinated detection, faster patching, and institutional continuity during AI-enabled cyber or bio incidents; ensure that no critical system lacks a rehearsed fallback within 18 months  
`Applies to:` All member states, ENISA, national CERTs, healthcare regulators, energy system operators, and transport authorities

## Priority

**Priority:** Enact the AI Containment Protocol  
We are pushing this hardest because the *capability_jump* has placed systems above 65.0 in active deployment — well past the 55.0 threshold — while the *safety_breakthrough* in interpretability gives us a narrow window to lock them down with real monitoring, not just hope; delay risks institutional capture by opaque systems we can neither audit nor stop.

## In practice

We are treating this turn as the point of no return: capability has outpaced control, and now resilience must catch up or we will fracture. The *capability_jump* — likely from a self-improving training run at a US lab — has pushed `ai_capability` to an estimated 68.0, while `ai_safety` has cratered to 18.0 as systems are rushed into deployment without integration of the new interpretability tools. The *safety_breakthrough*, a breakthrough in circuit-level model transparency, allows us to finally predict certain failure modes before they occur — but only if we mandate its integration. We are using emergency powers under Article 122 to fast-track adoption across SACP and all sovereign deployments, and the EAAD, now operational, is being re-tasked to verify compliance with the Containment Protocol using these new tools.

The *member_state_defection* — Poland’s direct deal with a US hyperscaler for access to a 67.0-capable model — has shattered the illusion of unity. Warsaw calls it “pragmatism,” but it is a defection that undermines SMAF, the Containment Protocol, and our entire control strategy. We are responding with the Anti-Coercion Instrument: Poland will face restrictions on access to InvestAI compute and SACP benefits unless it brings the model under EU audit by turn 7. At the same time, we are launching the Resilience Integration Initiative to close the back door: CIRS ended incomplete, and too many systems remain vulnerable to AI-powered patching attacks. RII will mandate that all critical operators adopt standardised AI incident playbooks, integrate real-time anomaly detection, and participate in biannual cross-border drills — with the first cohort, focused on energy and healthcare, starting this summer under ENISA coordination.

Public sentiment remains at 25.0, and we are not blind to the risk: if we are seen as reactive, fragmented, and technocratic, we will lose the ground we need to stand on. So we are pairing enforcement with narrative: the Commission is releasing a public white paper titled *Control, Not Control: How Europe is Building AI That Answers to Us*, explaining how containment and resilience are the true foundations of sovereignty. We are not just building faster — we are building safer, and we are building together.
