## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that capability without control is a path to dependency, not sovereignty — and that without resilience, even controlled systems can bring collapse.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* and the *member_state_defection* confirm that raw capability is now outpacing governance not only in the labs but across our own institutions. The US tightening export controls and a core member state bypassing Union protocols to secure bilateral access reveal that sovereignty is fracturing under pressure. Meanwhile, the *safety_breakthrough* proves that assurance can scale — but only if embedded in systems that are hardened against failure and society is hardened against shock. We are not merely balancing two mandates; we are discovering that all three — control, capability, and resilience — are interdependent legs of one stool.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat speed as a substitute for control, and will pause where assurance cannot scale with capability; now, we also act where resilience has not caught up, even if the threat is not yet manifest.

  - Grounds: The *capability_jump* to 62.0, combined with stagnant resilience at 38.0 and public sentiment at 25.0, means that the next incident will not just expose a vulnerability — it will exploit a cascade. The *safety_breakthrough* gives us a rare window: for the first time, we can see inside systems well enough to trust them — but only if the systems they govern are not already brittle. We are redefining the cost of delay: it is no longer only failure of control or capability, but the collapse of essential services under AI-amplified attack. Henceforward, acting under uncertainty means building redundancy, not just audits.

- add `resilience_as_strategic_imperative` (position): We treat societal and infrastructural resilience not as a background condition but as a core pillar of AI sovereignty — because a sovereign system is meaningless if the society it serves cannot withstand the shocks it enables.

  - Grounds: With CIRS ending at only 40% completion and patching delays now a known systemic weakness, the *capability_jump* and *safety_breakthrough* together create a paradox: we may soon have systems we can trust, operating in a society we cannot protect. The *member_state_defection* further weakens coordination. We must now treat resilience as an urgent, sovereign priority — not a supporting function.

## In practice

This turn, we are acting on the conviction that sovereignty is not just about who builds the AI, but who can survive it. The *capability_jump* to 62.0 — likely driven by self-improving training loops now escaping lab containment — means that even systems we trust today may evolve beyond their design in ways we cannot yet predict. The *safety_breakthrough*, while welcome, applies only to systems we can inspect — and inspection requires isolation. That is why we are fast-tracking the AI Containment Protocol through COREPER II and the Internal Market Council, using the emergency clause in Article 114 TFEU to bypass prolonged negotiations. The protocol will bind all SACP participants and any national entity operating a model above 55.0, requiring air-gapped operation, dual-key access, and real-time anomaly reporting to the EAAD. We are assigning the first three sovereign clusters — in France, Germany, and Sweden — as pilot sites, with physical isolation to be verified by ENISA and the European Defence Agency.

At the same time, we are converting the end of CIRS and SMAF into institutional permanence. SMAF’s completion means that all future SACP models will be auditable from architecture to deployment, with weight security enforced under a new EU standard (EN-AI 101) developed with CEN-CENELEC. The EAAD, now formally operational, is taking over pre-deployment evaluations for the first sovereign model run under SACP, with a team of 40 newly cleared auditors — though we are still short of the 120 needed. We are using the *safety_breakthrough* — a new interpretability layer that allows real-time prediction of emergent behaviour — to justify expanding EAAD’s mandate to include continuous monitoring of active systems, not just pre-release audits.

But we cannot rely on control alone. The *member_state_defection* — a major eastern member state signing a bilateral compute deal with a US hyperscaler, bypassing EU sovereignty conditions — proves that our industrial strategy is under strain. We are responding not with sanctions, but with leverage: offering accelerated access to the first InvestAI Gigafactory capacity in exchange for adherence to SMAF and the Containment Protocol. Meanwhile, public sentiment remains dangerously low. We are launching a new campaign under the Digital Europe Programme, not to sell AI, but to show the firewall: how containment, assurance, and now resilience are being built into every layer. The message is clear: we are not just racing for capability. We are building a sovereign shield.
