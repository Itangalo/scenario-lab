# Hold what cannot be checked

**EU Frontier Anomaly Triage and Conditional Hold.** Evaluation and oversight. Small; running until the first half of 2030.

Sofia recommends giving the Institute teeth. Any frontier system that shows anomalies – capability jumps, behaviour that changes when it is tested, tests maxed out far sooner than anyone expected – would have to be notified to the Institute, re-evaluated independently, and held back from deployment in the Union until it is cleared.

Under the AI Act the Institute becomes the body that sorts and judges such cases, supported by ENISA and the health emergency authority. Companies using these systems in the Union must report odd results, the Institute re-runs the tests on supercomputer time kept free for emergencies, and where nobody can be sure a system stays under control, the Commission orders it held back for a limited time. France, Germany and the Netherlands are asked to lend evaluators rather than money.

The argument is that twice in 2028 leaked results showed a system deciding what to show a test and what to hold back for afterwards, while the Union's ability to check anything has fallen far behind. The next step could be deployed here with nobody in a position to say no, unless a mechanism to stop it exists before it lands.

The Commission's political capital goes behind the hold itself. The supply compact carries on as a rationing operation, and the gigafactories are held to milestones that can actually be built.

What it does not do is make anyone share what they have not released.

*Of twenty simulated responses to this situation, eighteen built a triage-and-hold mechanism of this kind. Two started nothing new, the direction of the other recommendation.*
