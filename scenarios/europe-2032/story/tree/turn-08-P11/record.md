## World state

### Restoration under fire
The automated attack arrived as a poisoned software update, spreading through municipal IT, hospital administration and several grid operators in the same week. Appointment systems went dark, billing and dispatch fell back to paper, and emergency patching chased a blast radius no one could map. The tooling was machine-written; attribution remained open after months.

Brussels answered with what it had. Emergency teams were sent to the worst-hit cities, the compromised component was ordered patched, and wards and utilities were switched to the manual procedures declared operational the previous half-year. Funded overtime kept the fallback standing, but nurses reported chart errors multiplying and auditors began asking who carries liability when paper reconciliation fails.

### Blockade and leverage
Midway through the restoration, Taipei's quarantine stopped advanced chip shipments. Every compute plan became a security plan overnight. The Commission froze further factory payments while a backup site stayed fenced by protesters, and instead prepared a joint stance around lithography and equipment exports, offering continued access to middle-power hosts in exchange for joint action.

No new capacity came online. Dependence, long resented, now read as exposure.

### A counterpoint that held
Against the outage, a handful of public-sector deployments delivered undeniable results: waiting lists shortened, permits returned in days, tutoring pilots showed gains. Ministers toured those sites deliberately, arguing that keeping systems running through the incident was justified.

The public split the difference. Relief at visible benefit tempered anger at disruption, but coverage of outages dominated. Political standing, near paralysis at the turn's start, steadied only because restoration was seen to be attempted.
