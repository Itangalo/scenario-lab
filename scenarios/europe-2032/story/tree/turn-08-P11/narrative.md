# Restoration under fire

> **February 2030.** A poisoned software update spreads through municipal IT, hospital administration and several grid operators in the same week. Appointment systems go dark, and billing and dispatch fall back to paper.

The tooling is machine-written, and months later nobody knows its source. Emergency patching chases damage nobody can map.

Brussels answers with what it has. Sofia's new measure, a recovery sprint, sends emergency teams to the worst-hit cities and orders the compromised component patched, while wards and utilities switch to the manual procedures declared operational last year. The President throws the Commission's remaining political capital behind it. Paid overtime keeps the fallback standing. The nurses report errors multiplying on the manual charts, and the auditors begin asking who is liable when the paper records go wrong.

In the middle of the restoration, the chips stop.

> **March 2030.** A quarantine around Taiwan halts advanced chip shipments.

Most advanced chips are made in Taiwan, so every plan for computing power becomes a security plan overnight. The Commission freezes the remaining gigafactory payments, with the backup site still fenced by protesters, and turns to its lever: ASML's chip-making machines. It prepares a joint stance on exporting them and related equipment, offering middle powers continued access if they act with the Union. No new capacity comes online. The dependence, long resented, now reads as exposure. The bio early-warning network completes its term with its pilot laboratories linked.

Against the outage, a counterpoint holds.

> **May 2030.** A second round of public-sector deployments reports, and this time it is not the hospitals. Planning permits come back in days, benefit appeals are decided inside a month, and a tax administration closes a backlog it has carried since 2027.

Ministers tour those sites, arguing that keeping the systems running through the incident was worth it. The public splits the difference. Relief at the visible benefits tempers the anger at disruption, but the outages dominate the coverage, and the Commission's standing steadies only because restoration is seen to be attempted.
