# Protect the checkable jobs

**Targeted Transition Income and Tier-One Retraining for Checkable Work.** Labour and social protection. Small; running until the first half of 2032.

Sofia recommends getting ahead of the next shock rather than the last one. The work machines can check – routine coding, standard analysis, first drafts, first-line support – is the work they are now demonstrably doing, and it is where Europe's school-leavers and young graduates start. The measure pays a wage bridge and fast retraining for anyone displaced from those jobs, funded by a levy on the employers who automate them.

It runs through the employment ministers and the Social Fund. Existing social envelopes are reprogrammed into six to twelve months of wage insurance, paired with short retraining delivered by the public employment services into cyber hygiene, care technology, grid technology and roles checking machine output. Employers above a threshold of separations caused by automation contribute to the fund. It is framed as flexicurity, not punishment, to blunt the resistance in Council. The islanding stock and the load-shedding sequence stay in place, with no new instrument. The Union seeks volume-licensed chips where it can get them, while the open models on municipal hardware do the work nobody can recall.

The argument is that two winters of rationing have used up the public's patience, and a visible answer on jobs is the precondition for anything else the Union attempts.

The President's standing goes on the transition income.

What it does not do is harden a single network.

*Of ten simulated responses to this situation, five built a transition income of this kind. The other five built a cyber recovery corps instead, the direction of the other recommendation – an even split.*
