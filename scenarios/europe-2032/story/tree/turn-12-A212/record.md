## World state

### Wards Lit, Ports Dark
The bio-detection network finished as planned in spring. Pooled sequencing hubs, emergency verification channels and trusted-care wards became the medical core of the shelter operation. Town registries still flickered, but clinics held without returning to paper. Nurses spoke of a routine again.

That routine did not extend outward. Repair ships for cut cables waited in foreign licensing queues for months. Satellite slots for neutral operators thinned further. Grid-balancing tools hosted abroad timed out, and restoration crews worked with local fallbacks.

### Machines With Hands
Through the same months, dexterous robots moved from demonstration to purchase order elsewhere. Chinese factories shipped them by the thousand; American control stacks steered them. European carmakers and logistics firms asked for units and were told to wait, or to accept terms that routed data and maintenance abroad.

Brussels offered no new industrial fund. Retraining money stayed tied to maintenance and care logistics, and coalition parts flows covered spares at the margin. Works councils in the west and south called it surrender of the last shopfloor.

### Invited In
The one opening came from shared harm. After winter intrusions and biological scares, a joint cyber command offering real-time telemetry and a biosurveillance pact with binding sample-sharing invited Union participation. The middle-power bargain became the ticket in: aligned export licences for shared intelligence.

Council held the non-belligerency line, patrols continued, top-ups continued. Queues for bandwidth, robots and jobs lengthened all the same, and blame that had pointed outward began to point at the shelter itself.
