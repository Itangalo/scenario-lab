# A win to copy

The autumn brings the first European win in years that anyone can attribute.

> **September 2031.** In a handful of cooperating cities, audited triage assistants and fast-decision desks cut clinic waiting lists and clear backlogged permits in days, with the logs published and every human override recorded.

The local press credits Brussels procurement standards rather than an American product. Sofia's note sets out to bottle it. The Visible Benefit Adoption Scale-Up offers willing municipalities a standard pack: workload limits, a funded redress desk and audit access, meant to coax the insurers and the unions back.

The uptake is real and narrow. A second wave of mayors sign up. The underwriters still refuse blanket cover, insisting that the liability pact is paper without a statute behind it, and two large unions hold out for a law before automated decisions restart in benefits. The civic repair completes its term and splits again: health triage expands, and welfare automation stays frozen.

> **October 2031.** Extended naval manoeuvres around the Taiwan Strait, rising shipping insurance and a diplomatic expulsion send energy and shipping desks into disruption drills.

The Union's seat in the pooled defence cell proves its worth. The President keeps the Commission's standing on the accession, and the shared intrusion feeds and backup playbooks keep early warning flowing without new money. The dependence shows too. With the chip slots still cancelled and the fenced gigafactory site still idle, officials concede privately that borrowed containment would be fragile if the shipping lanes choked.

The talks with The Hague, Tokyo and Seoul on lithography licensing stay polite and unmoved. The American laboratories, slowed by their own inward turn, still buy time without giving Europe capacity.

By December the mood has lifted slightly, from hostility to ambivalence. Legitimacy stays conditional: help that can be seen is welcomed, and systems ruled lawful while they harmed people are not.
