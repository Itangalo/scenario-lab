## World state

### Holding the line
Brussels did what it said it would do: nothing new. With coffers empty and trust at rock bottom, the Commission kept existing systems running rather than legislating. Grid control rooms kept segmentation kits live and continued pooling breaker logs, the safety institute cell kept incident reporting going under existing cyber and platform rules, and health emergency planners held bio-surveillance standards steady.

That restraint mostly worked operationally. The grid-shielding programme formally closed this autumn, and operators report faster isolation of faults and fewer cascading alarms. Hospitals kept lights on through summer heat stress, and the continuity stocks of spares and offline procedures built last year were not needed at scale.

### A pause that helps, and hurts
The pause on forced migration to European models held. Health ministers let clinics keep licensed American workflows on standby where the domestic replacement broke referrals, paying for fixes out of health and digital budgets. Walkouts eased in several states, triage lines recovered, and officials again cited two cities where home-grown scheduling cut waiting lists.

But the compromise cemented dependence. Managers now openly run two stacks: a European default and an American fallback that staff prefer. Frozen factory sites stayed fenced and silent, and guarded deployments of grid kits continued after cabinets were pried open and vandalised in a handful of districts — dismissed locally as protest, watched in Brussels as a pattern.

Rumours persisted of towns and hospital groups quietly pooling diesel and spare transformers outside EU schemes.

### Useful, finished
The half-year's clearest news came from ordinary offices. Studies across law, accountancy, administration and media showed solid productivity gains from AI assistants, strongest for juniors, with no matching fall in employment.

Relief was immediate — and then sour. If tools lift output without displacing workers, there is no transition dividend to borrow against: no surge of new jobs, no fiscal windfall to fund factories or retraining. Unions called it proof that weaker European tools are good enough to keep; industry called it proof they are not good enough to grow on. Trust ticked up from catastrophic, nothing more.
