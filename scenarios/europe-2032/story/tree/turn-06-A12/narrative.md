# Stop buttons

> **20 January 2029.** The new US administration takes office. Its first statement on technology promises allies "structured access on published terms" and joint testing of models.

> **March 2029.** An American laboratory demonstrates a system that plans, writes code and operates tools for weeks without a human stepping in.

Within weeks its control software is steering warehouse fleets and port handlers ordered from Chinese factories. European manufacturers who waited months for accelerators can buy a capable robot the same day – with a foreign update server attached.

The Commission does not try to ban what ports and warehouses are already buying. Sofia's Protocol sends inspectors to catalogue the fleets in Rotterdam, Hamburg and Lyon. They demand physical stop buttons and manual driving modes, and rehearse what a hospital or a food depot does when its carriers freeze. The port operators comply on paper. Many smaller logistics firms keep the machines running between audits. A stop test in one depot shuts down sorting for an afternoon, and the video goes round the building twice.

This spring the President puts the Commission's political weight behind the Trust Pact, the programme that pays for staff in small clinics and utilities. Wage bridges reach utility crews and care assistants in several regions, and mayors post videos of shorter hospital queues. Where the crews arrive, the boil-water notices end. In the smaller municipalities of the east and south, where they have not, the Pact is little more than posters, and anger grows as foreign robots take over warehouse shifts.

> **June 2029.** The tech sovereignty package closes, its permits and grid reservations now law. The bio-monitoring network, stockpiles and rationing rules are declared operational.

Engineers welcome both. Voters see little change. The computing sites in question are two fenced fields waiting on chips, and a monitoring network does not staff a clinic.

In Washington, the new team's negotiators speak warmly at the Trade and Technology Council about aligned controls and published terms. No European firm receives new access this spring. The terms are still being written.
