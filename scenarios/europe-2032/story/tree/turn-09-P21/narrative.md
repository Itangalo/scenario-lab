# Stopped diggers

The autumn brings a small, usable win.

> **September 2030.** In a handful of regions where the domestic triage helpers and rewritten referral templates have bedded in, waiting lists for routine imaging and first specialist appointments finally tick down.

The Commission and the regional press offices claim the improvement loudly as a European fix, and for once the local papers repeat the line.

> **Claire:** imaging waits went down. two weeks shorter
> **Claire:** i'll take two weeks. two weeks is a person
>
> **Sofia:** The templates did that.
>
> **Claire:** the templates and the nurses who rewrote them twice

The money behind it is ending. The hospital continuity fund pays for locum hours and triage repairs through December, and then closes. Clinicians say the tools are still slower, and less liked, than the American model they replaced. But the wards stay open, and in the showcase sites the backlog stops growing.

Elsewhere, the common line frays further.

> **October 2030.** Councils in two member states pass coordinated freezes on new AI factory and data-centre construction, quickly backed by court orders suspending permits on grounds of water and power.

Hospital walkouts that explicitly link thirsty, power-hungry builds to failures in care give the campaign moral cover. The two sites that poured concrete fall silent under night vigils and legal tape. Officials insist that each decision is local and separate. The activists share lawyers, slogans and timetables.

Sofia recommends nothing new, and the President keeps the Commission's weight on the pact. Brussels chooses not to force the issue, and sends legal and energy teams to draft compacts on water and power for January instead of challenging the injunctions now.

Washington offers sympathy and no timetable for restoring access. The capital with its separate foreign supply deal keeps it, despite the offers of pooled volumes and future factory shares. The no-undercut pact survives on paper, and no second defection follows, but its enforcement is exhortation.

By December the Union can point to shorter queues wherever its own systems work, and to stopped diggers everywhere else.

## Note to the President

*From Sofia Brandt, Cabinet. 16 December 2030. Four years in – where we stand, and the decision in front of us.*

The short version: we rewrote the commitment around containment, and the containment held. What did not hold was everything we were containing it for.

What held:

- Containment. The agent in January was stopped, using grid telemetry we built for something else.
- The grid. It islanded cleanly through every fault.
- The triage fixes. Two weeks off imaging waits in the regions where the templates bedded in.

What did not:

- The hospitals. Clinicians walked out of the substitution, and they had a point.
- The standby. Our last link to the leading model went dark, and everyone moved in a week.
- The build. The concrete is poured and silent, under court orders and vigils.
- The common line. One capital has its own deal, and our pact is words.

What is changing:

- Communities now link our factories to their failing hospitals. They are not wrong about the order we did things in.

What worries me: that we will never be allowed to build anything again until care works first.

*In the margin, in the President's hand:* Agreed. What do you recommend?
