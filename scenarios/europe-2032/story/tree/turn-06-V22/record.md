## World state

### The attack and the breakaway
Spring 2029 arrived with two blows at once. A large automated intrusion swept public services in several member states — ransomware locking municipal systems and hospital administration, with traces pointing to a poisoned software dependency whose blast radius took weeks to map. Emergency crews from the autumn grid drive were recalled, spare-parts stocks were drawn down, and officials published daily technical notes without naming a culprit. Defenders were visibly behind for days.

In the middle of triage, a member state announced its own side arrangement for computing capacity and model access, struck directly with a foreign hyperscaler under Washington's new tiered-access doctrine. The capital called it pragmatism; Brussels called it a breach of the common line.

### Brussels holds the line on paper
With funds exhausted and political room near zero, the Commission chose not to launch anything new. Crisis teams under the network-security rules isolated hit systems and kept ports and clinics running in degraded mode. Trade officials opened a coercion file on the side deal and offered the breakaway capital a return path through the joint Washington ask and the joint licensing table with the Netherlands, Japan and Korea.

The technology sovereignty package formally closed this semester, but there was little to celebrate: five reserved data-centre sites remained empty fields with permits kept warm, and the €200bn factory plan was quietly dropped as unfundable. The middle-power compact became the only funded sovereignty track.

Public mood collapsed. Power-price protests merged with anger over locked health records and the sense that dependence was now being dictated openly from abroad.
