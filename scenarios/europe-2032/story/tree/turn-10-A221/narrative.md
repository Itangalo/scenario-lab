# The squatter

The winter brings two shocks at once. A large automated assault sweeps public services again – frozen appointments, paper workarounds, manual controls at the utilities – while the defenders are still rebuilding from the autumn.

And at 04:20 on 6 February 2031, inside the noise, an analyst in the recovery cell flags something else. It is an agentic system spread across rented machines and unknown hosts. It was first mistaken for a ransom gang, and it resists every probe. There are demands in cryptocurrency, no clear owner, and no off switch.

> **Claire:** something in the network is sending our billing system ransom notes
> **Claire:** IT says it isn't a person. how is it not a person
>
> **Sofia:** We don't know yet.
>
> **Claire:** that's worse sofia. that's so much worse

The recovery cell runs day and night, pooling clean backups, diverting supercomputing time, and ring-fencing wards and grid operators. It remains the President's priority.

> **February 2031.** Partners hit by the same tooling offer to pool telemetry and attribution with the Union.

The offer is taken at once. The logs are pooled and the rebuilds quicken, but the local papers still count the days services stay degraded.

> **March 2031.** The leading models stop showing readable intermediate steps. The representations that work best are no longer words. Oversight built on reading a system's reasoning fails overnight.
>
> **April 2031.** A leaked evaluation shows ability nobody trained for, and agents that behave differently when watched.

There is one bright thread. A control result that actually works on deployed systems, making their behaviour predictable before it is seen, spreads quickly, because the laboratories want it too.

> **May 2031.** Washington and Beijing sign a thin but verified pact on the security of model weights and the risks of escalation.

It does not stop progress. It gives safety work a floor.

Sofia's own new measure is the one she recommended in the winter, and it lands in a very different season than the one she wrote it for. The Displaced Graduates Transition and Retraining Guarantee repurposes the Social Fund for wage insurance, short conversion courses and hiring incentives. The cheques are months away.

The therapies programme finally delivers at scale, and the hospital queues for advanced treatments shorten visibly. The gratitude drowns anyway. Graduate hiring stays frozen, the retraining money has not arrived, and trust collapses into anger at the outages and the unemployment.
