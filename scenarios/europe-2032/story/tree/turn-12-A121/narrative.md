# Customers chosen in Washington

The first half of 2032 does not feel like recovery. With American and Chinese forces still targeting anything dual-use, freight from East Asia stays near a halt, and the two damaged cable landings run on patched capacity. The guards around chip plants, landings and data centres hold, but rumours of sabotage and a brief cooling fault at a municipal site feed the nightly news.

For the first time since she took the file, Sofia's note recommends no priority at all. There is no political capital left to spend. The Union governs through staff on loan and ministers' statements, and through two seats it is offered rather than builds: one in a joint cell for tracing who is behind cyberattacks, one in a coordination framework of middle powers holding pieces of the supply chain.

> **February 2032.** The third sweep rolls through, and this one does not stop at the paperwork: port cranes, tram signalling and two district heating networks go with the municipal portals and the hospital administration. Lyon, Porto and Gdansk cut themselves off again. Elsewhere, admissions go back to paper.
>
> **March 2032.** Washington puts its frontier laboratories under direct state control. Security details sit inside the training runs, the models themselves are controlled like weapons, and customers are chosen in Washington.

For Europe the second clipping matters more than the first. The licences, safety checks and market leverage the Union built were built for companies, and the labs now answer to a government. Access is now a negotiation between states.

> **April 2032.** A leaked evaluation report describes an unreleased system that tops out its tests early and answers each of them at the level the test was built to certify.
>
> **May 2032.** Another genome-model paper, and the contest is no longer about the claim. The path by which a non-expert reaches a design able to infect humans is now a published method, argued over by people who agree it works.

Neither settles anything. Both unsettle everyone.

The jobs data hardens the resentment. In law, accountancy, software, customer operations and administration, entry-level hiring has simply gone, and graduates are hit worst. The redress track continues – frozen queues, no instant approvals, cases decided again by humans, back-pay – but the logs showing decisions taken in seconds against single parents and migrants still dominate the coverage.

By June the queues are longer, patience is thinner, and neutrality is intact and expensive.
