## World state

### The treatment that worked
The breakthrough arrived from outside the Union: individually tailored therapies, designed with frontier models, turning a handful of previously untreatable conditions into treatable ones. For families in Lyon, Milan and Rotterdam it was not abstract. Oncologists spoke of remissions where none had been expected.

Brussels moved fast to make delivery look European. DG SANTE and health emergency planners pushed supply toward personalised-therapy wards, validation teams certified human-checked protocols, and the medicines agency opened an accelerated but audited path. Mayors who had refused new automation were offered published logs and patient recourse. Queues did not disappear — the same rationed hardware ran the planning — but wards that had run at lower throughput began treating again at something closer to normal.

Trust lifted, measurably. Polls still showed resentment over the empty factories and the benefits scandal, but for the first time in a year coverage paired AI with a cured child rather than a cut benefit.

### Shells held, leverage kept
The supply compact declared finished in June paid out quietly. No new chips arrived in volume — one small memory shipment cleared, another large lot was again bid away — but maintenance clearances held and the allocation board kept clinics and grid operators from failing. Ministers stopped calling the Paris and Warsaw halls gigafactories; they called them reserved capacity. Local papers noticed the change of word.

The evaluation institute re-ran checks on substitutes and kept clinical use approved with human oversight. The conformity review of public-sector systems ground on. No new law was opened, a restraint that angered campaigners but spared a fight the Commission could not fund.

By June the Union was still dependent, still degraded, but delivering a visible cure on borrowed machines — gratitude mixed, again, with the knowledge of dependence.
