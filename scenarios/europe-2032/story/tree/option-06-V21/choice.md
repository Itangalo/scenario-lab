# Repair where people can see it

**EU Cyber Emergency Repair Cell.** Preparedness and resilience. Small; running until the first half of 2030.

Sofia recommends a standing repair capacity: joint teams led by ENISA, shared backups and fast procurement, ready to restore public services the day an attack lands rather than months afterwards.

It runs under the network-security directive's crisis powers, with the Joint Cyber Unit as its venue. The cell is stood up from existing rosters at the Union's computer emergency team and the national response teams, and paid for with reprogrammed Digital Europe emergency funds, so there is no vote on new money. When an attack comes, the teams go first to the worst-hit municipalities and clinics, restoring backups and isolating compromised dependencies. The gigafactory reservations and permits are kept warm without cash through the digital directorate and the EIB, and the new compact's table is used to hold the member states' licence line.

The argument is that the grid drive closed with the hospitals still unpatched and the legacy controllers still in place, and that in the winter of 2027 it took weeks to restore what the attack had broken. With the Commission's standing nearly spent, the only thing that rebuilds it is restoration people can see.

The President's standing goes on the repair cell.

What it does not do is add to the Union's leverage abroad.

*Of ten simulated responses to this situation, three built emergency repair capacity of this kind – a minority. Seven started nothing new and put the Union's remaining weight behind the supply-chain compact, the direction of the other recommendation.*
