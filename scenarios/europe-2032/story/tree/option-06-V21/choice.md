# Repair where people can see it

**EU Cyber Emergency Repair Cell.** Preparedness and resilience. Small; running until the first half of 2030.

Sofia recommends a standing repair capacity: joint teams led by ENISA, shared backups and fast procurement, ready to restore public services the day an attack lands rather than months afterwards.

It uses the crisis powers of the EU's existing cybersecurity law. The cell is staffed from the Union's computer emergency team and the national response teams, and paid for by moving money within an existing EU digital budget, so there is no vote on new money. When an attack comes, the teams go first to the worst-hit municipalities and clinics, restoring backups and cutting off compromised software. The gigafactory sites keep their reservations and permits, tended by the Commission's digital department and the EU's investment bank, but get no money. The compact's table is used to keep the member states setting export licences together.

The argument is that the Shield closed with the hospitals still unpatched and the old control computers still in place, and that in the winter of 2027 it took weeks to restore what the attack had broken. With almost nothing left to spend, the Commission can rebuild only through restoration people can see.

The President stakes what political capital the Commission has left on the repair cell.

What it does not do is add to the Union's leverage abroad.

*Of ten simulated responses to this situation, three built emergency repair capacity of this kind – a minority. Seven started nothing new and put the Union's remaining political capital behind the supply-chain compact, the direction of the other recommendation.*
