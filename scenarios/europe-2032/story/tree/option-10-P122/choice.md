# Fund the empty desks

**EU Entry-Level Transition Guarantee.** Labour and social protection. Small; running until the first half of 2032.

Sofia recommends spending the Commission's scarce new money on the shock voters actually feel: wage insurance, paid retraining places and hiring credits for the entry-level cohorts in law, accountancy, software and customer operations whose jobs are no longer being refilled, tied to the employers who automate them.

It goes through the employment and finance ministers, using the Social Fund and a legal basis modelled on the pandemic-era job-retention scheme, not a new frontier fund. Member states draw EU co-financing for income bridging, six-month retraining vouchers and credits for first jobs in the four frozen sectors, and large deployers of AI assistants must report under a levy condition that ties automating employers to the fund. The labour ministers who were told to plan without money get money. The finance ministers get a capped, temporary envelope. On the other fronts there is no new instrument. ENISA and the national response teams push new defensive tools out through the Shield certificates and the existing drills, any biological warning goes to the health emergency authority and the compact's pooled evaluation, and the capital with its side deal keeps its conditional offer of hosting.

The argument is that the commitment to absorb the shock fails if displacement is left as an unfunded plan, and that empty graduate fairs are what voters see.

The President puts the Commission's political weight behind the guarantee.

What it does not do is push the defensive tools beyond where the drills already work.

*Of ten simulated responses to this situation, two funded a transition guarantee like this – a minority. Eight built a defence and detection sprint instead, the direction of the other recommendation.*
