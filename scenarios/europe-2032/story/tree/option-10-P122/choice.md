# Fund the empty desks

**EU Entry-Level Transition Guarantee.** Labour and social protection. Small; running until the first half of 2032.

Sofia recommends spending the Commission's scarce new money on the shock voters actually feel: wage top-ups, paid retraining places and hiring credits for the entry-level cohorts in law, accountancy, software and customer operations whose jobs are no longer being refilled, tied to the employers who automate them.

It goes through the employment and finance ministers, using the EU's jobs fund and rules modelled on the Covid-era scheme that kept workers on payrolls, not a new frontier fund. Member states draw EU co-financing for income support, six-month retraining vouchers and credits for first jobs in the four frozen sectors, and large firms using AI assistants must report on it and can be made to pay into the fund. The labour ministers who were told to plan without money get money. The finance ministers get a capped, temporary budget. On the other fronts there is no new instrument. ENISA and the national response teams push new defensive tools out through the Shield certificates and the existing drills, any biological warning goes to the health emergency authority and the compact's pooled evaluation, and Dublin keeps its conditional offer of computing capacity.

The argument is that the commitment to absorb the shock fails if displacement is left as an unfunded plan, and that empty graduate fairs are what voters see.

The President puts the Commission's remaining political capital behind the guarantee.

What it does not do is push the defensive tools beyond where the drills already work.

*Of ten simulated responses to this situation, two funded a transition guarantee like this – a minority. Eight built a defence and detection sprint instead, the direction of the other recommendation.*
