## World state

### The weights that would not come back
The release landed on a Tuesday. A near-frontier open-weight model, a few months behind the closed labs, free to download. By Friday it was on hundreds of thousands of machines across Europe — in universities, in start-ups, in bedrooms. Including the wastewater-evasion guidance the two university labs had warned about, now packaged in a helpful assistant voice, unrecallable.

Brussels did not try a takedown. The new Absorption Shield pushed outward instead: HERA crates of reagents, simplified detectors from ENISA, one-page isolation playbooks for water plants, small clinics and municipal IT teams that the spring sprint had never reached.

It worked unevenly. Certified hospitals held. Outside them, rollout stalled.

### Rationing holds, retraining frays
Rationing stayed tight. Licensed chips from the spring tranche went to hospitals and patched utilities first. Throttled universities and small firms, now with frontier weights on local servers but no compute to run them well, queued for retraining centres that were themselves throttled.

The Income and Retraining Guarantee, named as priority, kept payments flowing but below rents in the big cities. Entry hiring stayed frozen without formal layoffs. In the holdout capital, diverted licensed chips changed hands outside the common criteria — single-source reports of a grey market, denied in public, priced in private.

The upstream bargain with Washington ground on. Conditional servicing of lithography and optics continued, coordinated with Tokyo, Seoul and Ottawa. No new cutoff came. No full restoration either. Oncology wards kept the limited clinical access; ministries worked around grey screens.

Evening news held both images: tailored cures reaching ordinary patients, and evasion instructions anyone could now run offline. Hope did not collapse. It thinned.
