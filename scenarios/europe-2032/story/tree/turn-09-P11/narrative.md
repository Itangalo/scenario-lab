# Wards on paper

The second half of 2030 opens with two emergencies running together.

> **July 2030.** A fresh wave of automated intrusions sweeps municipal systems and hospital administration, again through a tainted software component whose reach takes weeks to map.

The appointment and dispatch systems fall back to paper while reserve teams chase patches. At the same time, the wards and utilities still running on manual fallback since the February cut-off start to break. Chart errors, missed handovers and exhausted staff lead to walkouts in several regions, and the treatment backlogs grow.

> **Claire:** half the ward walked out today. not me
> **Claire:** i'm too tired to walk anywhere
>
> **Sofia:** I'm sending help.
>
> **Claire:** send people. not a memo. people

Sofia's answer is the Care Continuity and Cross-Border Medical Surge, and the President puts the Commission's standing behind it. It pays for overtime and surge hires, sends medical staff across borders under health-emergency powers, and keeps double-check teams on the wards to cut the errors. The hardening sprint finishes its work – backups restored, some grids re-segmented – and keeps the worst outages from cascading. The auditors and the unions still question liability and safety in the open.

> **Claire:** we got two nurses from porto
> **Claire:** they don't speak french. they don't need to. they know what a chart is

Any hope that restoration would buy room for leverage dies in the autumn.

> **September 2030.** Valuations across the AI sector reset violently. Announced data-centre expansions are cancelled, and the financing that European compute plans had counted on evaporates.
>
> **October 2030.** A large member state signs its own supply arrangement with a foreign hyperscaler, on terms that undercut the common export stance prepared in Council.

With the Taiwan shipments still quarantined and the backup site still fenced by protesters, the factory payments stay frozen. The Commission holds the joint lithography position on paper, and does not push it, lacking both the votes and the funds. Ministers tour instead the clinics where public-sector systems still cut waiting lists and permit times, and argue that the strategy is holding.

The public does not buy restoration as success. Outages and backlogs dominate the news, and relief at the services that work barely registers. The Union ends the year keeping its hospitals open, but poorer, more divided, and visibly unable to hold a single line on supply.

## Note to the President

*From Sofia Brandt, Cabinet. 16 December 2030. Four years in – where we stand, and the decision in front of us.*

The short version: the switch was built a few weeks before it was needed, and it worked. What it switched us to was paper, and paper is breaking.

What held:

- The switch. When Washington cut us off, certified hospitals kept working.
- The sprints. The backups are restored and some grids re-segmented, and nothing cascaded.
- The surge. Nurses from Porto are working wards in Lyon.

What did not:

- The fallback itself. Manual charts, missed handovers, walkouts.
- The build. The gigafactories reached the end of their schedule without a single rack.
- The chips. There is a quarantine around Taiwan, and the joint stance is on paper only.
- The common line. A large member state has its own deal.

What is changing:

- The money has left the sector. There will be no private rescue of our compute plans.

What worries me: that we switched to a fallback designed for weeks, and have been running on it for two years.

*In the margin, in the President's hand:* Agreed. What do you recommend?
