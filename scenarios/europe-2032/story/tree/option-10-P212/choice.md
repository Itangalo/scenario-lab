# Survive first

**No new measure.** The two-year commitment is rewritten.

Sofia recommends starting nothing, naming no priority, and rewriting the commitment for the last two years. It would be to survive the cut-off and the open frontier by hardening health, biological and cyber resilience first, while negotiating the water-and-power compacts that could unfreeze the sovereign builds.

With the Commission's standing at nothing, the builds under injunction and access to the leading model unrestored, any new instrument would fail. So the half-year goes on the compacts. The energy directorate and the legal service negotiate directly with the two freezing councils and the courts' technical referees, offering capped water and energy envelopes, sequenced grid connections and local health earmarks in exchange for lifting the suspensions site by site. The cut-off workloads stay on the continuity stack, and the showcase triage helpers keep running on maintenance money. The health emergency authority, the disease control centre and ENISA move to a low-cost posture: monitoring symptoms and the use of genome models, checking hospital cyber hygiene, and issuing guidance on open frontier models to municipalities and laboratories.

The argument is that the Union cannot rebuild trust in adoption on schedule with nothing to spend. It must first keep essential services standing and contain distributed misuse, so that the next commitment starts from something it can fund.

No priority is named.

What it does not do is fund anything new, anywhere.

*Of ten simulated responses to this situation, eight started nothing new like this – the large majority. Two built a shield against biological and open-model misuse instead, the direction of the other recommendation.*
