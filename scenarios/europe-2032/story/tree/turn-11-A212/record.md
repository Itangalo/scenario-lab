## World state

### A Market Breaks, A Queue Forms
Autumn brought a financial rupture to the technology sector. Valuations that had sustained a global building spree reset in weeks. Data-centre projects were cancelled, orders for accelerators paused, and several private hosting deals European hospitals and registries had quietly relied upon evaporated. For the first time, the frontier laboratories spoke of training less, not more.

In Brussels the rupture landed on top of war. With transatlantic cloud links severed or throttled and American systems unreachable, clinics flickered back to paper for days. Repair ships for subsea cables waited months for wartime licences; satellite capacity for neutral operators thinned to a rumour of spare slots. Nurses kept wards open with triage assistants running locally and pooled sequencing, but town halls stayed dark and graduate hiring did not return.

### The Bargain and the Shelter
The Union's answer was to join a new coordination framework of middle powers holding chokepoints in the supply chain — aligning export licences, bargaining jointly for compute, pooling evaluation. Council mandates passed quickly; coastal and eastern states were promised cable patrols and airspace protection from reprogrammed funds in exchange for holding the non-belligerency line on fabs and data centres.

It helped at the margins. Joint bargaining kept a few shipments of parts and maintenance contracts flowing when private deals collapsed. It did not restore lights or registries.

At home, the shelter operation became the visible priority, absorbing the finished transition placements into restoration crews and care logistics. Wage top-ups continued. Retraining for displaced graduates was tied to maintenance and care work, a thin bridge as entry posts in law, accountancy, software and customer operations vanished without replacement.

Blame stayed outward, but patience thinned. Cures delivered in pilot hospitals contrasted with queues for repairs and jobs that did not come back.
