# Street by street

> **September 2032.** A largely automated sweep of ransomware and tainted updates runs across municipal systems, ports and clinic dispatch in several member states.

The tooling is machine-made and fast, and it reinfects. Towns cleaned in September are locked again in October, through the same contractor software. Attribution slips away into months of forensics. What people see is the defenders behind.

Sofia's last new measure is the Repair-and-Keep-Clean Municipal Surge, and the President puts everything behind it. The joint triage queue stays open day and night, and the standing repair teams camp in town halls, cutting out the compromised dependency, restoring from offline backups, forcing password rotations, and staying until the systems stay clean. The containment pact, concluded this half-year, gives the crews common playbooks and isolation routines. It half-works. The cities that hold to the procedure stay clean and put their mayors on camera. Others relapse.

> **October 2032.** An openly downloadable system from a foreign laboratory lands within touching distance of the closed frontier. Hundreds of thousands of copies spread in days.

European security officials stop talking about controlling its spread and start talking about living with it: hardening, patching, and assuming the intrusion kits will keep getting sharper.

Against that, one European story holds. Where hospitals and administrations stayed on smaller EU-hosted models and paper-to-digital fallbacks, the waiting lists fall, and decisions that took months come in days. Brussels publishes the figures street by street, and credits the European choice by name.

It does not lift the mood. The queues elsewhere lengthen, and the news is dominated by fraud and outages and by resentment of the American systems that are still cut off by nationality. Repair reads as survival, not progress, except in the few cities where clean systems and shorter waits coincide.

## Note to the President

*From Sofia Brandt, Cabinet. 15 December 2032. Six years in.*

The short version: we turned a repair service into a way of running things. It is survivable. It is not yet progress, except on a few green streets.

What held:

- The fallback. When the leading model closed by nationality, the hospitals ran on ours, and the notes got shorter and more honest.
- The crews and the triage queue. Every sweep got through, and none of them stopped a city for good.
- The containment of the outbreak. Weeks, dozens dead, and it did not become hundreds.
- The green streets. Where everything held together, the waiting lists fell, and people saw why.

What did not:

- Clean systems everywhere. The same doors keep being opened.
- Access. The leading model is still closed to us by nationality.
- The build. No factory money in two years.
- Trust. People are too tired to credit survival.

What I would tell whoever holds this file next: the green streets are the proof. Make every street look like them.

*In the margin, in the President's hand:* One street at a time, then.
