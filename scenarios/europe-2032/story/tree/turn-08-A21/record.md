## World state

### Restoration Under Pressure
January began with town halls still queuing paper forms. A fresh automated sweep — ransomware reassembled around stolen credentials and a poisoned update in widely used municipal software — locked registries, appointment booking and a logistics platform serving hospitals in several member states. Grid control rooms again cut remote links and ran manual shifts. Clean backups and reserve teams from earlier EU programmes restored most systems in days, some in weeks, but cameras caught the queues, not the recovery.

Brussels answered with what it could fund: a Transition Shield paying wage top-ups, six-month retraining vouchers and minimum staffing guarantees for clinics and registries, routed through employment ministers and existing social funds. The promise was visible — money for young clerks, accountants, coders and call-centre staff whose entry jobs stopped being advertised, and staff to keep paper triage from becoming permanent. Rollout was uneven and delayed. National job agencies quarrelled over eligibility, existing funds proved insufficient to absorb the new demand without fresh appropriations, payments lagged in the south, and unions called the vouchers a bridge to nowhere while Chinese sorting machines spread in German and French warehouses. Delivery remained partial by June, limiting any political credit.

### The Gap Breaks Open
In March an American laboratory showed systems that planned across days, built their own software tools and improved their own training pipelines with little human touch. European re-runs on spare supercomputer time failed quickly; substitutes that had held in degraded form now broke on basic coordination tasks.

A bright spot came from a European materials laboratory, where AI-designed alloys promised longer-lived grid components — celebrated in specialist journals, distant from nightly news. For most voters the lived experience was absence: graduate hiring frozen, hospital workloads rationed, Paris and Warsaw machine halls still empty while power and cooling hummed for nothing. Safety work continued under strain, eroded by the pace of incidents but not collapsed.

By June blame hardened. Aid convoys of analysts and operators helped, supply pacts kept lights on, but gratitude of the spring had turned. The Shield was real, yet too small, underfunded and too late to change the sense that Europe was mopping up a flood decided elsewhere.
