# Forty seconds a file

The autumn of 2032 brings one piece of genuinely good engineering news. A new generation of the defensive tools – patching pushed at machine speed, and detectors tuned to swarm behaviour rather than signatures – finally closes off a whole class of intrusion that has plagued grid operators and hospitals for years. ENISA folds it quietly into guidance for the operators already on the grid shield and the continuity stack, and for weeks the incident logs are calmer. The technicians notice. The public does not.

The credit drowns in two other stories.

> **October 2032.** Washington tightens export licensing again. European buyers keep their licences in name while the volumes are withheld in fact. The Dutch transit hubs comply, reluctantly and on camera.

With nothing to buy and no retaliation Brussels could sustain, it reads at home as one more demonstration that access is someone else's gift.

> **November 2032.** The ombudsman's full finding, backed by court judgments, shows that the suspended social-insurance system wronged claimants for years. Conformity files had passed on paper. Human review had been reduced to a caseworker clearing a queue at forty seconds a file.

Sofia watches the press conference on a screen in the cabinet's meeting room. A journalist asks whether the system broke the law, or the law allowed the system. The Commission's answer, which Sofia helped to write, is the first: high-risk duties existed and were hollowed out, a breach and not a design. It is meant to save the AI Act. The people in the room with her, she can tell, believe the second. So do the lawyers and the press, who draw the opposite moral from the same file: enforcement discredited, and redress crawling through a few thousand re-examined cases and back-pay in tranches.

> **Claire:** a patient's back-pay came through. she brought us cake
> **Claire:** she said the machine owed her more than a cake
>
> **Sofia:** It did.
>
> **Claire:** she knew that. she brought the cake anyway

The pact completes its term and pays out modestly. Where towns trusted the flat reimbursements, the spares stayed. Where they did not, the habit of two sets of books hardened. The winter's break-ins, torched depots and refused night runs are handled as national sabotage cases and logged in Brussels, without a new operation. Sofia recommends nothing new, and with nothing in flight there is no priority to name.

By December the lights stay on, and collapse is avoided again. Nothing else can be claimed.

## Note to the President

*From Sofia Brandt, Cabinet. 15 December 2032. Six years in.*

The short version: we kept the lights on by hand. The hands kept two sets of books.

What held:

- The lights. Nothing stopped for good, on maintenance money.
- The dispersed stores. Where towns trusted the reimbursement, the spares stayed.
- The redress. The system is suspended, the logs are public, and the back-pay is being paid.
- The patching. A whole class of intrusion is closed, and the technicians noticed.

What did not:

- The map. We listed the stores as a lifeline, and it became a target list. That was my recommendation.
- The law. A system that passed every conformity check wronged claimants for years, and the public now thinks the Act is the problem.
- The tools that worked. Two health systems cut waits from months to days, and were heckled as the same machine.
- Access. Licences in name, volumes withheld.
- The table. Washington and Beijing agreed a floor without us.

What I would tell whoever holds this file next: trust was the stockpile. We ran it down to a reserve that nobody declares, and we do not know where it is.

*In the margin, in the President's hand:* Find it.
