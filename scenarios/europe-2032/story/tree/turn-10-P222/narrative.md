# An attack on the workaround

The first half of 2031 brings no new blackout. It brings something more corrosive.

> **February 2031.** In several member states, generator pools, transformer stores and the sheds where breaker logs and segmentation kits are kept are broken into, drained, burned or smashed. Leaflets left behind call the stockpiles collaboration with the machine system.

The police call it coordinated. The grid engineers call it the loss of the only restoration capacity they have left.

As Sofia recommended, nothing new is started, and the President keeps the Commission's standing on the black-start network, now on a protection footing. Under the civil-protection and critical-entity rules, ENISA, the crisis network and national police bring the off-books pools on-scheme: dispersed, guarded, reimbursed, and publicly listed as a lifeline.

The map is approved at a meeting in March that runs long. Sofia has written the case for it, and the case is good: a stockpile everyone knows about is harder to call a secret collaboration. Near the end a police liaison from one member state asks, mildly, whether a public list of guarded stores is also a list of where the stores are. There is a pause. The room moves on.

It partly works. Some municipalities report fewer incidents once the guards appear. Others report holders refusing to declare their stocks, for fear of being listed as targets.

> **Claire:** the hospital's diesel store has a guard now. he's nineteen and very polite
> **Claire:** we're on your map apparently
>
> **Sofia:** The map is how the guard knew to come.
>
> **Claire:** the map is also how everyone else knows

The outside tightens again.

> **April 2031.** Washington tightens the volume quotas on allied buyers of advanced chips and model access. The Hague complies under protest.
>
> **May 2031.** Private capital flees the AI sector. Valuations collapse, and announced build-outs are cancelled rather than delayed.

There is nothing to buy even where the licences exist. The clinics stay on a European default with an American fallback that is openly metered. Factory managers speak of being fenced in twice over.

The one bright spot is administrative. In two health systems, among them the two cities that started it, and in a handful of municipalities, European-run scheduling and decision-support tools cut waiting lists and decision times from months to days. The digital directorate and the health ministries co-brand the deployments and pay the teams, without a programme of their own.

It does not shift the mood. The coverage folds the sabotage, the quotas and last year's intrusion wave into one story of dependence and machines beyond their minders. The containment protocol completes its term this spring and helps the mutual aid arrive faster, but restoration still crawls wherever the parts are gone. Hospitals postpone care when their spares run out, and towns run on whatever is guarded.

The common line holds, but only just.
