## World state

### The jump
Autumn began with a release that made spring look incremental. Two American labs within weeks demonstrated systems that planned multi-day work, used tools without prompting, and improved parts of their own training pipelines. European deployment teams said their evaluation checklists were obsolete on arrival. Human review of frontier behaviour became a formality.

In Brussels the shift was felt in concrete and queues. Fenced compute sites faced another season of cut cables and halted works, now guarded as routine. At the same time law firms, accountancies, software houses and customer-operations centres simply stopped hiring juniors. Graduate unemployment maps turned dark in university cities; the absence of offers was not disputed, only its cause.

### Dividend pays, partially
The Trusted Adoption Dividend closed as scheduled. One-stop permit modules and therapy-scheduling assistants cut waiting times sharply where they were installed on EU-anchored capacity — clinics in Lyon, Porto and Gdansk posted falling queues. But chips reserved on paper were still arriving late, installations stalled in half the member states, and the side-deal for hyperscale capacity remained outside common terms. Conditionality on funds contained the split without healing it.

Washington's allied-access track stayed in drafting. No European firm was onboarded; insurers kept their surcharges.

### The Corps that could not move fast enough
To answer the hiring collapse, the Union launched wage-insurance and paid placements — retraining juniors alongside AI, and 12-month posts in hospitals, municipalities and guarded build sites. Councils welcomed it, mayors asked for slots immediately.

Delivery lagged the announcement. ESF+ top-ups and underspend took months to clear, placements numbered in thousands against tens of thousands missing jobs, and firms kept juniors only where subsidies covered most of the wage. Voters saw EU-paid work in some wards and empty job boards in others. Gratitude for visible help mixed with anger at dependence, and opposition parties ran openly against data-centre expansion.

Hospitals and ports held — degraded, not stopped — but the Union entered winter with thinner assurances, tighter budgets and a public that used AI daily and resented it just as daily.
