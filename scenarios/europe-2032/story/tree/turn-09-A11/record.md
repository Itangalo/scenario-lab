## World state

### Cures arrive on someone else's machines
The autumn brought the first tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions into ordinary clinics. Waiting lists filled within days in France, Spain and Poland. DG SANTE insisted procurement run only on certified, European-accessible systems, and the filmed triage assistants finally scaled beyond pilots. For a moment the public story shifted from frozen graduate hiring to lives extended.

The relief curdled quickly. Doses, scheduling software and the top-end models behind the treatment plans were licensed abroad, with allocation queues set in California and compute billed by the hour. Commentators called it healing by permission. Mayors who had kept bans on logistics machines now asked why Brussels could guarantee a therapy appointment but not a job interview.

### One capital walks, many haggle
The defection broke in October: a member state signed its own compute and robot-supply pact with a foreign hyperscaler, with discounted capacity and early humanoid deliveries. At home it was sold as pragmatism; in Brussels as a breach of the common line. Finance ministers openly quarrelled over who would pay for the Guarantee if capitals could freelance.

The Commission's answer was the new supply and evaluation club with other middle powers — pooled bargaining for frontier access, aligned export-licence criteria, mutual recognition of tests. The Council and EEAS moved fast, offering the wayward capital re-entry on better pooled terms. Talks were cordial, communiqués bold, but lawyers noted nothing yet compelled a lab to open a checkpoint or a vendor to re-route chips. Grid priority for the two permitted gigafactory plots stayed stuck in energy councils.

On the ground, Chinese-built humanoids moved to full commercial shifts in ports and warehouses. Blockades in Germany and Italy hardened; two fenced pilot sites suffered night fires, reported as sabotage on single sources. The humanoid register existed but without staff to enforce it, and certification lagged. Works councils co-signed a few reopenings under the Guarantee, but take-up of bridge pay stayed slow.

Frontier labs released again at a pace no roadmap had announced, with training loops running largely unattended. Brussels evaluators kept reading release notes after the fact.
