# Two sets of books

The first half of 2032 is a holding operation. With no money and no goodwill left to spend, Brussels does not legislate. Sofia recommends nothing new, and the President keeps the Commission's standing on the pact, which is kept alive by procedure.

The flat reimbursements for the unmapped micro-stores keep flowing through the civil-protection machinery, with guards only where towns ask for them. Where the holders trust the arrangement, the spares stay in place. Where they do not, a parallel practice hardens: one small declared stock for the inspectors, and the real reserve kept off any list.

> **Claire:** the inspectors came to count our diesel
> **Claire:** we have two diesel stores now. one is for inspectors
>
> **Sofia:** I didn't read that.
>
> **Claire:** you did though

> **February 2032.** Drivers in two member states refuse night runs to fallback depots without a police escort, after a winter of torched and broken-into stores.

National police treat the attacks as sabotage of essential services. The coordination cell in Brussels logs every incident and launches nothing new.

On the benefits scandal the contested system stays suspended. Case review crawls forward, a few thousand files re-examined, the back-pay paid in tranches. The logs are published in full, and almost nobody reads them except journalists and lawyers, who keep finding more examples of human sign-off that was barely there.

Sofia reads them too, one evening in April, at her kitchen table, because nobody else in the cabinet has. The caseworker's name is blacked out. The timestamps are not. Approval follows approval a few seconds apart, flagged family after flagged family, through an entire afternoon, and then the entries stop at the end of the shift and start again the next morning at the same pace. She closes the laptop and makes tea, and does not drink it.

The two health-scheduling tools keep running under different names, with manual checks, still cutting waits where they work and still heckled as part of the same machine administration.

> **April 2032.** Vandalism of grid kits flares again through the spring, mostly minor.

Abroad, the tiered quotas keep biting: licences granted, volumes withheld. The Dutch transit hubs comply, visibly reluctant.

> **May 2032.** Talk of a crisis in the Taiwan Strait fades. Shipping insurance does not fall.

There was nothing in Europe to buy with it anyway.

By June the Union has avoided collapse and demonstrated nothing else. Restoration by hand is doctrine on paper and patchwork in practice. The common line holds because nobody tests it hard.
