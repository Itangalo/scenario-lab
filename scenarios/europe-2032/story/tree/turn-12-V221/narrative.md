# A letter from the hospital

The spring of 2032 brings the first health news people can feel.

> **February 2032.** New tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions reach ordinary clinics.

In three hospitals with long backlogs, diagnostic support running on European servers cuts waiting times from months to weeks. Sofia's note turns it into the European Clinical Deployment and Visible Benefit Guarantee, and the health ministries brand the rollout jointly as a European decision. For the first time in two years, the local news carries patients naming Brussels as the reason a letter arrived.

> **Claire:** we're one of the three. waiting list went from months to weeks
> **Claire:** a woman cried at reception today. the good kind
>
> **Sofia:** Did she get her letter?
>
> **Claire:** she got her date. she said to thank whoever did it. so. thank you

Public-sector adoption helps too. Permit and appointment backlogs fall in the cities where the rebuilt booking systems finally hold. Where the engineers have cleared out the compromised component, the queues shorten, and the councils freeze the discredited green dashboards and publish their restoration figures next to the actual waiting times.

The improvement is uneven. The municipal rebuilds are still stalled in a third of the affected towns, over procurement and a lack of cleared staff. The transition income formally closes its set-up phase with the registration lists growing and the disbursements still symbolic. With no levy and no borrowing authorised, the job centres pay small one-off sums, and youth unemployment in checkable work barely moves.

The offices keep reporting gains from augmentation, now with some rehiring of juniors, and the bills and the queued licences limit any goodwill. The specialist press dwells on leaked foreign benchmarks and evaluation anomalies that European laboratories cannot reproduce.

On campuses the leafleting against data-centre expansion and automated hiring grows louder, and brief blockades are reported in two university cities. It is still treated as a mood, but it is now a persistent hum in the background.

By June the power is on and, in health at least, something feels delivered: narrow, visible, and at last attributed to Europe.
