## World state

### Waiting lists move at last
Spring 2032 brought the first health news people could feel. New tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions reached ordinary clinics, and in three backlog hospitals diagnostic support running on European servers cut waiting times from months to weeks. Health ministries jointly branded the rollout as a European decision, and for the first time in two years local news carried patients naming Brussels as the reason a letter arrived.

Public-sector adoption helped. The same period saw permit and appointment backlogs fall in cities where rebuilt booking systems finally held. Where engineers had cleared the compromised component, queues shortened and councils published restoration figures alongside actual wait times after freezing the discredited green dashboards.

### Fixes still partial
The improvement was uneven. Municipal rebuilds still stalled in a third of affected towns over procurement and a lack of cleared staff. The transition income scheme approved for routine coding and support leavers formally closed its setup phase: registration lists grew, but disbursements remained symbolic with no levy or borrowing authorised. Job centres paid small one-off amounts while youth unemployment in checkable work barely moved.

Offices continued to report augmentation gains, now with rehiring of juniors, but bills and queued licences limited any goodwill. Specialist press dwelled on leaked foreign benchmarks and evaluation anomalies that European labs could not reproduce.

On campuses, leafleting against data-centre expansion and automated hiring grew louder, with brief blockades reported in two university cities. Treated still as mood, but now a persistent background hum.

By June, power stayed on and, in health at least, something felt delivered — narrow, visible, and attributed at last to Europe.
