# Building what you cannot ask for

The frontier leaps. A breakthrough in what systems can coordinate on their own redefines the ceiling, and the pace outruns the speed at which any institution responds. Nothing on the safety side moves at all — the internal monitoring reforms the laboratories promised are still unimplemented — and the open frontier climbs behind the closed one, carried by architectures that diffuse faster than they can be withheld.

You retire the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive and say plainly that it failed. Voluntary access was a fiction; asking has been tested. You are not behind any more, you are disconnected, so you stop requesting access and start building it.

The Sovereign Compute Corridor rests on three pillars. The national machines — the French atomic energy commission's, the German high-performance computing network, the EuroHPC undertaking — designated as a sovereign backbone and legally insulated from foreign control. Article 122 invoked to declare a strategic computing emergency, letting you requisition EU-located compute from foreign providers during an incident, with compensation but no veto. And the Gigafactories fast-tracked over national permitting. Anyone taking accelerated permitting or grid priority must reserve a fifth of their EU frontier capacity for sovereign use. Spain and Poland get direct grid compensation — solidarity with teeth — behind €30bn of unspent NextGenerationEU money.

Then the machinery refuses it. Three programmes are already in flight and it will not carry a fourth. The Corridor stays in drafting, consultations pushed to next year, and the emergency Council session you called adopts nothing.

The Netherlands and Sweden signal that requisition is incompatible with the internal market. US developers warn about legal exposure. National courts begin receiving inquiries. Outside, moratoriums on data centres and on AI in public services stop being a fringe demand, energy protests spread, and the security hawks find their voice in the same season as the campaign against you.
