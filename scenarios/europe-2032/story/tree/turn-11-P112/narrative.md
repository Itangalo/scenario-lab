# Cures behind a locked door

The autumn brings two shocks at once.

> **September 2031.** Clinicians in three countries report tailored therapies clearing conditions long written off as untreatable, with doses tuned patient by patient and results visible on ordinary wards within weeks.

Since the cut-off of 2029, some of the leading foreign model had crept back into European hospitals through contracts and resellers, and hospital planning tools, triage assistants and procurement systems had been built on it.

> **September 2031.** Days after the therapy reports, that access goes dark for European users. There is no explanation and no window for appeal.

The systems freeze in the middle of shifts. Staff in the two states still clearing the containment wards go back to paper for the second time.

> **Claire:** planning tool froze mid-shift
> **Claire:** we just got the containment wards back. now this
>
> **Sofia:** We're building a medical channel on our own computers.
>
> **Claire:** build it before the next cure. i want our patients to get the next one

The cross-border care surge finishes its work. The funded transfers, shared intensive-care beds and overtime teams kept the containment hospitals open, and no cascade closed a region. The nurses and the logistics crews earn genuine praise. It does not turn into political capital. Closed outpatient clinics, slipped appointments and fresh walkouts frame the story, and the headlines ask why the breakthrough cures depend on a door someone abroad can lock.

Sofia's answer is the EU Sovereign Medical AI Deployment, and the President makes it the Commission's priority rather than any one capital's: European computing ring-fenced for medical use, licensed copies of therapy models run on European computers, and delivery through the containment and transfer network to the hardest-hit states first. Health ministers trade joint procurement money for pledges to move their critical systems off the supplier that cut them off.

In practice, the launch stalls. The containment work still consumes the same overtime pool that is now asked to stand up the new deployment. The licensed models arrive slower and weaker than the one that was denied. A few wards run their first tailored treatments on domestic capacity. Most patients are told to wait.

Italy's separate supply arrangement continues untouched, undercutting the joint plea to move. With Brussels conceding it cannot audit the newest unreadable systems, no new law on the most capable models is attempted. The grids and the wards hold, and the public counts dependence itself as the failure.
