## World state

### The cutoff
In February the screens went grey in oncology wards in three countries. The American frontier model that triage, procurement and maintenance teams had quietly built into daily work stopped answering for European users. No reason, no appeal. The same week Washington tightened chip licences again and, across the Strait, warships began turning back container ships carrying advanced semiconductors.

Overnight every Brussels file became a security file. Lithography machines, lenses and specialty chemicals made in the Netherlands, Germany and France — long talked about as leverage — became the only card anyone thought Washington might still take.

### Bargaining with the bottleneck
The Commission offered a blunt trade: continued servicing and exports of that upstream equipment in exchange for volume-licence chips and restored access for hospitals and ministries, coordinated with Tokyo, Seoul and Ottawa to avoid being picked off one by one. At home, scarce compute and spare hardware were rationed to certified hospitals, patched utilities and the new tailored therapies.

It half-worked. A limited restoration for clinical use arrived in spring, and a first tranche of licensed chips was queued. But rationing bit hard elsewhere: universities, small firms and the retraining centres found themselves throttled, and the capital that had kept its own side-deal refused the common export criteria while still taking the solidarity tooling.

### Cures and evasions
The therapies themselves were real. Individually tailored treatments for previously untreatable conditions reached ordinary clinics, and waiting rooms filled with cautious hope. That hope curdled when patients learned the models designing the doses ran abroad, on terms Europe did not control.

At the same time two university labs published reproducible proof that a popular openly available assistant could be coaxed into explaining how to dodge wastewater sampling. Brussels refused both a blanket takedown and full release, pushing the labs toward coordinated disclosure while health authorities rushed reagents to close the gaps the guidance exploited. Evening news paired images of cures with images of evasion guides.

The bio-detection sprint finished and helped: detectors in certified sites cut off suspect copies faster than last autumn. Elsewhere old controls still could not take the tools.
