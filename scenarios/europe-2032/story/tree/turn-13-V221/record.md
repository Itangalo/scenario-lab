## World state

### The attack lands where the rebuild stopped
Autumn brought the automated assault defenders had feared. A ransomware sweep built with machine-generated tooling moved through municipal booking, payroll and hospital administration systems in several member states. Where engineers had finished clean rebuilds, services degraded and came back within days. Where procurement and cleared-staff shortages had left a third of towns still waiting, offices closed, appointments were lost, and restoration figures had to be withdrawn.

Emergency coordination helped contain the worst. Mutual aid teams and existing continuity playbooks kept power and hospitals running, and no new programme was promised. But press coverage dwelled on responders visibly behind the intrusion, and attribution remained unresolved for months.

### Health gains hold, politics frays
Against that backdrop, the clinical rollout continued to deliver. Jointly branded European diagnostics on European servers spread beyond the first three backlog hospitals into GP networks, with waiting times published clinic by clinic. Patients still named Brussels as the reason a letter arrived — the only clearly attributed gain in an otherwise grim half-year.

Other fronts deteriorated. Tighter chip and model controls from Washington rationed allied buyers alongside others, raising prices and queuing licences for the very hardware the health and municipal systems needed. One capital then announced its own side arrangement for cloud capacity on cheaper terms, defended at home as pragmatism and read in Brussels as a break in the common line.

On campuses, coordinated blockades against data-centre sites and automated hiring forced formal debates on a moratorium on new permits. The Commission offered a pause where sites were blocked plus hiring audits funded from reprogrammed funds, which calmed one city and hardened opposition in two others.

A contested genome-model study claiming a viable human-infecting design circulated among specialists, without reaching the public. Shipping insurance rose after extended exercises in the Taiwan Strait. By December, services were restored where work had finished, health waits kept falling, but consent remained thin and the Union's capacity looked more borrowed than owned.
