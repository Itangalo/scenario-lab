# The screening surge

Brussels moves fast on paper in January. Sofia's note becomes the Bio-Uplift Detection and Hospital Shield Surge, and it copies the audit playbook of the isolation gates. Health emergency funds go to wastewater sequencing in the major hubs, screening at emergency-room triage, and renewed checks on DNA-synthesis providers. Recovery and health money flows only where the screening data is shared. Labs, pharmacies and synthesis firms get visits. General wards do not.

Implementation is uneven. Large university hospitals in the west plug in quickly. Municipal clinics, eastern laboratories and private synthesis intermediaries haggle over costs, staffing and who owns the sequence data. The French and Dutch medical teams stay on call and are stretched thin across follow-up visits. The programme buys coverage, not closure.

> **March 2032.** A genome-model study claiming a viable design for an organism able to infect humans circulates in biosecurity circles.

The methodologists attack it, and others accuse the authors of publishing too much. The fight stays technical. There is no public alarm, but inside the health agencies the mood darkens: with the controls still largely on paper, the next attempt will not be caught by a checklist.

> **April 2032.** Entry-level hiring in routine coding, standard analysis, first drafts and first-line support fails to recover.

The firms insist this is targeted automation, not a general wave, and point to juniors kept elsewhere and early cuts reversed. The unions call it a broken promise. The evening news leads with graduates without offers.

Against that, the city halls can point to something working. Several municipal health administrations using European-hosted models cut their waiting lists and decision times measurably, and the Commission claims the credit loudly. It steadies the talks with the unions. It does not shift a mood shaped by paper wards and fear for jobs.

The lockdown completes its term. Where the isolation gates hold, the winter's intrusions stop at the boundary. Where the new bio screening lags, officials admit in private that the gaps remain.
