# The lights flicker, the models go dark

> **January 2027.** A wave of automated ransomware and corrupted software updates sweeps through municipal administrations, regional hospitals and logistics firms in half a dozen member states. Emergency departments revert to paper, and two ports queue for days.

The tooling is plainly machine-written, fast and adaptive, and the defenders are weeks behind. The attackers came in through shared software; nobody knows how far it reached or which backups are clean. Nobody names a culprit.

At 14:30 on 9 February 2027, in the middle of the response, access to the leading American model is suspended for users based in the Union. No detailed reason is given, and there is no appeal. Clinics using diagnostic assistants, ministries using drafting assistants, and firms with contracted workflows find the tools refusing prompts. In some places the cut-off is brief. In others it persists. The rival American laboratory keeps serving throughout and picks up the traffic within the month, which makes this a migration rather than a blackout, and does nothing to make the migration quick or to make anyone feel less dependent at the end of it.

> **Claire:** the diagnostic assistant says "service unavailable in your region"
> **Claire:** my region is a hospital
>
> **Sofia:** We know. It's everyone.
>
> **Claire:** that's not the comfort you think it is

The message lands anyway: core tools can be withdrawn at will. Sofia's Institute, recommended in the autumn to find out what the models can do, is still hiring. What the winter needs is protection, and her note becomes the Grid and Public Services Shield. The President moves political capital off the gigafactories and onto it. Grid operators are ordered to wall off their networks, the passwords that switch power off are changed, and cross-border response teams drill under the EU's joint disaster-response scheme. Energy ministries grumble about costs and about who is in charge. Protests over power prices flare around two sites shortlisted for gigafactories, and slow their permits.

Two counterweights soften the blow.

> **March 2027.** A control technique from frontier laboratories makes certain agent behaviours predictable before deployment.
>
> **May 2027.** Public-sector deployments of AI triage scheduling cut waiting lists, and permit decisions arrive in days.

The first is copied quickly into European testing protocols, and gives the evaluators something real to check. The second is claimed loudly, and it is European.

By June the grids are walled off but not secure. The gigafactory plans have been rebranded as independence plans, and nothing has been built. Public sentiment has soured: using the tools, resenting them, and newly aware of the dependence.
