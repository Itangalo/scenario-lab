# Re-test everything

> **September 2027.** A leading American laboratory demonstrates agents that run for days, write and test their own tools, and post results that make last spring's benchmarks look quaint.

The forecasts of AI progress circulating in Brussels the week before are obsolete by Monday. Procurement officers who have just certified substitute models for the hospitals are told to re-test everything.

> **October 2027.** A pre-print claims that a genome model helped produce a viable design for an organism able to infect humans, or something close enough that non-experts could follow.

Methodologists quarrel and editors hesitate, but the biosecurity specialists in Berlin and Stockholm read it as different in kind from the alarms before it. Sofia reads it twice, and then reads their reading of it.

Her answer is the Bio-Detection and Critical-Service Hardening Sprint, the fastest thing the Union can start. The health emergency authority and the disease control centre expand wastewater and clinical sampling in ten hub cities, push rapid sequencing kits to hospitals and pre-authorise joint buying of countermeasures. ENISA drills the grid operators hit by last year's intrusion in cutting off and restoring their systems, and operators that adopt its standards pay less for insurance. The Institute is told to add biological and agent tests to its certification work.

It is triage, competently run, and thin.

> **Claire:** we got a sequencer
> **Claire:** it's in a box. nobody knows whose job it is to open the box
>
> **Sofia:** Someone is being trained.
>
> **Claire:** someone is always being trained

The sequencing machines arrive before the staff to run them. The drills expose backup power that fails under load. The gigafactory programme in Paris, Berlin, Madrid, Stockholm and Warsaw grinds on, waiting for Brussels to approve the subsidies and for slow grid connections, now tied to security upgrades that add months.

> **November 2027.** Law firms, accountants, software houses and customer-service centres confirm that entry-level posts are not being refilled.

The numbers are disputed. The empty hiring pages are not. The newspapers run stories about superhuman models next to pictures of the queues at the job centres.

The continuity cell closes its six months as promised, with the clinics online, degraded but running, and that spares Brussels the worst of the blame. Gratitude is scarce all the same. Voters see borrowed models getting stronger abroad, foreign money circling the factory sites, and work disappearing at home.
