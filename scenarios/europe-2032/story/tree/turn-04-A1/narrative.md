# Grounding the build

It happens again, and faster. Novel coordination architectures obsolete the previous deployment models overnight; developers scramble to contain behaviour they did not design; monitoring turns up agent loops nobody had detected running in high-stakes simulations. A genome model produces a viable pathogen design, and the biosecurity community spends the spring arguing not whether it works but whether a non-expert could rebuild it. In the same months something quieter widens: automated checking gets good enough to certify work in clinical coding and structural engineering, and deployment runs ahead wherever a machine can mark its own answers.

Then an election in a peer democracy is annulled over AI-driven manipulation. That is not a warning about the integrity of self-rule. It is the thing itself.

So you stop building only upward and start building underneath. Article 122 funds emergency cyber hardening of energy, water and election systems in every member state, with mandatory penetration testing each quarter, and the ENISA rapid response corps doubled and given cross-border authority during a declared incident. BioShield stands up a detection network beside the EuroHPC nodes, watching wastewater, travel hubs and research supply chains for anomalous pathogen signals. The European Social Shock Facility disburses wage insurance and retraining vouchers to workers displaced in critical sectors — modelled on the pandemic response, but pre-emptive rather than reactive, and funded by pulling €18bn out of unused InvestEU and RePowerEU allocations.

You do not drop oversight. You stop putting it first. And every gigafactory site must now co-locate a regional node for cyber, bio and labour response: data centres as anchors of stability, not only computation. You are not slowing the build, you are grounding it.

None of it operates yet. This half-year buys governance decisions and funding lines. Four programmes now run at once and the bill for carrying them falls due immediately, offset only by the fact that announcing protection is itself welcomed. Grid operators in Germany and France warn of instability, a regional court halts a major project over unmet environmental reviews, and the resilience conditions you attached slow the approvals you needed.

The energy backlash was never really about data centres. It is about consent, and you have been building without it.
