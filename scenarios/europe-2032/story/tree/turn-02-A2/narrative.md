# Dead keys

At 08:40 on 3 February 2027, the leading American AI model stops answering European users.

There is no notice period, no technical reason and no appeal. Hospitals in three member states, two ministries and dozens of firms that ran customer service, coding assistants and clinical summaries on it find their keys dead. Washington offers a single sentence about national security review.

> **3 February, 10:15.** Oncology wards in several member states revert to dictation. A national tax administration pauses its automated triage.
>
> **4 February.** Manufacturers report that copilots embedded in their production software have stopped working.

> **Claire:** dr roux is dictating letters into his phone
> **Claire:** like it's 1995. he's thrilled. he always hated the machine
>
> **Sofia:** And everyone else?
>
> **Claire:** everyone else is not thrilled

Sofia's Institute, recommended in the autumn to find out what the models can do, is still hiring. What the Union needs this week is not sight but somewhere to go. Her note becomes the Continuity Fallback, and the President puts the Commission's weight behind it. A six-month continuity cell under the health and digital directorates inventories every cut-off deployment and pays for emergency re-platforming onto European models and open systems hosted on the shared supercomputers, with procurement waivers and bridge loans. Paris and Berlin lend spare capacity on their own models.

The substitutes hallucinate where the old system had been steady. By June most critical users are running again anyway, degraded but running. The Institute gets a job it had not planned for: certifying the substitutes as safe to use. It gets no power at all to compel the American provider to come back.

The politics are harsher than the triage. Opposition MEPs and the national papers ask why years of warnings about dependence produced only factory blueprints and an institute still hiring. A Gulf offer to co-finance the gigafactory sites, whispered about in Council corridors, stops looking like leverage and starts looking like necessity. Insurers still nervous after the autumn audit raise cyber premiums and slow the grid connections for the very data-centre zones meant to be the permanent fix.

The site race and the permitting talks grind on, reframed as the long cure the fallback is buying time for.

Europe stays running. It does so, visibly, on borrowed time.
