# A shield on cracked foundations

The money goes first. Investment in frontier AI collapses, budgets shrink across the leading laboratories — and capability keeps advancing anyway, carried by efficiency gains and the reuse of infrastructure already paid for. The lesson is not comforting. The open models climb too, now handling supervised work measured in hours rather than minutes, and the distance between what is published and what is held closely narrows.

You answer the grid intrusion with the Hardened Cyber Shield: a binding regulation under Article 114 requiring every critical operator in energy, water, transport and health to run AI-driven monitoring, trained on adversarial emulation data, with automatic isolation triggered by agent-like behaviour. ENISA writes the technical baseline. €18bn comes from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, seventy per cent of it earmarked for retrofitting legacy systems in hospitals and municipal utilities. Member states appoint national coordinators and run a cross-border drill modelled on the 2023 Cyber Europe exercise, but with machine-generated attack vectors.

Germany and Poland immediately question who pays for municipal utilities. Italy does not appoint anyone. Retrofitting turns out harder than costed, because a great many critical systems have no telemetry at all — they cannot report their own status, let alone an intrusion.

The Gigafactories take the investment collapse full in the face. The EIB absorbs €48bn of stranded private commitments as direct loans, backstopped through the budget revision, and you go looking for mothballed semiconductor sites in Dresden and Grenoble to repurpose. France demands reciprocal access to Dutch chip sites as its price. Binding energy pacts — a fifth of each site's output to regional hydrogen and district heating, a third of the jobs ring-fenced locally — calm Brittany and inflame northern Sweden.

And the sovereignty package narrows. The levy on hyperscalers is dropped before the WTO can kill it, the private capital target is cut, and what remains is a condition: keep your model weights on EU soil, and let the Directorate inspect. Less growth, bought for more control.
