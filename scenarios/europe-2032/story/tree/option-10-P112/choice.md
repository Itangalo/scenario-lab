# Surge and treat

**EU Bio Containment and Treatment Surge.** Preparedness and resilience. Small; running until the first half of 2032.

Sofia recommends preparing for a biological emergency by building the capacity to treat, not only to isolate: weeks-long containment, triage and distribution of countermeasures, run through the health emergency authority, the disease control centre and the cross-border health powers.

It acts under the treaty's public-health and solidarity articles, through the Health Security Committee. The health emergency authority funds countermeasure stocks and surge isolation capacity. The disease control centre leads tracing through the federated early-warning pilots. The cross-border patient mechanism, from the medical surge already running, is ready to move casualties off the paper wards. ENISA's reserve teams stay on patching without taking medical staff. No new AI law is imposed, because the Union cannot govern the frontier from where it stands. The gigafactory disbursement stays frozen, and ministers are asked to defend the lithography position on paper without spending votes on it.

The argument is that manual wards already carrying two years of backlog cannot absorb casualties on top. What the Union can hold is its hospitals, open through containment, and that is where the overtime, the double-check teams and the cross-border hires should go.

The President puts the Commission's political weight behind the treatment surge.

What it does not do is take automated triage out of the wards.

*All ten simulated responses to this situation built biological containment. They divided on its emphasis: some around a treatment surge, as this one does, and others around cordons and manual triage, the direction of the other recommendation.*
