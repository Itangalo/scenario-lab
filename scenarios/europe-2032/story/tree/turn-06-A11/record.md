## World state

### The jump and the machines
Winter ended with two announcements weeks apart. First a foreign lab showed a system that planned, coded and ran tools for days without drifting — benchmarks leaked, then a demo. Every deployment timeline in Brussels became scrap. Weeks later Chinese and American firms put humanoid robots on commercial sale: warehouse units first, then municipal pilots for digging, sorting and mine clearance. Videos of dexterous hands spread faster than any safety sheet.

In Europe the shock landed on factories that still led in machines but not in models. German and Italian integrators queued for foreign control stacks while talking about domestic alternatives. Works councils warned of a second displacement wave with no trade to retreat to.

### Washington opens a door
In January the new American administration took office and offered what it had promised: published tiers for allied access to frontier systems, researcher seats in joint evaluations, alignment on export controls. EEAS teams shuttled to Washington. Draft protocols circulated. American officials were cordial but slow; access tiers remained PowerPoint while lawyers argued over audit rights.

At home relief curdled quickly. Parliamentarians asked why two narrowed gigafactory sites should still get grid priority if American models would be available on terms. DG COMP held the line, pairing every access paragraph with continuation of the domestic build.

### Small towns first
The Commission put its weight behind mayors. Wage bridges were extended, clinic shifts backfilled, and civil protection teams drilled manual chlorination fallback in village water plants. The insurer backstop kept one major reinsurer from exiting small-utility cover, though premiums still rose.

Large hospitals held. Triage assistants cut waits and local news crews were invited to film it. In smaller municipalities where staff never arrived, boil notices and premium letters still set the mood. Polls stayed bleak, job-loss coverage now joined by footage of robots in logistics halls.

By June the sovereignty package's data-centre permits and capital lines were formally closed, and the bio-shield stockpiles and monitoring network were declared operational. Engineers called both real but partial: capacity permitted, not powered; monitoring wide, not deep.
