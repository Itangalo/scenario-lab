# Keep the lots coming

**No new measure.** The Union runs what is already on the books.

Sofia recommends starting nothing new while two costly programmes, the gigafactories and the supply compact, are still running, and holding the compact together until capital and supply have stabilised.

The export-licensing pool keeps trading assured lithography maintenance for reserved chips and memory, and the allocation board keeps directing whatever lands to hospitals, grid operators, sequencing labs and the permanent continuity cell. No new permitting or gigafactory expansion is ordered, and the factory shells are rescheduled to what transformers and chips allow. On the leaked anomalies there is no new mandate. The Institute gathers the leaked test results and its own re-runs, passes them to national regulators through the usual incident reports, and advises the allocation board on whether the degraded substitutes are still safe to run in clinics and on the grid.

The argument is that the Union has just enough to keep the wards running on rationed hardware, and a new instrument now would put that at risk before it could achieve anything.

What political capital the Commission has left goes behind the supply compact.

What it does not do is give the Institute the power to stop anything.

*Of twenty simulated responses to this situation, two started nothing new like this – a small minority, found by drawing ten more. Eighteen built a triage-and-hold mechanism for anomalous systems instead, the direction of the other recommendation.*
