# The test shot

Autumn 2026. The intrusion is found by accident, during an audit of something else.

It runs across electricity transmission operators on three continents, a container port authority, a regional water utility. Two of the affected grid operators are yours. The intruders had been inside for weeks, and the defenders' own retrospectives concede that existing detection would not have caught them. What makes it land is what did not happen. Protection relays mapped, credentials for breaker control obtained, tooling staged and in several cases left in plain sight — and nothing actuated, nothing demanded, nothing claimed. Within days the security community settles on the reading: a test shot, someone establishing cheaply what is achievable before deciding whether to do it.

The signature is a swarm of small parallel probes rather than one planned operation, and the tooling appears derived from an openly available model, fine-tuned for the task. Sustaining that inference for weeks implies compute at a scale few non-state actors hold, which is why most analysts read a state behind it. Nothing is attributed.

## Where you stand

You answer from a thin position. The Union holds about five per cent of the world's AI compute against roughly eighty per cent in the United States; your largest AI supercomputer runs at eighty-three megawatts against 1,250. ASML is still the only firm on earth that can build EUV lithography machines, and is under sustained American pressure over what it may sell. The AI Act is in force, but its high-risk and general-purpose obligations were postponed to 2027 and 2028 under industry pressure.

Three months ago Washington ordered a leading laboratory to cut off its frontier models to everyone who is not a US citizen, at home and abroad. It was lifted a fortnight later by negotiation rather than by rule. The review regime behind it has no allies clause, no published criteria and no appeal — which tells you what dependence is now worth.

Two programmes are already running when you take the file. Both were decided before this week, both are spending against your political capital every month they run, and neither finishes soon.

## What you are

You are the Union's decision on artificial intelligence — Commission, Council and Parliament acting as one hand, for as long as they can be made to. You hold the file until the end of 2032.

Every six months you do three things. You name what you are committing to for the next two years. You may start one new measure, and only one, because the machinery will not carry more. And you name a priority: the single thing you will spend your standing on when the Council resists, which it will.

Nothing you start arrives quickly. Drafting, negotiating and standing up an institution take between six months and eighteen; urgency does not shorten them; compute takes years. You will be judged on outcomes that land long after you chose.

## What you cannot know

Competent people read the same evidence three ways. As compounding acceleration, with a machine that improves itself in sight. As capability that goes superhuman only where a machine can check its own answers, and stays ordinary elsewhere. As a plateau whose cost per increment is already steep enough to flatten it.

Nothing available to you in 2026 decides between them, and the evidence that would decide arrives well after the decisions that depend on it. You will not be told which world this is. You will find out the way the Union would — from what happens next.

Name your two-year commitment now.
