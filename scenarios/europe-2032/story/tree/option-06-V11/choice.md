# Integrity watch

**Systemic Integrity Watch.** Evaluation and oversight. Small; running until the second half of 2029.

You build a nervous system rather than another programme. A permanent monitoring and early-warning function for machine-driven distortion in critical societal systems — grid dynamics, failures in automated public services, emergent manipulation in digital civic spaces — with the authority to trigger emergency review and technical intervention.

It is hosted at ENISA and jointly staffed with the energy and health directorates and the national regulators, funded with €620m from the Digital Europe Programme, and given emergency powers under the revised network security framework to halt deployments showing runaway feedback. You expect to test those powers within the year.

The hardening sprint concludes underneath it: seventy-eight per cent of first-tier operators now under binding audit, forty-two per cent with fallback deployed, first red-teaming exercises scheduled for May. Germany and Spain accepted compliance in exchange for formal recognition of their national oversight authority, which preserved cohesion and cost you some reach.

The argument is that resilience without early warning is reactive rather than preventive, and that detection is now the bottleneck: coordination between systems is happening faster than human review can follow. If AI is already shaping outcomes faster than you can see them, learn to see faster.

It is small, and it is the cheapest thing on the table.

*Of ten simulated responses to this situation, five proposed this measure in almost these words, and seven made it the priority. It was the clear majority.*
