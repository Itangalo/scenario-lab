# Grids first

The spring belongs to the grid operators. After the autumn's discovery of intruders who had lived inside the networks for weeks, Sofia's first new measure is not the Institute she recommended – that is still hiring – but the EU Grid Shield Hardening Programme, and the President puts the Commission's standing behind it. ENISA pushes emergency segmentation standards through, and the two transmission operators that were hit, along with their peers in the five states hosting the planned gigafactories, are ordered to install network monitoring and pool the telemetry from their breaker logs.

> **March 2027.** Energy ministers endorse the mandate.

On paper it moves fast. On the ground the vendors slip, the telemetry formats clash, and eastern operators bargain their hardening contracts against cohesion money before they sign anything.

The factory programme grinds on alongside. The digital directorate advances site selection and the permitting zones, but land deals stall over grid pledges, the financing still needs multi-year budget sign-off, and no EU borrowing is tabled. The Institute begins hiring, focused on testing cyber tooling, and has no independent results yet.

> **April 2027.** Washington tightens chip and model export licences again. Early drafts suggested volume licences for allied buyers. The final text rations allies alongside adversaries, capping high-end shipments and access to cloud computing.

European buyers face queues and paperwork. Talks linking cooperation on the factories to better terms produce communiqués, not licences. Leverage over the lithography supply chain is discussed in Brussels, and not used.

> **Claire:** our new imaging software is delayed
> **Claire:** licence queue in washington apparently. for a hospital in lyon
>
> **Sofia:** Everyone is in the queue.

> **Claire:** that's what worries me

The evening news ties the autumn's intrusions to freely available models, and councils in one candidate region freeze their permitting hearings after residents cite the strain on power and water. There is no blackout; the grid stays quiet. But the costs of containment and the delayed investment leave the mood more anxious than before.
