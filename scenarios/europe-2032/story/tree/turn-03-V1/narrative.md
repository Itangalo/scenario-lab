# The attack that got through

At 06:40 on 23 October 2027, a routine update to a widely used piece of maintenance software starts opening doors.

It comes as a wave, not a single strike. Automated ransomware spreads across municipal IT, hospitals and two grid operators in three member states. Appointments are cancelled, billing freezes, and substations are forced into manual operation. The new behaviour-based detectors catch part of it. The slower movement from system to system slips past them. Television shows emergency departments working on paper forms.

> **Claire:** ED is on paper. the whole hospital actually
> **Claire:** the new detector caught the first bit. then it just walked around it

> **Sofia:** We know. We're sending teams.
>
> **Claire:** send them to lyon first. i'm biased

Brussels activates the emergency reserve. Joint response teams go to the worst-hit sites, and EU-paid recovery licences are pushed out. When insurers threaten to pull cover for essential operators, Sofia's note becomes the Incident Continuity Backstop, and finance ministers quietly agree to a temporary public reinsurance line behind it. The President puts the Commission's standing on it.

The backstop holds. No insurer exits outright, and services degrade rather than stop. The recovery is ragged all the same: smaller municipalities lack staff to use the tools, mutual aid arrives late in one country after a quarrel at its interior ministry, and the costs overrun. Attribution is still unresolved in December.

> **November 2027.** Washington tightens chip export controls again. Allied buyers keep volume licences, but new paperwork and compliance checks slow deliveries of advanced accelerators to Europe and raise their price.

The Commission chooses not to retaliate. It keeps its equipment leverage in reserve and talks about wider coordination with other middle powers next year. One gigafactory site sees its foundations poured. Two others stay tangled in court injunctions.

> **December 2027.** Studies across law, accountancy, administration and consulting show clear productivity gains from AI assistants, strongest among junior staff, and no layoffs. Firms that cut early are rehiring.

The public holds both realities at once: AI is useful at work, and frightening in hospitals. Trust wobbles, and does not collapse.

Europe ends 2027 visibly behind its attackers, kept afloat by emergency money, and still waiting for its own machines.
