# Paperwork becomes practice

Nothing happens in the first half of 2027, in the sense that nothing arrives from outside. No attack, no cut-off, no leak. What happens is the Shield.

It moves out of Brussels and into hospitals, municipal utilities and grid control rooms. There are mandatory reporting drills and two cross-border exercises run through the cyber agency, and detection software is bought jointly with civil-protection money topped up by cohesion funds. The President keeps the Commission's standing on it.

The results are mixed, and they are public. Finance and telecoms operators pass. Several hospitals and mid-size city networks fail openly: paging systems that do not fail over, backup restores that take days, staff who do not know whom to report to.

The interior ministries that resisted the new obligations take the deal on offer: EU-paid hardening in exchange for signed, exercised continuity plans. It is uneven, and it is moving.

The gigafactories do not move as fast. Choosing four or five sites turns into a contest between three capitals over grid connections, jobs and water. The Commission holds the line that priority power requires legal anchoring inside the Union, which stops an open subsidy race and does not stop the bargaining. One shortlisted site meets coordinated local opposition over its use of power and water, with permit questions raised in council meetings and in the press. Officials talk of renegotiation, not cancellation.

Quietly, the insurers start asking harder questions. Brokers let it be known that cyber cover for smaller utilities and hospitals will soon require proof of hardening, now that everyone knows intruders were sitting in transmission systems, a port and a water utility last autumn. Nothing is repriced yet. The risk managers notice.

Elsewhere the frontier keeps advancing, and the gap widens between what openly available models can do at scale and what European defenders can assure.

By June the capitals describe a Union spending heavily to hold three large programmes at once, with little finished to show for it.
