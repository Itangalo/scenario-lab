# Triage semester

Brussels spends July to December trying not to lose more ground. Sofia's notes recommend nothing new, and for the first time the President names no priority at all; the Commission's standing is too low to spend. The emergency teams stay on the spring intrusion, tracing the poisoned component through municipal servers and hospital administration. Ports and clinics run in degraded mode for months, with spare parts shuttled between regions and daily technical notes meant to show that defenders are no longer silent. Nobody names a culprit.

The political damage is harder to patch. The member state with its own compute deal keeps it. The Commission keeps its coercion file open and does not escalate, offering again a joint approach to Washington and a shared licensing table. Neither side moves. The five reserved data-centre plots stay empty, with the permits kept warm by administrators who have no budget to build.

> **October 2029.** An openly downloadable model family appears only months behind the closed frontier. Hundreds of thousands of copies spread in days.

For the European security services it means that the ransomware tooling seen in the spring can now be reproduced on private hardware, beyond recall.

> **Claire:** someone in IT is running the new free model on a laptop
> **Claire:** they say it's better than what we had. nobody authorised it
>
> **Sofia:** Nobody can stop it either.
>
> **Claire:** so it's authorised then

> **November 2029.** A laboratory across the Atlantic announces machine-proposed materials structures confirmed in the lab, hailed by specialists as a landmark for battery chemistry.

European papers report it as proof of what disciplined automation can do where results can be checked, and as a reminder that the advance happened somewhere else.

The middle-power compact formally concludes, the Union's only funded external track. It brings polite communiqués about diversified sourcing, and no new capacity at home.

By December power prices are rising again as the wind output stalls. The street protests over bills merge with the anger over locked health records. The Union has held its line on paper, and the sense of dependence dictated from outside deepens.
