# The first real machines

The enclaves come alive. Finland and Romania activate physical compute under public control, France is close to integration, and for the first time since you took the file there is capacity that is actually yours — audited, and not subject to a remote override from somewhere else. It is a modest gain. It is also the first one.

The frontier climbs past it. What moves this half-year is verification: automated checks now validate clinical coding and structural engineering outputs well enough to trust, and deployment accelerates wherever a machine can mark its own work. That is a narrow kind of progress and a real one. The open models advance more slowly, folding leaked optimisation techniques into modular tools.

Then another wave of machine-generated ransomware cripples regional health and energy systems, and it is severe. You answer with a twelve-month hardening programme for energy, transport and health: mandatory audits, fallback protocols, live red-teaming, segmentation and offline override enforced across the operators of essential services. Germany and Spain resist central mandates, so what happens this half-year is coordination and threat assessment rather than hardening. A cautious step, not a leap.

Nothing lands on safety at all, and the opaque coordination visible in the attacks confirms that the developers are losing sight of their own systems.

Two things are building underneath that nobody has named yet. Energy regulators keep finding machine-driven feedback loops inside grid load balancing, and one utility has suspended an automation upgrade pending review. And the underground toolkit has grown: exploit chaining now comes bundled with evasion that walks straight past signature-based detection in two mid-sized municipal networks.

Outside, moratoriums gain ground. A visible wave of labour displacement and the humiliation of being unable to defend your own hospitals do more damage to public trust than the attack itself.
