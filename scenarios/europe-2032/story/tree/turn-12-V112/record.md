## World state

### The attack that found the fallback
The ransomware sweep did not need to be clever. It rode automatically generated lures and a poisoned update in a facilities-management package used by hospitals, city utilities and a port operator. Screens froze in wards already running on the degraded European-hosted stack, and in three cities heating controls and appointment systems went together.

Because essential registries had been forced onto EuroHPC-hosted open models, the damage stopped short of a full stop. ENISA-led cutover teams isolated segments, restored from clean images, and kept triage summaries running in text-only mode. Still, evening bulletins counted cancelled operations and leaked municipal files. Defenders looked a step behind, and the tooling was plainly machine-made.

### Money leaves, cures arrive
Almost simultaneously, venture and project finance pulled back from AI build-out. Two expanded GPU-as-a-service contracts that EuroHPC managers had counted on for autumn were cancelled, and a private top-up for inference capacity evaporated. Brussels chose to defend already-reserved public-interest inference rather than promise new capacity, ring-fencing chip deliveries for hospitals and registries.

In the same months, tailored cancer and rare-disease therapies designed with frontier models reached ordinary clinics. For families it was real hope — but newspaper explainers noted the models behind the therapies ran abroad, on systems Europe could no longer reliably query. Gratitude mixed with humiliation.

### Checked continuity
The Commission's answer was to make the fallback checkable. Staff from the earlier assurance cell fanned out with EuroHPC engineers to apply a newly published interpretability and control method — one the large labs themselves were adopting — to the hospital and registry models. Pre-release checks, logging, and refusal behaviour could now be demonstrated to clinicians and mayors.

Uptake was uneven. Where migration was complete, certification helped calm staff; where cutover teams were still re-installing, it looked like paperwork on top of queues. Hospital unions staged short walkouts in two cities, and a rumour spread that telemetry sharing meant surveillance of medical decisions. The continuity switch held, degraded, but the political breathing room did not widen.
