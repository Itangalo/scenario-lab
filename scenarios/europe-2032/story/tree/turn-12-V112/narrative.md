# Checked continuity

> **February 2032.** A ransomware sweep rides automatically generated lures and a poisoned update to a facilities-management package used by hospitals, city utilities and a port operator. In three cities, heating controls and appointment systems go down together – and in two of them for the second time, on networks certified clean in the autumn.

The sweep does not need to be clever. Screens freeze even in the wards already running on the slower European systems. Because the essential registries have been forced onto open models hosted on the Union's supercomputers, the damage stops short of a full stop. Teams led by ENISA cut off parts of the network, restore from clean copies and keep triage summaries running in text-only mode. The evening bulletins still count cancelled operations and leaked municipal files, and the defenders still look a step behind plainly machine-made tools.

> **March 2032.** Venture and project finance pull back from AI build-out. Two expanded contracts for rented computing that EuroHPC, which runs the EU's shared supercomputers, had counted on for the autumn are cancelled.

Private money for more AI capacity evaporates with them. Brussels defends the computing time already reserved for public services rather than promising new capacity, and ring-fences chip deliveries for hospitals and registries. The President's political capital goes behind the switch.

> **April 2032.** Tailored cancer and rare-disease therapies designed with frontier models reach ordinary clinics.

For families it is real hope. The newspaper explainers point out that the models behind the therapies run abroad, on systems Europe can no longer reliably query. Gratitude mixes with humiliation.

Sofia's answer is to make the fallback checkable. The Deployed Assurance Fast-Track sends staff from the closed black-box testing cell out with supercomputer engineers to apply a newly published method for seeing why a model answers as it does and steering it, one the large laboratories are adopting themselves, to the hospital and registry models. Checks before release, logging and what the models refuse to do can now be shown to clinicians and mayors.

The uptake is uneven. Where the move is complete, the certification calms the staff. Where teams are still reinstalling, it looks like paperwork on top of the queues.
