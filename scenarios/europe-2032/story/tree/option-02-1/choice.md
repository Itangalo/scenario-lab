# Build the capacity

**Your commitment for the next two years: secure independent AI capacity to act with sovereignty in an accelerating world.**

Compute is power, and power cannot be outsourced if sovereignty is to mean anything. You draft the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive under Article 114: any operator running more than 100 petaFLOPS of AI throughput on your soil must reserve 30 per cent of it for uses you define — critical infrastructure resilience, public health modelling, verified safety research, state-authorised defence. Not nationalisation. A use condition for operating at scale in your market, like grid or spectrum licensing.

You expect the fight. The Council's Competitiveness formation is already signalling resistance, and fears both capital flight and retaliation from US cloud providers, so the directive travels with an offer: fast-track certification into the Gigafactories fund, a liability shield for operators acting in good faith, and reserved capacity scheduled through a transparent auction rather than political favour. Alongside it you negotiate AI Acceleration Zones with seven member states — permitting capped at six months, grid connection guaranteed within eighteen, local opposition answered with binding community benefit agreements.

This answers the test shot by owning the machines. It does nothing directly about what runs on them, and nothing you build arrives quickly. The Directive is what you will spend your standing on, and it is law by the second half of 2027.

*Thirty independent simulations of this decision were run. Twelve answered with sovereignty and industrial capacity, as this one does — the largest single group.*
