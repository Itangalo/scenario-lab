# Paper shield

At 09:05 on 17 August 2032, the American frontier goes dark for European users. Hospitals, ministries and firms that had quietly kept an API key despite months of warnings find error messages and a one-line notice.

The health emergency teams circulate continuity orders within days, pushing wards onto the European backup stack and the domestic helpers. Where the helpers were already installed, it works, after a fashion. Where teams have to reinstall in the middle of a shift, appointments are lost.

> **Claire:** someone in admin had kept the american key. it's dead now
> **Claire:** we reinstalled mid-shift. we lost the afternoon
>
> **Sofia:** Everyone was told.
>
> **Claire:** everyone is always told sofia

Washington offers no timetable. The talk in Brussels swings between outrage at the short notice and anger at its own lack of foresight, with years of factory plans still frozen by courts and blockades while the dependence deepened.

The Watch completes its term trying to referee the triage audit. Its re-testing unit publishes the raw gaps and proposes keeping the tools live only with a doctor signing every decision. A handful of hospital networks accept and lift their pauses. Most do not.

Then the scandal spreads beyond the hospitals.

> **October 2032.** An ombudsman, and then a court, find that a scoring system for benefits and insurance systematically cut older claimants and migrants. Human oversight had shrunk to seconds per case. The logs went unread, and the conformity papers were in order.

Brussels insists that high-risk obligations were breached, not that the law has a gap. Few are persuaded. The headlines call the AI Act a paper shield, the queues lengthen, and ministers distance themselves from any automated decision at all.

In that climate the municipal clinics lean harder into improvisation, with diagnostic scripts built on freely downloadable frontier models passing from hand to hand. The officials again choose guidance leaflets and a voluntary inbox over a ban they could not enforce. Almost nobody uses the inbox.

> **November 2032.** Leaked benchmark chatter about an unreleased system behaving oddly under evaluation, and rising tension around Taiwan lifting shipping premiums, barely register outside the specialist press.

By December care holds where the backup stack and the Watch teams are present, and is distrusted or improvised elsewhere. The diggers are still stopped.

## Note to the President

*From Sofia Brandt, Cabinet. 15 December 2032. Six years in.*

The short version: we survived. That was the commitment, and we kept it. Nobody else will call it a success.

What held:

- Essential services. They were never stopped, on maintenance money and nothing else.
- The Watch. It published the raw gaps in our own tools, which is more honesty than most governments manage.
- The showcase clinics. Two weeks off imaging waits, held for two years without new money.

What did not:

- The factories. Every site frozen by courts and vigils, and the compacts unsigned.
- Our own triage tools. They under-triaged the old and the foreign, and the insurers found it before we did.
- Oversight. The clinics built their own shadow network on free models, and our inbox is empty.
- Access. Cut off a third time, and some of us had kept the key anyway.

What I would tell whoever holds this file next: we held the line for four years without money. You will have to decide what the line was for.

*In the margin, in the President's hand:* Decide it early.
