## World state

### Queues and verdicts
Two stories about the state ran side by side through the autumn, and voters believed both.

In several cities, rebuilt public-sector systems finally delivered what ministers had promised for years: hospital waiting lists that visibly fell where cross-border teams and surge stocks arrived, benefit decisions returned in days, classrooms where tutoring tools showed measured gains. Commissioners toured the sites and claimed the gains as European choices, not imported software.

At the same time, investigators and an ombudsman laid out what a benefits and policing support system had done. Thousands of files had passed with a caseworker confirming a machine recommendation in under a minute, logs recording the pattern and nobody reading them. Conformity papers were in order; oversight had been a queue. Opposition papers called it proof the rulebook worked on paper and failed on people. Legal commentators argued the darker version — that parts of the deployment had never been classed as high-risk at all, leaving everything done entirely lawful.

Brussels answered with a redress guarantee: retention and machine-reading of logs already held, mandatory human re-review, interim payments and compensation through the social fund, and a directive reading that any system materially shaping eligibility, policing or court outcomes falls inside the strict regime. Ombudsmen opened desks; re-review began in the worst districts.

It did not land cleanly. Re-review was slow, staff were the same exhausted staff from the containment wards, and early payouts varied by region. The member state with its own hyperscaler arrangement accepted surge reimbursement only when routed through logged European infrastructure, but kept its separate contract. Data-centre and supply freezes stayed frozen.

The medical surge programme formally closed, and in the regions it had reached, clinics degraded rather than stopped during late outbreaks — the one clear absorption success of the year. It was drowned out by the scandal coverage. Fear of sealed wards gave way to anger at automated refusals, and adoption, even where it worked, became politically toxic again.
