# Hold the line as it is

**No new measure.** The Union runs what is already on the books.

Sofia recommends changing nothing – not the commitment, not the portfolio – until there is fiscal room again and the finished continuity and bio-cyber capacity has been given a chance to work.

Containment comes first in practice. ENISA, a cell from the Institute and the grid shield's control-room teams are put on it: pooled breaker-log and incident reporting from the shield's telemetry, checks on weight security and agent behaviour from the Institute, and the automated patching tools wherever they are already deployed, with the health emergency authority's sequencing standards on standby. No new thresholds are legislated; the existing incident-reporting bases do the work. The complaints about the replacement tools are treated as a crisis of legitimacy for the substitution, not a procurement dispute. Forced migrations are paused where the European replacements break referrals, the old workflows are kept on licensed standby, and the two cities where European scheduling cut waiting lists become the argument for the rest. The frozen factory councils stay frozen, and no standing is spent on siting.

The argument is that with the Commission's standing this low, the Union should run what it has, and fix the tools people are complaining about before asking them to trust anything new.

The President's standing goes on the grid shield.

What it does not do is change what the Union is trying to achieve.

*All ten simulated responses to this situation started nothing new. Seven held the commitment as it was, as this one does – the majority. Three rewrote it towards containment, the direction of the other recommendation.*
