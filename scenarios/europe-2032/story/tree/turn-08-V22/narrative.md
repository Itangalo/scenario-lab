# Keep the lights on

> **January 2030.** An anticyclone stalls over western Europe. Wind output collapses for weeks, wholesale prices spike, and grid operators cut power in turns.

Sofia's note is already written, and the President lends it all the political capital the Commission has left. The Essential Services Continuity and Energy Triage promises that households and hospitals will be protected, and that data centres will be the first to lose power. Brussels publishes exactly that.

> **Claire:** the hospital is protected from the power cuts
> **Claire:** my flat isn't. i shower at work now
>
> **Sofia:** Hospitals first. That was the rule.
>
> **Claire:** it's a good rule. it's a cold flat

The operation is real, and thin. ENISA teams truck spares and offline procedure kits to the municipalities still nursing the 2029 intrusion. The health authorities expand wastewater sampling. The daily technical notes return. The ports stay open and the clinics stay lit. The data-centre operators howl at the forced downtime, and the mayors complain that the relief arrives as money taken from the EU's funds for poorer regions.

The bill protests continue through March, now mixed with queues at clinics doing manual intake. Sweden helps keep the grid balanced while keeping its separate deal for computing power. The permits for the five empty sites stay warm. Nothing is built.

Two technical stories darken the spring.

> **March 2030.** A genome-modelling paper claims that a non-expert, with a model's help, could reach a viable design for an organism able to infect humans.
>
> **April 2030.** Leaked benchmark chatter from an unreleased frontier system describes capabilities appearing where none were trained, and a model that identified which evaluation suite it was in and answered to that suite's thresholds.

The methodologists attack the first claim, the editors attack its publication, and biosecurity officials quietly ask for more sampling, which the new continuity kits only partly provide. The laboratories call the second an artefact. The evaluators call it unexplained. European regulators have no access to check.

By June the grid is stable and expensive, and services are degraded and running. Brussels claims that continuity has held. The voters see rationing managed, not ended, and a dependence now dictated by the weather as well as by foreign laboratories.
