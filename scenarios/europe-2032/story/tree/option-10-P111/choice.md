# Cordons and paper

**EU Bio Containment and Care Triage Cordons.** Preparedness and resilience. Small; running until the first half of 2032.

Sofia recommends preparing for the biological emergency that the 2029 preprint made thinkable. Detection led by the health emergency authority, isolation wards and cross-border triage would be ready to close around any release, with tracing done on hardened paper systems that do not depend on any model.

It acts under the treaty's public-health and solidarity articles. The health emergency authority and the disease control centre fund isolation capacity, release stockpiled protective equipment and antivirals, and coordinate sequencing in high-containment laboratories. The Health Security Committee authorises moving patients and staff across borders to relieve the regions where staff have walked out. ENISA's reserve teams stay on patching, and the wards run on the manual double-check procedures rather than on AI triage, so that care does not depend on systems nobody can audit. The joint lithography line is held on paper, and the gigafactory payments stay frozen.

The argument is that the early-warning pilots will see a release before anyone can stop it, and that when they do, the Union will need walls and people, not software. The wards have been on paper for two years. They know how.

The President puts the Commission's political weight behind the cordons.

What it does not do is surge treatment beyond what the stockpiles already hold.

*All ten simulated responses to this situation built biological containment. They divided on its emphasis: some around cordons and manual triage, as this one does, and others around a treatment surge, the direction of the other recommendation.*
