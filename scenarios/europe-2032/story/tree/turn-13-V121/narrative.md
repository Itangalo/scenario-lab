# Dark wards, bright offices

The autumn brings two Europes into the same news bulletin.

> **September 2032.** Another largely automated ransomware sweep, riding a trusted administrative update, takes down systems in several municipalities and two hospital groups. Records vanish, appointments collapse to paper, ambulances are diverted.

The recovery teams cut networks and pull locked backups off the shelves, and where the rehearsals held, the booking desks are back within days. Elsewhere, staff rebuild registries by hand for weeks. The investigators agree the tooling was written by a model. Nobody can name who ran it.

> **October 2032.** Law firms, accountants, local administrations and newsrooms report measured output gains from AI assistants, largest among juniors, and no new layoffs. Firms that cut early are quietly hiring again.

Work is changing shape rather than disappearing. Brussels pushes its triage cell hard, and the President keeps the Commission's standing on it. ENISA teams fund restores from existing envelopes, run isolation drills in the towns that were hit, and keep the pooled allied feeds flowing. It stops the cascades, and it underlines the dependence: the detection is borrowed, and the spare parts are counted.

That dependence sharpens when Washington tightens its licences once more. The thresholds fall again, and European orders are delayed or downgraded. With domestic fabrication still fenced and idle, the procurement officers have stopped speaking of autonomy at all.

> **October 2032.** A near-frontier open release is downloaded hundreds of thousands of times. Whatever it can do is now on private machines, beyond recall.
>
> **November 2032.** A genome-model result suggesting a path by which a non-expert could reach a design able to infect humans circulates in biosecurity circles.

It is contested and alarming in equal measure. Sofia's last new measure is the Bio Early-Warning and Response Surge. The health emergency authority and the disease control centre upgrade sequencing, wastewater screening and hospital reporting in the cooperating cities, and pre-position diagnostics and paper-triage kits on the model of the offline kits.

> **Claire:** we got a bio kit. same idea as the cyber box
> **Claire:** a box. with paper forms and swabs. i know exactly where it lives
>
> **Sofia:** That's all we need you to know.
>
> **Claire:** that's all anyone ever needs me to know

The civic repair inches forward. Health triage with workload limits and published logs keeps cutting waiting times where mayors signed, and the office gains give it quiet cover. The insurers still withhold blanket cover without a statute, the unions keep welfare automation frozen, and gratitude where help arrives sits beside anger at rationing everywhere else.

## Note to the President

*From Sofia Brandt, Cabinet. 15 December 2032. Six years in.*

The short version: we borrowed our defence from allies and built our legitimacy out of published logs. Both held. Neither is ours to keep.

What held:

- The accession. Twenty minutes of warning, from a feed we do not own, is the difference between a dark town and a working one.
- The standard pack. Where mayors signed it, the waiting lists fell and patients read the logs.
- The boxes. Offline kits for cyber, and now for biology.

What did not:

- The chips. The controls tightened twice this year, and our fabrication is fenced.
- The insurers. They still want a statute.
- Welfare automation. After lawful harm, it is still frozen, and rightly.
- The towns that did not plug in. They rebuilt registries by hand, twice.

What I would tell whoever holds this file next: the defence we could share, we shared. Now pass the statute the insurers are waiting for, and let the tools that work reach everyone.

*In the margin, in the President's hand:* Draft it. I'll carry it.
