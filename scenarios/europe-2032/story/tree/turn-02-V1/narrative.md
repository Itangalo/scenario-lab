# A tool that works

> **February 2027.** Research laboratories on both sides of the Atlantic demonstrate automated patching and detection that catch swarming probes by their behaviour, not their signatures. In trials it closes off an entire class of intrusion at machine speed.

For the first time in years, the defenders get good news. European operators, still shaken by the dormant footholds found in their grids and ports in the autumn, are eager buyers.

Sofia moves fast, because for once there is something to move with. Her note becomes the Swarm Defence Deployment, and the Shield, already running drills with grid, port, water and telecom operators, becomes its delivery vehicle. Through joint procurement, EU-paid licences for the new tools go out in exchange for telemetry sharing. Interior ministries that fought the Shield's obligations are told the obligations shrink if they adopt the tooling now. The uptake is uneven, and real.

> **Claire:** we got the new detection thing on the hospital network
> **Claire:** IT says it's the first software they've ever been excited about
>
> **Sofia:** It's good. It isn't everything.
>
> **Claire:** don't tell them that

It is not everything. The new defences cover one attack pattern well and others barely at all. Integration with legacy control systems breaks repeatedly. Municipal utilities complain that the licences arrive without staff to run them. And the attackers adapt within months, shifting to slower, quieter methods the detectors miss.

The gigafactory drive grinds on. Choosing the final four to five sites takes the whole half-year, with regions lobbying fiercely and two sites facing court challenges over grid priority and environmental permits. Ground is broken in one place. Everywhere else the paperwork moves faster than the excavators.

> **May 2027.** Extended military exercises around Taiwan push shipping insurance up on Asia routes.

In Council, the Union's leverage over chipmaking equipment is deliberately held back. The insurers, meanwhile, quietly reprice cyber cover for essential operators, citing the autumn intrusions, and several finance ministries prepare backstops without saying so.

By June, Europe is measurably better defended against a repeat of last year's rehearsal, more exposed to a different kind of shock, and still waiting for its own compute to arrive.
