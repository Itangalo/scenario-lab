# Permanent residents

> **20 January 2029.** The new US administration takes office on moratoriums and job guarantees. It offers Europe sympathy, and no bandwidth.

Washington slows its own frontier push for domestic reasons. The winter turns on two technical shocks anyway.

> **February 2029.** A leading laboratory demonstrates a sharp leap on tasks with checkable answers – code, mathematics, intrusion tools. Security teams find that attacks which used to take a team can now be drafted in hours.
>
> **March 2029.** New architectures go into service whose intermediate steps can no longer be read. The tools that flagged suspicious plans by reading a model's drafts go dark overnight.

Engineers call it efficiency. The overseers call it blindness, and are left with pass-fail tests and slow laboratory probes.

> **April 2029.** An open model almost as capable as the best is copied hundreds of thousands of times in days, the add-ons that let it act on its own included. Running it no longer takes a laboratory.

Municipal IT staff joke grimly that the exploit kits are now permanent residents.

Sofia's patch is ready for them. Pre-packed offline recovery kits, locked-down backups and reserve licences go out to towns and hospitals, and emergency money is tied to streaming live network data and pathogen sequencing into the allied joint command. The health and cyber agencies' staff finally sit in the same room. When the spring phishing waves come, services degrade rather than stop.

The President keeps the Commission's political capital on the build, and politics moves the other way. Poland signs its own cloud and chip deal with a hyperscaler – one of the giant cloud companies – from outside the Union, breaking the common line on delivery to Europe and vetting of buyers. Brussels threatens to withhold recovery funds and offers joint buying with Japan and Korea instead. The gigafactory site in mediation is held to a final go-ahead. The second site stays in court.

The tech sovereignty package for data centres and the tie to the allied command both close, with permits and staff in place. European papers lead with unreadable models and fear for jobs. The assistants stay popular, and trust slips.
