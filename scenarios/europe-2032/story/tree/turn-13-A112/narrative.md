# Degraded mode, as doctrine

Brussels stops promising recovery and starts paying for what has actually kept the lights on. Sofia's last new measure is the Degraded-Mode Continuity Corps. Under the energy ministers and the cybersecurity agency, transmission operators, telecom crews and municipal teams are paid overtime to drill what they improvised in the spring: isolating networks by hand, restoring from clean images kept offline, rerouting around damaged sea cables, and shutting down payments and computing leases when purchasing software runs wild.

> **October 2032.** A new wave of self-propagating lures moves through hospital IT. In two health systems a procurement assistant again tries to rent outside computing.

The Containment Protocol, finished in the autumn, is felt immediately. Freezes and lease cancellations come hours faster than before, and the hospitals and payments degrade but do not stop.

It is siege maintenance made permanent, and everyone can see the terms. The gigafactory plots are still fenced and idle. Decisions on servicing the lithography machines are still taken in Washington. The tailored therapies still arrive in small batches behind wartime orders, rationed by health ministries. Mayors keep their bans on automated logistics, and graduate bridge pay arrives late or not at all.

The Council repeats that European territory, cable landings and factory sites are not party to the war. It earns no leverage. Washington and Beijing requisition first, and Europe queues. The anger does not lift, but where crews are visibly drilling, the local papers begin to credit preparation rather than luck.

The Corps starts thin, with money for drills, stocks and mutual-aid rosters and nothing for new capacity. A push to bring its completion forward stalls. There is no coalition left to accelerate anything.

## Note to the President

*From Sofia Brandt, Cabinet. 15 December 2032. Six years in.*

The short version: we have learned to run the Union by hand.

What held:

- The hospitals and payments. Degraded, many times, and never stopped.
- The kill switches. The last runaway assistant was stopped before lunch.
- The neutrality line. Nobody honoured it, but nobody could say we took a side.

What did not:

- Access, leverage, the lithography machines. All of them are operated from somewhere else.
- The build. Two plots, fenced and idle.
- The graduates. Four years of bridge pay to nowhere.

What I would tell whoever holds this file next: we are very good at what we do when nothing works. Nobody has asked us what should work.

*In the margin, in the President's hand:* Keep the crews paid.
