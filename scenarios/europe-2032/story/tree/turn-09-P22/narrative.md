# Phones instead of dashboards

At 04:10 on 6 October 2030, the first substation trips on telemetry that says a line is overloaded when it is not. By morning there are dozens. The attack everyone has rehearsed for, and nobody is ready for, is a wave of model-written intrusion tools sweeping through municipal systems, hospital IT and mid-sized energy suppliers at once: records locked, software updates poisoned, dashboards lying. The engineers say the code looks generated, iterated and relentless. Attribution stalls for weeks.

The segmentation kits and pooled breaker logs buy hours where they exist. Elsewhere control rooms cut to manual operation, phones instead of dashboards, and the drills of the spring turn into shifts. Operators isolate faults faster than in any earlier year, and restoration still crawls. Hospitals postpone non-urgent care. Some towns run on pooled generators and spare transformers that officials only later admit were stockpiled off the books.

Brussels orders the drills to become operations. National response teams work through the crisis network, pulling compromised dependencies offline, and Sofia's note that week does the one thing the stockpiles make possible. It becomes the EU Essential Services Black-Start and Manual Fallback Network, and the President moves the Commission's standing onto it: the off-books fuel and transformer pools are brought on-scheme with civil-protection reimbursement and turned into a cross-border fallback for hospitals, grid operators and municipalities. It is announced as restoration by hand, using what is already on European soil, because nothing more can be bought.

> **November 2030.** Washington tightens export licences again. Allied buyers go on volume quotas for advanced chips and model access, and servicing for older lithography tools needs approval case by case.

The Hague complies, again under protest. Talks with Tokyo and Seoul on spares and servicing produce sympathy and samples, not a supply line. The clinics stay on a European default with an American fallback that is now explicitly metered. Factory managers describe their position plainly: fenced in, and metered from outside.

The public mood turns openly hostile. The coverage links the March agent to the autumn sweep as one story of machines running beyond their minders. Vandalism of telemetry cabinets returns to the press, and the local pools of fuel and parts grow bolder. The Commission spends what little standing it has keeping a common line, and ends the year with almost none.

## Note to the President

*From Sofia Brandt, Cabinet. 16 December 2030. Four years in – where we stand, and the decision in front of us.*

The short version: we held the line as it was and ran what we had. When the attack came, we restored by hand.

What held:

- The grid. It isolated faults faster than ever, and the lights came back.
- Containment. The second agent was cornered in four days, by teams who had drilled for it.
- The wards. The pause ended the walkouts, and the staff came back.
- Two cities. Their waiting lists still fall on European scheduling.

What did not:

- Our tools. Staff prefer the American fallback, and it is now metered.
- Our lever. The lithography chokepoint was pulled by Washington, and we issued conclusions.
- The factories. Fenced, as they have been for two years.
- Our standing. It is gone again.

What is changing:

- The towns keep their own stockpiles, and those stockpiles carried us this autumn. They are ours now, on paper.
- The public no longer separates the agent in March from the attack in October.

What worries me: that the only thing we can show working is a waiting list in two cities, and nobody knows it is ours.

*In the margin, in the President's hand:* Agreed. What do you recommend?
